What’s the luckiest you’ve ever felt?

I jolted awake in a horrible falling sensation. Light boiled my eyes. I covered my face. I felt the light still, clawing through my fingers and pounding on my eyelids. My mouth was dry as salt and I was hungry. Deeply hungry. I opened my eyes. I saw my feet on the floor, my knees bent. I was sitting on a plastic chair. It was faded, like it had been bleached in the sun. There was a drain in the floor. I lifted my gaze. Concrete wall in front of me. At my eye level a window. The light. That’s where it was coming from. Bright, bold colours played on the other side of the glass and filtered in. Leaning to one side, I could dodge some of the twitching beams. Concrete walls bore in on me on both sides. At my left and right, more windows, at the same height of the first. I could only see more concrete through them. I looked closer through the window to my right.

The top of a head peeked at me. I sat up and saw a woman with pearl white hair. Her eyes were wide as dinnerplates, and she was staring out the front window. One of my ears popped. In rushed the sound of the place. First, the gurgling. There was something, some liquid, babbling from the drain below. It was running downstream, from my right to my left. Then, distant popping. Like corn kernels. Then I heard something familiar. A sound that lit up something behind my eyes. Like a hit of sugar or the first toke of a cigarette. I heard the sound of the machines, and I felt drool pooling in the corner of my mouth.  
I wiped my face quickly and listened for that noise. Yes, there was no mistaking it. The cacophony, the *digital* chorus of slot machines. The whirring and chiming. I heard the panicked glory of a jackpot. *Pop*. The woman to my right was listening too. The cell on my left was empty. Behind me, the light from the window jittered around my shadow. There was light panel where the wall met the ceiling. I blinked the sleep out of my eyes to read it.

JACKPOT

$243,977.65

I craned my neck to look into Pearl’s room. She had a number too. 392.12. She watched her window, rocking back and forth in her chair. Mumbling. I tried to stand but my legs weren’t ready. I leaned close to her window.

‘Hey.’

Her ear twitched but she stayed fixed on the window before her. Beams scored her face. What should have been the whites of her eyes were a deep pink. Was she not blinking?

‘Hey.’

Louder.

‘Where am I.’

She peeled herself from the screen to look at me. She was pale as silk and quickly snapped back to watch the light. I felt my confusion shrivel and rot into impatience. I shouted.

‘Where am I.’

She just kept on watching the screen. I heard another jackpot. Far away, *pop*. I felt my tongue tingle at the sound again. Pearl stiffened. I felt strong enough to stand. I came closer to her window. I watched beyond, heads occasionally bobbing up into view on her left. A pinkish light filtering through the infinite cells. Her mutterings leaned up against the glass.  
*Don’t*. *Don’t give him. Not the cherries*.  
Suddenly she snapped to face me.

‘There’s no way out. Sit.’

Not waiting for me to respond she jerked back to the screen, whispering. Her spittle misting the glass. I watched her. Pressing my face against the window. I could see shapes on the screen she was watching. Bars of vertical light, spinning with every colour. Bars on a slot machine. They would slow and come to a stop, clicking into place, one by one. Pearl would lean closer, her muttering would louden, and she would rake her fingers along her palm, ridden with deep red tracks. But the colours would never line up. I felt it stronger now. The tug on my brain. I felt my vision narrow on the screen. They were fruits. Beyond them, a deep shadow twitched minutely. Straining past the slots, licking the corner of my mouth, I saw them finally. The filmy eyes reflecting the screen.

Someone was on the other side of the screen.

I tore myself away and pressed myself up against my own screen. There, enshrined in the sickly colour, the dead gaze of someone on the other side. I waved. The slots flickered and span. I tapped at the glass. Fruits began to grow from the slowing fuzz. I shouted finally.

‘HEY.’ 

Nothing. The sounds of the casino out there were pounding at the glass. I watched him. He would blink quickly, sparingly. As if he couldn’t bear to miss a second. His face was haggard, his hair long and greasy. He would lift a hand up to the screen and flick at the spinning slots. Was he trying to hurry them along?  
I remembered suddenly.  
Sitting on the other side. My own hand over the same numb screen. I felt something tracking down my cheek and wiped my face. Was I crying? Drool slipped out the corner of my mouth again. The light still burned my eyes and I turned away. Standing up, holding the chair in case my legs quit, I took in the cubicle. The walls were seamless, with no sign of a door, only a metal grate near the floor behind the chair. A trapdoor? There was no light on the ceiling.

I realised how dark the room was. How bright it was out there. God, I could not remember my own name but I *knew* I wanted to be in there. I *knew* I had spent a lifetime in that bright crackling candy paint world.

Underneath the screen was a small button. Metal, square, polished from a million presses.

DOUBLE THE BET

The drain babbled underneath me. Far away jackpots called like birds in some daydreamt forest. *Pop. Pop pop*. I sat down. Reached for the button and felt its engraved surface. It was oddly warm. A shriek from Pearl’s room stole my attention. She was lashing out at the screen, babbling. Cherries. Fucking cherries shit piss cherries. The screen behind her: 155.3. I snatched at the memories of these last twenty minutes. Had it always been that way? No. No, it couldn’t have been. It was three something. White hot and immediate another memory stung me. A screen. Words and numbers flashing in and out over a line of bananas.

JACKPOT  
JACKPOT  
JACKPOT  
$256.55

Back to the shrieking. I shook my head to clear it. This only made me dizzy. I had to put my hand against the concrete wall to stay steady. *Fucking cherries*. I hit the window a few times. She kept on. I felt angry. Really fucking angry. Why was I here? Locked in with crazies. No memories outside of a casino. I slammed her window with an open palm and it shook a little. She whimpered and quieted, returning to her loony mumbling.  
I looked back at the button. It caught the light above it from the window. Softened it. Glistened like a gem. I felt the rage die down and desire let itself in. God, I wanted to press that button. Why? But I was alone and there were no answers. None except for *cherries.* And there was no way out, or so Pearl said. I reached out for it. Waited. I felt my heart punching in my chest. Was it always beating this fast?

A jackpot jangled somewhere. *Pop*. I flinched. The room was the same. Pearl still babbled to herself. The light still hurt my eyes. I felt the engraved letters on the button and I remembered. Pressing the button. Double the bet. Sliding off a golden ring. Passing it under the glass window at a pawn shop. Back in front of the machine, feeling my fingers where the ring used to hug. Where it had clung for years. Back in front of the machine. Pressing the button. I would get it all back. All of it back and more.

Another jackpot. Close, loud, ringing in my ear. A scream. And a great clap.  
Like the sound of a thick balloon popping. Something wet slapped the window on my right.  The light coming through went pink. There were little pieces sticking to its surface, slowly tracking down, like a little girl’s birthday cake had been hurled against it. Pearl was gone. I stood up, shaky. What remained of her slumped against the wall, legs hanging over the chair. Her legs jerked senselessly. Her neck abruptly stopped in a stump of glistening gore. Her twitching palm ran red with deep tracks of blood, and buried within was a single long white nail, torn from its bed to stand like a milky obelisk in the crimson river. Far away, calling to me now like sirensong, rang the jackpot, *pop*, jackpot, *pop,* the compressed, synthetic moment of triumph, then the sound of its terrible prize. I saw myself again, sat in front of the machine.  
I felt the popping candy in my brain as the machine rang out, watching the slots click into place, the screen spotted with dead pixels and cigarette burns jumping into frenzy, and seated behind the orchestra, that great chorus of momentary appeasement, Pearl had popped and now her screen, coated with thick blood and gray matter, blinked:

$0  
$0  
$0 

I won’t write any more. Can’t. Won’t. There’s things I need to do. She’s knocking again on the door. I have enough money to pay her for two more weeks. Then what? 

I won’t write any more today.
