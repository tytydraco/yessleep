The polar night in Nefteyugansk was heavy, freezing the whole city in pitch darkness. The dash thermometer in our armored truck showed minus thirty-two. The cabin smelled of ozone, gun grease, and shitty instant coffee from a thermos.

I sat in the passenger seat, slowly squeezing a steel hand gripper in my jacket pocket. Forty-five gigs of a stiff metal spring gave in to my fingers one, two, three. It kept me grounded. Inside my vest, under all that heavy tactical fabric, my heart was racing like crazy. Tachycardia. The doc at the Transneft clinic told me it was just a sudden growth spurt, but in the Breach Containment Unit, you don't argue with your pulse. You don't control your motor, you get eaten. Simple as that.

The radio on the dash crackled, cutting through the heavy white noise. The dispatcher from the Rosneft central HQ sounded completely dead inside:

"Post three, heads up. Sector Four, a five-story residential building on Naberezhnaya Street, building fourteen. We have a confirmed Space Breach in the basement. Repeat, the spawn point is in total shadow."

I stopped squeezing the steel right away. Everything inside me went cold. A Breach in a basement is the absolute worst. If a purple tear opens up out on the street, the sun and daylight shrink the shadows' lifespan to a couple of minutes. But in a pitch-black basement where no light ever gets in, a Space Breach becomes permanent. It will just sit there endlessly pumping out entities until the Unit burns it to the ground.

"What about the Saturation Phase?" my partner asked grimly, slamming the truck into gear and flooring it.

The radio groaned back with a heavy sigh from the dispatcher:

"It's fucked. There was a civilian shelter set up in that basement. The local housing utility didn't check the locks. Clean Breach Shadows got in through the vents. Eleven consumptions confirmed so far. The entity gained mass and went into 'Fat One' status. The biomass analyzer just triggered the max form-retention timer. You have exactly ninety-nine hours before total biological decay."

"Fuck," my partner muttered, gripping the wheel. "A hundred-hour tank in a residential building."

When a clean Shadow comes out of a Breach, it looks like a completely black human silhouette, like it was cut out of the night itself. No weight, no flesh, you can't stop it with a bullet. But when that shadow finds a human, it eats their life in seconds and steals their physical mass. The thing becomes real. Bones snap, muscles blow up, and a heavy, fleshy killing machine is born. Eleven people gave this bastard enough juice to walk around on its own for almost four days. It didn't even need the Breach to stay alive anymore.

Five minutes later, the truck screeched to a halt in the courtyard. The streetlights around the building were already dead - the "Fat" Shadow was siphoning power straight from the grid. We jumped out. I had my full assault kit on. The heavy ballistic helmet was pressing on my skull, but the wide visor on the new PMK-4 mask gave a perfect view without any blur. I grabbed the heavy "Helios" quartz projector - our main weapon.

"The plan is basic," my partner said, racking the bolt on his incendiary rifle. "We don't fight this mountain of meat head-on in the dark. We go for the source. We break into basement, find the purple tear, and blind it with directed UV light. Cut the connection to the Breach - and the 'Fat One' loses stability. Its hundred-hour clock will drop to zero in seconds. Ready?"

I took a deep breath, forcing my heart to slow down. My gloved fingers locked onto the projector's handle with a rock-solid, iron grip.

"Ready. Let's move."

We charged right at the heavy metal basement door, with a hundred hours of permanent darkness waiting on the other side.
