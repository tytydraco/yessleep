White, sandy beaches, palm trees swaying with the breeze, a picture perfect coast line, what the hell could be scary about any of that? 

Guess you’ve never been out after dark around here, sober that is. Plenty people like walk around at night, often late. I stay getting choke dummies and drunk tourists stumbling into my lobby because it’s got white shiny marble tiles and AC. Can count on one hand the number of sober people that have showed up. 

I’ve worked at this hotel a little over a year now. I not going name em name cuz I no like get fired. Boss going get pissed probably, mostly cuz it’ll scare away the tourists. The things i’ve seen, however, give me plenty chicken skin, like walking through downtown at night. 

Now, if drunk people were all I had to deal with at night, I could manage that. They’re easy enough to point in the right direction back to whatever hole those fakas crawled out of, and on the off chance someone like act up HPD comes pretty quick. Thing is, they only make up about little bit of the traffic I get in the lobby. 

The real traffic comes from, well, I’m not really sure what to call them. Boss says I shouldn’t put too much thought into it, “try look wit ur eyes and shaddap with the mouth” he’d say. 

The Nightshift Employee handbook was made specifically for our hotel and specifically for use by the nightshift. Dayshift aren’t even allowed to know about it but its kind of an open secret. The handbook get plenty pages about spooky kine action and quick reference guides to make em go away, or just not get killed, as well as overall nightshift occurrences on an hour to hour basis.

9:00 PM is when my shift officially starts. For nightshift I am always to arrive no earlier than half an hour before my shift starts. If I arrive earlier than 8:30 I am to wait somewhere off property until 8:31 PM at the earliest. I asked my boss about this one and he said it had something to do with union agreements, I kinda tuned him out cuz I don’t really give a fuck about the hotel union shit, dues are expensive. 

From 9:00 PM to 11:00 PM there should be few to no people in the lobby area, less than 5 people at any given moment excluding staff. After 11:00 its ok for more people to be present in the lobby area.

11:00 PM to 12:00 PM is when our “guests” start to arrive. 

I could try to describe these “guests” but honestly, kinda hard. Like, when you see someone at the bus stop but like they aren’t tweaking or whatever but like, you know? They just give off that feeling? That gnawing unsettling feeling that when you look at them they can tell, they can hear what you think, that they’re going to jump you if you think something that provokes them? That searing, agonizing anxiety that courses through your bones when your brain realizes it can’t make sense of what it’s seeing? When it realizes that what’s been sitting in my lobby this whole time has never… 

I don’t get paid to think about what the hell those “guests” are, so I try not think about em too much. Boss says that if I got paid to think I’d be doing his job. Boss says he fuckin hates his job. 

Whenever a guest is in the lobby area I am not to leave my desk under any circumstances. No matter what.

Gotta go bathroom? Use a water bottle or “the bucket” and notify sanitation. Get heart attack? Call your supervisor and then call 911. Anything else? Call your boss but stay put. 

These “guests” will usually sit on the lobby couches for a few hours and then leave without ever interacting with anyone or doing anything remotely weird at all. This is the ideal case and they are ideal guests who are always welcome at our hotel. 

The less than ideal cases are when they interact with other, regular, hotel guests. I haven’t ever been in earshot of them when its happened, boss says its better that way. These “guests” are not like the others and do not represent “guests” as a whole and as such are not welcome at our hotel.

Once one of our patrons accidentally bumped one of the “guests” as he stumbled his way over the lobby furniture. I told the faka plenty times to stop fucking around and had just gotten off the phone with police when it happened. At first it seemed like nothing happened. Then the “guest” turned towards the drunk and appeared to say something to him as he mantled another set of lobby couches. He then fell forward as he did last time, diving into the couches and out of my sight. 

He did not come back up. 

When HPD got here the “guest” got up and left. The officers didn’t attempt to stop him and just stepped aside before coming straight to me. I told them what had happened and they quickly began searching for the man. Other than some sandy footprints around the lobby they couldn’t find the drunk. They left for the night and told us to call if it came back. 

Fast forward to the following day and I see the drunk’s face plastered all over social media as a missing person’s report. As of my writing this he still hasn’t been found. 

When I told my boss about this he seemed surprised that there wasn’t a mess this time. I tried asking him what he meant by that but said I’d have to clean “the bucket” if I kept asking. 

There is one other incident I am aware of where a “guest” had interacted with a night shift clerk, but beyond that I know nothing about it. Every attempt I make to bring it up with my boss he just shuts down. Says that I should stop trying to piss off our customers and just let them be. He says just follow the book and you’ll be fine.

After the initial period of “guests” arriving the activity slows down again until about 2:00 AM, this is the only timeframe that is noted as being flexible in the handbook. At 2:00 AM busses will arrive from a tour company that I am fairly certain doesn’t exist and drop off many regular hotel guests who will all need to be checked in. Apart from their insanely odd arrival time and their suspicious tour busses they seem to be normal every day hotel guests. Now there were a few times where a “guest” would also be dropped off along side the others but they would usually find a secluded corner and sit until eventually leaving as the others do. This is not very strange at all and is completely normal as they are paying customers who are always welcome at our hotel. 

No matter how many people there are or how long I seem to take, I always manage to check the last person in exactly at 3:00 AM. I mean like, the millisecond I hand them their room key the clock ticks over. Handbook doesn’t mention that, boss doesn’t care, but it still freaks me out. 

From 3:00 AM to 6:00 AM is considered a dangerous period. By this time most of if not all the “guests” along with hotel patrons will have vacated the lobby if they aren’t already doing so. They are simply getting a good view.

I have had the misfortune of encountering one phenomenon during this time, out of the many different ones listed. 

The night marchers.

Well, encounter is strong word. Heard is more accurate. Heard means you’ve survived, seen mean’s you’re dead. 

The night marchers are an old legend here in Hawaii, and everybody get one aunty or uncle or cousin who swears they seen or encountered em before, each story always a little different depending on who’s telling it but ultimately here’s what I experienced.

First thing you hear is the Pū, a conch shell that was used in ancient times to signal important events and especially arrivals. While not uncommon in Waikiki with the myriad of Hula shows and Luaus often using them, they are non-existant at night time and should you ever hear one in the wilderness you must do exactly as I did.

You must immediately strip naked. Everything. Including jewelry. Place your clothes in a folded pile off to the side and any jewelry or valuables in front of you like an offering. Then you must get on your hands and knees and bow, like you’re kissing the ground, shrink yourself as low to the ground as you can. By this time you should hear a rhythmic drumming, even if you don’t just stay exactly like that until you see sunlight. Until either the drumming passes and fades or the sunlight hits your eyes you must remain on the ground. If for some godforsaken reason you see torches immediately avert your eyes and beg for forgiveness out loud until the sun comes up. 

Under no circumstances should you ever look towards the source of those sounds if you hear them in the middle of the night. In the old days, before the Kingdom era, such sounds would herald the descent of the Ali‘i from the mountains, as they were considered living gods the punishment for simply looking upon them was death. At least thats the story of them I grew up with. 

It took a while for me to be able to look my boss in the eye after that night, he didn’t really seem to mind it though, comes with the job. Granted he lived out west side and apparently had shit like that happen to him a lot so I guess it made him more tolerable to seeing naked men crouched on the ground in terror. 

I’ve got lots of stories to tell, and tons of time to do it when I’m not baby sitting the “guests” in the lobby, our most esteemed and gracious patrons who have done no wrong to our staff and our establishment. Boss said that I should journal this anyway, maybe he even wants me to tell these stories, might draw more visitors, more esteemed guests to our hotel. 

They’ll know where to find us.
