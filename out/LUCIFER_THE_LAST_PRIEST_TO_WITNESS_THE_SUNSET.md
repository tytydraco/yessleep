*A set of night-watch instructions from a monastery — 1897, Vesper Monastery.*  
The owner of the monastery, Father Elias, was found dead on October 17, 1897. He had served as a priest at Vesper Monastery for forty-three years. Those who knew him claimed that he had behaved strangely during his final years.  
A man who once watched every sunrise had, during the last three years of his life, stopped going anywhere near the windows. He demanded that his meals be left outside his door and refused to let any of the monks enter his room.  
Two days before his death, he stopped every clock in the monastery.  
When he died, no body was found in his room.  
There was only a deep impression on his bed, as if someone had been sitting there for a very long time. A Latin sentence had been carved into the wall, and a sealed envelope had been left on his desk.  
Written on the envelope were the words:  
*To the new night watchman. After Elias's death.*  
The monastery authorities decided not to close the building. Instead, they continued the night watches, claiming that the ancient manuscripts and sacred relics in the basement had to be protected.  
On the day you were hired, you were given a key, an old pocket watch, and the following instructions.  
The man who handed you the envelope said something peculiar:  
"Elias was a good man. But toward the end, he stopped praying to God. We never discovered why."  
Then he fell silent.  
"One more thing... Don't assume that everything you see at night belongs here."  
**VESPER MONASTERY**  
Shift begins: 22:00 — Shift ends: 06:00  
**Rule 1)** The monastery's main entrance must be locked at 22:00. If someone asks to enter after that time, look at their feet first. If their feet are touching the ground, do not open the door. Those who wish to enter this monastery have forgotten how to walk.  
**Rule 2)** You may encounter monks in the corridors. They may greet you with a nod. You may return the gesture. However, if one of them smiles at you, do not stop walking. None of the monks smile. Especially the dead ones.  
**Rule 3)** Never enter Father Elias's room. If you notice light coming from beneath the door, report it to the administration. If you hear Elias coughing inside, do nothing. During his final week, he had already lost the ability to cough.  
**Rule 4)** All paintings throughout the monastery must face the walls. If you notice that a painting has turned around by itself, close your eyes and wait ten seconds. If you recognize the face in the painting when you open them, do not tell anyone what you saw tonight.  
**Rule 5)** If you count thirteen candles instead of twelve in the chapel, stop counting. Do not attempt to extinguish the extra candle. Someone needs its light.  
**Rule 6)** If the bells ring at midnight, do not check the time. Bells are only supposed to ring for the dead. However, no funerals are ever held at Vesper. The dead are not buried here. They are kept waiting.  
**Rule 7)** If you hear children laughing from the basement, do not approach the stairs. There have never been any children at this monastery. Yet Elias constantly mentions a child in his diaries. Whenever he describes it, he uses the same sentence:  
*"It looks older than I am."*  
**Rule 8)** Do not look into the mirror at the end of the corridor. Records indicate that this mirror was removed from the monastery in 1841. Despite this, it reappears every night. If your reflection moves before you do, look away. Do not wait for it to imitate you.  
**Rule 9)** If the chapel door opens at 01:15, do not look at the person inside. It is normal to see someone praying in the chapel at this hour. However, if their hands are joined in the wrong direction, there is no one praying in there.  
**Rule 10)** You may find Latin inscriptions on the monastery walls. Most are passages from the Bible. However, if you find your own name written on a wall, do not read it aloud. There is a sentence in Elias's final diary:  
*"Something that knows your name does not need to call you."*  
**ADDITIONAL INSTRUCTIONS — THINGS THE AUTHORITIES DO NOT KNOW**  
This section was recovered from a second envelope found in Father Elias's room.  
**Rule 11)** If a monk appears at the end of the corridor at 02:00, do not examine his face too closely. From a distance, it may look familiar. The closer he gets, the more you will realize that something about his face is wrong. His eyes are slightly too close together, his mouth sits lower than it should, and his nose looks as though it was attached afterward.  
He may smile as he approaches.  
Do not smile back.  
**Rule 12)** If a monk asks you, "Where is Elias?", do not tell him that Elias is dead. Instead, say, "At the morning service." After hearing this, the monk will lower his head and walk away.  
However, if you do not hear footsteps as he leaves, do not look behind you.  
**Rule 13)** You may encounter someone sitting at the same table as you in the dining hall. This person may ask about your childhood, your family, or a memory you have never shared with anyone.  
You are not required to answer.  
But if that person takes a childhood photograph of you out of their pocket, do not look at it. Everyone in the photograph may be facing the same direction.  
Except you.  
**Rule 14)** At 03:00, human silhouettes may appear in every window of the monastery. They are not standing outside. They are not on the other side of the glass.  
There is nothing behind the windows.  
They are simply watching.  
If one of them raises a hand to greet you, do not return the gesture. If all of them raise their hands simultaneously, go to your room and lock the door.  
**Rule 15)** Do not open the black book on Elias's desk. It is said to contain no writing whatsoever. Yet, on certain nights, something can be seen moving between its pages.  
There are three symbols on its cover:  
An inverted crown.  
A serpent devouring its own tail.  
A broken sun with an eye at its center.  
A small note beside these symbols reads:  
*"When a king loses his throne, he does not become poor. Only the borders of his kingdom change."*  
**Rule 16)** If you hear your name whispered at 03:33, do not respond. The voice may sound very close. Sometimes, it may even sound as though it is coming from your own mouth.  
But remember something more important:  
Just because something knows you does not mean it knows who you are.  
**Rule 17)** Just before sunrise, you may see a white-haired man standing at the chapel entrance. He may resemble Elias. Do not approach him.  
If he says, "At last, you've arrived," begin searching for a way out of the monastery.  
Because you are not the first person to arrive here after Elias's death.  
But you will not be the first person to leave, either.  
**FATHER ELIAS'S FINAL DIARY**  
*04:00 — Two hours remain until the end of your shift.*  
You find another page hidden behind the envelope. The handwriting belongs to Elias.  
"People misunderstand the Devil.  
They imagine him as a horned creature who lives in darkness, a monster that appears to frighten people.  
Yet the Devil's greatest achievement is making people believe they already know what he looks like.  
Lucifer is not merely a name. It is a memory. The memory of something that once belonged to the heavens, before it fell from them.  
I saw him for the first time at sunset.  
I was watching through the chapel window. As the sun approached the horizon, every monk fell silent. None of them looked at one another.  
Then I noticed.  
Their eyes were all the same.  
They were all looking toward the place where the sun was setting.  
But they were not watching the sun.  
Something inside the sun was watching them."  
On the next page, the handwriting changes.  
"A person's face allows us to recognize them. But a face can be a mask.  
Sometimes, when you look into someone's eyes, you realize there is no human being behind them. Sometimes, you realize this too late.  
They have learned how to smile.  
But they do not know when they are supposed to."  
Beneath the final line, there is a sun symbol that has been drawn over and over again.  
**THE FINAL INSTRUCTION — THE SUNSET RHYME**  
It is 04:47.  
Every light in the monastery goes out simultaneously.  
Footsteps begin to echo from the chapel. First one pair, then two, then dozens...  
A voice rises from the corridor.  
This time, it is not a man's voice.  
Dozens of identical voices speak at once.  
You find a message on the back cover of Elias's diary, one that you had not noticed before:  
*"If you wish to know them, wait for the sunset. But never smile while looking into their eyes."*  
Then you hear a nursery rhyme.  
A child's voice, soft and cheerful, begins to sing:  
**"Have you ever seen the sunset in their eyes?**  
**Have you ever seen their eyes at sunset?**  
**Have you ever wondered if they're smiling?**  
**The sun goes down, the shadows grow,**  
**A pair of eyes watches from below.**  
**Look once, and they become two,**  
**Look twice, and they recognize you.**  
**Now tell me, my dear friend...**  
**When the sun goes down, which one of you disappears?"**  
The rhyme ends.  
The clock reads 05:00.  
You look through the window.  
The sun has not risen yet.  
But the sky is orange.  
Twelve monks stand in the courtyard. All of them are facing you. None of them move.  
Among them stands Father Elias.  
All thirteen have the same smile.  
After a while, one of them tilts his head slightly.  
The other twelve do exactly the same.  
Then they all open their mouths.  
But only Elias speaks:  
"Have you read the rules?"  
You nod.  
Elias smiles.  
"Then why haven't you read Rule Eighteen?"  
You look at the paper on your desk.  
There were seventeen rules before.  
Now there is an eighteenth.  
**Rule 18)** If you are watching the sunset from inside the monastery, the monks outside can see you. If you are watching the sunset from outside the monastery, those inside can see you.  
**Never make sure that both sides can see you.**  
There is a small signature beneath it.  
**LUCIFER.**  
Suddenly, you hear your own voice from behind the door:  
"May I come in?"  
Your phone rings.  
A message appears on the screen.  
**INCOMING MESSAGE**  
**05:01**  
"Father Elias never died.  
He simply stopped watching the sunset.  
Now it is your turn."  
A shadow stretches beneath the door.  
But it is not extending into the hallway.  
It is stretching into your room.  
And for the final time, you hear Elias's voice:  
**"The sun is rising, my child. It is time for us to open our eyes."**
