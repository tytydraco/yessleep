I took up a job at a lighthouse.

It wasn't anything particularly special. 

A rather isolated place, far from the city, with little more to offer than the occasional passing ship and the endless sound of the sea. 

A cabin with basic necessities, a bed, a kitchen, a working radio, an outhouse and working heat.

The work was simple enough, and the solitude was something I had grown accustomed to. 

I suppose that's why I took the position in the first place.

There wasn't much to do besides maintaining the equipment, keeping the light running, and occasionally checking the surrounding coastline.

At least, that was how it started.

One morning as I was doing my scheduled maintenance of the light I saw something in the distance, it was, like a big lump on the ocean surrounded by oil, at first I thought it was a crashed ship, i’ve heard stories of cargos hitting reefs or bays and sinking but it was so far in the distance, there couldn’t possibly have hit anything. 

The lump sunk slowly back into the water, living this big puddle of black on the surface.

I decided to think nothing of it at first, but as I went down back to my cabin, I just kept looking over my shoulder, at the ocean, at the puddle it left.

Even in bed I couldn’t stop thinking about it, i tossed and turned almost all night before finally getting some rest.

On the second day I had been listening to the radio, old country music, not a bad choice. 

But then for some reason the radio stopped, and thats when I heard a sort of music, like wind coming out of an organ in church, but it was deep and resonated through the ground.

I got out to see what it was, despite the wind and the rain, I climb up the lighthouse and looked out, there it was, the lump, i felt almost relieved seeing it, it looked a bit closer now.  

I couldn't tell you how long I sat out here, just looking out at the sea from the top of the lighthouse.

Three... maybe four days. I don't even think I slept.

I just watched it breath, with the deep slow rising of it’s back.

I couldn't look away.

I wanted to leave, to rest, to eat, but something was keeping me here. It was mesmerizing.

The waves knocking against its sides as it rests above the water, its chest rising and falling with the rhythm of the tide.

Its voices howling across the ocean, intertwining into something resembling an orchestral melody.

I didn't dare leave the top. 

I didn't think about anything. 

I just couldn't bear to miss a second of it.

It was beautiful.

And yet, so far away.

I've tried looking through the binoculars, but they hardly do it justice. 

Whatever it is, it seems to spend most of its time submerged, occasionally rising just enough for me to catch a glimpse of its back before disappearing beneath the waves again.

And yet it kept above the water for almost five days now, like it was a personal reward for waiting.

I've started keeping track of its appearances. 

At first, I thought they were becoming more frequent, but I can no longer be certain, maybe it just never left. 

The days seemed to blend together, and the nights were hardly distinguishable from one another.

I didn't mind.

I had all the time in the world.

It'd been almost two weeks now.

I could see it clearly now. It's was close, closer than it's ever been.

Reaching out my hand, I feel as though I could almost touch it.

I could clearly see its beauty. Its burning amber eyes staring back at me, its black back glistening in the moonlight piercing through the clouds.

It simply stays there, unmoving in the water, its tar-like back leaking around it, creating a black pool of colourful reflections dancing through the night.

Sometimes, I think it looks back at me.

I wonder if it knows I'm here.

I wish it did.

I know it did.

I had been there for a month.

I stopped counting the days some time ago. 

There was little reason to, anyway. 

The lighthouse still stood, the light still turned, and the creature was still there.

I've started talking to it.

Nothing particularly important. Just little things, really. 

Things about my day, things I remember, things I wish I could tell someone. 

I don't know if it understood me, but I like to think it listened.

Its calls had become more familiar, too. 

I can almost distinguish one from another, like notes in a song I've heard a thousand times.

I think it knows me now.

I caught a glimpse of it out of the water, it jumped out.

It’s bigger than anything i’ve ever seen. 

It’s nose was dull and narrow, it’s flippers wide and sharp like a knife, it’s tail seemed to have thinned near the edges where a sail like paddle rested at the end of it, connected to one continuous girth, a body like a tree trunk.

I could see clearly what it was. 

It was a sort of whale.

It seemed to effortlessly pierce the water without so much as a noise.

But I didn’t hear much of anything anymore, it’s call was all my ears would receive.

I would go mad in the silence.

It had been almost 4 months since I came here. 

It was right in front of me, looking up at me.

Its amber eyes shining in the stormy night.

I couldn't help but smile at its magnificence.

For the first time, I could see almost all of it without binoculars. Its enormous silhouette beneath the water, its pale underside occasionally catching the faint light from above. It is so much larger than I ever imagined.

I reached out my hand, desperate to feel its skin from on top of the lighthouse.

But as soon as it saw my hand, it sank back into the depths.

I don't know why, but I felt a terrible sense of dread.

I wanted to follow it. 

I wanted to call out to it, to ask it to come back, but the words wouldn't leave my mouth.

I waited.

And waited.

The storm grew worse as the night went on. 

Rain battered the windows, and the wind howled around the lighthouse. 

The sea was almost unrecognisable beneath the violent waves.

Thunder was cracking throughout the sky.

I thought it had left.

Until it suddenly jumped out of the water in my direction, its maw wide open, strands of black oil stretching against the wind like a wave of silky black hair.

It happened in less than a second, and yet it felt like an eternity.

I could see the inside of its mouth.

It’s teeth were thin and dull, almost like hay but too far apart to filter water, it looked more like needles.

It’s mouth stretched unbelievably wide, it had no tongue.

It’s throat was dark, so dark no light shined on it.

Like staring into an abyss that desired nothing more than to embrace me.

I wasn't frightened.

I closed my eyes, and spread my arms.

And without any consideration, it gulped me.

And for a moment, just a moment, it felt like I no longer was.

I felt at peace.

I woke up on the shore, near the rocks.

I was soaked, slimy, and dazed.

But I was here.

The storm had passed, and the ground looked dry. 

The sky was clear, almost as though nothing had happened.

I don't remember how I got there.

Or how long i’ve been stranded on the rocks.

The owner of the lighthouse found me and took me back to the city. 

I spent several weeks in the hospital, my system having sustained considerable damage from the salt water.

I didn't care.

It didn't matter to me.

All I could think about was the black whale.

I got back to my apartment after being discharged and immediately called the owner of the lighthouse, asking when I could come back.

He told me I wouldn't be coming back.

Then he hung up.

I didn't understand.

I tried calling him throughout the week. I must have left him a hundred voicemails, but he never answered.

I tried explaining that I was fine, that I could return to work, that there was nothing to worry about.

I even offered to work without pay.

I even screamed at him to let me go back.

Nothing.

Eventually my calls wouldn’t go through.

So eventually, I stopped calling.

Everything felt wrong.

I was left in a world that no longer felt like it belonged to me, or that I belonged to it.

My family tried to call me after learning i was in the hospital, I didn’t answer. 

I didn’t want to talk to anyone. 

I just want to see it.

I don't think I could ever go back to before.

The black whale swallowed me whole, but whatever was spat out, it wasn't me. 

Just some mangled pieces that don't fit together.

I feel lost.

I feel empty.

I feel incomplete.

It feels like my soul was peeled and scorched beyond repair, scattered across the horizon.

Everything is too loud, too dull, too bright.

The world looks dead.

I just wish I could see it again.

I just wish I could see its glistening back.

I just wish I could see its bright amber eyes.

I just wish I could see its white marble underside.

I just wish I could see its multicoloured reflections hitting my eyes, dancing through the tainted water just for me.

I just wish I could see its beauty.

Just once.

I can’t stand it. 

Every day i sit near the ocean, looking out.

The wind is beating in my face and the waves spit on me with each blow on the rocks. 

Hoping to see that familiar shape in the distance. 

Hoping to hear that familiar chant resonate through my body.

Hoping it can fix me.

Nothing else matters.
