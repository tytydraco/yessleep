As a rule, I avoid internet rabbit holes, but I stumbled down this one entirely by accident and found it difficult to claw my way back out.

I was doing research for a novel, wanting to base a house in the novel on one from a book my parents read to me when I was a kid. The story features a cozy house on a rainy night, and a boy who just can't find his favorite bedtime book, no matter how hard he looks. You might know the one.

I wanted a picture for reference, so I did a Google image search and found what I was looking for right away. But among the familiar illustrations, there was a low-resolution photograph of a real house that resembled the house from the story. The houses weren't identical. Whereas the house in the story was inviting and colorful, pink, with yellow trim and a yellow door, its windows all brightly lit in the night, the house in the photo looked abandoned, or at least not very well maintained, its paint chipped and dirty, its windows dark. I followed the link, and it led to the author's website, which had some real old internet vibes: bright colors, silly fonts, low-quality images.

The photo was at the top of a page titled "Did you knwo?" (the typo is the author's, not mine). This page contained a single easter egg about the book: the house in the book was based on a real house that the author and her mother used to live in. Below a side-by-side comparison of book house and real house there were pictures of others who "lived in the house". There was a picture of a dog—a black Brussels Griffon—stretched out on a hardwood floor, with a note about how much the dog likes chasing rats. There was a picture of a parakeet named James that the author claimed could speak 7 languages (but not English). And there was a photo of a worn copy of a children's book that I didn't recognize—apparently the inspiration for the bedtime book in the story. And then at the bottom of the page, there were two photos depicting grotesque, life-size humanoid dolls or puppets in what looked like Victorian dress, with the caption:

"And don't forget…Mary and Marie."

Their bodies looked to have normal, human proportions, but the "normal" pretty much stopped from the neck up. The heads were a little too long and wide, and the facial features were all slightly misaligned—not exactly Picassoesque, but in that ballpark. They both wore these big creepy smiles too, that make my hands get clammy just typing about them. These images were very low-resolution too, making the figures appear particularly distorted and uncanny. They were so unsettling that I actually shut my laptop, just slammed it closed on instinct when I scrolled them into view, and then opened it again very slowly.

Who were these figures, and what did they have to do with this beloved book from my childhood? I spent about half a day and half a night digging, and found nothing. I went through every page of the author's website, watched interviews with the author, scrolled and scrolled through the author's various social media timelines—in their heyday, they had been very active, really allowing the "infinite scroll" to earn its name. But finally, I made it to the bottom. The bottom of a decades-old social media timeline is a place that no light reaches, and there I sat at the bottom, at 1am, having learned nothing more about Mary or Marie.

That night I couldn't sleep. I couldn't stop thinking about Mary and Marie. It wasn't just because the images were unsettling. It was because this was the second time I had encountered Mary and Marie. The first time was over three decades earlier, on a summer night, while babysitting my little sister.

I was 11 at the time, so she would have been 7. We were watching The Lost Boys, because I am a bad brother who thought my little sister would be fine with it, and she was not. She was terrified. She was so scared in fact that it made me scared, even though I wouldn't have been otherwise. We were already on edge and in a weird mood from the movie, and from being home alone at night, and we both all but jumped out of our skin when someone suddenly started banging on the door: an overcomplicated version of the "shave and a haircut" knock, with extra notes, and too rushed to the point that it sounded aggressive, threatening.

The rule was that we were not allowed to answer the door for strangers when our parents were out, but how would we know if they were strangers or not without checking? So I checked.

I tiptoed up to the door, so that if it was a stranger, they wouldn't know we were home, and looked through the peephole. Standing on the porch, their faces bulging in the concave glass of the peephole, were Mary and Marie—though we didn't know them by those names at the time. I turned to my sister, and I must have looked pretty freaked out, because when she saw the look on my face she got freaked out and started crying.

Then the knock again, and we both jumped and my sister screamed a little.

Now they knew we were there, and they started talking to us. It was too hard to hear clearly through the door, and they spoke so low and so strangely that we had no idea what they were saying.

I looked through the peephole again, hoping I could read their lips or something and get a better idea of what they were saying, but their lips weren't moving. One of the pair appeared to be dancing, but it was hard to tell because they were just at the edge of the peephole's field of view. The other continued to speak unintelligibly, and then suddenly stopped speaking, and the other stopped dancing, and they stood very still, looking like papier-mâché statues. Then the one that had been talking started moving its arm in creepy, jerky motions, and appeared to stick its hand inside its stomach, where there must have been a pocket or something, because when it pulled its hand out again, it was holding several colorfully wrapped pieces of candy in shiny foil wrappers, extending the handful right up to the peephole. It smiled in a way that I did not like at all, but the smile was always there because it was wearing a mask or the face was maybe paralyzed and covered in thick makeup, or God knows what.

In those days, we couldn't just call our parents' cell phones, because they didn't exist—or at least not in our lives—and I was afraid to call the police in case we were just overreacting, and these turned out to just be well-intentioned neighbors we just hadn't met yet, or Jehovah's Witnesses or something.

But we knew we wanted to get as far away from them as possible, so we went upstairs to my room. I tried to get a better look at them from my bedroom window, but by the time we got there, they were gone from the front porch. I looked around the yard, but couldn't see any sign of them. I left my sister in my room and I went to her room for a different angle, and couldn't see them from there either. Then I walked into my parents' bedroom, just in time to see one of them walk by the second-story window.

Once my soul returned to my body, I ducked down to the floor, where I hoped someone in the window wouldn't be able to see me, and I crawled across the floor, and stuck my head up into the window to look out, but they had disappeared. I went back to my room and locked the door, and I turned around to see my sister there looking like she had been holding her breath ever since I left the room, and then I followed her gaze to what she was looking at, and there were Mary and Marie standing in the window. They were rocking back and forth on tall wooden stilts. And now they were both speaking in that creepy low way where I couldn't tell what the hell they were saying, but the tone was unpleasant to say the least.

And my sister and I were just frozen at this point, and the pair of them went on like that for a bit, just talking low, and bobbing back and forth on the stilts. And then they started speaking faster, but still low, but faster, and faster still, until the "words" got so fast that it just became a sort of tone, and the whole time they were just staring at us with their uncanny, papier-mâché smiles, their mouths never moving.

My sister had been crying the whole time, and it was getting quickly worse, and the whimpering turned into full-on chest-heaving sobs, and tears and snot covered her face. I took her by the hand and pulled her out of the room to the hall where they couldn't see us, and we couldn't see them either, and as soon as they were out of sight the tone stopped and the house was quiet, and then I heard the door opening downstairs and I had a moment of OCD panic because I didn't remember if I had locked the door after our parents left, or if it was locked when I was at the door looking through the peephole, and I could hear someone coming into the house followed by another someone and I didn't know what to do.

And then we heard Dad saying, "Terrible film, just awful!" and then my sister and I were both crying because we were so relieved.

We told our parents what happened and my dad just laughed and asked, "Did this group of murderous ghouls show up before or after you kids watched the movie about a group of murderous ghouls?" And we were forced to admit that it was after, and my dad was already in a bad mood because the movie they had seen at the theater was bad, and because I had shown my sister a scary movie, and so we dropped it.

For a few weeks after that, it was all my sister and I could talk about—though we made sure to do it only when my parents weren't around. And then over time, the whole thing did take on the quality of a movie, or a nightmare—something imagined, misremembered, and then eventually forgotten altogether.

And then I opened the wrong door to a dusty corner of the old internet, and there they were. They'd been out there all this time, dancing, whispering, and creeping past bedroom windows on impossibly long wooden legs, but now with names to put to those terrible faces: Mary and Marie.
