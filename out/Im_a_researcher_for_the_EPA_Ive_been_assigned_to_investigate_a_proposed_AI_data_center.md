I guess I should start this off by introducing myself.

My name is Adam, and I’m an analyst for the EPA who specializes in determining the environmental impact of industrial projects.

Most of my career has been what you’d imagine in your head. Inspecting oil fields to make sure don’t destroy the local ecosystem. Going over chemical factory plans to verify they dispose of their waste in the least impactful ways. And determining if the proposed steel mill is close enough to the forest to cause a wild fire.

It was boring and a bit tedious, but I liked my job. I do truly believe in preserving and protecting our planet. But my current assignment has me questioning everything. I suppose I should start from the beginning…

I got the call a month ago while I was still on my last project. A small town in Michigan had gotten together and petitioned their local government to get some sort of report done about the environmental impact of a proposed AI data center. I had not done any first hand research or investigation on a data center. Only paying close attention to what’s said in the media and academia, so I was somewhat excited for this opportunity.

I was shocked however, to learn the company pitching the data center had volunteered to foot the bill for any resources or manpower needed for my study. I hope they knew that my palms were not prone to being greased. Even by some of the wealthiest and most influential companies on the planet.

My flight was booked for the day after I finished my current project, which was a bit frustrating considering I’d have no time to see my wife and children in California. They’d have to settle for more zoom calls and FaceTime for another couple months unfortunately. Luckily, my return date was set for a few days before my son’s 5th birthday.

I arrived in Michigan in early spring. I was met in the airport by the local sheriff and the mayor of the town I’d be surveying. The sheriff was a stout man in his mid 50s with a white handlebar moustache. The mayor was a slick, middle aged man standing just a tad taller than me

“Are you Adam?”

The mayor asked through a pearly white, campaign sign grade smile.

“Yes sir, and you are?” I responded.

“I’m mayor Tinsley. And this is sheriff Jewels.”

I nodded and shook both their hands. 

“Nice to meet you both. I assume you’ll be escorting me to my hotel?”

“Absolutely!” The mayor responded.

“We’ll take you to your fine accommodations, and I’ve already arranged for a rental car to be dropped at the hotel for your use during the survey.”

“Wow there still is something to be said about small town hospitality.” I responded cordially.

“Very kind words sir and we thank you.” The mayor responded in kind.

The sheriff however was a man of few words, having nodded in the affirmative for most of the conversation. I suppose a man that does more listening than talking is wiser for it though.

We made our way to the sheriff’s cruiser and piled in. I hadn’t thought to call shotgun so I was the lucky one to sit in the back behind the bulletproof glass and doors that only open from the outside.

After a few minutes of the quiet ride. They mayor turn his head to look toward me and asked:

“So, I’m sure you’ve seen at least some of the controversy in the news about the sort of project we’re thinking of building here?” 

“An AI data center?” I responded.

“Of course, I don’t think there’s anyone left in the country that hasn’t picked a side on them. I haven’t made up my mind personally yet. I’m hoping this survey will help me find where I stand though!”

“That’s a fine way of thinking.” The mayor responded with a grin and a nod.

“Just be warned. There’s some crazy’s around that’ll tell you anything to sway your judgment.” The old sheriff had finally spoke. The mayor looked a bit shocked as to his choice of words and quickly tried to play it off in true political fashion.

“Hahaha! Now don’t go scaring him jewels! We’ve already paid for 3 months worth of nights at the plaza for him!” He said through a slightly annoyed stare.

The old sheriff simply shrugged and returned to quietly chauffeuring us there. After about 30 minutes of driving. I could see the sign marking the city limits. “Pale river welcomes you!” The sign read in bold blue letters below a hand painted mural of townspeople waving. The paint had long faded and the clothes worn by the townspeople indicated it was probably painted sometime in the late 40’s to early 50’s.

“And here we are. Our own little slice of heaven just 15 minutes from Lake Superior.” The mayor sounded like a travel brochure.

“It’s a quiet place. We hope to keep it that way…” the sheriff muttered in his gravelly voice.

“I can’t guarantee my work here won’t attract a bit of media attention, and maybe even a couple protesters. But I can assure you, sheriff. I do not intend to say, or do anything to stir up more controversy or hysteria. I’m an investigator at the core of things. And this is just my next case. Not my shot at 15 minutes of tabloid fame.”

The sheriff gave me a nod of respect in rear view mirror before Mr mayor chimed in again.

“Speaking from experience. That attitude can go either way when it comes to public relations. People like black and white, red and blue, democrats and republicans. So they don’t have to spend hours doing their own research.”

“Well I’m glad we all 3 seem to fit the qualifications for our offices.” I responded in jest.

“What can I say? We all wanna keep our jobs right?” The mayor chortled.

A few minutes later we pulled into the parking lot of what looked to be, a two and half star hotel called “the plaza.” No cigarette burns on the drapes. But no designer bottled water in the mini fridge either. The lifestyle to which I’ve become accustomed. The sheriff helped me unload my bags and the mayor handed me the keys to my room and rental car.

“Here you are, we’ll see you at 8AM tomorrow at city hall. We have a press conference with local media. And you’ll be meeting the rep from the company bidding for the data center.”

I cut in as the slick politician tried to make a quick exit.

“Speaking of, the company trying to build here. Do you have any idea why they suddenly flipped and are now wanting a survey done before starting construction.”

The mayor gave a quick shrug.

“It’s good PR I guess. There’s a lot of money on the line here and both sides want a quick and smooth conclusion.”

Ah, so the marketing department thinks I’m a pretty face to slap on this pig. Well if half of what I read is true about these things, they better start packing up now. I said none of that out loud of course. I simply nodded and bid the kind mayor and sheriff goodnight as I went to my room to unpack and then headed to bed.

My alarm woke me up at 6:30AM the next morning. I went through my morning routine and started to head down to my rental car. But as I opened the door I found a white envelope with a red ribbon attached neatly placed on the floor in front of me. 

I picked it up and opened it. The text read:

“Dear Friend, on behalf of the Saturn corporation we’d like to thank you for your valuable time and energy. The protection of our Mother Earth is of the utmost importance to our company and we look forward to charting new, clean paths to a brighter future with your help. And last but not least, we hope you enjoy your stay in beautiful pale river!”

Looks like the charm offensive has already begun. It’s going to be a long day….

I arrived at the court house at 7:45AM. Walking in I was surprised at the architecture put into this single building in a small midwestern town. Solid marble pillars lined the perimeter of the main hall. Freshly polished black, granite floors reflected my face back up at me like a mirror. And the vaulted ceiling had hand painted murals of scenes from ancient epics. Odysseus sailing around the sirens. Icarus falling after getting too close to the sun. Gilgamesh cutting down the cedar. And a different interpretation of the garden of Eden. This one had the setting you’d expect. Eve and Adam both covered in fig leaves near the tree of knowledge. But the serpent was put front and center. With a faint yellow ring around its head, similar to what a saintly portrait would portray. Maybe it was just a fade of the old paint causing it? Or just an eccentric artist trying to put his own spin on things.

Though I spent a few good moments admiring the works of this hall. I snapped back to reality and walked over to the directory to find the press room. 3rd floor, room 29, got it.

I made my way upstairs via the elevator and opened the door to the press room. The local media was already seated and conversing back and forth. Off to the right of the podium I saw the blue curtain behind the stage fling open and the mayor somewhat frantically calling me back. I hurried over to him to find out what was up.

“Geez man, guess you haven’t done this before.” He said a little red in the face.

“Uh, no. I haven’t actually.” I replied, a little perturbed.

“Well just to let you know, I personally like 30 minutes of prep before I make any public statements.”

I nodded in agreement “noted, but I’m planning on just answering them without any agenda.”

“Oh, of course, of course.” The mayor replied 

“But in my experience you can never count out some reporter coming out of left field with a completely irrelevant question just to make you look corrupt or incompetent.”

“I do not envy your position Mr. Mayor.” I replied with a chuckle.

The mayor peered down at his gold watch and then snapped his head up. “Oh, looks like it’s show time.”

We both walked out as the mayor took the page for an introduction.

“Thank you all for coming today. I do appreciate how much our local media as well as the citizenry who started this petition have taken ownership in protecting our town’s environment. And to that end I’m proud to introduce Adam here. He’ll be heading up the survey and investigation into whether or not the proposed data center project is in our town’s best interest. Adam?”

And with that he nodded and stepped aside as I took the podium.

“Thank you all again for coming and for having me in your beautiful town. I just wanna start by saying the gracious offer by the Saturn corporation to help fund this survey will in no way impact my objectivity in the investigation. Rest assured, all research and conclusions coming from this survey will be created and presented independently by me on behalf of the EPA and the federal government with no corporate influence. I’ve shut down projects by oil companies before so that should be good evidence I’m not prone to being swayed.”

That line got a chuckle out of some of the press.

“I’ll now open the floor to questions.”

As the hands went up i randomly picked a younger looking, slim man in the front row. He stood and asked:

“Could you say exactly why you were picked for this survey?”

I chuckled myself a bit. 

“Your guess is as good as mine at this point. All I could really say is I’ve been doing this job for 10 years now and to date I’ve never been accused of being anything but fair and objective.”

I could see the mayor nodding behind me. Whether or not him approving of my answer was a good or bad thing is still to be determined.

Next I pointed to an older, portly gentleman towards the back.

“I’m sure you’ve read the myriad of articles about the effects of the data centers. Do you have any personal knowledge or findings that will help with this investigation?”

A good question. I liked that guy.

“I do not actually. But I’m looking forward to studying it first hand here. And hopefully collecting enough objective data that some of the confusion on both sides of this debate can be settled.”

Another nod from the mayor.

Up next, the young blonde lady in the middle.

“Given the controversy nationwide around these projects. Are you taking into account how your statements and finding my fuel more harsh, and perhaps explosive feelings about AI data centers?”

There’s the left field.

“I can certainly say contributing to any kind of mass hysteria is the last thing on my mind here. In a world of fad left and right, I’ve found the answer is usually in the middle. And finding that middle is why I’m in pale river.”

That response got a nod from the mayor and the reporter.

“One final follow up. Have you been privy to-“ 

\*WHAM!\*

The door at the back of the room slung open mid sentence for the young reporter.

“That data center is a threat to this community!” The disheveled man in a worn army jacket and jeans as he approached the podium.

“The Saturn corporation is a part of the globalist, trans-humanist agenda and this data center will only further their goals of enslaving us all through constant surveillance and mind control!” He shouted. 

The room quieted to let him speak. Even though he sounded like a discount alex jones. I looked over my shoulder to the mayor for guidance. He nodded to the sheriff and he calmly approached the man. 

“Okay Danny we know, we hear you shout this same thing every Saturday at the flea market. Let’s just go home.” The sheriff said calmly.

“I say it because it’s true and I can prove it! Look here, the photos I downloaded showing what working conditions in Saturn corporation facilities are really like!”  
The man scrounged threw the old satchel hung from his shoulder.

“Okay Danny, look, I’m the sheriff. Why don’t we go out in the hall and you can show me personally.” 

The disheveled man sighed and nodded and they walked out together. Next I heard the mayor approaching me from behind. He gently pushed me aside and commandeered the mic and the podium.

“Okay folks, that’s enough excitement for the day. And I’m sure our friend Adam was able to answer all your questions? Right. I’ll see you all back here next week for Adam’s first status update.” And with that forced conclusion, I’d somewhat successfully ended my first press conference.

“Who was that guy?” I asked the mayor.

“Danny? Oh, he’s just one of our more colorful characters. Former army ranger, big name to come out of this town. But ever since his wife and son passed in a car accident, he spends all his time “warning” the community about various plots of the new world order.”

“I suppose that kind of loss would make a man go crazy.” I replied somberly. Poor Danny, give your all for your country and lose the two biggest reasons you fought for it. “Well if he wants to interview me, I’m free after 5 every day.”

The mayor looked at me a bit shocked. “You sure you wanna do that? You think reporters are bad, he’s got the best gotcha questions I’ve ever heard.”

I shook my head in the affirmative. “That man deserves to ask a question after what he’s been through.”

Mayor Tinsley smirked a bit. “You’ve got a big heart Adam. Don’t go into politics. We better get going now. Your meeting with the Saturn corp rep is in 15 minutes.”

I smirked as well and then followed him out of the room and down to his office. We were both shocked to find the rep from Saturn corp already seated inside. He quickly stood.

“Mayor tinsley, pleasure to see you again. And Adam, a pleasure to make your acquaintance. I’m Aaron.”  He pushed his hand out towards me.

“A pleasure to meet you as well.” I responded as I shook his hand.

“Well I suppose we should get started right?” The mayor cut in.

“Oh, i apologize Mr. Mayor. Today I’m just a chauffeur you see, the CEO himself would like to meet with Adam.”

The look of shock appeared again from us both. “The CEO? That wasn’t discussed with us?” The mayor appeared slightly frustrated.

“Yes sir I do apologize. This was quite the last minute decision. The board of directors met last night and agreed Saturn corp’s top priority is presenting an accurate picture of our dedication to preserving the environment. And to that end, the CEO himself has flown out to welcome Adam.” 

“Geez their projected profit margins must be insane to warrant this. Either that or the company is struggling big time.” I thought to myself.

“Well, I guess it’s goodbye for now Adam.” The mayor relented. “I’ll see you back here tomorrow.

“Yes, now if you’ll come with me sir, the car is ready out front.”

I followed him down to a very nice Mercedes sedan and we drove out of town to the nearest city. There we pulled into what was definitely a five star hotel.

“He’ll be on the top floor in the penthouse suite.” Aaron added as he handed me a keycard to the room.

“Thanks Aaron.” I added as I took the card and headed inside. 

I walked into another extravagant lobby. The pillars and trim were painted gold with red wallpaper and carpets. I spoke to the lady at the front desk and she directed me to the elevators. The doors to elevator opened to reveal a man standing next to the buttons in a similar uniform to the lady at the front desk. “Wow, this place still has elevator attendants.” I thought.

I told him the pent house suite and up we went. I arrived at the top floor with a satisfying ring that can only come from an actual bell being used instead of a speaker. The doors opened to reveal a magnificent suite. The full size kitchen sat just to the right of the living room area that bordered the indoor lap pool. A huge 4K tv sat just above a beautiful brick fireplace. The wall to far left was nothing but glass giving a gorgeous view of the Great Lakes. This place was fit for a king.

“Ah, our esteemed guest has arrived.” Came a mature, suave voice from the top of the stairs. I looked of the top of the spiral staircase leading to the second floor of the penthouse to see a grey haired man in an Armani suit smiling widely down at me. 

“Please, have a seat on the sofa.” He said as he descended the stairs. I nodded and walked over to the comfy sofa and took a seat. “I hope my taste in accommodations didn’t come off too egregious. My staff does most of the hotel bookings for me you see.”

“Not at all sir.” I replied. “I would expect a CEO of a billion dollar company to enjoy the finer things.”

“Your understanding is much appreciated.” He said with a kind smile.

“Of course. I do have to ask though, what exactly were you expecting to speak with me about in this meeting sir?” The man sat back and chuckled

“Oh just to get to know the man that holds the fate of our endeavors in his hands haha! And please, call me Lucius.”

“Yes sir Mr. Lucius. And I do appreciate your company’s hospitality immensely, however I must inform that no gifts or services will sway me from providing only the facts in this report.

The old man chuckled with a slight nod.

“Of course sir. The largest thing that stood out to me when your file came across my desk was your unwavering objectivity.”

“Wait, what? What file?” My eyes widened a bit

“Just a standard client file filled with publicly available information. For example, the only less than stellar grade you achieved in college was for political science. Your teacher said your research and presentation skills were phenomenal. However, you would never take a firm side in an argument if the facts didn’t support it. And that makes you uniquely valuable. You’re a pragmatist, not bound by dogma or political idealism. Whatever you find in this investigation is all you’ll report. No spin or opinion. And that’s exactly what we need sir.”

I was taken aback by his knowledge of my life. I was no stranger to veiled threats from rich CEO’s. But his casual nature about it, and how he turned it around as a way to praise me. It was all very off putting.

“I’m flattered and impressed by your company’s research abilities Mr. Lucius. And I must agree with your assessment of me. But I’d appreciate it if we had minimal contact from here on out for the sake of the report.”

The old man nodded again

“Oh yes of course, you’ll only see me once more tomorrow for the facility tour.”

My eyes went wide and I sat up in my seat. 

“Facility tour? I was told I was supposed to investigate the effects of the project before construction started?”

The man chuckled and waved his hands nonchalantly.

“You’re correct sir. The full facility will not be built until you sign off. However, the best way to find the answers you need are from an accurate scale model of the facility and its conditions. So a small wing of the full facility has been set up in temporary housing so you can study the full effects of its systems in real time. Doesn’t that sound helpful?”

His salesman act wasn’t working anymore on me.

“Helpful or not, I’m sure the people of pale river wont be happy once they find out you violated the contract you signed with them.”

The old man smirked.

“Contracts are fickle things Adam. You must read the finest of print to keep yourself from inconveniences like these. We care very much for the welfare of this town. But simple people sometimes need encouragement in the way of progress, no?”

I shook my head in disapproval. 

“Being from a small town myself sir, I don’t much care for your view of our intelligence.”

The man chuckled again 

“I meant nothing by it, I assure you. Only that the residents of this town need help loooking out for the best interests at times.”

I stood up in frustration and turned to leave.

“This has been quite eye opening Mr. Lucius. But now I must go. Thank you for your time.”

The old man stood as well and walked me to the door. Still as friendly as ever.

“It was a pleasure meeting you in person Adam. I have the utmost faith in your abilities to find the truth.”

I only responded with a nod as I made my exit and back down to the car. Aaron took me back to my hotel and I now sit in bed writing this for you all.

Tomorrow I’ll be touring this “temporary” facility and beginning the process of research and investigation. 

Until then, farewell.
