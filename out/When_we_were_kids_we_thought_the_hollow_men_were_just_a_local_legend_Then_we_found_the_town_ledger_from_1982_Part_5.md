[\[Part 4\]](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/s/vO4GQD94ae)

The floor of the gymnasium buckled upward with a sound like a gunshot.

Planks exploded into showers of splinters and dark, sodden earth. The crowd erupted into blind panic, fifty people screaming and clawing toward the exit doors, only to find the heavy steel push bars chained shut from the outside by the panicked town council.

My dad flipped on his flashlight, piercing the dark. Rising through the ruptured gym floor was a dense, jungle of petrified roots and tangled black vines, coiling up the bleachers like living iron wire.

And then came the figures.

Dozens of them poured out of the breach. They were the long lost townsfolk swallowed by the soil over the last half century, their bodies preserved, caked in clay, and moved by the root hive like puppets.

In the front of the swarm was my Uncle Mikey, who disappeared in 1982.

Sheriff Miller raised his gun, his hand shaking wildly. He fired three rounds straight into Tommy's chest. The lead punched clean through the dry mud, but Mikey didn't flinch. He just kept walking.

Don't shoot, my dad roared, shoving his way past the panicked crowd. Lead doesn't work! Fire is the only thing that breaks the grain!

My dad unscrewed the cap on our last jug of kerosene and sloshed it across the court, creating a barrier between the survivors and the swarm. He struck his lighter and dropped it.

A wall of yellow flames roared to life, blindingly bright in the pitch black gym. The root creatures shrank back, their dry husks crackling and screeching as the heat hit them.

Through the shimmering heat, Uncle Mikey stopped. His milky eyes locked onto my dad. For a fraction of a second, something human flickered in his face, a brief, agonizing flash of the nine year old boy who had been dragged into the woods forty four years ago.

He raised a stiff, dirt caked hand and pointed past my dad, straight at me.

Nic grabbed my shoulder, his voice frantic and rasping through his damaged throat. It's not about the town harvest, Jackson! The original ledger from 1880 was signed by your great-great-grandfather. Your family didn't just participate in the debt. They started it.

The root needs the origin, my dad realized, his face turning pale in the firelight. It won't stop taking the town until the founding bloodline goes back into the earth.

My dad looked at me, then down at his shotgun, and finally at the ring of fire starting to sputter and die on the polished gym floor. Beyond the flames, the leviathan roots were pounding against the concrete ceiling above us, bringing down showers of dust and cinder block.

No, my dad whispered. In 1982, I let them take my brother because I was too scared to step up. I am not letting them take my son.

Before I could grab him, my dad racked his twelve gauge, dumped the remaining kerosene over his own heavy canvas coat, and kicked open a secondary fire exit leading toward the boiler room.

Dad, NO! I screamed, lunging for him, but Nic held me back with all his strength.

Jackson, take Nic and get to the highway! my dad yelled over his shoulder, striking his lighter against his soaked jacket sleeve. Break the line! Let it end with me!

He burst into flames, a towering beacon of fire in the dark, and charged headlong into the center of the root swarm, driving them backward into the deep breach beneath the school with a deafening roar.

The impact shattered the main taproot anchored to the foundation. The creatures shrieked as the fire spread violently through their interconnected root system, turning the breach into an inferno.

Nic and I shoved through the side exit into the cool night air just as the gymnasium floor caved completely into the burning earth below. We ran through the darkness, not stopping until we hit the state route miles away, watching the glow of Blackwood Creek burn green against the horizon.

Did my dad's sacrifice actually destroy the root network, or did it just buy me enough time to realize I am the last remaining origin of the debt?
