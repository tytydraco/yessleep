Something hard dragging against the tent woke me up later.

At first, my sleep-addled brain thought I was still dreaming, but when my half-open eyes caught a hand pushing against the fabric near my face, I snapped awake. I shuffled back, swore, and searched for anything to defend myself.

My whispered curses must’ve been louder than I’d intended, because guttural chirping answered my voice. I stayed quiet, but somewhere out in the bushes, something else answered the chirps. More responses came from the opposite direction, and it became clear that, whatever these things were, they had surrounded the tent.

Fear made me consider the impossible: were these the imps? But that made no sense. Rudy had made it up. Had he based his stupid fucking story on a real legend?

I grabbed the zipper and slowly pulled it down tooth by tooth. The small noises were sonic booms in the dark. Once there was a hole just large enough to peer through, I glanced over to where the campfires should be roaring. Instead, only a faint trail of smoke rose from dying embers. Both fires had gone cold. Worse, Rudy and Brad were gone.

“Fuck,” I mumbled.

Maybe this was another prank. Brad had driven our boat into the rocky shoals, shattering the prow. All was lost. Maybe he wanted to rub it in my face? Nails slowly dragged against the nylon behind me. Several hands pushed against the tent, their little fingers feeling for flesh and a way in.

This wasn’t a prank. This was legitimate. I had to get out of here and find those two, or else I’d end up in a tree with my blood and guts missing.

The boat was taking on water. It was time to go overboard.

I quietly slid on my shoes, tied my hair back into a ponytail, and gripped the zipper hard between my fingers. I counted down from three and ripped it down quickly. In my panic, though, the tent fabric caught again, and my exit hole was barely big enough to squeeze through.

The zipper had roused the imps, and they started to chatter more. Grumbles, squeaks, and grunts. More aggressive. More angry. A free meal was on the other side of the nylon, and more of them pressed against the tent. Looking for the elusive rear entry.

One of them found it.

A sharp object poked a small hole in the nylon. I glanced back just as a beady red eyeball pressed against the opening. Once the monster confirmed I was in there, two small hands replaced the eye, and they started tearing the hole wider.

That was my cue to leave. I pushed through the opening and got my shoulders through, but my hips got stuck, and I fell flat on the ground. My arms gave out, and my nose hit the dirt with a crunch. Blood gushed from my nostrils. The coppery tang sent the little guys into a frenzy.

One of them strayed from the melee at the back of the tent and rushed around. We locked eyes. It was holding a small pitchfork made from discarded camping gear. The tines hewed to a fine point. It jabbed my elbow and a searing pain rippled up my arm. It pulled the fork out, yelled for others, and stabbed me again.

I snapped. I wiggled out of the hole like a fish struggling on a line and damn near tore the stakes out of the ground. Once I was free, I stomped on the little bitch that stabbed me and ran over to Rudy’s stupid-ass tarp. They weren’t there, either.

Above me, a burst of white lit the sky, and a shooting star streaked high above the canopy. The meteor shower. These fools were probably up on the rocks.

I scampered over to the rock formation as dozens of the imps poured into the tent, searching for a Sunday meal that had become fast food. Before they understood their feast was gone, I reached the rocks and started my ascent.

I wasn’t trying to be quiet, but as soon as I reached the summit, I froze. Rudy and Brad were up here together. Rudy’s words stopped me cold.

“It was never gonna work with Bella. She doesn’t understand us. What we have.”

I peered over the ledge. They were close together. Their hands clasped under the stars streaking across the sky. Brad and Rudy. Had it not been for the murder imps, this would one hundred percent be the most shocking development of this trip.

“She might be open to sharing.”

“Bro, that’s not going to happen. And if you propose that and she rejects it, she’ll tell everyone about us. How we are. Ruin both of our reputations.”

“She wouldn’t. Bella’s not a bad person,” Brad said.

“She’s a woman scorned, dude. Honestly, you’re miserable, right?”

“It’s been rough lately.”

“Then listen to me. It’s better to do what I said earlier. Alpine divorce. Nobody will blame us because nobody will find her out here. I’m a hunter…I can make it quick.” He mimed slitting a throat.

My mouth went dry. Swallowing became a pipe dream. This trip was never intended to be a restart. This was an ending - either as a couple or as a living person. The chorus of imps grew louder. Their calls finally reached these two. Curiosity finally got the best of them, and they both turned toward the campsite, only to find me peering over the ledge. My eyes went wide, and I ducked down.

“Bella!” Brad said, pulling his hand away from Rudy. “What are….”

I didn’t stick around to find out what he was asking. I had to get away from everything. I scrambled back down, lost my footing, and fell five feet, landing on my back. My body hit the ground with a thud that knocked the air from my lungs. I sucked in as I rolled onto my hands and knees, struggling to stand.

“It was a joke, Bella! Rudy was kidding!”

Oxygen finally met my lungs and color returned to my face. I glanced over at our tent as a wave of imps came rushing toward me. Death gleamed in their glowing eyes. A roiling wave of murder minions crashing toward me.

“I’m coming down. Let’s talk about it!”

This unholy combination got me moving. I pushed off the ground and ran towards Rudy’s tent, putting distance between myself and the imps. If Rudy and Brad wanted to get to me, they’d have to get through them. Considering they wanted to kill me and leave me for bear chow, I was fine with that arrangement.

I grabbed Rudy’s backpack, and something firm cartwheeled out of it. A knife. The large, throat-slitting kind. I placed it back in the bag, ran to the water’s edge, and hurled it into the pond. The placid mirror reflecting the streaking stars above shattered into ripples as the bag hit the water and sank.

“No!” Rudy screamed. “That was my dad’s knife, you bitch!”

“Go fish, motherfucker!”

Brad had climbed down the rocks and finally noticed the imps. He’d been focused on me sprinting away from him and carrying his reputation with me. When he finally spied the actual threat, he let out an ear-piercing scream. Real fear laced throughout the holler.

The imps quickly moved on from me to him and Rudy, who’d just skidded down next to Brad. The stars reflected off the pitchforks and knives the imps held, disappearing as the blades entered their flesh.

I found the stream we’d followed and ran into the dark woods. If there were bears out here, I was willing to risk it. I had to put distance between us on the off chance they survived the murder imp encounter.

Eventually, the meteor shower ended and dark returned to the woods. Still, I kept moving forward, following the stream and praying it kicked us out near the wash where we’d hidden our car. Brad or Rudy never came crashing through the brush behind me. They must have started screaming at some point, but I didn’t turn back to check. Leave that to the professionals.

After hours of running, then walking, then damn near crawling, I found the road. I hate to admit it, but I kissed the asphalt. It was gross, and I instantly regretted it, but it meant I’d survived Brad’s latest “adventure”.

Another hour later, I tracked down the cars, liberated my cellphone from the locked jeep by smashing the window with a rock, and called the cops. Another hour passed before the red and blue lights came screaming to my location. I told them we’d been off the grid camping and there was an animal attack. I didn’t think admitting that murderous, baby-sized monsters were the real culprits would help my case.

I was questioned and whisked away in an ambulance. Once inside, I finally broke down. A flood of competing emotions hit my brain, and the only way not to go crazy was to cry it out.

A week later, search teams found their remains and I was officially cleared as a suspect. Their bodies were high in a tree, splayed out on the top branches. All their innards gone. No blood on the scene. Mountain lion - they’d seen them around here. Searchers blamed the rain and animals for cleaning the site. I didn’t dissuade them from that conclusion.

It was dumb luck the searchers found them at all. A ranger happened to look up. Rudy’s nearly skinned face hung on by a thread and waved in the breeze. Their bodies had started to decompose, and flies swarmed them. Reporters wrote that the Rangers claimed it had been years since he’d seen something so brutal in these woods.

I’m better now. I’ve sworn off dating for a full calendar year to recalibrate myself. All I know is that, when I’m ready to put myself back out there, I’m looking for dudes who are homebodies. PJ night watching a bad movie never led to murder imps stabbing me with forks.
