**We are all cogs in a machine, we must go on to allow the machine of society to function. When a gear jams, a spring kinks, or when death makes itself present, all must go still until an adjustment has been made. Timing is an underrated phenomenon, without it all would run haywire. Time is also a construct, and without it all of God’s creation rest in their peace. They care not for when their next meal may come, they have no anxieties about whether or not they will wake when the sun rises, or if their loved ones are still living, breathing, returning to their embrace. They are gifted the ignorance of time and the thought that one day said time will run out. I remained cursed by the knowledge of futures untold, futures untrue, futures that may only exist in the recesses of my anxious mind. Knowledge is a curse while ignorance can remain a blessing. This was the revelation I had when my father told me Uncle Tyler committed suicide.**

**It was the first day of eighth grade. Mrs. Trout had us discussing our summer reading projects in-length. All summer long I had spent playing down by the tributary casting my few worries away one line at a time. Tyler knew I had some summer reading to complete, but he had never pushed it on to me. Instead, we stood in long periods of silence reflecting upon old memories instead of discussing the importance of Milton’s Paradise Lost.**

**You see, Tyler was like a second dad to me. He raised me and Greg throughout our adolescent years, adopting me when I was two and Gregory had just been born. Though you wouldn’t know it now, my father was a user who had been in and out of rehabs his whole life, and it was at one of these programs where he met my mother. According to Tyler, he thought it was a horrible idea for his sister to fall in love with an addict while in recovery. He told me that he’d often push her towards finding love on the inside first before settling with my dad. Dad had been quick to marry her and proposed just several months after she graduated from the program. Everyone had been against their marriage, but it was what eventually brought me and Greg into being, so I didn’t protest much.**

**After I was born, Tyler said my parents fell off the wagon again. My dad had been in a bad rope swing accident and broke his back on the shore of our property's river. The doctors had prescribed him some heavy narcotics, and just like that, Tyler said he was hooked like a catfish. I don’t know where or when, and neither did Tyler, but evidently my mother had been using his medication too. The two of them got pregnant once again and neglected my brothers well-being by sleeping all day and partying all night.**

**“It was Ambien and hydrocodone  in the day and mushrooms and LSD in the evening. It truly is a miracle your brother wasn’t killed in the womb, Hank,” Tyler threw a line into the slow-moving river.**

**“I almost wonder if it would have been better if he had,” I said bleakly.**

**Tyler set his fishing pole into a PVC holder.**

**“Don’t say that Hank, Gregory is amazing in his own right. He deserves a life like you and I,” He walked over to me, laying his hand on my shoulder and staring deep into my eyes.**

**“It’s just that, well- Dad is gone all the time, and I do love Greg, but he’s just a lot to look after.”**

**Tyler laughed at this. “Believe me, I know! I spent eight years raising you boys to be the young men you are today. But, that doesn't mean you wish your brother never existed. You would be devastated if something happened to any of us, let alone him. You remember when you got into that fight with Duncan last year?”**

**I did.**

**“Why was it that you stood up to a boy who’s got a foot over you and a face built like a brick wall?” Tyler asked with his signature smirk.**

**“Because lard-face called Greg a [Slur],” I thought about the encounter at the bus stop and laughed, “The idiot thought he could pick on my nine year old brother without me doing a thing about it. I gave him a black eye.”**

**“Exactly! Do you know how proud I was of you? I heard about the fight from my own students. You had high schoolers talking about Hank “The Tank” [redacted] for days weeks! When I saw Duncan’s face during sixth period, I had to hide my own less I be reprimanded for laughing at a high schooler who got his ass kicked by a thirteen-year-old. Pardon my French,” He said.**

**“I’m in public school Uncle Tyler, you know I hear worse.” I was smiled as he lightly bumped my arm with his fist.**

**“That may be true, but its no excuse for me. I’m trying to work on my mouth.” He paused, took in a long breath and said, “Point being, Hank. We are family, and family looks after each other, even when we are a pain in the- butt. You don’t know how many…” He started to tear up, “How many times I had to help Susan. I blame myself really. I had smoked my fair share of weed back in my days and I was the one who introduced it to your mom. Say what you want about the stuff, but it was her gateway.”**

**I hugged my uncle tight, “It was her choice, not yours.”**

**He hugged me back, “Still, I often wander if she’d be here still if I hadn’t encouraged her. I see her you know. At night- not while I’m sleeping, but when I’m… When I’m somewhere in between. I see her. She blames me. She blames me for not taking her away up north like I promised her I would. She blames me for giving her a hit of my joint. She blames me for letting her be with that man.” The way he said it made me question whether or not he had meant my father or someone else. “I see him there too. He laughs at me from the corner of my room. I try and scream to tell him to go away, Sarah isn’t able to hear me and I am unable to move. He just laughs’ and laughs. He grows in the shadows and is prideful in my miscarry.” His accent made me think he said misery, but now I do wonder. “He leers over me and I cannot stand to see his shadow. Even when I’m awake, even when I’m around you I hear her call to me. She’s screaming, Hank. Your mom is screaming, and it’s because of him.”**

**I was crying. I didn’t cry much, but Tyler knew how to get to me. “Well,” I said, “I might not be here if she would have never met my dad in rehab. Maybe some things happen for a reason, I suppose.” My breath was filled with nervous raps. I hadn’t noticed the bags under his eyes until now.**

**“That’s really mature of you, Hank.” He wiped away his tears and returned to his pole. We didn’t catch a thing that day, but that was alright.**

**“Hank,” Mrs. Trout called, “You will be doing your presentation after Julia. Do you have your flash drive ready?”**

**“Yes ma’am,” I lied.**

**“Good, okay Julia, if you’d please step forward-“**

**Suddenly the classroom intercom chimed. “Hello Mrs. Trout, This is Franky from the front office calling in for Hank \\\[Redacted\\\], he will be leaving for the day.”**

**I gave a quick prayer to God and thanked him for buying me time on my summer reading.**

**Ignorance is bliss.**

**My shadow lagged half a step behind me as I walked to the office as if it knew what would be waiting for me. I saw my dad and Gregory standing in the foyer waiting for me. He had looked as though a truck had ran over his face. Out of habit I checked his pupils. THey looked clear, just distracted and red-rimmed from tears. He had clearly been crying.**

**“Is everything okay?” I asked already knowing that it wasn’t.**

**He didn’t tell me yes or no, he didn’t even acknowledge the question. All he said was, “Are you boys hungry? I’m thinking of taking us out to Mo’s.”**

**Greg began pumping his fist in the air chanting, “Mo’s! Mo’s!” Franky gave him a soft smile from behind her desk.**

**“Um- yeah. I can eat.” Already I felt the pit in my stomach growing. We loaded up into my dad’s Chevy and arrived at Mo’s Diner by ten-thirty. “So dad… What’s the occasion?” I asked sternly.**

**Our server came to take our orders, took one look at my father’s face, and turned right back around.**

**Tears ran down my father’s face, heavy as bullets. “I’m sorry,” Is all he could say for the next five minutes.**

**Our server came back. She was an old student of Tyler’s. “Mr. [redacted] do you need any tissues?”**

**He raised his palm up and shook his head. “I’m fine, just give us a moment please, Jan.”**

**She nodded her head and walked away.**

**“Dad. What the hell is wrong?” I was beginning to grow nervous, Greg was flipping back and forth through the drink menu, unaware of any social cues.**

**“Something’s happened,” He said at last.**

**The pause was too unbearable, to cowardly to not allow my interjection. “What happened?” I asked sternly.**

**“It’s your uncle,” Still no detail persisted.**

**“Uncle Randy?” I asked. He had cancer for years, and last we heard it had started to flair up.**

**He shook his head no.**

**“Out with it, Dad,” I was nearly begging at this point.**

**“They found him with weights around his ankles. He was in the pool,” My dad seemed to have glued his eyes shut, avoiding any reality of my reaction.**

**“Dammit! Who, dad?” A couple patrons turned to look over at us before continuing on their meals.**

**“Tyler. Tyler is dead.”**

**It was in the stillness that I began to understand. I didn’t need my dad to tell me how. I already knew. I knew from the long conversations. I knew from the way he spent time with me. I knew from the look in his eyes and the longing in his heart. I didn’t need my dad to tell me that he killed himself. It was evident through my last moments with him. I knew Tyler hated my dad, and for the longest time I thought my dad hated Tyler, but I couldn’t have been more wrong. If I learned anything from that day, I learned that my dad might have idolized Tyler as much as I did.**

**“No,” I said. “No, I saw him before school started. He was going to grade some summer assignments and went to his room early. I saw him,” My heart starting racing. “I saw him hours ago,” It beat on the door of my chest like the emotional junkie I was: craving a fix. “Dammit, I didn’t get to say I love you!” Gregory threw the menu down and began to scream-cry at me.**

**“Hank!” my dad yelled. He ran over to Gregory with some Crayola crayons in his hands. He began breaking them and handing them over to Greg. This calmed him in a simple way that I grew envious of.**

**“He told me he’d see me later…” My head fell to the table, echoing the loud thump in my innermost being.**

**“I’m sorry, Hank,” He reached out his hands to hug me, but I refused. I believed that somehow my dad was the root of Tyler’s suicide. I care that was not the extent of the full reason. But I needed something to blame.**

**That night I sat in bed, thinking of all the things I might have been able to do to stop Tyler. I could solve all my problems with the gift of delayed hindsight, but lacked the foresight in the moments that led to my now. I didn’t sleep, and somehow time went still. In that stillness I wrestled with rest, finding no peace and comfort as I listened to the loud snores coming from my brother's room. The analog clock ticked, every second slower, until the fullness of the moon cascaded light into my room. Abruptly, Greg’s snoring stopped mid-breath I could hear the voice of Tyler, begging for me to help him before it was too late. My breathing shallowed, my body sweat, and my eyes twitched, seizing in fuzzy flutters. My whole room grew blurry, and the shadows began to grow teeth around me. They engulfed me, swallowed me, excreted me out like the vomit I was. I did not sleep, nor was I awake. I was somewhere in between.**

***This is all your fault.*** **I heard. It wasn’t my voice, it wasn’t Tyler’s voice. It was the voice of unreality, the voice of discord, the voice that had no tongue and spoke through its teeth. It was the voice of shadows and the voice of shame. In my room I felt the presence of evil projected onto my own conscience. I felt internal blame, and for the first time in my life, I wished myself dead.**

**I slept little the night my uncle died. Hell, I don’t know that I slept at all. If I drifted, it was only for a few minutes at a time, and my own crying jerked me awake. By the time my alarm went off, the wish I’d made in the dark was still sitting on my chest. Part of me wondered if it had all been a dream, if I’d look at the date and find it was still the first day of school, with Tyler alive and nothing ruined yet. I prayed it was only a nightmare. In a way, it was.**

**My eyes felt like sandbags. My arms and legs had gone numb. I dragged myself to the dining room table, poured a bowl of cereal, and was crying again by the time the milk hit it.**

**My dad walked in with Greg by his side.Greg looked like his usual self. Dad looked as wrecked as I felt.**

**I envied my brother sometimes. He understood less than I did, and I wondered how much easier life would be if I carried his simplicity.**

**“Morning, Hank,” Dad said. He moved through the kitchen like clockwork: cereal, school, shift. Everything was turning around him, yet I could feel myself grind against his effort.**

**“Morning,” I said back.**

**Greg walked over to me and gave me a hug before saying, “M-morning, Hankie. D-did you sleep g-good?”**

**I gave him a weak smile and said, “No, I was pretty much up all night. You snore like a steam engine, you know,” I forced a smile.**

**Greg laughed. “So I’ve been t-told. I slept like a b-baby.”**

**I was envious of my brother at times. Though his grasp on the situation had not been the strongest, sometimes I wondered how much better life would be if I carried his simplicity.**

**“I was planning on taking you boys up to school today. That is if you are wanting to go. I completely understand if not,” Dad said while pouring Greg’s cereal.**

**I hadn’t heard him the first time. I was lost, staring into my bowl as if it had the answers to life’s mysteries. It did not.**

**“Hank?” Dad asked again.**

**“Yes, sir?” I looked up, nearly breaking tears as I looked into the face of my father.**

**“Do you want to stay home today or would you like to try and go to school?” He brought up his wrist and eyed his Fitbit. It was seven fifteen and my dad’s heart rate rested at a cool sixty-five beats per minute. Mine on the other hand felt as if it were about to explode.**

**I did not like his use of the word try. He always paired it with your best, and whenever he did, saying no felt like admitting defeat. No, I did not want to try spaghetti squash. No, I did not want to try getting to know Dad’s new girlfriend. And no, I certainly did not want to go to school. I couldn’t stomach a hallway of classmates offering condolences for a relationship none of them could begin to understand.**

**Yet, I was a people pleaser. “Why not? I’ve got nothing better to do.”**

**I was also sarcastic.**

**“Good,” he said. “Sometimes I find filling my day up with things helps me move past things that have disturbed me. Maybe today will  be better than you think.” He rubbed my hair. I grimaced at the weight of his hand. “Anyway… be ready in fifteen minutes if you can. I’m having to show up to the hospital early today. Richie called in sick, so Lyles is letting me work his shifts to collect some overtime.”**

**My dad left the room, leaving me and Greg to our own devices.**

**“Wh-when does Tyler come b-back?”**

**I dropped my spoon. Tyler had been as much a father to Greg as he was to me.**

**“What do you mean, buddy?” My eyes were swelling again, ready to burst.**

**“Tyler is gone. When does he come b-back?” The earnest question gutted me. Dad had walked out right when I needed him, the way he always did. Why couldn’t he be here to explain this?**

**“Tyler isn’t coming back, Greg. He’s gone to be with Momma.”**

**I watched him work it through. “In heaven?”**

**“Yes. Tyler’s in heaven with Mom. I’m sure he’s telling her about all the good times we had together.” I don’t know when my tears dried up, but somewhere in that conversation, I ran out.**

**Greg frowned. “No. Not yet,” he said, matter-of-factly. “He’s not in heaven yet.”**

**I tilted my head. “What do you mean, he’s not in heaven yet?” It was a strange thing to say, and I wasn’t sure it was wrong. Tyler had been an agnostic at best, and I wasn’t about to wade into theology with Greg for either of our sakes.**

**“Tyler is mad at you. I heard the man last night. He was screaming. He said it’s your fault, because you didn’t see he needed help.”**

**I started to shake. Somewhere in the middle of that sentence, Greg’s stutter had disappeared. How could he know the guilt I’d felt in bed last night?**

**“Greg, stop.”**

**“Tyler’s with Momma. But Momma isn’t in heaven either. Momma’s with the man.” His eyes never blinked. “The dark man with the hat. You can’t see his face, just his smile, and it’s all teeth, teeth that want your heart.”**

**“Greg!” He looked like a boy in a trance.**

**“He’s coming for you, Hankie. He loves to take things. He’s a collector. He trades in guilt. You give him a little, and he gives you back everything you’re afraid of.”**

**“Gregory, s-stop!” I was sweating, shaking. My teeth clattered, and the stutter my brother had lost slipped into my mouth as if it had been handed over.**

**“Are you ready, Hankie?”**

**I want you to believe me when I say this: I do not remember doing it. I remember a deep black filling my vision, and then color rushing back all at once. I was on top of Greg. He lay on his back, crying, blood running from his nose. Dad ran in and hauled me off him.**

**He slapped me across the face. My cheek burned, and part of me felt it was owed.**

**“What the hell is wrong with you!” he screamed. “What the— why—” Then he shoved me aside and scooped up my brother. Greg clutched his nose, screaming as if it had been torn off.**

**“Room! Now!” Dad shouted, turning away to soothe him.**

**My fist ached. My God, I thought. How hard had I hit him? I knew from the Duncan fight that my knuckles would bruise. I went to the bathroom and ran cool water over my hands and face.**

**Come with me…**

**I could barely hear it beneath the trickle of the faucet, a voice reaching for the innermost part of my mind. I pushed it away and kept washing.**

**When I looked up, the dark had gathered around my reflection and draped over my shoulders like a cloak. It felt less like a shadow than a claim. At the edge of my vision something moved, a tendril, and I jumped. Nothing was there.**

**“You need to cool your ’tude, mister.” Dad stood in the doorway. “You nearly got suspended last year for that fight. I don’t need you punching everything that upsets you. I know you’re upset, Hank, but we just have to deal with this, okay?”**

**I dropped to the floor and began to sob. “What do you even know? You’re not my dad! Tyler was! I hardly even know you! You killed Mom, and then you took Greg and me away from Tyler and Sarah. You don’t deserve us! I hate you!” I slammed my fist on the tile, and pain flared through the bruise.**

**“Hank, I love you more than you could ever know.” I could hear the tears in his voice. “Your mother did too. I’m not proud of the things I’ve done. But I’m grateful to have you back in my life, and I’m proud to be your dad, even if you won’t let me be. I hope you can understand that one day.” He reached down a hand. I took it, and he pulled me into a hug. “I miss him too, you know. If it weren’t for him and Sarah, who knows who would have raised you boys. Even at my worst, I took comfort knowing family was keeping you safe, even if it couldn’t be me.”**

**I stood in silence taking in his embrace.**

**“Go ahead and grab your backpack. I’ll meet you in the truck. Also, you owe your brother an apology. He’s hurting bad.”**

**I grabbed my belongings, a cup of coffee, and passed Greg on the way out. “Hey…” I said, avoiding eye contact. “I’m sorry for hitting you. I am upset and don’t know how to process it. Do you forgive me?” I looked up at him. He had two wads of napkins stuffed up his nose, the tips dyed crimson.**

**“Of c-c-course I forg-give you,” He lunged forward and gave me his signature bear hug. He forgave me so fast it felt like another accusation.**

**“Thank you, Greg. I’ll be in the truck.”**

**I walked outside and took a deep breath. It was only the beginning of September, but there was already a coolness to the air. Kids ran and played tag at the bus stop, loud and weightless, blessed with ignorance. There was an ambiance of joy that filled the street. My fist pounded in a dull throb. My heart echoed this. I opened the passenger-side door, sat down and sighed, placing the cup of coffee over my dad's sobriety chip laying in the cupholder. My head hit the dashboard.**

***How am I supposed to carry on?*** **I thought.**

**Tyler’s voice came into my head, the way it did whenever I wanted to quit.** ***One step at a time, kiddo. One step at a time, and you’ll reach the mountaintop.***

**I think that was the only time I smiled that day.**
