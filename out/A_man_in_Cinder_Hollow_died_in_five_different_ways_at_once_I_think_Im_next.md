\[Detective Helzer – July 10, 2025\]

I don't usually start my reports with personal impressions. Twenty-two years as a detective on the Cinder Hollow Police Force and I've kept my case files clean of anything that isn't fact. But I'm making an exception here, because I think whoever reads this after me deserves to know what it felt like to stand in that apartment.

Jacob was found dead in his apartment. No forced entry, no apparent trauma, but the scene was wrong in a way I didn't have words for yet.

A broken ceiling fan with a torn rope still hanging from it. A bottle of antipsychotic pills on the floor, unopened, the seal still intact. Any detective would call this a suicide and move on. Until they saw the body.

His eyes were open. Wide, dry, fixed on the ceiling like he'd been watching something fall toward him. His face had the look people get in the half second before impact, except it hadn't moved past that half second. It had frozen there.

His body was worse. Bent in ways a body isn't supposed to bend, joints pointing directions joints don't go, ribs pressed inward like something had stepped on his chest and kept stepping. There were rope marks around his neck, deep enough to bruise, though the rope from the fan was still knotted and whole on the floor beside him.

I've seen bodies. I've seen what falls do, what cars do, what rope does. I had never seen a body that looked like it had died from all three at once.

I asked my partner to give me the room for a minute. He didn't argue. I don't think he wanted to be in there either.

Everyone who's lived here long enough has a story like this one, or knows someone who does. It's not something people bring up at dinner, but it comes up eventually, usually late, usually after a few drinks, usually followed by someone changing the subject before it goes further. My mother used to warn me as a kid not to walk home past the water tower after dark, and when I asked her why, she just said some things in this town don't need a reason. I stopped asking after a while. Everyone does.

I've got a drawer at my desk with three case files I never officially reopened. A woman two years ago who drowned in a bathtub with no water in it, ruled accidental despite there being nothing to accidentally drown in. A teenager who fell from a roof he had no business being on, found without a scratch except lungs the coroner couldn't explain. And an older one, before my time, that I only know from a file so thin it barely counts as a record, a man who was found sitting upright in his own kitchen with no pulse and no apparent cause, a half-finished meal still warm on the table in front of him. I've read through those three more times than I'd admit to anyone at the station. I don't have theories for them. I just have the drawer.

I'm not writing this report to open any of those back up. I don't have it in me, and I don't think anyone would thank me for it. I'm writing about Jacob because his file landed on my desk and because something about the shadow he described in his own hand, days before he died, matched something I can't quite explain away. I'll leave the rest of the drawer where it is.

I couldn't work out a cause of death just from looking, so I sent him to the coroner. Looking around the apartment might tell me something the body wouldn't. At first it only made things stranger. Every appliance in the kitchen was unplugged. Pillows had been stacked against corners of furniture, taped in places, like someone had gone through the apartment trying to childproof it against himself.

I found the notebook under the mattress. Spiral-bound, cheap, the kind you'd buy at a drugstore. His handwriting got worse as the pages went on, tighter and smaller like he was trying to fit more fear into the same amount of paper.

I'm including excerpts below. I've read them more times than I should have.

\[Jacob's Journal — June 11\]

I almost died today. I'm not sure how to write this without sounding crazy, but I have to get it down before I forget or it slips away.

I was crossing the street near the corner store. Just the usual route home from work. The light was red, and I stepped off the curb because the pedestrian signal said to go. Then, out of nowhere, a truck ran the red light.

I didn't see it coming. I only caught a glimpse of the grill, my face inches away from it. The noise was deafening, the horn blaring, tires screeching, metal grinding. I stumbled back and hit the sidewalk hard.

My heart was racing so fast I thought it might stop. Cars stopped, people yelled. Someone asked if I was okay, but my throat was dry, my mouth frozen.

I don't remember how I got home. I sat on the floor in my bedroom for what felt like hours. Then I saw it.

In the corner of the room, just outside my direct line of sight, a shadow. It didn't move or make noise. It was just there. Watching.

I blinked, and it was gone.

I don't know if it's real. Maybe I'm losing it. But it felt real. Like something from the edge of the world.

\[Jacob's Journal — June 14\]

The shadow isn't leaving.

Everywhere I go, it lurks just out of sight. In the corner of my vision when I'm brushing my teeth. Behind the bathroom door while I shower. When I turn off the lights at night, it waits, silent and still.

I try to convince myself it's a trick of the light, some hallucination brought on by stress or lack of sleep. But this isn't like the voices or visions I've had before. This is different.

I can't look directly at it. When I try, it disappears, as if it knows I'm watching.

I don't know what it wants.

I haven't told anyone. I'm scared they'll think I'm crazy, more so than usual.

I took my medication today. I'll try harder tomorrow.

\[Jacob's Journal — June 17\]

I almost died again today.

I was coming down the fire escape stairs at work. On the second flight, my foot slipped and I nearly fell three stories onto the concrete below.

I caught the railing just in time, bruised my shoulder pretty badly. My hands were shaking for hours.

Tonight, when I got home, there was a new shadow. It looked like me, but distorted, twisted. Its neck was bent at a terrible angle, eyes sunken and blackened.

It stood just outside my bedroom door, unmoving. Unmoving but watching.

It looked like what would've happened if I'd fallen.

I'm terrified. What the hell is happening.

\[Jacob's Journal — June 18\]

Another appeared.

I realize what's going on now. Every time I nearly die, another one appears.

I counted three today. The original shadow that showed up the day of the truck. The one outside my bedroom door after my fall. And now there's a new one standing silently by the kitchen window. What even happened for that one to spawn? When could I have almost died?

But it doesn't matter. They don't speak, don't blink, don't breathe. They're statues of death, waiting.

I don't know what they want. I don't know why they're here.

I'm exhausted.

\[Jacob's Journal — June 20\]

Last night, one of them touched me.

I was lying in bed, trying to sleep. I felt something cold and misty brush up against my shoulder. I rolled over suddenly, but there was nothing there.

Then the pain came. Sharp and crushing. Like someone had punched me in the ribs.

I gasped for air. It was like the truck almost hit me.

I can't keep doing this.

\[Detective Helzer – July 1, 2025\]

I dug into Jacob's history. Paranoid schizophrenia, diagnosed several years ago. On medication, but apparently inconsistent. His psychiatrist's notes described him as compliant but private, someone who kept his own counsel about symptoms he didn't fully trust his doctor with.

His journal reads like a delusion shaped by the diagnosis. Shadowy figures, doubling deaths. That's what I told my partner over lunch, and he nodded like it settled things.

It didn't settle for me. My captain calls cases like the ones in my drawer accidents and closes the folder. I've started calling them something else in my head, though I haven't found the word yet.

I mentioned Jacob's file to an old partner of mine, retired now, over the phone. He went quiet for a long moment before he told me to leave it alone and hung up. He used to work this district for fifteen years. I don't think he was joking.

None of this is leading to a cause of death. I'll keep transcribing the rest of the journal in case something in it does.

\[Jacob's Journal — June 24\]

There are now four shadows.

The newest appeared after I nearly choked on a vitamin this morning.

This one wheezes. It gurgles quietly. I saw it crouched outside the bathroom while I showered.

I thought I was having a panic attack, but it was real. I felt it. Like I was drowning, suffocating.

It's like they're trying to kill me, or maybe relive the moments when I almost died.

\[Jacob's Journal — June 26\]

I'm trying to protect myself.

Unplugged the stove. Padded the sharp edges of the furniture. Removed locks from doors and windows.

But it's no use. I stub my toe, almost fall again. A glass slips from my hand. They watch every mistake.

I'm terrified to be alone. And they won't leave me alone.

\[Jacob's Journal — June 28\]

I tried to end it.

I tied a rope to the ceiling fan, stood on a chair, and jumped.

The fan broke, snapped loose from the ceiling. I fell to the floor hard.

When I opened my eyes, the newest shadow was there.

It had rope marks around its neck.

Now there are five.

\[Jacob's Journal — July 1\]

I woke up with blood in my mouth today.

I'm not sure how it got there. No cuts or nosebleeds.

All five shadows were in the apartment when I woke.

One by the bedroom door. One crouched by the window. One at the foot of the bed. One just inches from my face.

They don't blink.

They don't breathe.

They just watch.

\[Jacob's Journal — July 3\]

Tomorrow I will end it.

For real this time.

No half-measures. No broken ceiling fans.

I've planned it carefully.

I'll use the pills. All of them.

I'll down the whole bottle and then wait.

This will be the end. The one thing they can't copy.

I'm so tired.

\[Detective Helzer – July 12, 2025\]

It looks like Jacob never got the chance.

I got a call from the coroner. He was baffled, and he's not a man who baffles easily. The autopsy report was unlike anything he'd filed in his career. Signs of death by multiple means, present simultaneously. A fractured skull. Bruised ribs. Strangulation. A broken vertebrae. Each one on its own would explain a death. Together they made less sense than if he'd simply dropped where he stood.

I asked the coroner if he had a theory. He didn't answer right away, and when he did, he only said he was recommending the file get closed as quickly as possible. He didn't want it sitting on his desk.

I drove home that night, exhausted and distracted. On the highway, a deer jumped out of nowhere. I swerved, lost control, and slammed into a tree. A large branch tore through the hood, close enough to graze the windshield. I should have died. I sat there afterward with my hands still on the wheel, engine ticking, and I thought about the pedestrian light Jacob said was green.

I arrived home and took a long shower to wash the day off. When I stepped out and looked to the bathroom door, I caught something in the corner of my eye. A shadow. Not moving. Not speaking. Just watching.

And it looks a hell of a lot like me.
