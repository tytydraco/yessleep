I walk through another series of beds. Dozens of them packed into this room. Each one looks as comfortable as I could ever hope for, my body weakly begging me to give in. My eyes droop further as I look away, kicking myself in the shin awake. Have to keep going, can’t end up like the rest. None of these are mine anyway.

Day 2? 3? Don’t know, don’t care. Just gotta get out. I open the door to another foyer. A soft leather couch set up in front of a large TV. The room is quiet as ever, the dim glow of the static being its only light. I flinch as I look at the recliner, remembering how the last one took her from me. Not the exit, have to keep going.

Stay awake.

Another door, another gamble. A child’s room. Toys strewn about the floor, a fuzzy carpet at my feet, and a glowing nightlight highlights the centerpiece. A pair of bunks, the bottom one shaped like a racecar. Doesn’t even tempt me, I couldn’t fit. I sign and walk into the closet. I’ve been up for too long, but not long enough. Maybe just a few more hours and I’ll be out of here, I’ll figure it out. 

The next room stopped me, these were always the worst. Open air, walls hundreds of feet apart. Every type of bed sprawled over what felt like acres of land. So many had given up here, so many had fallen away from consciousness and never awoke again. Not me, not here. I began to carefully navigate between the wooden monstrosities, sure that if one so much as touched me I’d be gone. Minutes went by and not an exit in sight. The way I came in was gone so I had no choice but to keep wandering, hoping for something new.

With the time I had, I could only think of what came before. I could hardly recall how I ended up wherever the hell here even was. All I know is that I need to get out, make it back to reality. I was so dazed and confused, I could’ve sworn I’d seen someone lying on the cot in front of me, beckoning for me to finally rest. I knew it wasn’t real; I had seen the damn things swallow a woman whole and she never came back out. Can’t be me, won’t be me.

I keep walking, seeing everything possible that could tempt me to fall. Glasses of warm milk, melatonin, noise makers, all of them want me to fall to this place. I blink. When my eyes opened, my head was pounding and blood was dripping from the back. I was on the floor, sprawled out and woozy. I had fallen asleep for a few moments long enough to fall to the floor and crack my head on the back of a wooden post.

I stand up uneasily, legs wobbling and. My skull is shaken and one of my eyes won’t open. Despite that I try to keep up, limping across the sea of cloth and wood. I wander for hours, days, weeks, however long I spent was too long for me to bear. I see people I know aren’t real, begging for me to try and go. Swinging between the most vile silence I could imagine and screaming in my ears. The walls yell, the floor claws at me, and I soldier on.

My mouth was dry as ash and my body felt like jello. I knew I couldn’t last forever, but I could last long enough. Long enough to find a better place to die at least. A door off in the distance taunted me, telling me I’ll never make it. I kept going forward out of spite.  

My left leg gave out. I fell down to the ground hard, but it wasn’t my time yet. I crawled to the door for the next hour, inching my way along the carpet until my skin was rubbed red and my arms weren’t strong enough to push myself up. When I made it to the handle, I had to balance myself on the door as I opened it, stumbling forward to a scene I had never considered an option.

Home.

My bedroom, as I remember it. Bars on the windows and locks on the door, safety guaranteed. I was too far gone to think any more. I just wanted it to stop. There wasn’t a way out and I didn’t even want one anymore. I lumbered to my grave, each step reverberating throughout my body in pain. I fell like a tree chopped down and landed on pure bliss. 

The sheets pulled me down as if they were wrapped in concrete. The pillow is light as air and all the pain in my head is soothed. White blankets draped over me, words whispered in my ear of how it’ll be alright and how my struggle was over. I couldn’t listen, I was already gone. I felt the world and my worries melt away, along with everything else. I understood why they gave up now.
