CW: Suicide, Child Abuse

Hey guys, kinda a more personal story from my childhood. Figured you guys might be able to help me make sense of it all.

I was six years old when I learned about Grubby the Clown. You see, I grew up in a very rural community. I’m talking about five houses per square mile. Middle of nowhere USA. A town full of dead end people with dead end lives. The only color in this gray society was a small round ray of sunshine whose name was Grubby, or Mr. Brown as the adults called him. He was such a kind and gentle old man. The kind who would carry candy and toys for the children he interacted with. He had been a clown since 1918, entertaining the children of our small town for decades. Personally I loved Grubby, moreover the man behind the red haired persona, but in the year 1997, after Bundy and Dahmer, but more so Gacy, clowning had become a more frowned upon practice. 

My parents, children of the seventies, were especially critical of the old man. Every time he came over for a birthday, or dressed up as clown Santa for Christmas, they stood idly behind the couch. Watching him with cautious apprehension. I recognized their discomfort for what it was. Prejudice. Even as a kid it seemed wrong to dislike Grubby. In fact, I was one of the few children who didn’t like the notion of not having him around. He was always kind, always willing to listen to me and my childish rants and raves. Even writing this fills my heart with nostalgia. God couldn’t have given us a better Grubby. However, as with all good and wonderful things, it is bound to attract some contrarians. As Jesus had the Pharisees, Grubby had the Five O’clock news. 

When the story aired about a child’s disappearance in the “nice” neighborhood subdivision, it didn’t take long for the fingers of accusation to land on Grubby. As the disappearances occurred after the parents instructed Mr.Brown to not show up. He took the allegations in stride. He never admitted guilt for anything. With no evidence, there was no crime. Charges were dropped against him, and the old man returned to his ruined home with his ruined name.

The sight of the sweet old rotund white man in handcuffs, with a brazen title card emboldened and italicized underneath his visage saying: ***Charges Dropped Against Prospective Child Killer.***** **Was enough to completely erase his credibility around town. A few petitions were even passed around, including one which would force Mr.Brown to register with the Sex Offender Registry thus ensuring that he would never hurt anyone else ever again. That one gained the most traction. Even arriving at the mayor’s desk. At this time the old man stayed isolated in his hermitage. Never even attempting to clear his name. That all changed on the day he was found dead in his closet. The cause of death, suicide.

Shortly after, the proposed bill was thrown out. Police investigated his home, finding nothing but odd clown trinkets and tricks. I cried for a few hours after I discovered the news. My birthday was in a couple of days and I was really banking on him being there. I wanted to see how he made all the queens disappear from his deck. Now, to my horrified dismay, that day would never come. My parents, more so my mom than my dad, attempted to help my six year old brain make sense of the old man’s passing. It hadn’t worked.

I was a small kid, fat with the dry humor of a British sitcom. Needless to say, popularity was not an option for me, and in a way I was ok with that. All things considered I knew I had two friends, Roy and Junior. Both of them had it worse than me. Roy was a pencil thin kid suffering from a lisp, and a coughing condition, and Junior was a ginger  who was known to be a sickly kid with a severe case of asthma. Both amazing people who blessed my early years greatly. 

My 7th birthday rolled around, and my parents threw me a small get together. Roy and Junior were there, both of them sharing in my birthday bounty as my parents prepared my special meal. A delicious tater tot casserole. This dish is almost vulgar to my adult body, but as a child it was magical. It had been an amalgamation of tater tots topping a cheddar mashed potato base, all topped off with shredded cheddar. A delight to eat but also a delight to smell. As my adult hand writes this, the scent anchors my stomach to my past. God, it was delicious.

That aside, Roy, Junior, and I sat in the center of the living room, GI Joe’s strewn across the floor in selective vantage points only known by our childish imaginations. It was warm. Not just in the way the air felt, but in the way my mind found solace in its cushiony relaxed state. That was until, a frantic knock erupted from the door. As if on a pendulum, my brain swapped into its default survival mode, and I grabbed my friends and we hid behind the couch’s armrest. To my relief when dad answered the door, my withered old grandmother stepped in. She had always been this way, in my mind's eye I don’t think I could see her any other way. She was quickly followed in by my grandfather. He was a ripe, mean old man, set on making his life easier at the expense of others' comfort. This statement made even more true, as the first action completed upon entering our home was a rapid advance to the thermostat, where he cranked it up to 76 degrees. 

My dad followed after, guiding the sodden old grump to the couch, then stealthily slinking back to drop the thermostat back to 68. Of course grandpa didn’t notice, it was July, and an easy 98 degrees outside. He wouldn’t notice. Mom came out of the kitchen, finding her parents sitting on the couch, their attention vested on me and my friends. Grandpa winced as he got comfortable. Reaching under his buttocks, he pulled out something familiar and smiled at GI Joe he now held. Following the tinge of jealousy one feels when seeing one of their beloved belongings being molested by another, I closed in, holding my hand out like a beggar.

Grandpa’s smile grew even wider, as he handed me the toy. He leaned in to where I stood, now holding my action figure. His smile grew softly devious, “hey buddy. Did I ever tell you about the krauts in Versailles?” My mom shook her head exhaustively. “Picture this, my squad and I, in our Sherman, going up against a Panzer in the pines.” The odd image of an old man grandpa driving a robotic Sherman (Peabody's adopted son) attacking a massive black panther. My confused expression caused my grandfather to belly laugh. It truly was such a beautiful sound. That’s how you know you really stuck it. Those laughs would roll, and roll. God, I miss him. 

Mom stepped in, kindly guiding me away from him. *Knock, knock, knock.* I stopped moving, but my mom kept softly pushing. My legs gave in to the greater force, and I began shuffling again. I looked upward to my mothers face, recoiling immediately. A wide smile stretched across her face giving her inhuman smile lines, her eyebrows cartoonishly arched over her equally stretched eyes. I dropped to the floor, screaming and retreating towards any wall, anything, anyone. Once against my back on the wall. I closed my eyes tight, doing as mom had instructed me whenever I saw the dull eyed man in my closet. I closed them tight, wishing, no, praying this beast away.

When the soft touch of a child’s hand grounded me to earth. Roy stood above me. I felt as an ant might upon seeing a man for the first time. He helped me to my feet, and whispered softly. “Are you okay?”

I attempted to maintain my grip on my assured position as leader of the group, and smiled dismissively. “Yeah I’m fine. I just got scared.” He smiled sympathetically.

“Your mama ran crying. She says it’s cause you keep doing this.”

“Huh?”

“You looked like you saw a ghost!” He jabbed at my protruding belly in a friendly manner. 

Grandpa looked at me, his eyes taking on a confused concern. “Hey buddy, come on over here. I have a question for you.” I did as instructed, although I did so with extreme trepidation. Upon sitting down next to him, I noted that my grandmother had fallen asleep. Soft snores coursed through her mouth as she rested her head against grandpa. He reached out and softly took my hand. “Have you noticed anything odd about your mother lately?” I shook my head. “Maybe she spanks you more than you deserve?” His voice wasn’t accusatory, rather it sounded like a soft blend of stark concern, and tender love. Again I shook my head. 

My father re-entered the room. His gaze immediately fixating on my grandfather and I. “So what is going on here?” He asked, blending his suspicions with good humor.

“Grandpa wants to know if mama spanks me a lot.” Grandpa winced hard.

“Oh. Does he?” Dad motioned for grandpa to go with him, and in that moment I turned to go play with Roy and Junior. Who had vacated the room? With that I began my expedition to locate my friends. I immediately checked my room and the play room. Nothing. That left the kitchen, the guest room, and my parents room. I checked the kitchen first, where I found grandma fingerprints dotting the thick buttermilk frosting of my cake. I moved on, to my left was the first door in the hallway. It was the guest room. Hollow yells echoed in harsh procession behind the thin door, it was my dad. I stepped closer to the door, softly pressing my ear against the cold plywood. My dad sounded vicious, his voice practically dripped with venom. “You don’t have any idea what you’re talking about! She’s getting better!”

My grandfather defended himself in a calm, controlled tone. “She has had these fits since she was a child, Mark. I don’t want the boy to suffer because of something preventable.”

“That’s rich. Coming from the king of mean.” I backed up from the door confused, then reapplied my ear to the door. It was completely silent. The only sound emanating was a dull buzz in my ears. I leaned in closer, pressing firmer upon the door than before.

 “WHAT DO YOU WANT, YOU LITTLE SHIT!”

The screaming snake-like voice ripped down the hallway, I recoiled, my gaze immediately fixating on the door centered at the end of the hall. My parents' room.

*Did it come from here or-* Suddenly the brass knob of the guest room began to shake, rattling with violent intensity. I ran from the guest room, sprinting towards my parents door. I  quickly turned the knob and slammed it behind me. I locked it then pressed what little weight I had against it. The guest room door opened then slammed shut behind it. Heavy dull footsteps echoed from behind the door, approaching my secure room within seconds. It set something heavy, a box of some kind, on the floor then made its retreat. I was safe. The monster was gone and I could breathe.

Well, that breathing was excellent for the moment it lasted. As soon as my guard was down I felt a heavy, thick, rivulet drop on my shoulder. To my horror, something loomed over my head in the darkest corner of the room. I knew what it was, more importantly though I knew who it was. Its shadow lowered itself on the bed, the creaks of the springs in the mattress allowed my mind to run with an estimation of its weight. I couldn’t see the figures' features, aside from the beady dull eyes which gazed at me with murderous intent. I grasped the handle desperately pulling at it, until finally my senses caught up with my brain.

The figure growled wet snarls behind me, then lunged. In the same moment the door came loose and I leapt out in the hall. It all happened so quickly, the box the original creature set out lunged like a tiger, and a gigantic toy clown landed a hit against the monster. The dull man was thrown into the wall behind it, and I ran. Not just a measly jog, I sprinted away from where the events were transpiring.That’s when I found myself in the living room again. A clown with red hair and crazy makeup stood in the corner, staring at me in a pitiful manner. It rushed forward in a gentle yet ushering manner, and I awoke in my grandfather’s arms. The warmth of his presence made me realize I had nothing to worry about.

This was thirty years ago. When the firefighters arrived at my childhood home, they subdued my mother, who was drenched in the blood of another. It didn’t take them long to find Roy, Junior, and Dad.

Mom was sentenced to a mental asylum, and I was sent to live with grandma and grandpa for the last bit of my childhood. Whatever could be salvaged anyway. As for the missing children, nothing has come of it. Yet, it seems that if I go to their home in costume, everything turns out alright.

I don’t think I ever fully recovered. At times the dull man watches me from my closet, yet he no longer advances. Just watches, waiting for me to drop my guard. It won’t happen, not anymore, because now I understand something which I hadn’t grasped before. Not every clown goes to hell.
