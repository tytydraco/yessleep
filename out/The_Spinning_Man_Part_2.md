The night deepened like a bruise. The trees creaked, and the stars seemed to vanish one by one. 

The appearance of the hunter had ramped up our nerves. While the guys joked with each other in lame attempts at bravado, I wrestled with the horrid meat of what we were doing here. If I was to believe what I saw on those home movies, then basically my entire life had been playing make-believe. I mean, I never thought we were alone in the universe. But, I figured first contact would be a hundred years from now and involve a land rover on a distant moon, its robotic microscope greeting wriggling worms thawed from a drilled core of deep-sea ice. 

It was too preposterous. It had to be a hoax. Cody, Ryan, and Bryan were simply tricking me so I would release my little doc into the world, ripe with my genuine terror, and fool a bunch of gullible morons on Reddit and YouTube. It would legitimize the *Lone Gunmen* and cement their status as gurus in the field of UAF or whatever the fuck it was called.  

My lip curled. Yes, it was all a hoax. And if they were dragging me all the way out here into the forest, then they had one last hurrah in store for me. I balled my fists, my loathing tinged with fear.

The forest thinned out, and we came to an overlook, the ground dropping away. Below us resembled a dark, primordial sea. It was the forest canopy stretching out for miles in all directions. Despite being in a clearing, it was even darker than before. The stars were gone. The sky was completely overcast, the moonlight turning it into a spectral sheet of gray. 

“Well, that’s just great,” Ryan said.

I started recording again. Even if this was a hoax, it wouldn’t be at my expense. I would spin it as a cautionary tale about three manipulative incels with too much time on their hands. 

“It’s 11:53,” Cody said. “If it’s here, now’s the time.”

We stood around and waited. I refused to be the butt of their joke, but a small sliver of me kept asking ‘what if, what if this is really real?’ 

Ryan lit another cigarette, and I asked for one even though I didn’t smoke. It tasted like shit, but it distracted me. Any time there was an opening in the clouds, my cousin aimed at it with his telephoto lens.

“What got you into this kind of stuff, Bryan?”

“Me?” He smiled uncomfortably. “When I was nine or ten, my mom had all of her fillings replaced, like, on the same day. And when I asked her why, she told me it was because aliens had installed a tracking device in her teeth.”

Bryan tilted his thermal camera up at the sky and began to pan it.

“Yup, my mom to this day is convinced she was abducted by aliens as a teenager. She has really bad sleep paralysis, though. I think that’s what caused it. She lives a normal life otherwise. But needless to say, that admission left a permanent mark on me. I’ve been into space and aliens my whole life.”

I shifted uncomfortably. It didn’t seem like a story someone would concoct to freak me out. “But, that sounds like you’re not a believer,” I said.

“I want to believe—”

He began mumbling to himself. 

“What is it?” 

“It-it’s there,” Bryan said, holding up the thermal camera. “Th-the sp-spinning man.”

His knees were literally knocking together. He wasn’t acting. He couldn’t be acting. 

“Are you fucking with us?” I blurted, stepping back. 

“I’m serious,” he whispered. 

“I know you’re all messing with me.” 

The guys didn’t respond. They were staring up into the sky. I considered leaving. I glanced behind me at the impenetrably dark mouth of the forest and reconsidered. Cody and Ryan were huddled around Bryan now, gawking at his thermal camera screen. No way, no goddamn way, I thought. I swallowed hard and squeezed myself between them, willing myself to keep recording. Bryan switched the mode to something called ‘black hot.’ The overcast sky turned white in the thermal camera, revealing a thing black and bubbling far up within it.
 
“Maybe it’s a plane,” Cody said.

“It’s too close,” Bryan said. “It’s in front of the clouds.”

"A drone?"

"It's too quiet."

“Let’s go closer,” Ryan said. “Keep the lights off.”

“We will not get closer,” I hissed. 

They started following the edge of the forest. 

“Are you all fucking with me?” 

Ryan wouldn’t stop walking. “A little closer,” he kept whispering.

Soon, we were underneath it. The image was clearer. Too clear. The thing slowly spun. Its body shrunk into something spherical and then the limbs bubbled out of it again. Cody was trying to see it with his camera, but it was too dark. It was invisible to the naked eye.

“Are you recording this, Ellie?” 

I couldn’t even speak. I nodded, my voice frozen in my throat. 

Ryan was laughing, as if we were witnessing the finale of a fireworks show and not some disgusting creature flying in the sky. He reached into Bryan’s backpack and pulled out a huge Maglite. 

“Maybe we can communicate with it.”

I grabbed his hand. “Are you insane?”

“My uncle filmed this thing for over a decade. What’s the worst that could happen?”

“Your uncle went crazy and killed himself!”

“Are you going to keep filming your shoes, or are you going to capture the most important thing to ever happen to us?” Ryan wrenched his hand away and pressed his thumb down on the switch. The flashlight beam lit up the sky. We collectively gasped. 

The spinning man was there, glasslike, its surface rippling as the light touched it. A white hue began to seep into its skin, the grotesque white of an eyeball.

“What’s it doing?” Cody whispered. 

It disappeared. We stood there, blinking. Nobody moved.

“You scared it—”

The spinning man reappeared closer, too close. You could throw a rock at it. I heard it now… a squelching sound. It was like when I sat with my sister during her pregnancy ultrasound, the baby’s heartbeat, but louder and stranger. The sound began to speed up, undercut with an excited chittering. My legs weakened. The blood left my body. The creature was floating down to us… too fast.

“What the fuck? *Bo?”*

“I haven’t seen him.”

“Just do it!”

Cody was looking at his camera. “I got it. Holy shit, I got it on camera.”

They huddled around it. “Ellie check your phone,” Cody said.

“You should have been there!”

You have proof on your phone of an extraterrestrial. An *extra terrestrial,*” he enunciated. “Not even the moon landing is as important as what we’ve got right here. I don’t know about you guys, but I don’t plan on leaving tonight or in the foreseeable future. I want as much evidence as possible of the spinning man. I couldn’t bring myself to look at the footage, at least right now. I couldn’t bear to see that thing. Instead, I plugged the phone into the charger and threw it on the cushioned wicker chair beside me. 

The worst part of it was that I knew Ryan was right.

“Isn’t it obvious? Someone doesn’t want to share the wealth.”

“What? No. Bo is genuinely the most innocent person I’ve ever met. He visits his grandma every week and cooks for her. Besides, where would he hide everything? You think he’s going to come back to this cabin all alone? He was scared enough of this region before the alien.”

“…I didn’t look.”

“You didn’t look?” Cody said.

“I was scared, alright?”

“Well, that’s suspicious,” Ryan said.

“I was terrified. I’m still terrified.”

I got up, and we continued our bickering in the living room. Bo and Bryan were sitting on the futon, which had been folded out into a bed.

He had a shoulder sling on the other side of the window and put it back on. He then offered me his hand and pulled me out onto it. A wave of vertigo hit me in the dark.  

We had to walk slowly, the roof creaking below us while the stars blazed overhead. My knees wobbled so much that I thought I might collapse and roll off to my death. We eventually got to the edge. The roof ended above the front porch railing. Bryan said it would be easier if he went first so he could help me down. I nodded and offered a weak smile.

He laid down on his stomach and dangled his legs off. He then scooted lower until only his arms and head were visible. We flinched as the gutter squeaked. But the tree frogs were out in full force, their ugly peeping helping mask our sounds.
 
“I got it,” he finally said. 

He lowered himself and then disappeared. His hand then waved in the dark, his silver fingernails glinting in the moonlight. I took it as a sign and lowered myself on my stomach until he got ahold of my feet. I then hung by my fingers and let go as he pulled me in by my waist. He lifted me and set me down onto the deck. It was pretty manly for a UFO geek.

We stood outside of the bathroom, right before the living room windows wrapped around the house, and knelt down. The living room lights lit up the remaining deck and the front yard beneath it. 

Bryan groaned and rubbed his right ankle. “I think I messed it up again when I picked you up.”

So much for manly. “Can you walk?”

“I don’t know.”

We could see the van. It was about thirty feet from the other end of the house. We could climb over the railing and go around the back of the house, but it didn’t look like Bryan would be able to endure it. 

“We keep under the windows and crawl,” I finally said. 

Bryan nodded.

We began crawling. When we got to the first window, I stopped and peeked in. I could just make out Bo, the back of his head as he sat on the futon. His stillness disturbed me. I thought of the animated Bo I knew and my chest throbbed with grief. 

I checked on Bryan, and he gave a thumbs up. We crawled under three more windows and then stopped just before the porch door. I wanted to keep going, but I needed to make sure Cody and Ryan were in there. I slowly lifted my head again, this window open. I looked through the screen, and the hairs on my neck stood up. Bo and Ryan were on the futon, and my cousin was in the arm chair, all of them sitting dead still, their mouths open. There was no talking, no TV, no beer drinking. They just sat there, staring at nothing. 

Bryan’s face came up beside mine. He took my hand and squeezed it to quell my panic. I motioned to continue. ”Wait,” he said. “I need to know.” 

He ducked down and pulled the sling off of his shoulder. He then pulled out his thermal camera. I remembered what he said, about seeing a third person with Bo and me in the woods, and my eyes grew wide. Bryan turned it on. He slowly brought it up to the window, and I inhaled so hard it was like a fist in my stomach. I clapped my hands over my mouth to contain my scream. The screen revealed five people in the room, captured in acidic hues. What looked like deformed children were standing beside Cody and Ryan. No, they weren’t standing, they were floating slightly off the floor, floating and spinning, their limbs shrinking and expanding, their heads disappearing only to be reborn on their chests and sliding back up onto their necks. And from each of their lumpy mid-sections, a thin umbilical-like cord trailed out, vibrating wildly, one cord passing through Ryan’s jaw and vanishing inside his skull, the other intersecting my cousin’s face.

I looked again at Bo. He was sitting alone—

A huge face lit up the thermal camera, blocking our view. It was pinched and vaguely human, just on the other side of the window screen. It was inches from us. Two unaligned eyes grew out of the flesh, like bubbles surfacing in a puddle, and then a small hole opened in the center of its face. A tube snaked out of it, white hot in the thermal, and extended towards us. It was the spinning man. We looked up over the camera, and nothing was there. This time, we both screamed.

I fell back, pulling Bryan with me. We rolled down the porch steps, and then I was tripping and running towards the van, Bryan hobbling behind me. As I got closer, I saw it. *The tires.* They were all deflated. 

“We have to run!” I screamed back to Bryan. 

“Oh my god, oh my god, oh my god,” he was saying over and over. 

We ran through the grass in moonlight, the stalks brushing our shins. The porch door slowly squealed open. Halfway across the pasture, I stopped and turned as Bryan caught up. The silhouettes of Cody, Ryan, and Bo stood at the base of the porch, silently observing us. Bryan raised the camera again, his hand shaking so much that he had to hold it with both hands. The screen was a blur of colors, and then it revealed the two smaller creatures, untethered, speeding towards us. 

“Let’s go! Let’s go!” I shrieked and began to run.

My legs took over my brain. Bryan’s voice faded into the distance.

“Wait!” he screamed. “I don’t want to die.”

I looked over my shoulder, and he was hopping on one foot. I had already put thirty feet between us. I should’ve helped him. You see that in movies, like war movies, the hero risking his life, backtracking to stick his head under his wounded comrade’s arm and dragging him to safety. But, I didn’t do that. I  kept running and running. And when I looked back again, Bryan was on his knees with his back bent. I could see it then, the spinning man, who wasn’t a man, who if it even had a sex was likely a female—a mother. Whatever the fuck it was, it was shimmering in the moonlight, like a demented angel, holding Bryan’s hands, bending him backwards, as a cord-like tendril passed through his quivering cheek and disappeared into his head, his jaw yawning open in reflex. The two smaller creatures swirled around them, their limbs bubbling and shifting at a frenzied speed. It almost looked like they were dancing.

I screamed and continued to run. I ran through the pasture. I ran through the darkness of a copse. I ran and fell down and ran again. I slid down a trench and then stumbled out onto a patch of dirt. And then a light came down on me as I fell to my knees, my hands in the air. I bent back and screamed.
 
———

“You awake?” 

I blinked and then jerked back in my seat.

“Where am I?” 

The giant hunter smiled next to me. “Didn’t I warn ya to wear a huntin’ vest? I nearly ran ya over.”

He was stooped behind the steering wheel of his truck, driving through a town. I was in the passenger seat. He told me he was on his way to buy cigarettes when I nearly ate his grill. He was planning to drop me off at the local hospital. 

“Unless ya want me to take ya back to Rita’s place?”

“No!” 

He pulled into a gas station. I tried to tell him what happened, but I was babbling. I knew I didn’t make sense. The man insisted that I go to the hospital, but I refused. We were at a 24-hour station, and I told him I would call a taxi from here. I told him that he should stay too, that it was dangerous to go back. I wondered why the aliens hadn’t possessed him too or whatever it was that they did to people. Was it because he was  so tall?

“A word of advice,” the hunter said. He lit a cigarette while peering down at me from the driver-side window. “Lay off Rita’s magic mushrooms.” He chuckled and then drove off.

I cleaned up in the bathroom, bought myself Mountain Dew, and looked at magazines in a well-lit section of the store without really reading them. The cashier, a teenage girl, was nice enough to let me hang out and even use the phone behind the desk. A taxi came about an hour later from the closest city. Thankfully, I had my wallet and credit cards. That’s about all I had. 

The driver drove me all the way to Bangor, Maine, which cost me a ridiculous amount of money. And from there, I took a bus, nodding in and out of sleep the entire time. At one point, I woke up shrieking and had to apologize to the other passengers. 

When I got home, I quickly packed, bought a plane ticket, and flew someplace I won’t disclose. I’ve been here ever since.

———

I don’t sleep nearly as much these days. Sometimes I wake up in the middle of the night hearing that horrible sucking sound and seeing Bo leaning back on his knees with his mouth gaping open; my Bo, who wasn’t so much a coward as he was the smartest of us for staying behind. And, I think he truly did run out into the woods to find me after he heard our screams. Unfortunately, it found him first. 

Other times I wake up to Bryan’s voice, Bryan in the moonlight, screaming, “I don’t want to die.” 

The crazy thing though is no one did die. I called the cops, who filed a report about stolen goods, ignoring my more outlandish claims. And from what I can gather from my relatives, Cody is still up at that cabin. Ryan and Bryan are there too, along with a new friend, a handsome fellow named Bo. “It’s odd though,” she said. “I’ve heard they’ve started a church.”

I don’t know what to think of that. I want to think that the trauma of what they experienced had made them find God. But, I shiver at the alternative, that maybe a church is the perfect place to propagate.... 

So if you’re in the Allagash region, maybe get yourself a thermal camera. Hell, wherever you are, you should probably get yourself a thermal camera. And then count the people around you, and then count them again with the screen on. I wish I could tell you I’m doing the same, but I don’t go outside so much these days.

Ironically, a month or so after that trip to the cabin, I opened up my laptop, and there in my Photos app was the first video I’d taken for my documentary film. I had forgotten to turn off iCloud, and it had synced from my iPhone before maxing out the space. The sound was pretty bad, but it showed a bunch of goofy kids off on an adventure. It was funny and pretty compelling. I think the teacher would have given me an ‘A.’ Of course, the clip didn’t get up to the part with the spinning man. It ended with us toasting to close encounters.
