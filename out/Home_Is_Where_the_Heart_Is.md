I've always hated the taste of coffee. It doesn’t matter what you add to it, its still the same dirty bean water that's sweetened to be tolerable to some people. Or if you're super hardcore you’ll just drink it straight black like you hate yourself .

I work long, hard shifts at a local factory. Sometimes up to 12 hours because we are understaffed. So I’m often at the brink of total exhaustion. To try to fight off sleep I've been trying a couple methods to get around drinking coffee. I’ve tried gum and sodas, but the thing that I really tend to fall back on is energy drinks. I don’t have too much of a preference of what kind I drink or flavor, just whatever's on sale at the gas station across the street. I always feel much more focused and productive afterwards, and that usually is enough to power me through the rest of the grueling hours. I started off drinking one during my lunch break, then it quickly progressed into me drinking multiple throughout my shift. Almost embarrassingly 5-6 a day. Which is way over the recommended amount. But  I didn’t care at the time.

After a couple months of the same routine, I was so dependent on them. I couldn’t even get through my off days without the cravings and sleepiness. My head would hurt If I didn’t give into the urge to chug one. 

Then came the final crash and burn. It was just a normal Tuesday, I had just finished moving pallets with the forklift and gone to the breakroom when I started to feel off. My heart started pounding and I felt incredibly weak. I thought I  was just feeling the effects of the heat inside of the warehouse. But then the ground came rushing up to meet me and I was engulfed in the void of unconsciousness.

Thankfully one of my co-workers had just finished his smoke break and heard me fall and called an ambulance. I don't remember it hurting, or really anything leading up to the cardiac arrest. I just woke up under the harsh lights of my hospital room. I was conscious before they gave me surgery, but I guess due to all of the medication they put me on to stabilize me I wasn’t in legal condition to consent so they had to inform my wife of the proposed heart transplant. They said I was eligible for one and they just happen to have a donor one being held in a hospital in the neighboring county. 

So they did the procedure. The surgery was successful and I had to stay in the hospital for a week and a half so they could monitor me.  When I explained my \**ahem*\* caffeine addiction they said it was a miracle that it hadn’t happened sooner. Or that I survived at all. They mentioned other cases where people weren’t as lucky as me, which rattled me a little bit so I've sworn off all forms of caffeine. I definitely dealt with some headaches after I stopped, but they have medicine for that. 

I was sent home and I took off work for a while. I mostly just spent time with Jamie, my wife, and daughter since I couldn’t really do that too much before. 

Then I started noticing things. The first time it happened I was getting ready for bed, brushing my teeth in the mirror. I gazed into my reflection and I didn’t recognize myself. Not in the "I've gained weight” way, but in a way that frightened  me and filled me with heart sinking dread. It didn’t feel like my reflection was my own for a brief moment. Like I was locking eyes with a stranger who mimicked my every move with exact precision. I didn't really think of it too much at the time because I was still recovering from my almost life ending experience, but its all adding up now. 

The next time it happened I was taking my daughter out for a walk. She rode her little pink scooter alongside me as we went around the block. The best way I can describe what hit me was a flashback. In my mind’s eye I could see a young girl, almost my daughter's age. She had long, black hair that went almost all the way down her back and a magenta long sleeve shirt. She was riding a bicycle next to me. I swear I’ve never met this child before in my life. But for some reason it felt so real. Like she was my child. I felt a deep parental love for this strange girl at that moment. I snapped out of it and continued on with my day, but the weird memory lingered in my consciousness.

It kept happening more frequently after that. I would be doing a simple task and then BOOM! I'm struck with some all consuming, emotional flashback. Like I was driving to the store and I was hit with a not-memory of the same girl, but older, driving a car that I have never owned with me in the passenger seat. Somehow I knew that she had just got her learners permit, that this was one of her first times driving with me. I felt excited and nervous. So nervous that she was growing up and I would lose her. Then I came back to my reality, confused, but arrived at the store. 

I was dropping my daughter off to school and we usually arrive early and just sit in the  drop off zone until they let the kids in. We always listen to our favorite morning talk show on the radio, as it was pretty much the only time I would get to spend with her before I was sentenced to my job. She was typically asleep when I would come home again very late in the night. As I was looking at the school building I was mentally transported to the school’s gym. I was sitting in a little blue chair watching that same girl I keep seeing walking across a stage. I knew that it was her 8th grade graduation and I knew that my wife, who I've also seen a couple times in my flashbacks, who was also a person whom I've never met, were planning on taking her out to her favorite restaurant to celebrate afterwards.

My actual wife, the one who I’ve been married to for the last 7 years, said that I've  been acting strangely recently. She brushed it off as something to due with my recovery. She thanked me for making a delicious meal a couple days ago, one that I've never made before but cooked like it was just simply second nature. I never cook, I'm a horrible cook. I do the dishes and in return Jamie handles all of the meals. The weirdest part is that I don't remember doing any of this. I just remember washing the dishes that night. I assumed that Jamie was trying out new recipes, but I hadn’t remembered what it was.   
Random thoughts keep popping into my head as well. Usually when I'm home I’d prefer to have time to myself to decompress, but a voice inside of my head told me that I need to spend more time with my daughter. So I took her to the park, which is something I never do.

Last night I dreamt about that same car that the girl was driving me in. I was driving it this time, listening to a song that has recently become my favorite. I drove by a billboard for a movie that just came out, a billboard I past by a couple weeks ago when I was visiting a friend in the next county over. Then a deer ran in front of me. I panicked and swerved to try and avoid it. I ran off the road and smashed into a tree. It was like hitting a brick wall. Pain exploded all over. I was slipping in and out of consciousness, it felt so vivid. Eventually I succumbed to my injuries and bled out before the ambulance arrived.             
  
I think he may be taking over. I feel less like myself everyday, every second. The sands of my life are falling away. I don’t know what to do
