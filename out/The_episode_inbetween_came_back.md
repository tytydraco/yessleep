I pressed play by the dumpsters with my gloves off, because not pressing had started to feel like its own kind of answer.

Not wrong like different. Wrong like edited. The man in it still had a sister. She still called the landline in their mother's house, dead four years, the phone dead with her. The fourth night, three in the morning, the new phone lit up on the nightstand. No ring. One voicemail, a minute and nine seconds, from a number I knew by heart — my old one, the one I'd had eleven years, the one the kid said would eventually go back into the pool. 🙃

She listened to the end, then sat through the silence after.

Last night it used the other one. The name she called me when we were kids, the one nobody has said in twenty years, the one that isn't on any form anywhere. It said it once, careful, like a word being put together. Then the breathing. Then, like it was nothing: "You're still in the house."

It learned the official me from the paperwork. It's learning the rest of me somewhere else. It has the episode to read now — my whole year of it, in the hosts' voices, an hour and forty-two minutes long.

I don't know whose house. I was at work for most of this, or across the street from the house I grew up in, watching somebody else's trampoline in the dark. But I know it started with a word in pieces, and it has my name the way she said it, and nobody alive taught it that.
