Rain drummed my windshield to the tune of a Louis Armstrong song playing through the crackly night radio as I pulled up to the twins' house. Normally, Casey and Lacey’s dad made sure that his mighty two-story suburban dream house looked in top shape to keep up the family’s image; a perfectly manicured lawn, trimmed hedges, rows of lights along the front fence to illuminate the status symbol of the town’s main employer.

I knew something was wrong immediately when those yard lights were dark, casting the whole property as a silhouette against the dull glow of amber streetlights. All the windows were dark as well, save for one in the top east corner; the twins’ parent's room. The perfectly manicured lawn was disrupted with abnormal shapes piled up between the garage and front door, and a figure moved between the haphazard stacks with a flashlight, illuminating them as they went.

The icy white beam revealed mounds of boxes and plastic totes getting soaked by the downpour, and the figure, Casey, worked hard to press the valuables against the house, hoping the lip of the roof might mitigate the damage.

He had texted me asking if he could borrow my truck right away for a few hours, and though the hour was late, Casey wasn’t the type to be so spontaneous, so I knew the issue had to be important.

*‘Sure, man. Everything alright?’* I had asked.

*‘Just head over. I’ll explain in person.’*

Backing into his driveway, he paused his shuffling and crossed the lawn to meet me. I hopped out and pulled my hood up, then moved to the back of my bed and popped it.

“Hey man,” Casey greeted me with a warm, yet tired tone, “thanks so much for coming out. I hate to bother you, but Lacey’s using my truck, and…” his voice trailed off as he turned his gaze back to the stacks of boxes.

“Hey, it’s no problem,” I told him, giving his shoulder a clasp, “What’s going on? What is all this stuff?”

Casey didn’t answer at first. His eyes went from the boxes up to his parents' window, where a figure stood looking down on us. It appeared to be his mom, and as soon as we’d noticed her, she softly raised her hand and pressed it to the glass, before turning quickly and stepping away.

Casey sighed, then spun, heaving one of the totes on the ground into his hands, “Lacey and I are moving out.”

That answer shocked me. We’d been hanging out regularly over the last few weeks, and neither twin had mentioned anything about leaving their parent's home. We had just graduated, and their family had plenty of room; there didn’t seem to be any reason why they needed to rush to migrate. Given the time of night and current weather conditions, however, it didn’t seem like this was the standard moving day.

“What happened?” I asked, secondhand anxiety growing in my chest as I watched the flap of a cardboard box droop beneath the weight of the downpour. I moved to the passenger side of my truck to grab a tarp from under the seat.

“Mom and Pop met Anna tonight.”

I made a confused face, “I thought they’d already met Anna? She and Lacey have been friends for a while now, yeah?”

“Well, they met *Anna and Lacey*; the couple,” Casey elaborated.

That set a grim silence over us that was only stifled by the rain, and all I could offer was a small, “Oh…”

“Yeah,” Casey agreed, hauling another box into the bed.

I aided in the next few and shook my head, “They took it that badly, huh?”

“Dad did,” Casey explained, “Mom wasn’t happy either, but… I don’t think she wanted to lose her children over it. Either way, it was a long talk, and she ended on his side. Dad’s pissed that his little girl isn’t the ‘ideal woman’ he raised her to be, and you know anything that isn’t perfect doesn’t get to stay in this household.”

My stomach sank for Lacey, and I looked back up toward the home where her bedroom window was. It was dark and empty.

“Where is she?” I asked, “Is she alright?”

Casey shook his head and snickered with a bitter venom, “Not really. I think she feels relief that it’s finally out, but I mean, her parents disowned her in a single night. Imagine being raised and loved by people you care about for 18 years, just for them to throw you out like last week's garbage over who you like to kiss.”

I had finally finished stringing the tarp over my tailgate, and I cinched the rope hard with bubbling rage. I’d never made it known, but I’d always somewhat disliked Casey’s parents. Even though they were always cordial and provided a lot for our friend group over the years, we’d heard enough stories through the twins to form a more critical opinion.

I didn’t appreciate the way they seemed to treat their kids more like accessories than actual living, breathing human beings. If either of the twins tried to pursue a hobby or passion that didn’t align with the typical roll of their gender or class, they were quickly shot down or punished until that dream left their heads.

It figured that now that they were adults and could no longer be pushed around, their parent’s first option would be to go nuclear. If it was clear to them that their angel little girl had been sneaking around behind their backs seeing a—heaven forbid—*woman,* for God-knows how long, they would assume the matter was a lost cause. ‘If we can’t tell you what to do, then get the hell out of our house.’

Casey spoke again, “She and Anna took off a bit ago with the first half of our stuff. I let her take my truck so she could get out of here. She wanted to get far away as fast as possible.”

“I don’t blame her,” I agreed.

Casey grabbed another box and began loading it up, and I joined his side. It wasn’t until a box was in my hands that was loaded with his things, that a detail finally dawned on me.

“So they kicked you out too? Even though they’re pissed at Lacey?”

Casey snorted, “Oh, no, they’re pissed at me too. Sides’ I think I’m done with this house. Lived under their rules for long enough, and I don’t think I need to stay with parents who can’t love the identical other half of me.”

I paused my process and looked at him, “What did you do?”

Casey avoided eye contact and continued boarding his stuff onto the lifeboat, “When Dad started freaking out on Lacey, she sort of shut down. She knew it was coming, but I don’t think she’d fully planned out what she was going to say. I didn’t like seeing her like that, so I sorta’ freaked out on her behalf.”

 Casey went to lift the next box, but the rain had gotten to the cardboard so badly that the side of it ripped open, spilling its contents like gutted innards. He wordlessly scooped the stuff back in and tilted the container to keep it inside, but one last piece caught his attention before he lifted the box. A framed picture of him, Lacey and their parents at the town’s harvest festival when they were young kids. The smiles seemed genuine, but they were far away now, and Casey tossed the frame like a frisbee into the center of the lawn.

“Dad was going to kick her out no matter what unless she ‘renounced her gay’, and when I gave him a piece of my mind, he told me that I was welcome to go with her if I had an issue. I don’t think he thought I was going to call his bluff. Clearly, he never paid enough attention if he thought the bond I had with him was stronger than the sister I’d spent my whole life beside.”

“He didn’t even hesitate?” I asked.

“Nah,” Casey darkly scoffed, “I think it pissed him off more that I was so certain in my decision. He went on about how ungrateful we both were after everything he gave to us. Told us we had until midnight to get all of our shit and leave, or he was calling the cops.”

I discretely checked my watch. The time was 12:42.

“Where are you guys heading now, then?” I asked him.

“Anna luckily just got her own place out at the Glade apartment, and she told us we could come stay. The place is a studio, so it’s going to be tight, but we’ll find a way to make it work. At least until we find someplace new.”

“You’re gonna’ stick with them?”

“Lacey’s idea. We just lost whatever accounts our parents had set aside for us, so we’re going to be strapped on cash for a while. Once we get on our feet, rent three ways will work the best.”

“Shit, Casey… I’m sorry all of this is happening to you guys.”

The boy turned to me past the brim of his cap, and in the dull light reflecting off his face, I saw a genuine smile, “Honestly, Jess? I think this was all for the best. We don’t have to pretend anymore, and now we have an excuse to get out of this hellhole of a town. No more family obligations tying us down.”

His words made a warmth blossom in my chest that combatted the chill of the rain. I was happy to see him have hope during such a dark time. Still, the thought eventually made the warmth cool and harden like a sharp volcanic rock. Bryce had already left town for college, and now I was about to lose two more friends.

Casey only plunged the knife deeper with his next words, “Kait was mentioning the other day that she’s looking for somewhere outside of town too,” he heaved another box and smirked cooly at me, “You should think about hopping on the train with the rest of us and getting out while you still can.”

My eyes darted to the final tote as I heaved it into my hands, and they stayed there, “Yeah, she was just talking with me about that, too. I’m thinking about it,” I lied, “It’s hard with my mom, though. Can’t really leave her alone with all my dad’s debt.” As I dropped the crate into the bed, then covered it with the tarp, I gave a smile to lighten things up again, “Besides, who’s going to look after Carly if you all abandon us?”

Casey laughed, “Alright, fair point. Guess you’ll just have to join us later.”

The two of us made sure everything was secure and then tightened ropes down over the tarp. I already knew where the Glade apartments were, so I silently let myself into the truck while Casey remained to triple check that he hadn’t left anything on the curb. I caught him casting one last forlorn look at his parents' window before he joined me.

I started up the truck and let it idle for a moment while Casey stared out the window at the dark, his eyes focused on the picture he’d thrown glinting beneath the streetlights. I reached across the seat and put a hand on his shoulder.

His eyes turned to me, tears hidden among the rain drops, and I gave him an earnest nod, “You’re a good brother, Casey.”

He smiled and wiped his face, “Yeah, well, Lacey’s an even better sister. I know if things were reversed, she’d never give up on me. Not for anything.”

And he was right. I couldn’t even begin to imagine the bond of a twin, especially as an only child. Having someone right by your side from the moment of birth, always being able to face anything that came your way, knowing you had an unfailing presence by your side. If you could love a friend on such a level, then your own flesh and blood would be a whole other level of depth.

That’s why, as we stood there before the yawning mouth of the red door, I didn’t blame Lacey when she couldn’t bring herself to raise the gun again when Casey shuffled closer, no matter how far from his usual self he looked.

The boy's eyes were glassy and fogged over with a milky decay, but the brown within still held a spark of light. They flickered around the space, taking in the paint job of carnage we’d plastered the place with over the past weeks. His expression was placid and perturbed, as if he’d just been rudely jolted from a long nap.

From the jaw down, Casey’s body was exactly how we’d last seen it. The same flannel, tee, and jeans dyed as red as the door from the gaping tear in his throat. My stomach somersaulted at the sight, the nausea threatening to bring me to the floor beneath the weight of my friend’s suffering. It wasn’t even the most grotesque part, however.

His left arm up to the shoulder was missing, but it wasn’t gore in its place. The creature that had taken him had not feasted on his now sickly flesh as we had imagined. It was worse—it was *unraveled*. The veins and sinew of his muscle had been twisted and tangled away, spun into fine spools that travelled somewhere back into the darkness he’d just emerged from, like a leash keeping him from moving too far. From within the knots of meat, I could see silvery flashes speckling through; strings of fate that were being tussled free from his body by something within the halls.

The damage began at his shoulder, then chewed its way to where his neck had been slashed, as well as trailing down to expose his ribs. The sight rendered us all unable to move—unable to speak even. I could barely hold the axe in my hands.

After all this time, I had become mentally prepared to see anything; I kept telling myself. Twisted beasts, grotesque monsters—none of it could phase me anymore. Whatever came out of the door, we would kill it as ruthlessly as it had killed Casey.

But now Casey was here again. The threat that came through the door *was him.* It was like the string in my chest had been reading my worst fears and whispering them to whoever was on the other side, and they knew the one thing that I just couldn’t bear.

This wasn’t Casey, and we all knew it. Casey had died weeks ago, and even if this thing standing before us was his true corpse, there was no way that we could force the life back into it. And yet, something else had. No matter how small, there was a flicker of Casey’s spirit that had been ignited once more to pilot this husk. A small bit of hope as an extra knife twist that maybe we could kindle that small flame back into a living fire. How could we snuff it out again?

Casey’s dead eyes finished taking in the room as he shuffled two more shaky steps closer, then he fixed on his sister once more, “L-Lacey?”

The girl had already begun silently shedding tears, but her brother’s desperate, rasping voice shattered her into harsh, loud sobs. She shook her head in terror and backed away.

Casey didn’t seem to be bothered by this, “What’s going on?” he stuttered, “What happened? I… I don’t remember anything… why don’t I…?”

I took a step forward and heaved my axe, but it was all for show. I knew if it really mattered, I wouldn’t be able to swing it. “Casey?” I breathlessly gasped, “Is that really you in there?”

His eyes turned to me, and he swallowed, an action that made the raw meat of his neck squelch and shift, “Jessie? What’s going on? I feel funny…”

He tried to take a step closer, and against the aching wishes in my chest, I threatened the axe at him in warning, “Hey now—just stay right there. We’re going to take this slow, okay? Just stay calm.”

I still held very little belief that this actually was my friend, but on the small chance it was, I didn’t want to hurt him anymore than he’d already been.

His eyes looked scared and concerned as he moved his hand to his head, “Is something wrong? Did something happen?” his eyes traced to one of the basement windows, and when they collided with the beams of the setting sun soaking through, he stood a little taller, as if invigorated by the sight. Memory seemed to bloom beneath the healing rays, “I remember we found a door… something came out… we were attacked.”

Lacey choked out another mighty sob, and covered her mouth, “Oh God—Casey…”

The air in the room felt so heavy in that moment that it was nearly impossible to push new words from my mouth. I’d already spent any confidence I’d had, and all I could do was wait for Casey to continue with whatever he’d come here to say.

“Did… Did I die?” he croaked out, “Am I dead?”

Lacey buckled over into herself, unable to take anymore. Behind me, Carly let out a noise like she’d been punched in the stomach. Kaitlynn stepped closer with tears in her eyes.

“Casey…” she said slowly, “What is this?”

We were all hoping that her words would call some sort of bluff, and the shell of our friend would show that he truly was a monster. Instead, it had the opposite effect. His confusion compounded, and he almost looked afraid.

“I-I don’t know what’s happening. I just woke up, and it was dark—I was in this place, a tower or something, and—”

His words were cut apart in his throat as the threads making up his left half were rudely yanked back into the darkness. The sound fell into pained gurgles, and we all jumped. Lacey looked as if she was ready to run forward to his aid, but I stopped her.

When Casey looked back up at us, his eyes had lost any and all sense of clarity he’d gained. He spoke again, distant, as if in a dream, “There were voices. There *still* *are* voices. I… I can hear them… do you guys all hear that?”

A panicked dance of glances passed between us before landing back on our friend, and I spoke on our behalf, “No, Casey. What are they saying?”

“They’re saying…” his words trailed off, and his brow scrunched in confusion, “They say you know what they want. That if you give it to them, it can all be over…”

Bryce’s breathing picked up, “W-What does that mean? Who is saying that? Is it the door?”

“What do you mean we know what they want?” Kait asked. She quickly realized she was asking the wrong person, and I wished she hadn’t, “Jessie, what does he mean ‘we know what they want’?”

I opened my mouth to answer her, but found myself only able to address Casey, “Who is saying that, Casey? Who did this to you?”

“They say… you know that too. That you already know them.”

“It’s the witches,” Carly shuddered breathlessly, “It has to be them. They really are still alive…”

Casey’s milky gaze cleared with understanding once again, but it only brought fear to his face. The threads jerked, and he winced with a yelp, more words pouring out with his pain, “They say that if you give it to them, they’ll restore me and let me go…”

The words rang so loudly in my ears that all at once, any other sound stopped. The ominous breeze from the doorway, the trickle of Casey’s blood; even the beating of my own heart seemed to go hush.

Casey’s corpse began whimpering, and he lifted his head to us in desperation, “I… I don’t know what any of it means… what’s going on? Guys, I-I’m scared.”

For a moment, rationality left me. The mere words that Casey offered was reason enough for me to step forward and take his hand. If there was even a modicum of a chance that Casey would be restored should I give my thread up to the witches, I needed to take it. After all, he was only standing here bloodied and dead before us because he’d given his life to save me. It was already my plan to give myself to them, I may as well return the favor.

Luckily, Bryce hadn’t yet lost his wits to the emotions, “That can’t be real,” he said to us, “It has to be some sort of trick. They’ve been trying to kill us this long; why would they make peace now?”

“Bryce is right,” Carly agreed, “This is all too convenient; right when we’re this close to figuring something out?”

Lacey turned to face them, tears streaking through the sweat and grim coating her cheeks, “But what if its not? What if this is their form of surrender *because* we’re getting so close?”

The puppet of our friend thrashed again, its puppeteers taking advantage of the doubt. Through gritted teeth, he said, “They’re saying that you can’t beat them… That they’ll just keep sending more of their subjects until all of you die. They say this is the only chance you’ll get…”

“Lacey, we *cannot* listen to this. It’s not real,” Bryce insisted.

She looked between him and her brother, raw uncertainty in her eyes, “What can I do? That’s my brother, Bryce!”

“Not anymore, it’s not. Lacey, *look at him*, Casey is *gone.*”

“P-Please,” The corpse croaked, bloody tears now spilling from his eyes, “It’s so cold, and I’m so scared—someone just tell me what’s happening.”

Kait stepped in before the bickering could escalate and tried to sound as defiant as possible through her trembling breath. She spoke not to Casey but to the space beyond the door, “Even if we wanted to we can’t! We have no idea what you stupid fuckers want!”

Casey’s breathing picked up, “T-They keep screaming that you do. That one of you knows.”

All eyes in the space turned to me, and if I didn’t think it was possible for my stomach to knot even more, I was quickly proven wrong.

“Jessie,” Lacey begged, “What do they mean? I thought you said that you didn’t know—that you didn’t see anything in that last vision.”

I thought carefully about my response. The jig was up now, and I knew that, but telling them would make this situation go from bad to worse. Right now, there was a clean and easy solution: I trade my life—something I was already going to do—and in return, we possibly get Casey back. If my friends found out this was a life for a life, however, then not only would I never get the chance to get them out of danger, but they would never take such a bargain.

“I… I didn’t. Not exactly,” I told them.

All my friends kept their eyes drilling into me, but my gaze turned up to Casey once more. So far his autonomy had felt uncertain, played straight enough that it might actually be our old friend, but far too suspicious to be trusted. Something about the way he fixed me in that moment said otherwise.

There was a new level of tenderness to his eyes. An emotion besides fear and confusion. It was like the clarity he’d been gathering had collected into one final burst, and somewhere between the voices tugging at his threads and our terrified sobs, Casey finally realized the gravity of what was happening.

“They say you have three hours to decide, or it’ll be too late,” Casey told us, delivering one last message. Then, he shook his head and looked squarely at me, “Whatever it is they want, don’t give it to them, Jessie. Don’t—”

The tendons of his body went violently taunt, causing him to scream in agony and fall over. Like a fish on a line, he was dragged backward across the carpet into the darkness. The last thing we saw of him was his head lifted, looking back at us, desperate eyes glinting in the glow of our flashlights. That iridescent shine faded, his head lolled back like a rag doll, and his limp corpse was taken away.

“Casey!” Lacey screamed, leaning to rush after him.

Bryce had been expecting this to happen, because he quickly lunged forward and grabbed her from behind. Lacey thrashed and sobbed, trying to break free, but ultimately the strength left her, and she crashed to the floor on her knees, the gun clattering from her lap.

The rest of us stood there, staring at the dark opening in shock. For once, we had no desire to shut the door, as if doing so might cut off any chance we had of seeing our friend again. Why did he have to say that last thing? Why couldn’t he have just kept begging for us to help him instead of saying the rational thing? The *Casey* thing? It had seemed like such an obvious trap until that point, but now, none of us were certain. Had we really just been talking with a flicker of our friend's soul?

Even if it was him, there was no way we could trust the words the witches had been feeding us. These were the same women who manipulated dozens of children to their graves in pursuit of their own goals; what honor would they have in such a deal? Clearly this whole thing was just an attempt to fix a snag in their process. If they got what they were looking for, they would almost certainly leave Casey to continue rotting, and at best, spare the rest of my friends.

But even swallowing that truth still left behind a bitter taste. Like it or not, there had been *something* in that corpse. The witches had been able to imbue life, and if it really was Casey, then my promise to bring his body back home was now for something far greater than just a corpse.

I thought about the threads that his body was tangling into; the way that it was tied to something deep in the guts of the door the same way my thread was. They were using him in some way. Perhaps biding more time to carry out their twisted plan. If they hadn’t completely ripped free his thread, did that mean we could somehow save him?

Only I knew the true crossroads of this junction. None of my friends knew about the strings to the extent that I did, or the chip that we were bargaining with. All they knew from what we’d just witnessed was that the corpse of our brother had been desecrated and flaunted before us as a threat. It was only fair that they would no longer buy my lies. Not after what Casey had just told them.

Kait wiped her tears and turned to me. The look on her face told me she was doing her best to stay calm, but restlessness was boiling just beneath the surface, “Jessie, what did he just mean?”

I tried to buy myself another moment, even just a few seconds so I could find some way to pivot my ultimate plan, “Kaitlynn, whatever just happened, we can’t trust it—”

“*Jessie*,” she pleaded, a tired ache in her voice so strong I thought it would give out altogether, “You know… I *know* you know.”

One by one, the rest of the group lifted their gazes until I was the center of the room.

“Please. Just tell me,” Kait begged. “I can handle it. *Please,* Jessie\*.\* This has to end…”

I knew what she wanted me to say. It had already been made clear what she was expecting the answer to be. There was almost a relief living in her eyes as my mouth hung open, contemplating a response, that the words to follow were going to be what she wanted to hear. That *she* was the bargaining chip. That *she* was the lid to close this Pandora's box. That she could redeem herself for the death of Mindy and Casey, and that it would free us all from this curse.

But Kait wasn’t the one who needed redemption, I was. And with how vehemently she’d fought against the idea so far, telling her that would only break her spirit the rest of the way.

It was her desperation to be the martyr that finally sparked a new plan. The only way I could still get myself isolated and seal the door. A wicked, evil deception that no one who called themselves a ‘friend’ would ever dare to think. I was past the point of no return now in my determination, though, and I was willing to do anything to keep them all safe. Dad had been right. I was poison and filth, and for once, embracing it might save lives instead of ruin them.

“The ritual needs a sacrifice,” I began slowly, shying my eyes from Kait in shame. The guilt racked me so much that they began to well with tears, but it only aided my performance, “They had their last sacrifice picked out, and they started the process, but they weren’t able to grab the child in time. They tried to fill it with someone different, but it didn’t work, and that’s why the door is the way it is now.”

Carly shook her head in disbelief, “Jessie, why didn’t you tell us all of this before?”

Bryce gave me a similar look. Lacey stared at me with something akin to disdain. It cut like a knife, but I focused back on Kait to ground myself. Her stare was the only soft one; the kind of someone who thought they already knew the answer.

I fed her the words she wanted to hear, “Because in order to fix this, they need to finish the original ritual. They need the child who got away.” I swallowed the lump in my throat, then forced the lie out, “It’s you, Kait. You were right.”

The breath that exhaled from the girl was halfway between relief and despair. She wiped her face, then kept her hands to her mouth, breathing into them slowly to keep herself composed. Turning away from me, she paced to the wall, deep in thought. While she did that, I positioned myself firmly between her and the door, just in case she got any hasty ideas.

Instantly, the judgmental looks I was getting from the others melted away with realization. Bryce spelled it out bluntly, “So what they’re asking for… it’s a life for a life.”

Kait turned, her face already returned to a wet mess, “I’ll do it.”

“No,” Lacey barked instantly, sobs once again tightening her throat, “Kaitlynn, *no.*”

“Lacey, there’s no other way,” Kait argued, “Think of everything that’s happened to us so far—all the people who are dead because they only wanted me! If that’s what these stupid freaks want so bad, then just let them take me!”

“We aren’t trading lives, Kait,” Carly told her, stepping close and taking her hand, “It’s not going to help anything.”

“Yes it will—it will *end this.*”

“You think we can believe them?” I asked her, “Kaitlynn, look at how badly they’ve fucked everything up. You really think that they know how to fix this even if they get you? That they’ll patch Casey back up and send him out, good as new?”

I’d never felt so rotten, lying so effortlessly to my friends, but so far, it was working exactly as I’d hoped. It didn’t matter which one of us the witches wanted, I knew none of the others would allow a sacrifice anyway. Letting Kait think she was the martyr wouldn’t put her into any true danger, and it also would take the eyes away from me, making it so that when the time came to seal the door, no one would notice that I was missing.

“So what do we do then?” Kait tossed her hands, “We know it’s me they want—you think I’m just going to sit here and let everyone else suffer and die for my sake?”

“Kait, this isn’t your fault. You had no choice in what happened to you,” Carly told her.

“It doesn’t matter whose fault it is; people are still dying over my life.”

At the time, I didn’t see the hypocrisy in the situation, or at least I chose not to so the pain would go down easier. Kait’s reasoning seemed so nonsensical to me, but inside I was too blinded by the same thoughts myself to be rational and come clean.

“Well, we aren’t giving you up, Kait; it’s not an option,” Bryce said, “Any one of us will drag you away from that door if you try it.”

“We need a new plan,” Lacey agreed through her sniffles, “I want my brother back, Kait, but I would never give any of you up. Besides, Jessie is right. We can’t trust this. Not when they did *that* to his body.”

I took the chance to slip into the reins, “I know you all have your reservations, but I’m telling you, we’re out of options. We need to go through with what we were talking about before they sent him to throw us off.”

Kait whirled toward me, “Jessie—”

I stepped forward and took the side of her arms, “Kait, if we get more help, this can all be over. With a threat this big, the authorities will send so much firepower that nobody else will die—not if we prepare them first. The things from the door won’t stand a chance, and the witches can sit and wallow in their filth for the rest of eternity.”

Kaitlynn shook her head, “So we just pass the problem onto everyone else? Make them suffer with this? Just to keep me alive?”

“Yes,” I said firmly. “There might even be a chance that they figure out how to close this thing. But we’re just kids, Kait. You said it yourself at the beginning of all this: we aren’t equipped to handle this. Even if you give yourself over, there’s no guarantee that it marks the end of this for the rest of us.”

Kait’s eyes buckled to the floor, and I took in the rest of my friends to see what they were thinking. It appeared all of their reservations about my former plan had vanished beneath the weight of the alternative, and only solemn nods of agreement were returned.

“What do we do, then?” Kait finally asked, defeatedly.

It took everything I had to conceal a sigh of relief, “Do we still have the last monster’s body?”

“Yeah,” Bryce nodded, “There was too much going on; we never got the chance to burn it.”

“Good. Then we throw it in the back of my truck and take it to the police as proof. With how long the drive down the mountain is, three hours isn’t a lot of time to get all this done, and I don’t want to know what happens once that timer runs out. I think we need to split into groups.”

“Split up?” Bryce scoffed, “Why would we do that?”

“Because we aren’t all going to the police,” I told him, “you and Carly head for the city and wait outside the local news station. I’ll message you once the police are heading to the manor, then go inside and tell them whatever you have to for them to send reporters out to the mile marker. You all may be right about higher up’s trying to bury this, so let's make it loud enough that everyone hears.”

“We’re going to get them caught up in this too?” Carly questioned.

“I’m only going to show the police how to get through to the manor,” I told him, “But you saw Mrs. Thatcher’s house after she was taken. If we sell this as a big enough threat, there’ll be enough cop cars and emergency vehicles to get a show from the road.”

“What about *us* then?” Kait questioned, still not sold on this plan. “What are the rest of us going to do?”

I only could stand to face her for a few seconds before I had to turn away, “Lacey, you keep the gun and stick with Kait. The door wants her, so drive as far away from here as you can and don’t stop. I doubt these women are going to give up their prize so easily. We’ll get in touch with you all as soon as it's safe to come back.”

Lacey scraped the gun off the floor in a tired stupor and nodded.

“Wait, what? No—I’m not just going to sit this out,” Kait argued, “If we’re already surrendering, I at least want to help.”

“Kait, you’re the most vital person here,” I reminded her, “You ‘helping’ is making sure that the witches don’t get you.”

My point seemed to stick with her, but I could tell that my confidence was making her suspicious of this plan. Never was I usually so certain of anything. “So that just leaves you.” She noted, “Why are you the only one alone?”

I made sure not to let my poker face slip, and did my best to bluff, “Because I have the truck to shuttle the body. I won’t be alone once I get to the police station.”

“*And* lead them back up here,” she guessed, “So you’re the only one of us that’s going back behind the lines of fire once we leave?”

“I’ll just show them where the path to the house is. I won’t go with them.”

“I don’t believe you.”

It was hard for me to be snippy given that this was the last time I was ever going to talk to her, but selling this story was literally life or death, “Look, believe me or not, Kait, we’re burning precious minutes sitting here arguing. If it will make you happy, I can trade with Carly or Bryce and they can drive my truck.”

My casual bluff to not be the loner seemed to finally sell her on my lies, and she sheepishly backed down, “Whatever. It’s fine.”

I looked around the space to make sure everyone else felt the same, and when there was no disagreements, I moved to the red door. Peering into its maw that I’d soon be swallowed by, I pushed it shut.

“We should get moving then.”

It took the collective of us to heave the body of the last beast into the bed of my truck. When it was heaped in there, I pulled the tarp from under my seat to cover it, familiar visions of a rainy night with soggy boxes flashing across my eyelids with every blink. Casey wasn’t in the memory this time, however. The newest picture of his rotting corpse was the only thing that remained burned into my mind.

I’m sure that was the case for everyone. Barely anyone spoke as we worked, and each of us kept a tentative eye on the front door of the manor as we did so. For the first time in weeks, there wasn’t a single person in the house watching the door. It had become a part of us over our time here—something etched into the fabric of our very threads—and neglecting it felt like going against a bodily instinct. Especially knowing now what we were leaving behind down there…

When we were done, we all gathered in the center of our parked cars, Mindy’s old grime covered Honda still among them. We allowed ourselves a few seconds to breathe, then Carly spoke.

“Okay… we’re really doing this then?”

Lacey nodded, “I’m with Jessie. This seems like our best option.”

The guilt began gnawing at me again, but I swallowed it down. A cold, calm wind swept up the cliff side from over the Appalachian hills, cutting straight through me and leaving me hollow. For weeks I’d been wondering what our last day on Earth would be. How abrupt my death would come. What my last few moments would look like. Funnily enough, I missed the uncertainty. Living recklessly and accepting that death was a possibility somehow weighed lighter on me than knowing the end of my life was a mere three hours away.

It had to be that way, though. Kait was right, even though her words were spoken under the pressure of my lie. Even with the most powerful forces guarding the door, there would be mistakes. There would be new rules and forces of the door that would ruin the innocent lives we dragged out to the manor, ultimately resulting in them being torn from existence. For anyone who didn’t die, they would be bound like we were. Unable to destroy the thing. Unable to burn it down. Forever hearing the godforsaken chimes of the clock that slept within the heart of the halls.

I had to seal it. I had to spare anyone else from even another second of torment. Maybe that’s what I was telling myself, or maybe after seeing what had been done to Casey—what *should* have been done to \*me—\*that’s what I just wanted to believe. That I needed to die.

There was still a lot to do over the remaining hours; prep to make sure that after I was gone, the door wouldn’t be opened again. For the moment, I set it to the back burner, though. Instead, I allowed myself to think only of the things in my life that had ever made it worthwhile. The four people standing before me.

“Well, I guess we’ll see you all on the other side,” Bryce said. The sigh that followed held more age than his body.

Lacey nodded, “Everyone be safe, and keep us updated. Once this is over, I want to see everyone still in one piece.”

There were nods of agreement, and Bryce and Carly gave each of us one last look over before turning and climbing into Bryce’s car. I wanted desperately to step forward and take them both into my arms one last time before they went. To tell them how much I loved them, and how they’d kept me alive through the hardest times. Doing so would blow my cover, however, so I just choked back my tears and stood quietly by the door of my truck.

Kaitlynn made this harder to do with her and Lacey’s goodbye. The latter packed the shotgun into the back seat while the former approached me, looking up through her messy bangs. There was still suspicion in her eyes the way there always was with me—an attempt to see through the mask that never slipped—but mostly there was grief.

“I’ll see you soon, yeah?” She asked me.

I nodded. It felt like trying to lift the sky to pull my lips into a smile, “See you when we’re safe again.”

“Right,” She nodded with a halfhearted chuckle. She wiped her face, then fixed her hair before turning back to me, “Once we are, do you think we could talk? Just the two of us?”

Her words shot through me like a bullet ripping my skin. My eyes began to blur, and my limbs began to shiver, but I breathed slowly and forced both to stay calm.

“What about?” I couldn’t stop myself from asking. It was something I knew I’d regret.

She reached up and took my cheek, her words falling out gentle as snow, “That night at the park…”

A shiver shot through me, and I couldn’t stop the water in my eyes from pooling. Before she could see it fall, I quickly stepped forward and pulled her into my arms, burying my face into her hair. Despite the grime and filth that caked us, it still smelled sweet as ever.

“I’d like that. I’ve had a lot of regrets on that front,” I let her know. A hollow gesture. A single sentence that I hoped conveyed the litany of words I wanted to tell her in the conversation we’d never have.

She pulled away and analyzed me again, but my mask had been re-affixed. She smiled dimly and said, “I’ll see you then.”

Then she turned and climbed into the car with Lacey.

I did the same with my truck, and only once the others were pulling out the driveway with me in tow, and I was certain they could no longer see my face, that was when I finally let the tears fall.
