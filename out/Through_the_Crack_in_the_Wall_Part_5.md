In my last entry, I mentioned returning to Paradise on Saturday. I don't even know why. All fear of the place had disappeared with my visit just the day prior, even with the events that unfolded. I never did hear from Randy again, but he didn’t occupy a single thought when I drove up that dirt trail. 

It was like I was in a trance. The only thing I can point to was an insatiable curiosity. What *would* my poison of choice produce? Every time we went, the place presented itself differently. Not unlike a different trip produced by different drugs. It always seemed like it was trying its best to mirror the effect of what we were taking, just amplifying it and getting a lot wrong in the process. But that “amplification” was the desired outcome, and everything begged me to find what it would do with my stash of heroin.

I went at night like always. The cool breeze from outside rustled nearby leaves and chilled me as I sat looking up the massive stairway. My backpack, though not filled with a whole lot, felt heavy like a burden on my shoulders. I questioned why I was even here, how I even got here, but something unseen tugged at me so I made my ascent. 

I went up the stairs, took a right, then another right, and followed the corridor down to the corner. A path that had become more of a bad habit than anything.

Facing the wall, I stared for an eternity only occupying minutes. Dope makes you lethargic and relaxed, making it hard to move as the dopamine hits you, so I took out the dusty bottle of old whiskey from my bag. My plan was to get drunk off the whiskey, enter Paradise, then check the timer. After that, everything else would be pretty experimental. If I had ample time, I would set the timer before taking as small of a dose as necesaary. Then I would see if anything would change, if not then I’d come back Sunday with the black tar as my key. If it did work though, well, I would experience its effects. 

It didn’t take long for me to get drunk. Every gulp was terribly bitter, I don’t even know when I bought the bottle before I left it to rot in my kitchen cabinet. It was somehow better than the vodka me and Randy drank though. 

When I slipped through the gate, I was greeted with a familiar pulsing pleasure and the sight of brilliant murals. Old-timey music followed me on my way to the timer. I reached the Gatekeeper’s intersection and read “Limits of Paradise”. Next to it, a looping gif of a winking smiley face and below a timer that read: 01:12:52(more than enough time)

I decided not to do the process too far from the gate. In case anything happened, I wanted to be as close to an escape as possible. I understood that I was already playing a very dangerous game cross-fading it with alcohol, but it didn’t really seem to matter to me. Hell, I wasn’t even sure the stuff would work in Paradise. I admired the characters as they celebrated in their settings and gripped the strap of my backpack tightly on my way back. When I got there, I sat down and began to unload my supplies.

The process didn’t take long. I carefully measured the sticky substance to offer me the longest high I was comfortable with before putting it in a newly cleaned spoon. I heated it up with a lighter, periodically absorbing it into my only syringe(they’re hard to come by) while trading the dissolved substance for new clumps when I’ve absorbed everything the spoon could hold. By the time I was finished, the metallic spoon had singed my fingertips. I didn’t even notice, as the place between dimensions I ventured into didn’t allow for pain. A pulled out a water bottle, and pulled just a little into the syringe to help further dissolve the fluid in it. Then, I tied off my arm and put a cotton swab where I planned to inject it. 

I made sure my phone had the timer for 40 minutes set before plunging the needle into my arm. When the fluid found its way inside me, everything got real warm. 

To my surprise, my experiment worked! 

The former paintings disappeared and were replaced with clouds from a child’s picture book that slowly traversed along their flat surfaces. Arrows sailed through them and glided across the canvas with superb speeds and crudely drawn birds sailed alongside them. The moon had turned into a radiant, beautiful sun and the sky had become a mix of relaxing shades of blue and jaw dropping purples. A blissful melody coming from a harpsichord echoed across the realm.

The rush was beyond immeasurable. 

Absolute euphoria. Nothing, in both heaven and Earth, could ever compare to that sensation. It hit me so hard that I didn’t even realize that I was quite literally floating out of my body. When I looked down to see my body, I saw nothing but warmth in the smile that lit up my face. I flew past the rotted ceiling, and soared into the endless skies. I dipped down towards the shimmering ground near the factory, a lake that mirrored the radiance of the sky above. The sun’s glow bathed me with its brilliant warmth, and I felt utterly free for the first time in my life.

I spent half an hour in the skies of Paradise, before the drug wore off and I fell softly back into my body. I looked at the timer I set, and saw nearly 8 minutes were left before it would go off. I had even more time than that according to the graffiti numbers by the Gatekeeper’s cage, but I was satisfied. Perfectly satisfied. I gathered my supplies, turning to see the painted clouds and birds on the, and left, returning Sunday to repeat the process.

  


That Monday was probably the first time I’ve shown up to work on time. My manager almost couldn’t believe it. “Way to go champ, that’s the initiative I’ve been looking for!” His praise fell on deaf ears, I was too busy thinking about when I should go up to the factory next. The only thing that snapped me out of my trance, was seeing that Jane hadn’t showed up yet. 

She was late, she arrived at work maybe an hour later. I tried asking her what was wrong after the manager chewed her out. It was definitely a first for me to hear him ever raise his voice at her, she usually had a complete opposite work ethic from me. She completely ignored me though. It didn’t take long for me to piece it together, the blank look in her eyes told me she was already in its web. We continued our shifts that day in silence, but I’d catch her constantly popping aspirin in between her duties. 

It wasn’t until Tuesday when she finally spoke to me. It was after another silent shift at the burger joint when I got a text on my phone. Usually I was the one who’d message her first, almost always about having her cover shifts for me when I was in a slump.

“Hey.” It read.

“Hey, whats up? U doing okay?” I texted back.

“Yeah. I’ve just been thinking. You should take me back!”

“Back to the factory?”

“Yes. You really didn’t let me get enjoy it haha”

“Have you been getting headaches?”

Several long minutes of no reply. It didn’t entirely make sense to me, she barely spent a few moments in Paradise so how was she already going through withdrawal? I must’ve been attributing it too closely to a drug for whatever reason, and I began mulling over my decision to ask her to join us that night. 

In retrospect, I know how stupid I was over those couple of weeks. How casually I treated everything that was happening, despite the fantastical elements of it all. It’s so easy to see it now, especially with how I let it completely ruin my life. But I cannot stress enough how warped it all was in my head when I was under its facade.

“Yes. Terrible ones. That’s not it though, I just need to get out of the house. I was hoping we could go there soon to have some fun? Idk. I’m calling off this week so lmk!” She finally replied.

I wish I could say that I wanted to reject her offer, but the concept of enjoying that heaven with someone else, especially Jane, was far too enticing.

“Sure. Wednesday work fot u? I was already planning on going.”

“Yes. Whatever works. Just pick me up when you’re ready.”

She sent me her address, and I reveled in the idea of sharing a trip through Paradise with her.

Wednesday night was bitter and cold. I put way more effort into my raggedy outfit than was necessary. Somehow I thought it would impress her, which is why I was sad that I had to cover up my Metallica shirt with a thick jacket. She lived remarkably close to the factory, essentially off the dirt road herself. When she got in my car, I was surprised with how pale she looked.

She didn’t even say anything to me when she got in, just buckled her seatbelt and stared ahead. 

“Everything alright?” I tried asking.

Her response timidly came through her lips. “Rough week I guess.” She would dart her eyes from her house to the windshield. 

The drive was short, but the awkwardness made it feel horribly long. Every time I tried to ask a question she’d shoot it down.

“Are your headaches getting any better?”

“No.”

…

“How is your mom doing? Any progress?”

“I don’t really wanna talk about my family right now.”

…

“Did you bring anything to get in?”

“I thought you had that covered.”

Silence followed for pretty much the rest of the drive until at last, I found a question she resonated with. 

“Have you been getting hallucinations, you know, like hearing things?”

Silence then, “Yes.” She said it so quietly I had to strain to hear it over the engine in my beater.

“Who was it?” 

For the first time in that entire car ride, she looked at me when she spoke. “My dad. He was talking to me through the closet, telling me I needed to go back.” She put her hand to her head and massaged her scalp with her fingers.

“It’s like it’s been invading my thoughts. It’s all I ever dream about, I was planning on going anyway so I don’t understand…” a tear slipped off of her cheek.

“How long has he been dead?”

“Since I was a kid. I forgot what he sounded like until I heard him through the door. God, I’m still wearing the same clothes I was yesterday.”

“I’m sorry. Don’t worry, this will fix it. He won’t be there after we go back.”

The building’s tattered shell glowed in the moonlight when we pulled in towards it. I grabbed my backpack and hoisted it over my shoulders when I got out of my car. Jane let out a sigh of relief and shot me a slight smile when she got out. I led her through the path most familiar to me. Up the stairs, take a right, another right down the hall, and finally to the corner. I pulled out the bottle of whiskey. The dust that had previously accumulated on it had almost disappeared in between my uses. It was about a third full. 

We ended up downing the rest by the time I felt a buzz coming through. “You see it?” I asked. “Yeah, I see it.” She replied. Before us sat the gate. A crooked hallway with a beam of white moonlight shining on the furthermost wall. On it, a large fissure ran from the ceiling to the floor, separating the graffiti letters that said: “Gate to Paradise”. They were multicolored and almost glowed in the effort of their invitation. 

I gently grabbed her by the hand, and led her with me through the crack. Where our forms twisted flat and bent to fit in. The sights and sensations were familiar to me. The sound of music ushered our arrival and the orange moon beamed down on us. Jane couldn’t help but to smile, and it warmed me seeing her in relief. 

She walked over to the wall, admiring the busy artwork literally animated with life. “It’s so beautiful.” she said. Theatres full of people, restaurants with greasers sitting around at their tables, and city roads filled with pedestrians. “Hold it here, I just gotta go check something.”

I ran over to the timer. “1:31:12”. I set a 1 hour 20 minute timer on my phone. I ran back to see her laughing at a rendition of Elvis singing through speech bubbles to a stage of people. She smiled when she saw me.

“Even without the high, this place is jaw dropping. Did I ever tell you I wanted to be an artist when I was a kid?” She said as I pulled up to her.

“I think I remember you saying something about it at work. Follow me, I got something I wanna show you.” I threw my backpack down and waved her to follow me down the stairs. The massive murals of the pub interior was just like how it was when me and Randy first came down here drunk. His sobbing wails were pushed to the furthest recesses of my mind.

I showed her how to manipulate the paintings. She giggled when I messed up someone’s perfectly lined shot. We took turns manipulating the art in different ways before sitting down and talking for most of our time in Paradise. It was mostly dumb things, jokes about our manager, family stories, and funny things we’ve seen on the internet. 

“Gosh, I don’t know how things could get much better than this.” She said, almost at random.

The idea immediately struck my mind.

“I know how to make it better.” I said with a smirk. I wish I knew how much I’d regret those words.

“What do you mean?” She inquired.

I pulled out my phone and looked at the timer. 45:13, plenty of time. “Wait here!” I exclaimed excitedly, then I ran up the stairs to grab my bag. I brought it to her and rested it on the floor. I wasted no time in pulling out my “works” and the ziploc bag full of my stash. 

“That isn’t what I think it is, right?” her face was painted with a mix of concern and apprehension. 

“No it’s okay, I know what I’m doing.” 

She paused for a moment, her face initially showed that she wasn’t okay with it. However, it slowly rested into a blank stare and she asked out of curiosity, “What’s it like?” 

“Like being sent to heaven. Have you ever wondered what it's like to fly?”

Her face remained blank, and her voice was oddly flat when she followed up. “Let’s do it.” 

It didn’t take me long to get it all situated. Heating of the tar was what took the longest and it was only several minutes. I measured doses for the both of us that I knew would give me a high that lasted around 20 minutes, maybe a little less. I asked her again if she was ready for it, she nodded and I injected her first after teaching her how to tourniquet her arm. I looked at the timer before doing the same to myself. We still had 32 minutes. 

Flying with her unfortunately marks the happiest moment of my life. We glided through the factory together, racing arrows before ascending past the roof and taking to the skies. In my entire time working with her I’ve never seen her so happy. She would blaze past me before taunting that I was too slow, before I would soar past her returning the favor. 

“This is incredible!” She shouted at me. She started twirling through the air before soaring over to join me.

“Every substance is a new key. And every key brings a new experience. This one is my personal favorite!” I exclaimed gleefully. 

She laughed, “I can see why. I wish I knew about this place so much sooner. It really is a paradise.” 

Here, in this moment, we were freedom incarnate. The only emotions afforded to us were ones of joy and sensations of euphoria were taken to new heights. I thought about spilling my guts on my feelings towards her, but I couldn’t bring myself to sully the excitement. Instead, I just let myself be truly happy. I let go of all my burdens. I traded my sore memories of my brother with ones of flying through a warm sky. I unshackled myself from the chronic pains and gave myself over to pure bliss. I let myself wash away my cowardice with Randy and his brother’s demise in the lake that sat beneath the building, all while we pointed at our reflections as we flew just a few short feet above it.

Eventually, I sobered up and gently reentered my warm body. The room around us still had paintings of clouds and birds. I sighed with great relief and turned to see Jane still smiling with glassy eyes. I checked the timer on my phone. We still had 11 minutes. I sat patiently for her return. 

Then 5 minutes passed by, and she still didn’t move. 

Then another 2, nothing.

When another 2 minutes passed by I started to panic. I grabbed her by the shoulders and shook her. “Okay Jane, we gotta get going now!” She didn’t respond, instead she kept smiling. 

Shit.

Why wasn’t she coming to? I measured that dose carefully, I knew what I was doing. I shook her again, this time more violently. Again, she just kept smiling with no response. I put my hand over her mouth, to my short-lived relief she was still breathing. But why wasn’t she sobering up? I didn’t fuck up the dose, I know how strong my own stash is and I’ve taken this exact amount plenty of times. 

*Buzz. Buzz.* 

My phone was alerting me that the timer I set had counted down to zero. A pit was beginning to form in my stomach. “What the hell is wrong with you? We gotta go NOW!” Her smile twitched for a moment but settled back into its warmth. “Come on Jane, you need to snap out of it!”  Again no response. I sat her against the wall and paced around the room. Thoughts raced through my head, the facade of joy had already completely vanished and I was trying to wrap my head around it. 

Do I carry her up? I’m not exactly a strong guy, but maybe I could do it on my own.

Did I really mess up the dose? No, I came to around the same time I expected to.

“Shit. Shit. Shit. Shit. Shit. SHIT!” I screamed.

Finally it hit me what was wrong, and I realized I should’ve just tried to carry her up the stairs. The dose was tailored to last us around 20 minutes, but that assumption was based on my experiences with the drug. I somehow let myself not even take account of that fact this was her first time, and therefore, hadn’t built up the tolerance that I have accumulated over years of abuse. Not to mention her smaller frame, just how irresponsible could I be? It was a complete and foolish mistake. 

I tried dragging her and made it to the stairs, but I was too late. The color drained from the world and fear took hold of my heart while I sat trapped at the bottom with her.
