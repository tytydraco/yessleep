The twisting and tearing through my stomach ravaged all day, but still I forced myself to note down the effects to the best of my ability. Months ago, I came across an ad in a crumpled newspaper promising compensation for chemical exposure incurred by the plants I had worked at my entire working life. Never in my dreams would I ever have planned on turning my back on the industry that had taken me in straight out of high school and given me pay a college graduate would have given everything for, but after blood continuously became ingrained with my morning coughs and I withered until I could no longer work without compensation, I felt I could only put myself first.

The first meeting with the entity was strange. What I had once read to be some kind of legal operation I discovered was actually a type of medical start-up. As I listened to the spiel, I was bombarded with medical jargon that a guy like me could not understand in the slightest. The very best that I could pull from the presentation, which must have run forty-five minutes, was the fact that it would rely on the use of an experimental medication. 

“It is completely safe,” they said. “The process has been run on many test subjects, and now it is time to watch how it can be used to help the people.” At first, I hated the idea of subjecting my body to even more stress after all I had been through, but after the promise of free checkups multiple times a week, I was drawn in.

Every morning since that day I woke and took the pill which they provided me, a single glass cylinder that barely covered my worn-down pinky nail. Despite its unintimidating look, the thing was bitter and required a full glass of water and a few moments of gagging before it would go down. However, walking down the streets of my town for the first time after, I felt as if I were a person again. For so long I had been looked upon with pity at best and a disturbed, malevolent disgust at worst. Becoming sicker as I did, I ran out of options quickly, and without a stable family to rely on, I am ashamed to admit I fell to living on the streets when the last of my funds ran dry. In just a few short weeks, showers during checkups and seeming positive effects from the pills lifted everything about my life, getting me thinking about employment once again.

Things moved so fast from this point that my beliefs, which had always been vague at best, began to turn incredibly spiritual. I was hired quickly at my first interview and assigned to a construction site where I worked harder and faster than I had when I was ten years younger. After just a few weeks, I found an apartment in the city that was dingy and cramped but meant the world to me still.

Things began to go wrong three months after I began taking the pills. It started with cold sweats and cramps, causing me to miss work and call the providers of the medication as instructed. Picking up immediately, I was asked if I had missed any doses after describing the symptoms. I told them I had missed a single day the morning before, to which I was met with a grim digital silence on the other side.

“Mr. Bailey, we are more than grateful for your participation in our experiment, and we wish you the best of luck going forward.” When the connection shorted out, my hand shook so terribly that the phone fell out of it and crashed to the floor. A terrible and sickening burning tore through my flesh and I hallucinated a terrible dripping from my fingers down to the floor. Through my clouded vision, I could see only bone shaking limply out of my wrist, and I screamed a terrible scream until my lungs ran out of air, making me pass out.

When I awoke in another room, I stared at the ceiling for a moment, gaining my bearings before rushing my hand in front of my face to see that it was just as intact as it had been before my panic attack. Sweat drenched my entire body, and dryness consumed my mouth as I trudged to my feet to get a glass of water. Lightness covering my body, I walked to the sink and filled a cup to the brim, guzzling it down. As I did, Something across the room caught my eye and caused the glass in my hand to slip, making it shatter on the floor.

Just a few yards away, resting still, was a small pile of bloody flesh which looked to be the perfect amount to cover a hand. Terror struck through my body, and a tingly feeling, like wearing a glove, covered my hand.

I have no idea what is happening to my body, and no matter how many times I call, my contact who provided the medication will not respond. I am completely at a loss as to what to do next. Should I continue taking the medication that has done this to me? What will happen if I stop taking it? Questions fill my head constantly, but answers are nowhere to be found. I will follow up with updates as soon as I can and hopefully with some answers.
