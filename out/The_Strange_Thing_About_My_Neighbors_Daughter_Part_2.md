I was extremely on edge for the next few days. Every time I leave the house, I feel eyes on me. I don't dare walk by the Hartleys' property anymore. *She* might be waiting there. I told both my parents to keep an eye out after the other night, for what or who I still wasn't 100% certain, but they understood my concern and went along with it. When I mentioned how I ran across Mora the other day and that it was my hunch she was the one creeping around the house, they didn't seem all that shocked.

"Yeah," my Mom said, "she has a bit of a tendency to run at large sometimes. She's always been an odd one but, in the grand scheme of things, I think she's harmless. Just let us know if you catch her out there again and we'll talk to Glen or Annette about it."

I thought Maybe she was right. Maybe Mora was just a weird girl who drifted a little too far on the edgy side and got a kick out of getting a reaction from people. Maybe I was just working myself up over nothing. Certainly wouldn't be the first time.

Either way, I didn't want much to do with her. Harmless or not, she still creeped me the fuck out.

Later on in the week I went into a little 50s diner called Fatso's. I had only been in a few times in my years of living in Clearwater. It's for sure dated and hasn't had a lot of upkeep over the past few decades but the "Help Wanted" sign on the door lured me in. My other options for work at this point were pretty well depleted.

The place was practically vacant aside from the middle-aged woman at the counter and a lone old man eating in a booth at the far end of the restaurant.

"Welcome in," the woman said in a practiced, worn out tone. "Just a heads up, we don't have any hashbrowns for today."

"I was actually stopping in to ask about your 'help wanted' sign." I responded.

She perked up instantly, "Oh! Well great! Lemme getcha' an application then." She knelt down below the register and dug around for a moment. She resurfaced above the counter, letting a yellow sheet of paper glide for a brief moment across the polished granite surface. I picked up a pen from the basket on the counter and started filling it out.

"We've had that sign up for months and hadn't had a single shred of interest from anyone." she proclaimed.

"Well, I've been looking for a while. I need the work." I told her, my eyes focused on the application.

"Sounds like a match made in heaven then!"

When my mission was complete, I stepped out of the diner and into the blazing sun. I started walking to my car... I only got about three steps before I paused.

There was a long, stringy-haired homeless junky leaning up against the hood of my car. At least he looked like a homeless junky: he was covered in grime and appeared to weigh no more than eighty pounds. He was staring dead at me. After living in LA for a few years, I knew very well how to handle vagrants. The best thing you can do is give them a wide fucking berth and don't come off as assertive. I'd never had one plant themselves right on my car, however. I looked at him only for a few more moments before I managed to let out a very meek "Excuse me..."

He didn't budge, just continued to stand and stare. I tried again, "You're uh... you're on my car. Can you move please?"

With that he began to grin. Nearly every rotten, brown tooth in his mouth was glistening in the harsh sunlight. "Pleeeease?" He mocked. He then began to snicker at me, spraying saliva in the process.

I didn't know what else to do. I simply just turned and ducked back into the diner, keeping a cautious eye on him the whole time.

I approached the counter once again. The woman looked up with her brow furrowed in concern.

"Excuse me," I began, "there's a homeless guy leaning up on my car and refusing to move. Could you possibly phone the cops for me?"

She craned her neck around to get a look out the windows. She let a very casual frown drop.

"Aw, damn him." She muttered. Without hardly another thought, she barged out from behind the counter and right out the door to confront him.

I watched through the glass, the muffled sounds of her belligerent tone demanding the man to move on. His face was still twisted with the same vile leer he was giving to me only moments before. He didn't seem to be even looking at the angry waitress accosting him, but right past her. Right at me.

I felt my stomach sink, like it was packed with cement. Behind his mocking, rotten grin, I could sense, was an animalistic urge to slaughter me with his own bare hands. Why, I wasn't sure. I had never seen this person before in my entire life. What did he have against me.

Finally, he turned to look at the waitress. His smile dimmed just slightly. I could make out the words she was saying clearly this time: "Go on! Move it!"

With mild resignation he eased himself off the hood of my car and lumbered on down the street with his head hung low, looking like a discouraged coyote. He cast one last look at me, malevolent grin returning in full force before ambling out of sight.

The waitress came back in frowning and shaking her head. "Sorry 'bout that, hun. Didn't even realize he was out there." She said. "He usually just sifts through the trash cans and moves along. Been a while since he's been a serious problem but I still don't like to take any chances with him."

"A serious problem?" I asked.

"He got violent with some of our customers in the past. He'd accuse people of all sorts of wild nonsense, like about them being the antichrist or demons or... whatever. He almost strangled the life out of one of them."

"And what? The cops ever did anything about him?"

She sighed, "Well, they had him locked up in the pin for a while after that. They couldn't keep him for some reason or another but he must've learned his lesson because he never did it again. Just kinda... stalks around now."

I went home, the weird encounter fading into nothing more than a mildly uncomfortable memory with each passing mile.

As I pulled into the driveway I noticed my Dad pacing around in the front yard wielding a shotgun and staring up into the trees. I squinted in confusion. It wasn't too unordinary to see him out with a shotgun; we live on a farm and there are plenty of predators lurking around from time to time. I assumed he was looking for a hungry hawk that had decided to peruse our live chicken selection.

He noticed me step out of the car and gave me a wave. I waved back and hollered, "What's going on?"

"Something made a mess out of two of our hens."

"Hawk?"

He shrugged, "It'd have to be a pretty damn big one. My money's on a bobcat."

I joined him in glancing up into the dense thicket of branches.

"Did you see anything?" I asked.

He shook his head, "Only what little was left of them. Ain't much." He let the gun go slack in his arms, determining the threat had likely long since vanished.

I went and took a look at the remnants, myself. It managed to be even worse than what I was picturing: there were two massive splotches of crimson soaking into the earth, still wet, and white feathers strewn around every which way indicating a fierce struggle. This didn't add up to me. If this were truly the work of a predator there wouldn't be nearly this much blood pooled up in two distinct spots. Sure, there would be some but when something like a bobcat, coyote, or especially a hawk gets ahold of a chicken it's quick to make a getaway with its lunch. Whatever did this waited and practically bled them both out before leaving. Why?

After dinner that night, I decided not to join my parents outside. Instead I went straight to my room, shut the blinds, and put on a movie. Being out there just spooked me now. Whenever I looked out into the darkness I could sense eyes on me. Prowling. Waiting. Watching my every move.  I got a hint that my parents both thought I was being a bit paranoid but I didn't care. Maybe it was somewhat true that my survival instincts, or lizard brain, or whatever you'd want to call it had been altered a bit from living in LA for close to seven years. But I sure as hell would've hated to find out if the tingling hairs on the back of my neck were right.

It would have been nice to let the cool night air flow in though my window but there was no way in hell I could sleep with it open. I simply chose to let the warm, stuffy oxygen cogitate in my room. The sweaty, sticky discomfort paired with creeping unease made it difficult for me to fall asleep for close to three hours.

Then, right at about 4 a.m., a hard "CLANG" outside my window jolted me awake. I nearly met the floor from how fast I shot up. I froze in an upright position. My chest heaved. As tempted as I was to peek out my window, I refused out of fear for what I might see... or for whatever might see *me*. I sat and listened, dead still, expecting to hear that familiar chittering laugh from before. But there was nothing.

I then looked around my blackened room; shadows covered every corner. If it were possible for anything to make it's way in it would have had no trouble hiding itself. I tried to let my eyes adjust but it was useless. In the dark every object could be suspect. My mind raced. I tried to reason with myself and affirm there was no possible way for anything or anyone to break into the house without it being known, but that too was feudal.

Goddamn, how I wished my parents were dog people. Wouldn't have been stuck hanging in this stomach wrenching suspense if they were.

Finally, I collected whatever nerve I had left and eased myself out of bed. The floorboards gave a faint creek as my feet settled on top of them. I crossed the room at the anxious pace of someone passing through a lion's den. My heart pounded. Keeping my eye on every corner, I brought myself to the light switch. I felt around for it... then I found it. I tensed up and held my breath, dreading what I was about to see, and flipped the lights on.

There was nothing. The room was just as I left it when I shut my eyes. Even though there was no one else around to witness, I felt totally humiliated. My hypervigilant mind paired with my overactive imagination had reduced me to an oversized six-year-old. Afraid of the dark... that's what I was. My breathing and heartrate began to regulate.

"POUND! POUND! POUND!" on the glass. The shock sent me falling back against the door.

"FUCKING JESUS!" I cried out half involuntarily.

Shuffling footsteps accompanied by a high-pitched cackle could be heard on the other side.

Approaching footsteps came down the hall and up to my door. There was a concerned knock.

"Jeff," my Mom asked from the other side, "what's wrong?"

I jumped to my feet and spun around to open the door. She stood in the doorway, my Dad right behind her, both jarred and wide-eyed by the abruptness of my reaction.

"Mora was just outside my window!" I yelled.

That next day, my Mom called Glen and Annette Hartley to inform them about their eldest daughter. They both offered their sincerest apologies - something that's routine for them, I'd expect - and said they would be taking more "drastic measures" with her... whatever the hell that was supposed to indicate.

I was promptly offered the job down at Fatso's Diner and they put me to work within a week. Business has a tendency to be slow, as I had rather expected, but I was just glad to finally have a steady paycheck. It's easy work: keep the booths, counters, and bathroom clean; mop the floor for open and close; and take customers' orders about three to five times a day. Really the hardest part is managing the boredom.

I was closing up just the other night, following a fairly typical work day, when a girl with medium blonde hair, lightly freckled skin, piercing green eyes, and draped in a floral white sundress breezed in through the doors. I looked over in response to the chiming of the bell to see her approaching the counter.

She was beautiful. By far the most attractive human being I'd seen in this entire town. Or possibly ever. I walked down to greet her.

"Good evening," I said in my usual customer friendly tone "sorry to let you know but we're actually getting ready to close down for the night."

She seemed slightly taken aback, "Oh... I'm so sorry. I always thought you guys were open for a little later."

"No, you're totally cool. We usually lock up at about 11 but everyone thinks we go until about midnight."

I could tell she was really disappointed. It's not like there was any place else in town that could help her at this time of night. It felt like a complete sin to just send her out like that.

"I can get you something to go, if you'd like?" I opted.

She eyed me for a moment. A gentle smile appeared on her face.

"Wouldn't say no to a slice of cherry pie, if you got." She said.

I nodded as I turned, almost tripping over my-stupid-smitten-self. I could sense her giggling at me at a polite volume as I went to put a wedge of cherry pie into a Styrofoam box. I brought it back and rang it up for her. As she paid she glanced at me a bit, studying my face and squinting.

"Okay, I definitely know you from somewhere." She insisted.

I scrunched up my brow. There was no way.

"Oh?" I asked, "From where?"

"I'm positive we went to high school together."

I paused and studied her face the same way she had studied mine. Finally it clicked. I couldn't believe it.

"You're Donna Hartley!" I blurted.

She smiled and raised her eyebrows indicating I was indeed correct.

I continued "Well yeah, I'm Jeff Rumson! I grew up just down the road from you!"

This brought her to a full stop. She lightly bit her lip and looked down.

"Oh... yeah," she mumbled, "should know about us all pretty well then."

I could tell she was embarrassed. I immediately felt so stupid, like I had said way too much.

"Sorry about my sister." She sighed, still refusing to look at me.

I really had no idea how to respond. I reached up and scratched the back of my head.

"Well... I mean, that's not your fault." I countered, "You shouldn't feel like you need to apologize or anything."

"Somebody has to. God knows she won't."

She finished paying and looked back up at me, forcing a smile to hide her humiliation.

"Appreciate the pie."

I nodded and smiled back, "Hope to see you around some more, Donna!"

She briefly flashed her teeth with a quick, shy laugh as she turned and headed back out into the night.

I stood for a moment after she had left, still going over the interaction. Never in a million years could you convince me that that girl was the sibling of the rabid wolf-woman who was terrorizing me for some odd reason. What a weird-ass world.

When I went home that night, I was baffled to see that neither my Mom's car nor my Dad's pick-up were in the driveway. The house was completely dark. This made me stop for a moment but I decided not to think too much of it. I went up, unlocked the door, and let myself in.

An eerie sort of calm hit me upon entrance. There was not a single shred of movement anywhere in the house, not even the natural creaking sounds of settling. I looked up the stairs; they ascended into dark nothingness. I very carefully switched on the light and made my way up.

Down the hall I could see into my parents bedroom. I tilted my head for a better view. Even in the excessively dim lighting it was clear to see that the bed was empty and neatly made. I thought maybe I should call them and see what was going on. When I reached into my pocket, however, I realized I didn't have it on me.

"Fuck." I muttered under my breath. I remembered that I had left it in the console of my car, as I usually did on work days.

I was about to turn and go back out to get it... when I noticed a strange smell lingering in the air. A rotten smell. I sniffed a bit more to confirm I wasn't imagining things. I stared back down towards my parents' bedroom. Stepping closer and closer to the doorway, the view of the bed widened. The horrendous smell grew stronger. In the dark I could see something coiled up on the bed. Some strange shape I could not decipher.

My heart started beating faster as I crossed the threshold into their room. There I paused and stared. The smell was unbearable. I still couldn't make out what was on the bed. Christ, did I even want to know? The still, foul air hung tightly in my throat. I considered for a few more moments before I reached for the light switch... and flipped it upward.

The brief white flash nearly stunned me for a moment before my eyes adjusted and the room was illuminated. I stood in a horrified stupor at what laid on the bed:

A barbed-wire necklace of teeth and four bloody chicken legs.
