My mom stayed after Léa left.

\[Part 1 here: https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/s/xHxMC1Dkci\]

Léa left yesterday afternoon. She didn't slam the door. She took her keys, her phone, said "I'm going somewhere, don't wait up" in that calm voice like she was just going to get bread, and left. She didn't come back. Her phone goes straight to voicemail.

The two bags were still by the front door when my mom got here around 8pm. Léa's small bag and my old backpack that I swear I didn't pack. We both saw them. We kept looking at them all night.

I called my mom because I didn't want to be alone. I didn't tell her everything I posted here. I just said Léa left and I was spiraling. She came with a blanket and soup. We sat on the couch with only the small lamp on in the corner, the one that makes everything orange. The big light makes the apartment feel too empty. It was quiet. That quiet where you hear the fridge humming and your own breathing feels too loud.

We talked about nothing for a long time. But every few minutes we both glanced at the entrance. My mom had opened the backpack earlier. Inside it was folded way too perfectly. My clothes, my charger, even an old high school hoodie I thought I threw away. On top a note that looked exactly like my handwriting. It said JUST IN CASE YOU HAVE TO LEAVE QUICKLY - L. I didn't write it. My mom read it and didn't say anything. She just zipped it back up and pushed it a little farther from the door with her foot, like she didn't want it near us.

Around 11pm she said she was going to try to sleep on the couch. She laid down facing the front door with her blanket. I got up to go to the bedroom. Six steps from the couch to the bedroom. The bedroom door was open a crack, exactly how Léa always left it when she promised she wouldn't sleep and would watch me breathe.

I pushed it and froze.

Léa was standing just inside the bedroom, where the orange light from the lamp cuts across the floor. That's how I could see her feet so clearly. She was barefoot and her feet were filthy. Not a little dirty. Gray, caked with dust, like she'd been walking outside a long time on concrete without shoes. The light was hitting her ankles and I could see the dry dust cracked on her soles.

She was holding my shoes. One in each hand, down by her sides like they were heavy. And at her feet, on the floor, were the two bags. Both of them. The small one and my backpack. Both of them that were supposed to be by the front door.

She took them. She must have taken them without a sound while we were sitting three meters away looking at that exact spot.

She wasn't looking at me. She was looking past me, at the living room, at my mom. Her mouth was open in that smile held too long, stretched so you could see all her teeth. She wasn't blinking. Her face was wet like she'd been outside in the cold and came in.

I whispered "Léa?"

She tilted her head really slow to the side, like she was listening to something behind me, and took a small step forward into more of the light. I could see her better and I wish I hadn't. Her eyes were red and glassy. She does this thing sometimes when we're up late and she's tired from work, where mid-conversation she just stops for a second and comes back normal. This time she stayed frozen for a long time. Too long.

Then she mouthed something. No sound. Just lips, exaggerated.

Four seconds.

My stomach dropped. That's the number I count in my head when I wake up at night thinking I stopped breathing. I never told anyone. I never wrote it in Part 1. I just said I was scared of dying in my sleep. How does she know four seconds?

I backed into the living room so fast I almost fell.

My mom wasn't lying down anymore.

She was standing up. In the middle of the living room, between the couch and the front door. Barefoot, blanket around her shoulders like a cape. Completely still, staring at the front door.

My heart started beating so hard I thought it was going to explode. I could hear it in my ears, in my throat. Thump thump thump so fast it hurt. I've been scared of Léa for a week, I'm kind of used to that fear now. But I have never in my entire life seen my mom act like that. Never. My mom is loud, she talks too much, she moves all the time, she is the most normal person I know. She never freezes. She never stares at nothing.

Seeing her like that, standing alone in my living room at midnight, barefoot, staring at the front door, that was more terrifying than seeing Léa holding my shoes. Way more. Because if my mom is scared like that, it means it's real. It's not me overthinking or being paranoid. It's something that can make my own mother forget how to be a mother. Something that makes her forget to even look at me.

The whole scene felt lunar. Like not real life. Like a nightmare where your house is your house but everything is wrong.

I said "Mom? She's in the bedroom. She has my shoes. She has the bags."

My mom didn't turn her head. She just stared at the front door and whispered, so small I almost didn't hear it, "Don't look at her. Look at the door."

I looked at the entrance. The spot where the two bags had been for a week. Where they were twenty minutes ago when my mom pushed the backpack with her foot.

It was empty. Completely empty. Not a bag, not a shoe. Just the bare floor and a little rectangle of dust where they had been sitting. A cold draft was coming from under the front door and I could see it moving the dust a little.

Both bags were gone from the entrance. Both of them were behind us in the bedroom at Léa's feet.

So I stood next to my mom, as far from the bedroom as possible, right against the front door. We were both staring at the handle. My mom had her feet pulled up off the cold floor, standing on the edge of the blanket. She was breathing so fast.

Behind us, Léa took another small step forward. Bare feet on wood. I heard it. Then another. She wasn't rushing. Then she crouched down.

Zzzip.

Zzzip.

Slow. She was zipping and unzipping my backpack. Over and over. Really slow, like she wanted us to hear every tooth of the zipper.

Then she stopped and I heard the other zipper. The small one. The one that was Léa's.

My mom grabbed my wrist. Her hand was ice cold. She whispered again, still staring at the front door, "Are your shoes by your feet, honey?"

They're not. Léa has them. One in each hand. And she has both bags that were by the entrance. The entrance is empty.

I'm not turning around. And I'm not opening that front door.
