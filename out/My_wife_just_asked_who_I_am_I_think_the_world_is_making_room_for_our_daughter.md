I'm posting this here because there's nowhere else to go. If I tell anyone in real life, they'll put me in a psych ward. I need to get this down.  
   
It started small. A few months ago my wife Julia and I bought this huge, clunky dark blue sofa for the living room. Getting it up the stairs was a nightmare. That same night I went to look for my favorite denim jacket in the closet. Gone. I checked the car, the laundry, everywhere. It just vanished.  
   
A week later Julia brought home a stray cat we named Marmalade. That night I went to use the microwave. It wasn't there. And I don't mean there was an empty spot on the counter — the counter was shorter. Just a solid piece of granite from the fridge to the sink. When I asked Julia, she looked at me like I was stupid. "We've never had a microwave, what are you talking about?"  
   
I figured I was losing it. Sleep deprivation, stress, whatever. I was wrong.  
   
The nightmare actually started three months ago, when our daughter Lily was born.  
   
She's perfect. But she takes up so much damn space. Her crying, her breathing, her tiny fingers, the exact shade of her eyes. She's too much. Too real.  
   
The night we brought her home from the hospital, I went to the bathroom to brush my teeth. I looked into the mirror, and there was no reflection. Not like a movie vampire. The glass was just... gray slate. A flat, non-reflective piece of smooth stone. I touched it. Freezing cold. The world deleted my reflection to make room for Lily's lungs.  
   
I didn't tell Julia. How could I?  
   
Last month Julia tried to call her mom because Lily had colic. She stared at her phone for ten minutes, completely lost.  
   
"What's her name?" she whispered. "I know her face, I know her voice, but what is her name?"  
   
I grabbed the phone. The contact list was there, but the letter M was gone from the alphabet entirely. My mother-in-law wasn't just forgotten. Her whole identity was compressed into nothing.  
   
Last week it got aggressive.  
   
I woke up at 3:00 AM because Lily was crying. I swung my legs out of bed, but my left foot didn't hit the floor. It dropped into a void. I stumbled back, shaking. Where our bedroom door used to be, there was just flat, solid drywall. The exit was gone. The window was gone. Just a sealed white box.  
   
I started screaming, throwing myself against the wall. On the other side I could hear Julia crying, asking why I locked myself in. But she wasn't knocking on a door. She was thumping her fists against a solid wall she believed had always been there. It took me four hours to scrape a hole through the drywall with a metal clothes hanger just to get out.  
   
And now, today.  
   
An hour ago I went to check on Lily in her crib. She was sleeping fine. So real, so heavy.  
   
When I turned around to leave, the nursery door was gone. Just smooth, painted plaster.  
   
I ran toward the bathroom to grab a hammer, but the bathroom door was gone too. The hallway is getting narrower. The ceiling feels lower. The whole house is losing its shape, room by room, squeezing itself around the crib.  
   
Julia is sitting on the floor next to me, holding Lily. She's crying, but she doesn't know why. She keeps looking at me, her eyes going blank for a few seconds.  
   
"Who are you?" she just whispered to me. "Why are you in our house?"  
   
She doesn't know who I am. The world deleted my marriage to pay for Lily's first tooth.  
   
Lily just woke up. She is looking right at me, and she's smiling. She is getting brighter and more vivid by the second.  
   
I just hope... I just hope whatever gets deleted next, it leaves enough of me to hear her say her first word.
