I will never forget that sound the unnatural, echoing cries of that thing.

The story I'm going to tell takes place in what some might see as a cliché location for a scary story a forest. But I assure you, there was nothing normal about this one.

That day, the weather was deceptively good. Not gloomy, not foreboding good. The kind of day that almost makes you forget how quiet isolation can really be. Birds chirped above me as I sat alone in a half-covered tree stand, surrounded by autumn leaves burning orange and red in the sunlight. Their glow painted the forest floor in warmth, even as the air bit with a sharp autumn chill.

I had been waiting for hours. Patience waning. Hope fading.

Stillness. Nothing.

By afternoon, hunger gnawed at me more than boredom. With a frustrated sigh, I climbed stiffly down from the stand and trudged toward my truck parked crooked beside a dirt road that was nearly buried under fallen leaves.

I yanked open the driver's side door and dropped into the seat. My fingers rummaged through the center console until I found the half-crushed bag of jerky I'd bought at a grimy gas station the night before. I tore it open with my teeth and chewed greedily, reaching to switch on the barely working radio.

A low voice droned about recent "accidental deaths" in a small town a few miles upriver. I half-listened, more focused on the tree line ahead. The radio was just background noise something to fill the silence that had begun to feel too heavy.

Then: static.

/Kshhhhh/

"—and for today's weath—"

/Kshhhh/

I slapped the radio, cursed under my breath, and managed only to kill it entirely. The sudden silence made the forest seem even louder. I sighed, climbed out, and stood for a moment in the sharp afternoon air.

The smell of wet leaves and soil hung thick. The kind of scent that feels ancient like the earth is breathing beneath you. I found myself admiring it again as I wandered back toward the tree stand, thinking maybe I'd sit out the last few hours before sunset.

By the time I climbed back up, the woods had settled into that strange, half-silent calm that only comes in autumn. My eyelids grew heavy, lulled by the stillness. I drifted off.

I woke with a start.

The sunlight filtering through the branches hadn't changed. The forest looked exactly as it had before I'd fallen asleep. For a moment I wasn't sure if I'd slept for minutes or hours. With no watch, I decided to check the time in the truck.

Yawning, I climbed down and walked back through the leaves but as I reached the truck, something hit me. A wave of déjà vu so strong it made my skin prickle. Everything the smell, the light, even the angle of my parked truck felt repeated.

I brushed it off, opened the door, and fished through the glove box. My old leather watch lay inside, its strap broken, frozen at 12:03 p.m. I frowned, shoved it back, and tried the radio again.

It crackled to life instantly. I jumped.

And then I froze.

The same voice. The same broadcast. The same exact words about the "accidental deaths." I waited, heart thumping and right as the man began to mention the weather…

/Kshhhhhhh/

Static. Again. The same pattern.

My stomach twisted. I turned the radio off and stepped outside, scanning the trees. Everything looked normal. Too normal. The air felt heavy, charged like a storm was building, though the sky above was clear.

"Get it together," I muttered. I wasn't leaving without filling my tag.

Halfway back to the tree stand, a faint humming filled my ears like a phone vibrating right next to my head. It faded into a high-pitched ringing, then back to a hum, before disappearing entirely.

"What the hell…" I muttered, shaking my head. I took another few steps and suddenly the ringing roared back, sharp enough to make me stumble. My balance vanished, my vision tunneled and then, darkness.

I jolted awake.

Back in the tree stand.

Sunlight filtering through the branches exactly as before. The forest unchanged.

My breath hitched. "No," I whispered. "No, I'm losing it."

The humming started again faint, but unmistakable. This time, the light seemed to dim with it. Shadows deepened. The birds fell silent. The entire forest held its breath.

"I'm going fuckin' crazy," I murmured. My hands were shaking as I gripped the rifle to my chest.

Movement caught my eye below a flicker between the trees. Relief surged through me. The buck. Finally.

I steadied my aim, watching as the creature stepped into view a broad-shouldered whitetail buck. But its gait was off. Too stiff. Too slow.

I ignored the unease crawling up my neck and sighted behind its front leg.

BANG!

The rifle's recoil slammed into my shoulder. I looked back through the scope and froze.

The buck hadn't moved.

No flinch. No cry. No fall.

A deep crimson hole smoked through its ribs, thick black blood pouring out like oil. Slowly, it lifted its head and looked straight at me eyes pure white, expressionless.

My breath caught in my throat.

Then came the scream.

A sound that didn't belong in this world a mix of human agony, a boar's squeal, and the piercing shriek of an eagle. It echoed through the forest, but it felt far away, like it came from everywhere and nowhere at once.

I scrambled for my rifle, fumbling with the bolt. When I looked back down it was gone. Only the black blood remained, soaking into the dirt.

My heart pounded like gunfire in my chest. The ringing returned, louder than before. And then

A voice.

Above me.

My own voice.

"I'm going fuckin' crazy."

I looked up. Hanging from a branch was a twisted, malformed version of myself my own face stretched over an elongated, pale body. It hung upside down, saliva dripping onto my forehead. It smiled.

Then it clicked its tongue. My tongue.

I screamed and fired upward.

The thing shrieked, that same distant, echoing wail. My vision blurred, the ringing swelling until it drowned out everything. The forest spun sideways and I blacked out.

When I came to, I was freezing.

Mud pressed cold against my chest. My clothes were gone. My skin was slick with blood that wasn't mine. The sound of a river filled my ears.

I pushed myself up with shaking arms, the last light of day flickering through the trees. My body ached like I'd been thrown through hell. I looked down at my hands raw, trembling, red with blood and dirt.

And then, just as I tried to stand, the ringing returned.

Deafening.

Everything went black.

The last thing I heard before I hit the ground was that same, echoing screech distant and wrong, fading into the dark.
