I grew up on a ranch in northwestern New Mexico. My family had lived there since the late 1800s, and we had a deep connection to the land. Because of what I am about to tell you, I have not been back in over forty years. It all started when I was sixteen years old. It was a typical, mundane day, and I was fixing one of the fences on the property. I was zoned out, trying to drive the posts in as quickly as possible so I could get back to the house before dark. Out in the distance, I spotted a coyote about a hundred feet away. This was not a strange sighting for the area, so I did not pay it much attention at first. Typically, the coyotes would wander in from the desert and run away as soon as they realized there were humans in the area. I finished tying the last bit of barbed wire on, grabbed my tools, and started walking back towards the truck. I looked back one last time to admire my work and noticed that the coyote was in the same place. The way it behaved was unlike anything I have ever seen. It stood there, completely still, and watched me as if it were observing me, trying to learn how I behaved. It looked at me the way a curious kid watches an animal at the zoo, as if it was trying to learn everything about me. I was a little spooked at this point and quickly walked back to the truck. As I drove away, I looked back through the rearview mirror, and it was gone. I laughed a little bit to myself and thought that I was just letting my bored mind make things up. I got in just as the sun was going down, and went to bed early that night, thinking more about the game of Monopoly that I had just lost to my little brother than the coyote. That night, I woke up at two in the morning in a cold sweat. Every instinct I had seemed to be telling me to run, and the adrenaline nearly made me jump out of bed. I was trying to figure out what in the world was wrong with me when I heard a soft knocking on my bedroom window. The knocking was so faint that I had to be completely still to hear it. Then, I heard a voice saying, “Please let me in, I’m hurt, please John.” I recognized the voice instantly; it was my older sister. I rushed out of bed and sprinted to the front door. I had my hand on the lock to open the door, but something stopped me. I could hear the T.V. playing in the living room. I went to see if anyone was watching, and my sister was right there, sleeping on the couch. At this point, I was about to lose it. I blinked hard and then blinked again, trying to figure out whether I was dreaming or losing my mind. But there she was, my sister Olivia, sleeping right there on the couch. I walked back to my room, trying to convince myself that it was all a bad dream. I got into bed and was nearly back to sleep when the knocking began again. This time it was louder and more aggressive. The voice started again, this time my mother's, saying, “Please, John, you have to let me in, I’m scared out here.” It sounded just like my mother, but something was off. The voice had zero emotion or pitch changes. It sounded like a monotone robot was trying to imitate a human voice. I pulled the covers over my head and covered my ears. I tried to ignore whatever was outside, but the knocking kept getting louder and louder, and the voice rose almost into a shout. Just as I began to think that the window might break, my dad burst into the room. “ What are you doing!,” he yelled. “ You’re going to wake the entire house up banging and shouting like that!” I started to explain what was happening, but I did not even fully believe it myself. After he left, the voice went away. I did not sleep at all for the rest of the night. As soon as the sun came up, I went outside to check for any sign of animal or human tracks. I saw what looked like coyote tracks, circling all around the house, but something was weird about them. Instead of four tracks, it looked like the coyote had been walking on only two legs. I looked around for any more evidence, but did not see anything else. Two years went by, and I almost forgot that this even happened. I had just finished high school and was planning to go to college in California at the start of the next semester. All I had was one more summer of working on the ranch, and then I would get to go study in the city. The summer was mostly uneventful, but I still remember what happened the day before I left. I was out on the edge of the ranch, checking on a calf that had wandered away from its mother. When I finally found the calf, it was standing motionless next to a dried-up creek bed. I started to walk up to it, but I noticed something was wrong. Its fur was pristine, as if it had just been cleaned a few hours ago. This calf had been out in the desert for two days alone; it should have looked dirty and on the verge of death. Then, the smell hit me. It smelled like an animal that had been dead for months, but the calf looked pristinely clean. Suddenly, the calf turned its head nearly 180 degrees and stared right at me with eyes that can only be described as human. I jumped back and began sprinting to my truck. I got in and drove away as fast as I could. When I got back to the house, the calf was already waiting for me. I kept the truck locked and stared straight at it, ready to reverse out of there. It watched me for a second, and then walked away slowly into the desert, on two legs, just as a human would. I left the next day to start school, and I have not visited the ranch since. When my parents died, I quickly sold it to whoever would buy it. My cousins could not understand why I would sell something that our family had owned for so long, but they did not see what I saw. I do not know what that creature was, or if it meant any harm, but I do know that it was watching me, learning exactly what it needed to know to lure me out to it.
