I'm 19. I've never been good at talking about my feelings, so my therapist suggested I start journaling. Writing things down helps me process them better than speaking ever could. I guess that's why I'm posting this here, because maybe typing it out will make it more real. Or less real, I don't know.

My twin brother, Marcus, died three weeks ago in a car accident. He was driving. I wasn't with him. If I had been there, maybe things would be different. But that's not helpful thinking. That's what Dr. Chen says.

Marcus was everything I'm not. He was confident, loud, the kind of person who'd walk into a room and immediately own it. Everyone gravitated toward him. He played lacrosse, dated beautiful people, got into his first-choice college without even trying. He was always laughing. Always had a plan, always knew what he wanted. I was the one who preferred being home, reading fantasy novels, staying up late on Discord with online friends I'd never met. I was quiet. Invisible, really.

We looked identical, but people rarely confused us. Marcus had this energy that made him unmistakable. He was alive in a way I've never been. I was just... the other one. The accident happened on a Friday. A truck ran a red light. Marcus died at the scene. I found out when my mom called me crying, and I just shut down. I didn't cry. I didn't scream. I just sat there in my room, staring at my computer screen, thinking about how he was always the one living while I was just existing. Dr. Chen says the lack of emotion is normal (shock, dissociation, self-care). That's why I started the journal. Three weeks of entries. Just me, trying to understand that the world is still turning without him. That people are going about their lives. That he's gone and I'm still here.

I've been writing about memories. About the time Marcus jumped off the high dive at the community pool when we were six, even though it was technically against the rules. He did it without hesitation. I was terrified, stuck on the ladder while everyone watched. He surfaced, laughing, and yelled for me to jump. When I wouldn't, he laughed harder. He had no patience for fear. I was thinking about that memory a lot yesterday. About how he never hesitated. How he'd probably be embarrassed of me for being so careful. So timid. Always waiting for permission, always worried about what could go wrong. Marcus would have hated that about me.

This morning, something strange happened. I was reading over my journal entries, and I realized something. When I was writing about that pool memory, I didn't sound like myself. The tone shifted halfway through. It started with me being sad about the memory, and then suddenly I was being kind of mean to myself in the writing. Like I was judging my younger self for being scared. That's weird, right? I don't usually do that.

Work has been interesting. I stock shelves at Target in the evenings, which is boring and mindless, but I've always kind of liked it that way. But yesterday something was different. I was in the back, and I had this sudden urge to do something reckless. To climb the really high shelves even though we're not supposed to. To move faster, work harder, prove something. It was strange because I'm not like that. But for a second, I felt invincible. Like I could do anything. I actually knocked over some boxes. My coworker asked if I was okay, and I just laughed it off. But here's the weird part, I found myself saying something I would never normally say. I told him he was being too cautious, that he should take more risks. It was such a Marcus thing to say that I heard myself say it and felt embarrassed. I think grief is making me act strange.

I've been thinking a lot about how I always admired him. How he never second-guessed himself. How he would have looked at my life now (hidden in my room, writing in a journal instead of living) and probably felt disappointed. He was so much braver than me. So much more "alive" than me. I wish I could have been more like him. The journal thing is getting weird though. I'll write something, and then later I'll reread it, and parts of it sound like they were written by someone else. Not in a supernatural way or anything. Just like... parts where I was particularly harsh on myself. Or parts where I'm being kind of arrogant about things. Marcus was arrogant. He had the personality to back it up, but he was definitely arrogant. I've never been like that.

But when I reread those parts, they don't sound wrong. They sound like they're calling out my weakness. Calling out how boring and safe I am. People keep saying that Marcus lives on in me. Because we're twins, because we share DNA... They said it at the funeral. They keep saying it now. At first I thought it was meaningless, something people say. But I think they might be right. I feel like... like I'm waking up to things. Like parts of me that were sleeping are opening their eyes. Yesterday I laughed. Actually laughed. Not the sad, hollow laugh I've had since he died, but a real laugh. I was alone in the apartment, and something was funny, I can't even remember what now, and I laughed and it sounded exactly like him. It sounded like Marcus. My mom called me today and said she was relieved that I'm "doing better." That I sound more "like myself." But I'm not sure what that means anymore. Who am I if not him? I spent nineteen years being half of something. Marcus was the bright half. I was just the other half.

I think maybe I'm understanding him better now. Understanding what it was like to be him. To move through the world with that kind of confidence. To not be paralyzed by everything the way I usually am. It's not a bad feeling. It's actually kind of... liberating. Dr. Chen texted asking if I wanted to reschedule my appointment. I don't think I need therapy right now. I think I'm finally okay. I think maybe the best thing that could happen is that I stop being me and start being someone better. Someone braver... Someone like him.

UPDATE: It's been a few days. I'm not sure why I'm updating this, honestly. Nobody probably cares.

Things are going really well, actually. Better than they have in a while. I've been spending more time with people. I hung out with some of Marcus's friends, our friends now, I guess, and it was easy. I was funny. I was confident. They kept saying how surprised they were to see me opening up. How I was "finally coming out of my shell." They seemed really happy. It made me realize how much time I wasted being afraid. How much I missed by hiding. I quit the Target job. It was boring and it was beneath me. I can do better than that. Marcus would have quit months ago. I'm already thinking about what I want to do instead. Something that matters. Something that puts me in the center of things instead of on the sidelines. My mom asked me how therapy was going, and I told her I'm not going anymore. She seemed worried for a second, but then I took her to lunch and made her laugh, and she just seemed happy to see me doing well. She said I look healthier. That I have "color in my cheeks" now. I think she meant it as a compliment.

I don't remember the last time I was afraid of anything. I don't remember the last time I questioned a decision. It's like part of my brain that was always loud and doubtful just... quieted down. And it's the best thing that's ever happened to me. Sometimes I'll catch my reflection and barely recognize myself. Not in a bad way. Just in a way where I look at myself and think, "Oh. That's what I was supposed to look like." The confidence looks good on me. The swagger. The way I take up space now. I'm going back to school this semester, and I'm going to change my major. I want to do something that requires guts. Something where people have to pay attention to me. I'm done being invisible. The journal sits on my desk, but I don't write in it much anymore. I don't need to. I'm not processing grief right now. I'm just living. The way he lived.

Sometimes people ask me how I'm coping so well. How I lost my brother and I'm doing better than I ever have. I don't really have an answer for them. I just tell them that maybe sometimes terrible things have to happen to shake you awake. That maybe I needed him to die for me to finally understand what it meant to be alive. They usually get uncomfortable when I say that. But I don't care. I'm not afraid of making people uncomfortable anymore.

The funny thing is, I don't really feel like I lost him at all. It feels more like I finally found him. Like he was always supposed to be part of me and now he is.

I'm going to delete this post soon, actually. I don't need strangers on the internet to validate my grief. I'm past that. I'm past all of it, cause I'm exactly where I'm supposed to be !

EDIT: (One week later)

People keep asking why I've changed. My friends. My mom. Acquaintances from school who haven't seen me in months. They all say the same thing: "You're different. What happened to you?" I smile and tell them I'm finally becoming myself. It's the truth.

My therapist (even though I stopped going) reached out through my mom. She said she was concerned that I'd gone through a "personality shift." That grief sometimes manifests as a kind of manic episode. She suggested I come back in. I won't be going back. I don't need therapy. I don't need medication. I don't need anyone telling me that what I'm experiencing is wrong or unhealthy. This is the healthiest I've ever felt. This is clarity. This is finally understanding what it means to be exceptional instead of "exceptional-by-accident-of-birth".

My neighbor said something weird when he saw me today. He said I remind him of someone, but he can't remember who. Then he said it was probably just my imagination. But he looked unsettled. I wonder if he could see it. See him. See that something's changed.

I'm happy now. Truly happy. And I don't think I'm ever going to be sad again. I don't think I'm going to be anything except what I am now.

The journal is staying closed. I don't need to write to understand myself anymore. For the first time in nineteen years, I understand exactly who I am.

And he understands me too.
