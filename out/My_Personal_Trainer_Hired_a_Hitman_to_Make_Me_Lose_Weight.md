When your weight is as stubborn as mine was, it starts to feel like you were meant to be fat—that it’ll take a reckoning for your fatness to ever change.

I’ve struggled with my weight ever since I was in kindergarten. Although I’ve never been morbidly obese, I’ve been on the deeper end of an overweight BMI for the bulk of my life. I was always the chubby friend growing up, the one who couldn’t find her size at stores and needed to cover up at the beach. Oh, I’d wanted things to change. I’d jumped at every fad diet or new weight loss product that popped up, without success.

Okay, I’ll admit I did slip up frequently. If I was presented with the opportunity to cheat on my diets, I would. In my head, I would rationalise it with the excuse that one slip—in isolation—could never be bad enough to set me back significantly. Of course, it would never be just one break from the diet. These extra candy bars, pastries and sodas would all add up throughout the day. Sometimes I’d get seconds at meals.

This would inevitably halt any weight loss on the scale. And, each time, once the realisation that the diet wasn’t working hit, I’d invariably allow whatever weight I’d expunged to return. It was a tiring cycle that eventually put me off trying to change my size at all. At 30 years of age with a kid under my belt, I expected I would never beat it.

That was around when my friend Violet started doing the impossible: she lost weight.

At every mommy group meeting we had, I noticed the pounds starting to fall off her week by week. It was incredible the results she was having. Violet had put on weight after having her first child and, like me, seemed destined to keep the weight on. We had a similar lack of self-control around food and exercise, and used to catch up over various unhealthy foods.

Now, here she was, showing off her weight loss in slimmer and slimmer clothing. Her answer wasn’t anything unbelievable when I finally asked what her secret was.

“I have the most *incredible* personal trainer” she gushed to me at a meet-up while eating her salad. “He really keeps me accountable and prioritising my progress—training with him is heart-pounding but it’s *so* worth it for the results he got out of me!”

I was sold. I just had to meet this trainer and give him a try. The handful of trainers I’d used previously had given up along with me when my dedication faltered. But it sounded like this guy wouldn’t let his clients off the hook for their weight loss dreams—and that was exactly what I needed. Whatever he was doing to motivate his clients, it worked.

Ashton was a handsome dude, with tan, chiseled calves and golden blonde hair he kept cropped into a crew cut. If I’d been a bit younger and flightier, I could have seen myself developing a crush on him. The second I walked into my local gym for our first session, he was all smiles and wanting to know about my weight of 238lb and my plan to change it.

Our first session training together was a relatively light one. He walked me through some cardio on the treadmill, bicycle and stairmaster before demoing how to use all the various weightlifting machines, ending the day with some dumbbell lifts. The mantra he kept repeating to me throughout the workout was to treat the exercises like a life or death battle—like I was fighting for my life instead of a small waistline.

“My workout philosophy is to lose weight or die trying—I want to know that you’re fully committed before we go any further” he explained warmly, his chiseled face grinning.

Like all his other pep talk, it revved me up to achieve my goals and I thought nothing of promising my commitment to him. Sure, I’d give working out my all—but it wasn’t like anything bad would happen if I didn’t.

And so I began working out with Ashton twice a week. In addition to the previous workouts he’d shown me, Ashton also put me on a balanced meal plan, high in lean meats and vegetables, moderate on carbs and fruit and very low on unhealthy, fatty, sugary foods—the kinds I liked. He taught me to count calories and adjust my portion sizes so they were reasonable. Nothing I was asked to do was too hard.

Yet, within a month of starting my weight loss plan with him, my motivation already began to falter. It was too easy to do what I always did with exercise and diets, which was to abandon them. I started arriving late to gym sessions and cheating on my diet whenever I could. The unchanging scale reflected this back at me, as did the pounds that had fallen at first starting to return.

I arrived to my next session with Ashton late again, finishing a chocolate frappe that I tossed in the trash before entering. So what if I went off my diet, I reasoned. It wasn’t the end of the world if I gave into temptation here and there. Maybe I didn’t need to lose this weight after all.

“Hey Audrey, good to see you” Ashton beamed. “We’ve got a great session ahead planned. However, before we get started, I wanted to mention that I have noticed your motivation slipping a bit the past few weeks.”

Here it comes, I thought to myself. I’m getting ditched as a client.

“It’s *no* problem and totally okay to struggle with weight loss. But because your progress has slowed, I decided to take your program to the *next* level. It’s something I reserve for my more *reluctant* clients.”

“And what’s that?” I asked skeptically. Great, so instead of dropping me as a client, he was going to start shilling supplements to me.

“Well, to up the ante and keep you on track, I’ve hired a professional *hitman* to execute you in the event that you haven’t lost 100 pounds in 6 months time.”

Immediately, I burst out laughing. What a silly joke to start the morning with.

Ashton didn’t laugh along with me. He smiled and squinted like he was trying to understand where my head was at.

“*Yeah*, so I do need to reiterate that a hitman will be locating you 6 months to the day from now, conducting a weigh-in and killing you if you haven’t met your weight target. *No biggie*, just all the more reason to take your weight loss journey seriously and commit to being the *best* version of yourself possible!”

He sounded matter-of-fact and bored, like this was a pre-workout disclaimer he’d given many times before. Now I felt angry at being clearly mocked. If I hadn’t already been straying from his workouts, here was an excuse for me to bail on my ill-guided weight resolution.

I told that weirdo to get stuffed and left the gym, discontinuing my physical activity and healthy eating for weeks. There was just one thing left to do, which was tell Violet about the bad experience I’d had with Ashton. She had a right to know who she was recommending to people.

When I next saw her, however, she seemingly had bigger concerns than my offended feelings. She seemed both jittery and exhausted, as if she had been exercising for days on end. Indeed, it had been hard to arrange a catch-up with her at all as she’d been so consumed with her workout regimen. She’d also lost steadily more weight—yet not enough to put her at ease.

“I can’t s-stay for long,” she muttered to me, her top hanging off her smaller frame. “I n-need to go for a sprint on the beach and then squeeze in another aerobics routine.”

“Honey, you’re pushing yourself too hard, this isn’t healthy” I said sympathetically, taking Violet by the hand. “You haven’t even ordered anything to eat or drink besides black coffee.”

Her heartbeat was practically racing in my hand. Her eyes darted around the cafe in restless fear—a far cry from the confident, slimmed woman who’d inspired me to lose weight.

“You don’t *understand*, I can’t afford those calories right now!” she yelped back. “I’ve plateaued with 3 pounds left to go—I have to drop that weight in the next week or I’ll…I’ll be…”

Comprehension dawned for me about what she was referring to. That ridiculous, irresponsible trainer had given her the same mocking insult as me. I tried to reassure my friend in vain that there was no way this could be true but she didn’t listen. Apparently, other clients of his had been told the same warning as us. With that, she took off from the cafe before I could calm her.

It had to be an eating disorder that Violet had developed, that was making her so obsessed with reaching 120 pounds when she was already skinny. I resigned to get her the help she needed.

I never got the chance to.

A few days later, I received the horrible news of her murder. The words chilled my spine as I heard them. A failed home invasion had left my dear friend shot dead, stripped down to her underwear. The details were already alarming but grew stranger. According to print evidence around the bathroom, whoever had killed her had forced her to stand on a scale seconds before shooting her.

These chilling facts confirmed the reality that Violet had tried to tell me, that I’d been so desperate to deny: Ashton had truly hired hitmen to weigh and kill us.

All the fear my friend had experienced in her last days instantly rocked me. I’d already been gaining weight back in the past few weeks—I now had even less time left to meet my target before the 6 months.

Going back to see Ashton at last, I screamed at him for minutes on end. Cursed him for paying a ruthless assassin on the dark web to eliminate me. Threatened him with calling the police on him for initiating the death of my friend. Begged him to contact the hitman and call off the sworn hit on my life. And finally, when he told me that the contract was unbreakable and nothing could be done to halt the assassin, raged at him for making my life contingent on my weight.

I had never felt as dehumanised as a heavy person as I did then. This sadistic, heartless man literally thought I was better off dead than fat.

“No, Audrey,” Ashton told me sympathetically but firmly. “*You* did, when you agreed to train with me. Now are you motivated to reach your goals or not?”

In that moment it was clear that the only way to escape the same fate as Violet was to reach my weight target. I simply *had* to lose this weight.

And so, for the first time in my life, I started exercising and dieting like my life depended on it.

Each and every day for months, I pushed my body to the absolute limit. I went from my original routine of working out 2 times a week to working out 5 times a week, and then 7 times a week. I poured as much energy as I could into each machine: I sprinted wildly on the treadmill, spun madly on the pushbike, climbed intensely on the stairmaster. I lifted bigger and bigger sets of weights, before eventually shifting to cardio entirely to avoid building too much heavy muscle.

Meanwhile, I meticulously followed my assigned diet like it was an exact science. Every one of the few meals I permitted myself a day would get put through a meal planning app, to count its calories and macronutrients. I weighed food portions, sent orders back and continuously ruled out more and more things I could eat. Breakfast was the first meal to go. Eventually, I was eating just dinner. All the while, I fought the urge to stress eat from panic—something I’d given into my whole life.

This time, I didn’t. The stakes were real now. Delicious fattening food might as well be poisoned because it would bring about certain death.

During my frenzied and frequent workout sessions with Ashton, I channeled every drop of the immense loathing I had for him into my exercising. I imagined I was punching his face during kickboxing, ripping his limbs apart during weight lifts, stomping on his chest during running. Throughout it all, his unphased chipper demeanour remained, like I was just another client on his schedule.

“Come on, you can do it! Look at how much you’ve achieved! Think of what you’re fighting for!”

It made me hate him more.

My weight loss was swift and huge in the first few months. I was losing multiple pounds every week on the scale. My clothes were loosening around me, necessitating buying new sizes every month. I was getting faster, stronger and capable of pushing myself even harder.

But I had been given a nigh impossible task, and this reality became more present to me as I neared the final month of my weight loss target. Pounds became harder and harder to shake. When I’d lost the majority of weight, already transformed more than I’d dreamed, then came the dreaded plateau.

Panicking, I cut out food entirely and lived on meal replacement shakes. Paranoia followed me into every workout. I imagined seeing the assassin lurking around the gym, stalking me, aiming his reticle at me. It felt like I was sprinting away from him, running for my life. I ultimately was. 

Ashton was completely unhelpful at this point. The only advice he had for pushing through the plateau was sleeping better and eating more protein. I was barely sleeping and not eating from terror. I wasn’t interacting with my husband or child at all. Exercising was all I did or thought about.

With just one week left to go until the deadline, it became apparent that I wouldn’t make my target. I still needed to lose 10 pounds and I’d plateaued despite my vigorous efforts. Losing that amount in 7 days would be impossible.

Racing away on that treadmill, I felt the life draining from me—I knew that if the assassin didn’t kill me, this non-stop exercise would.

Then I blacked out.

When I finally came to, I was in a dark hospital bed. Blearily checking the nearby clock, I was shocked at what I saw: a week had passed. I had been in a coma all this time. All that time wasted.

Just as shocking was the shrouded figure who stepped out of the shadows moments later. I didn’t need to check the clock again to know that I’d passed my deadline. The assassin was here to collect on his bounty at last.

The masked, black-wearing figure—with a build much taller than Ashton’s—approached my hospital bed and clicked a button on its side. All this time I’d imagined myself stepping terrified and barefoot on a scale for the weigh-in. But the ICU bed could tell him my exact weight without me even getting up—which I likely couldn’t easily do after my one week coma, anyway. 

The digital numbers flickered in the dark. I waited, knowing the gun was trained on the front of my head, that this was my final moment alive.

*137.8lb.*

I blinked in confusion. That couldn’t be right. The scale was saying I’d lost just over 100lb…which would’ve been possible in my atrophying, comatose state. By the time this registered and I looked back up, the hitman had already departed the hospital. I’d really done it.

Lost the weight, and *survived*.

I wish I could say that I kept the weight off over the ensuing years. But trauma is deep-set and my 6 months of starvation and overwork left me with lots of it. As I regained much of the weight,  I wondered if Ashton had ever really cared about my weight loss success, or if he’d just arranged the deaths of women like Violet to avoid having any client fail stories.

There is some consolation for my fat, resentful heart.

Poor Ashton’s personal training business has fallen on hard times. Last I heard, he’s had trouble getting new clients, what with things like Ozempic, gastric bypass and bad word of mouth taking away his clientele. After months of research on the dark web, I’ve found the assassin Ashton paid to dispatch me. To repay Ashton for my former weight loss and motivate him, I’ve decided to give our hitman friend a call.

He has 6 months to get 100 positive client reviews, or he’s getting assassinated.
