Maybe I should have used the word “ex-girlfriend,” because the only way you’ll ever see us together again is if I’m a rotting corpse. And it’s all because of an old woman named Layla. At least, that’s what I think. Admittedly, I don’t even understand what happened, but maybe someone here can help me make sense of this insanity.



Some quick background: my name is Carter and my ex’s name is Haley. Before I met Haley, I was a deadbeat nobody. No money, no education, no future. The most productive thing I did in life was work the register at a convenience store, and I use the word “working” loosely.

One day, Haley dropped in the store to pick up a snack. I figure she was looking for a deadbeat to piss her dad off, because she came onto me instantly. Over the span of a year and a half, we went from a one-night stand to a summer fling to a committed relationship. And during that time, she turned my whole life around.

She got me to start taking classes at the community college, lay off the weed and liquor, eat healthier, and even get a better job. I mean, I was just a stock boy for a supercenter chain, but it paid twice as much as the convenience store.

Now let me be clear. I loved that she helped me clean up my act, but I loved *her* more. She was everything: gorgeous, thoughtful, brilliant, loving. And, man, did she have her life together. She worked at a fancy restaurant where she made bank, and she was nearing her master’s in finance. I couldn’t believe someone like her spent more than two minutes with me.

Things were going great. We were planning our future together, and we had big dreams. Without a doubt, I was at the height of my entire life. I was happier and healthier than ever, with nothing but excitement for what’s next.

And then the world gave a big ol’ “fuck you” to us. All of us.

COVID-19 reared its microscopic head, and our plans came crashing down. Suddenly, Haley had no job to go to, and my income alone didn’t cover so much as half of our expenses. There were protections in place to help prevent people from losing their homes and stuff while the country was at a standstill, but Haley knew money better than I ever have, and she was panicking.

Looking back, I think she was just anxious. Like maybe she had OCD or something, I don’t know. But she went from having full control of her life to almost no control, and she did not handle it well. She started spending all her time on her computer looking for some kind of solution.

Then, one day, she told me it was in our best interest to downsize for a little while. Considering that before I met her, I just couch surfed from one friend’s place to the next, I had no problem with that. After all, I figured she knew best.

That’s when she told me about Layla, the old lady we’d be moving in with.

Up until this point, I had never heard of Layla. Haley never mentioned her. Not even in some vague way, like, “Remember that one time I told you about the old lady who taught me the value of a dollar?” Nope. Nothing. So I asked her directly, “Who’s Layla?”

“She’s just a sweet, old lady who’s going to help us get through this.”

And every follow-up question I had was answered with an equally innocuous response.

“How’d you two meet?”

“Friend of a friend.”

“How long have you known her?”

“Long enough to trust her.”

It was weird. I realize that. But the woman I loved needed me to make a small sacrifice for her, and considering all she had done for me, I bit my tongue and go along with it.

Most of our stuff just went into a rental storage unit, so one day at a time, our apartment became less of a home. Once the place was all but empty, we loaded up the car with our necessities, and near the end of the day, we drove to Layla’s.

Although I still had my reservations, I have to say that on our way there, I started to feel optimistic. We drove through a quiet neighborhood surrounded by green trees, chirping crickets, and fresh air. After living my whole life in the city, I thought this was going to be like a little vacation.

We arrived at Layla’s home around eight PM. It was a simple, two-story house; well-maintained, simple architecture, and minimalistic in style. The foundation was edged with gravel, and a low-cut lawn poured down the hill toward the street. On its left was a tall oak tree, and on its right was an empty driveway.

I parked the car and when I opened the door, I noticed a strange scent. It was like a strange mix of peat, fresh-cut grass, and wet stone. The smell wasn’t terrible, but it wasn’t all that pleasant either.

As Haley emerged from the car and stretched, I just looked around a bit. Didn’t wander or anything, but something felt off.

Every curtain was drawn, and there were no flowers, flags, windchimes, or birdhouses; hell, not even a welcome mat. The closest things to decorations were the potted herbs on the porch. But none of those were the issue.

It wasn’t until Haley opened the door to the back seat to retrieve our bags that I realized what was wrong. It was like we pulled into an indoor movie set. Sure, things kind of looked real, but the details weren’t right. There weren’t any birds or bugs in the tree, no fallen leaves in the yard, no dirt on the porch. Even the gutters and windows were as clean as dinnerware.

“Hey,” Haley called as she lugged a few bags toward the porch. “Mind giving me a hand?”

“Yeah. Sorry,” I replied.

I grabbed the rest of our bags and joined her on the porch.

Haley knocked on the heavy, dark-stained wooden door as I looked around for a cobweb or dead bug or something. Anything that might make the place seem just a bit more normal. But nope. It was as though the whole property was scrubbed with a Magic Eraser.

The lock turned, and the door cracked open.

Between the door and its jamb peered a glassy eye. The iris had a milky hue to it, and where the white should have been was the veiny pink of someone who hadn’t slept in two days.

“Hi, Layla,” Haley said cheerfully. “I hope we’re not too early.”

I gave Haley a weird look. Did she mean to say, “too late?”

Layla’s eye flicked from Haley to me. But not to my eyes. She looked me up and down for a few seconds, like she was studying me. Just as I was about to introduce myself, she stepped back and opened the door a bit wider. “Haley,” she said quietly. “Welcome.”

Haley looked at me, smiled, then entered the dark home. There were a few flickering flames around the corners, but that was it. It was like looking into a jack-o’-lantern.

I took a step closer but stopped at the threshold. Besides not knowing whether I was welcome too, a new smell wafted toward me. A deep, musky odor; it was slightly sweet and damp, like rotting wood wrapped in fresh leather. I really couldn’t tell if I liked it, and I guess my expression made my uncertainty perfectly clear.

“It’s oud oil,” Layla said.

I hadn’t even noticed that she had leaned around the door, but that’s probably because she blended in with the shadows. It turned out that the reason I could only see her eye earlier was because that was the only part of her that wasn’t covered by black fabric.

Layla was wearing a niqab paired with a jilbab. I didn't know the names then, but together they covered almost everything except her eyes. One of those full body coverings that women in the Middle East might wear. Suffice it to say I was caught completely off-guard.

“Oh. I, uh. Um…” I stammered as I searched for something resembling an appropriate greeting.

But Layla saved us both from that embarrassment. “Welcome,” she said, gesturing for me to enter with her veiled arm.

I let out a nervous chuckle as I stepped inside. “Thanks.” After placing my bags on the floor, I offered a hand and introduced myself. “I’m Carter.”

To say that the following silence was awkward would be an understatement. Layla did not meet my gaze, nor did she acknowledge my introduction. Before my bags touched the floor, she had shut the door and turned to face me. With her head slightly bowed, she stared at my feet as I waited for her to respond.

“Please remove your shoes,” she finally said. Then, as she slipped around me like a shadow, she simply added, “Thank you.”

I remember just freezing up, like a computer program with a bug in its code. I didn’t know how to react. Like, was it alright to be upset with Haley for keeping this from me? Was it okay to be uncomfortable living with someone whose religion and customs I knew nothing about?

On that first night, the introduction was the weirdest part. After I removed my shoes and went into the next room, Haley was sitting there alone. It was a large, mostly empty room. A few candles were lit in the corners, and cushions were lined around a patterned rug in the middle of the floor.

I sat with her and, frankly, began to interrogate her. We kept our voices low, but I wasn’t happy, and however Haley felt at the start, she sure as hell didn’t feel great by the end. When all was said and done, I asked if Layla was going to show us to our room.

“I already know where we’re staying,” Haley said, pouting.

Turned out she was already perfectly familiar with the house, like she had been there at least a few times before. And she seemed to know all about Layla’s customs. Any question I had, she was quick to answer.

“Will she care if we’re up late?”

“Oh, she’s a night owl. You don’t have to worry about that.”

“Why does she keep all the doors shut?”

“Just privacy. You should close the doors behind you too.”

Haley then showed me around the dark house. At least, everywhere I was allowed to go. I was shown which doors were “off-limits,” such as Layla’s bedroom—which I understood—and a couple of rooms that were for “women only.” There was a bathroom and one room Haley simply called “Layla’s sitting room.”

“I sit,” I argued. “Why can’t I go in there?”

Following the least subtle roll of the eyes I’d ever seen, Haley explained, “It’s just a cultural thing.”

Apparently, that was also the reason why the curtains had to be drawn 24/7, and why there wasn’t a single picture of Layla or her family anywhere in the house. It felt like I was moving into a cave. Especially when I was shown the basement.

Every surface down there was dry, gray stone. There were a few shelves and a couple of chests. And by far the worst odor to burn my nostrils since we arrived.

“Ech!” I shouted. “The hell is that?”

Haley gave me a quizzical look, like she didn’t possess the sense of smell. Then an understanding expression formed on her face, and she pointed me to a large box in the corner filled to the brim with black and brown mush. “It’s liquid compost, silly.” With a smile, she added, “Layla loves to garden.”

Unable to bear the stench, I insisted we return upstairs. And with that, the tour was over. I unpacked just enough to get ready for bed and laid down.

It didn’t take long for me to fall asleep even though I was still in a rotten mood. We had an exhausting day, and tomorrow we had to finish unpacking and run some errands. But I remember one of my last waking thoughts being, “What’s taking Haley so long?”

As far as I know, she never came to bed that night.



Over the next few days, I did my best to settle in. Haley and I talked things through. She apologized and made it up to me with a few private favors. I’ll let your imagination fill in the blanks there.

Meanwhile, it turned out to be a pretty comfortable living situation. The only major downside was that there was no internet, so Haley had to sit outside a McDonald’s to catch up on her online classes. It was about a fifty-minute walk, but she said she liked the time to clear her head.

As far as I could tell, Layla was never really around. I never knew when she was home or out and about. It was rare that we’d cross paths. I’d try to be friendly—say hello, smile, whatever—but she would just avert her weary-looking eyes and give me nothing more than a quiet one-word response.

It wasn’t until a couple of weeks in that I realized she was *always* home. Like, I figured she had a job or an errand to run here and there, but I don’t think she left the property once since we moved in.

This first became apparent to me on a Tuesday. I didn’t have work that day and slept in. At this point, Haley’s sleep schedule was getting pretty far off-track. She used to be up at the crack of dawn or earlier and be in bed by ten PM. But I woke up around nine AM, and she was still fast asleep.

Anyway, since she was sleeping, I thought I’d make some progress toward my COVID resolution. Rather than wasting my days away waiting for things to change, I wanted to establish healthier habits. Daily jogs, more reading, maybe Pilates. So, I thought I’d do some meal prep to save time later in the week.

Now, I gotta admit, remembering to do little things like shutting doors every time you walk in and out of a room seems pretty easy to do, but it’s far easier to neglect. I was going from the kitchen to the dining room to our bedroom over and over. And the first time I returned to the kitchen and the door was closed, I hesitated.

“Did I close that?” I wondered. I didn’t remember doing so. In fact, I thought I left each door open since I knew I would be rushing back and forth. But, okay, it’s only been like a minute since I left the kitchen. I must have closed it and not realized.

After reopening the door, I stirred a few things on the stove, grabbed mayo from the fridge, and headed to the dining room. The door was closed there too. I sneered and looked back at the other door. “Alright,” I thought. “It’s still open.” I opened it and continued prepping a couple of sandwiches when I heard a click from the kitchen.

When I peered through the dining room doorway, I could see that the kitchen door was now shut.

For a few seconds, I just kind of watched. Not like I expected much to happen, but I guess I thought I’d see Layla stroll by or something. But there wasn’t a hint of movement. That is, not until the dining room door slammed shut.

I jumped and dropped the butter knife I was using onto the floor. “Layla?” I called quickly. After a few moments of listening to my heart thrash about, I said, “I’m sorry I left the doors open, but I was gonna close them when I was done.”

Still no reply. I figured she was upset or something, and I needed to grab a towel to wipe up the mayo, so I went back into the kitchen. As I opened the door, I started, “I didn’t mean to—”

But Layla wasn’t there. Other than the opposing door being closed, the room was just as I had left it.

Now, I knew it had to have been Layla. I even checked on Haley and made sure she was still asleep. And then it occurred to me just how often stuff like that had happened before. If I left a door open, it’d be closed moments later. If I left food on the table, it’d often just disappear.

At first, I thought I was just forgetful. Like, I had heard that COVID caused memory problems, and I got the vaccine, so maybe I was doing stuff without realizing it. But there was no way I could have slammed that door shut from the middle of the dining room.

So, I had a problem. The place was strange enough as it was, but knowing that our host was sneaking around watching me gave me the creeps. I decided I would have to talk to Haley about moving out. And the opportunity to do so came the very next day.

When I got home from work, it was late and Haley wasn’t in bed, so I wandered around the house and looked for her. She wasn’t in the kitchen or the guest room, and she didn’t answer when I knocked on the bathroom door, so the only other place I figured she might have been was the sitting room. She would spend a ton of time with Layla there.

I knocked on the door and called her name, but there was no response. “This is stupid,” I mumbled. It felt like I was playing hide and seek. The house was constantly shrouded in darkness, and with every door constantly being closed, Haley could have entered the room I had just left and I’d have no idea.

I know it wasn’t my house, but it was my home, and I felt that I had the right to know whether my partner was behind a fucking door!

So I just grabbed the doorknob and turned. But before I opened it, a voice growled from behind me, “*Don’t* you go in there.”

Every single muscle in my body tensed and I spun around. At first, I didn’t see anyone. But then I saw the slightest hint of movement in the shadows. There she was. Layla, standing at the end of the hall, watching me. She was close to the wall and hunched over. She had to have been *trying* to stay hidden.

“Layla?” I asked, half-relieved, half-agitated. “What the hell?”

She didn’t move or respond. I could just barely make out her milky eyes as they monitored me.

I took a deep breath and shook my head. “I just wanna know where Haley is. Alright?”

After a few more silent moments, Layla finally responded. This time normally, not in some demonic grunt. “She was helping me garden. I’ll tell her to meet you in your bedroom.” And then she just disappeared around the corner.

Simply put, that shit freaked me out. I went to the bedroom to wait for Haley, and I was actually shaking. I couldn’t believe it. An elderly woman that I thought I could have snapped in half if I had to scared the living hell out of me. That was enough to convince me it was time to go.

When Haley arrived, she sat down beside me and smiled. “Hey, babe,” she said. “Welcome home.” She leaned in to give me a kiss, and I almost recoiled. I know it was late in the day and she probably didn’t realize it, but her breath was unbearable. It smelled like something was rotting in the back of her mouth.

But I didn’t want to get off-track. I just clenched my jaw, gave her a quick kiss, and leaned back on the bed to get some breathing room. “Listen,” I said, “I think we ought to look for another place to stay.”

As soon as I said it, I could see that she was surprised. Almost offended. “What? Why?”

“Well, honestly, I just don’t like it here. I know you want to save money right now, but Layla makes me uncomfortable.”

A look of disgust twisted her lips. “I didn’t think she even talked to you.”

“That’s true,” I agreed. “But she follows me around and watches me. It’s fucking weird.”

Haley rolled her eyes. “Come on. Don’t be dramatic.”

“I’m not! Just now, I was looking for you and when I was about to go into the sitting room, she shouted at me from the end of the hall. I didn’t even know she was there.”

Her eyes widened in surprise.

I continued, “I mean, literally the second I was about to open the door. And she *knew* I was looking for you. I was calling your name! She knew where you were, but instead of saying something, she just watched me and waited. Like, what the hell is that?”

For a moment, Haley didn’t respond. She just looked at me with big eyes that looked twice as wide in the darkness. Then she finally said, “You *know* you’re not allowed in there.”

“God,” I huffed as I threw up my hands. “Why is *that* the only thing you heard?” I was about ready to have another fight. Ever since we moved in, I had been working double shifts and hardly ever got to see Haley. And when I did see her, she was either sleeping or on her way to do something else. Usually something with Layla.

And I suppose my frustration was obvious, because Haley’s expression softened. She relaxed her posture and placed a hand on my leg. “It’s not,” she said. “I understand why you’re upset. Layla’s just very particular, and since she doesn’t know you, she keeps an eye on you. But I know that doesn’t make it any less creepy.”

I nodded and replied, “So can we move out?”

She pursed her lips and looked at the floor. It was obvious that she didn’t want to, but after a minute of thinking, she said, “Tell you what. We really can’t afford much, but if you can find a new place to stay for cheap, I’ll work with you on it.” Then she looked up at me with a sweet smile. “Fair?”

A wave of relief flowed over me as I answered, “Fair.”

I didn’t love the idea of having to find a new place to stay that was competitively priced during COVID all on my own, but I was sure as hell motivated. At the end of our conversation, she and I had our first night of fun since the week that we moved in.

Although, as I mentioned, I couldn’t tolerate her breath. I didn’t want to ruin the mood by telling her, so I just redirected her. When her mouth was low enough, I couldn’t smell a thing. I finished and fell asleep thinking things were on the right track… but what I saw in the bathroom the next morning almost made me puke.

I swear to God there were smears of compost between my legs.



For the next week or two, other than work, my sole focus was looking for a new place to live. I even asked a few of my old friends who I had lived with before I met Haley. One of them said he’d be fine with me staying with him for a while, but he just didn’t have room for two people. In reality, I think he just didn’t know Haley well enough to feel comfortable with her living there, but I get that.

The whole time I was doing my part, Haley continued to spiral. At least that’s how I saw it. It was a slow enough process that, at first, I didn’t quite notice the subtle changes. But at a certain point, they became too obvious not to see.

As I mentioned earlier, her sleep schedule had changed since the day we moved in. But by now, the only time I saw her awake was at night. And since she never got any sunlight, her skin was paler and her hair became a tangled mess. I don’t think I looked too hot either—how many of us did during that time?—but for her, it was just so out of character.

Her breath always reeked, her eyes became glassy, and she never seemed to be focused on anything other than what was going on in the house. I’m ashamed to say it, truly, as it makes me look like an inattentive ass of a boyfriend, but I didn’t even realize she stopped taking her online classes.

I figured she was still going to McDonald’s when I was at work or sleeping. But one day when I was at work, I had one of those fleeting thoughts that turn out to be a revelation. I remembered seeing a few papers and a book sitting on top of her laptop that morning. And I knew that they were in the exact same position they had been in at least a week earlier.

That led to me realizing that she wasn’t taking her classes. Which meant she wasn’t doing *anything* other than hanging out with Layla. Haley was far too productive, far too focused, to do something like that.

And that’s when the obvious explanation finally came to me.

Haley was on drugs, and Layla was her dealer.

I felt like such an idiot for not seeing it earlier. That was why they were both so secretive. It was the reason that Haley stopped caring about her education, her finances, her appearance, her hygiene. She was getting high all night and sleeping it off all day. That had to have been what she and Layla were doing in that damned “sitting room” all the time.

And I got mad. Like *really* mad.

Not at Haley. I knew plenty of guys that went through that shit. Addiction is no joke, and I know better than to blame the addict. But I was beyond pissed at Layla. For hiding it from me. And for taking advantage of Haley, who was on an amazing path before that bitch pulled her off course.

I could hardly focus on work for the rest of my shift, but I had less than two hours left so I powered through them. When I finished up at ten PM, I drove back to the drug den I had been calling home for nearly three months and went inside. I knew I had to keep my cool, but I was fuming.

Unsurprisingly, Haley was not in bed. Since it was nighttime, I knew she was probably getting high somewhere else. I never found out where she had been last time I was looking for her, but I decided to check out every “permitted” area before touching the doorknob to the “sitting room” again. That way, if Layla tried to tell me Haley was in the garden or something, I would know she was lying.

I walked through every room I had been shown in the initial tour, and in each one, I was met with cool stillness. The closest thing to movement was a single lit candle in the dining room. It sat behind a dirty serving tray, casting small shadows around residual bits of slimy meat.

It stunk. But whoever had eaten it wasn’t in the room.

I even checked outside by the garden. No one.

On my way back inside, I was headed for the sitting room when I remembered the basement. Nothing was down there but dried herbs and gardening supplies, but it was the last place I hadn’t checked.

At night, the basement was pitch black. There wasn’t a single window, so not even a sliver of light could sneak in. So, using my phone’s flashlight, I went down the stairs and looked around. I kept my breaths as shallow as possible, trying not to let the overwhelming odor get the best of me. It wasn’t a very large space, but the shelves and chests made it feel cluttered. As I moved my flashlight, dozens of shadows moved with it.

“Haley?” I whispered. “Are you down here?”

As I turned, I thought a shadow moved on its own. I spun back and shined my light on a wooden chest. There were shelves on either side of it, each filled with differently shaped jars that contained various mystery materials. But no movement. No Haley.

I moved the light to the back right corner, where the liquid compost was stored. Unlike the last time I saw it, it wasn’t filled to the brim. It was less than half full, and splotches of the brown goop dotted the floor and walls around it.

I was just about ready to give up and head upstairs when I heard what sounded like something scraping softly against the stone. I immediately pointed the light toward the sound, and I found myself staring at the same chest as before. Only this time, I was certain I saw movement. From behind the chest, on the floor, I saw skin. A foot or a hand slid out of view as soon as the light touched it.

Despite the awful stench, my breaths became fuller and faster. “Haley?” I asked again.

No reply.

I slowly stepped toward the chest, the cold of the stone permeating my socks and making the soles of my feet ache. As I grew nearer, I could see tangled hairs sticking up from behind the chest’s lid. And as the light bent over the top of the chest, the person there tried to shrink, to disappear into the shadowy space between the wood and stone.

Only a few feet away, I leaned to the left where I had seen the hand or foot vanish and filled the crevice with light.

My phone nearly slipped out of my hand when I saw her.

There was Haley, crouched with her arms and legs folded in toward her body. Her head was arched at an angle so she could peer back at me. Had her shallow eyes, which sunk into her pallid face, not been tethered to me, I might’ve thought she was dead.

“Haley,” I murmured through a fearful breath. I wanted to ask if she was okay, what she was doing, but I couldn’t say another word.

When our eyes first met, she looked like a terrified animal hiding in a bush. Wide eyes watching you, waiting to see if you spot them before they try to escape. But when I said her name, her face contorted.

Her eyes winced until they were nearly shut, and her mouth opened as her lips twisted into an almost unrealistically deep frown. Then a deep, bawling groan rolled out of her as she removed herself from her hiding spot.

“*Carter*,” she whined through a sob, “*I didn’t mean to!*” Cries poured out of her like a sorry child, but she didn’t shed a tear.

She stepped around the chest, and I could see the liquid compost on her hands and smeared across her pajamas. She stretched her hands out toward me, as though pleading for a hug.

But I took a step back. And I’m sure that I failed to hide the fear that painted my face.

As she lowered her hands, she asked in the same distraught voice, “Are you mad at me?”

It took me a few seconds to muster up a reply. I didn’t know how to react. The most beautiful woman I had ever seen was a twisted mess, possessed by something I couldn’t make sense of.

I knew addicts who rooted through trash. I knew they ate some nasty shit too. But this… This wasn’t a drug.

“N-no,” I finally said. “I’m not mad. I just… I want you to come with me. Okay?” I reached out my free hand, and it trembled in the space between us. “We’re gonna get you taken care of. Come on.” I stepped aside so I could lead her upstairs.

I was going to take her straight to my car so we could drive to the hospital. She was scaring me, but I knew her. Deep down, my Haley needed me. And I wanted to be there for her. Unfortunately, things did not go as planned.

What happened next is a bit fuzzy to me… I know Haley stepped toward me, placed her hand in mine, and smiled. It was like every bit of fear or sadness or whatever emotion her face was trying to represent faded in an instant. And in its place was pure joy.

I remember feeling the way one might when cornered by a predator. They call it fight or flight, right? Well, my body wanted to fly far, far away at that moment. But I’m not sure what stopped me.

I know things went dark really fast. I think I dropped my phone. If I was super unlucky, maybe the battery happened to die; I’m not sure. Regardless, the next thing I knew, I was being restrained. I felt cold stone on my arms. At one point, it was pressed against my cheek. I tried to yell I think, but something was wrapped around my throat. I couldn’t breathe. I could hardly move.

And then… nothing.

 

Regaining consciousness was a hazy process. I was in and out for days. I’d start to wake up, try to speak, try to move, but something kept stopping me. It was like I was trapped inside myself. Then things would fade back into blackness.

I could never tell if I was in a dream or truly awake. I’d hear voices—sometimes Haley, sometimes, I think, Layla—but I never got the full context of what they were talking about.

“No, we’ll just do a bit at a time.”

“But I want it all now!”

“Put your faith in me, child.”

One time, I just heard moaning and gnawing, like a glutton devouring their favorite meal.

Eventually, I opened my eyes, and I was back. I couldn’t taste or smell a thing; my mouth was terribly dry. But my entire body was stiff and sore. My head ached, my stomach was sour, and then I felt the worst pain I’d ever felt in my life.

My left leg throbbed like it was crushed under the weight of a semitruck. I tried to move it, but I couldn’t. I couldn’t move much at all.

I began to shift around, moaning and gasping as my eyes adjusted to the dark, unfamiliar room. When I tried to sit up, I was stopped by leather straps that held me in place. One was across my chest, two held down my hands, one was across my waist, and I figured there were two more around my ankles.

I started to scream. With each beat of my heart, the pain in my leg grew stronger. “Help!” I shouted. “*Please! Someone help me!*”

A door in the room opened, and I heard a familiar voice. “Hey, baby!” It was Haley. “Hey! It’s okay.” She appeared by my side and leaned close to me. She looked worse than before. Her hair was falling out. Her eyes looked faded. And bloodless lesions dotted her face.

“Don’t worry,” she said. “Everything’s okay.” She smiled at me, cracking her dry lips and revealing her yellow teeth.
