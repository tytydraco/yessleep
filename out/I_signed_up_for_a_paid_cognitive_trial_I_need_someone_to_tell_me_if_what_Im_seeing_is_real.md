I need money, that's the whole boring reason this started. Rent's late, my car needs a new alternator, and three weeks ago I saw a flyer taped to a corkboard at the campus library:

>**PARTICIPANTS NEEDED: Cognitive Enhancement Study**  
Healthy adults, 18-40. Six-week commitment. $4,200 compensation.  
Trial designation: **AG-7**  
Contact: Dr. L. Nera, Halloway Behavioral Research

I called. A woman with a flat, pleasant voice asked me a list of screening questions: sleep habits, family history, did I have any metal in my body, would I be willing to cut out caffeine. I said yes to everything because $4,200 is $4,200, and three days later I was sitting in a beige room signing a consent form that used the word "reversible" four times.

They gave me a weekly injection. Said it was a "neurotrophic compound," nothing more specific than that. I'm not a science person, so I didn't push.

I'm posting this because I want a timestamp on everything before it gets worse. I'll update as it happens.

**Day 1**

Nothing much. A little soreness at the injection site. Dr. Nera, who I still haven't actually met, it's always a nurse named Priya doing the shots, said effects "typically onset around day 4-6."

---

**Day 5**

Okay, something's happening. I did the crossword in the break room at work in eleven minutes. I have never finished a crossword in my life. My manager asked if I'd cheated.

Also, small thing: the vending machine on the 3rd floor has been stocked with the exact same five items in the exact same order for five days straight, and I only just noticed. Vending machines probably do get restocked in patterns, I just never paid attention before. I feel like I'm paying attention to everything now.

---

**Day 9**

I read a 400-page book in one sitting last night and I can still quote parts of it. Picked up a guitar I hadn't touched in years and by midnight I was playing scales clean. My coworker Danny told me I "sound like a different person," and he didn't say it like a compliment. More like it unsettled him a little.

I like it, if I'm honest. First time in my life I don't feel like I'm underwater.

---

**Day 12**

Small thing but it's bugging me. My neighbor across the hall, older guy, says the same thing every time we pass: "Cold one today, huh?" Rain, heat wave, doesn't matter what the weather actually is. "Cold one today, huh?"

I used to just say "yeah" and keep walking. Today I actually looked at him when he said it. His face did something for half a second that I don't have a good word for. Not confusion, more like something loading. Then he smiled and went into his apartment. 4C. Except I don't think 4C exists. I counted the doors on our floor once out of boredom and there are only five: 4A, 4B, 4D, 4E, and a service closet. Weird thing to fixate on, I know. Writing it down anyway.

---

**Day 15**

Longer update, sorry, a lot happened.

I've started noticing seams. That's the closest word I have for it. Places where the world looks stitched together instead of just being there.

The coffee shop I go to every morning has a bulletin board by the door: lost cat flyers, guitar lessons, a church rummage sale. I've walked past that board probably 400 times in three years and never once read it closely. Yesterday I read all of it. The rummage sale flyer says it's happening "this Saturday." I checked my memory against it, and it's said that on every single visit I can remember, going back months, through two actual Saturdays that came and went with no rummage sale that I ever noticed.

I brought it up to Priya at my next appointment, half joking, testing the water. She didn't laugh. She wrote something down and said, carefully, "That's a great observation, Marcus. Bring that one to Dr. Nera directly." I asked to meet Dr. Nera. She said Dr. Nera "prefers written correspondence during the active phase."

I looked up Halloway Behavioral Research that night. The address on my consent form is a UPS Store. The number I originally called to enroll is disconnected. But I have three appointments logged on my phone and I've physically walked into that beige room five times. The building is real. I was standing in the lobby an hour before I looked any of this up.

---

**Day 19, edit below**

I found the room.

My apartment is a one-bedroom. Lived here two years. Tonight I was digging around for a phone charger and opened the hall closet, the one that's always had a water heater and some winter coats in it, and there's a door at the back of it I have never seen before. Not new either. Old. The paint's chipped the same amount as the rest of the apartment, like it's always been there and I just never looked closely enough to see it.

I didn't open it. I'm typing this from my car in the parking lot. I couldn't tell you what I thought would happen if I did. I just couldn't make myself.

**Edit, two hours later:** went back in. Had to know if I imagined it. Door's still there. Put my hand on the knob and it was warm, like something had been leaning against the other side recently. Still didn't open it. Packed a bag, staying at Danny's tonight.

Keep thinking about the neighbor. Cold one today, huh. Every day, rain or shine, like a line of dialogue that never got a second draft. Starting to feel like I'm the only thing in a five block radius that's allowed to actually change.

---

**Day 23**

Haven't gone back to my apartment. Staying at Danny's, told him some vague story about a mold problem. He's letting me crash on his couch and honestly he's the only stable thing in my life right now, which is not a sentence I ever thought I'd say about Danny.

Skipped my day-22 injection. I can feel it already. Not withdrawal exactly, more like a light dimming down to half power. Things that were sharp a week ago are getting fuzzy again and I hate that it scares me, because a week ago I would've told you I wanted this whole thing to stop.

Went to the trial building anyway, not for the shot, just to get answers. Beige room was there. Priya was there. I asked her point blank what AG-7 stood for and she actually looked at me, really looked, for the first time, and said, "You're doing better than Six did. Six stopped noticing things by day fourteen." Then she caught herself, got very professional very fast, and told me my "affect was showing signs of study fatigue" and maybe I should take a rest week.

I asked who Six was.

She said, "We don't discuss other participants." Then, quieter, almost like it slipped out on its own: "He's back to baseline now. He's fine. He just doesn't remember any of this part."

I don't believe her.

**Day 26, edit**

Sorry this one's disjointed. Having trouble organizing my thoughts, which three weeks ago I would not have believed I'd ever say again. It's getting dim in here faster than it should if this were just the compound wearing off naturally.

Went back to my apartment today for more clothes. The door in the closet is gone. Not closed, gone. Water heater, coats, unbroken drywall, like it healed over.

I don't think it's gone because I stopped looking for it. I think it's gone because I stopped being able to see it.

**Day 29, final update I think**

Danny's typing this part because my hands aren't cooperating and my eyes keep sliding off the screen. He says I've been repeating myself for two days straight. Says I keep asking him if it's Saturday, and when he tells me it isn't, I say "okay" and ask again nine minutes later. I don't remember asking the first time. Don't remember most of the last four days at all, if I'm being honest, which he's telling me to be, because he thinks somebody should have this on record in case it happens to someone else.

He wants me to say I'm scared. Not sure I have that word anymore, not the way I used to mean it. Closer to watching a light get carried into another room while you're still standing in the dark one, and you can still see the doorway, and you already know the light isn't coming back through it.

Danny found a flyer in my coat pocket I don't remember taking. Rummage sale. Says it's happening this Saturday.

He's going to keep posting updates for me. Says if I go quiet for good, or start saying "cold one today, huh" to strangers in the hallway unprompted, like it's the only sentence I've got left, he wants someone, anyone, to have this on record.

**Update from Danny, 4 days later:** He's staying at his sister's place upstate now. He's calm. Genuinely calm, not the good kind. Doesn't ask about the trial anymore. When I brought it up he just smiled and said, "Cold one today, huh," then went back to eating his breakfast like he hadn't said anything at all.

I called Halloway Behavioral Research's number again this morning, one more try.

It's connected again. A woman with a flat, pleasant voice answered and asked if I was calling about the flyer.

She asked if I was 18 to 40. She asked if I had any metal in my body.

She asked if I'd be willing to cut out caffeine.
