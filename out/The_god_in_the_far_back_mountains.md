It's been 30 summers since Aneesha died. It's my 20th winter on this planet. Counting everything by seasons is something that is common in my family. When my family left India for here, they brought with themselves a strange tradition of counting time only by seasons. 

Dad, who is 55 autumns old, sits in the patio all day, reading whatever books he finds in the nearby town library, which he brings home. It's been 30 summers since mom smiled. I have never seen any other expression on her face except the one she wears everyday. Every night, she wails herself to sleep, something that hasn't changed since before I was born. Aneesha was my elder sister. She died during the same summer she was born. My parents had me 10 winters later. 

I live on a huge property that spans more than 300 acres.  I am rich, well my parents are. They love solitude more than anything, something they never get bored of. The nearest neighbor is 15 minutes away, leaving us alone with a silence that makes even a pin drop echo. On the backside of the property, where the eyes meet the horizon, they are visible. The far back mountains. 

Also lives with us my paternal grandfather, who went insane 25 summers ago, when he suddenly gouged his eyes out. He is kept in a room on the ground floor, after he managed to jump and fracture both of his legs from his first floor bedroom. Thought completely blind as a result of his psychotic breakdown, he loves to carve on whatever surface he seems to like, which is why there is an unlimited supply of wooden planks. He always carves one thing; the god he worships - the god in the far back mountains.

The god is a tall, extremely thin figure, at least 15 feet tall, with arms that reach down his hips, each fingernail a foot long. He has a thin, elongated skull, and from it protrude antlers that are 6 feet tall. The god always wears an expression one cannot read. Grandfather is an extremely talented artist, always carving the god with extreme precision and detailing. All the three thousand carvings look exactly the same. I am the caretaker of my insane grandfather, who seems to love no one in the world except me. He touches my face lovingly, and hugs me everyday, always asking for a good night kiss when I tuck him in bed. He loves to tell me about his god, how his god is the protector of our family, and that he watches us from the mountains. He says that his god is the lord of the far back mountains, where every living creature lives under his rule. He says that the mountains are a paradise, which is why no human could ever explore them. He says his god is a very protective one, never letting harm come his followers' way. 

My mom hates my grandfather, and whenever he comes into the living room, screaming praises of his long antlered god, mom cries until he goes away. She always asks him why his god couldn't save Aneesha. My mom stopped living the same day Aneesha did. She is physically alive, that's it.

 I saw the god in my dreams several times. He looks exactly like grandfather's carvings, except his bright red eyes, and the sheer majestic nature of his antlers, both of which cannot be carved. They are not mere antlers, but a crown, signifying his greatness as the absolute lord of the far back mountains. Grandfather always asks me every morning whether I saw his god in my dreams the previous night. 

Mom hates me; she always did. Whenever she looks at me, she only sees the futile attempt of my father to forgo his grief. Dad made her pregnant in hopes of both of them leaving the past behind, and live again, something that failed disastrously. Maybe it is because I look like the male version of Aneesha (something my mother uttered a million times whenever she cries), or maybe because my dad forced her into birthing me when she wanted to abort. The fact that never changed is that both of them never let go of Aneesha. 

Since last week, my grandfather didn't sleep for a second.  He attempted to commit suicide yesterday, and attacked my mom today. We knocked him out medically. Even when he was drifting away into sleep, he kept repeating the same words - "The god doesn't look like this, the god doesn't look like this". I do not blame him, all his carvings since the last week showed a different god - a god that is 10 feet tall, his hands covered in fresh blood, his face covered in blood too, and his expression so hungry. The most noticeable change is his antlers - they are now taller than his body, at least 20 feet tall. I don't blame my grandfather for losing it.

Grandfather became too insane now. He is far too gone to remember that it is his fault Aneesha died. That the only way to escape the curse early on is by killing the family member you loved the most. Grandfather killed Aneesha when she was 2 months old in your terms. In that brief span, he loved her more than he loved anyone on this earth. He was tired from the curse slowly killing him, and in his desperation, killed her to bypass the curse, but he forgot that my father would inherit it. 9 generations ago, a forefather asked for wealth and material prosperity for our family that would last as long as earth, in exchange for the soul of every patriarch from each generation. The curse turned every patriarch into the horrific "god with antlers", a monster that fed on any flesh, always craving more and more each passing day - an appetite that only grows. No further details exist today, all lost when my grandfather burned down his ancestral home in a rage, and moved here with the family. 

He knew that sacrificing the most loved family member would neutralize the curse for that individual patriarch, which is why he gave up Aneesha. My father fell victim to the curse, and chose the far back mountains as his hunting grounds each night. My grandfather, too insane to remember anything properly, created his own story where the antlered monster - my dad, is a god protecting our family. Mom does not know the truth, she went insane too. Father forced her to birth me because the curse must continue on. She hates me for looking like Aneesha; my grandfather thinks that I'm Aneesha. Never in my life did he call me Rajeev, my birth name. He forgot that he killed elder sister, and tells me that I am of age now, and I must be married off to a capable man. 

It's the 8th day since dad died, tomorrow will be 9th day from the day I killed him. Dad was too tired, too guilty, too remorseful. He hated living, he couldn't live a life where his father murdered his daughter, where he forced his wife for an heir, where his only son has to see all this. So I granted him mercy, a *coup de grâce.* Now I am the antlered god my grandfather worships. My dad says the antlers represent resolve. 

I will kill my grandfather, and my mom tonight. They have suffered enough. Every sunrise turns me into a human again, which I am not. I will release all of my family from their suffering, and will kill every single one who carries my surname. After I purge them out of goodwill, I will travel to a land where sun never rises, where there is no flesh to eat, nothing to satiate my hunger. I will either die one day, from starvation; or live eternally starving. Either way, the family won't have another heir. I'll make sure that I'm the last patriarch. This ends with me. I am the god in the far back mountains, and my antlers represent my resolve.
