Our town's public pool is nothing special. Surrounded by a towering thick and springy chain link fence, the concrete rectangle ran about 30 feet by 60 feet, with a shallow end, middle area, and a deep end for the local kids and families to cool off.  The bathrooms were small and gross, and the vending machine snacks were always expired, but that didn't stop us kids from using both. Along the pool deck were about a dozen of the steel tables with seats directly attached to the table tops.

I don't know about all of you, but everyone I know has had a universal experience. They will fiddle with the openings, poking fingers, straws, or even food in between the holes on the waterproof tables, mostly as an act of mindless stimulation. Eventually, the finger goes too far in and gets wedged between the knuckle joints.

"Get me out, get me out!" I still remember the panic in my little brother's voice that summer ten years ago. Franky and I were sitting together when he got his finger stuck in one of the openings between the serpentine-like wiring of the metal tables by the public pool.

Something about the question of "will my hand fit through this?" captivates children. When it does get stuck momentarily, the twinge of fear that shoots down your spine is as real as ever. One might think "am I about to lose a finger?" or "I'll be trapped here forever" floods the mind in that millisecond before yanking the finger out back to safety.

Only with my brother, it didn't come out. My mother and the lifeguards had to call the fire department to safely cut around the finger. Franky's hand was saved, but his reputation in middle school was cemented with a new nickname, "fat hands".

The fear and embarrassment in that moment was overwhelming, but Franky was a confident person, more so than me. He never paid the cruel names any mind, or would just fight them after school if he got too pissed.

Despite the humiliation, Franky and I continued to frequent the public pool often. Not much else to do in a small town, and with the insanity of Minnesota winters, us kids had to enjoy every Summer day the best we could. Franky, myself, and the local kids would go all the time without supervision. Since our town was so small, no one suspected any dangers lurking outside the pool's gated grounds. Besides, the lifeguards and supervisors were there in case anything were to happen.

About a week or so after his accident, on a broiling day in late July, my brother went missing. I don't remember much, besides eating salty caramel with him and the other kids. I wish I could better describe the circumstances; one second we were all sitting together, and Franky fiddling with the holes. He even got his finger stuck again, panicked slightly, but yanked it out successfully this time. But the next second he was gone. Just vanished. I was so tired at that point that I figured he just got up to use the restroom. After 20 minutes, I began searching for him.

Once I failed to find him, I got the life guards help. The life guards searched everywhere, but when they failed, they got the police involved to search. Even my mother scoured the facility, tears trailing down her face as she inspected every crevice in the bathrooms, between the vending machines, and on the deck. He was nowhere near the water. It was like Franky was just plucked out of existence.

The search for Franky lasted for weeks, but with no new leads, the police were just about ready to call it off. My parents became lethargic, and as an older brother, the guilt I had for not keeping a closer eye on him began to weigh on me. I was supposed to protect my little brother, and now, I'll never be able to play marco polo with him again.

Things changed after Phillip, one of the local maintenance workers came forward with a lead. Well, more like a raving, according to what all our parents told us. He was screaming about the water, over and over again. Phillip was known as a diligent worker around town, but mentally dissident from everyone else. Mom said he always had a 'condition', while my dad just called him a 'deadbeat druggie'.

But given the circumstances and general distrust towards him, Phillip was brought in as a prime suspect.

The trial didn't last long. My parents didn't take me to Phillip's prosecution, all I knew was that the jury unanimously convicted Phillip for kidnapping my brother. The details were always murky, but talking about it today only upsets my mother. I know how this looks about our town, but given the circumstances, I can't blame her. How else can you make sense of your own child just disappearing one day?

The town needed an easy answer, and Phillip was the easiest to blame. My family wasn't looking for justice, only catharsis knowing someone paid for their little boy's death.

\--

For the past three months this Summer, I've been working at the public pool as a full-time lifeguard. I'm back from University this summer and need extra cash ahead of the next semester for parties, food, alcohol, you name it. While the pool wasn't my first choice, I made pretty good money after getting my certification to look after the Lance University swim meets and decided $15 an hour just to watch runts run around would be a relatively easy gig. Mom and dad were apprehensive, but they understood that a job is a job. Even if it opens old wounds.

Let me tell you, being a lifeguard is boring. Sitting around all day, staring out at the same 20 faces, and occasionally shouting at kids to stop fighting or splashing is the crux of what I do. It'd be easier if I could listen to podcasts or music on the seat, but regulation requires 100% attention with no distractions.

The staff overall is pretty nice. Mark is the pool's supervisor, paid by the city. After Phillip, the town employed various servicemen to support the pool's operations. Since they were also paid by the town, the servicemen are often late to properly survey and clean the water's quality. I learned how to measure and adjust pools' pH levels while at university, so I often have to step in to do their jobs for them. Lazy assholes, at least Phillip knew what he was doing.

Aside from them, it's just myself and a handful of part time and full time life guards. I was the oldest of the group. Most of the other guards were part-timers from High School.

The closest person I could call a friend was Ray. Ray and I went to high school together, but never really talked. Different cliques and all that. While I was busy with water polo, Ray was busy studying for the knowledge bowl. He even made nationals his senior year, which is honestly more impressive than anything I've ever accomplished.

We got to talking, and it turns out Ray and I have a lot more in common than I thought. We enjoy scary movies, the same books, and even the same League of Legends champion (I know, it's cringe). He also lost his older sister to leukemia when he was in Elementary School.

"I remember the town losing it after Franky went missing," Ray'd tell me at the end of one of our lunch breaks, "I didn't realize he was your brother though. I'm sorry for what you had to go through."

It was nice to be seen by someone who understood. My parents are supportive, of course, but it's hard to help one another when you're all a part of the same proverbial sinking ship. We don't talk about it much, but I wonder if my parents ever had someone like that in their lives.

\--

The first few weeks went as expected. Sitting around, training the part-timers, chatting with Ray, and so on. It was Wednesday, and with parents busy with work and no care for homework, the pool was packed. At least, more packed than usual.

Around noon was the peak of the pool's business, and I kept counting the kids around the pool. One, two, three...twenty five. Yes, twenty five kids swimming around. No struggling, no crying, just good old fun on a sunny day. The children's shrill yells of excitement splintered my brain like a nail in ply wood. Despite the annoyance, it reminded me of how Franky yelled before diving in.

Sitting in that chair requires you to pass the time creatively in your mind. I've found making up stories about strangers is the easiest pastime while still keeping a stern look out for signs of drowning. The group of kids playing sharks and minnows will one day be olympic athletes competing for team USA. The fat kid with the swimshirt doing laps is working out to stay healthy, maybe one day becoming a famous actor. The young girl reading by the poolside is collecting ideas to inspire her own national best seller.

*There was always something so...delightful about them.* I thought to myself, before snapping back to my reality.

Pretending that this place plays a part of their origin story gives more meaning to this crappy pool, and I get to protect them. All 24 of them.

*Wait, that can't be right*, I thought to myself. *Did I miscount?*

*Who's missing? I didn't see anyone get out.*

I got up to inspect the water. On the left-hand side, the group of Olympians had a puzzled look on their face, squirming around in silence looking stern, muttering the name '*Kevin*' between themselves. One of the boys approached me.

"Hey, there's no actual shark in this pool, right?"

I felt my brow ease. Another stupid kids game.

"Not since I last checked," I sighed. "Just be sure he doesn't catch you minnows."

The young girl next to him looked up at me, snot spilling from her nostrils after pool water ran up her nose.

"Kevin said sometimes pools have sharks, even though they can't breathe. He was scared, he said the shark got into the water and was after him."

I jumped in the pool to look around. I gave Ray a signal letting him know to be on high alert. With my goggles on I observed each corner but only found the small forest of kicking legs and darting hands of children fighting to stay above water. No Kevin.

Coming back up for air, I softly asked the group "Are you sure he didn't just leave to use the restroom?" They shook their heads.

I could feel the aching bubble of anxiety rise in my chest. *There is no way a kid in a pool this size would go missing on my watch,* I thought. *Not again.*

After calling a water break to remove everyone from the pool, Ray and I got to work. Employing the help from Kevin's friend group, we searched everywhere. Ray searched the perimeter outside the pool grounds to check for any openings while I searched the interior with Mark. I searched in the bathroom stalls, behind the vending machines, underneath the tables and chairs, even the mess room where the lifeguards take their breaks, but no luck. Kevin was gone. The search went on for about an hour before swimmers began getting antsy and calling their parents.

The static truth is, whether I want to admit it or not, a child went missing on my watch. I knew what this story would do to residents, let alone my employment. Unlike Phillip, I'd get ahead of the game and call the police, but before I pressed the call button, I turned to the children one last time.

"What is the last thing you remember about Kevin, any marks, anything at all that might help us find him?"

I could see the shimmering sea of cars filling the parking lot just beyond the gates. A few parents are bolting towards the entrance, their faces already filled with distress knowing someone's child is gone. A police siren roaring in the distance, approaching.

The group of children looked at each other to recollect their thoughts, then back at me.

"He was really afraid of the shark. He said it came towards him, and that it tasted salty."

"What do you-?"

The little girl next to him interjected, "It tasted funny. Like caramel."

\--

Kevin, age 9, went missing at the local public swimming pool. Last seen by his friends wearing maroon swim trunks with blue shark details.

As you'd expect, the whole town went into a frenzy. It took a few days for the police reporting to finish. Of course, Kevin's family accused the manager of not maintaining the pool's security, citing negligence, but the cops couldn't find anything that would suggest otherwise. After the incident with my brother, the town was scrutinous in protecting the pool's safety standard, and having worked with Mark for two months, I can tell you confidently that he took his job seriously.

Modern cameras were installed, and while Mark, Ray, and myself personally reviewed the tapes in addition to the police, no one could tell what happened to the boy.

Looking from the back left pillory into the corner of the pool, the video footage showed a group of children playing and splashing in the pool.

Kevin was a blonde haired, skinny, wearing navy blue swim trunks. He was there amongst his friends, splashing a little harder than his friends, when he ducks under the water never to go back up. I approached the kids about a minute later when they started jolting their heads around searching.

Somehow, Kevin had vanished from the face of the Earth. But with history repeating itself, the police had a lead of where to search. The pool's filtration system hasn't changed much these past few years. It is a massive cylindrical iron orb filled with gravel, sand, and gallons of dirty pool water.

The police found no evidence of the boy's body this time, but servicemen found an unusually high amount of chlorine in the filtration system waters. This revealed a hypothesis that the higher levels of Chlorine in the pool could have exacerbated Kevin's prior heart conditions, killing him in the process. While I suppose it made sense medically, it still doesn't explain what happened to the body.

\--

Because of these events, the pool closed its doors for the remainder of the Summer. I was out of a job, and the growing hate mail from Kevin's extended family was weighing on me.

I was at my wits end, when my mother came to comfort me.

"I know it's not your fault, Rory," she whispered to me, hugging my arm tightly as she rested her head on my shoulder. "They just don't understand. These are simple folk, they just are looking for someone to blame. A boogieman."

"Oh, so I'm just another Phillip then?" I darted, raising my voice out of frustration. Did she think that would help my anxiety, my guilt, my regret? "If that's the case, then they might as well lock me away for Franky too?"

I could feel the tension in her face when I spat these words, like acid on skin.

"That was a different son. We couldn't..."

She paused, before looking down at her feet.

"I know you blame yourself. But trust me when your father and I tell you that it was beyond your control. Phillip was a bad man, honey, we just didn't want you to feel like a victim, and..."

"What?" I blurted, angrily, "What the hell are you talking about?"

Tears welled up in her eyes. Her voice quivered when she let out two words: "Salted Caramel."

Confused, my anxiety became bewilderment.

"We thought Phillip was just being nice. He didn't have much since his incident, you see. We knew Phillip attended festivals back in the seventies and eighties, and the acid fried his brain. Frankly, it was a miracle he could hold a job at all. I don't know what he took back in those days, but the damage to his brain was irreversable. He never really stopped hallucinating, you see, that's why he was always so strange. Some kind of disorder, it's...irreversible Dr. Casey told to the court that day. But he was always so kind to you kids, we didn't think..."

She paused, stammering her breath.

"We didn't think he'd try to hurt any of you."

My bewilderment turned into dread. I don't know much about Phillip, but dark thoughts raced across my mind waiting for her next sentence.

"Son, if anyone failed that day it was us. The salted caramel...he laced it the day Franky went missing."

My whole life, the weight of my brother's disappearance had dragged me down. The guilt I've wracked up since that day has motivated nearly my every decision in some way. *Why would my parents let me believe that? Why didn't they say anything sooner*, I thought to myself.

"What the fuck..." I muttered.

"That's why you got sick. That's why during the trial, none of the kids were questioned--they couldn't remember anything. Once we got Phillip to testify, his words no longer made sense. He said he was everywhere, and that he was a part of them, and that Franky..."

She choked violently on her spit. I couldn't blame her, I was so light headed from this news that I was just about disassociating.

"'Franky was the first to find him', he told the court. Your father nearly threw a chair at Phillip when he said that in that courtroom, the police had to escort him. Please son, some monsters don't have reasons, and even if they did, would you really want to know what they were? It's best not to think of it. It's just not."

*--*

The revelation that night made me realize three things; one, that the town's hatred of Phillip would far surpass Kevin’s parents' hatred towards me, two, my folks would never tell me the full story that day, and three, Phillip may be the only one who knew the whereabout's of my brother. If I wanted to get answers about Franky and Kevin, I'd have to confront Phillip personally. I left the house, telling my folks I was going to Minneapolis to hang out with a friend over the weekend.

I did just that last week. I found through the judicial system site that Phillip was still residing in a control unit facility near Oak Heights. The complex was large, with egg-shell colored walls lining the hallways.

I petitioned the prison for a visitation to meet with Phillip. Though Kevin’s case was ongoing, the police had no reason to interview Phillip given his incarceration. But I had every reason.

Maybe it was self-serving, maybe I just wanted to yell, blame, and scream at. But if I didn't confront this man, It would weigh me down for the rest of my life.

The guards waved me in. "Good luck getting anything out of him, kid. This guy's been mostly nonverbal since being locked up in this joint."

The guards took me to a room lined with clear windows with a microphone connected on each side to speak through. The guards sat me down at window 11, where four minutes later they brought out Phillip to sit in front of me.

They say the windows are the eyes to the soul. If that is the case, then Phillip's soul points in two opposite directions. It was difficult to tell if he really was looking at me or through me. I was about to speak, when he gasped out.

"Caramel!"

The word curled around my brain. Did he see me as a past victim? How could he even remember me after all these years?

"So you know why I'm here then?"

"Caramel."

Ok, maybe he wasn't all there. Perhaps my parents were right. Maybe this man was just some acidhead whose brain had decayed over the years from abuse and mental decline.

I tried asking him again.

"A boy went missing again. At the pool. Did you know that?"

Phillip stared at me for a second, silently muttering "cara-caramel."

His name was Kevin. I knew him. I knew his family. They think my boss and I are to blame, but he vanished just like my brother. I know you took him, you sick freak. I also know you knew that pool better than most. Hell, they still use your maintenance notes in the guidebooks. So I'm asking you, what happened to the boys?"

Phillip's eyes glowed with a newfound life. "C-cun-notes?" The guards faced shifted, almost surprised.

I sighed calmly. "That's right, Phillip."

Maybe yelling at him wasn't productive. I tried changing my tone.

"I know what you did to us that day. Drugs in the candy you gave out, is that right? We all loved the candy you gave us. What was it about Franky that drew you to him?"

I was expecting the worst, as much as I prayed it wouldn't be. As much as I hated to think about what this man could have done to my brother, I needed to at least hear him out. Maybe there would be some clues to his ramblings.

To my surprise, Phillip's voice resonated with profound clarity. It was almost hypnotic, like you'd hear for one of those smooth talking animal documentaries. Each word drew me in closer to him.

"you were all just so...precious. Being here and there and across the town gave me insights to those who could see, who could *feel* the echoes of consciousness in the air. The drugs, the infection of these parameters, waived around us since we were born. I was only trying to be free, and I found my peace. But it was so lonely, Rory. I wandered throughout the years with this static in my head. It became clear when working at the pool gave me purpose. I could see, I could feel again, but the static remained. Separate clouds move together in the storm, Rory. Those like me, with this affliction, connected with me. Think about it, electrons flow too and grow between the space between worlds and atoms. Synapses link, and sensations are shared in tandem not just within you but the world around. Trees are connected to the earth, its roots communicating to one another. Two dogs hunting the animal don't talk, they attack. Birds fly with magnetism, all from learning to communicate, no, contact through their mind. To those of us with the vision, sight and sensory become one, even from miles away. Projection. We are the orchard, Rory. We just have to see our own roots."

I couldn't wrap my head around anything Phillip was saying. Thank god I was recording this on my phone, otherwise recollecting all of it would have been a nightmare. Phillip continued,

"I'm lonely, god, I'm so lonely. Why don't they love me?" Phillip sobbed gently, tears welting up in his eyes.

"It is blinding. Lashing out, not at reaction, but towards sensation. Potency was the key, the right amount of LSD would help me find you and your friends. I remember Franky. I remember the sweetness of his thoughts. The sudden spark of fear lured me like a bright sour candy in a tunnel of darkness, begging to be devoured. Delicious, the best, better than all the others, yes. Yours was plain, but in a good way. Like caramel. Yes, yes! Caramel, you found me!"

He lunged at the window, the guards rushing over to detain him.

"The calling is still there! I can feel your thoughts still, I can find you! The candy never left your system, did it? Just like the others!"

And with that, my visit with Phillip came to a close. Connected? What did he mean by that? The confusion was silenced only by my disgust.

My brother was 'delicious', he says.

\--

I went to bed that night feeling lightheaded. What else could I have expected seeking clear answers from the unexplainable, from the insane? *What a wash,* I thought to myself.

The cool air last night was rare for August nights. I walked up to close my window, when my head began to ache further. I hadn't eaten that much, so perhaps the brain fog was simply exhaustion.

There must have been something the police hadn't noticed. That *I* hadn't noticed.

Needing to placate my dismay, I rode my bike back to the public pool. I'm sure it looks like someone returning to the scene of the crime, but I didn't care, the aching memories needed to be placated. Even if it was for my own mental assurance.

The black chained gates loomed over me. Climbing over them was no easy task, but I was able to land safely on the other side.

The pool had been drained since then. The granite pit laid before me with such brittleness, it was difficult to imagine that just weeks before it was a place full of joy and excitement. The concrete slabs laid across the pool's perimeter like headstones.

I walked to the shallow end of the pool and stepped in. The pool grew deeper every ten feet or so, and I began my inspection of the walls and corners.

It took me an hour or so, but just like that day with Kevin, there was nothing but clean granite walls.

I laid down flat in the barren deep end, staring up at the stars. I thought that if this was Franky's final resting place, the view from down below was at least a pleasant one.

I cried. For the first time in forever, I let out my guilt and misery, my sobs and pained howls echoing between the firm walls of the pool. The dread that's been hanging over me ten years ago finally switched to a sensation of dismay, anger, and terror. A terror that all in all, my brother was mere accidents. There was no real rhyme or reason. Just unlucky timing and a man who was barely in control of his own thoughts. The fear of loss knowing that by failing to help Kevin, I'd never know my brother's true fate. Dread. Pure, unfiltered, dread.

I share this knowing that some of you readers understand the aftermath of such a sensation. Despite its weight, grieving and fear gives a sense of clarity in the mind. To me, it came like a gust of wind.

The sense of momentary peace is all it took for me to realize what was spilling out aside from me.

The filtration inlets that shot the water back out into the pool were drooling out what I could only describe here as some kind of hideous mist. I could see it, but its form and color is nothing that one could possibly replicate. The anti-chromatic miasma collapsed in on itself, rolling and vibrating viciously as it slowly creeped its malleable form towards me. The shape slithered closer towards me. It wasn't so much seeing as it was sensing it. I could almost taste it. It was sweet, salty. Like caramel.

*Projection*, I remembered.

I stood up to run, but the miasma whipped its appendage out, blocking my path towards the shallow end. I had nowhere else to go but back against the wall. The pool ladder was high, but I could just barely reach the last step. I hung there with my feet dangling as the foul mist pooled the floor below me, inching closer up towards the wall.

I shirked my arms up each ladder step until I could get my footing, vaulting myself forward onto the drab concrete of the pool deck.

I turned back around to peer into the lambent abyss. I vibrated and waved, a sea of psychic synapses filled with physical malice simmered at the bottom of the pool. Though I couldn't see it in the normal sense, I could sense its shape--seething, straining with malice from the mind of a perverted man. The shape inched its way upwards towards me. I could *feel* what this was. Phillip never truly left the pool.

In a burst of anger, I grabbed the closest thing near me and hucked it into the miasma - a five foot plastic pool skimmer. The skimmer whispered through the mist, but instead of a loud *thud,* there was only silence. It was like the skimmer just vanished, or as thought it kept falling to whatever was through the haze.

The miasma stopped its approach. In fact, it stopped moving entirely, like wet cement settling. Ripples formed, and rising out from its mysterious depths was a shape. It was hard to tell at first, but after my eyes settled, I could tell from the maroon coloring that it had to be Kevin's bathing suit.

"You son of a bitch." I said. Or, at least I think I said. If I could sense this thing, maybe it could sense my hate towards it.

I wish there was some mystical fight, where I slay the demon and save my brother. Save Kevin. But I'm no hero. I ran.

*We are the orchard. I could* hear it echoing inside me. *I found your roots.*

\--

I haven't told anyone what happened that night. I don't know if sharing my experience here interferes with the investigation or not, I don't care. Perhaps I'm just going crazy. But I can still hear that voice calling, mocking me. I don't know if Phillip is still searching for me, but I don't feel his presence any more. So long as I am alive, my thoughts put a target on my back.

I don't know what is happening. I don't know what Phillip did to us back then, but it opened something in our minds. It made us vulnerable. I don't want to feed into a madman's antics, but if what he shared was true, then he could target those who've been 'awakened'.

I'm not looking for help. But I need to know what was beyond the veil of that mist. It must lead somewhere with answers. I need to know. I need to explore it.
