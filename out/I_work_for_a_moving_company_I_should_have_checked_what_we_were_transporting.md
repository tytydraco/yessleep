“Yo, Mick.”

I groaned as I stretched out, attempting to relieve the ache in my back that was caused by falling asleep in an inhospitable position.

“Mick. Mickey man. Mick the mouse. You with me?”

“I’m awake. What do you want, Beav?” I groggily rubbed my face and cracked my neck. Earlier, I had closed my eyes when the sun was just beginning to set. Now the clock on the dashboard read a quarter past eight and the sun had long since dipped below the horizon.

The lulling hum of the moving truck purred as we coasted down a remote highway. It was a single ship in the night, lost in a sea of darkness. I curled my legs in and tried to shake out the numbness of the arm that I had fallen asleep on. It was unusual for me to sleep when we traveled, but I usually had better company than just Beaver.

The two of us and our third pal, Jake, work for Beaver’s uncle. We provide a service for people who are looking to move a lot of shit across the country but can’t be bothered to do it themselves. It’s mindless physical labor and lots of travel, but it pays well. Especially when Jake ditches last minute and I can claim a larger slice of the payout. The worst part about Flakey Jake bailing on us is that it meant that I would be trapped alone in the truck with Beaver for an extended period of time.

“Can you pull up your maps? All these roads are starting to look the same to me. Let’s try and get back on course here.”

I let out a resigned sigh. Beaver was the type of guy to feel emasculated by navigating without the assistance of the GPS. He was also the type of guy to lack the skills to follow interstate signs.

Despite my frustration, I pulled out my phone without commenting. He got enough flak from the rest of his family. I wasn’t immune to his tiresome behavior, but I also wasn’t going to beat him into the dirt over it either. Besides, once you spend enough time with someone, it’s difficult not to develop a fondness for them.

“No signal.” I let my phone fall into my lap.

“Did you try Google Maps or Waze?”

“It doesn’t matter what app I tried if I have no connection, Beav. Why don’t you pull over? I think I can get us back to the main highway.”

“No. No, I got it. I’ll get us there. You know that you aren’t allowed to drive my uncle’s truck.”

I was actually allowed to drive the truck. I think that his uncle actually preferred when I did, but it was something that Beaver seemed to be adamant about.

I put my head against the cold window, feeling the vibrations of the road with every bump. It was so soothing that I considered closing my eyes again.

“Alright well, pull over the next time you see a rest stop. I wouldn’t mind grabbing a coffee and maybe we could use their WiFi to get reconnected.”

“Roger that, chief.”

Beaver reached for his can of dipping tobacco that he kept in the cupholder. He took a wad of the disgusting product and put it in his mouth. After several long seconds he cranked down his window and began to spit performatively.

I was pretty sure that Beaver didn’t even like the stuff. I couldn’t prove it, but I had the sense that it was one of those things that he did because he thought it would make him look cool, not because he enjoyed it.

Based on how long I had been out, I guessed that we were probably somewhere in Missouri or Arkansas. This particular job was almost a full coast to coast ordeal. Which meant the rest of my weekend would be spent in this truck with a guy named after his two front teeth.

“Alas, an exit! There is hope!” Beaver exclaimed as he navigated the truck gracefully off the highway. We took a ramp that steered us deeper into rural countryside.

The moving van’s headlights cast an eerie glow on the empty road. I’ve done this exact type of drive dozens of times before, but there was always something unsettling about getting off the highway. The roads are smaller and the trees on the side are closer, creating a suffocating atmosphere.

“Oh, that’s nasty.” Beaver spit out another mouthful of dip as he skirted around a roadkill deer. Its neck was twisted, causing its head to bend over its back and it only had a single full antler. There were stains of dark blood on the pavement, indicating that the kill was fresh. Beaver slowed as we passed the carcass, giving me more time than I needed to wince at the grotesque sight.

“Did it say how close till the rest stop?”

“Hmm?” Beaver asked, turning his head towards me.

“When you took the exit and you saw the marker for food and gas, did it say how many miles?”

Beaver tapped his fingers on the steering wheel.

“I uh… I think two?”

“You didn’t see it.”

“I didn’t see it.” He admitted.

A few more moments passed, the silence filled with the low hum of the truck.

“Think we should turn back?” He asked, an utter lack of confidence in his voice.

I picked up my phone, checking again for a signal. Still no such luck.

“Ehh, just keep going. There is bound to be something soon.”

I had purposefully downloaded several offline games for occasions like this. I opened a simple bubble-pop color matching game and allowed myself to get lost into the addictive sights and sounds. I was overly fixated on my game when Beaver broke my concentration.

“Hey Mick, there’s another one.”

I felt the truck slow down as I glanced up. My skin went cold when I saw the same roadkill deer on the side of the road. Its neck was bent backwards and it had the same missing antler.

“Beav, that’s the same one. Did you turn around? Or did we drive in a circle?”

Beaver laughed in a way that would most accurately be described as a wheezing chortle.

“I think I would know if I drove in a circle. That ain’t the same deer. There are plenty of those stupid bastards around here that like to get themselves pancaked.”

I stared at the decaying body as we drove slowly by. Despite all of Beaver’s misguided confidence, I was not convinced.

“Well, just be careful then.”

“I always am.” He responded unconvincingly.

I put my phone down, realizing that it would be for the best if I focused on the road.

Beaver was uncharacteristically silent for the next few minutes. He fell into a concentrated state that I had rarely, if ever, seen out of him. I was enjoying the peace and quiet until I spotted something that made my heart skip a beat.

“Slow down!”

Beaver more than obediently obeyed and he slammed on the brakes. I cringed as the cargo in the trailer came crashing to the front of the truck.

“What? What is it?”

“Look.” I pointed at the side of the road. This time there was no denying it. The same roadkill deer with a single antler and a distorted neck sat before us.

“It makes no sense. I didn’t drive in a circle!”

“I know, I know.” I said, trying to figure it out myself.

We had passed no buildings, no other cars, not so much as a roadsign since leaving the highway. Beaver came to almost a complete stop as we rolled towards the deer.

“Maybe this is some sort of access road that goes in a loop?” He attempted to reason.

Before I had the chance to respond, the deer’s neck snapped to the side. We screamed as the reanimated deer contorted its body, rising to a standing position. With its neck twisted at a ninety degree angle, it kicked up its front legs and ran across the road, letting out a horrific shriek as it disappeared into the treeline.

“What the hell! What the hell was that Mick!”

My heart nearly beat out of my chest as I attempted to rationalize what we had both just witnessed.

“I guess it was still alive.” I said through short breaths.

I could see Beaver’s hands trembling on the wheel. He looked to me with wide eyes for guidance.

“I think we should turn back.”

I nodded, in agreement.

“You’re right we’ve been driving for-”

I froze as I saw the time on the dashboard. The clock read 1:57 AM.

“That… That’s not possible. How is it already two in the morning?”

Beaver looked at the clock anxiously. “Time zone or something?”

“Beav, no- ugh.” I rubbed my temples. “Just let me think.”

“It’s a shitty old truck, the clock could be broken! I bet the airbags don’t even work!” Beaver forced a laugh, trying to lighten the mood with a joke that I had no interest in.

I had no explanation for the deer, and I had no idea how nearly six whole hours had passed in the blink of an eye. I was certain that I had not fallen asleep. Another horrific thought suddenly struck me. I craned my neck, peeking around the steering wheel to note the fuel gauge. Barely an eighth of a tank left.

“We can’t turn back if we wanted to.” I looked out of the windshield, the grip of terror on my chest tightening. The road had narrowed significantly. The width was barely more than a single lane and there were trees brushing up against the roadside. There was no way we would be able to turn the truck around under such conditions.

“We can’t turn around. Even if we could, we would run out of gas before we made it to the highway. We either need to call it here and pick it up in the morning, or we keep driving and cross our fingers we find something.”

Beaver’s foot was barely on the gas as we deliberated.

Something in my heart told me that stopping was the wrong idea, and Beaver seemed to agree.

“What about the radio? We can check the local stations and see if we can figure out where we might be at?”

I raised my eyebrows in surprise.

“That ain’t a bad idea, Beav.”

He smiled sheepishly as he flipped on the radio to the sound of distorted static. He used the knob, manually changing the frequency. He did so with no discernable rhythm or pattern. It was mostly static, with a few low-quality songs and advertisements in Spanish.

“Woah, what was that one?” I said, catching a strange tune.

Beaver stopped his pawing at the radio long enough for us to hear the strangest music I had ever consumed. I listened intently, fully entranced by the otherworldly tune. My mind began to wander, and my entire body reacted with warmth as I absorbed every note of a song so cosmic and alluring. I felt like I was a moth flying straight into a flame as Beaver slammed the off button on the radio.

“Nope. That’s enough of that.”

Beaver scratched the back of his neck. He reached for another wad of dip. He had almost gone through the entire tin.

I was more than unsettled, but Beaver was completely irrational. He was a squirrely guy, but I had spent enough time and gone through enough stressful situations to know that something wasn’t right.

“What is it?”

He shook his head and frowned, unable to meet my eye.

“I told him. I told my uncle that we shouldn’t do it.”

“Beaver, what are you saying?”

“We never should have taken this job, Mick.”

I blinked. I tried to remember what the job even was, but I couldn’t. What were we transporting? I opened the glovebox, where we kept the manifest logs and I found them all blank.

“I can’t keep going. Mick, I’m scared. I’m not going to make it.”

“What is in the trailer?”

Beaver shook his head, and brought his hat low, the frayed brim covering his eyes.

I opened my door and stepped outside. The large step down from the passenger seat made me wobble, and I began to shiver as I realized how cold it was out. I marched towards the back of the truck, refusing to look into the woods that felt as if they were closing in on me. My breathing became ragged, almost suffocating.

I pulled the cargo key from my pocket and fit it into the lock.

Beaver appeared at my side, despite the fact that I never heard his door open.

He nodded at me, completely pale-faced. Whatever was in the trailer, he wanted me to see it, even though it terrified him.

Hesitantly, I twisted the key and threw open the heavy sliding door. I pulled out my phone from my pocket and turned on the flashlight.

“What the…”

Everything in the moving truck was mine.

A lamp from my apartment. My old bed from my parents house. A jacket that I had lost in college. The smell of mildew and dandelions hit me like a sack of bricks. It was exactly like my grandmother’s house had smelled before she passed. My name and handwriting were on at least a dozen different cardboard boxes. I opened one labeled “family pictures.” Frantically, I flicked through a scrapbook. The images were familiar, but hazy, like some sort of distant dream.

“Beaver, what the hell is going on?” My voice shook. In the distance, I could hear sirens.

*Sirens.*

That meant that there were people close.

Beaver smiled at me as he tilted his head towards the sirens. He stood in the red glow of the taillights, holding the keys to the truck in front of him.

“It’s time for you to drive, chief.” Beaver’s voice sounded like it was coming from underwater.

I hopped down from the truck.

There was nothing behind Beaver. Just a black endless expanse that felt as if it were creeping forward with every wasted second.

“There ain’t time for me. But you can still make it.”

“What are you talking about?”

Beaver dropped the keys into my open palm. They felt heavy and hot. He pushed my chest and my body began to move before my mind could process what I was doing. I simply knew that I needed to get into the truck and drive.

I found myself behind the wheel. Everything was beginning to feel numb. I no longer felt the ache in my back, I no longer felt the cold temperature from the rolled down window. My foot was on the gas as I drove towards the sirens.

Just drive. Just drive. It was the only thing that made sense to me at the moment.

I heard the sirens getting closer, I could see the bouncing red and blue light as they danced off of the truck’s windshield.

I looked out of the truck’s side mirror in time to see Beaver smiling melancholically at me. He spit a wad of tobacco onto the street just before he completely disappeared into the darkness.

“He’s breathing!”

Bright lights blinded my vision as I forced my eyes open.

My body refused to move as I stared at a white ceiling. The concerned face of a stranger in scrubs looked down at me. I was on a stretcher and I knew that at least a dozen bones in my body were broken.

“Hold on! You’re going to be okay!”

I craned my neck forward. I saw out of the back of the ambulance that we were driving away from the moving truck. The front of the truck was completely squashed by a tree that it had rammed into. In the middle of the road was a smear of blood and the carcass of a deer.

I didn’t see Beaver anywhere.

“Easy there!” The EMT put his hand gently on my shoulder, pushing me flat against my back.

“Be.. Bea-” I tried to speak, but my voice was impossibly weak.

“Don’t speak. Just look at me and keep breathing.”

“Beaver.” I forced out the word, exhausting myself completely.

The EMT looked at me with heavy eyes that told me all that I needed to know.

The next day in the hospital, the doctor told me what had happened.

I was asleep when Beaver swerved to avoid a deer. He was too slow and he still hit the stupid bastard. The truck was already off course and in a split second decision, Beaver threw his body in front of mine. When the medics found us, his body was covering mine. The airbags never deployed and if he hadn’t made that choice, we would both be dead.

The nurse came in later with my personal effects that they recovered from the wreck. I searched through the clear plastic bag, groaning as I inspected my phone’s shattered screen. There was something else in the bag that caught my attention. I pulled out the cylindrical container, revealing a can of chewing tobacco, its lid still sealed shut.
