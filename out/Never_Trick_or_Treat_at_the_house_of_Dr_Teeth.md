It's almost Halloween again, and I find myself feeling anxious again. I remember that night long ago. I remember my friends; I remember having fun every year going out to trick-or-treat with them. But I also remember what happened the last year I ever went trick-or-treating. I remember the horrible things that happened, and now I fear it could all happen again. The nightmare we lived through could happen to a new generation, and part of me wishes for the bliss of ignorance. Because I know what that light in the distance at this time of year means. I know someone else will try to visit that damn house. I know because I have been there before, and I wish to God I had not followed my friends into that nightmare.

I was twelve years old when it happened. It was All Hallows' Eve, and I was with my friends Jace and Becky. We were talking about the route we wanted to take. It was going to be the first time we would be completely unattended by any parents, free rein to go and trick-or-treat our hearts out.

“Well, what do you think? I need to finish my map, and I need some input!” Jace snapped at Becky and me as we were considering the small paper that showed us the outline of the city streets we would be walking tomorrow.

“I think if we hit Cobble Hill we can make out like bandits; it's a bit of a hike, but they give out full-size candy bars there.” Jace smiled maniacally, and Becky and I both couldn't hold back laughter; he was really serious about planning the best route possible for Halloween.

“I think it's a good plan; that route sounds great,” I said, eager to wrap up and head out to play a bit more before it got dark and we had to go home.

“One last stop, the final one. This area up here, they say that some rich guy gives out king-size bars!” Our ears perked up at that, and Becky leaned in, scrutinizing the claim.

“According to whom? I haven't heard of any place up there, only stories about the weird house at the end of the road. Some strange dentist lives there, named Dr. Teeth. They say he really, really loves Halloween. He promises to give out all the candy you can eat, as long as he can take care of your teeth afterwards. Maybe it's some kind of business plan to entrap parents as customers.” Becky chuckled, and we joined in before she continued,

“But if it really is some sort of candy jackpot, maybe we should go see this dentist.”

I thought the story sounded weird. Though I was also too absorbed with the idea of all the candy I could eat. We all agreed to head to that neighborhood on Halloween and see it for ourselves.

It was Halloween night, and I was headed over to Jace’s house to meet him and Becky. I kept fussing with my hat as I walked; the thing was too small and kept sliding off and pushing my glasses. I wondered if I could still pull off the pirate look without my hat. Becky arrived at the same time I did; she was in a fairy costume and greeted me with a playful bow and sweep of her wand.

Before we could even say a word, Jace burst out the door, holding both ends of a flowing black cape behind him and baring his oversized vampire fangs,

“Come with me, my fellow children of the night; the music we make will not be the only sweet thing we partake in.” He swept his arm out toward the waiting neighborhood and started marching.

“A little dramatic, don't you think, Count Dorkula?” Becky quipped, but Jace was already out of earshot, practically jogging to the first house. I shrugged and followed. Trick-or-treating had begun.

After hitting many houses on the way, we finally reached Cobble Hill with room left for our bounty of treats. The next hour was a riot of activity. We sprinted from house to house collecting candy as we went; it was amazing. All the houses were covered with awesome Halloween decorations, and when we finally finished with the last house on the block, we had tons of candy and wanted to go through it all again.

While we stood on the edge of the neighborhood, Jace looked frustrated, comparing his map to the area around us.

“Hmm, it can't be there, but…wait a minute.” He went quiet and paused for a moment, then Becky did the same. They held up their arms over their faces like it was too bright all of the sudden, then Jace explained, “There it is!” He gestured for us to follow, reminding us of the final spot. I looked up the hill, but I couldn't see anything.

“Are you sure anyone is really living up there?” I asked. They both looked at me, confused,

“Yeah, don't you see the lights?” they said in unison. I was about to respond no, before they each went rushing up the path toward the lonesome house on the hill.

“Come on, guys, we have to hurry; it is getting late, and this is the last stop, the motherload!” Jace raced ahead, and we followed. I thought it was strange that if this dentist was supposed to be giving out so many treats, then why were there not more trick-or-treaters there? The path up to the house seemed empty, not another soul around. As we started on our way, I took a glimpse back and saw a few other kids start walking up the path to follow, but another stopped their friend and shook their head.

I was troubled by the hesitation I saw and the glimmer of fear, but Becky pulled me along when I started to lag behind, and I was sprinting to catch up to Jace, who was almost at the door. My glasses nearly fell off my head as I ran. I had to be careful; they were new and had special lenses to help mitigate blue light, and they were expensive, so my parents would have freaked out if they broke.

When we arrived at the house, we saw it was also decorated; the lights were on, and several pumpkins were illuminating the large patio by the door. As we walked, I was finally able to see the lights around the area and was surprised I had missed them from down the hill. I saw that the decorations and the pumpkins featured hideously designed creatures and animals; they ranged from cartoonish to almost lifelike. The one consistent amongst the strange decorations, was instead of having teeth, the gums of each creature or animal were lined with different types of candy.

A large werewolf cutout twice my height had king-size Twix instead of fangs. An animatronic witch toiling over a cauldron had Butterfinger in her mouth. A Vampire resting in a coffin had Snickers for fangs. Even the ghosts seemed to have candy wrappers gleaming from underneath the gossamer sheets they hid under.

“This is crazy, I wonder if we can …ya know, take the teeth?” I asked cautiously. Becky shook her head,

“No, those are just decorations. This guy's a dentist after all; let's go to the door.”

I agreed and followed my friends to the front door. We all stood there in front of a large iron wrought door with a creepy-looking knocker that appeared to be a tongue lolling out of a grinning maw that comprised the center of the door.

Jace pulled it back, and it fell against the metal with a metallic clang that rang out so loud it hurt my ears. There was a long pause, and he was about to knock again when the door creaked open.

There, standing in the threshold, was a tall, rail-thin man with graying hair. He wore a white coat and had an embroidered patch on his coat that read “Dr. T DDS”

His lips curved into a closed-mouth smile that did not betray a single one of the dentist’s own teeth.

“Welcome, welcome. You came!”

We were caught off guard by the odd sense of familiarity, but we remembered why we had come and looked to each other for reassurance before looking back at the doctor and saying,

“Trick or Treat!”

Dr. Teeth chuckled as if the gesture was amusing and bent down closer to us.

“Well now, I must say you are all such fine little trick-or-treaters. You found my house, you saw it, and you came so you could get more than a few pieces of candy. You can have all the candy you could ever enjoy, as long as I can take care of your teeth.”

“My parents already have a dentist for me; I’m sorry,” Becky spoke up, unsure of the proposition just like we all were.

“Oh, not like me, I assure you.” Dr. Teeth chuckled again. The gleam in his eyes was unsettling, and I was considering just turning around and leaving, but Jace stepped forward,

“I can give your business card to my parents. Come on, I want some candy now.” I couldn't believe how Jace had not seemed bothered by what was happening.

Dr. Teeth grinned,

“Oh, candy of course. Coming right up. You just put on a smile and…” He paused a moment, and I saw a brief flash of strange blue light flicker before he continued,

“Don’t be so blue...”

I thought it was an odd thing to say. Then I looked at Jace and saw a strange glint in his eyes; it was the same unnatural color that the lights from the house had thrown earlier.

I shifted my glasses and caught a strange tint on the edge of my vision that made my head swim and made me hungry for something…sweet. I looked over to Becky, who was similarly mesmerized. I had no idea what was happening, and I almost took my glasses off to check them until I realized that they had special lenses. I wondered if I was seeing, or rather not seeing, something my friends were.

I started to protest, but Dr. Teeth held out his hands, and Jace and Becky both stepped forward as if in a trance and started to move with the doctor into his house. He paused for a moment and looked back at me,

“Come along, little one, you saw the light too, didn't you? The candy to take care of your teeth, doesn't that sound nice?”

I started to panic. What had he done to Jace and Becky? They were not responding, just grinning with their mouths open. Dr. Teeth’s eyes narrowed, and I had only a moment to react.

I just decided to play along; I smiled, opened my mouth, and started slowly following behind him. He nodded his head in approval, and I took step after shaky step into the house.

We arrived in a small parlor, and I saw a small room adjacent to it, the light was on inside and it had the same sickly blue glow emerging from it that the front porch lights had.

“Now then, what is your favorite candy?” Dr. Teeth asked us all.

Jace and Becky both sat there a moment with the same creepy grins plastered on their faces before Jace mumbled,

“Milky Way”

Becky followed shortly after,

“Skittles”

He looked at me, and I tried to think through the panic I was feeling.

“Snickers,” I finally stammered out.

“Very good. Well, the first person to answer is the one who gets to go first. Come with me, young man; you will have all the Milky Way you could ever eat."

Jace followed Dr. Teeth wordlessly into the parlor, and the door closed.

As soon as it did, the blue light started to flicker, and the sound of dental equipment could be heard. It instantly made my blood go cold. The squealing of a drill, the sound of cutting, buzzing, and humming made me terrified about what was going on in there. I had to do something. I looked at Becky, and she just watched on in a confused stupor.

She couldn't seem to focus on anything other than the blue light. I realized that both my friends had seen it first, that they had noticed it and been captivated by it until we got to the door. Then something happened where they stopped acting normal.

On a complete hunch, I decided to repeat the phrase,

“Don’t act all blue.”

Becky's eyes blinked rapidly, and she suddenly looked confused,

“Where are we? What is this?” She mumbled before looking like she was on the verge of panic. I held a finger to my lips and hissed out,

“ssshhhhhh”. She calmed down, and we waited anxiously for the noises from the room to die down.

As we waited, we searched around for something to use to make an escape. Becky fell back and cried out before catching herself in a silent scream. I went over to look at what she had found. It was a large bowl of teeth in a jar. At first I thought they might be Halloween decorations, but I made the mistake of picking one up, and it felt…real.

I felt nauseous and terrified. We looked behind a drape-covered cabinet and found a collection of human bones; many of them were small, small like the size of children.

I tried to catch my breath and not hyperventilate, but I was horrified. We needed to get out of there somehow. I found a set of fire pokers nearby, what appeared to be a bricked-up fireplace. I grabbed one and held it behind my back, trying to steady my breath. The sounds had died down, and Dr. Teeth could emerge any moment. Becky had grabbed something as well and was holding back ragged gasps of fear, trying to suppress her own terror.

Dr. Teeth and Jace emerged from the room, and the blue light flooded out again.

“My best work yet. I promised I would take good care of these teeth; as for you, enjoy your new candy.” Dr. Teeth smiled horribly, revealing a disturbing row of dentures that looked cobbled together from many different teeth.

We saw Jace, and he looked oddly placid; then he smiled at us, and I nearly screamed. His teeth were all gone! In his mouth were dozens of pieces of unwrapped candy that had been stuffed in like a Halloween bag. They spilled out as he smiled, and Dr. Teeth turned back to us,

“Alright, who is next?” I could barely move, could barely breathe, but I had to do something. I summoned my courage and held up my hand.

“I am, but can I say something first?”

Dr. Teeth frowned,

“I suppose so. How are you feeling? You are acting…odd.” He was suspicious of the fact I was not mesmerized as the other had been, and I quickly blurted out,

“Don’t be so blue.” Jace began screaming; the horrified look on his face was all it took to know that he could see and feel everything now.

I used the distraction to rush forward and swing the fire poker at Dr. Teeth’s left knee. The blow knocked him off his feet, and he crashed to the ground cursing. Becky followed up with a stiff strike to the monster’s head with a piece of wood she had salvaged.

“Run!” I screamed at the top of my lungs. The three of us bolted for the door; I almost got turned around, but I managed to lead us out of that nightmare. I flung the door open, and we ran for our lives down the hill and to the relative safety of town.

In our terrified flight, we managed to flag down a police officer and explain the horrible situation to him. He called an ambulance for Jace, but when we told him where it had happened and who had done it, he frowned. He looked up to the hill and then back to us.

“There has been no one living in the old Manor since Doctor Trelawny died years ago. It's been abandoned. You are sure it was there?” He asked doubtfully.

We were confused, but when we looked back up the hill, the lights were gone- not just the eerie blue light, but all the lights up there; it was completely dark. I couldn't believe it.

We were taken home and explained our terrible night to our parents, except for Jace, who was taken to the hospital. We were told that the police investigated the area and, sure enough, no trace of anyone was found. No creepy decoration, no strange iron door knocker, no Dr. Teeth.

My parents tried to tell me that it might have been some deranged squatter who knew the story and tried to kidnap us, but even they did not sound like they believed that was all it was. When I asked them about who Dr. Teeth was, they looked nervous and tried to change the subject.

After pressing them, eventually they told me the story about a Doctor who had lived in that house. He was a dentist who had gone crazy and began killing his patients, and one Halloween he had kidnapped and stolen the teeth of a dozen children before he vanished.

The mansion was abandoned and fell into disrepair, but the rumor about Dr. Teeth persisted. Some years, people would whisper they could see the strange light coming from the house, and the doctor would promise all the candy you could ever ask for, before taking your teeth and, in some cases, your life…

My parents reassured me that they had heard that story when they were kids and that the real Dentist that lived in that house would be over a hundred by now. They said that the person who attacked us and mutilated Jace was probably some sick copycat who snuck into his house. I tried to take comfort in believing their version, but the lights, that face, the way it all disappeared afterwards- I was not so sure.

Now many years later it is almost Halloween again. Enough time has passed that I no longer fear the neighborhood and lonesome house on the hill. It has sat dormant for years. But I always watch, I always wait. Just in case I see it again, just in case I need to warn someone.

Just last night I saw it, just a flicker of familiar light, a terrible omen of what will happen soon. It was the light; I know it was. It is there again; he is there again. I have to do something before it is too late. I have to warn anyone who will listen to stay away from that house.

If you see the light, if you feel the call to go there for all the candy you could want, don’t. The friendly facade of a generous dentist is hiding a horrible secret, and you could wind up as the next Halloween victim of Dr. Teeth….
