I sat in the lobby of the police station, my eyes tracing the lines of the tile floor as I attempted to ease the thoughts racing through my mind. I closed my eyes and attempted to steady my breathing, but the outline of that God forsaken door was burned into my brain. It had been a few hours since the police entered my home, since I discovered it wasn't just a hallucination.

The door was real.

I still couldn't process it. Some small part of me had grown comfortable with the idea that there was a mundane explanation, that I was insane, that anything besides some supernatural phenomena was flipping my life and understanding of the world upside down. That idea seemed so far away now that it may as well be on the moon. I sighed, finally peeling my gaze away from the floor to the officers who now sat in front of me. They were all quiet, the only sound coming from the typing of keys as they filed an official report. I don't even know what they were meant to say. What, that a door magically appeared in a bathroom mirror, and that something came out of it? I would've laughed at the absurdity if the situation wasn't so grave.

"For all we know, it could still be some sort of optical illusion." The older officer spoke, though he sounded very unsure of the idea himself. His colleague merely shook his head and sighed. "No chance. Everybody saw it, on camera, on video, hell we saw it with our own two eyes. Why can't you just accept that this is something beyond our understanding?" He looked at his older colleague, whose wrinkled face coiled into a scowl.

"Yeah, sorry kid, I must've forgotten that doors just appear out of thin air all the damn time! Oh, and don't forget that something opens it up from the other side like something out of a horror flick! Nothing hard to believe about that!" He was shouting at this point, but his expression dropped and his gaze softened a bit before he continued, quieter this time. "Look, I'm sorry, but not everybody is just so readily accepting the only explanation is ghosts or demons or whatever the hell else. Maybe there's still something we're missing."

I just sat there in silence, keeping all my thoughts to myself. I didn't want to be here at this time of night under these circumstances. Hell, I didn’t ever wanna be in a police station, let alone be the center of attention in one. I just wanted this to blow over so I could be left alone.

Eventually, there was nothing to do but leave. I couldn't go home, of course; who knows what that would entail. Instead, I was going to be escorted to a motel, where an officer would stay in the room next to mine. "I can't even be afforded a night's rest in my own bed," I muttered to myself as I took the turn into the parking lot.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------

The motel was pretty unassuming, which helped with their goal of keeping me hidden. I wasn't sure that whatever this thing was used conventional means of locating me regardless, but the idea was good enough I suppose. The clerk at the front barely looked up as the police explained the situation and worked on getting me checked in. I stood silently behind them, the chatter as the situation was explained (excluding the fantastical details, of course) fading into white noise as I dissociated from reality. The events of the past few weeks seemed to finally be setting in, and the toll the constant fear had been taking was becoming noticeable.

My gaze drifted up aimlessly, as I drank in my surroundings in an attempt to reconnect to reality. I noticed an officer walking towards me to hand me a room key, the buzzing of the fluorescent lights, and the beige wallpaper that was starting to peel in the corners. I blinked to re-adjust myself a bit as he handed me the key. "Here you go. Try to get some rest tonight, I'll be right next door if anything happens." I nodded as we turned and headed down the hallway towards our rooms. He gave me one last nod before walking into his room and shutting the door.

I stood outside for a moment, now left alone in the hallway. A breath I didn't realize I had been holding finally escaped as my shoulders relaxed. Even in a situation like this, I couldn't help but be tense around people. I rubbed my temples, shaking my head at my own demeanor. I didn't even know whether my guard was up more for the paranormal or the ordinary world at this point.

I unlocked the door to my room and stepped inside, the cool air from the AC blowing giving me a sense of comfort that was welcomed in my current state. I pulled my belongings out of my pockets and tossed them on the nightstand, flopping down on the bed in an exhausted heap. Finally truly alone with my thoughts in the dark, my mind wandered to the most horrific corners imaginable. It lingered on the door, and the more mundane, but it would not quiet or still. Eventually, even the racing of my mind could no longer compete with the grasp of sleep, and I was slowly taken into slumber.

It was not peaceful.

I was assailed by dreams of a door opening, a dark maw revealing itself as the old oak wood barrier gave way to the void. The emptiness was somehow more terrifying than if an apparition had just plainly shown itself. It was as if I could sense a presence just out of sight in the shadows, watching with a hungry gaze.

Waiting.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------

I awoke with a start, the dream having rattled me to the point my heart was already racing as I sat up in bed. The room seemed much colder than when I had fallen asleep earlier. I glanced at the clock on the nightstand, reading that it was nearly four o'clock in the morning. I sighed, rubbing my eyes and blinking the sleep out of them as I haphazardly scanned the room.  As I my gaze fell upon the corner to my right, my heart skipped a beat and I froze, suddenly very awake.

The door was in the corner, and it was slightly ajar.

For a long moment, I just stared. I suppose I was frozen by disbelief, maybe shock had taken over my body. Maybe I was still dreaming, or hallucinating. Had it really followed me here? Was it now beyond the bounds of the mirror, having manifested in the physical world? The growing pit in my stomach felt as if it could swallow the room whole, but I tried not to panic. I slowly began to reach over to the nightstand for my phone, hand trembling a bit as I attempted to steady my breathing. Just then, a car passed by on the freeway, and the headlights caught the window, causing a few slivers of light to peek through the blinds.

Fingers rested on the frame of the door.

Time seemed to stop for a brief moment as my brain registered what I was seeing. My eyes now adjusted to the dark, I could see the fingers gripping the frame of the door as if whatever was on the other side was waiting to pull itself through. My dread had now turned to primal fear, and instinct finally took over as I launched myself out of bed and flung myself towards the door to the hallway. As I flung the door open and darted into the hallway, I could've sworn I heard the creaking of rusty hinges.

I screamed at the top of my lungs for the officer in the neighboring room as I made a mad dash towards the lobby, not daring to look back as one foot fell in front of the other in rapid succession. I heard the officer burst out of his room, and as I swung around finally, I watched as his wild gaze met mine for a moment before he turned and drew his gun, kicking the door to my room open and stepping inside, weapon drawn. I waited for gunfire, or screaming, or anything, but nothing came. The silence was worse, as it gave me no idea as to what would come next. I nearly had a heart attack as the officer's head popped back out from the room, a bewildered expression on his face. "What the hell is going on?!" he shouted.

I wished I could offer an answer.

\-------------------------------------------------------------------

I was filled in on the details of what was found in the room as the rest of the officers arrived and poured inside the motel, while the deputy and I stood outside, barely paying mind to the whole scene. Officer Sanders, as I came to learn his name was, entered the room after being alerted by my outburst to find the door in the corner of the room, only this time it was shut as opposed to wide open like last time. He reassured me a bit, saying that whatever it was likely retreated. "You spooked it about as badly as it spooked you, I'd wager. Sounds about like a wild animal." he said, squinting a bit as he seemed to be lost in thought. I wasn't so sure of this explanation, however. Why show itself at all if it was only going to flee? A realization dawned on me.

"What if it shut the door behind it? What if it's smart enough to cover it's tracks?" He only stared at me in return, barely masking the concern rising behind his stoic expression.

I cast a look towards the motel as I watched the officers swarming the building. The previously sleepy motel was now crawling with armed men, all because of me. I just observed as they made their way throughout the building, and one in particular caught my gaze. He was entering the room where Sanders had been staying, seemingly alone. I couldn't explain why, but this got my attention. I watched as he stepped over the threshold into the darkness of the room, the shadows seemingly swallowing him whole. I continued staring as if waiting for something. I had tuned the world around me out completely.

"Darren?" I heard Sanders say, as hearing my name finally snapped me out of my trance and brought me back to reality. "Yeah, sorry. I get a bit spaced out sometimes." I said, rubbing the back of my head in an embarrassed apologetic gesture. "I've noticed," he said with a bit of a chuckle, before continuing, "Like I was saying, we're combing the whole place right now and setting a perimeter. If something did come through that door, we'll know. Nothing is getting in or out withou-"

*BANG*

A single gunshot rang through the air. For a moment, everything was still as Sanders and I merely stared at each other in astonishment. Then, just as quickly as it arrived, the silence ended as the environment erupted into a crescendo of shouting and boots slamming the ground. Sanders' walkie exploded immediately, overlapping shouting and garbled radio static assaulting my ears. I could make out bits of "shots fired" but that was interrupted by a louder voice. "Walker's gone! No sign of him anywhere!"

"Someone went in your room alone" I blurted, as Sanders threw me a puzzled gaze. "Huh? What are you talking about?" He asked, still taken aback by the sudden cacophony.

"I was watching through the window, someone went into your room alone. It had to be him." As I turned to face the motel again, my dread returned in full force. It waited until I was alone. It waited until that officer entered the room alone. This thing, whatever it was, was more than just a mindless creature. It stalked, calculated, and moved when its prey was isolated.

It was a hunter.
