I was a police officer in a town in California's southeastern desert, not far from the Arizona state line. This town has one highway running through its center and is about 2 hours removed from the next town in any direction. 

Due in no small part to its isolation from the rest of the world, many of the town's residents experience poverty stemming from a weak job market, drug or alcohol addiction, and mental illnesses. Some struggle with all four. As if this wasn't enough, the town itself does not generate enough money to keep up with most of the old, failing infrastructure. Because of this, many of the town's homes, schools, businesses, and city buildings lie in a state of disrepair. If you were to exit the highway and drive the town's main street, you would find it consists of what you might find in any other passing town: some fast food chains, a few strip malls, a gas station, a motel, and not much else. 

Despite this, a natural beauty surrounds the hopeless souls, sun bleached buildings, and wind torn roads. The morning sun paints the rocky desert hills with a radiant, orange glow;  while the shadows it casts plunge the cracks and crevices into a deep, inky blue. In the evening, after the coyotes have ceased their howling and the sun has retreated beyond the horizon, millions, if not billions, of stars are revealed in the night sky. I used to stare up, immersed in the stars. I thought about the vast distances between each point of light in the sky and what, if anything, is out there that we will never know about. When I let my mind wander, I often pondered on our place in the vastness of space, what lies beyond our depth of knowing, and what awaits us all beyond the curtain of death. I've always hoped that there is something more to all of this, something that makes all of our suffering but a speck of misfortune in the universe's grand scheme. 

I've gotten off track. 

Given the town's small size, our department's numbers were few and we were all required to work patrol. However, most in the law enforcement community understand that homelessness is one of the most complained about issues in any city, ours included. With a highway running through its center and a drug infestation plaguing the streets, the town held a homeless population of transients, addicts, and the mentally ill. To combat this, our department had a two-man collateral team to handle these issues as they popped up. I was on this team and though my primary duties were on patrol, we were occasionally granted overtime opportunities to saturate the homeless community with resource outreach or enforcement. 

Before I go any further, it is important to understand that the town's homeless community is a tight knit one, and they did not relinquish information about others to us often. Given our goal of providing long-term, healthy solutions for their problems, we generally tried to maintain good relationships with those we knew to be suffering from homelessness, it just made our job go a lot smoother. This is not to say we did not enforce laws when they needed to be enforced, the town's leadership primarily paid us to improve its residents' quality of life after all. Ironic given the town's already struggling economy and decaying infrastructure. 

My partner, D, and I meshed well together, but to keep things on track, I'll just say D was a great partner. By this point in our careers we had made countless contacts with people within the homeless population and were fairly familiar with their habits. I quickly learned how resourceful people could be when they'd been stripped of their livelihoods. 

By the summer of 2023, I had been a police officer for about 9 years, and worked on the two-man team for 1. By this point I was familiar with most of our town's homeless population and knew where they usually congregated. I had developed an interest in analyzing their illegal encampments and I admired the complexity of some of the more sophisticated shelters, from makeshift showers and exercise areas, to multi-room tents, complete with makeshift fencing around "their" property in some of the more rural areas of town. 

D and I generally tried to squash problem areas before they snowballed into bigger problems, and so we were headed to an old KFC where many of our homeless residents congregated. This KFC had been vacant for some time, and after countless break-ins, resecuring, and more break-ins, the property owner finally gave up and allowed the building to fall into complete disrepair. It was about 7 AM, and as we drove through the area we noted the parking lot was clear of the regulars and the building itself did not have any obvious signs of break-ins from when we had seen it the day prior. It was cold outside, colder than to be expected with direct sunlight and lack of shade; and so quiet, no crows, no wind, no one talking to themselves in the bushes, just an overwhelming silence. 

One of the regulars, Bucket, liked to set up his encampments inside the empty trash enclosure in the parking lot. Bucket was a 50 year old man who had been on the streets for about 20 years after getting hooked on methamphetamine. His frail, decrepit frame polarized by his loud, nonsensical ramblings. Though he often found himself in our jail for warrants or drug possession, Bucket generally didn't cause many issues for D and I. He stuck to himself most times, and usually cleaned up and left when we asked him to. 

Bucket's House, as D and I called it, was a perfect shelter for someone trying to escape the elements of the desert. The enclosure itself consisted of three cinder block walls and a large iron door for the dumpsters acting as the fourth wall of the structure. There was a smaller, iron door on one of the other walls which Bucket used as his entrance and exit. There was also a partial overhang over the top of the enclosure which allowed Bucket to easily build a makeshift roof out of scavenged tarps and cardboard. 

D and I approached the smaller door to Bucket's abode. I read the "Bucket" scrawled on one of the cinder blocks near the door. This was the homeless community's way of letting each other know who was staying where. Most times D knocked on Bucket's door and called his name. This was usually all it took to get Bucket up and moving but today was different. Instead of the usual shuffling, groaning, and long, gravelly "fuuuuuck", we were met with silence. We knocked and called to Bucket a few more times with no change. 

"I'll pop the door, you hold lethal." D said. 

Understanding D's concern for a potential ambush upon opening the door, I drew my firearm. "On you." I said with my weapon pointed at the door. D flung the door open and I could already see the simple structure was mostly empty. I peeked the hard corner to my right just to be sure and holstered my weapon. Bucket was not there. 

It was dark inside Bucket's House, but instead of the usual damp, musty stench of Bucket's body odor, our nostrils were filled with the smell of a recent fire. Bucket's home was undisturbed. His furniture and belongings were still there, an old torn couch next to a small circular table, a used meth pipe, torch lighter, and old cigarrette butts sat on top. A duffel bag containing Bucket's clothes and hygiene supplies, some stolen power tools, and a blue bicycle sat in a corner. 

A large purple sheet separated Bucket's living room from his bedroom, which provided only enough space to house his mattress. That day, however, Bucket's mattress was missing. In its place was a number of melted candles arranged in a circle in the center of the space. In the center of the circle, Bucket had removed a roughly 1'x1'x1' chunk of concrete from the ground. The void was filled with gray, murky water. What the significance of this water-filled crater was to Bucket I'll likely never know. 

"Oh shit dude, look at that." D said. 

D pointed his flashlight at the wall opposite to us. Covering the entirety of the wall was a crude image, painted with black spray paint, dripping at its edges. Given the image's size, singular color scheme, and the fact that it was likely drawn by a tweaked out Bucket, deciphering it took me a few minutes. I think the image meant to be depicted was two long black arms emerging from a body of water, their emaciated hands gripping a man by his shoulders. The twisted and bent black limbs held the man suspended in the air; but whether the hands were lifting him to the sky or pulling him into the water I could not tell. D and I stared for a few seconds, silence encroaching on our ears as if in some cavern in the belly of the earth. The thick silence was wrong for Bucket's dwelling. We had been in there before and never experienced that level of oppression. It was like we were being embraced by silence in physical form, intent on keeping us inside that room. 

"You good bro? Let's head out." D said. 

I hadn't heard him right away. 

"Come on man, its cold and we have to go meet James. Try to find him a shelter if he hasn't changed his mind already." I heard D this time and we left. 

On our way to meet James, I asked D what he thought of Bucket's creation. 

"Bucket probably just got on a sick one last night. You saw that pipe right? Fool is probably running around one of these streets right now." 

We never saw Bucket again. It was normal for our regulars to disappear for days at a time, participating in whatever activities they participated in, but mainstays, like Bucket, always popped back up. Needless to say we didn't think much of Bucket or his house for a few weeks until D and I realized how long it had been since we had last seen him. Like I mentioned, Bucket had been on the streets for a long time and knew a lot of people in similar situations as his. Despite this, no one in our homeless population seemed able, or willing, to tell us where he was. This wasn't alarming either, many in the homeless community did not share information about others with us very often. Some did, but most did not. 

When we asked Dog, a homeless 26 year old fentanyl addict, he stared blankly at us. Lights on but no one home kind of thing. Dog sometimes pointed us in the right direction when we were looking for someone, but not that day. 

"I don't know what kind of response you were hoping for." D had said 

The closest we got to a confirmation of Bucket's wellbeing came from Rachel, another mainstay in our town who had been on the streets for about 17 years. Rachel was about 40 years old but enjoyed smoking meth, stripping naked, then running around the town's businesses harassing townspeople. We arrested her often but only as a last resort because without fail, she would shit or piss in our backseat. We did our best to stay away from her. 

D and I were driving behind a gas station when we ran into Rachel. She sat on the asphalt next to her bedding. The fact Rachel was sitting at all meant she was probably coherent enough to talk to. 

"Rachel!" I called. 

"Yeah! Hey!" She replied in her raspy, excited voice. 

"You know the employees here are going to call us to have you removed. We've been over this Rachel. You need to clean up and keep it moving." 

"I'm just waiting for my friend here." 

"I understand, but you already know you can't be here unless you're conducting business. Pack up and get going Rachel." 

Annoyed, Rachel stood and started gathering her things. "Rachel, have you given any thought to going into a shelter like we talked about?" 

"Yeah I just need to find James, he has my keys. Plus Mouse is watching my cat. If you can find a shelter that will take me and my cat..." Unfortunately, if its not one thing its another. 

I shook my head. "Hey before we take off, have you seen or heard from Bucket?" 

Rachel's face darkened. In a cold tone she said, "His business is none of yours." 

We left the area. Over the following months we continued asking for Bucket from time to time but never learned anything more of his whereabouts. 

A few months had passed and it was now winter of 2023. D and I were receiving complaints of a growing encampment on a hillside on the outskirts of town. The residents who lived nearby said people could be heard yelling and fighting at all hours of the night. Understandably they did not want this kind of activity so close to their homes and so D and I were tasked with handling the problem. 

The encampment was well hidden behind some rocks halfway up the hillside. It took D and I about 30 minutes of scrambling rocks to find the place. The "growing encampment" was little more than a single tent covered with a tarp and some trash strewn about. To us, it looked like no more than one or two people had been staying in the area. 

The tent flap was closed and so just like Bucket's House, D unzipped the flap and I held my firearm at the tent's opening. The tent was empty. 

It was at this point I realized the sharp drop in temperature, followed by the same suffocating silence we experienced in Bucket's House. Inside was a grimy, dusty sleeping bag shoved to the left side of the tent, the right side occupied by a small, waist high table. 

On the table was a large metal bowl filled with clear water. Inside the bowl was a shaped rock, about the size of my hand. The stone was gray in color, and caked in what looked to be algae buildup; entirely different from the red and brown desert rocks we were used to. The stone was not naturally shaped, and had been carved to resemble something vaguely suggesting the shape of a human sitting cross-legged; however, its features were eroded and difficult to decipher. There were several shallow pores I assumed represented the figure's eyes. The figure was slim but had several carved lines suggesting four arms and more than two legs. It seemed to depict an emaciated being, its ribcage and spine contouring the heavily eroded effigy. Every detail on the small statue had been worn down, suggesting old age. 

"Any idea who stayed here?" I asked. 

"No, this spot is new. Let me take some pictures for public works and we'll go." 

Neither D or I had known there was an encampment here, and there was no nearby graffiti to indicate who it belonged to. In short, we likely wouldn't be getting any answers to who had been here, where the carving came from, what it depicted, how old it was, or why it was here in the first place. 

After a few weeks, the town's public works department made their way to the site and disposed of everything there. The complaints of encampments in that area ceased and did not return. A few months passed and I had mostly forgotten the incident, but there was something about it that was eerily similar to what we found in Bucket's house. I often found myself asking: 

What was this figure supposed to be? Why was it out here in the middle of this desert? Where did it come from? And why would anyone care to carry around the hideous thing? 

These questions, and others, would gnaw the back of my mind for years to come. 

Fast forward to late Spring 2024 when we contacted Boogie. D and I picked up a call for service involving a naked male sitting in a dirt planter near a gas station. 

Upon arrival, we found Boogie sitting naked in the dirt planter. Despite the lack of shade and 90 degree weather, Boogie paid no mind to his bare skin that was likely being cooked by the ground. He was smoking meth from a glass pipe when I called to him. 

"Boogie! What are you doing, man?" 

Boogie waved. "Hey, smoking meth." 

"Smoking meth. Ok bro, we've talked about this before. Place the pipe on the ground and put your hands on top of your head." 

Boogie complied, and as I placed the handcuffs I asked, "Where are your clothes?" 

"Oh, well when I went inside the store they told me I didn't need any. So I took them off." 

As Boogie stood I saw the extent of the burning to his buttocks, palms, and soles of feet. I assumed he'd been without clothes for at least a few hours given the blistered skin, and raw, pink meat underneath. 

"Boogie, this is no good. Your body is scorched. Last thing you probably want is for this to become infected." 

"Yes," Boogie mumbled, likely completely ignorant to what I had told him or to the state of his skin. 

Boogie was completely "smoked out." This type of behavior was typical for him and, often times, taking him to jail did nothing to solve the problem of allowing him to roam free. He would be released from jail, and resume the same activities that got him into jail in the first place. Sometimes, if able, D and I elected to place him on a 5150 hold to at least get him off the streets for a few days. 

"Let's do 5150, gravely disabled." I said. D nodded in agreement. 

There was no hospital in the town able to accept 5150 holds and so this usually meant taking a two hour drive to the nearest hospital. During the drive we sometimes made small talk with people in our backseat. Boogie, given his state of mind, was always interesting to talk to. 

"What was the plan for the day, Boogie?" 

"I was going to go the army office. I need to report them for forcing me to take drugs," Boogie chuckled. "Last time I went to county (jail) they told me my cellie was going to be Chinese." Boogie smiled as he said this. 

"And?" 

"So I've been having some medical issues. Can we go to a hospital? My brain just feels like its gone." 

"That's where we're going man." 

"I need to get back to church, Bucket invited me again but-" Boogie began whispering something under his breath. 

D and I were caught off guard. 

"You've seen Bucket?" 

"Well no, but I hear him." 

"When was the last time he spoke to you?" 

"Can I get some food at the hospital?" 

"Yeah, but what else can you tell me about Bucket?" 

Boogie had lost his train of thought and continued rambling about Chinese cellmates and people jacking off on him. Boogie was accepted by the hospital and we began the drive back to town. 

"Look, I know his mind is fried but what do you think about what Boogie said?" I asked. 

"Nothing bro, he says random shit." 

"Yeah but no one has so much as mentioned him until today. I just think its odd." 

"What? You really think Boogie can form a coherent thought?" 

"I'm just saying its weird man. Like what was with all the shit we found inside Bucket's spot? The candles, the waterhole, that drawing? That shit looked evil, dude. And where the fuck did Bucket even go?" 

D didn't respond. 

"Then we find an empty tent, virtually in the middle of nowhere. We have no idea who it belonged to and no one has been able to tell us who stayed there." 

"You're thinking too hard on this, dude. Bucket is gone. That's one less we have to deal with. Trust me, he probably got high and took off with some other tweaker or he OD'd out there somewhere. We'll find whats left of his body in a few years." 

"Did you even look at what was inside that bowl? Think what you want but it was wrong. What tweakers have you met who create shit like that? Skulls, demons, pentagrams, sure, but that statue wasn't those things, and neither was Bucket's drawing." 

"You watch too much horror. Bucket got high, drew some bullshit on his wall and now he's gone. Probably dead." 

"Boogie said he still hears Bucket!" I just needed someone to tell me I wasn't thinking too deep. Everything we'd experienced up to that point felt connected, I just couldn't fit the pieces together. 

D answered a phone call from his wife and we didn't speak for the rest of the drive. 

The next few weeks came and went as normal. D and I rode together like we normally did. I would occasionally try to start a conversation of what we'd experienced but D would shut it down, saying I was thinking too hard and all these things were normal behavior for the demographic we worked with. After a little while I stopped bringing it up altogether, there was nothing I could do, no information I could dig up; and so I carried on, keeping all my questions in the back of my mind. 

The next event didn't occur until July 2024. There was a short, rocky hill on the outskirts of town. On top of the hill sat an old, partially burnt house. Despite its scorched, blackened interior, the house still stood and was structurally stable enough to act as a shelter for our homeless residents. Neither of us knew who owned the house, only that it had been standing in a state of disrepair since before we became police officers. Before I came onto the team, D would occasionally venture up the hill to clear out transients from the area. 

Though we obviously didn't receive any complaints from the property owners, it was important we didn't allow hubs to form. Allowing them to congregate in groups typically brought significant spikes in violent, property, and drug-related crimes, not to mention fire hazards and unsafe living conditions. 

We parked in the driveway at the bottom of the hill and began the steep climb to the house. The immediate area surrounding the house wasn't much to look at, just a lot of rocks, trash, and an old firepit dug into the earth in the middle of what I guessed would be the front yard. Three men who were unfamiliar to us sat around the unlit pit talking, and smoking cigarrettes. Though not familiar to D and I, these men did not seem unfamiliar with the lifestyles we regularly encountered. D initiated conversation with the trio. 

"How's it going?" 

One man immediately stood and ran down the hill. The other two stared at us, completely disregarding their evasive friend. We didn't chase. 

Of the two who stayed, one man looked to be in his 40's, skin heavily weathered by years spent in the sun. He wore a patchy beard, black beanie, faded, sweat-stained, black Nike shirt, a pair of stretched basketball shorts, and was missing several of his teeth. The other looked about 35, wore an old blue hat, paperclips for earrings, blue shirt, dusty gray pants and Crocs. The man had drawn several "tattoos" on his arms with colored markers. 

"Hey sirs, we were actually just getting ready to leave," replied the older of the men. The pair stood and began packing their belongings into nearby backpacks. 

"Why did that guy take off?" D asked. 

The pair shrugged, though obviously knew more than they let on. 

"What are your names?" 

"Gilbert," replied the older man. 

"Andrew," replied the younger. 

"Either of you guys on parole or probation?"

"I just discharged," said Gilbert. 

Andrew shook his head. 

I ran both of their names and confirmed Gilbert was clear. Andrew had an outstanding warrant for drug possession. Around this time I noticed a foul smell, the matted and moist left pant leg of Andrew, and the flies buzzing around it. 

"What's going on with your leg Andrew?" I asked. 

"Oh man its all fucked up. All the way up to my knee." 

"What do you mean?" 

Andrew lifted his left pant leg about 3 inches, revealing a leg eaten through by disease, leaking pus, with exposed bone and fat. 

"What the hell man. How long has it been like that?" 

"Like a year." 

"That looks terrible, Andrew. You're done if you get sepsis. How did that even happen?" 

"I ran from LAPD. I tried to climb a fence and got cut on some plant. I didn't think it was anything but yeah." 

"You have a warrant, Andrew. Get your stuff, and get the warrant taken care of. If we see you guys up here again you're going to jail." 

"Yeah I know. Thank you guys." 

"And do something about that leg." 

"I'm not worried sir. The church heals." 

"What?" 

The pair started down the hill. 

"Don't." D said. 

We waited for Gilbert and Andrew to make it out of sight before heading towards the house. Since I'd never been to the location, D suggested clearing the interior of the scorched structure. We paused at the doorless entryway where I called out: 

"Police department! Anyone inside come out and make yourselves known!" 

We waited a few seconds before I tried again. No response. 

We waited once more and I called: 

"Police department! Anyone inside come out and make yourselves known! Final warning!" 

We entered the building with our weapons drawn. The building was dark inside, the windows had been boarded up and electricity had likely not been present for a very long time. Despite the midday sun outside, we were forced to use our gun-mounted flashlights while searching, our heavy footsteps and gear echoing in the empty shell. 

The layout of the interior was odd, not of standard construction. The entryway opened with a hallway, no doors on either side. At the end of the hall was another doorless frame leading to what I assumed was a bedroom. A couple of empty sleeping bags and old food sat on the floor of the otherwise barren room. From the end of the same hall, it cut to the right, a locked door sat across from the barren room we had just searched. Continuing down the hall and we were met with a large family room, also bare of any effects. The only place left to search was the room behind the locked door. 

The thick stench of burnt wood still lingered in the air and I found myself wondering at the house's previous (permanent) occupants. This place was blackened by fire and yet was completely devoid of almost any and all furniture or other belongings (aside from those belonging to transients). Who owned this place? 

We approached the locked door in the hallway. 

"You know what's back there?" I asked D. 

"No, it's been locked the times I've come up here." 

"Hm, I'll boot it. Hold the door." 

"Yup." D said, pointing his firearm at what would become the opening. 

I was aware that kicking down a locked door in an old burnt building may not have been the safest thing to do, but curiosity took me and D didn't protest. 

The door was weak and gave after one kick placed near the locking mechanism. What we were met with was not a room but stairs leading down into a basement. At the end of the stairs was a wall, and the majority of the basement was out of sight. Our lights would do little to help unless we descended further down the stairs. 

D paused before continuing. 

"Wait. You hear that?" D asked. 

I listened. Somewhere deep within the basement was what sounded like weak, faint, whispering. It was difficult to make out but from the broken pieces I gathered: 

"Thy will... Deathless stars beyond a black sea... Emperor... On the shores between..." 

"Make an announcement," D said. 

"Police Department! I hear you down there! Come to the sound of my voice with your hands up!" The whispering continued but proved too difficult to decipher. 

We tried one more announcement which went without acknowledgement. D and I began descending the stairs. Darkness engulfed us, our gun-lights hardly penetrating the thick blackness. My nostrils filled with the smell of stagnant water and mold. My skin chilled with the surprisingly cold, damp air. I stepped off the last step and felt cold water gush into my left boot. 

"Goddammit. Hey D, there's water down here, about ankle deep." 

"Want to just come back up?" 

"I'm already down here man, and my socks are already wet. I'll go a little farther, I'll scream if I need you." 

"Ok." 

I shone my light around the rest of the basement. My light swept over the grimy cinderblock space, there wasn't a single piece of furniture there, only cloudy water and black-stained walls. Then I saw it, in the center of the space was a lone figure sitting cross-legged in the water, facing away from me. From a distance of about 10- 15 feet, I could see the person sat naked. Their hair was short and tangled, their sun-baked brown skin stretched thinly over a frail skeleton with a visible ribcage and spine. 

Their whispering had stopped and all I could hear in the oppressive silence was my own breathing and the shifting of my own weight in the shallow water. I called again. 

"Hey! Police! Put your hands on your head and come back to me." 

No response. Not even so much as a simple body movement, just a completely silent, completely still figure. 

"D, I need you down here." 

"Coming. What you got?" 

"They're not giving me a response. Let's walk up, you hold lethal and I'll take hands-on." 

"Got it." 

Slowly, D and I sloshed through the pungent water and approached the sitting figure. I placed my hands on the person's shoulders and prepared for handcuffing when I noticed how cold, and stiff their body felt. It was a coldness and stiffness to the touch I had come to associate with the dead. I peered over their shoulders and saw the person's withered hands, palms up, one over the other, in their lap. I immediately recoiled. 

"What the fuck." 

"You good?" D asked, a few inches behind me.

I didn't answer and walked around the seated figure. This person was a female, her tangled black hair had stopped growing some time ago. Her eyes sunken and dry, mouth hanging open, skin shriveled, almost mummified, over her broken frame of a body. She was dead. 

"D. She's dead." 

"What are you talking about?" D walked to around to where I stood and was greeted with the same ghastly scene that met my eyes. 

"Oh what the fuck is this man? Who the fuck is that?" D said in a trembling voice. 

I didn't say anything. I was terrified. Someone had been talking down there, but there was only one person we found in that crypt and she had clearly been dead for a while. There were no exits from the basement aside from where we came from. 

"Bro..." D pointed his flashlight at the ceiling. 

I looked up and saw a vast painting covering the entirety of the basement ceiling. The entirety of the ceiling had been blacked out, with vague curves and coils barely visible throughout the artwork, along with hundreds of small white dots covering the mural. Between the dots and coils were what looked like small, barely legible eyes staring back at whoever stood in that basement. I'd never seen anything like it but felt it was meant to depict some kind of writhing mass, somewhere in the stars. 

D and I left that building and radioed for the necessary resources. The county sheriff's homicide division responded and took over the scene. D and I described our contact with Gilbert and Andrew to the handling detective, as well as the presence of an unknown third person who immediately fled the area upon our arrival. The detective said he would contact us if he had any questions and released us from the scene. D and I didn't say a word to each other until we reached the station. 

"See you next week?" I asked. 

"Yeah." 

A few weeks later I was contacted over the phone by the detective handling the mysterious woman's death. 

"Hey brother. So I spoke with Gilbert and Andrew, I'm confident they weren't involved with what you found in that basement. But I'm still trying to ID that third guy who ran from you guys. You said you'd never seen him before?" 

"That's right." 

"Can you describe him again?" 

"Male, white, in his 30's, shaved head, full sleeve tattoos, wearing a white tank top and and blue jean shorts. I didn't get a good look at his face." 

"Fuck. Ok man. Sorry to bother you, I'm just trying to see if I can pick him up on some CCTV." 

"Who was that lady in the basement?" 

"We've been unable to ID so far." 

"Any other updates you're able to share?" 

There was a long pause, "This wasn't the first, likely won't be the last. That's all I can say. Give me a call if you learn anything about the third guy." 

"Will do." 

Some time passed with no word from the homicide division and the rest of July flew by without much incident. By mid-August 2024, in the peak of the summer heat, D and I saw Andrew walking out of a McDonald's. 

"Let's jam him up." D said. We pulled alongside Andrew and exited our vehicle. 

"Andrew, what's up man. What are you up to?" I asked. 

"Sit down Andrew, you have a warrant." D interjected. 

"Dawg are you guys serious? You're going to jam me up with some petty ass shit like that?" 

"Shut up and sit down Andrew. Don't make me force you." 

I pulled D aside. "Dude he has that infection, we can't put him in the car." 

"Andrew, show us your leg." D demanded. 

"No man, come on. I'll leave, I won't come back I swear. I was on my way to a job interview, this is petty man." 

"I'm not asking you, I'm telling you. Show us your leg Andrew." 

Andrew lifted his pant leg, revealing a healed leg. No scars, no scabs, it was as if his leg hadn't been on the verge of falling off just a few weeks ago. 

"Hands on your head Andrew." D ordered. 

Andrew reluctantly complied and we placed him in our patrol car. On our way to the station I tried to make small-talk with Andrew. 

"I see you got your leg fixed. What did they give you? We just saw you last month and I would've thought they were going to take the leg." 

Andrew, less irritated by this point, said: 

"Go to church and find out. I prayed for healing and received. He accepts all with open arms." 

"Where's your church at?" D asked. 

I was confused. D hadn't even mentioned what we found in the house since it happened, much less entertained the connections I felt were present between Bucket's house, the empty tent, and Boogie's statements. 

"Pull up your maps. I'll point it out for you." 

"Just tell me what it's called." 

"You won't find it like that. Pull up your maps." 

We arrived at the station, completed the booking search, and I removed the handcuffs from Andrew. D produced his phone and opened Google Maps, satellite view. He offered his phone to Andrew. Andrew searched the surrounding areas, looking through several hilly areas and dried riverbeds. After 2 minutes, a group of rocky hills about 4 miles north of our town piqued his interest. He moved the screen just passed the hills and stopped over a dried lakebed. 

"Here," Andrew said, pointing his finger at the center of the lakebed. 

D looked up at Andrew. "No, don't fucking play games with me right now. There's nothing there." 

"Trust me, it's there." 

"Just tell me the name of your church and I'll look it up that way." 

"I told you, you're not going to find it like that. Our church has no name." 

There was venom in Andrew's words and the toxins were working through D's veins. We finished booking Andrew and got back in our car. 

"D, you good man?" 

"We're going tonight." 

"Going where?" 

"This place Andrew mentioned." 

"D, you saw the map, there's nothing out there. You really going to trust Andrew's word that there's some unmarked church out in the desert?" 

"I'm going to ask sarge if he's cool with us staying a little later tonight so we can do some enforcement at the parks. We'll go when it gets dark, I'm not riding out there in the heat." 

"Dude, this is not smart. You're suggesting we ride out into the wilderness in search of some church for what? Something is off here and-" 

"Fuck! Stop!" D snapped, "You've been going on about all this bullshit the last few months and now you're hesitant? You heard the same shit I heard in that house, saw the same shit, and you don't want to find out what's going on?" 

"The detectives are handling-" 

"Fuck them. Are you in or not? I'll go by myself if I have to." 

I didn't have it in myself to let D do this alone. Whatever we experienced in that burnt house a few weeks before obviously left its mark on D. He was searching for closure. 

"Alright, I'm in." 

A few years ago, the county had provided our department with two Honda dual sports for use in the parks and offroad areas. D and I used the bikes from time to time, mostly to justify our hazard pay for riding motorcycles and to keep our skills from getting too rusty. Getting our sergeant's approval for a little bit of extra overtime was easy enough, and once the sun started to set we started the bikes. 

"You remember where we're going?" I asked. 

"Yeah." 

Side by side, we rode the bikes to the northern edge of town. 

"We'll get off the road here. Looks to be straight shot from here and just past those hills out there. We'll stop every now and then to check the map." 

"Ok." I replied.
