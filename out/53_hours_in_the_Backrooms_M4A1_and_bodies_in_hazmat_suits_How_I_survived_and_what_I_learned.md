Hey. I know how this sounds.



I wouldn't have believed it myself if I'd read something like this a week ago. But now I have a hockey stick bag under my motel bed with a real M4A1 inside, and my lungs still remember that stale air mixed with ozone and burnt oil.



I'm a 4th-class electrician. I've been working on commercial sites for 12 years. I'm not crazy, and I don't have schizophrenia. I have a photo on my phone that shows a piece of yellow wall with a texture that's impossible to reproduce in reality. But more on that later.



\*\*Part 1. The Entrance\*\*



Last Thursday, I got a job from the management company: de-energize the old power panels on the underground level of the "Galaktika" mall (name changed, I won't name the city).



The electrical diagram was printed on microfilm in 1978. Complete mess: phases mixed up, neutrals broken, no grounding at all.



In one of the corridors, cluttered with old wooden pallets, I noticed a metal door with no handle. Only a keyhole for a square key. This door wasn't on any of the plans.



I moved the pallets aside. One of them fell apart. I counted three layers of paint: green, gray, and yellow.



The keyhole was empty. Just a hole. I stuck a flathead screwdriver in, ran it along the edges. No dust. No rust. Like it had just been drilled.



The door opened with a soft hiss. Like a vacuum had been released.



Instead of the concrete basement wall, I saw a corridor. Endless. Going down at a slight angle. Yellow walls with fine texture, like textured plaster. But to the touch: old plastic. Or hardened wax.



Humidity: exactly 60%. I checked with my Casio watch with a sensor. It worked for another 20 minutes.



Temperature: plus 22 degrees Celsius. No drafts. No air movement.



Light came from everywhere and nowhere. Fluorescent lamps on the ceiling. No flickering. They just glowed faintly. I didn't see any wires or starters.



I took a step inside.



The door closed behind me with the same hiss.



I tried to open it back. No handle on my side either. Flat surface. I knocked with my knuckles. Dull sound. Like behind the wall was not emptiness but several meters of dense material.



\*\*Part 2. The First Hours\*\*



First thing I did: checked my phone. Xiaomi 12, full charge.



The screen turned on. But instead of the main menu: pure blackness. At the very top: a "Searching for network" icon with an empty triangle.



The timer worked. The clock ran. I started the stopwatch to track my time. My internal sense of time was failing immediately.



Second: the compass.



I always carry a small liquid compass on my keychain. Suunto.



The needle twitched within a 20-degree sector. Constantly returning to the same azimuth. Like there was a powerful magnetic pole there.



I dropped an iron coin on the floor. 10 rubles. It stood on its edge. Like it was attracted to the floor. Even swayed slightly, like a pendulum.



Gravity there was normal. But with some strange vector.



The hum appeared in the third minute.



At first I took it for tinnitus from pressure change. I've dived with scuba gear. I know that feeling.



But the ringing didn't go away. It transformed.



It wasn't a sound. It was a vibration at about 40–50 Hz. Felt in the chest. If you closed your eyes, it felt like standing inside a running transformer.



After an hour, the hum became a monotonous background. I learned to ignore it. Like my own breathing.



I walked forward down the corridor.



I took 37 steps. I counted. The corridor smoothly turned right at exactly 90 degrees.



Another 24 steps. A left turn.



I decided to mark the starting point with a scribble on the wall. I took out an Edding 750 marker. Permanent, for wires.



The ink went on fine. But after 5 seconds it just soaked into the wall. Like water into a napkin. No trace.



Then I took out a knife. Scratched the surface.



The groove remained. But the edges started closing up after a couple of minutes. Like the wall was living tissue.



At one of the turns, there was a chair.



A simple office chair on wheels. No branding. Completely new. Even the protective film on the metal legs hadn't been removed.



I took it with me. Use it as a landmark.



After 200 meters, I came across exactly the same chair. Same position. Same film.



I'd left mine behind



\*\*Part 3. The Room with the Bodies\*\*



I wandered for about 4 hours. By my stopwatch. Although time there may run differently.



The corridors started changing. The wallpaper became darker. Almost mustard-colored. In some places, stains appeared. Like rust or grease leaks.



The ceiling dropped from 2.8 to 2.2 meters. I'm 178 cm. I was already feeling uncomfortable.



I entered a zone with metal walls. Sheets of corrugated aluminum. The joints filled with something like epoxy resin.



The air became heavy. A distinct smell appeared: formalin and burnt sugar.



I put on a KN95 mask I had in my tool bag. Barely helped. The smell passed right through the fabric.



The door to the laboratory (I called it that) was sealed. With a steering wheel-like handle. Like on ship compartments.



It took me 5 minutes to unscrew it. It gave way with a metallic screech.



Inside, cold white light was on. Not fluorescent. Some kind of strip lighting around the ceiling perimeter. LED strips. Uniform. No flicker.



The room was about 10 by 12 meters.



In the center: two long aluminum tables with folding sides. On them were devices. Looked like spectrometers and oscilloscopes. I've seen such at factory internships.



But the main thing: the bodies.



I counted 9 bodies.



All in yellow Tychem hazmat suits. Front zippers. Some had dosimeters hanging on their belts.



I know this gear. I worked on asbestos sites. They gave us these suits for demolition work.



They lay in different poses.



One fell face down near the instrument rack. His right hand was still gripping some kind of tube with papers.



The second sat leaning against the wall. Head thrown back. Mouth open.



The third: on his back. Arms crossed on his chest. Like a corpse in a coffin.



No signs of violence. No blood. No burns. No bruises. Just a stop.



One of them: a young guy. I saw his face. Sparse mustache. A mole on his left cheek.



His eyes were open. Pupils of different sizes.



Left pupil: 2 mm. Right: 6 mm.



I remember this symptom from first aid training. Cerebral edema. Or a stroke.



But all of them? That can't be.



The ID badges were all turned or torn off.



I found one that had caught on the collar of a suit.



Photo: a woman about 40. Blonde. Stern look.



Name: "Dr. A. Morrison."



Organization name: "Argo Dynamics."



Below, a logo: three gears inscribed in a triangle.



The badge had an issue date: 02/01/2023.



And an expiration date: 12/31/2024.



That means they knew the date of their death. Or the date the project ended.



Against the far wall stood a Pelican 1700 weapons case.



I know this model. Waterproof. Shockproof. Used by special forces.



The case wasn't locked. The two side latches clicked open with a light press.



I opened it.



Inside lay an M4A1.



Not a "semi-automatic rifle" with a limiter. A full automatic carbine. Judging by the markings, capable of burst fire.



The handguard had a mount for a light and laser. But the light was missing. Just an empty Picatinny rail.



The stock was telescopic. Six-position. Marked "B5 Systems."



The barrel was clean. No carbon. Clearly unfired. Or perfectly cleaned.



On the bolt carrier, I noticed a small scratch. Like wear from extracting casings. The weapon had been used.



Next to it lay:



\- 3 PMAG 30 polymer magazines. With windows for round count. Marked "Gen 3."

\- A box of 5.56x45mm rounds. 55-grain, FMJ, 120 rounds. I counted: exactly 4 boxes of 30.

\- A can of silicone gun lubricant.

\- And a piece of paper, folded in four.



The very one. On which, in trembling handwriting, with ink splatters, was written:



\*"It doesn't kill them. It disorients. RUN."\*



I didn't immediately understand what was written. My eyes clung to every letter.



The handwriting was feminine. Slanting to the right. Probably Dr. Morrison. Or someone from her team.



Under the note lay a Polaroid photo.



It showed a corridor very similar to the one I had walked through.



But in the photo, in the depth of the corridor, stood a tall figure. Blurred. As if the object moved during the shot.



The figure was white. Without clear contours. Just a spot.



The back of the photo was blank.



I took the rifle.



It weighed about 3.5 kg. Real combat weight.



I checked the safety. A lever marked SAFE/SEMI/AUTO.



Switched to SEMI. Pulled the charging handle.



A round went into the chamber with a pleasant metallic click.



I immediately ejected the magazine. Removed the round. Switched back to SAFE.



I didn't want an accidental discharge.



At that moment, the hum in the room changed.



It became higher in pitch. As if overtones had been added.



The ceiling light dimmed for a second. Came back on.



I turned my head toward the exit.



One of the bodies lying face down: his right hand twitched.



It wasn't a death spasm.



It was a conscious movement. He started pulling his knee under himself.



I didn't wait.



I realized that "they" in hazmat suits weren't dead. They were inactive.



And the sound of the weapon. Its metallic click. Activated them.



\*\*Part 4. The Chase\*\*



I bolted out of the lab. Sealed the wheel behind me. Turned it all the way. As far as I could.



In the corridor, I ran straight.



I held the M4 at the ready. Stock against my shoulder. Finger on the trigger. But off the safety.



I remembered the rule: safety goes off right before firing.



Behind me, a sound came.



Not footsteps.



It was the sound of a wet rag sliding on metal.



I looked back over my left shoulder.



At the end of the corridor where I'd just been, a figure began to seep out of the opening.



It wasn't flat, as I'd expected. Three-dimensional. Like it was made of viscous gray clay.



It didn't move by walking. It had no legs. It flowed. Like liquid concrete. Leaving behind an oily trail that slowly evaporated.



I ran faster.



My knees started hurting after a couple of minutes. The soles of my boots (CAT, high-top) squealed desperately on the linoleum.



The air was thick. Viscous. Running there was like running knee-deep in water.



I was exhausted after 200 meters. But I couldn't stop.



The first skirmish happened after 5 minutes of running.



I burst into a room with three exits. Realized I was lost.



And it. A "Skinner." I later read that name on forums.



It appeared from the wall to my left.



It just leaked out of the wallpaper. Like sweat from a surface.



Its face. I saw it for 2 seconds. It was a human face. But without eyelids. Without lips.



Skin stretched tight over the skull. The mouth was just a slit.



It made no sounds. But I heard its body hissing on contact with the floor.



I raised the weapon.



My finger automatically switched the lever to SEMI.



I fired into center mass.



The sound of a gunshot in the Backrooms. It's something else.



In an enclosed space, a normal shot gives about 160 dB of sound pressure.



But there, the walls didn't reflect sound. They absorbed it. Transformed it into a hum.



When I pulled the trigger, I felt my ears plug up. For a second, everything swam before my eyes.



The casing. 5.56mm. Marked "LC 22." Lake City, 2022.



It bounced right. Hit the wall. Left a black spot that instantly soaked in.



The bullet entered the figure.



At the impact point, a cavity appeared. Like I'd hit jelly.



The figure rocked back. Its contours became fuzzy. It seemed to lose focus.



It didn't explode. It didn't die. It just slowed down. As if disoriented.



But after 3 seconds, it started pulling itself back together.



And from the walls behind it, two more began to leak out.



I didn't wait.



I bolted into the right passage.



They pursued me further.



It wasn't a chase in the literal sense. They didn't run fast. Their speed was about a brisk walk.



But there were more and more of them.



Every corridor I ran through left a trail of sliding figures behind me.



They appeared from the ceiling. From the floor. From cracks between walls.



I had the feeling they weren't chasing. They were converging on the sound.



At one intersection, I stopped for a second. To catch my breath.



Behind me, about 20 meters away, I saw a column of 4 or 5 Skinners. Lined up in a row. Moving toward me in sync.



They all had the same faces.



My faces.



I remember this for life. I saw five reflections of myself. But with open mouths. Stretched into unnatural grins.



It wasn't a mirror reflection. It was a caricature of me. Of my exhaustion and fear.



I fired a long burst of 7 rounds.



The bullets passed through them. Left holes.



But I realized it was pointless. They regenerate in 5–7 seconds.



However. The sound of the shots confused them. They started darting from side to side. Losing orientation. Like blind puppies.



One of them bumped into a wall. Just "drove" into it. Merged with the surface.



At that moment, I understood their main weakness.



They have no sense organs in the usual sense. They navigate by vibration and sound frequency.



If you create a cacophony, they lose the target.



I started using that.



I fired short bursts of 2–3 rounds. Keep them confused.



Each shot gave me 5–7 seconds of lead time. In that time, I ran 20–30 meters.



When they started catching up, I fired another burst.



I went through 1.5 magazines. About 45 rounds. During the time I was getting out of the labyrinth.



\*\*Part 5. The Exit\*\*



In the third hour of the chase (by my stopwatch), I ended up in a zone where the wallpaper wasn't yellow.



It was whitish. With some bluish tint. Like an operating room.



The formalin smell was gone. Replaced by fresh paint and metal.



The hum weakened.



I was reloading the last magazine when I noticed a strange device on the ceiling.



An old Soviet IP-212 fire detector. The kind installed in the 80s.



It was bolted firmly to the plastic ceiling. But its red indicator was blinking.



This was the first flashing light I'd seen in over 50 hours.



I raised the rifle. Aimed at the detector.



The last 3 rounds.



Shot.



The bullet shattered the plastic body. From inside, with force (as if they had pressure), not water but light poured out.



White. Bright. Like a welding arc.



The walls around began to peel off. Like old paint.



I shielded my eyes with my hand. Ran forward by feel.



In this light, I saw a door.



It wasn't yellow. It was wooden. With number "302" embossed in gold. And a regular round knob.



The knob turned.



I yanked the door open. Tumbled onto the floor. Hit my head on concrete.



I was lying in the basement of the "Galaktika" mall.



Pallets were scattered nearby.



My watch showed exactly 7 minutes had passed since I'd opened that door.



My head was wet with sweat. I was breathing heavily.



In my hands, I was clutching the M4A1.



The bolt was open. The last round had gone into the detector. The magazine was empty.



I checked the carbine. The barrel had a slight heat haze. The weapon had indeed been fired.



I gathered the remaining casings. There were 8. I found them in my pocket. I'd automatically picked them up. To leave no traces.



They were hot. With carbon.



I hid everything in my bag.



\*\*What I Learned\*\*



\*\*1. The weapon works.\*\* But not as a killing tool. It's like a taser for the space. The sound and shockwave disrupt the monsters' "anchor" to reality. But they regenerate. Don't waste rounds on kills. Waste them on suppression.



\*\*2. The bodies in the lab.\*\* They were researchers. They were studying the Backrooms. Trying to find a way to close it. Or use its properties. Judging by the note, they were desperate. They knew there was a way out. But they didn't make it.



\*\*3. "Argo Dynamics."\*\* I Googled it. Found wifi in the motel. It's a private defense company. Based in the States. But it shut down in 2024. No website. No contacts. Strange, right?



\*\*4. The pollen.\*\* From the wallpaper. It does glow under UV light. I checked my jacket with a UV flashlight. Bought at a 24-hour store. My whole back and shoulders are glowing yellow. I washed the jacket with bleach 3 times. The glow faded. But didn't disappear completely. I don't know what that means yet.



Right now I'm sitting in the motel. Writing this on my laptop.



My hands are shaking. But I'm forcing myself to type.



I don't know if they'll come back for me.



But I put the M4 under my pillow. Loaded a fresh magazine. Just in case.



Don't look for that door.



If you see yellow wallpaper: go the other way.



And never open doors without handles.



If you have any info on "Argo Dynamics" or anyone knows how to wash off that yellow pollen: DM me.



I'll be at this motel for another couple of days. Until I decide where to go next.



Take care of yourselves.



Reality is fragile.



I know that 100% now.
