Working at a nursing home has always been fulfilling for me. My favorite resident is an old man named Joe. He has dementia, so sometimes he doesn't recognize where he is or even who he is. Other times, he's the same old Joe I've always known.

Over the past few days, though, his condition has started to deteriorate rapidly. He's become completely disoriented.

A few days ago, I was in his room when he suddenly burst into tears.

"Emma, you have to leave. If you don't, it's going to come for you. It says it's been watching you every day. It's getting stronger and stronger. I'm so scared of it."

I had to calm him down and give him sleeping medication because he was absolutely terrified. I won't pretend I didn't feel uneasy about the whole thing, but I knew the kinds of things people in his condition could say.

The next day, he was doing better, so I asked him what he'd meant when he told me I should leave. Fear flashed in his eyes, but he said he didn't remember saying anything like that and that he would miss me terribly if I left. We talked for a long time before I went home.

I ran a bath and sank into the hot water. Out of the corner of my eye, I suddenly noticed a shadow behind the glass door. I jerked around, but there was no one on the other side. Still, I quickly climbed out of the tub and searched the apartment. Nobody was there.

I spent the rest of the evening with the unsettling feeling that I wasn't alone.

That night, I had a horrible dream. Joe was sitting on a grave in the middle of a cemetery, laughing hysterically. When I looked closer, I noticed my name engraved on the headstone.

Then his laughter suddenly changed into a voice I knew all too well.

"I like it when you're afraid."

I woke with a violent start. I was drenched in sweat, and it took me a long time to catch my breath. I couldn't fall asleep again. By the time I went to work, I was exhausted.

It was a long day, and Joe was having one of his bad episodes. He sat on his bed, rocking back and forth.

"Emma, I can't hold him back much longer. I barely managed to push him back today. He was so close."

I stared at him in confusion.

"Who was close, Joe? What are you talking about?"

He looked at me, his eyes filled with fear.

"What can I do to help you?" I asked.

"When it comes, you mustn't call it by my name," he whispered.

Then he had another episode.

A few hours later, he was like a completely different person. I ran into him in the hallway and asked what he was doing there.

"I wanted to apologize for earlier. I haven't really been myself."

"It's okay," I told him.

I was working the night shift, and even though it was only ten o'clock, I briefly dozed off.

In my dream, Joe stood in front of me, crying.

"Emma, you mustn't call it by my name."

When I blinked, he was gone. But then I heard his voice right behind me.

"Emma!"

I turned around. His expression was horrifying. He lunged at me and began strangling me. I tried to push him away, but then I woke up.

Except something wasn't right.

Joe was kneeling over me, gripping my throat with both hands. His face was twisted with rage, and his fingers tightened around my neck.

Luckily, a security guard noticed us and rushed over, immediately pulling Joe away. Other staff members helped me sit up while security escorted him away.

I clutched my throat, gasping for air. Tears filled my eyes, and my entire body was shaking.

Meanwhile, Joe had stopped resisting. He glanced back at me over his shoulder.

And smiled.

I was sent home for the rest of the evening. Still trembling, I unlocked my apartment door and took a hot bath. By the time I'd calmed down a little, it was already two in the morning. I still couldn't sleep, so I put on some romantic comedy.

My eyelids were slowly beginning to close when I heard footsteps. It sounded as though someone was walking around in the hallway. I held my breath and closed my eyes. I pretended to be asleep, but then I heard Joe's voice.

"Emma, I know you're awake."

I opened my eyes and screamed.

This wasn't the Joe I knew. He was deathly pale, and a twisted smile played across his face. His eyes were the worst part. They had always been kind, the color of the sky on a summer day. Now, there was something vile in them, and they were completely black.

I stared at him, frozen and unable to speak. He began moving toward me. I jumped out of bed and ran. He didn't even try to chase me. He simply followed at a slow, steady pace.

His voice seemed to shake the entire building.

"You can't escape me!"

I remembered what Joe had told me.

You mustn't call it by my name.

I was almost at the door when the footsteps behind me suddenly stopped. I opened the door, and there he was.

"Tell me what I am, Emma!"

I shook my head, slammed into him as hard as I could, and bolted out of the apartment.

I could still hear him screaming after me.

"He might have told you what not to say, but he never told you what happens afterward!"

I pounded on my neighbor's door, and he called the police.

There was nobody in my apartment. When the officers called the nursing home, they told me that Joe had died of cardiac arrest at two that morning.
