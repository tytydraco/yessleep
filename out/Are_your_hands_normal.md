I need your help. Please. Just answer this question: Are your hands normal? I just need to know that I’m not alone, that there are other people out there with my problem.



Everything started a little over two months ago. I had just woken up and was going through my morning routine when I noticed a pimple on the back of my right hand. I'd never seen a pimple like this one. It was plump, and instead of the greenish yellow color of your standard pimple, this one was black. It was at least twice the size of any other pimple I’ve had. The surrounding skin was tight as it stretched to accommodate the mass.



I stared at it, more impressed than worried. I tenderly touched it and watched as it moved slightly as the dark liquid within shifted. Taking a little band-aid, I covered it up and moved on with my day. Hours later, halfway through the workday, I couldn’t stop thinking about it. It was the type of pimple that would be very satisfying to pop. Pulling back the bandage, I was shocked to see that it had changed. It had grown both taller and wider, and in its center, a long, thick black hair stood. The hair was at least three times as thick as the hair on my head and just as long.



I grasped the hair and pulled hard. It was rooted deep in the skin. It was like pulling a loose thread from a shirt; it just kept coming. Eventually it came free, and with its exit, thick black pus began to leak out. I pinched the pimple until the pus ran dry. Returning to work, the strange pimple quickly left my mind.



When I woke the next day, I realized something was wrong. Now my hand had three pimples—one on the back, one on the palm, and one on a finger. All three were far more swollen than the one from the day before. I felt my palms go clammy as I saw the start of thick black hairs poking through the three. Taking tweezers, I pulled the hairs out and drained the mounds. It was then that I decided to schedule an appointment with a dermatologist. Since it was the start of the weekend, the earliest I could book was Monday afternoon.



The rest of that day, there was no real change, though my hand ached with the burning feeling of an internal infection. But when I woke the next day, panic set. My hand was completely swollen. It was nearly double in size. My fingers were straining to move, and I couldn’t close my hand. The skin was smooth and tight; it was now darker in color, a sickly grey. I spent the day icing my hand in a futile attempt to reverse the swelling.



The next day was Sunday, and when I woke up, my hand was unrecognizable. It was as if one enormous black pimple had swallowed the entirety of my hand and wrist. I was losing feeling in the hand; early in the day, it tingled as if it were asleep. But by the time evening came, I found myself pinching the stretched skin, trying to feel anything. Right before bedtime, my fingers developed some strange twitches. They moved back and forth, open and closed completely on their own, without my direction. I hoped it was just a side effect of strained muscles in my hand.



In the morning, the twitching was worse; the fingers moved violently too and fro. And when they weren’t moving, they vibrated nonstop. I called in sick that day and iced my hand till it was time to go to my appointment. I walked into the doctor's office and walked over to the desk to check in.



“Hi, I’m here for my appointment,” I said, forcing a smile.



The receptionist looked at me and then at my hand; disgust filled her face, and I subconsciously pulled my sleeve down to hide it.



“Great.” She replied, “Follow me back, and the doctor will be right with you.”



I had barely been in the examination room five minutes before the doctor entered and got to work.



“Alright, what seems to be the problem?’ he said with the cadence of a man who had seen it all.



Only this seemed to stump him. He stared at my hand, examined its skin, and stared intently at the leaking pus. He watched in confusion as my hand violently twitched. At the end of the time, he said with all the confidence in the world:



“It seems that you have some sort of deep-seated infection, and it's straining your muscles, causing tremors. I’m going to prescribe some pretty strong antibiotics, and write you a note to get you some days off work to rest. Sound good?”



I nodded



“Whatever you say, doc.”



The first round of antibiotics made me dizzy and lightheaded. By the time dinner rolled around, I had developed a bad fever, bad enough to send me into a restless sleep. The next few days were filled with a feverish mix of waking dread and fitful sleep. My fever grew worse, and most of the time I wasn’t sure if I was awake or dreaming. The entire time, my hand grew worse. Its fingers were no longer twitching but now simply moving on their own. The dark purple skin was now hidden beneath a dense carpet of thick bristle-like hairs, and vibrated and shook as if they sensed movement.



In my feverish state, I swear late one night I woke to my hairy hand attacking me. It lay flat and heavy directly on my face with the palm covering my nose and mouth. The fingers crawled wildly along my face. It was lack of oxygen that woke me, and in a panic I threw the assailant from me before I realized that my attacker was part of me.



It took days for the fever to break. And when it did, my hand was dead. It was buried beneath rough, shuddering hair. I no longer had any feeling or ability to move any inch of my hand. It was like a dead branch on a live tree. The skin just below the wrist was a bracelet of decaying grey skin. I slowly reached out my other hand to touch this dividing line. It felt like dry, crunchy leaves. The moment I touched it, it shuddered and snapped under my touch. There was no blood. No bone. No exposed muscle. Just a dark, empty cavity where my wrist should have been. With a dry, crunchy plop, my right hand fell from my body and onto the floor. It didn’t hurt; I couldn’t feel it, but the shock of having one of my limbs just peel itself from me was enough to cause my throat to fill with vomit. I fled to the bathroom to release my stomach into the toilet. I vomited until I had nothing left to give. Slowly, I walked back to reclaim my hand, but it was gone. The spot it had been was damp with black pus, but there was no hand, and it was nowhere to be seen.



Over the next couple of weeks, I only saw passing glimpses of my hand. It was roaming my apartment, but it didn’t want me to see it. Now and then, I would see a finger peeking out from around a door frame, or notice it lurching from above the fridge or beneath the sink. It was changing, becoming something else, something that had started to grow when it was still part of my body. It had grown far larger, now becoming larger than a basketball. The fingers had grown fatter and more leg-like as it scampers across the floor. It no longer has five ‘fingers’; now it has eight. Or at least I think there are eight. It’s watching me; I’ve heard it creeping in the bathroom while I take showers. I hear it crawling beneath my bed while it thinks I’m sleeping.



A few days ago it grew bolder. It’s no longer hiding in the shadows or staying in the corner of my vision. Now it’s roaming freely; it’s no longer scared of me. Even now as I write this, it’s over in the corner watching me; its fat, hairy legs that used to be fingers move restlessly as if it’s preparing for action. It’s chattering to itself; it sounds like fingernails being tapped together. The eight eyes that it grew stare intently at me. They are blacker than the eyes of a shark, and filled with a strange mix of wrath and pride, like it's looking at a prize it will soon win, or an insect caught in its web, soon to be devoured.



Again, I need your help. Are your hands normal? And if they aren’t. If your hands are like mine, what did you do when it stopped being your hand? And more importantly, what did you do when pimples and hair started showing up on the rest of your body?
