This isn’t a story I made up. It’s something I actually experienced when I was a child.

I’m Chinese. In 2008, when the devastating Wenchuan earthquake struck, the area where we lived was hit very hard. The apartment building we were living in was damaged as well, and part of the top floor collapsed.

For reasons I never really understood, the people who inspected the building apparently decided that the structure as a whole was still safe enough to remain standing. So instead of demolishing it, the plan was simply to clear away the rubble on the roof and repair the damaged area.

The problem was that the earthquake had caused destruction on an enormous scale. Buildings, roads, and infrastructure everywhere needed repairs, so the rubble on top of our building remained there for quite a long time before it was finally cleared.

That was when strange things started happening.  
Almost every night, we could hear what sounded like someone climbing the stairs.

I should explain a little about our building. It wasn’t very tall, there weren’t many residents, and most of us had lived there for years. Everyone basically knew everyone else. So if someone was walking upstairs late at night, it usually wasn’t difficult to figure out who it was.

But these footsteps were strange.

They were extremely heavy, and you could clearly hear them climbing upward, one step at a time. They would continue all the way through the stairwell toward the top floor.

The families who lived closest to the roof said that almost every night, they could hear heavy footsteps above them, as if someone were walking back and forth up there.

But the problem was that the top floor had already collapsed.

At the time, it was basically a dangerous pile of rubble that had not yet been completely cleared away.

Who would go up there in the middle of the night and walk around?

Eventually, the residents started asking one another whether anyone had been going upstairs at night. Everyone said no. As far as we could tell, nobody in the building was regularly climbing up to the ruined top floor after dark.

And before the earthquake, we had never heard anything like this.

It continued for a long time.

I still remember one particular night. I had stayed up late playing in the living room. The building was already very quiet when I suddenly heard footsteps coming from the stairwell.

They were incredibly heavy.

One step.  
Then another.  
Slowly climbing upward.

The sound was so loud and so distinct that I can still remember it today.

It has been almost twenty years, but I still remember exactly what those footsteps felt like to hear.  
Most people in my family, and most of our neighbors, were not particularly superstitious. Many of them were atheists.

So at first, everyone decided to ignore it.  
Maybe it was something caused by the damaged structure. Maybe some kind of noise in the building was being amplified at night. People assumed that whatever it was, it would probably disappear on its own.

But it didn’t.  
Night after night, those heavy footsteps kept returning.  
They would climb the stairs toward the top floor, which was still nothing but rubble.

And according to the neighbors living closest to the roof, the footsteps would then continue moving back and forth above them, as though someone were pacing around.

Eventually, people had had enough.

Some of the residents suggested finding a trustworthy Buddhist monk or Taoist priest to come and take a look. It wasn’t necessarily because everyone suddenly believed in ghosts. It was more that nobody could come up with a convincing explanation for what was happening anymore.

In the end, they did bring in a Buddhist monk.  
I don’t remember exactly what he did. I was young at the time, and it’s also possible that my family simply didn’t involve me in that part of it.

But there is one thing I do remember:  
After that, the footsteps stopped.

Because of everything that had happened, the residents also pushed harder to have the rubble removed and the damaged roof properly repaired.  
Even today, I still don’t know what those sounds really were.

Was it somehow connected to people who had died in the earthquake and could not find peace?  
Or had the earthquake damaged the structure in some unusual way, causing steel, concrete, pipes, or other parts of the building to produce sounds at night that happened to resemble human footsteps?  
I honestly don’t know.

Later, for unrelated reasons, my parents and I moved away from that building.  
But over the years, I have occasionally gone back to see it.  
The building is still standing there today.
