The Gator-man Papa Caiman

I was lost in New Orleans at night, and I wandered near some swamp town I had never heard of. I had a few drinks and was dazed after waking up with a sore head, like I had gotten into a fight or fallen.

I was so dazed I thought I was back home in Australia, walking around trying to find home. I started to hear sirens in the background, but after the sirens I heard buzzing in my ears, like I had tinnitus, when I passed a certain part of the swamp where a raggedy old house lay, or should I say shack. I was scared when I saw voodoo dolls hanging from the door of this swamp shack and a raven skull spray-painted on the side. Then, out of a dazed curiosity, I opened the door. Only a weak chain was stopping me from opening it, but I managed to force it open. I saw, sitting on a chair, a bald Creole-looking man with cargo pants, boots, and a tattooed-looking chest that looked like scales and alligators painted on it.

He was drinking, and he was smiling at me. He spoke in a Creole accent and said something like, "Have you been looking for me?"

I said, "No, sorry sir, I'll show myself out."

As I headed for the door, his large hand stopped me from turning the doorknob. He spoke again:

"Well, don't go out until it's light. There are a lot of haints, sorcerers and gris-gris men out at these hours."

I did not know what a haint was at the time until I searched it up. It was some spirit. The bald Creole then tried what look like to steal from me rummaging through my bag. I felt too dazed to fight back well enough. He was strong as hell. I then heard the croak of gators outside and what sounded like whispers. The Creole man then ran outside to open the door, and I must have passed out.

I then awoke inside the ole shack, which was now empty. I walked out, and there were no longer any voodoo dolls on the door. I checked my bag to see if anything was missing and saw a gator-looking talisman inside, which I have no idea how it got there. I made my way back to the hotel not long after having gotten over my stupor.

I remember I came across a few armed robbers with knives and crowbars who had beaten and ended the lives of four men in an alley. I ran to help, as I thought they had run away. I then heard a whisper. It sounded like a ghostly Creole accent speaking in French, and then, as I turned, I saw two of the three men lying on the ground with pythons around them, and the third one had pissed himself, screaming.

I have since had that talisman in the backpack, and only now, three weeks later, have I had the courage to inspect it more. It was written in Creole, and I translated it. It said, "From Papa Caiman the Gator-man ,to the his blessed friend."
