I've been a music lover for fifteen years now. I have a dedicated room for my CDs and vinyl records, and I can confidently say I listen to at least an album a day. Music is one of my biggest joys, but lately it's been making me feel isolated and paranoid.

For the last two weeks I've been experiencing what I can only describe as auditory hallucinations. I'll put on a record, and after two to three tracks I start to hear this awful wailing noise. It's always faded in the background, but impossible to ignore. 

I pride myself on picking up little details in songs, but this is happening regardless of the album I put on. The strangest part is that the noise doesn't have spatial logic to it. It's not coming out of the speakers, as if it exists within the crackle of the vinyl itself. 

I tried switching the stylus and cleaning it as much as I could but nothing changed. Even if the old one was dirty it wouldn't explain why there's a consistent delay before the noise starts.

My mom came over to visit earlier this week, and I took the opportunity to do some troubleshooting. I put on "Rumours" by Fleetwood Mac since moms are legally required to love it. Sure enough, "Don't Stop" came on and with it began the noise. For such a motivational song, it felt especially disturbing to hear a voice so full of despair underneath it. Like it was mocking me, telling me it wouldn't end.

And then this line came up.

"If your life was bad to you 

just think what tomorrow will do."

Previously this had read to me as a cheeky call-out to the people who constantly complain. Somebody somewhere always has it worse, and there's a peace that comes with having a sense of humour about the bad times. This time though, it felt like an omen. I could even swear the noise was a bit louder during it.

How crazy is this? Being scared of Fleetwood Mac of all things? My brain was filled with contradictory thoughts, feeling ridiculous but truly anxious and unsettled. I still feel crazy for even posting this, I'm probably overreacting. This might all be completely manufactured by my brain. Could I be sleep deprived? 

Mom must have seen my confused face, because she joked about the song "not being that bad." I didn't laugh, instead awkwardly turning it off and changing the subject. If she didn't hear it then it must be in my head right? 

After she left I got curious about the method I was using, figured maybe I should change a variable. So I plugged in my CD player and dug out my old headphones. Over-ears, obviously. I actually own a copy of "Rumours" on CD as well. I don't think that's unreasonable given its iconic status, but it was perfect for the sake of direct comparison. 

To cut to the chase, it was still there. Same song and everything. Even in a different medium I was still hearing it. It's almost like a zombie groaning, but it has the tone of someone mid-yawn. Not too far off from someone making a fake ghost noise, but injected with genuine dread. 

After removing the headphones I sat and collected my thoughts for a moment. That didn't last long though, since I had the absolute worst migraine of my life only a few minutes later. Even after three Advils my head pounded for hours after. That was the last time I've been in my music room.

I realize this doesn't sound all that concerning, but you have to understand this is driving me crazy. This is my passion. My main source of joy. Suddenly I find every attempt at engaging with it tainted by something I have no explanation for. All of my routines have been thrown off from this. I always used to fall asleep to music, complete paperwork while listening, and exercise with headphones on. 

There was almost never a time where I wasn't listening to music, whether intently or in the background. Now I'm forced to reckon with the silence of my own mind. Is this always what I'm like? Is this who I am without music? 

The only logical option is that I'm the source of the problem. It's terrifying to think this happened so quickly. Just days ago I was fine. It feels like one of those monkey's paw situations. Or that episode of The Twilight Zone where the guy has all the time in the world left to read, only to break his glasses. To have everything you are and suddenly have nothing left. You never realize how precious some things are until they become rotten and decayed.

An hour or so ago I was Googling stuff about hearing noises that aren't there, and a lot of the results were for things like schizophrenia. I can't imagine it's that, since none of the other symptoms line up. 

The migraine makes sense. It could just be that I'm listening to too much music. I wouldn't say I play it at an abnormally loud volume, but that's the kind of rational thing doctors say right? Ironic that what we love the most comes back to bite us in the end.

My grandpa on my dad's side had Parkinson's, so I'm wondering if maybe that has something to do with it. Maybe a similar disease like Alzheimer's runs in my family. 30 years old is an early onset but at least it would be an explanation.

I think that's it. I might just have to accept that there are pieces of me that are going to be unsalvageable. I guess if any of you have advice or can confirm what could be wrong with me then please let me know. Not really sure what will come if this post but it's worth trying. It just feels so stupid. All the stories of people slowly going mad out there and here I am with my silly little record player. 

All I know is that I feel empty, perhaps the most I've ever felt. Music was my only real comfort, and it's poisoned now. Or I'm the poison. Does it matter which is true? 

In the meantime I think I'll give it another shot. I'd rather risk another headache than feel this disconnected.
