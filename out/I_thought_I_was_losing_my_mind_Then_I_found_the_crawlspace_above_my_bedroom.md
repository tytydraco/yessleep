I debated posting this because it still doesn’t feel real. The police couldn’t explain it, my family thinks stress made me imagine most of it, and I honestly wish that were true.  
About four years ago, I bought a small two-story house just outside town. It was suspiciously cheap, but the inspection came back clean. The realtor mentioned the previous owner had lived there alone for decades before passing away from natural causes. Nothing strange. Just an old house.  
The first year was completely normal.  
The second year is when things started happening.  
I’d wake up and find kitchen cabinets open. At first I blamed myself. Then I started noticing food disappearing. Not a lot—just enough to make me question my memory. Half a loaf of bread. A couple slices of cheese. Leftover chicken.  
I even accused my brother of sneaking in as a joke.  
He laughed and reminded me he lived three hours away.  
Then my dog started acting strangely.  
Every night around 3 a.m., she’d sit at the bottom of the attic ladder and growl. Not bark. Not whine. Just stare upward without blinking.  
She’d refuse treats.  
She’d refuse to move.  
The moment the sun came up, she’d act completely normal again.  
I called a vet.  
Perfectly healthy.  
I ignored it.  
Old houses make noises. Dogs are weird.  
Then I started hearing footsteps.  
Very slow footsteps.  
Always above my bedroom.  
I’d grab a flashlight, pull down the attic ladder, and search every corner.  
Nothing.  
No footprints.  
No animals.  
Nothing.  
Eventually I convinced myself the wood was settling.  
Months later I installed security cameras inside the house.  
Living room.  
Kitchen.  
Hallway.  
Front door.  
Back door.  
Every morning I’d skim through the footage.  
Nothing.  
Until one night.  
At exactly 3:17 a.m., every camera froze.  
Forty-three seconds later they came back online.  
The footage skipped ahead like nothing had happened.  
Except one dining chair had moved halfway across the room.  
I contacted the camera company.  
They blamed my Wi-Fi.  
So I replaced the router.  
The freezes continued.  
Always around 3 a.m.  
Always less than a minute.  
I started sleeping with my bedroom door locked.  
One morning I woke up and the lock was still engaged.  
But my bedroom closet door was wide open.  
I know I closed it.  
Inside the closet was a muddy footprint.  
Just one.  
Barefoot.  
Too large to be mine.  
That’s when I stopped laughing about everything.  
I called the police.  
They searched the entire house.  
Nothing.  
No signs of forced entry.  
No missing windows.  
No hidden doors.  
One officer actually suggested sleepwalking.  
I almost believed him.  
Then things got worse.  
I’d hear whispering through the vents.  
Not words.  
Just two people quietly talking.  
Every time I got close enough to understand what they were saying…  
Silence.  
One afternoon I came home from work earlier than usual.  
The house smelled different.  
Like sweat.  
Like someone had been living there.  
My dog was standing in the hallway, staring at the ceiling and trembling so hard her nails were clicking against the floor.  
I decided I’d finally inspect every inch of the attic.  
Near the back corner, hidden under old insulation, I noticed plywood that didn’t match the rest of the floor.  
When I pulled it up, I found a narrow crawlspace.  
Just wide enough for a person.  
Inside was a sleeping bag.  
Empty food wrappers.  
Water bottles.  
A flashlight.  
Phone chargers.  
Fresh batteries.  
A small camping stove.  
Someone had been living there.  
For how long…  
I don’t know.  
Then I found the photographs.  
Hundreds of them.  
Some were years old.  
They showed me mowing the lawn.  
Watching TV.  
Cooking dinner.  
Sleeping.  
There were photos taken through my bedroom doorway.  
Photos taken from inside my bedroom.  
One showed me asleep while someone stood only a few feet away.  
The shadow of their legs was visible across my blanket.  
I immediately climbed down and called 911.  
The police searched the crawlspace.  
Whoever had been there was gone.  
They estimated someone had been living inside my walls for months, maybe years.  
They collected fingerprints.  
DNA.  
Everything.  
Nothing matched anyone in the system.  
I moved out that same week.  
The house was eventually sold.  
I never went back.  
Or at least I thought I never would.  
Three weeks ago, I received a plain white envelope with no return address.  
Inside was a single Polaroid.  
It showed my new apartment building.  
The photo had been taken from across the street.  
I assumed it was some sick prank until I flipped it over.  
Written neatly in black marker were six words.  
**“This place has thinner walls.”**
