My name is Skye. I live in a suburb just north of Seattle, in an apartment complex that backs up against a stretch of woods. Beyond the trees there’s a graveyard beside a small lake.

It was a cool September night when I stepped out of my apartment to take out the recycling. The streetlights on one side of the complex had been shut off while maintenance work was being done. The boundary between the apartment complex and the trees was mostly hedges, and without the streetlights, the whole side of the property was darker than usual. The recycling dumpsters sat close to the hedge, right where the apartment grounds gave way to the woods.

I dropped off the recycling. Then, from somewhere beyond the hedge, I heard a low, deep whisper.

“Hey, man.”

I stopped. I pulled my flashlight from my pocket and swept the beam across the hedge, then farther into the trees. Nothing.

“Who’s there?”

No answer. The woods stayed completely still. I left in a hurry.

The next day, I decided to investigate. I wanted to see what was back there while it was light out. In daylight, the whole thing seemed a little ridiculous. I followed the woods toward where I thought the voice had come from. Beyond the trees sat the graveyard, quiet beside the lake. There was nothing unusual about it. Rows of stones. Old trees. Grass moving in the breeze. The lake beyond them.

I walked around looking for anything that might explain what I’d heard. A trail. Someone camping nearby. A house through the trees. None of it explained the voice. I wandered a little deeper among the graves. The longer I stayed, the more I felt that I shouldn’t. Just that quiet, instinctive feeling of being somewhere a little longer than you were welcome.

Then I heard someone breathe directly behind me, close enough that I could almost feel it against the back of my neck. I spun around. Nobody. Only gravestones. I stood frozen in place. Listening. Silence. Eventually, I headed home. When I looked back, the graveyard was exactly as I’d found it. Empty. Still.

That night, the streetlights were working again. That made the place feel less threatening. A week passed. By then, everything felt ordinary again. I carried the bag of recycling down around the same time as before and dumped it into the bin. The instant the lid fell shut, the streetlights flickered. Then every light along that side of the complex went out. The last glow faded from the pavement. It took my eyes a moment to adjust. I stood there for a few seconds longer than I needed to. Nothing.

Then, from somewhere deep in the woods:

“Hey, man.”

I stopped. The same low, deep whisper. I raised my flashlight toward the trees. I couldn’t see anyone.

Then another voice answered.

“Hey, man.”

But this one was farther away. Much farther. From the direction of the graveyard. I went back inside.

A few days later, I mentioned it to an older guy who had lived in the complex for years. I expected him to laugh.

He didn’t.

“What did it say?”

“Just ‘hey man.’”

His expression changed. “Both times?”

“Yeah.”

“Did you answer it?”

I hesitated. “The first night. I said, ‘Who’s there?’”

He stared at me. “Oh, no.”

“What?”

“You shouldn’t have done that.”

I laughed a little. “Why?”

He didn’t laugh back. “Because now it knows you can hear it.”

“What knows?”

The man glanced toward the hedge. “If you hear it again, don’t answer. Doesn’t matter what it says. Don’t talk to it.”

He hesitated. “And if it starts getting closer, leave. Fast.”

I stared at him. “How do you know this?”

He didn’t answer. He just walked away.

Another week passed. The recycling started piling up. I didn’t take it out. I told myself that wasn’t because of what the old man had said. Around 11:30 one night, I was watching TV when I thought I heard something outside. I muted the screen and listened.

Then, faintly, from beyond the hedge:

“Hey.”

I remembered the warning and said nothing. A minute passed.

“Hey, man.”

Closer. Still a whisper.

I stayed where I was.

Then:

“Skye.”

Every hair on my arms stood up. I waited.

“Skye.”

This time it wasn’t coming from the woods. It was directly outside my apartment.

I went to the window but didn’t open the blinds. Silence.

Then:

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Three soft knocks against the glass.

I stepped backward. Something whispered from the other side of the window.

“Who’s there?”

My stomach dropped. It was my voice. The exact inflection. Exactly the way I had said it beside the hedge weeks earlier.

My phone buzzed.

UNKNOWN

DON’T ANSWER YOURSELF.

I stared at the message. I typed:

Who is this?

My thumb hovered over Send. Then I remembered the older man.

Don’t answer.

I deleted it.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Then, from the other side of the window, came a low, deep whisper:

“Hey, man.”

I went to my bedroom and locked the door. I left the lights on. For a long time, I lay awake listening. Nothing else happened. Eventually, exhaustion won. I fell asleep.

I woke standing at my front door. Shoes on. Jacket zipped. Keys in my hand. My phone was in the other. 3:17 AM. I stared at the screen. Maps was open. Walking directions. 12 minutes. Destination: The cemetery. I had absolutely no memory of getting dressed. No memory of opening Maps. No memory of walking to the front door.

I reached for the deadbolt. Then I realized something. It was already unlocked. I slowly opened the door. The streetlights were out. All of them. I should have closed the door. Instead, I stepped outside. I walked past the recycling dumpsters. Past the hedge. Onto the road. Some distant part of me knew exactly what I was doing. Another part kept telling me I could turn around whenever I wanted. And technically, that was true. I just didn’t. Turning around felt possible, but strangely irrelevant.

Twelve minutes later, I stood outside the cemetery. The gate was open. My phone vibrated.

You have arrived.

I stared through the gate. I’d been here before. In daylight, it had been nothing. Grass. Trees. Stones. A lake. At three in the morning, it didn’t feel like the same place.

I looked down at my phone. The route disappeared. For a moment, the map showed only my blue location dot sitting at the cemetery entrance. Then the screen refreshed. A new route appeared. I stared at it. The destination had moved. The blue line now continued through the cemetery, winding between the graves and ending somewhere deep inside.

4 MINUTES

I didn’t move.

My phone buzzed.

UNKNOWN

DON’T.

Another message appeared.

TURN AROUND.

I looked back toward the road. Then at the route. I should have gone home. Instead, I stepped through the gate. The blue dot began moving.

The farther I followed the route, the quieter everything became. No cars. No insects. Not even wind through the trees.

3 MINUTES

I passed the section I had explored during the day.

2 MINUTES

The graves became older. My flashlight swept across weathered stones, names disappearing beneath moss and time.

1 MINUTE

The route took me off the main path. Between two rows of graves.

Then:

You have arrived.

I stopped. There was nothing there. Just another row of gravestones.

My phone buzzed.

UNKNOWN

DO NOT ANSWER THEM.

I stared at the message.

*Them?* I thought.

Then, somewhere ahead of me, came a low whisper.

“Who’s there?”

I froze. My own voice. My flashlight snapped toward it. Rows of gravestones. Nothing else.

“Who’s there?”

Now it came from my left.

Another whisper answered from my right.

“Hey, man.”

I backed away.

Then another voice whispered somewhere among the stones.

“Skye?”

I recognized it. Someone I knew.

“Skye, come here.”

Another familiar voice joined it. Then another. People I knew. People who had no reason to be standing in a cemetery in the middle of the night.

They were all whispering. From behind stones. Between the trees. Across the grass.

“Skye.”

“Hey.”

“Come here.”

“Who’s there?”

My phone vibrated again.

UNKNOWN

DO NOT ANSWER THEM.

“Skye.”

That one wasn’t a whisper. I spun around. No one was there. Another voice called from somewhere behind me. Then another. The whispers were becoming voices. The voices were getting louder.

My phone started ringing.

UNKNOWN CALLER

I stared at it. Every voice stopped. All at once. The cemetery fell completely silent. The phone continued ringing in my hand. Once. Twice. Three times. Whoever had been sending the messages knew what was happening.

I answered. I held the phone to my ear.

No one spoke.

“Hello?”

Somewhere among the graves, a voice answered.

“Hello.”

Another, farther away:

“Hello.”

Then one behind me.

“Hello.”

I slowly lowered the phone.

The cemetery erupted. Voices screamed from every direction.

“SKYE.”

“WE HEAR YOU.”

“HELLO.”

“WELCOME.”

“COME CLOSER.”

More voices joined them. Some shouted my name. Some sounded like people I loved. Others were complete strangers. The words piled over one another until I could barely separate one voice from the next.

“SKYE.”

“ANSWER US.”

“COME HERE.”

“STAY.”

“WELCOME.”

Louder. More voices. Dozens. Then hundreds. Every voice speaking over every other voice, the entire cemetery boiling with sound. The noise climbed higher and higher until it became almost impossible to bear.

Then, at the very peak of the chaos, every discordant voice suddenly shouted in perfect unison:

“JOIN US!”

I ran.

The voices followed me between the graves, surrounding me from every direction.

Then hundreds of voices began chanting together.

“JOIN US!”

“JOIN US!”

“JOIN US!”

The chant continued as other voices shouted over it.

“DON’T GO.”

“HE’S LEAVING.”

“STOP HIM.”

“SKYE.”

“COME BACK.”

Beneath it all, the chant never stopped.

“JOIN US!”

“JOIN US!”

I made it halfway to the gate before I looked back. At first, I couldn’t understand what I was seeing. Something hung above the gravestones. Ten feet tall. Maybe more. A black shape suspended over the cemetery, long and narrow. Its lower half hung in folds that almost resembled cloth, except they shifted and curled even though the air was completely still. There was no face. No feet. Nothing I could identify as a body. It hovered motionless above the graves.

Then it moved. It glided toward me. Fast. The gravestones disappeared beneath it as it swept across the cemetery. The voices began to laugh. Men. Women. Children. Old people. All laughing with the same breathless joy. Some sounded exactly like people I knew. And somewhere among all of them, I heard myself laughing.

The thing kept coming. Closer. I turned forward and sprinted for the gate. I reached it. The thing was closing the distance. I ran into the road. My phone vibrated over and over in my hand. I didn’t look. The laughter followed me. Across the road. Toward the apartments. Closer. Always closer.

I reached the complex. The streetlights were still dead. I crossed the parking lot. Past the hedge. Past the dumpsters. Almost home. Then every streetlight came on at once. The laughter stopped.

I stumbled through my apartment door and slammed it behind me. Locked it. Deadbolt. I stood with my back against the door, trying to breathe. My phone vibrated. I finally looked.

Six messages.

All from UNKNOWN.

DON’T LET IT FOLLOW YOU.

SKYE

DON’T LISTEN TO THE VOICES.

SKYE ANSWER ME

DON’T GO HOME

And the last one:

IT KNOWS WHERE YOU LIVE.

I stared at the screen. My hands were shaking.

Another message arrived.

DID YOU SEE IT?

I typed:

WHAT WAS THAT?

The response came almost immediately.

WHY DID YOU ANSWER THE PHONE?

I stared at it. I typed:

I THOUGHT IT WAS YOU

For several seconds, no response came. Then three dots appeared. Stopped. Appeared again.

A message came through.

I NEVER CALLED YOU.

I stopped breathing.

Another message.

DON’T MAKE A SOUND.

Then another.

IT FOLLOWED YOU INSIDE.

The apartment was silent. Living room. Kitchen. Hallway. Bedroom. Nothing moved. I stood absolutely still. Then, from somewhere inside the dark bedroom, came a low, deep whisper. In my own voice.

“Hey, man.”
