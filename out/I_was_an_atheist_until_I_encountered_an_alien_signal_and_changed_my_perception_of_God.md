
I was never a particularly religious guy. I always tried to believe that God existed. I liked to think that there was someone watching over us, or someone helping us behind the scenes. It would give my life a greater sense of purpose, a meaning I had never found.

Although I had always been afraid that God might not exist, I could never bring myself to believe in Him. No matter which Bible verse I read or which religion I studied, I always found myself trapped by the same question: “Why? Why create so many planets, so many stars, so many galaxies, just for us human beings? And what if we weren't the only ones? Where was everyone else?”

I never understood any of it, and I was always trying to find the answers. I was obsessed with these questions.

After a great deal of reflection, I came to the conclusion that if definitive proof of the existence of alien beings—or God—existed, it would be somewhere in space, where everything began and where all my questions lay.

That was why I decided to become an astronomer. I studied, enrolled in university, graduated, and became a radio astronomer.

My job was to detect radio signals coming from space, signals that could originate from pulsars, black holes, or any other object capable of emitting radio waves. I also knew how to decipher languages and codes that might be embedded in those signals.

I worked at a station located in a remote, wooded area. There were several antennas surrounding the place, all pointed toward space, capturing signals. I had to climb the stairs and open the metal door. Inside, there was a desk with several computer screens that were essential to my work.

My routine was always the same: I woke up, went to the station, and checked the signals captured by the radio telescopes, recording them and sending reports on each one to specialized companies.

Nothing unusual. No answers.

Until one day, when I went to the station as I did every other day. I analyzed several signals the receivers had picked up. Among all the noise coming from celestial objects, I found something different. Something that looked like a code.

For some reason, when I looked at the diagram, I had the impression that it was artificial. I was almost certain it was all in my head, but I couldn't let this opportunity slip away.

So I translated the code into several different alphabets. When I finally converted it into the numerical system, voilà, I found a sequence:

“123 123 123 123 321...”

The pattern repeated itself 150 times in a row.

It made no sense. How could anything natural produce such a consistent pattern?

A smile spread across my face. I had become the first person in the world to discover alien life—or, perhaps, the answer I had been searching for all along!

I was eager to send the report, but before doing so, I decided to find out exactly where the signal was coming from.

When I traced its origin, my eyes widened.

It was coming from Mars.

The right thing to do would have been to send the report, but if the signal was coming from so close by, perhaps I could respond directly and receive an answer within minutes.

So, on impulse, I sent another signal:

“456 456 456 456 654...”

I repeated the sequence 150 times and sat there, waiting anxiously, hoping to receive something in return.

Just as I had given up waiting and was about to sign the report, something was detected.

This time, however, it wasn't a code.

I knew the signal couldn't have come from any natural phenomenon, since it originated from the same source as the previous one. Even so, this one seemed different.

Trying to translate it into different alphabets got me nowhere. I spent several minutes staring at the terminal screen, trying to understand what I was looking at.

That's when I realized, after taking a closer look, that it was an image.

The data structure resembled the source code of a digital image. I took the code and used the data to create an image file on my computer.

When the image was complete, I saw something that looked like a photograph taken with a cheap camera.

There were several gray humanoid beings with triangular heads. Some were running, while others lay dead on the ground. The houses, strangely similar to human homes, had walls and roofs made of metal and steel, and they were burning.

In the brown sky, I could see a gigantic, deformed hand with long nails, pointing directly at the camera with its pinky finger.

As I tried to make sense of what was happening in the image, I received several more signals. It seemed that the being on the other side was desperate.

I read them all, one by one.

This time, they consisted of strange symbols that appeared to form a kind of alphabet. Then came combinations of these symbols arranged in straight lines, as if they were words.

I knew what I had to do.

At some point, the being stopped transmitting altogether. By then, I had completely forgotten about the report. Perhaps I finally held the answer to everything I had been searching for in my hands.

I returned home with the report and spent the rest of the week deciphering every letter.

When I finally completed the alphabet, I translated the words in each line.

There were 25 sentences:

“He found us.

Strangely, he learned our language in seconds.

He said he came from a different plane of existence than ours.

He was gigantic, and his face was abominable.

Eyes with a single bright point at their center.

A circular mouth filled with thousands of teeth.

An oddly humanoid body.

We needed telescopes just to see his body.

Because his arm was six light-years long.

We asked him where he came from.

He answered that he came from the Infinite Cosmos.

There were trillions of other universes out there, and thousands of beings just like him.

We asked if he would accept some kind of agreement.

And suddenly, his voice grew deeper and trembled.

He said he would accept nothing from insignificant beings like us.

And that we would pay the price for placing ourselves on the same level as him.

He pointed his pinky finger at us.

A pulsating blue beam shot out of it...

Everyone died.

Everyone.

Everyone.

Everyone.”

I froze.

I had found the answer I had been searching for, but if God existed, He couldn't care less about us, mere human beings.

I didn't want humanity to know about this reality. I locked everything away in my drawer and never sent the report about that signal.

I quit that job.

Today, I am an astrophysicist.

Even though I try to forget what happened, I think about the same thing every single day:

“If we were so inferior and insignificant to them, and they were so superior and incomprehensible to us, what was their creator like?”
