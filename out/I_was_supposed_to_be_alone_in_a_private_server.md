I had just finished extra coursework that morning, and with extra free time, I cleaned the apartment. It was practically my solo obligation at this point, since I was the only one usually home. My roommate Tara was always out; we’d see each other a few times a week, but with our schedules and her ‘extra-curriculars,’ we rarely ever interacted, at least not in a way that counted, but alas, it was great not having to pay full price for an apartment.

Sometime in the afternoon, I noticed my phone was pinging more than usual; catching my interest, I picked up the phone to see what my friends were talking about in the Discord group chat. Reading back, I understood that they were planning on playing a Roblox game together; I chimed in, asking if I could join in as well, and they were more than happy to include me in the mix. I got on my computer and joined their call, before prompting the website homepage, and from the friends tab, I could see that two of them were already in the game.

My cursor hovered on the green controller icon, and the title peeked through immediately—*Murder Mystery 2.*

I clicked join, and the game loaded quickly; I never had problems with the wifi, but if I did, it would usually be because of the weather outside, where the rain poured down in rivulets down the complex pavement, and even worse if there was an actual thunderstorm taking place.

My friends were already waiting for the round to start as we all were situated in the lobby. And it was right on time when I joined, as I made it to the first round along with everyone else. The game was fine, I got through a few rounds with my friends, and it was all in good fun when we’d cuss out one another for killing each other. We played for two hours, although it didn’t really feel like time had passed; but still, I hadn’t realized it was that long until I noticed dusk had already been peaking through my curtains. Candace—my best friend owned the private server, but she bid everyone farewell first, reasoning she and her mother had an errand to run in the bank.

Meanwhile, the rest of my friends and I still played a round or three before they, as well, had to leave. I bid my goodbyes to the group before leaving the call. I was about to close the game as well until I heard the sound of glass crashing on tiles, followed by a cat’s shriek; I paused, jolting up as I turned to the door.

“Damn it, Polly,” I muttered,

I left my desk abruptly to check the commotion, and just as I thought, my cat had inadvertently knocked off the glass from the kitchen island and onto the floor. The while shorthair sat on the counter with an innocuous stare, her tail swayed behind her, feigning innocence, while the shards of white glass scattered across the kitchen floor.

“I should starve you,” I sighed at the cat, earning me *Mrowl* in response.

Of course, I cleaned up the mess. Luckily, it wasn’t wet or else it would’ve been another inconvenience, but it merely used a broom and a dustpan, before chucking the shards into a separate plastic bag, and into the bin.

I fed Polly, I presumed she must’ve acted out for attention, and soon enough, the diva herself ate her dinner merrily.

I began to feel a bit hungry myself, not feeling the energy to cook today, so I walked back to my room to fetch my phone and order something to eat. Oddly enough, that thought went away as fast as it came when my eyes met the monitor.

A new round was starting.

Fixing myself back on the seat, I watched as the game proceeded. The round looked ordinary as can be, with the map loading the generic bank. I furrowed my brows, wondering who else was still in the server, knowing a game wouldn’t start without at least two people; checking the chatbox, it didn’t state anybody I knew had joined the private game, so I assumed it could’ve been someone else Candace knew, but I was wrong.

It was Candace—her avatar.

I watched her walk around the lobby of the map before deciding to talk to her through the chatbox; I had the gun in my tool options, which meant she was the Murderer of the round.

“Hi, you’re back already?” I typed,

No response.

“Do you wanna play something else?” I typed again.

Nothing.

I assumed she just didn’t have the chatbox open, so I proceeded to play the game with her regardless, but it mainly just involved me walking around the map while she stood around the lobby, before walking in circles again. I felt a bit impatient, waiting for a proper response, so I thought of just going up to her, but first I had to shoot her; after all, I still had a round to win.

But as I made my way towards her, with the gun pointed out, the moment my finger pressed the trigger, the screen went black, and the gun fired. Though it was odd, it was as if I was the one who died in the game instead, as the black screen indicates, but that was the least of my concerns; the gun sound effect had played on an egregious loop, as if the very first millisecond of the sound repeated rapidly.

“What the fuck,” I breathed, clicking my mouse rapidly, shaking it side to side while trying to find the cursor. The sound began to feel jarring and unbearable; the blank dark screen flashed different colors with static, as though the monitor had been struck on the screen.

*Oof!*

The death sound played, as the game resumed with me back in the lobby.

“Waiting For Other Players.” The screen blared.

I closed the game immediately, then closed the whole computer, shutting down the system. I felt a sense of unease for the rest of the evening, and I stayed out of my room for the most part as well, curled up in the living room with Polly as we watched whatever was on Netflix I could find. I didn’t bother thinking too much about what to watch; I just needed to take my mind off of whatever happened. I thought then that it was nothing, that there really wasn’t anything to be concerned about, but I just had a gut feeling that I couldn’t shake off right away.

About a few hours later, Tara texted. “I might come home late, or stay the night at Kate’s, just leave the main key under the rug again, thanksiess” followed by three pink heart emojis.

I felt a bit… off. I wish she had come home tonight. I just didn’t feel like being alone at home. I was going to text Candace if she wanted to come over or if I could go there instead, and even bring Polly with me, but the typing speech bubble already popped up under the screen.

“Fuck Fuck Fuck”

“Andi, I think I almost died.”

“What?! What happened?” I replied.

“The bank got robbed, we all had to lie on the ground while this person in a mask pointed guns at us, and at the people behind the counter.”

“Are you okay?! Oh my god, what about your mom?” My hands felt moist against the phone as my heart began to pump faster.

“Barely, but okay, we’re okay. The robber got subdued, thankfully, well… actually it was more traumatic than that… he got shot ut, but that wasn’t what killed him.”

“That’s fine, you’re okay. Should I come over?” I texted.

“He stood in the middle of the room, muttering under his breath. “All pieces bend and break,” He said over and over again before putting a gun in his mouth.”

“Stop thinking about it, Candace.”

“He pulled the trigger,r and I watched the bullet go through his skull, his brain matter splattered across the floor, or when his body dropped in a pool of blood, while the back of his head looked like an open crater of meat and splintered bones.”

“Cand, ace stop!” I closed my phone before pacing around the room. I stood there for a while, phone still in my hand. I didn't know what to say anymore, but I began to feel bad for brushing off Candace; she was still clearly reeling from the events. I decided not to go visit her; she most likely needed the space to process. As much as I wanted to be there, I probably would just overwhelm her with questions I can’t help but ask…

If she was in the bank the whole time… who was in the server?

I just sent one last message. I didn’t want our conversation to end on a weird note, so I typed a quick, "I'm glad you're okay, please be safe."

She never replied.

I barely slept that night. Every little sound around the apartment made me jump. Polly, who usually slept at the end of my bed, kept staring toward my bedroom door now and then. I laughed it off, telling myself cats were just weird, but after everything that had happened, it didn't really help.

A week had passed since that evening; Candace’s mom kept me from seeing her or anyone. She told us Candace just wasn’t in the right headspace to see anyone at the moment, and that it’d be best to give her some space. I had to respect that; there wasn’t really any choice other than that.

But that evening, I turned the computer back on, logged into Roblox almost immediately just to check that, within the Murder Mystery 2 game page, the private server was still there. I refreshed the page over and over again, thinking maybe someone or something would join. It was completely futile, a waste of time, but my mind drifted, thinking back to Candace.

But through the mindless refreshing, I finally froze. It wasn't until I refreshed again for the nth time that an avatar’s icon popped up in the private server tab. It now reads “1 of 5 Playing”

Me.

I logged out after that. I didn’t bother checking again, knowing it would just keep driving me to the wall. But the anxiety hadn’t faded; I still felt myself looking over my shoulder, especially when I would go outside on my own. Somehow, I could feel it, even when I was alone, the feeling of knowing you’re supposed to be the only one in the room, yet it feels as though you’re not, and that someone, or something, who wasn't supposed to be there was looming over.

Watching, and waiting, for you to start, for you to ***join***.
