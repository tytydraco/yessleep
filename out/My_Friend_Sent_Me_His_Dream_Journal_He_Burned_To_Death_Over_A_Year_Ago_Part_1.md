This is gonna be a long intro guys so please, bear with me. About a year ago a friend of mine was killed in a house fire. Freak accident, nothing salvageable, that type of thing. He’d already been going through a rough patch, he accidentally killed a guy and it wrecked him mentally. He pushed everyone away and nobody heard anything from him until, well, the fire. 

So this is where it gets real fuckin weird. I had a package arrive at my place earlier this week. I didn’t open it right away I just assumed it was some shit I ordered on Amazon a while ago. Yes, I know, I’m a but impulsive with my purchases. Thing was that this was a book, and I don’t really buy physical books anymore I usually listen to audio books. 

No return address, mine was printed on the packaging. 

Opening it revealed a huge ass book. It was black, I think its cover was made of leather and hopefully not skin. No title or anything, seemed like a diary. Thought it was a gift from my girlfriend for a second, then I opened it up and saw it was full of hand written entries. 

From my friend. The one who had died in a fire. 

Below was the first entry. When I read it I, didn’t really know what to think or what to feel. Again I knew he was in a dark place for a year but this seems different somehow. I mean I remember he always had trouble sleeping but, shit I don’t know. It freaked me out. Maybe some fresh eyes can be of use. 

Its just that, he’s never really written anything, like at all, and if it weren’t for his name plastered at the start of each journal entry (I have changed or removed any identifying information because I don’t want to get doxxed on accident) I’d have had no way that this was in any way connected to him.

Anyway, enough rambling. 

\-

A great and terrible evil lives here.

In this dream, in this world, in The Dreary, echoes of the real world are reshaped and repurposed into messages for my dream self to interpret. Trees become stone sculptures, cities become ruins, and monsters become fog. 

But concepts, ideas, that which is not corporeal, they form into something worse. They become futures I’ve yet to live. Premonitions and predictions are written in the incoherent script of an avant-garde student horror film, which I am forced to live out and experience every night as I close my eyes. 

For a time, my only charge, my only duty, was to clean off the thin layer of Dust that once coated this dead, forgotten city. 

Then I found The Book, a featureless book bound in leather blacker than night with a radiant, glowing, golden sigil on its spine. The book that had contained strange and incomplete quotes or passages from, what I am at best able to guess, events I have yet to experience. 

And when I found the book, The Statue found me. The Lava Stone Statue wearing a Golden Cloak. Unmoving. Unfeeling. Unyielding. It would watch over my dreams, my Dreary, changing it and warping it into the nightmare of nightmares. I confronted the insanity and the obsessions from my past that once shackled me, breaking free of the prison of my mind only to now find myself in a prison of the soul. Bound, imprisoned, banished to this world of dreams, to The Dreary, to this dead world.

But for once, I was not alone.

For the fog was deep and ever-present. For the fog was born of monsters and that which our feeble human minds need not try to comprehend. For the fog hid and obscured the Truth of this city and the world beyond. 

For there were monsters about. 

They were not lumbering, emaciated, malformed half-gods or bastard children of primordial ancients, no, they lacked any form at all. The only gift that would betray their presence was the wind. At first, this world did not breathe, it was still, stale, and cold, as a corpse should be. Then when The Statue appeared, the world would take ragged, rattling breaths in its presence, it’s cloak fluttering amongst the hollow gasps. Now the wind roars and howls and cries in voices that belonged to no creature, human or otherwise, whenever the monsters were near. 

Heavy, thundering, staggered footfalls would rush out and over these hallowed lands as they roamed in search, in desperation, in fear, or perhaps they roamed because it is simply all they could do. After all, it was all I could do. For no longer was my task to clean this world, the one who I had once cleaned it for had died long before me, only now did the universe realize that. Now, my task was simply to survive, maybe even escape, but especially to search, to find, to acquire- no, reacquire. 

The screaming of the twin suns was muffled by the thick fog that hung over this veiled city, their defiant light still managing to find its way to ground, albeit scattered and disjointed, radiating a soft ephemeral glow from the foggy horizon all the way down to the frosted harbor and the frozen ocean.  

I had long wondered what lay beyond that ocean, if it was truly frozen then I could simply just walk across its surface. Maybe that is the only way in and out of this place. Yet I can never bring myself to come near the harbor, though would appear that neither could the monsters. 

So why, why do I call them monsters? I do not know them, I do not see them, though what I hear does frighten me, and what I feel is nothing short of terror, what exactly makes them monstrous? Just what is this idea I have of these unseen things in my mind, these gross and horrid images that I cant help but conjure when I think of them? 

Do they have large, skeletal heads shaped like a dog draped in rotting skin? Does their spine protrude from the skin on their backs exposing decaying bone? Does their emaciated form force their ribs out from beneath their ghostly pale skin? Do they lumber about on all fours, a cross between a gorilla and a wolf, hungry for that which still draws its own breath in this dead city? 

For if they do, I have not seen it. For all I know they look like nothing. For all I know they look like wolves. For all I know they look like missiles. For all I know they look like me. Yet I fear them all the same. A fear that teeters on the edge of paranoia. Paranoia in a dream that at times feels realer than reality. A dream that often seems to guide Reality, a prelude to events yet to come, a warning for my mind to heed. 

So then what, what are these monsters that lurk in the cloak of fog? What are these ideas that my mind conjures when I search for answers? 

Of course, the realization has been with me for all this time, I simply refuse to acknowledge it. If this world is of my dreams, and it is my mind that shapes my dreams, then perhaps by acknowledging the Truth of it all could affect this world. Perhaps my fear had birthed all this. Perhaps I truly am insane as my doctor has insisted. That is what I choose to believe. 

But it cannot be ignored, and I fear that my forced ignorance has had the same effect I had hoped to avoid. The monsters still roam. The world is still dead. The Statue still watches.

Recently, I’ve found that documenting these dreams, these thoughts, helps retain The Dreary’s shape. Its cohesion. Of course, I don’t write it within the seemingly infinite blank pages of the Black Leather Bound Book, just an ordinary journal. At the behest of the on-site therapist. 

Looking back on the prior, more manic entries, seems to reinforce this hypothesis. Though I hesitate to be too definite, too precise, too clear, for this dream, My Dreary, seems to thrive off of uncertainty. It is always the same yet always changing. It is always empty yet always full. It is always dead but full of a life it had lived long ago. It was always my dream but it did not originate within me. I am its Shepard, I am its Caretaker, I am its Guardian. 

But why? 

The Dreary echoes and creaks as the world that once knew it slowly continues to forget it. Its grayscale ruins hinting at the once splendid radiance of what was clearly once a paradise, the domain of something higher than the monsters that call it home, something higher than this lonely soul who wanders its dilapidated streets. 

Shattered stumps evoke the memory of massive marble columns that once stretched into the heavens beyond the fog, tarnished and twisted metal bars tell the history of the grand and golden gates that once stood before the grand staircase that climbed into the sky beyond the twin suns. 

And at its feet stands The Lava rock Statue with the Golden Cloak. 

The Statue stands alone, apart from the monsters. To say that it was alive, like me, would be to greatly simplify its existence. Truth, we could not quantify its existence, like an ice berg floating in the open ocean, The Statue remains as all that I am able to perceive, and that alone appears to be more than most. It does not move the way I move. It does not breathe the way I breathe. It does not speak the way I speak. 

But yet it moves, but yet it breathes, but yet it speaks, and when I am near it, when I am within its presence, I am in the presence of something ancient. Something older than us. Something older than the city it stood in. Something older than the primordial concepts that shape and mutate this land, this realm, these ruins, this world.

Something that had died before Death could claim it. Something we killed. Something He killed. 

This land of monsters, this dead city, this corpse of identity, is shaped by us. That is what I believe. 

And maybe that is why I see monsters in the echoes of life here, because that is what we see when we cannot comprehend what stands before us, what see when sight fails us, what we feel when we experience sensations no mortal is ever meant to feel. We see monsters in our own shadows, because monsters can be understood. The worst monsters we know do not look like shambling, rotting corpses. They look like us in the mirror, yet we call them monsters because we cannot comprehend what we see, cannot accept that one that looks like us could do things so wrong that we must conceptually separate ourselves from the evil. 

But then, we only can call out the monsters we see, whose actions we can identify and understand, only when the Evil is made visible can we identify it.

So what of the things that have yet to make their evil known, so what of the things that linger among us, so far beyond us that we cannot ascribe human motives to its actions? 

Humanity is the worst of all the things I have seen, for it is the only thing I am able to comprehend, and I fear what I might see should I comprehend the Truth of The Dreary, and of The Black Statue in The Golden Cloak.
