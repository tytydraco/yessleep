I thought I was done writing these. We spent twelve good months living together without any issues. Without any major issues, anyway. Sometimes he’d just throw things in my face that I did in the past life, which honestly feels really unfair. Like the other day. We were getting a drink with my friend, the one who told me to assume I was compromised back in the day. I guess looking back on it now, she was right. Sort of right, anyway, in the sense that Clark already sort of knew what I’d do in any given situation. 

But anyway. The real issue was that he sometimes violated my privacy for things that I hadn’t told him yet. Things that maybe I would tell him in the future, when I trusted him more, and thus would have probably told him in the past. His past.

We were getting a drink with my friend. The conversation turned to mental health. My friend said something about how everyone should try therapy at least once. Clark mentioned that I was actually going to therapy still.

“I never told you that,” I said. I knew it was the truth.

Maybe it was weird and crazy of me, but I didn’t want him to feel like I was weird or crazy, so I would make appointments with my therapist in the middle of the weekday and take a slightly long lunch before working late. A bit secretive, sure, but it’s clearly something that’s well within my rights to do. 

“You did tell me that,” he said, looking confused.

“I know I didn’t.”

“Oh, that’s right,” Clark said, and smiled at my friend. “It was the other you. Sorry. I shouldn’t have said anything.”

“Who’s the other Julie?” my friend asked.

“Nothing,” I said through gritted teeth.

“It’s just an inside joke we have,” Clark explained. “I get confused which Julie she is sometimes.”

“Sometimes?” I said, incredulously.

“It’s a lot to keep track of!” Clark said, raising his hands, the same way he had on our first date. Our first date for me, anyway. *I wouldn’t show up at your house! Unless you invited me.*

I had invited him in.

“Well, that’s good that you’re going to therapy,” my friend said uncertainly. I rolled my eyes and turned back to my drink. “I won’t ask what it’s about. Just remember therapists are mandatory reporters, so no talking about killing yourself.”

“What? I’m not going to kill myself,” I said. “Do I actually sound like I’m going to kill myself or something?”

“No, not at all,” said Clark. “But sometimes... you know, in the interviews with their friends afterwards, that’s what they always say. Like oh, so-and-so is the last person I would have expected to kill themselves. Lit up the room, pleasure to have in class, et cetera.”

Despite myself, I felt the corners of my lips quirking up in to a smile.

“Yeah, you’re right,” I said. “Maybe a few more suicide jokes would do wonders for my mood. Get a little pressure-release valve up here.”

I tapped the side of my head. Nobody laughed.

A clank behind me made me jump. One of the waiters was putting the chairs upside-down on the table next to us.

“Well,” my friend said, slapping her hands onto her thighs. “It’s getting pretty late.”

“Uh-huh,” Clark said, looking at his watch and standing. “I gotta drive us home.”

Because of course he was driving. We said our awkward goodbyes and then left in the parking lot for our separate cars.

“You’re good to drive, right?” I asked.

“Yeah, of course.”

Those were the last words we said to each other for a while. The drive back was silent, while I stewed about the therapy comment. I couldn’t even figure out how to form words to describe the colossal unfairness of it all. Of course, it wasn’t a problem for Clark, who swam through these familiar-to-him waters like a predator. His metal watch clicked like a gun as he opened the bracelet and put it on the nightstand.

Thinking like that about him left a bad taste in my mouth, but I couldn’t let it go. I mentally worried at the concept like a dog, as we got into bed, and I lay awake thinking about everything, just like I’d done with my laptop so long ago.

I found myself thinking about a video I’d seen in a college science class. I’d been taking an elective about reptiles, and one day the teacher had pulled up a video of one toad eating another. The video was longer than it had any right to be. The smaller toad, the one being eaten, was slowly disappearing into the larger toad’s mouth. It spent most of the video with just an arm, a leg, and a piece of its head visible. Occasionally, it would spasm, but never enough to free itself. I always wondered why it never spent all its energy in one giant continuous thrash, one that was enough to jostle open the predator’s jaws.

I fell asleep wondering if the fights about the phone code and the laptop were my own spasms. Few and far between, while I was being slowly consumed. Not enough to free me.



Clark’s alarm woke me up, earlier than it had any right to on a weekend. I’d forgotten what I had been dreaming about. He had been up before me, working on his computer.

“Hey, we gotta leave in half an hour,” he said.

“What? What for?”

“It’s my dad’s birthday brunch, remember?”

“Aw, fuck,” I said, letting my head fall back onto the pillow. My headache was less to do with the single drink I’d had last night and more about dealing with his family while I still had to have a talk with him about outing me going to therapy to my friend.

“I actually wanted to talk about the therapy thing from last night,” I said, watching him put on a nicer sweater.

“Well, we can talk about it afterwards,” he said. His head poked out of the neck-hole and his eyes found mine. “That’s cool, right?”

I thought of a toad.

“Well, I was hoping we could talk about it now, actually,” I said.

“Okay, well-” Clark sighed, suddenly exasperated. He made a show of looking at his watch. “We kinda have to get on the road soon, so maybe we could talk in the car.”

I put my hands to my temples in frustration. In the corner, on top of the bookshelf, the chessboard was gathering dust.

“How about this,” I said, and got a quarter out of my purse. “Heads, I go with you and we have it out in the car. Tails, I stay here. Because I am fucking tired and the last thing I want to do is spend three hours at a restaurant with your family.”

Clark stared at the quarter like I’d pulled a gun on him. “You can’t do that.”

“Why not?”

“It’s not fair.”

“Fair?”

“Don’t be random. You should follow your heart,” he said, as if he were searching for the right words.

“Fair,” I retorted, “is not telling someone’s friend that they’re going to therapy, and then not implying that they’re schizophrenic.”

I flipped the quarter. Clark tried to grab for it in the air, but missed. We both peered at where it had fallen on the bed.

“Tails,” I said. “I’m staying here.”

“What am I gonna tell my dad?” Clark said. “You told him you were going to be there.”

“Tell him I got sick at the club,” I said, getting back into bed. “I’m going back to sleep.”

“Oh, you’re just gonna sleep?” Clark asked.

“Yeah,” I said, my eyes already slivers, “until noon.”

I saw him glance at his computer.

“Okay, whatever,” he said. I heard the tension leave his voice. “I’ll see you when I get back, then.”

I rolled over and heard him rustle around as he finished getting ready.

“Bye,” he muttered from the front door. It clicked shut.

I waited until I heard him get into his car and leave before I opened my eyes.



His computer was locked when I started it up. I tried to think of what kinds of passwords he’d set.

Of course it wasn’t my name.

It wasn’t Tammy2016, either.

After the second incorrect guess, the hint he’d set appeared at the bottom of the screen: *Reminder*.

Reminder, I thought. What would he need to be reminded of besides myself and his cat?

The apartment was silent, doubly so in his bedroom with the sound-absorbing carpeting. I could almost hear my own heartbeat. From down the hall, I heard the kitchen clock ticking. The digital clock on the nightstand flashed its time in the corner of my eye.

I leaned back in his chair. What else could I be missing?

My eye caught the chessboard on the bookshelf. Could it be something chess related? En passant? But no, I’d done that to him once when he’d given me an easy setup, and he’d acted surprised, like he’d forgotten or didn’t know about it. 

That was the thing I’d noticed about Clark. He liked to think he was smarter than he actually was, like most guys. But toss a little randomness in there and he would flounder if he actually had to think on his feet. All the chess games where he knew my moves and the weird Latin phrases he sprinkled into everyday conversation weren’t indicative of any actual brainpower.

I thought of our first date. How different he’d seemed back then, someone who wanted to be in charge yet vulnerable, someone who was willing to put it all on the line and look crazy for me, just for a chance it might work out again. Knowing one of my favorite songs, from the *Donnie Darko* intro. Well, the theatrical release intro, anyway. And I’d fallen head over heels for someone who offered me the chance to be known with no effort on my part.

Head Over Heels, another good song from that movie. It ran through my head as I started thinking about what else I’d do today since his computer seemed to be impenetrable.

*Funny how*

*Time flies...*

I froze. The clocks ticked in their separate rooms. Clark’s Latin phrases.

I returned my hands to the keyboard and typed *tempus fugit*.

The password field disappeared, to be replaced by a loading spinner, like it had when I’d guessed Tammy2016. Maybe it was my imagination, I thought in those split-seconds, but it seemed longer than before.

And then, without any more ceremony, I was in.



His computer had opened to a cloud file he’d been editing. I thought it was a journal, because he was talking about me and the fight we’d had. But the language was cold, clinical, the same way I imagined a scientist would describe an experiment that had failed. There were notes for other things to try, new angles of attack in the future. As if we’d be having a fight like that again.

That was when my eyes found the title. *Julie Loop Notes*.

My stomach dropped.

I frantically scrolled up to the top. It was a long ways. Clark had a header for important information up there.

*YOU ONLY REMEMBER THINGS ABOUT HER AND THE PROCESS*, it read. *NOTHING ELSE*.

I looked at the next line.

*Clock eater lives at the end of the left-right game BE CAREFUL ABOUT TURNS*

I remembered our walk in the park. Left and right again and again, until Clark had realized and taken us on two rights to get back to the car.

From there, it went into stream-of-consciousness style notes on the entirety of my relationship with him. One recurring theme was where he’d tried to remember how many times it’d been. His estimates varied depending on the mood he seemed to be in as he was writing these entries, but they never dipped lower than twenty. 

It was then I realized how old Clark really was. He’d been going back and restarting things with me every single time he failed. Even if some of the relationships never lasted more than a year, some of them probably lasted several. I thought about the old-soul energy he’d joked about when we’d started talking, and a wave of revulsion racked my body. He must have been at least fifty years old, but only in the timeline of our relationship. He was a stunted man-child who’d been sitting in the dark, watching reruns on the same channel for decades.

I scrolled back up, disgust curdling to fury, and reread all the things he’d workshopped to grab me and crack me open on the dating app. The teacher I’d had a crush on in high school. The place where I’d crashed my first car. What names I’d called my mother the first time I disappointed her. All these horrible, embarrassing things about me that I’d only tell someone I trusted so intimately and completely.

Maybe I had trusted him that way, the first time around, however long ago that was. Whatever had really happened to end our relationship could have been something mundane, or maybe the root of it all had been there from the start, and the past version of me had picked up on his evil much sooner, before he’d learned to bury it and hide it away from me. But now he was an old soul, grown twisted in the dark, adept at concealment.

And now that I knew, what else would happen? He’d go to the clock eater at the end of the left-right game, whoever or whatever that was, and do it again. The next me might not be as lucky. And his next cat, whose sudden absence had indirectly catalyzed my moving in with him. What was it he’d said that night? *It’s always hard, losing her.*

Oh, Tammy, I thought. She didn’t deserve him either.

There was a furious clattering noise. I jumped a foot in the air.

“Jesus!” I exclaimed, and sighed. “Fuck.”

My phone, still on the nightstand, had vibrated with a text.

I ran over and checked it, worried it was Clark, but it was my friend, following up from the last night’s conversation. 

*Hey,* she had written. *sry it seems like ur going thru a lot. Clark seems like hes looking out for u... Remember what I said abt therapists!! I can rec someone if u need 2 tlk abt living with DID!*

Clark seems like he’s looking out for me. I shook my head.

But now what do I do? My friend who I’ve been able to trust for everything in the past thinks I’m unstable, because of *him*. I used to revel in the knowledge that it was a supernatural, unbelievable story that was happening to me. Now it’s holding me hostage. I’m living with a monster.

I went back and sat down at his computer, intending to read the entire thing from start to finish, but something had changed. The cloud document had a little splash of color in it now. I looked closer, unsure, until I realized what it was: another little cursor at the top of the document, this one green, with Clark’s name on it.

And if I could see his, he could see mine.
