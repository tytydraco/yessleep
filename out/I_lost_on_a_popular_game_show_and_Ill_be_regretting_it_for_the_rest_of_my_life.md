This is a PSA for anyone trying to get on *that* game show. It's also a cry for help.

I’m not going to say which one, because if I do, I’m afraid they’ll see this post and find me.

I’ll just say it’s the one where you and two other people answer tough questions with a host, and—critically—if you get the answer wrong you can go into the negative.

That’s what happened to me on Friday.

I can still remember my sweat-slippery hands sliding on the buzzer only halfway into the first round.

“Jane Austen?” I said, unsure.

“Oh, sorry, Jack. Incorrect again,” the chipper host said, his big smile not quite matching the moment as he adjusted his tie and my score dropped even further.

I was trying to climb myself out of a hole I was busy digging myself into. At the moment that hole was $3,000. I’d been a fan of the show for a long time, but I’d never seen anyone drop this far.

I thought the worst part would be the embarrassment of watching myself lose horribly on national television.

I couldn’t have been more wrong.

Another heart-pounding round of questions later, negative $3,200, negative $3,800.

I decided to stop buzzing in.

“Not looking good, Jack!” the host said, making his customary round of small talk with the contestants.

“Uh, yeah. I guess I’m a little nervous.”

The other contestants didn’t even look at me. They just stared straight ahead. Maybe too embarrassed for me? They were stone-like.

“You should be!” He slapped me on the shoulder.

I flinched. I’d never seen him talk to a contestant like this before.

“Oh, I’m just kidding!” He moved on to the next contestant, shaking the sweat off his hand that he had picked up from my jacket.

I could feel my cheeks flush. It was so hot in here.

10 minutes into the next round and I had started to regain my composure. Standing completely still and not saying anything will do that to you.

“Next question. What is the battle known by the Lakota as Greasy Grass?”

Silence. Nobody knew.

“Jack?”

He wasn’t supposed to do that, was he? Call on someone?

I wasn’t sure if all the lights had turned on me, but that’s what it felt like.

“I don’t know.”

The audience gasped. The host smiled. His eyes started to get wide. “Are you sure? You’ll be kicking yourself.” He was leaning over his podium.

“I don’t. I don’t want to lose any more money. I’m not guessing.”

“Little Big Horn. It’s Little Big Horn, Jack.”

“Was that Custer’s Last Stand?” I asked, not realizing I was speaking aloud.

“I knew you’d know it!”

My score dropped another $500. 

Jesus Christ. When would this be over?

“This part? Soon enough, Jack!” The host shot me air pistols.

Did I say that out loud? What did he mean by this part?

“Final question. Place your bets. I’m sorry, Jack, you won’t be joining us for this one.”

What a relief. I stepped away from the podium into a backstage holding area.

Five minutes later, I heard applause. I sat up, ready to get back on stage and be a good sport.

Then the host appeared in the doorway of the waiting room.

“Hi, Jack.”

“Hi,” I said as flatly as I could.

“Not our best performance.”

“No. I guess not. Hey, shouldn’t I be out there?” I asked, moving closer to the door.

He stepped forward, "not until you pay us back.”

“Pay you back?”

“You were over $4,000 in the hole! What do you think, we just let our contestants go without settling up? It’s only fair.”

I stepped backwards.

The host stepped forward again.

“This is how it goes, Jack. The part you don’t get to see on TV.”

I fell back onto the couch. It was soft, and I sunk into it. I wish I could have sunk further and further until I wasn’t in the room anymore.

The host loomed over me.

“Jack,” his voice feigning sympathy, “we can do this the easy way or the hard way.”

“Are you crazy? This can’t be happening!”

“Oh, it can and it is.”

He started to shuffle around in his pocket. Was he pulling something out?

“Fine! Okay. I’ll pay.”

That big, toothy grin curled back into being on his face.

“Can I Venmo you?”

The host’s smile curdled. “Cash.”

“Who keeps that kind of money in their pocket!” I yelled, not really asking.

“Then you’ll have to bring it back next time. Don’t forget. We do collect interest.”

I’ll just call the police when I get home, I thought.

“I wouldn’t do that.”

“Are you reading my mind?”

“I can do a lot of things, Jack.”

The whole room got dark. All I could see were his big eyes and his even bigger smile. All I could feel was his tie drooping onto my chest. He was so close. His breath, surprisingly cold, fogged my glasses.

“Fine. I will! I promise! I’ll be back next week!”

The room brightened up again.

“Good! But just in case…” He pulled out a wrench. My vision blurred.

It’s been two days.

I’m typing with my left hand.

How am I going to get $4,300?
