I don’t really know how to start this, and I’m honestly not sure why I’m posting it here. I guess I need to tell someone who isn’t going to look me in the face and tell me I’m tired or imagining things. I’ve spent most of my life trying to forget something that happened when I was a kid, and until recently, I thought I’d finally managed to convince myself it never happened.

I’m 28 now. I’m married, I have two kids, a job, bills, and all the other boring adult stuff. I don’t believe in ghosts, and I don’t normally scare easily. I’m not the type of person who thinks every strange noise in the house means there’s something lurking in the hallway. But when I was six years old, I saw something in my bedroom that I still can’t explain, and I think whatever it was has found me again.

When I was little, I had a ridiculous number of stuffed animals. My mom was always threatening to throw some of them away because they took up half my bed, but I refused to get rid of any of them. My favorite was a teddy bear named Sunny. He was small, with faded brown fur, black button eyes, a little blue bow around his neck, and one ear that had always been bent forward. One of his feet was crooked because the stitching had come loose, so he never sat quite straight.

The thing I remember most about him was the patch on his stomach. It was pale yellow, with a little embroidered sun in the middle. If you pressed the sun, a tiny light inside the bear would turn on and make his belly glow this warm amber color. It wasn’t particularly bright, but when you’re six, a teddy bear with a glowing stomach is pretty much the coolest thing in the world.

I’d press it when I was scared of the dark and fall asleep with my hand resting on it. If I had a nightmare, I’d wake up, press the little sun, and watch the light fade while I calmed down. I took that bear everywhere. He went on car rides, slept beside me, and came with me whenever I stayed at my grandparents’ house.

I can’t remember the last normal night I spent with him. What I remember is waking up sometime after midnight and seeing him standing beside my dresser. I was six, so I don’t know the exact time, but I remember the red numbers on my alarm clock. I remember the pale yellow curtains by my window and the glow-in-the-dark stars my dad had stuck to my ceiling. I also remember being completely awake, although now I know that’s the first thing people are going to question.

I’d been lying there for a while, drifting in and out of sleep, when I heard something scraping across the carpet. It was a soft, uneven sound. Almost like someone dragging a piece of clothing across the floor.

I opened my eyes and listened. Nothing.  
A few seconds later, I heard it again. Shhk.  
I figured one of my stuffed animals had fallen off the bed. This happened all the time, and my mom was always telling me to stop piling them so close to the edge. I turned my head toward the middle of the room, barely opening my eyes because I was tired and didn’t really feel like sitting up to look.  
That’s when I saw Sunny.

He was standing near my dresser, hunched forward with his head hanging down and his arms dangling at his sides. For a second, I didn’t even recognize what I was looking at. It was dark, and his brown fur blended into the shadows. Then his right leg moved.  
It lifted off the carpet, dragged forward, and dropped.

His body tipped with it. His left leg followed,scraping against the floor. He took another step. Then another. I remember staring at him, waiting for him to fall over. I was a little kid, and my brain was trying to make sense of what I was seeing. I didn’t immediately think something evil was happening. I honestly thought my teddy bear had somehow come to life.

Which sounds ridiculous now, obviously, but at six years old, I didn’t have the same understanding of things that I do now. Part of me thought it was strange, and another part of me thought it was kind of funny. He was terrible at walking.

His legs were too short and stiff, and his body kept pitching forward like he couldn’t figure out how to balance. Every few steps, he’d stop completely, standing with his head hanging toward the floor before dragging one foot forward again.  
Step. Drag. Step. Drag.

I watched him make his way across the room toward the foot of my bed. I didn’t call for my parents because I was still trying to figure out whether I was dreaming. I kept waiting for something to happen that would make everything make sense.  
Then he stopped in the middle of the room.  
His little feet were planted unevenly on the carpet, and he stood completely still for several seconds. His head tilted slightly to one side, as though he were listening to something.

I heard a little click.

The sun on his stomach lit up.  
At first, it was just a faint yellow glow beneath the fabric. I assumed I’d accidentally pressed it earlier, or maybe the batteries were dying and the light was malfunctioning. But the glow kept getting brighter. It illuminated the patch on his belly and cast a little circle of amber light on the carpet beneath him.  
I’d never seen the light do that before.

It had always been a quick little glow when I pressed the sun, then it would fade. This time, it looked almost as if someone were slowly turning up a dimmer switch. Sunny took another step.  
He was getting closer to my bed, and that’s when I started feeling uneasy. Not because I thought he was going to hurt me, but because watching a stuffed animal move like that was deeply wrong in a way I couldn’t explain. His body wasn’t moving naturally. His head bobbed at strange angles, his arms swung when they shouldn’t have, and his legs seemed to bend and twist without any understanding of how walking was supposed to work.

It looked like something had watched a person walk and was trying to imitate it. And it wasn’t very good at it. I pulled my blanket up to my chin and kept watching. That’s when I noticed the shadow behind him.

It stretched across the carpet and climbed up the wall. At first, I thought it was coming from the dresser, but when Sunny moved, the shadow moved too.

The problem was that it was much taller than him.  
I remember squinting, trying to figure out what in my room could be making a shadow that big. Maybe it was my rocking horse. Maybe it was a coat hanging on the closet door. I kept looking away and back again, trying to make the shape fit something I recognized, but every time I looked directly at it, I couldn’t quite make it out.

Then I heard a wet clicking sound.  
It reminded me of water dripping from the kitchen faucet, except it sounded thicker somehow, almost like someone clicking their tongue with a mouth full of liquid. It was coming from behind Sunny.  
I was just about to call for my mom when his head turned toward me. Not his whole body. Just his head.

His little black button eyes caught the faint light coming through the window, and suddenly I knew he was looking at me.Until then, he’d been moving around the room as though he were alone. Now he was standing there, his body hunched forward, his head tilted up toward my bed.I stopped breathing.

I remember thinking that if I closed my eyes, maybe everything would go back to normal. Maybe I’d open them again and find him lying on the floor, exactly where a teddy bear was supposed to be.  
But I couldn’t look away.  
The little sun on his belly glowed steadily while he stood there staring at me.  
Then he started walking again.  
Only this time, he was coming straight toward my bed.

I was terrified by then. I wanted to scream, but nothing came out. I clutched the blanket so tightly that my fingers hurt, watching him drag his little legs across the carpet. I suddenly remembered the flashlight my dad had let me keep under my pillow. It was a heavy metal one he’d used during a power outage the previous winter. Sometimes he’d come into my room and make shadow puppets on the ceiling with it, and he’d finally agreed to let me keep it nearby because I was scared of the dark.  
I slowly reached beneath the pillow, trying not to make a sound. I kept my eyes on Sunny the entire time, terrified that if I looked away, he’d suddenly be right beside me.

My fingers found the metal handle. I grabbed it and pressed the button beneath the blanket. The light came on, its beam muffled by the fabric.  
Then I pulled it out and pointed it toward the foot of my bed. For a second, the flashlight illuminated the shape standing behind my teddy bear.

I still don’t know how to describe what I saw without sounding like I’m making it up. I’ve tried to find an explanation for it for most of my life, and I still can’t put the image into words properly.

It was crouched almost double, with a spine that curved sharply beneath pale, glistening skin. Its arms were much too long for its body, and its fingers nearly touched the floor. Its joints bent in places that didn’t look possible, giving it the appearance of something assembled incorrectly.

Its skin was the worst part. It wasn’t just pale. It looked spoiled, like meat left out too long in the heat. In some places, it hung in loose, almost transparent folds. In others, it was stretched so tightly over its bones that I could see the outlines of its ribs shifting underneath.

Dark purple and gray patches covered its shoulders and neck, and thin black lines ran beneath the skin like veins filled with ink. There was hair hanging from its head, too. Long, thin strands that fell across its face and shoulders. Some were nearly white, while others were dark and stringy, stuck against its skin as though they were wet. They moved slightly even though nothing else in the room was moving.  
Its head was tilted at an angle that made me wonder how its neck could possibly support it. 

One side of its jaw hung lower than the other, and its mouth was open just enough to show rows of small, uneven teeth. Some were pointed and jagged. Others were flat and yellow, almost like human teeth that had been left to rot. Its lips were barely there. The skin simply split around its teeth.

Its eyes were tiny and black, deeply recessed into bruised-looking flesh. There was no white in them, no visible reflection, nothing that made them look human. And it was looking directly at me.  
It had one long hand wrapped around Sunny’s middle and another gripping his leg. Its fingers sank into his fur, pressing hard enough to deform the stuffing inside him.

That’s when I understood what I’d been watching.  
Sunny wasn’t alive.

Something was holding him and making him walk.  
It was lifting one leg, pulling it forward, and dropping it onto the carpet. When his body tipped too far, it would straighten him again. It moved him with the awkward concentration of someone trying to operate a puppet without understanding how the strings worked.

The creature’s long fingers were pressed against the back of the yellow patch on his belly. The amber light shone through the fabric, outlining its crooked knuckles. I could see the patch stretching around something hard beneath it. Something that seemed to pulse with the same light.

Then my flashlight went out.  
The room went completely dark. I heard a soft thud, followed by that awful scraping sound. I couldn’t see where Sunny was anymore, and I couldn’t tell where the creature had gone.

I lay there, breathing in short, panicked gasps, too scared to move. I was waiting for the flashlight to come back on, waiting for my parents to hear me, waiting for anything that would make me feel less alone in that room.

Then my mattress dipped. Right at the foot of my bed. The springs shifted beneath the weight.  
I looked down, but I couldn’t see anything. The mattress dipped again, closer this time, as though something were climbing up. I pulled the blanket over my mouth and tried not to make a sound.  
Then I saw the amber glow.

Sunny was suspended in the air above the foot of my bed, his legs dangling beneath him and his little arms hanging limp at his sides. His belly was glowing brightly enough to illuminate his face.  
There was nothing holding him up.  
No strings. No visible hands. Nothing.  
He just hung there, swaying slightly in the darkness.  
His head tilted toward me.

Then I heard something behind him begin to unfold.  
There were several sharp, wet pops, one after another, like joints cracking after being held in the wrong position for too long. A low groaning sound followed, as though something was slowly forcing itself upright.

A long, pale hand appeared beside Sunny’s head. Its fingers curled around his shoulder.  
Then the creature’s face lowered into the light.  
I saw the wet skin around its eyes. The crowded teeth behind its split lips. The way its mouth stretched farther than the shape of its face should have allowed.

It looked at me.

And I understood, with the kind of certainty you feel when you’re too frightened to think, that it hadn’t been trying to leave my room.It had been coming to my bed.

I screamed.

I screamed so loudly my throat hurt, and Sunny dropped onto the carpet with a soft, stuffed thump. Something much heavier hit the floor behind him, making the boards creak.

My bedroom door flew open, and my dad rushed in, switching on the light. My mom came right behind him. There was nothing in the room.  
No creature. No long arms. No strange shadow.  
Just Sunny lying on his side beside my bed.  
My dad checked under the bed, inside the closet, behind the dresser, and everywhere else he could think of. 

My mom sat beside me, rubbing my back and asking what had happened. I told them everything. I told them Sunny had been walking, that something had been holding him, and that it had been standing right behind him. I told them about the skin, the teeth, and the way it had looked at me.  
My dad picked up the bear and turned it over in his hands.

“See? He’s just a teddy bear,” he told me gently.  
I started crying harder.

My mom held me and told me I’d had a nightmare. I kept insisting I’d been awake, but she said dreams could feel very real, especially when you were half asleep.I remember the look she gave my dad before she said it. It was quick, barely a second long, but I remember it more clearly than almost anything else from that night.

It wasn’t the look of someone who thought their child was being silly. It was the look of someone checking whether the other person was going to say something. My dad put Sunny on the dresser, away from my bed. I begged them to throw him away, and my mom eventually agreed. They let me sleep between them that night, but I barely slept at all.

Every time the house creaked, I squeezed my eyes shut. I kept thinking about the creature’s fingers wrapped around the bear and wondering where it had gone when the light came on. The next morning, Sunny was still sitting on my dresser.

I stood in the doorway staring at him until my mom came in to make my bed. I told her again that I didn’t want him anymore. This time, she didn’t argue.  
She put him in the kitchen trash, tied the bag shut, and carried it outside. I watched from the window as she lifted the bag into the garbage bin.

Later that day, I watched the garbage truck come down our street. I saw the mechanical arm lift the bin, tip everything into the dark metal container, and lower it again. I watched the truck rattle away until it disappeared around the corner.

I remember thinking that it was finally over.

For a while, I refused to sleep with stuffed animals. I wanted the hallway light on, and I wouldn’t let my bedroom door close all the way. Eventually, I got older and stopped thinking about it so much. School, friends, and normal childhood things took over, and the memory became something I occasionally thought about and then immediately dismissed.

I told myself it had been a nightmare. Maybe I had been dreaming and woke up at the wrong moment. Maybe I’d seen shadows in the dark and my imagination had filled in the rest.

I grew up, moved out, got married, and had two children. I stopped being afraid of the dark.  
Then last Thursday, my five-year-old daughter, Lily, called for me from her bedroom.  
I was brushing my teeth when I heard her ask if I could come look under her bed. She thought something had fallen beneath it, and she didn’t want to reach for it herself.

I was tired, and I had work in the morning. My husband, Ethan, was already asleep, so I went into her room expecting to find a missing toy or a book.  
Lily was sitting on her bed with her blankets pulled up to her chin, staring at the space beneath it.  
I turned on her bedside lamp and knelt on the carpet. There were toys everywhere, along with a basket of stuffed animals, a few books, and a cardboard box she’d shoved under the bed earlier that day.

I moved the books, pushed the basket aside, and reached toward the wall.  
That’s when I noticed a faint yellow glow.  
At first, I thought it was her night-light reflecting off something, but the glow was coming from farther beneath the bed. It was a little circle of amber light with rays spreading out from the center.  
A tiny sun.

I stopped moving. I knew that sun. I reached farther under the bed, my fingers brushing something soft. When I pulled it out, a small brown teddy bear slid onto the carpet with a faint scraping sound.

Faded brown fur.   
Black button eyes.  
A little blue bow.   
One bent ear.  
And a pale yellow patch on its belly, with a tiny embroidered sun in the middle.

I couldn’t breathe. It was Sunny. The bear my mother had thrown away when I was six. The bear I’d watched disappear into the garbage truck. The bear I’d spent twenty-two years convincing myself had only been part of a nightmare. My daughter leaned over my shoulder. “What’s that, Mommy?”

I couldn’t answer her. I was staring at the little sun on Sunny’s belly, waiting for it to do something.  
Then the light slowly faded. I dropped him.  
He landed face down on the carpet, and I grabbed Lily so quickly that she started crying. I carried her out of the room and into mine, trying to explain that everything was okay while my hands shook so badly I could barely hold her.

I woke Ethan and told him to come with me.  
He was annoyed at first, understandably confused about why I’d dragged him out of bed, but he followed me into Lily’s room. Sunny was still lying on the floor.

Ethan picked him up and examined him.  
“Where did this come from?” he asked.

“I don’t know.”

“Is it one of Lily’s?”

“No.”

He turned the bear over.  
“Then whose is it?”

I had to swallow before I could answer.

“I had that bear when I was little.”

He looked at me, then back at the toy. He pressed the sun on its belly, and the light flickered on for a second before fading again. I started crying.

Ethan immediately dropped his hand, and Sunny fell against the carpet. He looked at me like he was trying to decide whether I was having some kind of breakdown.I told him the bear had been thrown away when I was a kid. I told him I’d watched the garbage truck take it. Then I told him about the night I’d seen it walking around my room.

He picked it up again, examining the stitching on its back.

“Maybe it was packed away somewhere,” he said. “Maybe you brought it with you and forgot.”

“I didn’t. I wanted it gone. I begged my mom to throw it away.”

“Okay, but how do you know it was the same one?”

I stared at him.

“Because I know my own teddy bear.”  
He went quiet. I explained what I’d seen when I was six. I didn’t tell him every detail, but I told him enough. The way Sunny had moved, the shadow behind him, the creature holding him, and the way it had looked at me.

Ethan didn’t laugh, but he clearly didn’t believe me. He kept looking at the bear as though he was trying to find some ordinary explanation for it.  
Eventually, he wrapped Sunny in a towel and took him downstairs.

“I’ll throw it away,” he said.

I stayed with Lily until she fell asleep, then went into the kitchen to see if he’d gotten rid of it. He told me he’d put it in the trash. I wanted to believe him. I wanted to believe there was some explanation for how the bear had ended up under my daughter’s bed. I wanted to believe the light was just a faulty mechanism and that the memory I’d carried since childhood was nothing more than a nightmare.

But I couldn’t stop thinking about the creature.  
The wet skin. The long fingers. The black eyes.  
I asked Ethan if he’d noticed anything strange when he picked up the bear. He said no. I asked whether it felt heavier than a normal teddy bear. He said no.  
Finally, he told me I was frightening myself over a toy.

“You had a nightmare when you were little,” he said. “That’s all.”

It was almost exactly what my father had said to me twenty-two years earlier. I tried to explain that I wasn’t saying the bear was alive. I was saying something had been using it, and I was terrified that whatever I’d seen as a child was connected to what had happened that night.

Ethan rubbed his face and told me the bear was gone, Lily was safe, and I needed to get some sleep.  
I didn’t argue anymore. Lily has slept in our room ever since. Ethan thinks I’m overreacting, and maybe I am. I know how this sounds. I know there’s no logical reason for a teddy bear I watched get thrown away decades ago to turn up beneath my daughter’s bed.

But I haven’t been able to sleep properly since it happened. That night, after Ethan fell asleep, I heard something in the hallway.

A soft scraping sound. Shhk. I sat up in bed and listened. Nothing. Then it happened again. Shhk.  
My bedroom door was closed, and the hallway light was off. I could see a thin line of darkness beneath the door, and for a moment, I thought I saw something move past it.

Then a faint amber glow appeared in the gap.  
It was small and round, like a light shining from a toy on the floor. I didn’t open the door. I didn’t call Ethan.

I pulled the blankets over my head and lay there until morning, listening to the house settle around me.  
When I finally fell asleep, I dreamed about my childhood bedroom. I saw the yellow curtains, the stars on the ceiling, and Sunny standing beside my dresser with his little legs dragging against the carpet.

The creature was behind him.

Its pale hair hung over its face, and its mouth was stretched into that awful smile. It was holding Sunny by the back of his neck, looking directly at me.  
I woke up when the morning light came through the curtains. Ethan was still asleep beside me, and for a few seconds, I almost convinced myself that the whole thing had been a dream. I got up from the bed leaning over and tucking in my daughter who cuddled up to my husband as he slept. 

Then I opened the bedroom door.

There was a little blue bow lying on the carpet outside. I stood there staring at it for a long time. The fabric was damp, almost black in places, with a brown stain along one edge. I crouched down without touching it and noticed something caught in the knot.

A single strand of hair.

It was long, thin, and almost colorless.  
I immediately thought about the creature’s hair, the pale strands hanging over its shoulders and brushing against Sunny’s fur as it moved him around my childhood bedroom. I backed away from the bow and shut the door.

I didn’t tell Ethan. I couldn’t bring myself to hear him explain it away again, so I waited until he had woken up and left the bedroom and maybe when he opened the door he would see the same thing. 

The bow was gone.

I searched the hallway, the stairs, and the space beneath the console table. I checked the trash outside, even though Ethan had already taken the garbage out.

There was nothing.

I started wondering whether I’d imagined seeing the bow in the first place. I was exhausted, and I hadn’t been sleeping properly. Maybe I was letting my childhood fears get the better of me. But I knew what I’d seen.

I called my mother that afternoon and asked her if she remembered Sunny. She said she did.  
I asked if she remembered the night I’d screamed and insisted something had been in my room.  
She said she remembered me having a nightmare, but not much else.

I asked if she was absolutely sure she’d thrown the bear away.

She sounded irritated and asked why I was bringing all this up again. She said I’d been a child, I’d had a nightmare, and they’d gotten rid of the bear because it frightened me.

I wanted to ask her about the look she’d given my father that night. I wanted to ask why she’d looked at him before telling me nothing had happened. I wanted to know if she’d ever found anything strange in my room, or if she’d heard anything after the garbage truck left.

But I couldn’t make myself ask.For the first time in twenty-two years, I wondered whether my parents had been trying to convince me of something they didn’t actually believe.

Maybe they’d just wanted to protect me from being afraid.

Or maybe they’d been afraid themselves. I still haven’t asked them. I tried talking to Ethan about it one more time, but he gave me the same explanation as before. He said I was exhausted, that I needed to stop thinking about it, and that whatever I remembered from childhood couldn’t hurt me now.  
So I stopped talking about it.

I’ve kept Lily out of her bedroom, and I haven’t slept with my own bedroom door open since. I check the locks before bed, even though I know that wouldn’t matter if something were already inside the house.  
Sometimes, late at night, I hear that scraping sound in the hallway. Shhk.

I never get up to investigate. I don’t look beneath the beds anymore, either. If something falls under there, I leave it until morning.

I wish I could say that I’m writing this because I want answers, but I don’t even know what answers would make me feel better. I can’t prove what happened when I was six, and I can’t prove what happened last Thursday. I know that. I’ve gone over every possibility I can think of, and none of them explains how Sunny ended up under Lily’s bed.

There’s one thing I keep coming back to, though.  
When I was six,when I shone the flashlight at it, it didn’t run away or even seem surprised that I’d seen it. It just looked at me.

I remember those black eyes more clearly than anything else. There was no anger in them. No fear. It looked almost pleased, like it had been waiting for me to notice it. I used to think that night was the first time I’d seen it.

Now I’m not so sure.

Maybe it had been in my room long before that night. Maybe it had been standing behind the dresser while I slept, watching me press the sun on Sunny’s belly. Maybe it had been there every time I woke from a nightmare and held my teddy bear close, never realizing that something else was holding him, too.

I don’t know why it left. I don’t know how Sunny ended up in my daughter’s room, or why the light still works after all these years.

I don’t know why it waited until now. But I needed to tell someone. 

Last night, just before dawn, I heard that scraping sound again. This time, it came from inside my bedroom. It has been getting closer and closer to me I was lying perfectly still when I heard it stop beside my bed. I could feel the mattress shift slightly, just enough to make me aware of the weight near my feet.

Then I smelled something damp and rotten, like wet clothes left in a closed washing machine for too long. I kept my eyes shut. I could hear something breathing. Slowly. Wetly. Right beside me.  
Then, from somewhere near the foot of the bed, I heard a tiny click. I didn’t open my eyes.  
I couldn’t.

Because even with my eyes closed, I could see the amber light shining through my eyelids. All I could do was hope whatever it was just left. I closed my eyes so tight I don’t know how long I lay there, but eventually I heard Ethan shift beside me. He made a tired noise and mumbled, “Babe, why is the light on?”

The second he said that, everything stopped.  
The breathing was gone. The pressure beside the bed disappeared. When I finally opened my eyes, the room was completely dark except for the light coming in from the hallway. Ethan was sitting up, looking around like he’d just woken from a weird dream.

 I couldn’t even bring myself to tell him what had happened. I just grabbed his arm and held on until he fell back asleep. I didn’t sleep at all.

I know how this sounds, and I know there’s probably some reasonable explanation for it, but I can’t stop thinking about what I saw. I haven’t told Ethan the whole story because I’m scared of how ridiculous it’s going to sound coming out of my mouth. I keep checking Lily’s room, and I haven’t looked under her bed again.

This happened last night, and I genuinely don’t know what to do. I haven’t seen that bear since I was a kid, and I have no idea how it ended up in my daughter’s room. I don’t even know if I want to find out.

I just need to know if anyone has ever experienced anything like this, because I’m starting to feel like I’m losing my mind.
