I live in a quiet, unremarkable urban-type settlement in Russia. Tourists rarely visit because, honestly, there’s nothing to see here—just grey Soviet apartment blocks and empty streets.

However, we have one strict rule: no one is allowed outside after midnight. Nobody really knows where it came from; the locals wrote it down back when my grandfather was just a twelve-year-old boy. On the very first day of school, every kid is handed a small booklet titled "The Rules of Glazutong." It lists the basic safety guidelines for our town. Most of it is mundane stuff like "don't run near the road" or "don't play near traffic."

But the very first line, printed in bold, says: "Do not go outside after midnight."

Last week, around 1:00 AM, I heard a bizarre, guttural screaming sound echoing from the street. In the morning, after looking it up online, I realized it was the exact sound a moose makes. The problem is, there are no moose anywhere near our town or in the surrounding woods.

Curiosity got the better of me, and I decided to step outside. The security guard at the entrance of my building tried to stop me, practically begging me to stay inside, but I brushed him off and walked out anyway.

At first, everything looked completely normal—just the same familiar street, only dark and dead silent. Then, I looked toward the corner of the neighboring apartment building. Peeking out from behind the edge, at the height of the fifth floor, was a head. It looked like a grotesque mixture of a human face and a moose.

Honestly, I wasn't even scared at the time. I just figured some weirdo was leaning out of their fifth-floor window wearing a creepy mask to mess with people. I took a few breaths of fresh air, shrugged it off, and went back inside to sleep.

The next morning, I walked past that exact building in the daylight. That side wall doesn't have any windows.

That should have been enough to keep me locked indoors forever. Yet, a week later—last night, just before dawn—I convinced myself it was just sleep deprivation and decided to go out for a late-night walk again, completely forgetting about that thing.

I had already walked through an entire district when I glanced toward the local dump. Perched on top of a massive pile of scrap metal, something was either sitting or standing—I couldn't quite tell in the dark.

Then, it locked eyes with me.

And oh God, no living creature runs like that. It lunged off the scrap heap and sprinted straight toward me, its arms and legs completely rigid, never bending at the joints—like a massive wooden mannequin moving at terrifying speed. Unlike the idiots in horror movies who just freeze, I immediately turned and ran for my life.

It was too fast. Within seconds, it cornered me in a dead-end alleyway. Towering over me, blocking the only exit, it breathed heavily, sounding like grinding metal and wet lungs. Trapped and shaking, I blurted out, "What are you?!"

Like a cold gust of wind whispering directly into my ear, I heard a single word: "Glazutong."

The thing raised its massive, rigid arm to strike me down. Just as it swung, the first golden rays of the rising sun cut across the rooftops and hit the alley. The creature saw the sunlight too.

I squeezed my eyes shut, bracing myself for the end.

Nothing happened.

When I finally dared to open my eyes, the alley was empty. The thing had vanished into thin air without making a single sound.

I finally understand why that little booklet isn't named after our town. It's named after him.
