I noticed it because of the orange juice.



There's a grocery store about ten minutes from my place that stays open all night. I usually go around 11 because I work late and hate shopping when it's crowded.



Last Tuesday I used self-checkout six, bought pasta, coffee, dish soap and a frozen pizza, paid, grabbed my receipt and left.



When I got home there were two receipts folded together.



The first was mine.



The second was also mine, apparently.



Same store. Same register. Last four digits of my card. But the timestamp said Wednesday, 11:43 PM. Almost exactly twenty-four hours in the future.



Three items:



AA batteries.



Orange juice.



Gauze pads.



I assumed the machine had somehow printed somebody else's transaction with the wrong date. Weird, but whatever. I threw it on the kitchen counter.



Wednesday night my TV remote died.



I had no batteries.



That alone wouldn't have bothered me. What bothered me was opening the fridge afterward and realizing the orange juice I'd bought that weekend was sour.



I remembered the receipt.



At that point I decided I absolutely wasn't buying gauze because that would've been too stupid.



I went to the store anyway.



I bought batteries and orange juice.



At the checkout, I was feeling pretty pleased with myself. Two out of three could easily be coincidence.



Then I sliced my thumb opening the plastic packaging on the batteries in the parking lot.



Not badly, but enough that it wouldn't stop bleeding onto my sleeve.



I went back inside.



The pharmacy aisle had one box of gauze pads in the size printed on yesterday's receipt.



I stood there staring at it for probably a full minute.



Eventually I bought it.



Self-checkout six.



11:43 PM.



When my receipt printed, there were two again.



The second was dated Thursday.



Instant noodles.



A black permanent marker.



A pack of twelve tea lights.



I didn't own a permanent marker and I couldn't remember the last time I'd bought candles.



So Thursday I decided I wasn't going anywhere.



I worked from home, ordered dinner and stayed on my couch.



At around 9 PM, my neighbor knocked. She'd gotten a package for me by mistake and asked if I had a marker so she could cover the old shipping label before reusing the box.



I didn't.



At 10:30 the power flickered twice.



Then it went out.



I checked the utility company's website. Estimated restoration was 2 AM.



I had one flashlight, but the batteries in it were dead because I'd stolen them for my remote the night before.



I sat in the dark for maybe fifteen minutes before getting annoyed enough to grab my keys.



You already know where this is going.



Instant noodles because I couldn't cook anything in my freezer without power.



Marker for my neighbor.



Tea lights because they were cheaper than the big candles.



Register six.



11:38 PM.



Close enough that I actually felt sick.



This time I waited beside the machine for the second receipt.



Nothing happened.



I almost laughed.



Then, as I picked up my bag, it printed.



Friday, 11:51 PM.



There was only one item.



CUSTOMER ASSISTANCE - $0.00



I barely slept that night.



Friday morning I called the store.



I didn't tell them about predicting the future because I'm not an idiot. I said register six had been printing duplicate receipts with incorrect dates.



The woman I spoke to went quiet.



Then she asked me what time I'd been there.



I told her.



She asked for my name.



I gave it to her.



Another pause.



Then she said, "You need to talk to the night manager."



Something about her voice made me ask why.



She just repeated that sentence.



I went there in daylight.



The night manager was still finishing paperwork in the office. He looked exhausted and immediately knew which register I meant.



Apparently register six had been doing weird shit for months.



Not predicting purchases. Nothing like that.



It would sometimes print transactions after the customer had already left.



Usually identical purchases, a few minutes later.



They assumed it was a software issue.



Then one employee noticed something.



The duplicate transactions weren't duplicates.



The product lists were different.



And whenever the second receipt printed, the security camera above register six showed somebody standing at the machine.



I asked what he meant by "somebody."



He wouldn't answer at first.



So I showed him my receipts.



He read them.



Then he asked if I lived alone.



I said yes.



He actually said, "Fuck."



He took me into the security office.



I know how this sounds, but I need to explain exactly what I saw.



Wednesday night, the camera showed me buying batteries, orange juice and gauze.



I paid.



I walked away.



Eleven seconds later, I walked into frame again.



Same jacket.



Same jeans.



Same shoes.



I stood at register six with an empty basket.



I didn't scan anything.



I just stood there looking down at the screen.



The receipt printer moved.



Then the other me turned toward the security camera.



The image quality wasn't great, but it was good enough.



It had my face.



Mostly.



The eyes looked wrong. Not black or glowing or anything dramatic.



They just weren't looking in the same direction.



One was looking at the camera.



The other was looking somewhere off to its left.



The manager paused the video.



Neither of us said anything.



Then he pulled up Thursday.



I bought my noodles, marker and candles.



I left.



Eleven seconds.



There I was again.



This time the other me didn't look at the camera.



It looked directly behind itself.



At nothing.



Friday's footage was worse.



I never went to the store Friday night.



But at 11:51 PM, somebody who looked like me walked up to register six.



It stood there for nearly seven minutes.



Then an employee approached.



A kid, maybe nineteen.



I recognized him because he'd helped me find something a few weeks ago.



On the video, he said something to the other me.



The other me leaned closer.



The kid immediately stepped backward.



Then he ran.



Not walked away.



Ran.



The manager stopped the video.



I asked what happened to him.



He said the kid never came back to work.



He hadn't answered his phone either.



Then the manager looked at the Friday receipt in my hand.



CUSTOMER ASSISTANCE.



He told me to go home and lock my doors.



I asked him what register six printed for Saturday.



He said he didn't know.



The printer hadn't produced anything after Friday.



That should have made me feel better.



It didn't.



I came home around noon yesterday and didn't leave again.



At 11:51 last night, my banking app sent me a notification.



$0.00 authorization.



Same grocery store.



I froze.



A few seconds later another notification appeared.



This one wasn't a payment.



It was from the grocery store app. I forgot I'd connected my rewards account years ago.



DIGITAL RECEIPT AVAILABLE.



I opened it.



Saturday, 11:51 PM.



There were two lines.



CUSTOMER ASSISTANCE - $0.00



HOME DELIVERY - $0.00



Underneath was my address.



I've been awake since then.



Nothing happened overnight.



I started convincing myself that maybe that was the end of it.



About twenty minutes ago someone knocked on my door.



Three soft knocks.



I haven't moved.



They knocked again while I was writing this.



Then my phone buzzed.



Another receipt.



Today's date.



Current time.



One item.



RETURNING CUSTOMER - $0.00



Someone is turning my doorknob now.
