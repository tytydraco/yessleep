I never intended for this to blow up the way it has, but I think you all deserve a little update on Maggie.

If you're new, here's [part one](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/s/7D9vCi4s66).

After several hours of driving, I checked Reddit and found some advice from people who have dealt with other-worldly things. I ran into the gas station and grabbed every container of salt I could find, sprinkling it all over the car and all over Maggie while she slept. When we got to the motel, I made a salt circle around the single queen bed that we shared together. I didn't see anything that night.

Maggie was quiet the next morning. She didn't talk about "Him", but she also wasn't back to her normal, happy personality. I tried searching for more information on the internet, but one word from the comments on my original post kept ringing in my mind: 

Demon.

Now, I'm not religious. I don't know what to think about angels and demons. I don't believe in psychics or mediums. Until a week ago, I didn't even believe in ghosts or spirits or whatever the hell you want to call this thing. But I knew something was deeply wrong with my daughter. From my research, I gathered that, (generally speaking,) ghosts or vengeful spirits tend to haunt places or objects, rarely possessing people like you see in a Hollywood movie. Considering we've left our apartment and don't have very many objects of value or history, that pretty much cancelled out that option.

With demonic possession, demonic attachment... those experiences were different. I read personal accounts on supernatural forums of people who fled an entity only to find that it followed them, who tried exorcisms, priest blessings, holy water and salt, and failed to purge the evil from their lives. No one described exactly what I had seen, or what Maggie had drawn, but there were similar signs of possession or attachments. Children who began exhibiting negative behaviors, foul language, sinister imaginations. Family pets who suddenly disliked or showed aggression toward familiar people. The consensus: Ghosts haunt items or places. Demons haunt people.

I had a strong feeling in my gut that told me I wasn't going to be able to outrun this monster, so at 2:00 a.m. I stared at my daughter resting peacefully for the second night in our motel, chewing on a thought I wished I never had.

I don't know anything about demons. But I know someone who does. 

I called into work the next morning and let them know I had pneumonia. I won't be able to prove it, and I might get fired when they find out I'm faking, but at least it bought me some time. I woke Maggie, and she looked at me with a darkness in her eyes I hadn't seen before. It gave me the chills.

"Let's go, Mags," I said, gently. "We're going for a bit of a drive today."

Her eyes narrowed like she was skeptical of me and my intentions, but she followed me regardless. It was a strange emotion, almost too adult on her small, 4-year-old face. 

"He's coming with us," she growled. It wasn't a question. It was a threat.

I tried to swallow my fear. "Of course, baby. Whatever you say."

We drove 12 hours, only stopping for gas. The closer we got to our destination, the more tense my body felt. I hated coming back here, but I had no choice. When we finally pulled into the small suburban neighborhood, Maggie sat up.

"Are we here to see Grandma June?"

I gritted my teeth. "Not this time, love."

I pulled up the driveway and put the car in park. The sun was setting now; and as I got Maggie out of her car seat I looked around, noticing the wilted landscaping and overgrown grass. I wondered how she was getting away with that with the HOA. Her home looked really out of place compared to the neatly manicured lawns of her neighbors. She had really let this place go since the last time I was here.

I picked Maggie up and carried her up the stairs to the porch, taking a deep breath to steady myself. I knocked three times.

The white farmhouse door opened inward with a small creaking sound. Two beady, black eyes squinted at me from inside. The occupant made a loud huffing sound of which I took to be annoyance.

"Kristin."

"Darla."

She glared at me. 

"What the hell are you doing on my stoop?"

"I need help."

She guffawed, a sound that reminded me of a dying bird and a howling dog. "So you've come here?? Hilarious that you think I'd do anything for you."

I bit my lip, trying to control my anger. "I wouldn't bother you for myself, Darla. It's Maggie."

The door opened wider and she poked her head outside, getting a better look at us.

"What have you done to my grand-niece?"

I felt myself hesitate.

She stepped forward slightly. "My, my, what do we have here?" she murmured to herself. A small glint appeared in her eye, and she chuckled quietly.

"You've brought more than just Margaret, haven't you?"

I held my daughter closer to my chest. "Yes."

She nodded, the flab of her chin bulging like a toad. "Come inside, quickly."

She stepped at the side; but just as my foot broke the threshold she stopped suddenly, jerking her hand up and halting me in place.

"STOP."

I froze. 

Darla turned and looked at the empty space above Maggie's head. 

"NO. Not you. I invite only Kristin and Margaret into my home. Any others will have to wait."

She paused for a few seconds, cocking her head to the side, listening. When she was satisfied, she nodded once again and dropped her hand. We shuffled in through the doorway, and she closed the door quickly behind us.

By now, Maggie was asleep, and I laid her gently on the couch. 

"It's been so long! Make yourself uncomfortable," Darla smiled.

I scowled back at her.

Every Sunday for 2 years, my ex-husband, Dave, would drag me to his reclusive Aunt Darla's house for dinner. He would make a great show of admiring her furniture or her artwork, feigning interest in her recent trips, and gushing over the same terrible pot roast she made every freaking week. I tried not to be embarrassed by his obvious behavior. Everyone in Dave's family knew Darla had lots of money and no children, which meant her inheritance was potentially up for grabs. I had no interest in her money, or in her. She was a sour lady, never friendly, always blunt in the type of way that was just rude and meant to piss you off. I'm not sure why she let us come to dinner every Sunday, part of me assumed she enjoyed watching Dave make a fool of himself. I never thought she was worth sucking up to, plus Dave made plenty of money as an attorney. But enough was never enough with Dave. 

I knew Darla didn't like me right from the get-go, but I also knew she did like Maggie. Maggie was innocent, unable to deceive people or play fake games to manipulate them. I could tell Darla liked that about her. Darla made her distaste for me well-known, and Dave thought it was hilarious. I just thought she was an old witch.

It turns out, I was right.

"So tell me, what brings you here to my humble abode?" The twinkle in her eye was growing bigger, and I could tell she was amused that I would humble myself enough to speak to her again. "I haven't seen much of you lately, though I have seen Ashleigh. Dave brings her by now and again."

"Yeah, I try not to hang out with my ex-husband's mistress all that much. You know how it is." I rolled my eyes.

"Fiancé." 

"Whatever." I wasn't there to mince words. "Anyway, the point is I wouldn't be here if I wasn't desperate. Something's wrong with Maggie."

"Nothing is wrong with dear Margaret. There's an entity attached to her." She stroked the girl's hair while she slept.

Her comment annoyed me, but I ignored it and continued. 

"I'm worried it might be... A demon."

She laughed again, her obnoxious cackle like nails on a chalkboard. 

"We'll see about that."

She bustled into the kitchen, rummaging through cabinets and drawers until she found what she was looking for. She came back with a mortar and pestle, some dried herbs, and a teacup.

I eyed the materials warily. "No cauldron?"

She sneered. "No husband?"

I almost clapped back about how she's never been married, and she'll always be a miserable, lonely, old hag. But I kept my mouth shut for Maggie's sake.

Darla handed me the herbs and instructed me to grind them into powder. It seemed ridiculous to me, but I did it anyway. I never understood Darla's obsession with witchcraft, but I was already way in over my head.

She began muttering, pouring over an iPad in her lap. She caught me staring and smirked, amused.

"Not what you expected?"

"It's certainly not like any spellbook I've ever seen."

Now she rolled her eyes. "This is the 21st century. Everything is online now."

I decided to keep my mouth shut from then on. Darla continued to mumble and wave her hands around for the next 15 minutes while I sat in the corner, trying not to fall asleep. I realized I had dozed off when the kettle screamed, jolting me awake.

Darla gestured for me to bring the herbs. She sprinkled them into the tea cup and added the hot water. I watched as the unassuming mixture began to cool quickly into a thick paste which she then scooped out with a spoon.

"Careful not to touch it, girl," she commanded. "It will only work once, and it will work on contact with skin."

She carefully carried the small, grayish sample over to Maggie and smeared it across her small forehead. We waited in silence.

To my utter shock, the paste began to harden and crack, the color shifting until it settled into a dark, bloody red. The sight was unsettling, like a deep wound across my daughter's brow.

"Huh."

"Well?"

Darla stood, wiping her hands on her old blouse. "It's a demon."

Even though I wasn't surprised, I felt sick to my stomach.

"What do we do?" I whispered.

She sighed, a mix of annoyance and pity. "You have to complete a banishing ritual. Something in your life has welcomed this entity. It could be heavy drinking, pornography, vanity... divorce." She let that one hang in the air, that irritating smile playing at the corner of her mouth. "You have to find out what type of demon it is, why it would attach itself, and banish it from your life."

She turned back to her iPad. "You can sleep here, if you want. I'll do some more research."

I begrudgingly accepted her half-hearted invitation and slept awkwardly in the recliner in the corner. At about 5 a.m., I startled awake to find her standing over me, looking smug.

"Gluttony."

"I beg your pardon?"

"Arogor. One of the 49 servitors of the demon Beelzebub."

When she said the name, cold air whipped through the room, extinguishing her candles and sending a shiver down my spine. Something scratched at the front door, and the windows rattled in their frames. Darla flicked her fingers around the space, muttering words I didn't recognize. I swore I saw a shadow of something tall and unnaturally slender across the curtains. When the noise finally stopped, she settled back down in her old rocking chair, groaning as her knees crackled beneath her.

"He's a tenacious one, he is."

My head was swimming. Maggie still slept soundly beside me, unbothered by the eerieness.

"Beetlejuice?"

"Careful," she winked. "But no. Beelzebub, the Lord of the Flies, is a demon of gluttony, impulse, and obsession." She looked at me out of the corner of her eye. "Something in your life has become a gluttony to you or Margaret and has summoned this entity by accident."

"What can I do to get rid of it?"

"Purge," she said simply. "Get rid of whatever thing controls your thoughts and actions. Whatever obsession you feast on emotionally or mentally is encouraging your demon to feed on your daughter. Let it go, and cleanse your home and spirit."

My mind was completely blank. I thought back to my shoebox apartment, our worn out clothes and beat up car. There were nights I didn't eat just so Maggie would have enough. What else could I give up so this monster would leave us alone??

Darla was quick to invite us to leave, but I still had a million questions. I didn't understand what could have possibly summoned something to attack my daughter. We have literally nothing. No luxuries, no spending habits. I had zero clue how to get rid of this thing, and now I was on my way back home empty handed. I felt defeated.

Darla walked us to the car, and I rolled my window down to thank her. She just huffed back at me, the smell of tobacco and stale fruit permeating her breath. She got another evil twinkle in her eye.

"Will you be at the wedding in July? I hear it's in Paris."

I gripped the steering wheel. "Probably not. You?"

She snorted. "I hate my nephew more than I despise you." She leaned in and smiled, genuinely, for once, at Maggie in the back seat. "But I'll always help sweet Margaret. Come back and see me when your little problem is gone, yes?"

"Unlikely," I muttered under my breath. I hoped we'd never see this woman again. 

The drive home was long. Maggie stayed silent the entire time. I cannot tell you how strange it was to drive without her questioning why stop signs are red, how tires work, why people can't fly like birds, where does the sun go at night. She was just.... Quiet. Every time I checked on her in the rearview mirror, she was staring at me, darkly. I tried to ignore the creeping thought that I was losing her, but it made itself at home in the back of my mind.

We've been home for two days now. I've scoured our entire apartment looking for anything that could be considered "gluttonous". I've canceled our only streaming subscription and put the secondhand iPad where Maggie can't reach it. The salt circle around our room seems to be helping at night, but I know it's not a permanent solution. I've done more research on Arogor but there's not much out there about it.

Maggie hasn't talked about "Him" since we got back, but she hasn't spoken much at all. She mostly sits and stares into empty space, disinterested in everything she used to love. I'm trying my best to heal our little family, but I'm running out of places to look.

If anyone has any suggestions, please help.

I'm losing my daughter.
