My grandpa lived in this town for most of his life. He was born there, grew up there, got married there, raised his kids there, and eventually retired there. It's the kind of place where everybody knows everybody. Or at least, they used to.

I've heard stories about the town my entire life. My grandpa would talk about the diner he used to go to after work, the old movie theater that eventually closed, the house he grew up in, and all the people he knew when he was younger. He especially liked talking about the 1970s. According to him, life was simple back then. People didn't lock their doors as much. Kids stayed outside until it got dark. Neighbors would actually stop by without calling first. And if somebody disappeared for a few days, somebody usually knew where they went.

That's why I always found it strange that my grandpa never liked talking about a certain period of his life. Whenever the 1970s came up, he'd talk about everything except the people. A few months ago, I finally asked him about it.

We were sitting outside after dinner. It was getting dark, and he was looking through some old photographs he'd kept in a box. I found a picture of him when he was younger. He was standing outside some building with a group of people I'd never seen before. I asked him who they were.

He looked at the picture for a few seconds. Then he said:

"Most of them are gone."

I thought he meant they had died. He shook his head.

"No. I mean they're gone."

I asked him what he meant. He didn't answer right away. Instead, he put the photograph back into the box. Then he told me about a man who lived a few houses down from him.

I'll call him M.

My grandpa said M was one of those people everybody knew. He wasn't particularly interesting. He worked a regular job, had a wife, and spent most weekends working on his car. My grandpa said he used to borrow tools from him all the time. Sometimes they'd sit outside and talk after work. Nothing unusual.

Then one night, M disappeared.

He left his house after dinner. His wife said he told her he'd be back in an hour. He never came home. The police searched for him. People searched the woods. My grandpa and several other neighbors helped look. They found nothing.

For about three weeks, everyone assumed the worst.

Then M came home.

Just walked through his front door one evening like he'd been gone for an hour instead of three weeks. His wife called the police. People came over. Everyone wanted to know where he'd been.

M wouldn't tell them.

He kept saying the same thing.

"I don't remember."

My grandpa said that was the first thing that bothered him. M had always been talkative. Now he barely spoke. His wife said he wouldn't tell her anything either. He didn't even seem particularly happy to be home.

My grandpa gave him a few days before asking him about it. He went over one afternoon and found M sitting on his porch. My grandpa asked him where he'd been.

M just stared at him.

Then he asked:

"How long was I gone?"

My grandpa told him.

Three weeks.

M looked confused.

He said:

"That can't be right."

My grandpa asked why. M didn't answer. Instead, he looked across the street.

Then he said something my grandpa remembered for the rest of his life.

"I thought it was nighttime."

My grandpa assumed M had been through some kind of trauma. Nobody really knew what happened. So people stopped asking questions.

For a while, everything went back to normal.

Except M wasn't normal anymore.

He stopped going to work. He stopped visiting people. He stopped working on his car. And he started standing outside at night.

My grandpa said he would sometimes look out his bedroom window and see M standing across the street. Just standing there. No jacket. No shoes. Sometimes in the rain. Sometimes for hours.

My grandpa thought he was sleepwalking.

Then he noticed something else.

M never seemed to look at the houses.

He always looked up.

At the sky.

One night, my grandpa went outside and asked him what he was doing.

M turned around.

He smiled.

But my grandpa said it didn't look like his smile.

It looked like somebody had watched a person smile and was trying to copy it.

My grandpa laughed about it at the time. He said he thought M had just lost his mind.

Then M asked him a strange question.

"Do you ever feel like somebody is watching you?"

My grandpa said yes, jokingly.

M didn't laugh.

He just looked back at the sky.

A few days later, M disappeared again.

This time, his wife went with him.

Neither of them were ever seen again.

That was when people started talking.

Quietly.

Never in public.

My grandpa said nobody wanted to be the person spreading rumors. But people had noticed things.

A woman who had been missing for months suddenly returned. A man who disappeared during a hunting trip came back unable to remember how to repair his own truck. Someone else returned home and didn't recognize an old neighbor they'd known for twenty years.

There was always an explanation.

Memory loss.

Trauma.

Stress.

People moving away.

People getting sick.

Nobody wanted to believe there was a connection.

My grandpa didn't either.

Until one night.

He was driving home late. He said it was sometime around midnight. The roads were mostly empty. As he passed an old building on the edge of town, he noticed several vehicles parked behind it.

Most were government vehicles.

That was strange enough.

But then he saw three people standing outside.

Two of them looked like government workers.

The third person was standing several feet away.

My grandpa couldn't see his face. He could only see his silhouette.

He said the person was extremely tall.

Not impossibly tall.

Just enough that something about it looked wrong.

Too thin.

Too long.

My grandpa slowed down.

The three people walked inside the building.

He kept driving.

He told me he wishes he hadn't looked back.

Because when he did, he saw the tall person standing in the doorway.

Watching his car leave.

My grandpa didn't think much about it until several days later.

He was at work when someone mentioned that another person had gone missing.

Someone he'd known.

A man from the same neighborhood.

My grandpa asked who.

When they told him, he recognized the name immediately.

It was the same man he'd seen standing outside the building.

That's when my grandpa started paying attention.

He started noticing things he had ignored before.

People leaving town.

People coming back.

Government vehicles appearing at strange hours.

Neighbors suddenly acting differently.

And then there were the eyes.

He noticed it on several people.

Their pupils were always huge.

It didn't matter how bright it was outside.

My grandpa said he first noticed it during the summer. He was talking to someone outside in direct sunlight. The sun was so bright that my grandpa was squinting. The other person wasn't.

Their pupils looked almost completely black.

My grandpa thought it was strange.

Then he remembered M.

Three weeks missing.

Came back different.

Wouldn't talk about where he'd been.

And those eyes.

That's when my grandpa became scared.

Not because he knew what was happening.

He didn't.

He just knew that something wasn't right.

A few weeks later, he saw the man again.

The same man who had disappeared.

He was standing outside a store.

My grandpa recognized him immediately.

He almost went over to say hello.

Then the man turned around.

My grandpa said his face looked exactly the same.

Same hair.

Same clothes.

Same voice when he spoke to someone nearby.

Everything was right.

Except the eyes.

My grandpa froze.

The man's pupils didn't change.

Not even when he stepped into the sunlight.

My grandpa walked away.

He didn't tell anyone.

He said he was afraid that if he started asking questions, somebody would start asking questions about him.

So he kept quiet.

For years.

When I asked him why he never told anyone, he looked at me and said:

"Because the people who came back weren't the people who left."

I asked him what he thought happened to them.

He said:

"I don't know."

Then he corrected himself.

"I didn't know."

I asked him what he meant.

He looked toward the street.

Then he said:

"Sometimes I wonder if the people who disappeared ever came back at all."

I thought that was the end of the story.

Then he told me something I'd never heard before.

The night he saw the tall figure outside that building, he wasn't actually alone.

There was another person in his car.

His friend was sitting in the passenger seat.

And his friend saw it too.

My grandpa said they never talked about it again.

Not once.

I asked him why.

He looked at me for a long time.

Then he said:

"Because the next morning, he was gone."

I didn't know what to say.

My grandpa just went back to looking through his photographs.

After a few minutes, he pulled one out.

It was the same photograph I'd seen earlier.

A group of people standing outside a building.

My grandpa was in the middle.

I looked at the faces.

Then I noticed something.

There were twelve people in the photograph.

My grandpa told me there were supposed to be thirteen.

I asked him who was missing.

He pointed to an empty space near the edge of the photograph.

Then he said:

"That's the man who was sitting beside me that night."

I looked at him.

He didn't say anything else.

He just put the photograph back in the box.

I've asked him about the story since then.

He refuses to talk about it.

But there's one thing I can't stop thinking about.

My grandpa told me the people who came back weren't the people who left.

He never told me what happened to the originals.

And honestly...

I'm starting to think he didn't want to know.
