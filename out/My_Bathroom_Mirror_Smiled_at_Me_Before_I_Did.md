My Bathroom Mirror Smiled at Me Before I Did



I never thought I would post something like this.



I live alone in a small apartment, and until recently, I believed there was a logical explanation for everything.



I don't believe in ghosts. I don't believe in haunted objects. I always thought people who experienced "supernatural" things were either mistaken or looking for an explanation that wasn't there.



Then my bathroom mirror smiled at me.



Before I did.



I moved into my apartment about three months ago.



It's an old building in a quiet neighborhood. Nothing special. The kind of place where the elevator takes too long, the walls are thin, and you occasionally hear your neighbors moving furniture late at night.



The apartment was cheap, clean, and close to my new job, so I didn't ask many questions.



The only unusual thing was the bathroom mirror.



It was an old antique-style mirror with a dark wooden frame. The design looked like something you would find in an old house, not in a modern apartment bathroom.



When I first moved in, I asked the landlord about it.



He told me the previous tenant left it behind.



"You can remove it if you want," he said. "It's probably older than this building."



I thought about replacing it.



But moving is exhausting, and buying a new mirror wasn't exactly a priority.



So I kept it.



I wish I hadn't.



The first time it happened, it was late at night.



I had just brushed my teeth and was about to turn off the bathroom light.



I looked up at the mirror.



I saw myself.



Obviously.



Same pajamas. Same messy hair. Same tired expression.



Except my reflection was smiling.



I wasn't.



It wasn't a creepy smile. It wasn't some exaggerated horror movie expression.



It was just a normal, small smile.



Like someone who had just remembered something pleasant.



I froze.



For a few seconds, I just stood there staring.



My reflection stared back at me.



Still smiling.



Then I blinked.



And everything was normal again.



The smile was gone.



I laughed because I didn't know what else to do.



"Okay," I said to myself.



"Maybe I need more sleep."



And honestly, that's what I wanted to believe.



For the next few days, nothing happened.



I almost convinced myself that I imagined it.



Then one morning, I noticed something on the mirror.



A small yellow sticky note.



At first, I thought I had left it there.



But when I picked it up, I immediately knew it wasn't mine.



The handwriting was different.



There were only four words written on it.



Don't trust her.



I stood there for a long time holding that note.



I checked every room in my apartment.



Nothing was missing.



The front door was locked.



The windows were closed.



I lived on the sixth floor.



There was no obvious way someone could have gotten inside.



I threw the note away.



I told myself there was probably an explanation.



Maybe the previous tenant left it there.



Maybe someone with access to the apartment was playing a joke.



There had to be a normal reason.



There always was.



A few days later, I decided to remove the mirror.



I bought a new one and carefully took the old mirror off the wall.



I wrapped it in a blanket and left it outside my apartment door.



The next morning, it was gone.



I assumed someone from the building had taken it.



I didn't ask.



I was honestly just happy it wasn't there anymore.



For the first time in weeks, I felt normal.



Until I walked into my bathroom three days later.



And saw the mirror.



Again.



It was hanging above the sink.



The same dark wooden frame.



The same cracks on the glass.



The same mirror I had thrown away.



I immediately called my landlord.



He arrived about twenty minutes later.



The moment he saw it, his expression changed.



Not surprise.



Something closer to fear.



"Where did you get this?" he asked.



I stared at him.



"You gave it to me."



He shook his head.



"No."



Then he asked me something I still can't forget.



"Has it smiled at you yet?"



I didn't answer.



I didn't know what to say.



I asked him what he meant.



He just told me that the mirror had been in the apartment for decades.



He said every tenant before me had left it alone.



I asked him why.



He didn't answer.



Before leaving, he only said:



"Some things are better not looked at for too long."



That night, I covered the mirror with a blanket.



I pushed a chair against the bathroom door.



I know how ridiculous that sounds.



But once something happens that you can't explain, you stop caring how you look.



You just want to feel safe.



At around 3:00 AM, I woke up.



I heard a sound from the bathroom.



Tap.



Tap.



Tap.



Like someone gently touching the glass.



I stayed in bed for several minutes.



I was trying to convince myself it was nothing.



The building settling.



A pipe.



Anything.



Then I heard my own voice.



Coming from the bathroom.



"Lena."



My entire body went cold.



Nobody else was in my apartment.



Nobody else should have known my name.



Slowly, I walked toward the bathroom.



The door was open.



The blanket was on the floor.



The mirror was uncovered.



I looked at it.



My reflection was already standing there.



But it wasn't copying me.



It was just watching.



Then it smiled.



This time, I understood something.



It wasn't smiling because it was happy.



It was smiling because it was relieved.



Like it had been waiting for me to finally notice it.



Then my reflection raised its hand and placed it against the glass.



And whispered:



"Thank you for letting me out."



The bathroom light went off.



I moved out the next morning.



I didn't tell anyone the real reason.



I knew how it sounded.



A person saying their mirror moved by itself.



A person saying their reflection spoke to them.



Most people would assume I was lying.



Maybe I would have thought the same thing.



A few weeks later, I found out someone new moved into my old apartment.



She posted pictures online.



The apartment looked exactly the same.



Same walls.



Same bathroom.



Same mirror.



In one of the pictures, she was standing in front of it, taking a selfie.



She was smiling.



But her reflection wasn't.



I haven't contacted her.



I don't even know what I would say.



"Be careful with your mirror"?



She would probably think I was crazy.



Maybe there is a logical explanation.



Maybe stress made me see things that weren't there.



Maybe my mind filled in the gaps.



I still don't know.



But sometimes, when I look into my own reflection...



I wonder if the person looking back at me is really me.
