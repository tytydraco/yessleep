I could hear it. The ticking of the clock somewhere through thin bleak air of a silent little damp house. 

Tick.. tick.. tick.. 

The door bell brought my mind back to where I was. A hand full of cash in one and an open register waiting for something to happen. I put the bills in and dispensed the change into my pocket. A wide eyed man in a green windbreaker came up to the counter. He looked average as a man could I guess. Almost greasy hair as if he meant to take a shower last night but stayed up too late watching whatever boring thing was on until sleep took him right on a dingy used couch. "I'll take a pack of reds and twenty on pump 6" he squeezed out.     
Of all the places to work I guess a gas station isn't the worst. I could have done something remotely better with my time but its unique. There is this still hum when it's just me and neon lights at this time of night.     
People came in and out never more than three at a time but never within the same hour. Sometimes I would not see a soul. It was nice when that happened. As if it was just me playing pretend worker after an apocalypse.

The tail lights of my car popped back at me and I felt the cold weight of the keys against my skin. It was chilly outside since fall was over. I pulled my scarf off as I twisted the key. I saw the lights of the gauges and radio dance over my skin as I peered into my rear view mirror looking at myself. Bored green eyes looked back. Age does a pretty thing doesn't it? Not everyone agrees but its fascinating what life can leave behind. The cost of living through wrinkles and grays as if you owed a soul payment to whoever. I imagine they are the IRS or maybe the DMV type of force beyond that door we keep showing up to every birthday. Mine was mainly in my hair. I wasn't some old hag I joked about of myself sometimes. Mid 30s is certainly young but having gray at this age. What a sight, you know? 

The road home was always long and lonely. Occasionally some animals would show their presence every once in a while as much as the houses I would see on the outskirts of town. Some cd I had popped in days ago spoke lightly over the sound of my tires beating against the road. Good news about my drive is that I don't have to go through town to get to work or home but meant I never saw anyone except for my neighbors house who lived about 3 miles apart from each other in either direction. 

I drew out a sigh. "F me" muttered between my lips and I saw the familiar beat up maroon suv that had a "don't look up" bumper sticker.     
That toothy grin wasn't there as I gazed at her on my front porch. As the gravel and leaves beneath my feet collided together I made my way up to the door. "Guess come in. It's cold, late and not like you will let me make you leave so"     
She sauntered over to the couch. She didn't say anything just kept looking at everything and nothing at the same time. I sniffled as I sat in my usual recliner. I peered at her waiting for whatever beans she intended to spill at one a.m. I saw it. The look in the eyes when you've seen something you shouldn't. Guilty? Ashamed? I couldn't pin it exactly but she wasn't here for anything but some sort of confession as if I were a preacher to some church. Mumbling she whispered "Did you see the news?" I stared at her. "No, I keep it off at work and well I am certainly not going to watch it here." She tensed up. She knew I preferred ignorance to the outside world. "There has been a killing on Cauffmen Ave. They didn't catch anyone and are still looking." 

This town was used to low level crime. Not many people to decide that murder is up for grabs. Everyone knows each other. Too small of a place to hide. "So we have a killer on the loose huh?" I chuckled. "This isn't funny, you know how dangerous that is especially for you."     
She fiddled with her keychain. It was a gift I got her years ago at some dumb fair that had only a handful of rides but she knows I would take her to twenty more of those fairs. I won it through the ring toss. It was pure luck since every game was rigged there. It was a tiny frog with a grotesque smile to it. Poorly made and cheap but we both know the sentimental value it holds for each other. "Are I technically alone if you stay here majority of the time anyways?" I reasoned as I leaned closer placing my hand on her knee. Her blonde hair was tossed like she has been combing it with her hands due to stress. "There is a real psycho out there babe! Probably just watching us as we speak." I've seen her nervous before but never this bad. We sat in silence for awhile listening to the electricity hum. The house was dark except for a few lamps I kept on through out. I never liked the over head lighting. "I saw them" she muttered as she bit her thumb nail. I paused and looked at her. She was more than nervous. Like an image she couldn't erase was burned into her and showing on her skin now. "They were displayed out front like an art project for kids trick or treating." I didn't say anything. Part of me didn't want her to expose it but something was gnawing at me for her to go on. "It was.. their hands.. god their hands were pointing at me." Tears started falling from her cheeks. Gathering pools on faded jeans. I got up and pulled her gently to me. I could feel her shaking against me. 

I awoke to a thud. As I tilted my head I saw the empty spot she left behind. Resting my hand on her side I could feel it was cold. She must have been up for a while I thought. The wood floors were cold beneath my bare feet as they lead me to the living room. Seeing as it was empty I glanced out the window just my car in the drive. The leaves collected around it while wind continued to be persistent to break through my layers this afternoon. A folded white napkin was tucked in my wiper. I love you. Smiled as I tucked it into my jacket pocket. 

"Eight seventy two is the price" I expressed out to the lady behind the counter. She didn't look at me as she spilled the money to me and went on with her day as I did with mine. Colors spilled over the sky mixing with the clouds that rested there. It reminded me of fire a bit. The need of warmth itched at my skin on my walk to my car after the shift. As lights flickered I climbed in waiting for the heat to take over me. As my hands gripped into my jacket I glanced in my rear view mirror towards the parking lot. We only ever had two pumps and one for the truckers on the side. The street lamps were on and only illuminated spots that seemed scared to stretch out any farther as if something would blacken them out completely.  I turned my eyes away towards the front dash feeling my skin prick. I didn't like this feeling like I needed to run or that darkness would envelop me too. 

The road felt sharper tonight. Each turn costing me something. I saw my neighbors house first then saw a pair of tennis shoes that were placed perfectly in the middle of the end of their driveway like they were meant to be there all along. Passing by I noticed no lights were on as I continued on. As I reached my own I knew who was already inside. I opened the door finding Izzy on my recliner wrapped up in my blanket I got from Boston in college. We just looked at each other in acknowledgment then proceeded to the kitchen. "I brought you supper from that Italian place you like." She unwrapped the plate sliding it towards me. I hummed as I hugged her as thanks. We ate in silence until her phone broke out. Her face turned into a deep frown as if she's been sitting all day waiting for bad news from a doctor. She muttered a couple yeahs and okays before hanging up. I knew this play too well. She had to leave for work but this time I could see she hesitated. Something was off and it wasn't just reluctancy for working during off hours like the others. 

"There has been another one." She blankly stared at her food. I could hear her foot tapping against the floor. Running her thin fingers through her hair she finally looked at me. Fear, that is what made her pause. "It.. down the road.. I can't" I pulled her to me. There wasn't anything I could say in that moment. Her fears were rational. "I need you to move in with me" she stated while I hummed in acknowledgment. Not like I had a lot of things keeping me in this house anyways. We talked previously of moving in together anyways but we never thought under these circumstances, I mean who would ever. Last of my tiny amount of things was in her trunk. "I can ride with you since I will be off for awhile. Apparently the whole killer thing no one wants to get gas to go out so free vacation yay." I slid a sideways smile towards her as my shoulders shrugged. "You'll have to come with me to the site but do not get out."  Her tone was final and I knew better than to argue.

Lights from police cars flashed unto dead branches as we pulled up. Flood lights painted the yard and the neighbors house as if the sun decided to pop out at two a.m. "Deputy Lyers, I think you need to see this. Nice to see you again Jane even though the place could be better" Sheriff Whytt breathed out towards us as he walked up to the car. He messed with his tie too much like it was strangling him. He was an older gentleman mid 50's, two divorces, and an addiction to superhero comic books. Once said that even though he spent his life fighting crime, they really knew how to do it right while he did child's play. I watched Izzy pull her hair back and grabbed her camera. She thought relying on one person to take pictures of any crime scene wasn't enough and no one bothered to stop her.
