A while ago, I was in a critical financial situation, so I started looking for anything that could make me some quick money. I went around the street where I grew up, asking people if they needed their lawns cut, rubbish removed or backyards cleaned. One of the first people I asked was a neighbor I had known for years. I didn’t know him personally, but I knew his face and had spoken to him several times. He was in his fifties and had always seemed like a quiet, friendly man. He immediately offered me €200 per day, which was far more than I expected. The only problem was that his backyard was enormous\*\*,\*\* around a thousand square meters, covered with dead leaves, broken furniture, bags of rubbish and rusted metal. There were two houses on the property. He lived in the main house, while the other was a ruined five-bedroom house that had been abandoned for as long as I could remember. I didn’t own any proper equipment, so my cousin lent me his minivan to remove the rubbish. I offered him €100, but he refused. He had recently become a father and was struggling financially too, so he understood how important the job was to me.

On my third day, I brought lunch but forgot to bring water. I ate around midday, then continued working under the sun for several more hours. By that point, I was exhausted and extremely thirsty. I was standing about fifty meters from the abandoned house when I decided to walk to the main house and ask my neighbor for some water. At that exact moment, I looked at one of the old house’s windows and saw him standing behind it. He was staring directly at me. It was definitely his face, or at least I was convinced it was. We looked at each other for around five seconds before he slowly moved away from the window. I walked over and knocked on the old front door, but nobody answered. I knocked twice more and called his name. The house remained completely silent.

I walked to the main house, around two hundred meters away, and my neighbor opened the door within seconds. I asked him for some water, and instead of giving me a glass, he handed me a cold one-and-a-half-liter bottle from his refrigerator. “A glass won’t be enough if you’re working out there,” he said. “Keep the bottle.” Before returning to work, I asked why he had been watching me from the abandoned house. His expression changed immediately. He looked over my shoulder toward the ruin and asked exactly which window I had seen him in. When I pointed it out, he seemed genuinely uncomfortable. “It wasn’t me,” he said. “You’ve probably been in the sun too long.” Then he lowered his voice. “But if you see anyone in there again, don’t approach them and don’t talk to them. Come straight here and tell me.” I asked if someone had been breaking into the property. He didn’t answer. He just told me to drink the water and stay near the van.

I convinced myself that the heat had made me imagine it, so I continued working for another two hours. By then, the sun was disappearing and I was ready to leave. As I loaded the last bag into the minivan, I looked toward the abandoned house and saw someone standing in its doorway. From that distance, it looked exactly like my neighbor again. The person wasn’t waving or calling to me. He was simply standing there, with one hand resting against the doorframe, watching. I remembered what my neighbor had said, so I got into the van without acknowledging him. When I reached the entrance, I discovered that the gate was closed. It had never been closed while I was working there.

I drove back to the main house and knocked because I needed the gate opened, and my neighbor still had to pay me. Nobody answered. I called his phone and heard it ringing inside the house, but he didn’t pick up. While I waited, I heard slow footsteps crossing the gravel somewhere behind me. I didn’t turn around immediately. I stood there listening as they came closer, one step at a time, then stopped. When I finally looked, nobody was there. The abandoned house’s doorway was empty, but its front door was now closed. I knocked harder and called my neighbor’s name. From inside the main house, I heard movement, followed by what sounded like the front door being locked from the other side. A few seconds later, the gate at the end of the driveway began to open by itself.

I ran back to the minivan and started driving toward it. The gate moved painfully slowly, and for several seconds the gap wasn’t wide enough for the van. As I waited, I checked the mirror. The figure was standing behind the van, close enough that I could now see his pale face. He looked like my neighbor, but his clothes were different, and he was smiling in a way that made me feel as if he expected me to recognize him. I locked the doors. He walked toward the driver’s side, but the gate finally opened enough for me to squeeze through. As I drove away, he didn’t follow. He just stood in the middle of the driveway. I looked back once more and saw my actual neighbor watching from an upstairs window of the main house. He immediately stepped behind the curtain when he noticed me looking.

My payment arrived later that night, along with a message apologizing for not answering the door. My neighbor claimed he had been in the shower and had opened the gate remotely when he heard the van. I knew that wasn’t true. His hair and clothes had been completely dry when I saw him upstairs. Still, I needed the money and didn’t want trouble, so I decided not to go back and tried to forget about it.

A few days later, my cousin called me. He had been cleaning the minivan before installing his baby’s car seat and found a black plastic bag pushed underneath the rear bench. Inside were eleven printed photographs of me working in the backyard. Most had been taken from the windows of the abandoned house. Some showed me carrying bags, eating my lunch or drinking the bottle of water. One was a close-up of my face. The final photograph showed me standing at the main house that evening, waiting for somebody to answer the door. Whoever took it had been standing only a few meters behind me. My cousin wanted to know how the bag had gotten inside the van. I still had the keys the entire time, although one of the rear doors sometimes failed to lock properly. Someone could have opened it, but that didn’t explain why they had left the photographs there. There was no note and no warning. It felt like whoever had taken them wanted me to know I had been watched.

I became angry instead of frightened. My cousin was angry too, so we drove back together the following afternoon. We wanted answers, and we wanted them quickly. The gate was open, but my neighbor’s car wasn’t there. We knocked on the main house for several minutes without receiving an answer. My cousin then noticed movement inside the abandoned house. Someone had stepped away from an upstairs window. We walked over, shouting that we knew about the photographs. This time, the front door wasn’t locked. It opened slightly when my cousin knocked. The smell inside was terrible**,** a mixture of damp wood, cigarettes and spoiled food. Despite the condition of the exterior, someone had clearly been living there. There was a mattress on the floor, empty food cans, bottles of water and stacks of newspapers. On one wall were dozens of photographs. Most showed my neighbor entering and leaving his house, but several were of people walking along the street. There were even photographs of my cousin’s minivan taken days before I started the job.

Then the front door closed behind us. A man spoke from the hallway and asked why we had come back. At first, I thought it was my neighbor, but when he stepped into the light, I saw that he was thinner, older-looking and had a large scar running from his ear to his neck. He resembled my neighbor enough to be mistaken for him from a distance. He told us the photographs weren’t meant for me. They were meant for his brother. Before we could ask what that meant, my actual neighbor appeared outside and began shouting for us to leave the house. The man inside immediately became agitated. My cousin grabbed my arm, and we pushed past him before running outside.

My neighbor locked the door behind us and finally admitted the man was his older brother, who had serious mental health problems and had been secretly staying in the ruined house. He claimed his brother was harmless and that he hadn’t told anyone because he was afraid the authorities would take him away. When I asked why he had hired me to work so close to the building, he said he believed his brother had left weeks earlier. He also insisted he had no idea how the photographs entered the van.

We left and never returned. My neighbor transferred the money he owed me, plus another €400, and asked us not to involve the police. We reported everything anyway, but when officers searched the property, the abandoned house was empty. The mattress, food cans and photographs had all disappeared. My neighbor told them his brother didn’t exist and that we must have confused him with someone else. They couldn’t do much without evidence, especially because my cousin had left the photographs inside the house when we ran.

About a month later, my cousin called me after putting his baby to sleep. Someone had left another black plastic bag inside the minivan, even though it had been locked in his garage all night. There was only one photograph inside. It showed my cousin and me standing in the abandoned house, looking at the wall of pictures. It had been taken from directly behind us.

Written across the bottom in black marker were the words:

**I TOLD YOU NOT TO TALK TO HIM.**

At this point we decided to not talk about this anymore, although whenever we hang-out for a beer or something, it is briefly mentioned.  
I'm thinking of going back there, I know he is too, he fixed his lock on the back door of the mini van and texted me today telling me ''We need to talk''.

  
**UPDATE:**

“We need to talk” was about exactly what I expected. Neither of us had been sleeping properly. What bothered him most was knowing that someone had entered the back of his minivan, even after he repaired the faulty lock. With his wife and newborn baby at home, he no longer felt safe. His wife knew nothing about the situation; as far as she was concerned, we went out for lunch and decided to spend the day together. We returned to the property because we wanted answers. I have a black belt in karate, and my cousin has practiced MMA for around ten years, but neither of us wanted a confrontation. Still, he brought the metal bat he kept for home protection. I didn't mention he brought this bat the first time we were here earlier because it wasn't relevant, but now it is. You'll see.

We parked on a narrow road behind the property, where we could see both houses through the trees. Nothing happened for around ninety minutes. Then the owner called and immediately said, “I can see you.” He claimed one of his security cameras had detected the minivan, although he wasn’t home. I confronted him about the photographs, his supposed brother and how someone had entered the van after the lock was repaired. He avoided every question and told us to leave. Before we could push him further, we heard a single step in the dead leaves directly behind the van. A few seconds later, someone slowly pulled the rear door handle. The repaired lock held, but whoever was outside tried it again.

We remained silent for almost two minutes, listening for movement. Eventually, my cousin switched on the headlights and started the engine. The thin man was standing directly behind us, one hand still raised near the door. My cousin accelerated, and the man moved aside at the last moment. He didn’t chase us. He simply watched us leave. When I told the owner who we had seen, he ended the call.

We stopped at a petrol station and checked the minivan. That was when my cousin discovered a small listening device taped beneath the handle of the bat. He had carried the bat into the abandoned house during our previous visit and briefly left it on the floor. The device contained a small memory card. My cousin inserted it into his phone and found several audio recordings. One had been recorded at the same moment the photograph of us inside the house was taken. In the background, an unidentified man whispered, “He keeps bringing people here, and then he blames me.”

We sat inside the petrol station drinking hot black coffee. We could have contacted the police again, but we already knew how that would end: another search, another empty house and no answers. Eventually, I said I was going back and entering the abandoned house myself. My cousin stood so quickly that I thought he was going to stop me. Instead, he tossed me the keys. “This ends tonight.”

We removed the listening device and returned to the property. The atmosphere felt heavier this time, as though someone already knew we were coming. It felt like the suspense from a movie, but more intense. I was liking it, but not for long. I called the owner once we were inside, but he didn’t answer. We skipped the main house and went directly to the abandoned one. Its front door was unlocked, and the dining room no longer looked abandoned. A professional Sony camera sat on the table beside batteries, memory cards, tape and several devices identical to the one found on the bat.

The camera contained thousands of photographs organized by names and dates. There were pictures of previous workers, delivery drivers and people walking along the street. My folder began on the morning I knocked on the owner’s door. It included photographs of me working, eating lunch, asking for water and driving home. My cousin’s folder showed him at work, at petrol stations and outside his home. We also found videos of the owner and the thin man removing everything from the house before the police searched it. They appeared to be working together. Then we found photographs of the owner opening the minivan and pushing the first black bag beneath the rear seat.

The owner called while we were still examining the camera. He knew we were inside and ordered us to put back whatever we had found and leave. I confronted him with the photographs and told him the police had confirmed that he had no brother and that nobody in his family had ever been institutionalized. “If you know I put the photographs inside the van, then you found the camera,” he replied. He admitted planting them because he wanted me to stop cleaning the property. The thin man wasn’t his brother. He was a stranger who had secretly lived there for two or three years, watching the owner and threatening to kill him if he warned anyone.

The owner had contacted the police once before, but they found nothing. That same night, his dog disappeared. Days later, its collar appeared on his doorstep. After that, the owner stopped asking for help. He hired me because the man had been gone for weeks, and he thought it was finally over. When I saw someone in the abandoned house, he panicked. He couldn’t openly warn me because the man watched the main house, so he planted the photographs to frighten me into leaving.

He also admitted attaching the listening device to the bat. During our previous visit, he had followed us inside and hidden in a storage room. While placing the device, he saw the thin man photograph us from the hallway. That explained the matching timestamps: they hadn’t been working together; one had been hiding from the other. The video of them clearing the house was different. When we watched it again, we noticed the thin man holding what appeared to be a knife against the owner’s back.

Footsteps crossed the room above us. Then the thin man appeared at the top of the staircase and said we shouldn’t believe everything the owner told us. He claimed the owner brought desperate workers onto the property and that the photographs were warnings. For a moment, we didn’t know who to believe. Then my cousin asked how he knew where his wife and baby lived. The man smiled, pulled out a knife and came down the stairs.

My cousin struck his forearm with the bat, knocking the knife away. The man attacked anyway. The struggle lasted around three minutes. Even after losing the knife, he kept attacking us and repeatedly tried to reach it. It wasn’t controlled like training. It was frantic, ugly and fueled by days of fear. My cousin dropped the bat after the first strike because he didn’t want to kill him. We used our fists and kicked his legs and body while preventing him from reaching the knife. Three minutes is a long time in a real fight. By the end, all of us were exhausted. The man was bleeding and could barely stand, but he remained conscious. We stopped once he could no longer follow us and deliberately avoided causing permanent damage.

My cousin kicked the knife across the room, and we backed toward the door. The man remained on his knees, breathing heavily. What disturbed me most was that he was smiling. We ran to the minivan while the owner, still on the phone, told us he had called the police. He apologized for using us and admitted that his fear had placed us in danger. I understood that the disappearance of his dog had traumatized him, but that didn’t excuse his actions. In a strange way, he got lucky with us. We were angry and reckless enough to return when most people would have stayed away.

This time, the police found the camera, fingerprints and thousands of photographs showing the man stalking people far beyond the property. He was arrested the same night inside an empty building nearby. He was badly hurt and had left some kind of trail. Police identified him as someone reported missing almost four years earlier who had a history of stalking, burglary and violence. Inside the building, they found cameras, stolen clothing, copied keys, photographs of my cousin’s wife and baby, and the owner’s dog’s collar.

The investigation didn’t end at the abandoned house. Police found several more listening devices and miniature cameras hidden throughout the owner’s main house. He had no idea they were there. After that discovery, they searched my home and my cousin’s. In my bedroom, they found a listening device concealed beneath the base of the lamp on my bedside table. Another was discovered in almost the same place inside my cousin’s bedroom. We still don’t know when he entered our homes or how long he had been listening. Police told us the photographs, copied keys and recordings suggested that we weren’t merely being watched, we were probably going to become his next targets. Had we waited much longer before returning to the property, they believe he would have escalated.

The owner was investigated for planting the photographs, lying about having a brother and removing evidence before the earlier search. Ultimately, neither my cousin nor I blamed him. That doesn’t mean what he did was right, but after learning what happened to his dog, and discovering how thoroughly the man had monitored all of us, we understood his fear. The three of us have families and animals we care about. None of us can honestly say how we would have behaved in his position. We didn't press charges or anything.

My cousin sold the minivan, the abandoned house was boarded up, and the owner continued living in the main house. He apologized again and sent me everything he owed me, along with extra money that I returned previously. He asked me to keep cleaning his property. I probably will, there's nothing else dangerous lurking there and I need the cash.  
Police offered to show us the remaining photographs involving us. My cousin is still thinking about it.  
I probably will say yes, I don't know, tell me if I should.

The man is in custody, and the abandoned house has been sealed.

As far as I’m concerned, it’s over.

I still haven’t looked through the remaining photographs.

And I never will.
