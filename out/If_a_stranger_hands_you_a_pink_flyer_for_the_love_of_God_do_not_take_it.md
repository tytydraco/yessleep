I went to work, finished my shift and headed home just like every other day. Life has felt incredibly mundane lately, stripped of any real sparkle or excitement. Everything feels fixed, predictable, and quiet. 

An endless loop where nothing fun or out of the ordinary ever seems to break through. And as I walked home that evening, I fully expected today to be no different.

But as I was walking through the square, I noticed a guy handing out these weird, faded pink sheets of paper. It said lucid dreaming on them. He was pitching it to everyone who passed by, telling them what they were missing out on. 

He made it sound like an invitation, claiming it was a direct portal to an entire world waiting to be explored. He said, "Come live lives you have never lived before."

Something about the way he said it really stuck with me.

When I reached home, my friends came over and we went out for drinks. It was actually a good night—something I hadn't had in a really long time.

So when I came back home, I saw that single sheet of weird, faded pink paper sitting on the countertop near my bed. I thought, Let's give it a go. Why not make an already good night maybe even more exciting, right?

I started reading. As I went through the lines, the air in the room grew noticeably colder. At the same time, the light seemed to bleed out of the space, the lamps dimming to a dull, muted glow that made the shadows stretch out long and thin along the walls. I figured it was just my mind playing tricks on me. I had a few drinks in me, after all, so I gave myself the benefit of the doubt.

The method listed on the paper was some wake-induced technique. Vague instructions, written in jerky, disjointed sentences. Trick your body into falling asleep while keeping your mind wide awake. Just lie there completely still, don't scratch, don't twitch, and wait for your brain to start building the dream right in front of you while you're still watching.

Keep your mind awake, let your body sleep.

I stopped moving, ignored every phantom itch, and let my body sink into the mattress—waiting for sleep to take over while trying to keep my conscious thread alive.

THEN I SNAPPED UP.

I took a deep, desperate breath, my chest heaving with relief. Thank God. It's over. I swung my legs over the edge of the bed to stand up, but when I looked down, my pants were empty. From the hips down, there was nothing—just a clean, terrifying drop to the floorboards. I collapsed sideways, hitting the rug, screaming, but no sound came out.

My eyes snapped open. Back in bed. Facing the ceiling.

I sat up fast, cold sweat dripping off my chin. I looked around, trying to ground myself, but the room felt horribly wrong. The ceiling was beneath my feet; the light fixture was sticking up from the floorboards like a pale metal stem. Everything was flipped completely upside down, and the sheer, unnatural vertigo made my stomach turn inside out.

SNAP.WAKE UP.

Sunlight was streaming through the blinds, soft and warm. I let out a long shuddering breath, feeling the heavy cotton of my blanket. I reached out to grab my water glass from the nightstand, but when my hands came into view, they weren't mine. The skin was rotting, sagging off yellowed bone, tight and split at the knuckles. I tried to pull them back, but my arms wouldn't obey. They just hung there in front of me like heavy, decayed beef.

I thrashed against the mattress with everything I had, fighting the weight on my chest until my lungs burned—and opened my eyes.

This time, the room was gone. I was lying flat on my mattress, floating in the absolute middle of an endless, black ocean under a moonless sky. The water was dead still, slick as oil, stretching out forever into total dark. Then, right beneath the surface, something massive and slow brushed against the underside of the bed.

SNAP. WAKE UP.

I sat up in my dark room. I checked my pulse. I could feel it. I reached out and grabbed my phone—it felt cold, heavy, real. I unlocked it, typed out a text to a friend to ask if they were awake, and hit send.

The text bounced back immediately. The contact name had morphed into a string of jagged, unreadable symbols. I looked at the hands holding the phone, and as I watched, the flesh dissolved like wet paper, melting away to reveal raw, twitching nerves underneath.

SNAP. WAKE UP. Ceiling. Heavy chest.

SNAP. WAKE UP. My room, but there are no doors or windows, just solid, unbroken drywall sealing me in.

SNAP. WAKE UP. I try to breathe, but my mouth is fused shut, skin grown seamless over my lips.

It’s happened a thousand times now. Or a million. Every single time I think I’ve finally broken free—every time the air feels real and my lungs fill with breath—some horrifying, impossible detail reveals the lie. 

I try waking myself up by screaming, by throwing myself off the bed, by tearing at my own skin, but the loop just resets, twisting the nightmare into something worse.

I'm writing this down as a warning. It’s been almost five days since I last let myself sleep. I am terrified to close my eyes, terrified of being dragged back under. In my head, I’ve lived a million different lives, and every single one of them was worse than the last. I can’t go through it again.

But my eyelids feel like lead. The sheer, suffocating weight is dragging my head down toward the pillow. My body is completely giving out.

I’m afraid of sleeping. I’m afraid of what my mind can conjure up next.

 So if you ever see someone handing out a single sheet of that weird, faded pink paper... don't take it.
