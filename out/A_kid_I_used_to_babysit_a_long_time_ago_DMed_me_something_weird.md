So, I’ll get right to it before going into the history of it all: I got a cryptic DM yesterday from a kid I used to babysit, and it really has me on edge. 

The DM was from an account that had a profile pic of a hermit crab. I almost deleted it, but the text preview made me open it:

It read: “It’s me. Eric. You used to babysit me (if I have the right Dani). you used to drive an old blue volvo station wagon and we would watch Monsters, Inc. together. I’m reaching out because I want to tell you what happened.”

It was so wild to hear from him. He must be eighteen now, around the age I was when I babysat him. I messaged him back immediately:

“Yes! Hi Eric! You’ve got the right Dani. How are you?! We must have watched that movie 50 times that summer! What do you mean by ‘what happened’?” 

The message completely took me by surprise. I hadn’t thought about that babysitting gig in so long, and never saw Eric after that summer. 

I started babysitting him right after I graduated high school in the months before I started community college that fall, which was a decade ago. I was trying to stash cash for tuition and car insurance. I got a part time job at Old Navy, but I was needing a lot more money than that, so I started looking for babysitting opportunities at night in the area. I won’t say where I lived at the time, but it’s a suburb of a small city in the Midwest. 

Eric’s father’s name was Richard, and his mother’s name was Jan. Both worked at night, for a local hospital. I’m not sure if they ever told me that they were doctors, but I assume so. They were really educated types. I remember feeling very intimidated around them, and during the interview when they asked me what I was going to be studying in college, and I said I didn’t know, I remember they seemed a bit judgmental. They said something positive like, “Well, college will present you with so many options!” But it was the look in their eyes. Like, because I didn’t know what I wanted to be yet, I was somehow broken. It’s been so long, maybe that was my own insecurity interpreting the moment. A lot of people don’t know what they want to do in life at eighteen. 

Eric supposedly had a nanny during the daytime (I never met her), so that Richard and Jan could get some sleep after their work shifts ended. I would get there around six-thirty every evening, and Jan would always have a plate of whatever they had made for dinner set aside for me. She was a really good cook. To be honest, both of my parents were pretty much deadbeats who left me to fend for myself growing up, so I never really got home-cooked meals. I remember looking forward to whatever she’d made, but I especially liked her fettuccini alfredo. 

Richard and Jan would leave at 7:00, and Eric wanted to watch *Monsters, Inc*. every night before bed.  His bedtime was about 9:00, and I would sleep on the couch until Richard and Jan got home in the morning. It was seriously easy. And I got paid $100 a night for it, which was crazy for such an easy job. Also, with my part-time job at Old Navy during the day. I was making a lot of money for a teenager.

Honestly, I don’t remember much about it, except watching that movie with Eric every evening, and I remember waking up at Richard and Jan’s every morning when they got home and having to hustle back to my house to take a shower and make it to my 9 AM shift at Old Navy. It was all very routine and uneventful.

Which is why Eric’s reply caught me off guard: “I don’t know how much you remember but I’m really sorry for what they did to you.”

*Did to me?*

I wrote him back immediately: “What do you mean? Who?”

I waited for him to reply. My pulse was throbbing in my neck. I tried to keep busy by doing some laundry, but I was checking my phone every few seconds to see if he wrote back. Finally, after half an hour or so, there was a response. 

Eric: “My parents. You don’t remember anything?”

Me: “Remember WHAT?”

Eric: “I don’t want to type it. Let’s meet somewhere. Are you still in the \[city\] area?”

Me: “Yes. Are you?”

Eric: “No but I will drive to you. I can be there in 3 days. I will DM you when I’m there.” 

I ended up giving myself a migraine yesterday evening, racking my brain trying to remember, but there’s just the memories of *Monster’s, Inc*. and Jan’s cooking and that was about it. Also, I remembered the first time I met Richard and Jan and went to their house for the interview. It was early evening toward the end of May and hot already. Their house was in a pretty nice neighborhood (compared to mine) with a lot of trees in which the houses were spaced out from each other, all of them built in the seventies and eighties. Their house was dark unpainted wood with a pretty red door. You could barely see the house from the street because of all the trees, but I remember you could see the red door. 

I parked my old Volvo in the driveway and walked up the pathway to the front door and rang the doorbell. I barely had time to adjust my posture before the door opened up, and Jan stood there. She was tall with prominent cheekbones and ash-blonde hair which was cut in a style that was a little outdated. Her clothes were nice, but very proper: slacks, a floral blouse, dress shoes. Not a lot of makeup, but she didn’t really need it. She had natural beauty and very crisp hazel eyes. 

“You must be Danielle!” She said, opening the door wider.

”Yes, nice to meet you.”

I held out my hand for a handshake, and immediately felt stupid for doing so, but Jan graciously accepted it, and gave me a warm, gentle handshake. 

“Come in,” she said.

The first thing I remembered was that it smelled very light and clean, like one of those generic store bought candles that attempts to capture fresh linen or a summer meadow. I never remember seeing a candle, but I can still smell that fragrance in my mind now. My house never smelled like that growing up.

She led me into the living room. Because of all the trees surrounding the house, the interior was pretty dim. There was a bit of fading sunlight coming through the windows, but not enough to brighten the room. It was also very clean. No toys or stacks of mail or empty Coke cans anywhere. The furniture was all beige and the walls were a pale green, and the oak coffee table matched the TV cabinet and the end tables, like the whole room had been purchased out of a catalogue. I remember that a painting of a pond in a field of flowers hung above the sofa. Again, not real art, but rather something ordered online that had come already framed. I only remember it, because I used to stare up at it when falling asleep on the couch. Don’t get me wrong, the house was nice, but it felt, in hindsight, more like a TV set than an actual home. However, my childhood home was so messy and dysfunctional, I assumed this was how *normal* people lived. 

“Richard!” Jan called out. “Danielle is here!”

“I go by Dani, actually,” I told her. 

“That’s fun,” Jan said, gesturing for me to sit down on the sofa. “Jan is short for Janette.”

I nodded, not knowing if I was supposed to say her shortened name was fun, too, and sat on the sofa. There was the sound of feet on the stairs and a few seconds later, Richard entered the living room. He was tall, with a slender face and glasses. Sort of balding. He was wearing khakis and a button up shirt and clean sneakers. I’m sure he was probably early forties at that time, my parents’ age. 

“Nice to meet you, Danielle,” he said, shaking my hand.

“She goes by Dani,” Jan corrected him.

”Oh, well, that’s fun.”

Sort of odd that they said the same thing, and I didn’t see how shortening one’s name was ‘fun’, but I smiled.

Jan offered me some lemonade, but I was too nervous to drink anything. I can’t remember much in terms of what questions they asked me or what I told them, other than not knowing what I wanted to study in college. All I remember is that they worked at the local hospital, at night. I’m assuming they explained the schedule and all of that. I can’t remember, but I do remember my nightly rate, and that was all that mattered.

After we’d visited for a bit, Jan went to go get Eric.  

My first impression of him was that he was really pale, especially for it being summer. He was also very neatly dressed. Most kids wore t-shirts and shorts and had dirty shoes. Eric wore khakis, like his father, which was odd, and a tucked in polo shirt. His hair was parted and combed neatly. All of this was very unusual to me for an eight-year-old boy, but his wardrobe fit Richard’s and Jan’s. 

I also remember he didn’t say much to me, but when I was a kid I was shy, so it wasn’t a big deal. I can’t say we hit it off, but he was eight, and I was eighteen, so we didn’t have anything in common anyway. 

I found out the next day that I got the job and started the evening after that. For the next three months, it was the same gig Monday through Friday: eat dinner, say goodbye to Richard and Jan, Eric would take a bath and get into his pajamas, we’d watch *Monsters, Inc.*, then he’d go upstairs and go to bed. I’d watch TV in the living room until I fell asleep.  Jan was my alarm clock in the morning when she and Richard returned from work. It was honestly like that movie *Groundhog Day*. Pretty much the same day every day for the entirety of the summer.

I’ve been going through the memories I do have over and over again, thinking something will suddenly reveal itself, but I can’t remember anything except how nice Richard and Jan were to me, so what could Eric possibly mean? What could they have ‘done’ to me? I remember all sorts of things that my own parents did and said to me growing up, but being at Richard and Jan’s house was such a relief. A safe place with delicious food, no arguments, no cigarette smoke.

Anyway, I will post as soon as I meet with Eric (if he actually shows up).
