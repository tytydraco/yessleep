It happened when I was really, really drunk. I’d not even wanted to go out drinking that night. Angelica had called me and said her friend had found a man who she wanted to go home with. She wanted me to come out and keep her company. I got dressed, met her and danced away with her in some nightclub.

Somehow, the four of us ended back in my apartment. I’m not sure why. Alcohol, probably. Angelica collapsed straight into bed and I kept her friend and new man company on the couch. Third-wheeling as I put song after song on the stereo. Only when I’d realised my night was nearly over, and they were making out right in front of me, did I lean over and say goodnight to the fourth person in the room. 

The only problem was…he wasn’t there.

My weeks at work only funded the real living that I did on the weekend. It was a Friday night. Nobody wanted to go out. Everybody wanted a ‘quiet weekend’. 

So, I found myself alone again on the couch. Angelica was snoring loudly in the other room. I was out of my mind on gin and soda. As I played another song, I turned to the person to my right and asked if it was okay. I’d only played the Beatles all night. He may not have even liked the Beatles. He said, “Sure, play another one.”

In the morning, this time, I remembered it. I remembered the person more clearly. I fought back through the fog of my mind. I tried to remember who he was. Why he was there. What was happening. But my hangover took control. As I was vomitting into the toilet bowl, trying not to wake Angelica, I remembered him again. His face. How he was there. With me. When he shouldn’t have been. 

I wasn’t right at work the following week. My hangovers usually last two days. So on Monday, he wasn’t a priority. Getting through the day was. Completing deadlines. Helping with the merger. Small talk at the coffee machine. Anything until I could run right out of that door and hit the couch in my apartment. 

I had planned to meet a bunch of friends from school on Saturday in a new bar in town. We didn’t often see each other much these days. We all lived close. We enjoyed each other’s company. I never laughed like I did with them. 

The day turned into the night. We tried any bar that would have us. We karaoked Queen and Abba. We narrowly avoided a fight at the kebab shop. Soon, I was on my way home, starting to sober up and I remembered again. His face. The same face I’d not stopped thinking about all week. 

I passed the nightclub Silver’s, on my own, and considered heading in there. Angelica was out with her friends somewhere. In there, I wouldn’t have to go home. And face whatever might be waiting for me. I told myself I was being stupid. Strange. 

I got myself together and headed back to the apartment. It was empty. All alone. Lights had been left on around the apartment. I stood at the end of my bed and stared. I knew where I should be going. I knew what I should do. I just couldn’t. 

I moved over to the kitchen. By the sink, I stored my collection of various spirits. I took out a bottle of club soda from the fridge. Poured myself a gin and soda and topped it up heavily with lime. 

On the sofa, I began browsing through YouTube. Songs I never listened to unless I was wasted. The TV knew me so well. One gin and soda led to another. And another. Angelica still wasn’t home. On my fourth drink, I turned to him and asked him if he wanted to play a song. I put my arm around him and the colours changed.

I was back. Back on my sofa. Breathing heavily, I turned to him and asked was he okay? Why I asked if he was okay, when I was so clearly not, I don’t know. I felt sick. 

“I’m going to go, Brett,” he said and stood. I watched him move across the living room for his coat. “It’s been a great night, mate. See you sometime next week.”

I don’t know who left but he left. I didn’t stick around. I went to bed, woken only by the sound of Angelica coming in around an hour later. 

For the rest of the week, I couldn’t shake the feeling of unknowing. Who was in my apartment? Was it some guy I’d ‘befriended’ from the bar? Or did I just not recognise him?

I knew I was losing it. My grip on reality. Something was wrong.

I went on Google and tried to search for my symptoms. I spoke to CHATGPT who advised that I was having some sort of paranoid fit. That it was very obvious that I did know who that was - I was just suffering some sort of breakdown. 

On Friday, I blamed work. I surmised that it was a lot for me to deal with at the moment. The company was looking to relocate to the next major city. We were in the process of a big merger - something I was apart of very much. I put it down to that.

It was only while I was on Google that it occured to me. Instagram. If I knew this guy, I’d follow him. I followed over one thousand accounts. I spent my lunch break combing through each one. Worringly, I knew nearly everyone. Every person I’d followed drunk. People I’d lived with when I was backpacking. Random people I’d met at university. All of them. Except Scott Banks. 

Scott was the mystery man. On his account, I flew down his profile. Analysing his photos. Trying to replace him. Slot him into my social mind. There I was. In several photos. With him and my school friends. 

One of us in Dublin. Another skiing. I didn’t go to school with him, did I? Is he a friend of a friend? Is that how I knew him?

I didn’t realise what I was doing until I’d done it. I sent him a DM and asked if he wanted to go out for a drink this weekend. 

**HEY! GOOD TIME LAST WEEKEND. FANCY A PINT?**

He replied as I was getting into bed. Angelica was putting her eye mask on. I was casually browsing through TikTok when his name flashed up at the top of my screen. Confirming our date for Saturday. 

He wanted to meet up at Gold’s. He said he was there for some co-worker’s party or whatever but expected it to be a bust. Said it’d be a good place to grab a quick drink before dashing out. 

I Googled Gold’s. I’d lived here all my life. I went out most weekends. I’d never heard of a bar called Gold’s. No, that can’t be right. I typed in another name and got no results. Only a pawnbrokers twenty miles away. That was enough for the day. I locked my phone and fell asleep.

On Saturday, I stood outside the nightclub and couldn’t believe what I was seeing. I knew it had to be right. I knew it was me that was wrong. There was no possible way. I had casually dropped in the name Silver’s to Angelica several times earlier that day.

“I’m meeting Scott at Silver’s tonight.”

“Where?”

Something was happening. Something was happening, specifically, to me. I couldn’t think straight. I was drained. Exhausted. Work had clearly had a bigger effect on me than I’d ever realised. I knew I could stand outside in awe at the big luminious sign that read Gold’s all day…or go inside. 

I didn’t see Scott. I hadn’t felt comfortable messaging him much more than: SOUNDS GOOD. It hadn’t felt right. He was a stranger. No matter what everything around me had said. I did not know him. He was not a part of my life. 

I went to the bar and ordered a triple vodka and coke. Six pounds. The place was packed. Far busier than I’d ever seen Silver’s. Nightclubs didn’t really do well in today’s day and age. People swapped drinking and hangovers for run clubs and matcha. It was a world I wasn’t apart of. I was a relic from decades gone. I was old fashioned. I didn’t have skin fades and skinny jeans. 

I necked my drink. Ordered another and necked another. I couldn’t leave myself alone with my mind. Although, I needed to understand what was happening. But…what was I going to ask him? Was I going to ask how we met? How we knew each other? Would he look at me like I’m insane?

I could play it off as being too drunk. Anyone can say anything when they’re drunk and it’s allowed. You forget all about it in the morning. Those are the rules. I had another drink and knew I couldn’t stand up for too long. 

I moved over the sofa near the edge of the dancefloor. There, I watched the people dance around. Laugh and dance. Spend money. Have fun. Forget about life and the world and everything. I sipped my drink and looked out for Scott but couldn’t see him anywhere. 

A girl collapsed next to me, exhausted from dancing. I turned at her and returned her smile. I was impatient. I was feeling the confidence given to me by the alcohol. I needed to speak to him. Ask him. How did he know me? Who was he?

I turned to the girl next to me, her hand on her forehead, drunk and awake, ready to bounce back up and start the party all over again. I pulled out my phone and asked her if she recognised this guy. Said he comes in here a little bit. She reached out to touch the phone, her fingers gently laced over mine, and the colours swirled all over again.

I felt light-headed and woozy. The sickness in my stomach was ready to be launched. Nothing had happened and somehow everything had happened. I looked around me and the crowd had halved. The busy nightclub had emptied. Had I been sick? Had a fight broke out? What had happened?

“Sorry, I don’t recognise him,” she said. 

“It’s okay, thanks,” I replied. I knew better than to talk to a drunk girl in a club. Especially now. I stood up and headed to the bar. I was beginning to feel a little bit better. Starting to get my balance back and my head lowered its pounding. I leaned against the bar, now easier to get a drink and asked for a triple vodka coke. The barman nodded and sorted me my drink. Twelve pounds. I didn’t bother to question the hike in the price. I paid and put my drink on the napkin. Then I read it.

Silver’s Night Club and Bar. I looked around. Behind me, the flashing neon sign now flashed ‘Silver’s’. What the fuck is going on? I couldn’t wait for Scott anymore. Fuck Scott and fuck this. I’m out of here. I tried not to panic. I made my way to the entrance. I burst onto the street and charged in the direction of my apartment. 

I had to go home. I knew what was happening. I was becoming an alcoholic. All those weekends wasted away going to bars and drinking to excess was finally beginning to destroy my mind. What else was I supposed to do? I could play tennis on the weekend. Angelica would probably play tennis with me. 

Angelica. I looked at my phone to check her location and saw she was at some bar way out of town. I wanted her home. I wanted to tell her everything. She wouldn’t think I’m crazy. 

I scanned my fob into the door. I clicked the elevator button. I tapped my foot as I waited for it open. I clicked floor 20. And I stared at myself in the mirror as it ascended. I was losing my hair. I was getting grayer. Older. More wrinkles lined my face. I had to start taking care of myself. I had to do better. 

The door opened. I was out in the hallway. I put the key through the door, went to unlock it. Shit. I’d left it open. I really was going mad. 

I opened the door and went straight to the bathroom. I relieved myself. 

Everything changed when I opened the door to the living room. The reason why I’m even writing this is all because of what happened when I opened the door to the living room. I should have paused before. Gathered myself. Had a quick look around and said goodbye properly. I didn’t. I opened the door because that’s what I always did. This was my apartment. I don’t know if I thought I was going to sit down and have a drink. Or if I was going to reheat something from the fridge. I don’t know, honestly. 

When I opened the door, I was there on the couch staring right back. Neither of us made a sound. We didn’t speak a word to one another. It was like he understood the moment I did. But I’ll promise you one thing, I was there. On my couch. Watching TV. Sipping gin and soda. 

And that’s when I realised I’d been Replaced.
