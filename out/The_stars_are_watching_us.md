Our little family lived deep in the Appalachia mountains, so far from everything that the only road into town was sometimes the only road out. In winter, snow covered it for weeks at a time. Sometimes the town disappeared beneath the snow completely. It never seemed to dampen the mood of the community. My family had lived there for generations, we were well respected among the other locals.

My great-great-grandfather was one of the first settlers. He called the valley the Garden of Eden. They haven’t changed the name since.    
The valley had everything we needed. Passed down through time, according to him, there only a few rules to survive here.

One of those rules was simple.    
When a child turned ten. One at a time, they went to Mama Wei. Everyone did eventually. Once a year, in the middle of December, when the nights are longest and the snow comes down hardest, the children were taken to her house. A few days later they come back. They always came back.

The week before my birthday, people started treating me differently. They gave me gifts, my favorite was the bright green army figurines my uncle brought me.    
They hugged me longer than usual, people who I had known for my whole life. Some of them wouldn’t look me in the eyes. When I started to get uneasy, seeing all the commotion my mother brought me aside to boost my confidence.

“Honey, they’re just jealous of you.”

“Jealous of what, my good looks?”

She warmly smiled. Giggling behind pearly white teeth.

“Oh sweet heart! There’s nothing to be worried about.”

“I didn’t say I was, Mom.”

I look to my father, who was standing beside her resting his smooth hand on her shoulder.    
My father didn’t say anything, not unlike himself, cup of coffee in his other hand. He just looked at me, at least I think he was. His eyes seemed like they were almost staring past me, glazed over by the late nights at his job.

The night before my birthday, he had laid my new clothes across the bed. Garments of bright reds and golds, made of thick wool and carefully stitched silk. It looked so vivid on my grey shaggy blanket. Colors brighter than anything I’d ever worn.    
Outside, snow tapped against the windows.    
My father knelt beside me and brushed my shoulders.

“We’ll be here when you get back.”

“Arent you coming with me? I would feel better if you did.”

He smiled and knelt in front of me.

“Don’t be afraid. Mama took care of me.”

His hand paused on my shoulder, finally looking me in the eyes.

“ So she’ll take care of you. Now get some rest son. I will walk you to her in the morning.”

After that I drifted off to sleep, looking at the bright hues of the clothing that I will soon be wearing.    
—    
Eyes blasting open, My fingers ached from the cold screaming through my hands and into my chest, but I couldn’t take my eyes off the sky. The stars looked normal. Bright, beautiful, except for three    
Three red dots hung among the night sky, slowly rotating among the stars. I blinked.

Clunph.

Clunph.

Clunph.

I didn’t know what I was running from. I couldn’t see anything behind me, but I knew it was there.

I blink again

Clunph.

Clunph.

Clunph.

My pace has picked up now I’m sprinting.    
The hairs on the back of my neck tingled, no it burned. Almost causing me to become dizzy.    
Then I heard across the frozen lake.

A low whirring. A buzzing. A click. I started running faster. The snow swallowed my legs.    
Pressure against the back of my neck. As blinding light surrounds me from all directions.    
—-    
I turned around. My room was dark. I was soaked in sweat, I hope it’s sweat. The glass of water beside my bed was empty, even though I didn’t remember drinking it.    
Another night. Another dream, but today was my birthday. My tenth birthday. In our town, that meant something

In the morning, my father and I walked to the edge of town.

The snow was waist-deep in places. At the end of the trail stood Mama Wei’s house. It looked like an old cabin built against the mountain with a crooked roof and a stone chimney. Yellow light glowing behind the windows. Mama Wei stood outside waiting for me.    
She looked exactly like a grandmother should. White hair, soft cheeks. A faded cardigan carrying a wooden cane she didn’t seem to need.

She grabbed my cheeks between her cold fingers.

“Hmmm.”

She leaned closer,her breath smelled like decay hidden beneath peppermint.

“Ahhhh.”

Her smile widened.

“Ten. Chkk Ten years old.”

She laughed harshly. Closing her hands around my face, as if making me look like a fish.

“Come along, child.”

She looked toward the door.

“We’re waiting for you on the inside.”

I looked back at my family, how could they let me go with such an old crow. My mother stood beneath the trees with one hand covering her mouth. My father raised both thumbs beside her.    
When the door opened, I stepped inside warmth poured over me.

The house was nothing like I expected.    
The walls were gilded while a huge fireplace roared against the far wall.    
There were paintings hanging everywhere, along with shelves full of books written in language I couldn’t read.    
The wooden floors were polished enough to reflect the firelight. A large wooden ornate dinning table sat in the middle of the room with a bountiful display of all manner of pastries and desert. There was so much food.

Four other children were inside.    
One boy was laughing with a girl near the fireplace.    
Another was sitting on the floor, trying to put together a wooden puzzle.    
A fat boy sat at the table eating grapes off the stem, for the first time that day, I relaxed.    
Maybe I had been wrong.    
Maybe Mama Wei really was just an old woman.    
She smiled at me.

“I hope you’re hungry.”

I nodded unable to find words to describe my excitement.

“A growing boy such as yourself needs to eat.”

She gestured toward the table.

“Tonight, you feast like a king.”

So I did.    
I ate until my stomach hurt.    
Roasted pig, stewed potato’s, fruit pies, grapes.    
Everything tasted perfect.    
Mama Wei moved around the kitchen humming an old tune. There was something I found comforting about it. Like I’d heard that song before. I couldn’t remember where.    
When I finished eating, Mama Wei placed a bowl of my favorite candy in front of me. Saltwater taffy.    
Giddy I asked.    
“How did you know I liked these?”

She stopped. For just a moment, she didn’t move.    
Just looked at me like she was taking a moment to think. Then she smiled.

“Because Ive been watching you.”

She walked away. I started after her wanting to know what she had ment by that, but the boy tugged on my bright red silks.    
I viciously yanked it from his grasp.    
The boy still eating grapes looked at me.

“What?” He murmured

“What’s your name?” I asked, needing to know who I was tattling on.

He shrugged.

“I - don’t don’t remember.” Stuffing his face like a gorging animal.

I laughed. I thought he was joking.    
Nobody else laughed. I glanced around the room. The girl near the fireplace didn’t even look up. The boy with the wooden puzzle kept working even though he has been working on the same piece for the last few hours I’ve been there. Mama Wei continued humming, then suddenly the room felt heavy.

I looked at the boy again.

“You really don’t remember?”

He shook his head.

“No.”

“How long have you been here?”

He shrugged.

“I don’t know.”

Something very cold suddenly settled in my stomach. I looked toward the windows. There weren’t any, I thought there had been on the outside.    
At least, I couldn’t see any, just glass too dark to see through.    
I walked toward one of the windows.    
My reflection stared back.    
For a second, I thought I saw something standing behind me. Something tall and thin, smooth skin down to where I couldn’t see it anymore. I turned around and was greeted by nothing.    
When I looked back at the window, I looked up at the sky, I wanted to see the stars on my birthday. But I saw no stars, all I saw was red dots lining the sky where every star should have been.

I stumbled backward. Mama Wei was standing behind me. Her head was tilted slightly.

“Aren’t- aren’t you tired, child?”

Before I even could answer, she continued.

“Of course you are.”

Her humming started again. Only now I recognized the tune. It wasn’t a song.    
It was the sound from my dream.    
That same low buzzing started from the very back of my mind with that same clicking.    
My ears began ringing, pink and green ooze seeming to squirm its way out.    
The fire suddenly burned white. I tried to wipe the snotty liquid from my cheeks. The golden walls flickered, for half a second the illusion was broken. It was metal. Cold metal everywhere like a cube of incomprehensible shininess. Covered in strange lines of blue symbols. When I blinked I was back in the house, back with Mama.

Mama Wei stood in front of me but her face was wrong, like where the pieces should be wasn’t matching up. Not monstrous. Just… unfinished, skin so smooth it was almost shiny.    
it was like a someone had tried to remember what an old woman looked like and gotten the details almost right.    
Her eyes didn’t blink. I shrieked terrified of what I had just bared witness to.

“What are you?! I want to go home!”

In an instant, her eyes widened far too wide, almost stretching over her face like a misfortunate puppet, shedding its skin. Baring teeth sharp and contorted.    
A forced buzz in the back of my head went off like an alarm.

“YOU ARE HOME, CHILD!”

Then pressure in the back of my neck.

THUMP!

Suddenly everything went black as I hit the floor unconscious.    
——    
I woke up freezing. Snow pressed against my face, trying to move with all my might was no use. Something had wrapped itself around my arms.    
Roots. At least, I thought they were roots.    
They twisted and folded around my wrists and chest, pulling me against the cold, wet ground and then I felt the all too familiar warmth as I looked down, frightened to see the same metal I saw earlier just before I ran into Mama.    
There was blood running through the restraints.    
I tried to call out.

“Hello, is anyone there!? Anybody!? Dad, help me please!”

Looking up to the sight of dark trees again. The forest swallowed my voice.    
I screamed for my father for what seemed like forever, my voice even gave out. Then I heard something behind me, approaching, slowly. Wood bending under an enormous weight.

I looked toward the trees. Something moved between them.    
The stars were visible through the branches.    
Except there were more of them now. Hundreds, no thousands of stars they all were red. Then they blinked.    
My stomach dropped. They weren’t stars. They were eyes.    
Something poked my neck.    
Sharp pain protruding all down my spine    
I felt fingers touch the top of my head.    
Just like Mamas. Only these fingers were much longer than I remember.    
I started closed my eyes and started crying.    
Then I heard my father’s voice.

“Don’t be afraid.”

When I opened them he was standing in front of me. For one beautiful second, he looked exactly like I remembered. The same tired smile he gave me before he left me.

“I told you we’d be here when you got back.”

I felt a numbness start to overtake my head and cloud my thoughts.    
As he approached me I could see his face twitch    
His jaw shift, skin rippled beneath the surface.    
His eyes became huge and black.    
Completely black.    
His fingers stretched until they touched the snow.    
I stopped breathing. He tilted his head.

“Khhh—rrrraaa—tik… tik… tik… ooohh.”

His voice wasn’t his anymore.    
It was the same buzzing sound from my dreams.

“Graaah… nnnn… sketh… sketh… AAAAA—”

I shook my head in a fit of violent desperation as    
reached four hands outstretched toward me. The trees behind him began moving, beating. The entire forest seemed to be breathing violently now. Then I looked past him, and finally understood. There had never been a house. The thing I’d called a house was buried inside the mountain.    
All of it had been part of a much larger structure.    
Something that had been waiting beneath the snow, to reach beyond the stars. the town hasn’t been blessed by the old woman. They were being collected.    
The town had been sending its children to Mama Wei forever, who knows how many people it’s taken.    
They who has been here long before my family arrived.    
The place my ancestors had called the Garden of Eden, has been a gravesite for my mother, my father. I could only think of them as it started to inspect my body. Poking and prodding on my blue frostbitten skin.

The sky split open behind the eternal being.    
A vile shriek sounded off in the back of my head.  Waves of nausea hit me, stabbing pains in my stomach and my spine, as waves of blue-white light poured through the non-Euclidean structure in the sky to the snow over my head.

The clicking became deafening. Above me, the stars began winking, blinking, growing closer and closer. They started to circle around us as I continued to struggle.

The one sweet grandma leaned very close. So close I could smell the awful putrid sweetness from the form it had taken. No longer disguising its true nature. The mouth stretched into a smile that no human face could make. Without moving it I hear.

“Dont be afraid child, your family will see you again soon.”

As cold fingers tightened around my head.    
One star started down like a long proboscis inching towards my face until it sat above my eye as a needle. The all too familiar sounds of whirring metal. Inching towards my retina. The beings hand so strong I couldn’t move until finally it reached its destination, my conscious mind. Then everything went black. If anybody lives in the mountains and sees me in the village, run far away because they will want you next for your skin. They will take you under the mountain.
