Right now I’m in a stranger’s house. It's early morning, the mortician is doing its work right now, he told me. I don’t know him and I don't know if I can trust him... But he did save me, and it’s definitely better than going outside after what happened last night.

Look, I never thought these things were real… and I’m not much of a paranoid either. But I've spent an hour trying to find the words to describe my last night in Woodlan Town, and I just simply can’t…

The worst moment of my life has already passed. I’m safe now, I think. I’ll take it step by step and see if I can get some sort of coherence to this craziness. I also hope It’ll calm me down and distract me for a moment, so forgive me if I take it with some sarcasm at first.

For starters, my name is Pedro.

My family likes to go on road trips every two months. I usually don’t go, but this time I felt like going. Yeah, I just felt like it. I am proudly spontaneous and indecisive. I went with my little brother, my mother, and my father. The whole family was together. The road trip was, indeed, a road trip, so I’ll spare you the boring details. At one point, we were driving along just fine. I was chatting with my brother about some horses he had seen earlier, it was already late in the evening.

“Look! That one looks like the horse you have in that video game.”

“Really? Lemme see… uh-huh, yeah. It does, doesn’t it?” I said as I stretched over to look, purposely pinning him against the window.

“Get—back off… bro,” he said, trying to push me away with his hands. “You’re so annoying, gosh.”

“Hey, hey. Don’t fight now, Pedro come on, you’re too old for this.” my mother said.

“Old? I’m only 21, what are you on about?”

I might’ve been annoying, you know, I kept invading his space just to bother him. But I dunno, I was just bored. I could tell he wished I hadn’t come on this road trip.

Suddenly, right after that, the car stopped. I looked over at my father, who was driving, and he looked confused, just like everyone else. It was as if the car had simply given up on us. My father tried turning it back on, but it just wouldn’t respond. I looked over to see my mother fidgeting with her hands and, to me, it seemed like she was really worried we’d be stranded in the middle of nowhere.

“Why aren’t we moving? Mooooove! Mo-mo-mooove!” my little brother said, bouncing up and down in his seat. “Go!” he yelped one last time.

“Oh, fucking quit it, will you!? I’m working on it, goddamn it,” my father responded.

I sighed and leaned back while looking out the rear window. I expected us to be there for a while because my dad didn’t know shit about fixing cars. Most dads did, but mine didn’t. I didn’t even know how he managed to drive.

I took a moment to look outside and see just where the hell we stopped at. Around us, there was nothing. An endless road and massive treelines that wrapped around us. But there was also a man, slowly coming toward us in the distant horizon. His most distinctive feature was a scar running across his chin. He had dark hair, dark eyes, a green vest, some dirty pants, and he was carrying a deer on his shoulder. At first, I thought he was just some hunter that came to hunt in this area, but then he spoke.

“Hey! Need a hand?” the stranger yelled. “I don’t know much about cars, but down here…” He pointed to the right, toward a dark forest with a dirt road leading into it. “There’s a town with an amazing mechanic who’ll help ya.” 

His smile was off. Something about it didn’t sit right with me. At the time, I shrugged it off.   
  
I’ll probably end up asking for some advice, so here is mine: always trust your gut instinct. Those shivers you get… sometimes they feel like the world is speaking to you, warning you. And that time, I just… ignored them completely.

I know now I shouldn’t have.

My father walked up to him, shook his hand and thanked him for offering his help. 

“William, nice to meet you,” the man said.

“Joshua,” my father replied.

William told us we could leave the car there and follow him to the town, where he’d show us where the mechanic was. Luckily for him, there \*was\* a sign that indicated life existed beyond those pines.

"Woodlan Town," it read.

My father and I went back to the car and grabbed a couple of things before entering the woods. I reached for my backpack whose contents I shared with my little brother, and my dad reached for mom’s and his. He never told me, but I saw him grabbing the gun he always carried in the glove compartment. I guessed he’d gotten the same shivers I had and had brought it along just in case. Then again, I wasn’t sure. He was stone-cold. You couldn’t get any emotion out of the guy that wasn’t anger.

We followed the man and quickly reached Woodlan Town. When we arrived, the town itself looked like any other small town. It was tiny, with no more than twenty houses. It was surrounded by tall pine trees that made it look even smaller, almost insignificant compared to the scale of Mother Nature. Every step I took through that town felt as though the trees were closing in around me, as if the town was moving instead of me, and I was simply walking in place.

The moment we arrived, we got strange looks from the few inhabitants. I wouldn’t say they looked unfriendly; they just looked confused. Confused by our arrival, or maybe even worried. I definitely didn’t feel welcomed here. After a short walk, we reached the mechanic. My father greeted him and proceeded to explain what had happened to our car. I noticed the mechanic was hairy too. In fact, most of the villagers were.

I took a moment to look around. I noticed a church in the middle of town, a butcher’s shop, what looked like a town hall, a small plaza, several little houses and cabins, and a police station with a jail attached to it. But my search ended abruptly when a stranger placed his hand on my left shoulder. I jumped and turned toward the man beside me. He looked me up and down, slowly scanning me from head to toe. Before I had time to take a good look at him, he already got the first word out.

“You ain’t from here,” he said while chewing gum.

I took a second to respond. “Uh, no… I’m not.”

“You planning to stay for long?” His chewing grew louder. He had started chewing with his mouth open.

“No, I guess. I don’t know. We just came to get our car fixed. We won’t bother any of you. We’ll leave as soon as it’s fixed,” I said.

At that point, I started getting nervous. I knew we weren’t welcome there, and this stranger seemed dangerous. I noticed he was wearing a necklace with a whistle, and when I looked down toward his waist, I could see the grip of a gun slightly poking out from beneath his shirt. He noticed me looking and quickly pulled his shirt down to cover it.

“Name,” he said.

“Pedro.” I felt like I was on trial, or that I was being pulled over. He had a very serious tone.

“Pedro what?”

“Look, respectfully, you don’t need to know my full name. Like I said, we’ll be gone soon.”

“I don’t think so,” he whispered in a raspy voice.

“Excuse me?” I asked, taking it as a threat. For a moment, I thought these people were going to kidnap us, I didn’t understand what I did wrong, why this guy was so aggressive. 

“No one comes to this town and simply leaves. Have you gotten a tour?” he asked with a smile.

“I don’t think I need a tour. I can see everything from here.” I tried telling him I wasn’t going anywhere, that I wouldn’t follow him if that's what he wanted.

“You’re calling my town small, eh?” He stepped forward. A rotten odor filled my nose, and I instinctively leaned back so I wouldn’t have to breathe it in.

“Dude, no. I’m sorry. That’s not what I meant.” I said, letting out a nervous chuckle between words.

“And now you’re laughing in my face. DO YOU KNOW WHO I AM!?” he shouted, grabbing the attention of the entire town. His mouth opened wide, revealing sharp teeth. In the process, a few drops of saliva landed on top of my eyelashes.

“I’m the fucking vigilante here.” he said.

At that moment I froze, nobody around reacted to it. I felt overpowered, useless. The entire town had turned to look at us, and not a single person seemed surprised that this guy had just screamed in my face like a maniac. The guy with the whistle kept staring at me, breathing through his mouth. I could hear it. Wet and heavy.

Then my father stepped between us.

“That’s enough,” he said, calm as ever.

The vigilante looked him up and down, then smiled again.

“You the boy’s father?”

“Yeah.”

He reached for his mouth and took the gum out, shaking it off his finger. “You oughta teach him some respect.” 

“And you oughta stop shouting at my son.”

I was surprised he said that. I felt the tension between them, but the vigilante didn’t strike me as someone who’d fight over a stupid argument. Nonetheless, people started gathering around and it only made the situation worse. I looked behind and saw my little brother had grabbed my mother’s arm and was hiding behind her, peeking around her side. From behind the vigilante, the sheriff—at least I assumed he was the sheriff by his outfit—made his way as he spat onto the dirt.

“You folk always get defensive,” he said. “Makes people nervous.”

“We didn’t come here looking for trouble,” my father replied. “Our car broke down. That’s it.”

A murmur spread through the crowd, I couldn’t make out what they were saying. Then, a woman stepped forward, she was probably in her sixties, wearing a dark dress despite the heat.

“Sheriff, sheriff.” the woman repeated. “Sheriff, I’ve got some to tell ya.”

“Martha? What is it.”

“Him, there. Ask him where he was last night,” she said.

I looked at the woman pointing in my direction. Not only had I gotten into problems with the vigilante, I also had to deal with this woman trying to question me or something.

“Ask who? I just arrived.” I asked before I could stop myself.

“Nuh.” She shaked her head and waved her pointing finger past me.

I turned and saw William, just standing there. He had the same dead deer slung over his shoulders, its legs hanging down his chest. Blood dripped onto his boots. He looked exhausted more than anything else. A brief silence shut the town, for a few seconds only the distant crows could be heard. I looked to the sky and it seemed like the crows were avoiding flying into town, deliberately changing course.

“The hell does this have to do with me?” William broke the silence.

“Where were you?” the woman repeated.

“What’s this about, Martha?” the sheriff asked with open arms.

“I was hunting, as you can see,” he taps the dead deer’s thigh.

“All night?” 

“Yes, Martha. That’s usually how hunting works. You know I leave early in the morning.”

The sheriff looked at the deer, then at the scratches on the hunter’s forearms.

“Today, it took me a while, but I got the deer. I also met these guys on the way back, thought I’d help them, so I led them here.” William said.

“Yeah, yeah, he’s right. Our car broke down and he offered us help.” my mother said, stepping in the conversation.

The sheriff ignored my mother and fixated on William. “Say, you got yourself torn up pretty good.”

William looked at his arms and laughed, expressing some sort of discomfort. “It’s the woods, trees do that. It’s a fucking jungle out there," he points to the forest.

“Trees leave bark in the cuts. Yours are clean.” said the sheriff.

His expression changed, he looked annoyed more than anything. “Oh, come on,” he muttered. “We’re doing this again?”

My father leaned slightly toward the mechanic. “What is he talking about?” I heard him say, but the mechanic didn’t answer immediately, he kept his eyes on William.

“People have been losing animals,” he said quietly. “A few folks heard howling at night.”

“So you think he’s killing livestock?”

“Not exactly.”

“You’re all out of your damn minds,” William said. “Every year it’s the same thing. Something goes missing, and you start looking for a monster.”

I looked at my father, expecting him to say something, but even he seemed unsure. His hand rested near his jacket, close to the gun I knew he was carrying.

William must’ve noticed, he turned directly to my father and asked. “You don’t believe this crap, do you?”

I looked to my father and he hesitated to say a word. I didn’t know what to think of it, I felt dragged into a problem I had no idea about and no implications.

This felt oddly familiar, what did that remind me of? Well but of course, it reminded me of home, strangely. This kind of arguing… I’ve already heard from my parents. One side already knows what has to happen, and they know the truth. All they need is to confirm it, to get the other person falling right into their trap.

“You see.” Martha said. “Even he can feel it.”

Feel what? I wanted to ask. The guy looked dirty and irritated, not supernatural. 

Then the sheriff turned to the crowd. He took a breath in and opened his arms wide.  “We can’t ignore the signs anymore.”

“What signs?” William barked. “Scratches? Mud? I live in the woods, for fucks sake!”

‘The woods’. That last part echoed longer than any other word, the trees around shook slightly and the remaining birds started to flee. I felt a cold shiver, again. This time I felt it on the little hairs on the back of my neck. Dusk was not upon us anymore, it had quickly gotten darker. Some townsfolk had already started lighting their lamps.

“Wrap it up, sheriff. We can’t stay much longer without sunlight,” the vigilante said.

"Have you lost your mind?!" William shouted one last time. "You've all gone crazy."

Everyone kept quiet.

The wind shifted through the pines, carrying with it the smell of damp earth and pine sap. In a moment, the daylight had already disappeared. The sky had turned a deep blue, the kind that came just before true darkness settled in.

The church bell rang. Its echo bounced through the town. Every head turned toward the eastern treeline. I followed their gaze and saw a full moon slowly rising above the trees. The crowd grew louder, and some townsfolk hurried into their houses, locking their doors and switching off their lights.

The plaza had quickly gotten out of control. Whatever the sheriff or Martha wanted to get done, would have to wait until tomorrow. I wasn’t sure why they were running, at first I thought they were scared of the night. The vigilante blew his whistle and walked toward the crowd, trying to calm them down.

Even though a heated conversation had just started, everyone quickly dismissed it. Whatever it is that they’re scared of must’ve been really dangerous. I stood there, watching panic unfold, trying to find where my parents were. In front of me was the sheriff who, looking at him, revealed a strange expression. He was staring upward, at something behind me, at what I thought was the nothingness or maybe the trees. But I noticed his skin turned pale, his pupils widened, and his jaw slowly dropped. He seemed… scared too, but it was different.

I knew that look, everyone does.

It's the kind of look you hope you'll never see on another person's face.

I heard some rustling behind me, a weird sound. It sounded like meat being destroyed and torn into pieces, and then put back together, like slime. I swallowed, scared to turn back. 

Like I said, I don’t believe in ghosts, or mythological beasts or demons. But this time I felt like something was behind me. 

I thought of William. I was defending him, he seemed nice, he helped my family.  
  
He was standing behind me before the panic... since the panic.   
  
Ever since the panic, I just looked forward, I never turned to William.   
  
I lost sight of William, a man who was being accused or questioned by the town for god knows what.

That moment... I said to myself, "fuck."   
  
Many thoughts crossed my mind in a second. Like, have I gotten lured into this crazy town by a serial killer? Am I seconds away from being stabbed to death, just like that poor deer?

I blinked a couple of times. With every blink, the world seemed to grow darker. Faces began to blur together. People started disappearing into the darkness. The ground trembled beneath my feet, and a violent wind blew through the square, whipping my hair into my face. Maybe five seconds passed, maybe less. But suddenly, the town felt different. My family was nowhere in sight, and the townsfolk were running in fear.

I had no idea what to do. The sheriff and I stood there, frozen with fear and confusion.

“Sheriff?” I whimpered.

I'd started hyperventilating. I wanted to move, but my body refused to obey me. The sheriff's jaw trembled, he was trying to say something, but no words came out.

“MOVE!” the vigilante screamed at the top of his lungs. “BEHIND YOU! GET OUT OF THE WAY!”  
  
He reached for his gun and aimed it straight at me. He was standing not too far from me, where he had just turned away from the crowd to check on us.

“W-wha-what?” I said, my throat dry and a voice crack.

A split second later, I heard it. A horrifying growl.

It came from directly behind me. The sound was so deep it felt like it rattled my ribs. My body reacted before my brain did. I threw myself sideways, collapsing onto the dirt like a sack of potatoes. I imagined the worst, my life flashed before my eyes. Whatever was behind me wasn't human, and I knew there was nothing I could do to stop it. My head slammed into the ground hard enough to knock the air out of my lungs. Dirt exploded into my face, blinding me. All I could see were smudges of brown through watering eyes.

Then I heard the sheriff scream. “WOLF! SHOOT!”

A second later, something massive charged past me. Then it stepped on my leg.

The weight was unimaginable. It felt like a refrigerator had been dropped directly onto my tibia. A sharp crack shot through my entire body, followed by pain so intense it made me dizzy. I screamed and cried, but those sounds were not the only ones heard.

Bang.

A shot from a gun, then a man's scream and finally, a guttural roar. Something heavy crashed into the ground with enough force to make the earth tremble. Through the dirt in my eyes, I caught the silhouette of a huge, black, hairy figure collapsing no more than ten meters in front of me.

I lay there, my leg shattered, the adrenaline slowly wearing off. The last thing I remembered was someone grabbing me by the collar and dragging me away.

And...  
  
That was the end of it. 

A complete nightmare.

Whatever happened after, I wasn’t conscious to see it. I was dragged away by someone who had witnessed the events unfold.

After passing out, I woke up in a small cabin. I was met with the town's butcher. He told me what had happened last night and that he brought the doctor with him to treat my leg.

I can tell you now it's a… hell of an injury. I can’t walk properly, and I have to be hopping around until they find some crutches for me. I haven’t gone outside yet, almost no one has, it’s still very early in the morning. 

From here I can’t see the aftermath but I hope no one else was injured. I tried calling 911 but there’s no one on the other side.

I have no friends to contact because I’m a fucking loser, and all I’ve got is the internet to help me, and my family. God, I hope they’re okay.

I will try to keep everyone updated, I still don’t even know what’s going on, or what the hell hit me. But I’ll try to get the fuck away from here, somehow, even with a broken leg.
