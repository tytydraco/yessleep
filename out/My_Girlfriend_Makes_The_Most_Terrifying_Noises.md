March 5th

​Emily grabbed my shoulder and tried to pull me back as I approached the street that the screams were coming from.

 The fresh rain residue was slowly being overtaken by the dark viscous blood as it flowed down towards me.

​Further up, screams echoed out as bodies lined the ground.

 Some of them had stopped moving, but others were still writhing in their agony as they desperately tried to clutch their ears, desperately moving away from radios, phones, speakers, and anything else with an electronic signal that littered the area.

​Neither Emily nor I could hear what they were hearing. They begged us for help, but I didn't know what to do, or even if I should do anything.

​I was glad that the shock was taking over my body, because standing here, as this filthy town died, would be inhuman. But helping would be too kind.

​March 4th

​"You're a coward," Emily broke down in tears as her hands struck my chest. I felt the impact, but I didn't care. Instead, I let the bag fall from my hand and hugged her. "How can you just leave, Mason? Now, of all times?"

​"I'm sorry," I said as her tears soaked into my shirt. I didn't want to stay. I couldn't, not in this filthy and corrupt town.

​"Mason," she said, letting go and looking up at me, "the wedding was supposed to be tomorrow. You can't leave like this."

​"Then what do I do, Em?" I looked at the TV; she wasn't there this time. Instead, they were reporting on some kind of tech malfunction. The stoic reporter talked about how much of a tragedy it was that radio waves had crossed, causing every piece of tech in town to emit a small, distorted sound.

​What they didn't talk about was the real tragedy, the corruption, the hate.

​"Mason," she backed up and stood against the door, "we have to fight it. We have to find justice."

​"I failed, Em." I walked towards her and moved her out of the way. "I tried, Em. She's everywhere, and I think she knows I failed."

​"I know she is," Emily said, hugging me again. "I see her too, but please stay, for me.

​March 3rd

​The crowd chanted, as their screams echoed throughout the town. I had one last shot at this and I needed to do it right.

​I made my way under the cover of the hallways and passages until I was in the service entrance, leading directly to the school soccer ground. ​Outside, hundreds of fans had gathered to see him.

​"School Quarterback cleared of all charges, Ready to play"

​I stared at the headline all morning, my blood boiling inside as my veins pumped with anger. They were out here today screaming and chanting for him; they loved him.

​I raised the gun under the shadow of the stands as the announcer screamed Greg Martin and he burst onto the field. 

The crowd had lost their minds for this piece of trash; they worshipped him. They were as corrupt and filthy as he was.

​I was ready to pull the trigger. I was ready for the consequences. But my phone rang at that exact moment, her name on screen.

​"My love," she said, as I picked up. I didn't respond; the only sensation I felt was my own tears. 

​"My love, don't do this.

​I tried to ignore the voice and straighten the gun, but my hand kept trembling.

​The bastard was standing there smiling as he ate up the attention.

​"My love," she said, "drop the gun. Leave. I promise I will make this right."

​March 2nd

​I held Emily's hand softly as we waited outside the courthouse. The hearing would be over soon; justice, at least some of it, would be served.

​Instead, my body went cold as it realized what I was hearing before my brain understood it. It had been a tragic accident; she was at fault.

​Emily broke down. She was crying on her knees, but I couldn't move.

​Moments later Greg Martin stepped through the doors, his father, the sheriff, beside him. He looked at us as he passed, remorseful but fake.

​I didn't know what to do. I was angry, broken. Emily predicted my actions before I did and grabbed me; I pushed her away and lunged at him.

​I didn't land a single punch. Two deputies grabbed me. The last thing I saw before I passed out was my blood on the ground, as my head slammed into the pavement.

​It was night before I woke up. I was in my apartment as Emily wrapped up my head. She was bruised too, but she ignored it as she tended to me.

​"I'm sorry, Mason," she said. I wanted to apologize as well but I couldn't find the words.

​As she stood up to leave, the apartment was drenched in darkness.

​"I think I have candles in the kitchen," I said as I tried to stand up, but light enveloped the room again, this time coming just from the TV.

​Then slowly, as if it were a hallucination, Skyler appeared on screen.

​March 1st

​This town is beautiful, flawless, and she was the center of it.

​"My love," she said as I met her at the entrance of the library and she held out her hand for me, a book about sirens clutched in the other.

​"So you found it," I said as we walked down the steps together.

​"Do you think I'm weird?" she asked, throwing me a coy smile. She already knew the answer. 

​"Yes," I said, laughing. "Yes, I do."

​She chuckled back at me, as I mused over her reasoning for wanting a wedding dress based on mythical lore.

​"I mean," she said, "evil mermaid, what's not to love about them?"

​I thought about it and maybe she had a point, as her phone started buzzing and she let go of my hand to answer it.

​"Em," she said as she continued to move forward and I trailed behind to give her her privacy, "I think I found my perfect wedding dress idea and," she paused, "you're gonna design it for me.

​I admired her beauty. Emily wasn't my favorite person in the world, but she was Skyler's maid of honor.

​"But you have to work fast," Skyler continued, "because we only have—"

​I didn't see it; I don't think she did either. The car jumped the pavement, then it was past her and she was on the ground.

​I tried to scream her name but no words came out as I held her in my arms, not moving, not breathing. 

The only thing I could see was my dead fiancée in my hands and Greg Martin stumbling out of the car. The only thing I could hear was the distorted sound coming from her phone as it lay beside her.
