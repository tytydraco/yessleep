Hey Reddit. I've got something pretty neat to show you guys. Though I guess it's worth explaining how I got these first.

I've enrolled in mime school recently. I guess ‘recently’ is only relative though. I'm about done. I've been going for about two years now and I'm coming up on the tail end of my last year's final semester. I'm about to become a full time mime.

Honestly it's almost a little surreal. It's been my dream ever since I was a kid. Now it's a reality. That just goes to show you can do anything as long as you keep fighting. On that note I'd appreciate it if you guys came to see me at radio city. I'll post the dates as soon as they answer me.

I went to a family reunion this spring break. I got to tell my whole extended family about mime school. Well it’s more like I had to tell them I guess.

Anyway this uncle I have got really excited when he heard. I’ve like never once talked to this guy. I’ve heard a little bit about him though. They told me he killed a bear once cus it was lurking around his house too much. He told me he had something he thought would make for great inspiration and then he went through a closet and started digging. 

He was doing that for a while and I could see that his closet was filled with all sorts of old junk. There were a bunch of old outfits and some sign for a brand of beer and there was this really big brown rug that looked super matted and old hanging between the clothes. Anyway when he was done he handed me six crinkled and yellow envelopes. They had letters inside.

Well I read the letters. There’s like seven I think. And I don’t really know what he was talking about. They’re pretty weird though so I decided I’d post ‘em here. Seems like the right place to do so.

Anyway that’s enough preamble. Without further adieu I present to you the first letter. Here:

Good evening, Judge. (Or afternoon or morning. Wherever this letter may find you.)

Do you remember me? I guess a better question might be if you even remember that name. Though perhaps it's no question at all. If you're in the same position I am you remember it all far too clearly. Even then you might still forget me. I don't recall properly making your acquaintance until the ride home. You and I had a good laugh then. I was near you physically the whole time but we never properly spoke until then.

Anyhow I've come to you with a question. And no it isn't one of the several I've listed above. I've been turning this over in my mind for a while now and I still am yet to decide. I’ve wanted to bring it to someone for some time now but there’s no one to do so with where I live. Then I remembered how you let me know where to reach you years ago and then you seemed really the most proper choice from the beginning. There's a reason they call you Judge and not I. Ha.

I've been figuring to kill a bear Judge. Though I'm still unsure whether I should actually take up the task. I'm sure that might sound strange but I have various reasons. I guess it's best to be as thorough as I can.

For starters it's harming my sleep quite a bit. I find it awful hard to do so already living alone as I do but the bear only compounds that issue. It takes very little to wake me up now in terms of sound. The bear makes noises far worse. I hear it from my window. From the first rustle of its toe to the soil I jolt awake. I begin sweating. I have stained my sheets from this. And it keeps going. The sound does not stop for nearly the entire night.

I am in fact writing you this letter at night. It is perhaps two in the morning. I can hear the bear now rustling through my bushes. 

I have not slept in days. I think maybe a full week has elapsed since I've gotten an hour. When I look in the mirror I see puddles of violet spilling over my pale cheeks. And I don't nap Judge. I never liked to do so as a child and now it's simply impossible. I really ought to find someone to stay with. 

I guess this now leads me to my second and biggest reason for hoping to kill the bear. It disrupts my garden.

You see since I've moved here I've put a considerable amount of time and effort into the cultivation of my garden. I've got it all pruned like persian. The grass beneath your feet is all so soft. The blades all reach up to the same level and they shine so beautifully under the light. It's a beautiful green. And there are bushes all along its borders. Their leaves all a deep and healthy green as well and their little flower buds so soft and pleasant. In case you are wondering the garden is not fenced off. The bushes act as my fences. Sometimes it's sort of like they guide me as does a red carpet. And they lead right towards my favorite spot in the garden. 

In the corner of my garden there's this great big oak tree. Its wide berth of leaves and branches shade me from the summer sun as I lay under its roots(and I often do). These roots jut up under the dirt to make little waves. It would bother me how they disrupt the grass if the tree weren't so precious to me by now. And I hadn't even planted it there! How funny.

The tree actually belongs to the forest which surrounds the backside of my house. I understand that often neighborhoods are set so that the back of one row of houses is facing the back of another but the houses just end at my street. This is partially the reason I have no fence surrounding my yard. I have no reason to get one as you will see. It just opens to the forest. I like to keep my bushes free and all. Give them a little breathing room.

All in all this garden is my favorite place in the world. I don't believe there exists a single spot in this whole planet as welcoming as it. It's like the Garden of Eden relegated to my own back yard. It's a place where all things may exist as God intended them free from the corruptions of man. 

This brings me to another point actually which loops into What I was just harping on but bears consideration on its own: the garden itself is practically asking me to rid the world of this bear. 

A week or so ago I'd been resting under the tree. Its great roots cradled me and its branches kept me cool. It’s especially nice this time of year when it gets just somewhat chilly. The sky was grey overhead. Soft and murky. The soil below my back was softer than my own mattress and it seemed as though my bushes rose up far above my head like the walls of my cradle. I felt so sweet in that moment. And the world reciprocated. It was like I was picked up by its branches and rocked slowly back and forth. 

So as I stared up at the beehive which hangs above me I thought I might just fall asleep. I often nap out in my garden. It’s the only time I’m able to sleep. But this time before I could a bee descended from the hive and began buzzing before me. It woke me up in an instant and the bee was soon hovering before me.

“Buzz buzz,” it said. “Could you do me a favor and lend me your finger to perch on. All this flying really does get exhausting.”

“Oh,” I replied. “I’m sorry. I didn’t realize.”

I lifted my finger in a hook. It landed between knuckles and panted a sigh of relief. Its feet were all fuzzy. They almost tickled. 

“Thanks,” it said.

“No problem. What do you need my finger for?”

“To rest on. I told you this.”

“Oh. Okay. So what’s all this about? Have you got something to tell me?”

“Yes. Very big reason. Very important.”

“What is it then?”

“Well. You know of the forest surrounding your backyard.”

“I do.”

“Then you think you’d be aware of what’s in there. Wouldn’t you? But you aren’t. No one could ever tell what’s lurking out there. It’s dangerous. Every inch of it. And it's so large.”

“Yeah. I’m aware. Is that it?”

“No. I’m not done yet. I come with a message from the entire hive. We need you.”

“What for?”

“The forest has threatened us. It’s a bear. It comes at night. It rubs up against the tree you are resting against right now. It shakes us and scratches its marks into the bark. You hear it. Don’t you?”

“Yes. I hear it. Every night from my window. Why do you tell me then?”

“Why else would we tell you? We’re here to ask you to do something about it.”

“Actually?”

“Yes. Us bees are far too small to vanquish this bear as we would wish. It would take too many fatalities to ward it off with our stingers. So we need you to save us from this bear. We need you to make our lives right again. We need you to vanquish the evils of the forest.”

“Alright. Do you want a fence around the house?”

“Would you want a fence around your garden?”

“Not really. I like it more open. But it would help.”

“No. The bear could break it. It would break it. You should know this.”

“I guess you’re right. So now what? What do I do?”

“You know what you must do. You must kill it. Kill the bear.”

And then he flew off. And so it was settled. Though I’m still a bit reluctant to trust the expertise of a bumblebee. This is why I’m coming to you Judge. I want to know if it’s really worth it to kill this bear or if there’s any other solution. Surely it will destroy my garden if I let it. Or maybe I should just live with it and not risk my life. You know how bears are.

I’m not so great at deciding. I haven’t done so after six days. So I come to you. Plead Judge. Just tell me. What do I do about this?

Sincerely,

\[The name was smudged here\]
