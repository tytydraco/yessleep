I laid there, eyes foggy and every sense dulled, wrapped in bedding like a pretzel.

"This is bullshit." I mumbled.

It was the fourth time I had gotten up this morning, and every attempt to return to slumber only became harder to achieve. There had yet to be any natural light. Okay, maybe the curtains were closed, but there would always be a sliver of sunlight.

Untying my legs and rolling over to a nightstand, I picked up my phone.

It was 11:36 AM.

"Oh, crap." I say, with increasing panic.

I dash to turn a light switch, without success, and tried it several times before resorting to my phone battery. I was late to work, but the earlier the arrival the less of a scolding *(hopefully)* from that prick boss Anders. I swapped into a beige sweatshirt and grey pants, and proceeded into the hallway, phone in left hand. These windows didn't have curtains, so to hell with it and burn a few precious seconds.

All three windows were covered by layers of earth and bits of rubble on the other side. That's impossible. I'm not even sure if you could pile enough soil to the second floor of a subdivision, which I reside in. But inspecting the silt closely, with all the bumpy lines, it was genuine.

The concept froze me in place. If there was enough soil to reach the second floor, so was the roof. I despised even thinking the possibility of being buried alive.

*At least you don't have to go to work for a day, maybe more.*

I forced a chuckle at my own thought.

I shone the phone light, now at a solid 44%, towards the stairwell. The right side of the stairs had been crumpled inwarrds. My garage was on the other side. Although the damage was nasty and would probably be a thousand or more repairing, I couldn't see through any holes, even up closely. *I can check the room later. I'd kill for a meal.*

I'm in the living room. The kitchen is connected, and only separated by a wooden table for three. The walls and ceiling are painted storm blue. Every little bit of furniture was in its place, to my relief; there was no damage down here.

The front windows are covered with curtains, but instinct in me turned towards the door.

I twisted the lock and turned the door towards me. A small pile of dirt trickled through, going up to my knee.

"What the fu..."

A loud *creeeeeak* trailed across the front of my house. Not structural load kind of creak, but something moved above the building. It could be the soil shifting, but things felt off.

I stood for about a minute as the creaking moved around and then ceased.

My next course of action was to figure out what I had, because I sure as hell was trapped down here. Although most lights and electronics refused to start, a red dot shined from the corner of my TV.

I pulled the remote out from an aged, peeling grey couch and got jump scared by a rather futuristic startup sound. It only occurred to me how dominating the silence had become.

I tried NBC, CBS, CNN, all the large stations. All channels were snow, although a few local stations were stuck on test screens. Hopeless, I just muted the television. The light at least saved my phone for a while.

*It is getting stale in here.*

The kitchen seldom worked in any way. No appaliance was spared. I swinged the cabinet open, grabbing a glass and placing it below the faucet, switching on cold.

A magenta liquid immediately flowed down, and I let go, glass hitting the sink and cracking. The pink liquid was almost opaque and changed in shades of pink. I turned it off. Frozen, I watched the last fall into the train, traces of a reddish substance within.

"There has to be something."

I swung every cabinet door open, even if I knew a cabinet didn't have food or any other item. The refridgerator gave a foul smell, and I quickly returned to shoving contents onto the counter. Spice packets, condiments, dry goods, a few dollar store snacks... and not a single drop to drink.

A part of me increasingly wanted to lash out. I was running out of options. If this was really real, what will get me first? The roof or walls caving in? Or a slow death as I become a husk? I had no cooking, or reliable tap water, and I was noticing small but present cracks run through the drywall.

I got the better of myself and continued past the kitchen. This area was half-finished and was home to a few old shelves, a washer, dryer, and the garage door. I felt through the line of cleaning products until I found the flashlight and then turned to do the same for a battery case. Once complete, the flashlight shined to life and created an impressive ray of light, beige like old computers.

Holding it close, we felt like the last sense in this world.

The garage door opened without any resistance, which is relieving. The flashlight shined on an old Chevy from 2017, colored white, and pinned against the wall. Damage wasn't just at the stairwell, as it had continued along the frame below them. My car was also on an incline, where I saw that half of my garage had caved in.

Most of the garage contents were either crumpled or buried, but a wall rack on my side was intact and included several tools, including a shovel. I climbed over the roof and slid off the hood, freeing the shovel from its hooks.

"Finally."

My voice was starting to become raspy and the lack of ventilation didn't help. Only half of the garage door was covered. I can stop here and scavenge bottled water. Pretty sure I keep a case near the corner.

I was delicate with shoveling, only taking spoonfuls away from the top downwards. Peckish and dehydrated, my carefulness paid off and the shovel struck blue plastic that glistened like I found sapphire. I removed enough dirt from around to pry the case out with my hands.

As soon as I grabbed the case, the dirt started to fill the space, and it increased, realizing that a crescendo was just starting.

"Shhhit" I hiss in a low, almost whisper-like voice, retreating to the hood of the car and beginning to try and throw the case over, but I underestimated the landslide as it undid my excavation and the roof frames only groaned. Climbing over the roof of the driver's seat, one of the center beams gives way and starts piling a stream of dirt onto my leg that does not slow down. I thrust the shovel forward and continue forward as if I were swimming. I throw myself into the concrete, pain dull.

I have no time to assess the situation. The door slides the shovel towards the house as I watch the rest of the structure collapse for a moment.

I watched my life flash before my eyes, and in the end, I earned nothing.

I sat at the table for a long while. This was it. There was nowhere to go. I thought about the world. *Was I the only one? Where are the people I know? Am I the last man?*

Fury implodes within, and I slam multiple items around the house. A plastic plant. A calendar which I had not updated in four months. A box of Cheerios into the tiled wall. I watched the contents trickle away as the box descends.

*Oh my god.*

The side of the garage next to the house collapsed. If it's not enough to destroy the building, I would have reduced the elevation by a floor. This was my opportunity, one that I would not miss. With renewed vigor, I returned upstairs, placed the flashlight on a rustic end table, and dragged a few towels to form a crude tarp. I slid the window up and slashed the mosquito net, tearing it out with my hand. 

My short, curly black hair slowly became drenched, and my shirt stuck to my skin, but every scoop counted.

Ten minutes, maybe ten, had passed.

A glimpse of true light broke through.

*The sun.*

Glorious light broke through, and the air, oh my god, the air felt glorious. For too long I have been trapped inside this hole.

Never thinking I would place value in such an ordinary thing, I excitedly carved out a larger space for me to exit. As a cherry on top, there was less resistance, like if...

...if my neighbor had tried digging his way out too.

He laid on a slight incline backwards in the scorching sun. He in his typical attire: a pair of slip-ons, teal work pants, a button-up white flannel, aging skin. The man who sat behind the wheel of a sixteen-wheeler long before I moved here. I almost didn't recognize him because we never socialized outside of a "*hey"* or wave.

I looked up to his face.

The lack of skin made me freeze, but what was even worse was how it had been done. It had been neatly trimmed in a square. He lacked any notable facial features like eye sockets, a nose, or a mouth, like it had been flattened out. I tremble back and almost fall back into my window out of nausea.

Ice flowing through me, I slowly dig out a shaded area to see if his house was accessible. I wasn't risking my life for whatever eldritch being controls the sun now.

His window was closed, but a large hand-shaped hole stood in the middle of the pane. All edges were blurry and darkened, like something had melted its way through.

*"Damn. Like, I-*" Not even my inner thoughts can contemplate it. For the first time in a long time, I felt lost. Nothing guided me.

I focused on the choices I still had. Could an umbrella cut it? Should I furnish some weapon out of my living room? Maybe I can go towar-

*Whoosh.*

Silence filled the air. Then again. It was from my neighbor's house. It started distant, then became ever so closer.

"Holy shit. No."

Although I doubt it'll do a lot, I slide the window down, and after thinking twice, locked it. I grab the flashlight off the nightstand and navigate the zig-zagged stairs.

I've taken shelter inside a closet opposite of the fridge. I've set my phone on mute, the flashlight off, and hid behind as much clothing as I can. I hear it... *levitating.* There are no footsteps, just a rough estimate of where it goes.

If anyone is still out there, don't get up.
