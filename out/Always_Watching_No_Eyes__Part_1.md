Internet horror, a genre most are familiar with if you have spent any amount of time on the interweb. You got yourself ARGs, Creepypastas, Cryptids, Mythologies, and those videos you find late at night when you're half awake and not knowing if it's real or fake. Most people know it's fake, enjoy the good storytelling or shitty, cheesy, over-the-top, hyper-realistic violence, but then there's the younger audiences that have their wild imaginations and believe it's real.

I was the latter. Twelve-year-old me really wanted to believe there was more out there. I had experienced some "supernatural" stuff, like seeing a ghostly lady wearing overalls (which was what my mom normally wore when I was younger) sitting between my brother and I when we had shared a bedroom.

We had also seen those classic stairs to nowhere in the woods of Oregon. Those were always interesting. Whether they were covered in carpet, stone, or just wood, they were never connected to anything.

Then there was one of my favorites: "shadow people." Most of the time I just used it to scare my friends. Other times, I really couldn't tell if it was the summer heat playing tricks on me or if I really did see something—someone far out in the distance staring at us.

But that wasn't my absolute favorite. No... that crown belonged to the tall, faceless things I'd spent way too much time reading about online.

I consumed so much media surrounding those kinds of myths. Every creepypasta, every story, every video. I grew an obsession with them. Now, I didn't do anything over the top like that horrid friend group who murdered an unfortunate soul. No, I merely took my best friend at the time and went exploring into any wooded area just to see if we could find something.

Usually, nothing happens. It would just get dark, which spooked us into running back home, but there was one time... one time in which, to this day, I still don't know what we saw in Silver Falls State Park.

We were 14 at the time, nearing the bookend of our creepypasta phase but still having some whimsy. We would, out of shits and giggles, make up scenarios as we walked around about what character from the stories we read would be chasing us. It entertained us and annoyed my mother.

So one day she thought, what better way to get some "me time" than to bring us to Silver Falls State Park and let us run around while she read a book on a bench.

Let me tell you, it was one of the best times in our lives. Running down hills, throwing rocks into the river, accidentally bumping into strangers before running in the opposite direction. Honestly, it was the most free we had felt, and we didn't once think about the video games waiting for us at my home.

We spent almost the entire day there, even watching the sun fade behind the mountains, which had let us know it was time to head back to my mom.

We didn't have a working phone at the time, only a yellow iPhone 5c which had Angry Birds, Temple Run, and Jetpack Joyride. Just a Wi-Fi phone to pass time.

Now, it wasn't like we were scared when nighttime hit. We knew nothing was scary out there besides a few stray coyotes, maybe even a bear further into the woods. Outside of all that, we tried not to let our imaginations roam too freely and have us think there was something spookier just beyond our view.

We kept our conversations light, mostly focusing on what we're gonna do once we get back to my place and what are we gonna do after summer is over and we go back to school. None of these conversations were inherently scary, although my subconscious was starting to slip.

Regardless, I kept thinking I'd see the occasional shadow shifting just out of my peripheral. My friend started to notice as I kept glancing off to the side as we walked down the trail. I just reassured her that everything was fine and I was simply keeping an eye on our surroundings.

But then she started doing it as well... which spiked my curiosity, and I asked her if she was also seeing something.

She just nervously laughed it off and told me to focus on the trail and change the subject.

I wish I could've, but the thought was lingering in my mind and taking my eyes away from the path. Call me a liar as you please, but I swear it felt like something was watching me, watching us.

Thinking about it now, I am sure we weren't that far from the entrance of the park, but at that time it felt much further, only barely being able to hear my mom yell our names to get back to the car so we could go home.

We did pick up the pace, however, as we were really starting to get freaked out, with the added feeling of my mom making the classic joke of, "If you don't make it to the car, I'm leaving y'all here!"

Normally, I would've laughed off that kind of statement, but with the looming feeling of something lurking nearby, it felt more appropriate to dead sprint to the car as if it were a race.

I swear, with every five steps we took, it was like three steps taking away, and it didn't help that my friend ended up tripping over a tree branch, making her sprain her ankle.

It stopped us dead in our tracks, having to help her get off the ground, and this, dear reader, is where you really wouldn't believe it.

But hand on the Bible, I thought I saw him... or at least, something that looked enough like the things I'd spent years reading about to make my stomach drop.

Although he looked different.

Instead of a suit, he was purely naked, smooth with no details, looking decayed, almost alien. He was standing in the tree line, tendrils wrapped around the tree trunks, watching us.

My eyes wouldn't leave his sight, barely even dragging my friend off the trail until she snapped me out of it by being in front of my view.

The color was drained out of my face as I helped her walk. As I progressed off the trail, when I looked back, he was gone...

I know what my fourteen-year-old brain wanted to call him. I had spent years reading about the tall, faceless thing that was supposed to haunt forests and stalk people from a distance. I wanted to believe that was what I'd finally found.

But looking back now, I don't think it was.

Eventually, we made our way back to the car where my mom was impatiently waiting for us. She didn't even ask what happened to my friend until we were fully in the car and down the road.

The last thing I remember from that day was looking through the back window and seeing him... peeking around the trees, larger than life, watching.

I am now 23, and every now and then I get that creeping feeling that something, someone, is watching me just outside of my bedroom window, waiting for the next time I go out into the woods.
