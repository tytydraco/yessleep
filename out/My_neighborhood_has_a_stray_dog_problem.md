I was out for a walk one afternoon when I heard someone call for me. A woman stood in the driveway of a house about fifty yards away, trying to hold a dog on a leash that I assumed was hers back from attacking another. 

I approached her, mostly because I wanted to make sure it wasn’t one of my in-law’s dogs. Fortunately, it wasn’t. 

She said she had been walking her dog when the stray had come bounding out from between the two houses we were in front of, sans collar. Her dog, which normally never met another it didn’t like, had sprang at it with an energy she’d never seen before, nearly breaking the leash and her arm in the process. 

I was sitting there with her, the stray dog nuzzling into my chest while she calmed her pup down, when a couple stepped out of the house behind us and said they could take the dog.

“Are you the owners?” asked the woman, who had introduced herself as Jess.

“No. But we’re happy to watch it until it’s claimed.”

Jess clearly wasn’t super enthusiastic about this, but her dog was still going crazy and I had to get back to work, so she relented.

I was sitting on the couch that night, watching TV, when I heard an ambulance go by. My neighborhood had a large population of elderly people, so I paid it no mind. Then I heard a police car go by.

An hour later, I was watching a hockey game when my buddy called and asked if I was watching the news.

“No. Why?”

“Because they’re standing in front of a house in your neighborhood.”

I changed the channel and got quite a nasty shock.

The “house in my neighborhood” was the house down the street I had stood in front of mere hours before.

A reporter, her face somber, advised parents to have their kids leave the room, and then turned my world upside down.

According to a statement they had managed to get from the husband, he had been making dinner while the wife played with the dog in the adjoining room, when he heard a crunching noise followed by an agonized scream. 

He’d run in to find his wife lying dead on the floor in a pool of blood. Her throat had been torn open.

A man stood over her. He looked filthy and had a crazed look in his eyes and a smile like he was just copying something he had seen in a book. His hair was damp and matted and fell to the middle of his back. 

The husband would later recall that it was the same color as that of the dog, which was nowhere to be found. The man had thrown open the front door and scampered out into the night, disappearing within seconds.

I thought of Jess's dog, lunging at the end of its leash, and of the stray pressing its face into my chest.

Outside, something began to howl.
