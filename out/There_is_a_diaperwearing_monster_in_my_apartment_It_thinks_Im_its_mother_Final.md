The ceiling was about a foot higher up than it was the day before. The popcorn pattern on the plaster was swirly and wet, like it had just been painted. This was a possibility, except for the fact that the surrounding material was dry as a bone.

I made a mental note of the ceiling for my session tomorrow. Surely if reality was creating things like the creature in my apartment it could be screwing with other things?

I put it out of my mind after the initial shock had passed. There were more important things to think about after all.

Like the thing in my apartment.

I couldn’t get my mind off it. It may be a disgusting monster, but it didn’t ask for this either. Some trick of physics brought it into reality. Was it intelligent? Did it have the same false memories I had? Was I abandoning something that thought I was its mother?

The burning questions in my mind allowed little sleep. I left early for my appointments and decided to drop off some food for the man-baby. I wasn’t going to risk being beaten again so I kept my distance. Took a bag of fast food and tossed it into the front door and closing it just as quickly. It wasn’t much but it was enough to ease my guilty conscience.

The drive to the facility was different today. The skies were grayer than usual. The world outside felt dull—devoid of color and scent. I chalked it up to the approaching winter but I knew better. Things were changing. I was probably the only person to notice.

Dr. Han started our session with another lesson. This time, it was about “mends.”

“Mends are reality’s way of closing the gap between your reality and this new one.” He scribbled two versions of a stick figure with a bridge between them.

“If you allow it, your mind will come to accept this new reality by way of false memories. Well—false is a wrong word. These events DID happen, reality made sure of it. You just need to allow the recollection to settle in your mind in order to properly assimilate.” He slowly wiped the first stick figure from the board.

“So I just need to fight these ‘mends’? Seems simple enough. I’ve been doing it already.”

“Well, that is something I wanted to talk to you about.” He said hesitantly as he started a new diagram on the board.

“When you mentioned you might be experiencing mends, I thought it best to discuss the—uh—risks.”

“Risks?” I asked, louder than I meant to say it.

“Yes. You see, reality treats these breaks in continuity as a disease. Mends are like blood cells expelling the virus.”

I watched as Dr. Han drew an array of boxes, each one more formless than the last.

“I mentioned yesterday that these breaks operate almost like a computer instruction stack. Each break in reality is a new, corrupted version of the previous. Reality will correct the branches, starting with the top of the stack.” He said as he was erasing the boxes from the top of his diagram.

“If you fight hard enough, your reality can spiral. Everything will deviate exponentially until reality decides it’s best to erase the stack from the root.” My name sat eerily in the first box. Then, he erased it.

“So if I resist reality enough, I would just die?” I said plainly.

“Well, not exactly. I mean, you could. It may just erase you from existence. Or, you could just remain in a broken reality that has been isolated from other timelines until you—uh—pass away. Even then, reality could persist until the physics of the dimension collapse upon itself.”

I sat in silence for a few moments. It was all starting to make sense now. The ceiling, the sun, the street—it was all related. I was resisting this new reality, and it was coming apart at the seams because of it. 

But what was I supposed to do? Just lay down and accept it?

That's when it hit me.

“Dr. Han?”

“Yes Kara?” He answered, still facing the board.

“Will things ever be the way they were before? In my reality?”

It felt like a dumb question, and yet, I can’t believe I haven’t asked it already.

“Kara…we promised you we would help. And we will. Unfortunately, we don’t know what that looks like yet.” He remained facing the board, slowly erasing his latest work.

“Has anyone ever been fixed? Like, fully?”

“Can’t say. We operate in this theoretical space. Some people we meet and one day they’re just gone. We assume they got what they needed, but we can never be too sure…” He trailed off on the last sentence.

“We have evidence—nothing concrete of course—but evidence nonetheless, that we have met with people in alternate realities that have since reconciled. Are things back to where they were for them? We don’t know. But the fact that things have resolved means the things we’re doing is working at some capacity.”

“And how do you know for sure? That things have resolved?”

Dr. Han pointed at a secured cabinet in the corner of the room. “Unfortunately that’s classified. You’ll just have to trust Dr. Han that his doctorate in theoretical physics isn’t just for show.” He winked.

I offered a meek smile. Something else was eating at me.

“Do you know anyone that’s stuck? You know—like things are the same and their memories don’t change?”

“Uh—yes unfortunately. We tend to lose touch with those people. They find some way to reconcile this reality as their own in some way. I mean Suzie, for example—“ He stopped abruptly. After a few moments he turned back to the board and continued erasing.

Suzie? She did mention she was in my shoes once. I started to wonder what she has to do with all this.

“I’ve said too much. Just remember, fighting too hard can result in a recursive deterioration of reality that is unpredictable. Try to relax your mind these next few days. Maybe spend a little time with your uh—man child.”

“It’s man-baby actually.”

“Ah—apologies.”

I ignored his last comment and thanked him for his time today. My next appointment was coming up.

What he said about Suzie continued to bounce around in my mind. What was she hiding? Thankfully, I didn’t have to wait to ask.

Our session was well underway when Suzie picked up on my energy.

“Kara?”

“Hm?”

“You seem distracted today.”

I mulled the question over in my mind for a moment.

“Suzie, what made you decide to take this job?”

Suzie was about to answer but stopped herself. I could see a lightbulb go off in her mind. “I am assuming Han said something? Damn guy needs to mind his business.”

Suzie sighed then continued.

“A long time ago, things were different. Very different. And things were good. I had a son, Daren. He was a nice boy. Too nice—always said he needed to toughen himself up before the world did it for him.” Her words were quiet and strained. She was fighting back tears.

“It was so sudden. One second, we’re having a walk through the city. The next, nothing. My vision was just gone. It wasn’t like I saw black—it was more like I could never see at all. Like it had always been gone. You know?” She choked on the last few words.

She paused a few moments to compose herself and cleared her throat. The tension in her face lessened a little.

“I couldn’t find him. Not just because I couldn’t see—no—he was gone. I screamed and yelled and begged folks to help me but they didn’t understand. How could they? All they saw was a blind woman break into hysterics. To them, I was always blind. And my son? Never existed…”

I felt tense. I shouldn’t have made her relive this. It wasn’t any of my business.

“Suzie…I’m so sorry. I didn’t know—“

“The organization took my case shortly after. Couldn’t explain how a woman who was born blind could somehow describe colors, shapes, and faces. It was uncanny. Anyway, here I am now. Vision never came back. Neither did my son.”

She sat there stone-faced for a moments. Then, she adjusted herself and got back to typing on her device as if nothing happened.

“I hope you believe me when I say I understand your frustrations all too well Kara. I will do whatever I can to make things right for you. Now, let’s get back to your man-baby.” She said.

I didn’t ask her anything else after that. I felt embarrassed of course. I was an idiot bitching about a man in a diaper when Suzie had her world taken away.

When the session was over, I offered another pathetic apology and got on my way.

I was going to go straight to the hotel but Dr. Hans warning wasn’t leaving my mind. It didn’t help that the world continued to look…stranger.

I didn’t see a single person out on the road during the drive. The sky remained grey and colorless. A muted sun remained overhead despite the fact that it was almost 8pm.

My psychological fight with reality was causing things to spiral.

I knew Dr. Hans was right. I needed to reconcile with the monster if I hoped to keep things from getting worse. It had to be brief. Perhaps if I bring some food again and keep my distance I’ll be safe.

I arrived with a fresh bag of fast food in hand. My neighbor was perched on his rocker next door as usual. He didn’t have any wise remarks for me today. He just stared motionless out into the parking lot below. I noticed a black bloody mark between his fingers. His cigarette must’ve burned down to the nub and fried the skin. Another anomaly I guess.

I snapped my focus back to the task as hand. It was show time. I crept into the front door. Just like last time, the smell hit me fast. I held one hand over my nose as I entered. And the mess…

God, it looked terrible.

The place was even more trashed than the previous day. The tv was laying on its side in the corner. Trash that was once scattered is now organized into mountains of that touched the ceiling.

I flipped on a light switch and squinted into the dark hall beyond the kitchen.

“Hello? Uh—baby?” I whispered. “I brought you food. Was thinking we could—uh I don’t know—chat for a second?”

Loud footsteps echoed from the darkness. I could make out two white beads twinkling from the light. It was the reflection in his eyes. After a few moments, he came into view.

I jumped as the tv lying on the floor flickered to life. It was still max volume from when it was turned on the last time I entered.

A commercial was playing. A woman was playing with a large man-baby just like the one stumbling in front of me. The advertisement appeared to be for some sort of snack called “Lil’ Bigums.” A protein heavy graham cracker for hungry man-babies everywhere.

It was as if reality was creating a narrative around my situation. Advertisements. News. Real world events. Now, man-babies were a real and natural phenomenon to this world.

Just not my world.

I turned away from the tv as the monster approached. It appeared to stand a foot taller now. The food I left it earlier was smeared all over its plump, hairy body. The ketchup left a terrifying shade of red all over its face.

I was terrified. And yet, I tried to keep my cool. That strange “comfortable” feeling was tickling the back of my mind which kept me level. I was careful not to let it overtake me. I couldn’t let the “mends” take hold like Dr. Han said.

“Hey—uh—you must be hungry huh? I brought you more food.” I held out the bag. The brown paper bag rattled in my shaky hand.

The tv flickered to another program. An informational program played about “natural development of man-children.” A man in a white lab coat chatted with a young woman while a large man-baby played with enormous blocks in the background. I could hear it muttering swears under its breath as it played.

“Gosh, they sure do have potty mouths don’t they?” The man chuckled.

“They sure do, Dan. In fact, just yesterday, my child called me the C-word when I told him it was bed time. Big guy just didn’t want to stop coloring. Can you believe that?” She said it with the whimsy of a mother describing her child’s first steps.

“Wow! That’s amazing. Sounds like me after a few beers, right fellas?” Laughter erupted from behind the camera. The woman gave him a playful tap on his arm.

He pulled a white piece of paper up to his eyes. “So Pam, we’re here today to talk to new mothers of man-children about what to expect.”

“That’s right Dan! It’s important to be prepared for when your infant man is stumbling around your house asking what’s for dinner. And just to be clear ladies, I’m not talking about your husbands!” A laugh track played again as Pam walked off-screen. She came back moments later dragging the large creature by his arm. He towered over her, it’s impressive she managed to guide him at all.

“As you all know, man-children eat the equivalent of ten times the daily caloric intake as pheno-typical children.” She began rolling up her sleeves. The monster beside her started drooling buckets as its eyes became fixed on her bare arms.

“Babies need their mama for nourishment so it’s important to offer feeding as often as possible.” She smiled at the thing beside her and held her arms out playfully.

I started to feel nauseous.

The large man-baby gripped her left arm and brought its mouth down just below the elbow. It tore it off with its teeth in one swift movement.

I expected the cameras to shut off. I waited for the bloody screams to erupt just before the screen cut to black. Of course, this didn’t happen.

The camera stayed on and faint clapping could be heard from the studio. The woman stood smiling with both arms outstretched toward the munching creature. Her left arm was now a stump at the elbow. Blood splattered everywhere. Streaks of red coated the man-baby as it ate. Dan caught a few stray splatters as well.

Something pulled my attention away from the screen.

*Crunch*.

I was still holding out the brown bag of food as my man-baby clamped down on it—hand and all.

His large head tensed and his teeth came together just above my wrist. I screamed and pulled away but it was too late. Like a wild animal, he twisted his head left and right rapidly until my hand gave away with a loud crunch and pop.

I fell to the floor gripping my stump as the blood painted my surroundings, just like the lady on tv. The creature tore away into the darkness with its dinner as my wrist sprayed red everywhere. I felt like my arm was on fire.

God, the pain was unbearable.

I rolled onto my knees and started for the door. My missing limb left a trail behind me. The adrenaline kept me level until I made it to my car. From there, I began hastily covering the injury with whatever I could find. I felt so afraid. But somehow, above all, I was angry.

Screw Dr. Han. Suzie. The man thing up there. All of it. I’m never going back. This world can rot and die for all I care.

I just finished tying off a makeshift tourniquet just below my wrist using my purse strap. The wound stopped bleeding much more quickly than I expected. Man-baby injuries must heal up faster in this reality. Something whispered in my mind that it was a fact.

My brain was getting foggy now. The adrenaline was gone and the blood loss was making my eyes heavy. I needed to get to a hospital. Or did I?

Something snapped up there. In my mind. I felt hopeless. Dr. Han. Suzie. Nothing was helping. They sent me back into this hell to try and delay the inevitable and now look where it left me.

Before I could even finish the thought, my vision started fading. I slumped into the drivers seat and passed out.

When I awoke, my head was pounding. I winced as the throbbing pain in my stump pulsed with every heartbeat.

The sun was still beaming overhead, even though the clock read 5:18am. It cast a strange orange glow over everything, like a shitty florescent light in an old office building.

I wasn’t sure what to do from here. I couldn’t think straight. I needed a hospital. No—I needed Dr. Han. I needed to resolve things quickly. He would know what to do. Screw the “process.” If we didn’t try something now, we could all be dead by morning. Or worse.

The drive over was psychedelic. I passed no other cars on the road. Not a single soul could be seen. All the structures I passed appeared to be taller, stretching up like deformed daisies towards the dull sun. When I arrived, there was no security. No sign-in. And yet, everything worked? As if people were still manning public infrastructure without missing a beat. Everything was out of place and yet nothing was broken at the same time.

I walked straight into Dr. Han’s office. Despite the time of day and absence of everyone else, he remained.

Well, sort of.

He was a few inches from his dry erase board. His clothes were damp with sweat. He was scribbling something repeatedly.

“perdocreatumperdocreatum-“

The phrase went on and on from the top of the board to the bottom. His hand started trailing off the board. He was writing on the drywall beside it now. Each iteration of the phrase was shakier and more distorted than the last.

“Uh Dr. Han?” I asked anxiously.

No response.

“I’m really hurt. Things are falling apart like you said. I need to do something-“

“Kara?” He asked plainly. His tone was cold and emotionless.

“Yes—it’s Kara. Listen, the sun, the people, everything is just-“

“Your baby needs you, Kara.” He went back to writing nonsense.

I felt like I was going to explode. I walked up to him and pulled him from his trance. “Hey! I need your help. You know how this works right? How to fix this? No more process—no more paperwork. Just tell me what I need to do!” I waved my bloody stump in his face. He appeared indifferent to it.

“We are so deep now, Kara. We have recursed so many times. So…many…” He turned back to the board and started writing over the old writing. A new phrase I could not make out.

I scoffed and let him go. There’s gotta be something here. I turned away from him and started scrambling through his desk. I skimmed through various documents. A bottle of whiskey. An old revolver. What was this guy, a 1950s detective?

After a few minutes of tearing through patient documents, I found something. A highlighted procedure from an old case:

“…although we are unable to prove correlation or causation in this case, it is reasonable to deduce that the absence of the anomaly resulted in a return to the former state of reality. The “passing” of the observed deviation was a natural occurrence that reality could leverage to rationalize reconciliation…”

I re-read it multiple times to try and make sense of it. So…once the “deviation” died, things went back to normal? All I had to do was kill the damn thing?

It made sense, after all. Dr. Han and Suzie were very against trying anything until the “process” was finalized with their management. I didn’t understand then, but now it all makes sense now. In their minds, I would be doing something immoral. I would be taking a life without rhyme or reason. They needed explicit evidence that my reality is truth in order to clear their conscience.

In their minds, I was killing an innocent. In reality, I’m destroying a monster.

I grabbed the gun off the desk and thanked Dr. Han for his help. He muttered something in Latin and remained scribbling.

I didn’t check to see if Suzie was there. Of course I wanted to say goodbye, but I didn’t want my last memory of her to be like Dr. Han. And if she was lucid, I know she’d try to stop me from what I’m about to do.

I drove back to my apartment as fast as possible. I knew reality was fighting hard against me now. My head kept going fuzzy. Random memories popped into my mind that made little sense. I saw myself floating through vast empty spaces, standing atop machines I couldn’t describe, smelling and feeling things that didn’t exist. They felt like dreams that were equally abstract and visceral.

I winced as my stump banged into the railing as I ascended the apartment stairs. The pain kept me centered. I felt like without it, I could slip any second. This reality would take root in my mind and I’d never turn back. Just scribbling and babbling like Dr. Han, stuck in this world until it all fades away someday. Or never. It was all the same—a nightmare.

My feet started to stick to every surface I stepped on. The building was turning into a clay-like substance. My feet would sink if I stood still for too long. I needed to make this quick. I stepped into my front door, gun ready.

“Hey! Where are you?!” I shot once into the wall across from me. I only had one hand to manage the gun and the recoil from it almost sent it flying out of my hand. I held it between my knees and pulled the hammer back again. It was dumb to fire blindly but I needed to make sure the gun still worked. Bonus points if the test managed to coax it out of hiding.

I glanced around the dark, melting apartment. I couldn’t see anything. I listened for footsteps. Nothing. My vision started slipping again as a rush of false memories assaulted my mind. I screamed and bashed my head with the side of the gun to shake it off. That’s when I heard it.

*Click.*

It was the sound of a gun hammer being pulled back. Just not my own. This sound came from behind me. Then I heard Suzie’s voice.

“Drop the gun and turn around, Kara.”

I did as she said. It was Suzie, but she was different. She was slick and shiny, but not sweaty like Dr. Han. She was melting like the structures around me. Her skin was a dull waxy flesh color. She wiped and scratched at her face which only made it worse. A cloudy eye dribbled down her cheek and left a dark socket in its place.

“Suzie? What are you—“

“I need you to do something for me before this whole world goes to shit.” Her tone was different. She was definitely too far gone now.

“What is going on? We don’t have time for this—“

She took a step forward. The gun was just a few inches from my face now.

“I know what you’re trying to do here Kara. I need something first. I need to see my son one last time.” She pulled out an old Polaroid of a young boy hugging a young Suzie.

I started to back away. She took two long steps forward and pressed the end of her gun to my chest.

“Just because I can’t see you doesn’t mean I can’t kill you Kara. Don’t test me.” She wiped a mess of tears and wax away from her melted cheeks. Her voice also different now. It sounded like audio being pitched down and run through an old sound machine.

“You’re not like the other cases Kara. I’ve been searching for you for a long time. Most anomalies take things or change things. You’re different—you created something. The man-baby. It came from nothing. You did that. Now, I need you to bring my son back.” She held the Polaroid closer to my face. She choked back tears as we both stood in silence.

“Suzie. I can’t. I mean, I don’t know how I would—“

“The world is ending Kara. Whether you kill it or not, reality is tossing this all away. It won’t let you!” She pressed the gun harder and wailed.

“Now try! I’ve been talking to people like you for a long time okay—waiting and praying for someone that could help me. You create things. Make my son come back, Kara! I just want to see Daren one more—“

*Crunch.*

It was at that moment that the creature erupted from its hiding place and chomped down on Suzie’s shoulder. She screamed as blood splattered from the bite. In the midst of the chaos, she accidentally pulled the trigger.

*Boom.*

I felt the bullet rip through my side. Everything went quiet. As the creature tore through Suzie, she screamed and fired the gun blindly, but I couldn’t hear a thing. I was dying now, and everything was fading away.

I felt my body go limp and I dropped to the floor. Crimson soaked everything below my waist and started spilling onto the filthy carpet below. My good hand made its way onto the grip of the gun Suzie made me drop moments ago.

Without thinking, I picked it up and aimed it at the bulbous head of the creature eating away at what was left of Suzie. They were both melting now. I couldn't tell where the man-baby ended and Suzie started.

As I struggled to steady the heavy revolver in my hand, the bullets melted and dripped out the back of barrel. Nothing but soft, wet clicks erupted from the gun as I pressed the trigger. It sounded like a knife cutting through dough.

Damn.

It was time to accept my fate. I closed my eyes and let the blood loss and shock carry me away. The horrifying sight of Suzie melting and being consumed by the monster faded to black. I imagined my life before all of this. My job. My family. Tod.

I wonder if Suzie was right. Did I create all of this? I never told her, but Tod and I almost had a child. Maybe this was all some manifestation of that dark chapter of my life. I’ve been trying so hard to make it all disappear. Maybe, just maybe, there was another option…  
Something welled up inside me. A strange warm feeling. Acceptance? Gratitude? Hope? The blackness surrounding me gave way to a faint light. It grew and evolved into something tangible I could touch. I reached out to grab it. It pulled me forward, out of the black. Then, I was awake.

I was in my apartment again. It was clean. Put together. No more wax. No more man-baby. No more Suzie.

Was this what it felt like to die? Some sort of shock-induced hallucination?

No.

It was too visceral. Too real.

I stood up and straightened my blouse with two good hands. My clothes were new, but not clean. Remnants of markers and other child crafts left fresh marks.

I heard chattering from the other room. A small boy came running from around the corner with fresh glue sticks.

“Mom! I found more glue in the—whoa. Are you okay?”

He was probably 4 years old. No—5. I remember now. He had his birthday last month. We had a party at the park. It was Dinosaur-themed.

This was my first time seeing him, and yet, I felt like I knew him his whole life.

He was…perfect. The baby I always wanted. I could see myself in him. And my parents. And some Tod…but that was okay. He’s a part of his story just like he’s a part of mine.

I realize that now. We can’t erase the ugly parts of our past. The pain. The hurt. It’s part of me. Trying to remove one part will only corrupt the whole. You need all the help you can get to understand that part of you and make it into something beautiful. Dr. Han, Suzie, the man-baby…they helped me realize that.

I bent down and looked into the sweet boy’s eyes with tears in my own.

“Oh, yes I’m fine baby. Everything is…perfect.”

His smile melted me and I felt like everything was right in the world again.

I was going to be alright. *We* were going to be alright.

It’s been a few weeks since that day. I’m letting the “mends” take hold now. I hardly remember my old life at this point. There’s so much going on it’s hard to keep track of two realities anymore. I re-read these posts to remind myself but the words are starting to lose meaning. Sometimes I forget everything the moment I close my laptop. Other days it sneaks up on me in the middle of a nightmare or when I am deep in thought. I find it best to ride it out and move on with my day. There is so much going on with Mark so that makes it easy.

Mark started school this week. I was nervous for him but I was happy to find out he made a friend on his first day. A boy named Daren. His mom and I have been chatting at drop-off. She’s sweet. There’s something comforting about her. Something familiar.

It's nice to have friends again.

Anyway, that’s my life now. It’s time for me to get back to it. My old reality is gone. This writing is a testament to that journey. But it isn’t for me. Now that this chapter is closed, I’m never looking back. This story is for you, and anyone that finds themselves in a similar situation.

You may find yourself terrified.

Confused.

Angry.

That’s okay. Take the bad with the good and find a way forward. Your current reality is not set in stone. Find people you can trust and walk through those difficult seasons together.

It gets better. I promise.
