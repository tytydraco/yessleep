[PART 1](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/s/YP1LHtfmeL)

I woke up in the hospital with absolutely no idea how long I’d been there.

I remember opening my eyes because something was squeezing my arm. I heard the mechanical sound of the blood pressure cuff tightening.

I immediately knew, here we are again, the seizures are back.

When I opened my eyes, I saw double.

The last time it had been that bad was back in 2020, when I had my first seizure that landed me a long stay in the Neuro ICU.

I looked around, trying to adjust to my surroundings.

Ceiling.

Fluorescent lights.

The smell of harsh hospital cleaner.

Then I realized there was an IV in my arm.

Matt was asleep in a chair beside me, his head cradled in a neck pillow that pressed his cheeks up like a chipmunk.

It gave me a little needed comical relief. 

He must have been devastated. We had worked so hard to get me stable.

And then I remembered the car, I remembered pulling over, not being able to move.

After that, things got fuzzy.

“Hey.”

Matt sat up beside me, removing the neck pillow he leaned forward with a compassionate look in his eyes.

I turned my head toward him and I erupted into tears.

He rushed to my side and leaned forward, giving me an awkward half-hug from above.

He leaned back and smiled down at me.

“You scared the absolute shit out of me.”

He looked like he’d either been crying or hadn’t slept, but he wasn’t about to admit to either.  
   
“How long have I been here?”

“A few hours.”

I swallowed.

“What happened?”

“You had another seizure.”

I had spent years getting away from those words.

I stared at the ceiling.

“Fuck.”

“Yeah.”

Apparently I’d been brought to Brooke Army Medical Center.

They’d given me Keppra again. I’d been off the medication for months without issue.

I stared at the Keppra, I visually traced the IV tubing down into my arm. I hated it, it felt like poison entering my veins.

The working assumption was fairly straightforward: I had a complicated neurological history, somewhere along the line my seizure threshold had apparently decided to betray me again.

Nobody seemed particularly mystified by it.

But at least to me something about what happened in the car still felt wrong.

Everything before the seizure was just off.

The sound.

The paralysis.

The phone.

I asked Matt where it was.

“Your phone?”

“Yeah.”

He pointed at the tray table beside me.

I remember feeling stupidly relieved when I saw it.

A few moments later, a young nurse walked in. She looked about twelve, with blonde hair, pink braces, and an aggressively cheerful amount of pep in her step.

Given the circumstances, her overt joy felt almost insulting.

“Hey girly! I’m here to take you down for a scan!”

I looked at Matt.

I didn’t have to say a word for him to understand my disdain.

I had apparently hit my head at some point during all of this, or at minimum nobody could confidently say that I hadn’t.

First was the MRI.

The nurse rolled me downstairs and gave me the usual instructions.

Metal off.

Jewelry off.

Phone stays outside.

I handed it to her.

Nothing about that registered as important.

I’ve had enough MRIs that they’re almost boring at this point.

In fact, as a mother of two, part of me had come to enjoy the mandatory rest hospitals provided over the years.

A large Black male tech who looked more suited to be a bouncer towered over me and passed me the little emergency squeeze bulb.

The table started moving backward.

And somewhere between the edge of the machine and the first sequence, my right hand stopped feeling like my hand.

The pins and needles started to return, I tried opening and closing my fingers.

A wave of pure dread washed over me. The numbness and tingling slowly transitioned down my right leg.

I squeezed the bulb.

They pulled me out.

The bouncer tech didn’t look so intimidating anymore.

He tilted his head at that sympathetic forty-five-degree angle people do when they’re genuinely concerned.

“Are you okay?”

“I don’t know. My right side feels weird.”

Another nurse came in.

Neuro checks began.

Smile.

Raise your eyebrows.

Push.

Pull.

Grip my fingers.

Everything was a bit off, they decided to play it safe, message the doctor and wheel me back up to my room.

When they brought me back upstairs, my phone was placed on the tray beside me.

Within maybe twenty minutes, I felt completely normal again.

The CT scan went almost the same way.

My phone stayed in the room.

I went downstairs.

Halfway through waiting, I got this strange sensation.

It felt like hot water rushing up my spine, up my neck, over my head.

Then nausea.

Then tingling.

When I got back upstairs, it faded again.

I blamed the seizure and my autoimmune disease.

When you’re chronically ill, you have the luxury of approximately nine hundred plausible explanations for anything your body does.

Later that afternoon, Matt came back.

He’d gone home to shower, get me a change of clothes, feed the cat, all the normal things someone does when their wife abruptly decides to spend a night in the hospital.

He kissed my forehead and started cleaning up the absolute disaster surrounding my bed.

Empty cups.

Forgotten rubber gloves left by staff.

Wrappers from my meal.

“I need this,” he said, grabbing my phone.

“What?”

“Your phone. Your charger sucks. I’m checking something.”

He walked toward the door with it.

A minute later, my fingertips started tingling.

I flexed them.

The tingling began spreading up my forearm.

I sat up.

“Matt?” I called.

No answer.

He was somewhere down the hall.

My heart started beating harder, I could see it pounding through my chest.

I swung my legs over the side of the bed. The floor immediately felt wrong underneath me.

I hit the call button.

The dreaded braces nurse came skipping in just as Matt walked through the doorway behind her.

He was holding my phone.

“What’s wrong?”

“I feel weird again.”

He put the phone onto my bed.

The nurse started another neurological exam.

And while she did it, the feeling slowly disappeared.

At some point, a doctor came in pushing one of those mobile workstations.

He was an older gentleman with a perfectly snow-white beard and an arched back that made my spine hurt just looking at him. Tiny rectangular glasses sat low on his stoic face.

He parked the computer beside the bed and started scrolling through my chart. We talked about my old seizure history, my medication, and finally the Army.

Still staring at the screen looking a bit puzzled now he asked

“Your husband’s active duty?”

“Air Force.”

“And you were Army?” 

There was always something funny about this. I guess I get it. I’m not what people envision when they think veteran. I’m all of 110lbs, and at 5’2 I’m not exactly GI Joe.  
   
“Yeah. Seven years.”

He nodded, still reading.

“Looks like we’ve actually got quite a bit of your VA neurology stuff connected.”

“Makes sense.”

“Interesting you did the Lighthouse study?”

“Maybe? I don’t remember, I volunteered for a lot of things in neurology when we were trying to stop the seizures.”

A few seconds passed.

Then he stopped scrolling.

“Huh.”

Doctors should be legally prohibited from saying huh while looking at your medical records.

“What does ‘huh’ mean?”

Nothing.

I gave him another second.

“Doctor?”

For some reason, that was when I looked at Matt.

His mouth was open, like he had been in the middle of forming a word and someone had paused him.

“Matt?”

He didn’t move.

I looked back at the doctor.

His head turned away from me.

Only a little at first.

I remember actually frowning because I couldn’t understand why he was suddenly looking away when I was talking to him.

“Excuse me?”

I leaned forward, trying to catch his eyes.

His head moved farther away.

“What the fuck?” I whispered

I leaned slightly more as he seemed to hold his face away from me.

His head turned again, just his head, somehow the rest of his body remained perfectly still.

“What are you doing?”

I climbed off the bed.

The IV line pulled against my arm, and I had to step around the pole, but I barely noticed it because I was watching him.

I moved toward his right side.

I stepped farther.

His head twisted farther.

I stopped.

So did he.

For a second I just stood there staring at the side of his face.

Then I took another step.

“What…”

My voice came out quieter than I meant it to.

I moved one more step, trying to get in front of him.

His head snapped completely around.

I screamed.

There is no other way to explain what I saw.

His side of his body was now facing me.

His shoulders were still in the exact same position.

But his face was facing backward.

All I could see was the side of his head.

I ran.

I didn’t think about the IV. It ripped from my arm as I fled. I didn’t think about Matt, frozen back in the room. I didn’t think about anything except getting away from whatever the fuck I had just seen.

I tore out of the room and into the hallway.

“HELP!”

My voice echoed down the corridor.

“HELP ME! SOMEBODY!”

Then I saw someone step out near the nurses’ station.

Her.

The nurse who had been irritatingly cheerful every time she came into my room.

I have never been so relieved to see another human being in my life.

I rushed to her before I even thought about it.

I grabbed her and wrapped my arms around her shoulders.

“Oh my God. Oh my God, something is wrong. Something is seriously wrong please fucking help me, just help me please—”

She spoke but it wasn’t her peppy high-pitched voice instead it was electronic, deep and warped.

“Girly, you’ve gone too far.”

I looked up at her, her face was absent of her annoyingly cheerful smile.

I leaned away from her just enough to look at her face.

She smiled.

For a second it was the same annoying smile she always had, then it started getting wider.

I stared at her braces because something between them looked dark.

At first I thought she had food stuck in them.

Then something thick and dark red pushed through the wire.

A blood clot.

It squeezed through the brackets between her teeth and dropped against her bottom lip.

I let go of her.

“No.”

Another one pushed through.

Then another.

Blood started running over her lip and down her chin.

Her eyes opened wider.

Her pupils looked tiny now, like black beads suspended inside huge circles of milky white.

“No.”

I stepped backward.

“No, no, no.”

Blood dripped onto the front of her scrubs.

Then the smile disappeared.

Her entire face became completely blank.

I stepped to the left.

Her head turned right.

I stopped.

She stopped.

I moved to the right.

Her head turned further left.

I screamed and ran.

I turned down the first hallway I saw.

Then another and another.

I had no idea where I was going.

I just needed to put as much distance as possible between me and that nurse.

There was an EXIT sign ahead of me.

Bright red letters.

EXIT.

Thank fucking God.

I ran toward it, but as I got closer it didn’t look right.

Part of the word was normal.

Part of it had turned into those same symbols I had seen while I was driving.

Something like:

EX◬T.

I stopped so abruptly I slipped and was now flat on my ass in my hospital gown staring up at it.

I got up and kept moving.

Another sign appeared farther down the hallway.

EAST WING.

Except it wasn’t fully EAST WING.

It was the same thing.

Some letters.

Some symbols.

Like the language itself couldn’t decide what it wanted to be.

EA▱T W◉NG.

I stared at it for half a second too long.

That was when my right foot dragged.

I nearly went down.

I caught myself against the wall.

A burst of pins and needles moved through my fingers.

“No.”

I shook my hand.

The tingling didn’t stop.

It moved into my wrist.

Then my forearm.

My right leg started feeling heavy.

I tried to take another step.

My foot dragged again.

Then the nausea hit, I doubled over.

It was so sudden that acidic saliva filled my mouth and for a second I thought I was going to throw up right there on the floor.

Then came the heat.

It started at the bottom of my spine.

That horrible rushing sensation.

Hot and electric.

It shot upward through my back, climbed over my shoulders, and rushed over the top of my head.

My vision pulsed.

“Oh, fuck.”

My right knee gave out.

I caught myself against the wall.

This feeling I knew.

I had felt versions of this before.

The weakness.

The numbness.

That sick neurological feeling where your body suddenly stops feeling completely yours.

“Not now.”

I reached for my pocket.

Empty.

I stopped.

My phone.

My phone was still in the room.

I had left it beside the bed.

With whatever the fuck was happening back there.

I actually stood there for a second trying to decide if I would rather collapse in that hallway or go back.

Then my knee buckled again.

I didn’t have a choice, I turned around.

I started crawling.

Left hand.

Left knee.

Drag the right side.  
   
Repeat.

I don’t know how long it took me to get back.

I would crawl past a clock and it would say one time.

I would turn another corner and another clock would say something completely different.

Eventually the nurses’ station came into view.

I immediately dropped lower behind it.

She was still there.

The nurse.

Standing exactly where I had left her.

Her body hadn’t moved at all.

Her head was still backward.

The blood that had been running down the front of her scrubs was darker now.

Dry.

That meant time had passed, I just didn’t know how much.

I stayed crouched behind the nurses’ station.

Okay.

She can’t see me.

I can get around her.

I started crawling.

Her head rotated, as if she could sense me nearby.

I stopped.

She didn’t move.

Her arms stayed at her sides.

I crawled farther.

Her head kept turning in the opposite direction.

I moved left.

Her face moved right.

I moved behind her.

Her head continued rotating.

The skin along one side of her neck pulled so tight that I could see it stretching.

Then it split.

Small tears opened near the base of her neck.

Thin lines of blood started running down beneath the collar of her scrubs.

I covered my mouth.

“Please don’t move.”

I don’t know why I whispered it.

Maybe because some part of me still thought she could hear me.

“Please don’t fucking move.”

She didn’t.

Only her head.

I crawled past her.

Her neck completed the rest of the rotation.

Snap.

Her face was forward again facing down the hallway.

It looked almost normal.

Except for the dried blood and torn twisted skin wrapped around her neck.

I kept crawling.

My room was just ahead.

Matt was still standing exactly where I had left him.

His mouth was still open.

The doctor was still beside the bed.

I stopped at the doorway.

The doctor’s head moved.

I shifted slightly.

His face turned away from me.

I moved the other direction.

His head followed.

Anywhere I moved, his face turned away from me.

Like he could detect exactly where I stood even without seeing me.

I didn’t have time to understand why.

My arm was barely working.

My leg felt like dead weight.

My phone was sitting on the bedside table.

I pulled myself across the floor.

I crawled around him.

My phone was only a few feet away now.

I reached for it.

Missed.

“Fuck.”

I stretched farther.

My fingertips touched the edge and my hand finally closed around it.

The second I got close enough to pull it toward me, something changed.

The nausea vanished.

The heat disappeared from my head.

The pins and needles stopped.

I flexed my fingers.

Normal.

“What the fuck?”

I pulled the phone against my chest.

And that was when I felt something else.

Safety.

That is the only word I have for it.

It wasn’t relief because I had a phone again.

It wasn’t relief because I could call someone.

It was deeper than that, almost emotional. 

My whole body relaxed the second I had it against me.

It felt like finding someone you loved after being separated in a crowd.

I held the phone tighter.

“Thank God.”

Then I remembered where I was.

I needed to get the hell out of there.

I crawled backward.

Past the doctor.

His head continued rotating away from me.

Past Matt.

Out of the room.

The nurse was still standing there.

I kept watching her, waiting for her body to move, for her hands to reach for me.

Waiting for her to suddenly start running toward me.

Nothing happened.

I crawled until I was far enough away that I felt safe standing.

I checked my phone.

Signal.

Connected.

Everything looked normal.

“Thank God.”

I didn’t care where I went after that.

I only knew I needed to get as far away from that room as possible.

Eventually I ended up in another part of the hospital.

At least I think it was another part.

I couldn’t read the signs anymore.

That was when I really started noticing how quiet the hospital was.

It didn’t look abandoned.

That would have made more sense.

A pen had been left across an open chart.

Someone’s jacket was hanging over the back of a chair.

A purse sat beneath another desk.

Everything looked like people had been working here seconds ago.

“Hello?”

My voice echoed farther than it should have.

I walked a little farther.

“Is anybody here?”

Nothing answered.

Every patient room I checked was empty.

Every desk.

Every waiting area.

There should have been hundreds of people in that hospital.

I kept walking.

Then I heard something above me.

I stopped.

At first I thought it was static coming through the intercom.

Then I realized it wasn’t.

Typing.

Soft, rapid typing.

Click-click-click-click-click.

Pause.

Click.

Click-click-click-click.

I stared at the speaker mounted in the ceiling.

“What the fuck?”

Underneath it, I could hear a males voice.

So deep that under normal circumstances it probably would have sounded comforting.

“—please… ret—”

Static.

“—remain…”

I stood there staring upward.

“Hello?”

Nothing.

“Can you hear me?”

The speaker clicked again.

I ran.

I turned the corner and nearly slammed into a row of tall metal lockers built into the wall.

One of them was slightly open.

I pulled it wider and climbed inside.

I pulled the door almost shut, leaving just enough of a crack to see through.

I looked down at my phone.

The screen had lit up.

A notification was sitting across the top.

Wi-Fi Networks Available.

LIGHTHOUSE\_PARENT
