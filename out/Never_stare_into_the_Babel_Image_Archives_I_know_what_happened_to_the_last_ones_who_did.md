I was desperate for a thesis advisor. Dr. Kushner was a strange, short-tempered man but a brilliant experimental psychologist with a list of publications longer than the papers I’ve even read. Despite his reputation for being overly cutthroat and nasty to be around, I knew he would be invaluable to me. Besides, I figured he had some project I could work on where I wouldn’t have to spend a ton of time with him.

After a few dead end project ideas, eventually the old bastard got desperate and gave me something that a former grad student, Amanda, had taken interest in but abandoned. It was a test on how people perceive various static images, and it used a fascinating resource I’d never heard of before: the Babel Image Archives.

Basically, it’s a canvas of a certain pixel size with so many colors that represents every possible combination of those pixels using seeded numbers. If you’ve ever played games with world generation, then this might be familiar to you. In theory, it contains every image possibly imaginable. The day you were born, the day you died, the big bang from every scale and angle, and so on. You get the idea. The big problem is that it’s so impossibly unlikely to actually find a cohesive image by randomly searching that, for all intents and purposes, it acts like a multicolored static which is exactly what Amanda had used it for.

She documented her notes carefully. She used a room on campus with a one way mirror meant for this kind of experiment. The room had a projector and chair in it. Simply, the subject would sit in the chair, listen to white noise over the speakers above, and watch as the images on the Babel Image Archive flipped every few seconds. As it did, they would talk aloud and the lapel mic would pick up what they were saying, mainly to report what they were seeing to us in the observation room. Based on old tricks of getting “high” with blank paper sheets over your eyes or staring at TV static, Amanda had thought this might produce more potent effects.

According to her notes, she had only found one participant and gotten something surprising out of him. But, a better, more complete project came along, and she had to abandon it. The notes describe a 24 year old male sitting for a whole hour with a five minute break from the images in the middle. The first half went normally, but when she turned on the second slideshow, the man sat there for a few minutes whistling to himself. After using the mic in the observer room to talk to him, he told her that she hadn’t turned the slideshow back on. But, she had. She and her lab mate checked it, even walked in the participant room. They dismissed the man and speculated wildly on their findings but never got a good answer. Fascinated, I knew I wanted to try.

We had gotten three test subjects ready for the trial. The first one was a 19 year old female, the second was a 21 year old male, and the third was a 39 year old male. We’ll call them One, Two, and Three.

I had gotten Kaylee to be my partner for the experiment for One. We tried not to look at the participant room too often. If this slideshow had any subversive effects, we didn’t want to be subjected to them and taint the research process, so we mostly kept our heads down. Kaylee used it as an excuse to keep writing her own work.

One was a fairly soft spoken yet excitable woman. For the first half hour, things went completely normal. She sat down in the basically blank room and after ten or so minutes started listing things she saw: a smiley face, an apple, a puppy, a fly, and so on. As time went on, they got increasingly more detailed. Not the descriptions, really, which stuck to one word, but they all got seemingly harder to recognize from basic, abstract shapes. I glanced up when she said something to make sure she hadn’t accidentally rolled the one in a billion trillion odds of seeing something for real.

Every time she saw something, she got a little more excited about it. It was strange, but the energy was kind of infectious. I wasn’t sure exactly what happened to Amanda’s participant, but this one was doing exactly what I’d hypothesized: making abstractions from nothing.

After the five minute break, things really amped up. She started giving more detail about the things she saw more frequently. I was fascinated by the woman. I stared at her for some time as she listed things like green gecko or blue and black rose in awe of how strong the effect seemed to have gotten. I remembered not to stare too long before heading back to my notes, just entering a flow state of pacing around in my thoughts before writing down what she said and how she said it. All until near the end of the trial.

“Two women researching,” she said.

I looked up at her, confused to find nothing but colored static. She had a smile, though, a thin growing one. I looked back down at my paper to keep writing what she saw next.

“Two. Women. Researching,” she said.

I looked up and nearly fell out of my chair. Her face and hands were pressed against the one way mirror. She sunk her body into it as she gave a wide, open mouth smile of awe and wonder. Her eyes looked red and dry like she hadn’t closed them in a while. I looked back at Kaylee who was pale as death before shouting into the speaker.

“Ma’am! Please, sit down until the test has concluded!” I pleaded.

She blinked and jumped a little bit, causing me to again. She looked so confused before going back to her chair and sitting down. She didn’t say anything after that, and her whole demeanor seemed different.

After the trial, we opened the door to which she immediately went into an apology.

“I’m so sorry!” she said. “I don’t know why I even did that! I didn’t think I had moved, but it felt like ‘of course I did’ because I was there, y’know? I hope I didn’t ruin your experiment.”

I can’t say I did know, but we calmed her down and assured her nothing was ruined. In fact, this had been the most exciting development we could’ve had. Our interview was short and simple, consistent with everything we’d observed. She said she couldn’t remember much about the mirror incident but could remember most of the images she saw, even the later ones.

Kaylee seemed less enthusiastic about the results than I was.

“What the fuck was that shit?” she asked as soon as One had left, grabbing me by the shoulders.

“I don’t know!” I said. “But whatever it was, I’ve never seen anything like it!”

“And you’re happy about that?”

“Well, yeah, Dr. Kushner’s gonna love this!”

“That woman just looked through a one way mirror right at us! What the hell did you show her?”

“Just static, and she didn’t actually see us. I’m sure the static just triggered some memory and in some absentminded state, she wandered to where she knew we were.”

“Her eyes were fixed on you!”

“Lucky guess?”

To be honest, it was weird, but weird is what I went into this field for. So, when I showed it to Kushner, I was surprised at how nonchalant he seemed. His normal anger and general distaste for life was gone, though, so I figured I’d done something right. That’s when he made a few calls and got me Two.

Kaylee had noped out of the whole project by now, so I had to find a new friend to rely on keeping me company and double checking my work. Luckily, Diego owed me a favor from that time I did his homework for him so he could hook up with some girl from out of state. Anyway, he showed up a little after I got Two settled in, so Two never got to see or meet him. But, he had, like Kaylee, brought some stuff to do to pass the time.

Two was kind of bored of the whole thing, seemed like he was just in it for money or credit hours or something Kushner had promised him. It took about fifteen minutes before he even said anything.

“Cow or sheep or something?” he said, unsure, maybe out of feeling like he had to say something.

I wrote it down and kept an eye on him every few slides. He didn’t seem to be getting much of the same effect. I couldn’t figure out why. We took a five minute break, and then I asked Diego about it.

“Maybe just remind him to speak every few minutes?" Diego said. “Try to ask questions.”

“I’d like to avoid interfering if at all possible,” I replied.

“Oh, you can poke him a little! It’s just so you’ll have something to write! You wouldn’t wanna give a blank page to Dr. K, would you?”

He was right. If I failed to deliver something, Kushner might not let me figure out what’s going on. So, after the break and a few minutes of silence, I repeated what I’d told him before.

“If you see absolutely anything, please say it aloud.”

“Eh, most of this is just kinda grasping at straws?”

“Still, it’ll be useful to us.”

“Alright, alright.”

He then listed some nonspecific but at least workable stuff. Mainly he said things like “box” or “some lines” or “weird pattern,” and often I’d have to ask him follow-ups. There was one time, though, I didn’t. He said “strange round shapes,” and I failed to ask what he meant. Then, he laughed.

Diego and I both shot a glance towards him. A couple slides later, he laughed again, harder.

“Are you seeing something funny?” I asked nervously.

“No, no, it’s just what you said,” he replied, fighting back a laughing fit.

I couldn’t fight back the urge to ask.

“And what did I say?”

“Oh, you know! The joke you made!”

I fought back the urge to ask what he was talking about. Diego shot me a “wtf” look before I gave him an equally confused shrug. Two kept on his usual routine, but I stopped talking. He didn’t seem to think so, though.

“Straight lines,” Two said before pausing a moment. “Yeah, okay, more detail, I get it. I guess they’re parallel? What do they remind me of? Maybe shooting stars?”

Diego rolled his chair towards me.

“Stop the slideshow,” he whispered firmly though shakily.

“No, it’ll ruin the data,” I said.

“The data’s already fucked; look at him! He’s literally talking to himself!”

“He’s talking to me. I’m just not, uh, talking to him.”

Diego looked at me like I had lost it too before Two caught our attention again, laughing even harder this time.

“See something funny again?” I managed to get out.

“No, no!” Two coughed out, barely getting words through his choking laughter. “It’s just what he said!”

My blood ran cold.

“Who’s he?” I asked.

“The guy next-” he couldn’t finish speaking he was laughing so hard.

Diego looked as pale as Kaylee did. Two fell out of his chair, and I shot up out of mine with Diego. We ran into the hall and into the other room in what had to have been less than ten seconds, but we heard some great commotion from inside.

When we burst through the door, we saw a totally different scene than just a moment ago. The projector had been unplugged, yanked from the ceiling socket with the chair fallen onto its side. We thought we heard laughing from Two balled up in the corner, but it was quiet sobbing. He was rocking back and forth. He looked up at us and through a shaky breath mumbled something.

“What was that?” Diego asked.

“Is it over?” Two managed to get out.

“Yes,” I quickly affirmed. “It’s over. You are free to leave.”

He awkwardly crawled out of the floor and shuffled towards the exit. After he left, I stared at Diego who just stared back at me. We left without saying anything to each other. I think it took a week for Diego to start talking to me again, and when I tried to bring it up, he told me not to.

I brought my notes to Kushner who I expected to call this whole thing off. But, disturbingly, I saw the man smile for the first time. He said he was upset I didn’t interview Two after, but he seemed generally elated by the results and told me I could get access to Three right away.

“But, sir,” I said. “With all due respect, nobody in the department, hell, maybe the school, is willing to partner with me on this anymore! They’ve all started spreading rumors!”

“Nonsense!” he said. “You don’t need a partner anymore! It’s not like they ever do you any good, anyway. Look, just say I’m there with you, and I’ll corroborate that, alright?”

The last word sounded like less of a question and more of a demand, and I was both too frightened and curious to say no. So, when Three came in a couple days later as scheduled, I gave him the normal prep.

Three was much older than the other two, but he had a warmth about him like a friendly father figure with a kind smile and rosy cheeks on his round, plump head. I explained to him what the Babel Image Archive was, but he didn’t seem to fully understand. Still, I showed him to the room and sat in the chair.

It was slow going at first. I had my phone out to pass the time. Kushner wanted the trial time raised to three hours with five minute breaks at each hour. I was skeptical but planned to do as he asked.	

It took me by complete surprise when Three said the first thing he saw.

“Words,” Three said.

“What did they say?” I asked quickly.

“Can’t understand them.”

It sounded like he was in a rush to speak, and I just started to notice that he was more locked in on the screen than before. I sat back and just waited.

“More words,” he said again.

“What did they say?” I tried again.

“Not in English.”

His tone was exasperated, like he had tried and failed to read them over and over. I didn’t know how many words he was seeing or how he even knew whatever shapes he saw were supposed to be words, but the curiosity was eating me alive.

“More words,” Three said through gritted teeth.

“Sir, if you could just describe what the words look-”

“Can it, bitch!”

He seemed genuinely mad, his voice deeper, guttural. Worried, I thought about ending the slideshow, but something about this whole thing drew me like a moth to a flame.

During the first break, he just sat there, upset. I asked if he needed anything, but he didn’t respond, just huffed and puffed. I was too afraid to walk into the room to ask in person. I just slumped in my chair and informed him we were going to start again. He straightened his back and leaned into his seat.

This time, Three said even less, but I could tell he was seeing something. His eyes would get transfixed on the screen every so often, and he was getting evermore agitated at his inability to understand. I didn’t want to look too long, so I tried to force myself into doomscrolling on my phone or anything that would let time pass. I got this horrible feeling in my gut; I didn’t want to be there. I suddenly felt what Kaylee and Diego must have felt.

When I looked back in about half an hour later, Three looked totally consumed by something, his head subtly following his eyes as they scanned the projector screen like they were reading a bulk mass of text. Even when the slide changed, he just kept reading.

“Mom?” he said, almost sad.

He reeled back like he was looking at a horrible image.

“Who,” he struggled to get out. “Who did this to you?”

He jumped out of his chair.

“Who did this to you?!” he screamed so loud I could hear it even through the wall.

I stopped the slideshow, and when I did, his neck snapped towards me.

“Was it you?” he asked.

He threw the chair at the glass with such force I’m shocked it didn’t come in on me. It rattled the whole connecting wall. He sprinted towards the door. I quickly locked it right before the handle started to rattle violently. I felt sick.

I grabbed my phone which I could barely see. I had almost typed in 911 before the screen went black. Dead. I had never felt true terror for my life before that moment, sitting in a dark room with a man whose seen God knows what on the other side of a particle board door. I hadn’t prayed in a long time, but I asked anyone who was willing to listen to let me live this horrible nightmare.

It felt like an hour of waiting, my blood cold and sweat pouring down me. I waited for any sound I could hear, anything at all. But, there was nothing. No heavy breathing, no footsteps, no talking. Boldly, I pressed my ear against the door. No noise. I said something I don’t remember to coax a response. Nothing. I sat there for an eternity debating whether I should get out or just stay the night until someone finds me in the morning. But, then again, I couldn’t. It was a Friday, and nobody would check here until Monday came around. I had no other choice.

Going numb, I grabbed for the handle, staying there for another ten minutes before slowly, carefully, and quietly opening the door. It creaked loud suddenly and almost caused me to have a heart attack. I slammed it back shut before listening again. Nothing. I worked up the courage again to walk back out, peeking left and right. Nobody. With a sigh of mild relief, I started walking towards the exit slowly, ready to bolt if I heard anything out of place.

In my silent tiptoe, nearing the door, I heard something almost drone-like out of the cleaning closet which was strangely ajar. I froze until I could get a better listen. It sounded like crying, open weeping mixed with the sound of something indistinguishable.

I wish I could tell you why I opened that door. Maybe I felt sympathy for the man who’d just seen something hellish. Maybe it was guilt. Maybe it was just the same curiosity that drove me to do this to him in the first place. But, when I got closer, I heard the distinct and obvious sounds of cracking mixed with muffled cries.

Three sat in a small pool of his own blood holding a pair of pliers. His jaw was unhinged like a snake’s, and I watched as he lined the pliers up with a tooth, tightened his grip, and pried slowly at it until it gave loose. He didn’t scream. He just whimpered and cried. Until he saw me.

According to how they found me, I had blunt force trauma to the head. It left a nasty scar and soreness, but I’m just grateful he didn’t kill me. Maybe he should have. Instead, he just left me outside the building laying unconscious on the bench.

Kushner swept this all under the rug. I’m telling you now both as a warning and just so somebody knows what happened to these people. You have no power to bring anyone to justice or anything, but you can prevent it from happening again to yourself. Whatever you do, do not look at the Babel Image Archives.

They never found Three’s body.

They never found Two’s body after he went missing.

But they found One.

Her body was right next to Kaylee’s.
