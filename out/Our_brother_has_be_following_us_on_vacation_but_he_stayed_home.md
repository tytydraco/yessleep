I wouldn’t say my year in school was exhausting, but clearly everyone at home needed a break from their responsibilities. Between dad’s new promotion and my sister’s long list of shitty boyfriends, tension was high. So obviously everyone cheered when mom booked us all a trip to this new massive hotel across the country. It has it all: pool with slides, all-you-can-eat buffet and movie theatre with all the new stuff that comes out. 

There was just one problem. Erik, my older brother who just finished college, is stuck working two jobs to try to leave the house. After his fight with dad a few months ago me and my sister knew it was just a matter of time, but weren’t sure if he’d actually go through with it.  
“I already used up my time off when I was sick last month,” he said. “I really can’t come along this year.”  
“You’ve been working so hard all month long, I’m sure your boss would understand if you asked for more,” replied mom.  
There was a bit of back-and-forth between them but in the end dad sided with Erik, saying it was “good that he’s finally taking responsibilities”, probably in a more bitter tone than he intended.

We started packing up a week from then, our flight leaving on the next Thursday. I felt bad seeing my brother leave for work while we were busy daydreaming at how grand the room was going to be, but I kept telling myself it was best for him to be away from dad for a while. He was actually kind enough to drive us to the airport since it was right before his shift. Just like that, we left him alone at home. As soon as we landed around mid-day the cool sea air made me forget to worry about him, it was so refreshing. We managed to get a room on a high floor, allowing us to see the whole city we were in.

It all started this morning. The moment we woke up we were so tired from going to the beach late last night that we barely noticed him. The usual symphony of groggy “good mornings” ensued, like it had been our habit every morning for the past few days before going down to feed ourselves with as much grease our bodies could endure before slothing all day in the pool or our beds. Except this morning our usual table was missing a chair for one of us. Or not quite, rather there was one more person sitting at the table. Without us noticing or even acknowledging it, Erik had joined us in the room despite there not being a bed for him. We all assumed it was normal for him to have come along with us, and to have lost his card somewhere. It was somewhere along lunch time that someone finally noticed.  
“Lizzie,” asked mom to my sister, “could you hand your card to your brother so he can get his phone in the room? We’ll get you a new one later today.”  
“What, did you forget it in the pool?” I asked jokingly, recalling a time where this exact thing happened a few years back.  
“No, I don’t think the guy at the reception gave me one.”  
“But didn’t he hand us all one personally? So we wouldn’t forget?” Lizzie replied.

While dad rummaged through our bag of swim shorts and towels, I went to get myself more over-salted scrambled eggs, just to get my fill of unhealthy food before going back in the slide, when mom pulled me aside.  
“You shouldn’t follow your brother out to get changed. If you need just use the pool’s changing rooms.”  
“But why? I feel uncomfortable changing around other people and my clothes are all in my bag.”  
I could see the nervousness form on her face as she looked back at the table. “Because your brother stayed him remember? He wasn’t with us on the plane.”  
I realized and turned around just in time to see dad leave with Erik to the reception, probably to get a new keycard for him.

I’ve been trying not to think about it, but it’s been over 20 minutes since they left and mom’s stress is starting to affect me. For now we can only watch Lizzie at the pool, hoping for the best. 

UPDATE: Okay it’s gonna sound like a real conspiracy, but I looked at the reviews on the hotel’s pages and most of the one stars talk about guests refusing to leave their rooms, often acting to happy to be inside. Coincidentally after that I couldn’t find my mom and sister, but I just got a text from dad’s number telling me to join them at the room with a little too much enthusiasm. I tried calling him and mom, but their numbers went straight to voicemail. I’m scared, all I want right now is to get home. I don’t want to go to the room. I just want to leave.
