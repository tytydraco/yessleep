Have you ever dreamt of a place that didn't really exist? a place so detailed and lifelike that it felt like you had been there before?

Every so often when I was younger, I would experience locations from previous dreams months- sometimes years apart. Not that It was the same dream, just the same location, the actual events would differ wildly. And though the locations would stick in my memory, the actual events of the dreams would rapidly fade as I woke up. I was resting a lot around the time, as I had a heart condition and became fatigued easily.

But one dream never faded from my mind no matter how much time passed afterward. A recurring nightmare that stuck in my mind and haunted every shadow and shape in the dark that my child-eyes fell upon. Everytime the nightmare was the same, and everytime I would startle awake to a dim sunless sky and sweat-damped sheets. The fear was so strong it would cause chest pains, which greatly worried my parents.

The first thing I would recall was always floating or swimming through a dim void, feeling totally enveloped as if I was underwater. But it was more than just a pressure, the space around me felt alive. Like I was swallowed inside the mass of a formless being. It was all around me, but felt empty at the same time.

Then the shifting begins, a strong descending current so vast and powerful I'd be sucked downward toward the shifting darkness far beneath me. The light ended abruptly, as if the dark was a heavy gas sitting beneath a layer of air. The emptiness was so vast I couldn't tell if I was falling toward it, or if it was billowing up toward me, but no matter how hard I kicked, I couldn't outrun it.

Before long I would find light again, a crackling wooden torch lying on a moist rock, surrounded by an oppressive darkness. Reaching for it, a new round of sensations flooded in as my surroundings evolved with the newfound light. Stretching in all directions, was a sweltering, claustrophobic cavern deep within the earth. My torchlight glistened off slick grime coating the cave walls, and the air smelled of sulfur and death. Waist high water filled the cavern, its temperature so close to my own body that I hadn't noticed it at first.

Slowly I begin to trudge through the settled muck resting on the slick uneven ground, searching for a way out. In my hand the torch sizzles and pops against the humid air and dripping rocks, the light slowly begins to dim. And I hear a sound, something that sends a shiver down my body despite the heat. A deep chortle that morphed into a high pitched wail like that of an animal call, the sound causing the water to ripple and jump against its powerful reverberation. 

I freeze in fear like prey, as the call rings out again followed by the approaching sound of sloshing water from deeper in the cavern. I crouch instinctually, submerging myself to my neck in the water as I strain my ears trying to catch the direction of the sound. Another chortle like a choir of deep voiced men laughing as one, before it turned into the high whine of a whale like call. The sloshing continues toward me, much closer than before any moment it would be upon me.

The final sound of disturbed water stopped just before the edge of my dying torchlight, the wailing stopped. Against the shifting darkness, the thing begins to lean in, shadows dancing across its obscured form. Its eyes catch the fading torch light in their colorless reflection, painting them as six shiny wet orbs in the dark. Its eyes transfixed on the flame in my hand, I felt frozen in fear, neither of us moving. From my lips came some ancient speech that felt heavy on my tongue and thick with syllables I didn’t know in that order. My voice, not really my own, was laced with unmistakable fear.

Further still the creature leans, revealing two great ram horns as dark and sparkling as obsidian, protruding from its large skull. Its lips curled back like a dog bearing its teeth, but its maw was shaped like a deer with the thickness of a man's structure. Rows of predatory teeth shone with a wet reflection against the dying torchlight, but it made no sound. 

It leans in no further, only leering its head in from the inky blackness beyond the light. Finally the last flicker of flame dies, its orange embers hissing in protest against the heavy putrid air. My gaze transfixed on the smoldering embers, my last lifeline to sight dwindling into an orange haze before my eyes. As the glow finally dies with a weakening sizzle, the sounds of disturbed water continue with a renewed pace toward me. 

A loud whimper rose in my chest as it stopped no more than a foot away, choked sobs escaped my lips, the silence broken by my fear once more before I was interrupted for the final time. 

“Sssssshhhhhhh”

At my worst I’d have this dream multiple times a week, sometimes even several days in a row. But even stranger, the dreams all stopped once I had my final heart surgery. And sometimes I wonder, would I still have had that dream if I was born without a hole in my heart?
