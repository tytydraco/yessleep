I've found a new pastime, watching videos of people that say they can predict the future. I don’t watch these videos because I find what they have to say entertaining. No, I watch it to see if there is anyone that can predict the future, like I can.

Let me take a step back here. I can’t *actually* predict the future; that’s ridiculous. Your guess is as good as mine for how everything truly unfolds. Instead, I can feel the future. That probably doesn’t make much sense either. Let me instead just take you back to my early days.

I first remember feeling the future when I was four. I was dancing around my family’s living room when I suddenly felt a great burning sensation in my hand. I didn’t understand it at the time. However, a few minutes later, and after my dad had said to be careful, I tripped on my sister’s dolls sitting precariously in the living room and fell hands first into the glass of our family's fireplace, which was glowing red with burning wood.

There was this other time too. When I was eight, I was riding my bike down the street. No helmet, of course. This was the 80s, who wore helmets during that time. While I was riding, I felt a sudden sharp pain across my forehead while looking around a corner. I rode through it before wheeling around that aforementioned corner too fast and striking a stop sign that was bent over from someone wrecking into the night before. Hey, at least I got a gnarly scar from that one.

I know what you’re thinking; what a shitty superpower. If the only thing I could predict was my own pain, I’d agree, but I can predict more than that. The best story to encompass what I could predict probably comes from my first week of college. I ended up at a small get-together in a person on my floor’s dorm. This one girl kept eyeing me at the party, and I made eye contact with her, and for the first time in my life, I felt something different down there. I’ll let your mind fill in the rest. God, I didn’t forget about her for a long time… I’m getting off track.

 I realized then, I know eighteen years of life with this power, and I had just realized this, I could feel the future by looking at things. I couldn’t see how I would get there, but I would get a feeling of what is the final outcome for me, physically. Upon realizing that, I knew my life would be easy, and the greatest way to make money for an eighteen-year-old was to wait until I was twenty-one.

Walking down the rows of glistening lights of slot machines, I could look at certain machines and feel the faintest idea of money in my hands. I was salivating thinking about it, and when I sat down at one with the rest of my buddies and started spinning it, I was hooked. Bonus after bonus started piling on. I won about 40k that night before they kicked me out. They couldn’t ever prove what I was doing, but the casino knew it wasn’t fair. Looking back on it, I probably wasn’t the right person for those powers because I don’t ever remember feeling sorry for those people who couldn’t cheat like I could.

I guess it’s karma though that I would get arrested that night. I also learned in that same thought that not all my predictions were avoidable, even with interference by me. After winning so much, I decided to take my friends out for drinks. We, admittedly, might have had too much that night. As we left the bars that night, we turned the corner, and I saw a police officer standing on the corner. I instantly felt the cold metal of handcuffs closing in on my wrists, and I went to turn the other way. I thought I knew that if we continued that way, I was bound to get arrested. Thinking that I was too drunk, my friends tried to stop me from running away; I was too drunk, and in their scuffle of trying to stop me, I fell into an active road that cars were driving through and nearly got run over by a limo. You can get away with a lot in Vegas, but falling drunkenly into an active street is indeed a no-no.

I’ll be honest, and I will admit that I’ve used my powers to better my life a great deal. Knowing with pretty decent accuracy what things I should gravitate to in the short run, has led to a great amount of wealth in my life. Hell, I made 100k the night I was watching the Super Bowl, where Seattle decided to throw the damn ball on the Patriots' 2-yard line. I also didn’t care that I was essentially robbing people of their money. I always told myself that my “feelings” could be wrong, even though they never were.

Maybe my dirty betting habits led me to my future career path of day trading. I told myself that it was more morally fine to do since I would be stealing from people with all the money. I found out that was a lie and that both I and the corporations won while the little guys lost. I stopped caring about those things because in my plight I had set my family up well. My kids would never have to work a day in their life. My wife always got any clothes and vacations she’s ever wanted. However, my not-so-good habits and bad deeds finally caught up to me. It’s hard to describe how amazing it is to be able to feel a cigarette twice just by looking at it. That’s not conducive to a long life though, and it means that doctors now predict I’ll only live three more months.

But here’s the thing that scares me; in course of my lavish life, I forgot that I can’t see what the future holds any more than the white coats can. I know this because I woke up with a feeling today, and that has led me to being on the road since 6 A.M. Where to, I don’t know yet, but I would hug your family a little closer tonight and consider this a final good deed, or warning, because my family has been ringing my line off the hook, but I don’t dare peel my eyes off the road. I don’t know what’s going to happen, but I’m looking for somewhere, anywhere, where my whole body doesn’t feel like the burning sensation I felt in my hand when I was four years old.
