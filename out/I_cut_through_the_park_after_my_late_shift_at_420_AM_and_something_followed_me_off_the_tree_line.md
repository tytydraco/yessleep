I work a closing shift that usually doesn't let out until right around four in the morning. By the time I clock out, lock up, and start heading home, the streets are completely empty. Usually, I walk along the main avenue, which takes about forty minutes, but there's a large public park right between my job and my neighborhood. Cutting straight through the paved trail shaves fifteen minutes off the trip, so I take it almost every night without thinking twice.

Tonight, it had just finished pouring rain. The air was thick and cold, the asphalt path was slick with puddles, and a few of the older park lamps were flickering and buzzing in the dampness. Nobody else was around—not even the usual early-morning joggers or stray cats.

I had my earbuds in, though nothing was playing, just trying to keep my head down and get home before the rain started back up. About halfway through the park, near a dense stretch of oaks by the tree line, I noticed something sitting just off the edge of the grass.

At first, my brain registered it as a massive, overstuffed red contractor trash bag that someone had dumped illegally. I remember feeling annoyed that somebody would leave garbage out in the park, and I glanced over as I walked past. Even in the dim orange glow of the nearest streetlight, though, it didn't look quite right. The surface wasn't smooth plastic. It looked glossy, uneven, and wet, almost like raw meat or a heavy tarp soaked in something thick. A faint, metallic smell hit the back of my throat—like wet copper and spoiled milk.

I didn't stop to investigate. I just kept my eyes on the path ahead and picked up my pace.

I made it maybe ten or fifteen paces past the trees when I heard a sound behind me.

It wasn't footsteps. It was a heavy, rhythmic, wet clicking noise—like dozens of teeth snapping together or wet bones shifting inside a sack. And it was moving fast across the grass, closing the distance between us.

I spun around, fumbling to pull my phone out of my jacket pocket so I could turn on the flashlight. Before I could even get the screen unlocked, the ambient glow from the park lights caught it hovering just a few feet behind me.

It wasn't a trash bag. It was floating a couple of feet off the wet ground, pulsing slowly like a mass of torn red muscle and sinew. Right in the center of the mass was a pitch-black hollow lined with crooked rows of bright white teeth, clicking together in the dark.

I didn't stick around to see what it would do next. I turned and bolted down the trail, my sneakers losing traction on the slick pavement twice before I managed to catch my balance. The wet clicking followed right at my heels until I burst out past the park gates onto the main road, where the traffic lights and streetlamps were brighter.

I didn't stop sprinting until I reached my front porch. Now I'm sitting on my living room floor with every door deadbolted, the kitchen knives on the coffee table, and the blinds pulled completely shut. My heart is still hammering against my ribs, and I swear I can hear a faint, wet clicking coming from somewhere outside my back window.

Has anyone else ever seen or heard of something like this? Because I don't think I lost it in the park.
