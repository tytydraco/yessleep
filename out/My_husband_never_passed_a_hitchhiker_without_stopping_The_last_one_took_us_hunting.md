According to the police, a stranger killed my husband. They’re only half right.

The smell of wood and rubbing alcohol hung in the air. I tried to move my feet, but they felt stuck to the ground. At least I could move my hands. I rubbed my eyes. The bare autumn forest slowly came into view. My head spun. My stomach felt hollow. 

"Morning," a rusty voice said next to me. I slowly turned my head. An old, worn-down man in hunting gear sat beside me with a pistol pointed at my abdomen. He raised his head. The dim sun shone on his face. His soulless dark eyes stared into mine. 

A memory flashed in my mind. A hitchhiker standing by the road. He had the most soulless eyes I had ever seen. Bill had insisted on taking him. I didn't want to, but Bill would never leave someone stranded. We drove for a few miles. He barely spoke. Then something tightened around my neck. Bill was screaming my name. Darkness.

"You…you are."

"Yes." His voice was calm.

"Please don't hurt me."

"I won't. You just gotta do something for me." He nodded at the rifle propped on the stand’s railing. I looked down the barrel. My vision was slowly coming back. Something sat in the distance, attached to a pole.

"What is it?"

"Give your eyes a minute. It takes time after you wake up."

I looked down at my feet. They were shackled to the hunting stand's bench.

"Do I have to shoot something?"

He nodded.

The forest was quiet. The air was cold. I had never shot an animal before, and I didn't want to, but if it was what I had to do, I would. I looked back at him. He flashed an awful smile. This definitely wouldn't be so simple.

"Try looking again."

I rubbed my eyes and looked over the sights. The field slowly sharpened. A man was chained to the pole by his neck, with his arms tied. He wore only a deer mask and underwear. Something shone on his left hand. A ring. Bill's wedding ring.

I let out a loud gasp. The man tapped my foot. His eyebrows were furrowed.

"You'll scare the game."

"Hannah?" Bill's voice echoed through the field. "Are you okay, Hannah?"

The man shook his head.

"What's going on, Hannah?" Bill called again.

"It's okay. Answer him."

I gripped my dirty dress tightly.

"He…he wants me to shoot you."

Bill flinched back as the chain snapped taut.

"Can't you, you just let us go?" Bill stuttered. He’d only ever done that once before, when he first met my parents.

"Well, one of you is gonna have to shoot the other. I thought you'd be the man and let her shoot you, but you can switch places."

Tears rolled down my cheeks.

"I'll switch with Bill," I whispered.

The man flashed a big smile.

"Bill! She wants to switch places with ya."

"No, Hannah, no!"

"But Bill..."

"No, don't, don't even think about it."

"So which one is it, folks?"

"She, she, she will shoot me. Hannah, you have to."

I wanted to call out to Bill, but my breath caught. 

The man leaned closer. I instantly moved away.

"Listen. Take your time. Breathe. Aim for the heart. It will be quick. He won't even notice."

"Just, just, do it, Hannah."

"Bill," I whispered.

"I'll do it with ya."

He put the pistol down and grabbed my hands tightly. His hands felt rough and dirty. I tried to pull my hands away, but his grip was too strong. He put my finger on the trigger and pressed the butt of the gun into my shoulder.

"Look where his heart is."

I shook my head.

"It's gonna hurt him if you don't."

The whole gun shook. Bill did too. I moved the gun toward Bill's chest.

"Good job. Now shoot."

I held my breath.

"I love you," Bill called.

"Shoot."

A loud bang echoed through the forest. Bill's body hit the ground. The mask tilted sideways. Sunlight fell on the ring, throwing the glint across the field. The man let my hands go. They fell into my lap.

"Good shot."

He worked the bolt. The casing hit the wooden floor. He smiled and patted my back. My whole world spun again.

"You did well. I got a surprise for you. Open that bag."

"Wh…what?"

He pointed at the ground. An old gym bag lay at my feet.

"Don't be shy."

My shaking hand gripped the zipper. I slowly opened the bag. A deer mask stared at me.

"Now it's your turn," he whispered. "I'll give you a head start."

I looked at the pole.

"Go on, put it on."

I sat frozen.

"I'll help ya, darling."

He bent down, the pistol still in his hand. Without thinking, I stood up and took the rifle off the stand.

"Wh…"

He didn't finish. Both of our shots echoed through the forest. His body slumped down. My foot burned. I looked down. Blood poured out of my bare feet. There was a bullet hole, but I was alive.

Across the field, the sun hit Bill’s ring again.

The key from the shackles was in his pocket. It took me hours, but I finally made it to the road. 

The police found his truck a mile from the stand. In the glovebox was a coffee can full of wedding rings. Eleven of them.
