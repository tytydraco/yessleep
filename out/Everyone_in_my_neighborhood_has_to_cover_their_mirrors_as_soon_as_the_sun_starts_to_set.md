As long as I can remember there was only one rule my parents enforced without any hesitation. We had to cover all the mirrors in our house as soon as the sun started to set. 

This wasn’t just my families rule, it was a neighborhood rule. No one knows when the rule first became a rule, but it’s one the whole neighborhood takes seriously. 

10 minutes before sunset everyday an emergency broadcast went out warning everyone to go inside and cover all mirrors in the home. And they meant all mirrors. Bedroom mirrors, bathroom mirrors, if your child has a little dress up vanity that has a mirror, it needs to be covered.

Eventually some families in got rid of their mirrors all together. 

Occasionally you would hear something happen in a house close to you, cries, wailing, screams. And then the next morning you would see a black SUV in front of the house. You would never see anyone go in or out, but eventually the car would drive away slowly. And the house would lay empty for a few months before a new tenant would move in.

My parents ended up joining a welcoming committee. Their one task? Informing any new residents about the mirrors, and trying their hardest to get them to take it seriously. The turnaround in houses in the neighborhood had begun to become a concern. 

Our household procedure was simple. My parents would cover all the mirrors downstairs and I would handle the mirrors upstairs, there were less. My little sister was only two and just learning to crawl, so we would stick her in the playpen while we were covering mirrors. 

As far as I knew, our neighborhood was the only one in town that had this bizarre rule. It would get brought up at school sometimes, the kids from other communities would tease us about it. But the ones who lived in our neighborhood would just sit quietly, an air of solidarity about them. We knew. 

I had no reason to doubt the rule. I believed my parents, and I’d heard the rumors, and the screams. I always hated one part of the emergency broadcast, they always ended it with a phrase that sent shivers down my spine every time. “And remember neighbors, mirrors are doorways. Keep them shut!” 

It was a chilly fall evening, and the sun has started to set earlier than the summer months. I had gotten use to playing outside until well after 7:00pm. That’s why It took me by surprise when I heard the emergency broadcast begun to play over the speakers attached to the street lights. Immediately the whole vibe the neighborhood changed. 

The air seemed to get heavier. Everything went eerily quiet. You couldn’t even hear the birds anymore. Everyone dropped what they were doing and walked stiffly inside. Kids left their bikes in the road, no one bothered to wrap up the hoses they were using, trash bags stayed right where they were dropped. Everything stopped.

My parents were waiting for me at the door as I walked in. They immediately handed me a roll of duct tape and a stack of sheets. We didn’t exchange words, we didn’t need too. 

I walked upstairs and started in the bedroom myself and my sister shared. I always counted the mirrors as soon as I walked into the room. Same as yesterday, two mirrors. One on our dresser, and one on our closet door. Stretching the first sheet out, I began making quick work of the first mirror. I could hear my sister babbling in her playpen downstairs.

I hated covering the mirrors, I always felt like something was watching me from the other side. Waiting for me to mess up, to forget to cover a mirror, or not cover it fully. All it needs is a little sliver. 

I brushed off the feeling and started on the next mirror, I needed to hurry up, it was almost time. I was halfway out the door when I noticed something shiny under the bed. I bent down to pick it up and realize it was a mirror my sister used to play dolls with. I heaved a sigh of relief. This didn’t need a sheet right? I shrugged, walked over to the dresser and put it in a drawer mirror side facing down. Good enough.

I finished with the last mirror just in time. I walked downstairs, and nodded to my parents. After that the evening resulted in whatever crock pot meal my mom had prepared that morning. I wish we were able to order take out. No one in town would deliver to this neighborhood after sunset. 

Not after we found the pizza guys car; gas running, pizza boxes crushed and scattered on the asphalt around the car, all the mirrors shattered, and no pizza guy. The black SUV was there that day, we never found out exactly what happened. 

 Finally it was time for bed, the evenings lasted forever around here. As I crawled under the covers, I couldn’t help but thinking how badly I wanted school to start again. Then I would at least have something to keep me busy and take my mind off those stupid mirrors.

I don’t know long I was asleep for, or what time it was when I was woken up by the sound of my sister fussing in her crib. I groaned as I sat up and bleerily looked towards where I thought she was across the room. 

It was so hard to make out anything in the dark, but it was quiet again, so I assumed she just went back to sleep. With a yawn I rolled back over. She was always fussy, tonight was no different. 

The next thing I knew I woke up with a start drenched in sweat. I don’t know why I woke up, but I had a pit in my stomach, something was wrong. It was too quiet in our bedroom. I couldn’t even hear my sister breathing. 

“Katie” I cried out. Silence. I fumbled around with the sheets and crossed the room to turn on the light. She wasn’t in her crib. 

My heart started beating so loud I could hear it in my ears. I tore the room apart, she liked to play hide and seek, but she wasn’t there. Then a realization smacked me in the face. The mirror in the dresser. 

I tore the drawer open and was relieved to see it was still face down in the exact same place I had left it. The relief was momentary as I heard movement downstairs. 

I screamed for my mom and dad as I ran down the stairs. I was trying so hard to be quiet, to see if I could head her crawling across the floor boards. “Katieeeee” I called out. Nothing. “Katie do you want to place hide and seek?” 

I heard a small giggle, and heard her say “come find me.” 

I ran faster than I ever could across the house, my parents not far behind. I threw open the door to the study just in time to see my baby sister, smile at me, wave, and crawl through the uncovered portion of the floor length mirror next to my dad’s desk. 

My mother screamed, and my dad ran over to the mirror. It was solid again. 

The black SUV came the next morning. My parents sent me to my room while they were talking. 

We never saw my sister again. But every now and then I swear I hear her giggle at night, asking me to come find her.
