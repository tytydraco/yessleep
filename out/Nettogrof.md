I figured I just needed to get some rest and decided to take a quick nap. Mom or Dad would wake me up when dinner was ready. I just had to wash that bloody towel I threw in the shower first, then I would drop myself on my bed. 

I took my empty glass and set it on the kitchen counter before moving back upstairs again. As I walked back up, I was more aware of how many little objects were moved or replaced throughout our house.

I rushed into our bathroom again. I looked in the corner where I threw the towel, but couldn’t find it. I checked the entire bathroom floor and laundry baskets, but no towel.

Maybe Mom had cleaned it already? I’m sure Mara wouldn’t have done it.  And thinking of Mom, I don’t remember seeing her since early in the morning. I was pretty sure I would hear her complain about leaving the bloody towel later. 

I knew she didn’t like it when we left stuff like that around. But I was dazed and confused. I couldn’t help it.

I then realized how badly I needed to lay down. Maybe check my phone a bit. The thing has been charging on my nightstand the whole day. But its battery is horrible. It’s empty in just a few hours.

I’ve been told that overcharging it weakens the battery, so it’s probably wise to unplug it. I heard stories of phones catching fire because they get too hot from charging. With this anxious thought in mind, I suddenly had an irrelevant urge to get to my phone ASAP. Like the next 30 seconds mattered more than the 6 hours before that.

I darted past Mara’s room, which was still closed. My own bedroom door at the end of the hallway was open. I quickly rushed inside and shut the door behind me, then noticed that my own door lock was gone. I pressed my forehead against the door, closed my eyes and took a deep breath through my nose. I had no time to think further about the lock, because I picked up a strong scent of… paint?

What now? I thought. I wasn’t sure if I could handle another odd thing today, so I just decided to stay in place for about 20 more seconds. My head still pressed against my bedroom door. Eyes still closed. I finally recollected myself and turned around. The first thing I noticed was my Mom. She stood with her back towards me. In the corner where my bed should be. 

Next, I looked around to see if I could find my bed anywhere in the room. That’s when I realized that none of my furniture was in the room.

Instead, there was a single, large cabinet on one side of the room. The rest of the room was filled with paintings. On the walls and stacked against each other on the floor.

I looked back at Mom again. She seemed busy with something. Concentrated enough to not notice me closing the door at least. 

What did they do to my room? It looked like the storage of an art gallery. My parents couldn’t have done this in the time I was knocked out, right?

“Mom?” I asked. My voice sounded raspy. I cleared my throat, then asked again, but louder this time, “Mom?”

But she remained silent. She kept doing what she was doing. I took a small step closer and tried to see what she was doing. She appeared to be very focused.

I could see how she was moving a paint brush back and forth. I took another step and could now see the canvas she was working on.

It looked like she was painting a family of four. Upon closer inspection, I realized she was painting our family.

So my Mom had picked up painting again? In my room? There’s no logic in that at all.

I could see her adding strands of hair to one figure in her painting. Clearly it was Mara. 

Painting had been something she did many years ago. And it wouldn’t be weird if she tried to revisit her old hobby. But not if she completely cleared my room to do so. 

She took the brush from the canvas and dipped the tip of the brush onto the palette she was likely holding. At least I think she was. I couldn’t see it from my angle.

She carefully placed the tip of the brush back on the painting. Adding strands of hair to the figure representing me.

There was something off about my portrait though. It somehow looked like me, but also it didn’t. My hair was different. Also the shape of my nose and some facial features were not the same.

Mom suddenly stepped back, almost bumping into me. I couldn’t believe she hadn’t noticed me yet.

She stared at the painting and let out a deep sigh. I’m not sure, but it sounded like it was a sad sigh. While the painting actually looked really good.

I stepped back a bit and stared at all the other paintings in the room. There was no way my Mom found the time to paint all those quality paintings within the last few months. There are years’ worth of paintings in here. Let alone painting them all in one afternoon.

I grew more confused by the second and was also becoming more annoyed by all the strange things happening around me. 

“Mom!” I said. Loud and clear this time. Since I was pretty close behind her, I thought I might actually startle her.

But no. Just like before. No reaction. Not even the slightest movement.

“MOM!” I yelled this time. At this point I didn’t really expect her to react. And she didn’t. Like I was not even in the room with her.

She was still calmly taking in the painting she was working on. She stepped forward and continued working on her painting. While I just stood there. Motionless for a few seconds. Trying to understand what was going on.

I grew more concerned than annoyed at this point. For the fifth time I tried to get her attention. “Mom?! I asked as I stepped forward and grabbed her shoulder.

I tried pulling her back by the shoulder, but I couldn’t seem to get her to move. I gripped my fingers tighter. Her shoulder felt hard as rock. Like I was touching a statue.

With all my might, I pulled. But Mom just kept painting. Dipping the tip of her brush in a yellow-brownish color.

I felt the muscles in her shoulders move. I tried to squeeze, but she still felt like she was made of stone.

What was going on? I didn’t understand how my Mom could not feel my touch. In frustration, I slammed my fist on her shoulder. It didn’t change anything.

It frustrated me even more. Like a toddler, I repeatedly slammed both my fists on her back. “Come on! Do something!” I yelled at her.

Once I realized how stupid I looked, I stopped. I understood how that was a reaction to my helplessness.

I stepped back again and turned to face my open bedroom door.

...Open? No. No, no, no. I closed that door when I walked in here. I even leaned my head against it. It was closed. I was sure of it.

Suddenly a dark, anxious feeling came over me. No. I couldn’t be... Was I... dead? 

I looked down at my hands. I turned them around, somehow expecting myself to be semi-transparent or something. 

My own silly ideas actually calmed me down a bit. That’s just dumb, Eliza!, I thought.

But still… My best explanation of the weird things happening to me was that I could truly be dead. But how? And when?

It surely had to do with the Nettogrof thing. That was the exact moment when everything changed. Did that blast… kill me?

That still wouldn’t explain Mara’s behavior when I woke up again. She just seemed to be going on with her day as normal. Just like the rest of my family by the way.

Maybe I actually was out longer than I realized? Not for minutes, or hours. Maybe it was months? Or even years?

In my mind I almost confirmed it. I had died and woke up as a spirit, but a year or so later. 

Mom redecorating my room. Mara back together with Thom. It could also explain why Mara looked slightly different to me. She had just aged a bit.

It would explain at least half of all the weirdness. But I wasn’t convinced this was the truth. I felt too alive to be dead. If that even made sense.
