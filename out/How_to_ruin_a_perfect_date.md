Tonight was hands down the worst date of my life.

The first disaster was that I failed to land a table at Anna’s favorite restaurant, so I had to switch to another place that wasn’t quite as high-end. Anna wouldn’t let it go, picking at the issue through the entire dinner. God, does she have any idea how hard it is to get a Saturday night reservation at Dorothy's? 

By the time she finally cooled down and was willing to have a decent conversation with me, I called her by the wrong name—and that was disaster number two. She had changed her name six months ago; she goes by Ivy now. I swear it just slipped out. Calling her Anna for ten years makes a habit pretty tough to break.

Ivy looked gorgeous tonight. Her skin was glowing, and her beautifully shaped lips kept opening and closing as she went on and on about how stupid and insufferable I was. She said she regretted accepting my confession because she knew Simon had always been in love with her, ever since we were kids messing around together. If she were with Simon, that guy would treat her like a goddess.

I helped her cut up her steak, picked out the shredded carrots from her salad, and moved the glass away before she could knock it over in her heated gestures. Then I said, "Yeah, right. But Simon would never have the guts to confess to you, and your pride would never let you confess to him. Now can we just get back to eating dinner, please? Sweetheart."

Ivy looked at me and took a deep breath. Her expression softened, and she took a bite of salad right from my hand, chewing like a timid herbivore.

"Sorry, Joe. I didn't mean to snap at you. The girls on the cheer squad... they've been putting a lot of pressure on me."

I knew, because Ivy isn't the type to throw fits for no reason. I’ve always known that. She’s been eating very little lately because we have another game coming up against Hyde High soon, and the cheer squad has been practicing almost every single day. To make time for our camping trip next weekend, Ivy had to make up for that day's practice in advance. She’s been living on nothing but eggs and cucumber slices for a solid week.

But this restaurant is famous for its grilled king prawns, so tonight she planned to indulge just a little. Ivy ate only a third of her steak before pushing the plate toward me, waiting for the main event.

"Did you let Simon know?"

"Of course. Couldn't leave him out."

"Honestly, I really wish he'd make some other friends. He's way too quiet. Joe, do you think..."

Just as everything was getting back on track, the third disaster struck. After the grilled prawns were served, the waiter brought us a few pairs of plastic gloves. Ivy shot a quick glance at the waiter, then glared resentfully at me. I knew what she meant: See? This place just isn't high-end enough. If you ordered grilled prawns at Dorothy's, they'd have a dedicated server peel them for you instead of handing you disposable gloves like they're handing out cheap trash.

But I was too busy trying to put the glove on to cater to her mood. You pry the edges apart, blow a puff of air inside, and then shove all five fingers in until they get stuck somewhere inexplicable, forcing you to meticulously check what kind of ghost friction is stopping your hand. Alright, now pull hard—

Snap. It ripped wide open. The momentum sent my hand flying forward, knocking over the wine glass in front of me. Wine spilled everywhere—over the salad, the steak, and the very plate of grilled prawns Ivy had been looking forward to all night. Now it was prawns soaked in red wine.

Having been together for ten years, Ivy and I usually have an unusually high tolerance for each other. Despite her earlier mood, seeing this mess, she simply pulled out a few napkins to wipe down the table, rearranged the plates, and told me not to worry. She said she didn't mind her prawns tasting like wine, but I shouldn't think this got me off the hook for peeling them—she was still starving.

If things had ended right there, it would have been nothing more than a minor hiccup followed by a sweet reconciliation.

Ivy always calls me an idiot, and she's right. If I hadn't struck up a conversation with that man, tonight wouldn't have turned into the absolute worst date of my life.

Holding the torn glove, I tried to spot a waiter in the crowded, noisy dining room to ask for a new pair. But they all seemed busy running food back and forth from the kitchen. This place really didn't compare to Dorothy's. That was when I noticed a man sitting at the table next to us. Though not dressed formally, he was very neatly put together, wearing metal-framed glasses, his hair combed without a single strand out of place. He looked to be in his nineties or fifties—around forty, I'd say—exuding an elegant air that felt entirely out of place in this somewhat chaotic restaurant. 

In front of him sat an identical plate of grilled prawns. At that moment, he was gracefully slipping his right hand into an impossibly snug glove, preparing to enjoy his meal.

I was honestly just desperate for a quick fix.

"Sir, excuse me, sorry to bother you," I leaned over slightly and said with an embarrassed smile. "Do you happen to have a spare glove? Mine accidentally tore."

Ivy tugged at my sleeve under the table. She never liked interacting with strangers.

He paused what he was doing and slowly turned his head, looking at me—or rather, scanning me—through his metal-framed glasses.

"Oh, certainly."

He shot a brief glance at Ivy before quickly looking back, his voice deep and magnetic.

"It is my honor to assist a gentleman in distress."

He reached into the inner pocket of his jacket, pulled out a cigarette case, and slid out a glove to hand to me.

Yes, just one single glove.

Ivy gave me an disapproving look. According to her logic, any older, single man who appears completely normal on the surface must harbour some bizarre, hidden quirk. But I didn't care at the time. I thanked him and took the glove. The moment it touched my palm, though, I felt something off.

The glove felt impossibly supple, with an astounding amount of elasticity. My right hand slipped inside effortlessly, with almost zero resistance.

It was like a layer of melted liquid, clinging perfectly to every inch of my fingers. It wrapped so tightly that I could clearly see the outline of my fingernails—even the tiny scar on my right index finger from childhood was visible underneath this thin film.

It felt incredible, almost like a second layer of my own skin.

"Good lord..."

I couldn't help but marvel out loud, looking back at him.

"Your glove is amazing! It fits like a dream. Mind telling me what brand this is? Maybe next time I can prepare in advance..."

He cut off a piece of prawn, placed it in his mouth, chewed slowly, wiped his lips with a napkin, and only then did he speak.

"It's custom-made. By myself."

It took my brain a few seconds to process that. "You can custom-make plastic gloves?"

"No, no. Plastic is terrible for the environment; polyethylene takes centuries to decompose in nature. We all have a duty to be eco-friendly, don't we? This isn't plastic. It's human skin."

Ivy's face instantly turned sour. She knit her brows, stomped hard on my foot, and glared at me to shut up and stop talking to this creep. I choked up for a second too, but looking at the subtle, faint smirk on his lips, I figured he was just joking—a classic piece of "guy humor," pretending to be a cold-blooded psycho to see who flinches first. The guys on the team did that all the time. So I pretended not to be phased at all and played right along with his absurd claim.

"Isn't that a bit crude for a manufacturing process? I thought we'd have better options. Personally, I prefer lambskin... um, much softer..."

His smile widened, as if he had just heard a child say something adorably naive.

"How do you think this is made, son? Do you think I'm some low-class serial killer who grabs random people off the streets late at night, drags them into a basement, and peels the skin off their hands with a knife?"

He shook his head, a chilling sense of pride dripping from his tone.

"This is a civilized era; we moved past such barbaric methods long ago. Listen closely, look at the glove on your hand. Soft, thin... virtually translucent, isn't it? It even captures fingerprints perfectly."

My eyes uncontrollably darted down to my right hand. It was true. Under the dim brass light, the film was nearly colorless, but it carried a faint, milky sheen. I could almost make out the whorls of a thumbprint on my right thumb—wait. That wasn't my thumbprint.

It seemed to be the pattern of the glove itself.

I could feel cold sweat slowly oozing from my pores, trickling down my forehead. Unaware of—or indifferent to—my reaction, he leaned in a bit closer and whispered his secret.

"The method is actually quite simple. First, you must find a willing donor. It must be voluntary, because we live in a civilized society and we are civilized people. Then, using a specialized wavelength of simulated UV light, you bake their hands for an extended period. The ultraviolet rays break down the connection between the dermis and epidermis. After a few days, the skin on their hands begins to peel away in large sheets. That's when you have to be extremely delicate, peeling off that intact layer of epidermal film while preserving the complete shape of the palm."

"Finally, you soak it in a special tanning reagent. This locks in the proteins, allowing it to be preserved indefinitely without tearing, all while maintaining the natural elasticity and suppleness of human skin."

He pushed his glasses back up his nose.

"Or do you happen to have a better method, son?"

"This is disgusting!"

Ivy bolted up from her seat, clutching her handbag tightly to her chest. She grabbed my arm, trying to yank me out of my chair.

"Joe, we're leaving. Right now!"

But my mind was an absolute blank. His story had far too many specific details, making me genuinely start to question what the hell was wrapped around my hand. I desperately wanted to peel this film off right then and there. But Ivy was standing right there; I couldn't show weakness.

"That is... quite a fascinating story. Thank you for sharing, sir."

The moment I forced those words out, I regretted it. My voice sounded weak, pathetic, and completely lacking any backbone. I lost.

He finished the last bite of his prawn and unceremoniously peeled off the glove on his right hand. The moment he pulled it off, I swear I heard a wet, tearing sound, like wet cloth being pulled apart. The removed glove hung limp in his grip, resembling a freshly shed snake skin.

He casually tossed the discarded glove onto the corner of my table.

"I have no further use for it. You may keep it."

He adjusted his jacket, gave me a slight nod, and said, "Farewell."

I stared at the crumpled glove on the corner of the table, then down at my glove-wrapped right hand. A wave of overwhelming nausea surged from my stomach, sending chills straight up my spine. Was this real? Was this actual human skin? And if it was real... whose skin was it?

Lost in a spiral of indescribable horror, I completely failed to notice Ivy calling my name over and over again.

She thought I was playing a prank on her— revenge for when she suggested pulling a prank during our upcoming camping trip and I had refused.

"Jonathan, I am so sick of you!"

Her face pale with rage, she slammed her wine-stained napkin right into my face, grabbed her purse, and stormed out of the restaurant without looking back once. In the ten years we had known each other, this was the very first time she had ever walked out on me in the middle of a date. By the time I snapped out of it and tried to text her on my phone, she had already blocked my number.

You have no idea how many humiliating conditions I had to agree to just to get Ivy to forgive me. It was only afterward that I realized what a ridiculously low-tier scam I had fallen for. That guy had used a creepy, elaborate ghost story to paralyze me with fear, taking advantage of my momentary shock to pull a hit-and-run on his bill.

Seeing how intensely we were chatting, the waiter naturally assumed we were together and tacked that guy's expensive grilled prawns and vintage wine straight onto my tab. I ended up having to pay for two meals—plus two tips.

As for the glove he left behind? I looked it up online later and found the exact same thing: basic PVC medical gloves, a huge box of them for barely ten bucks.

Ivy was right. I really am an idiot—getting scared out of my wits by a disposable glove and ruining a date with my girlfriend in the process.

So go ahead, laugh all you want. But if I can leave you with one piece of advice: never, ever talk to strangers on a date, especially when your girlfriend clearly doesn't want you to. Listening to her will always save you a world of trouble.
