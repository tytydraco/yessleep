>!CW: Brief mention of animal abuse and child abuse.!<



I’ve been trying to make sense of everything that happened in my childhood home for nearly twenty years. I’m posting here in case anyone else has experienced something similar and before I decide what to do next.



I guess I’ll start from the beginning-ish. 



My parents lived in a rough part of the city when they found out they were pregnant with my sister. Rough to the point their dog was shot point-blank for being a German Shepherd. So, naturally, they moved to the country. 



It was a modest little farmhouse on almost an acre. Corn fields across the street to the south and rolling hills to the north. The house was a dusty blue, but my parents, being the Hendrix fans that they were, wanted to paint it red. If I had to pinpoint the moment strange things started happening, that would be it.



My dad made the mistake of telling my sister and me about a bizarre encounter he had shortly after they moved in. 



He was actively painting the front of the house when the crunch of tires over the gravel driveway caught his attention. It was an old car, but in pristine condition, a rarity for the Northeast. The engine cut off, and out stepped a man even older than the car. My dad said he looked at least 100 years old. He had a cumbersome oxygen tank that he dragged out after him.



My dad rushed over, asked if he needed help, or if he was lost. The man didn’t answer. He just gawked at the house with a massive grin as if he was happy to see new owners there. The man finally looked at my dad deadpan and said “There’s a man buried in the backyard.” 



The old man went on to tell him that the dead guy’s name was John, and he had been quarantined in a shed at the back of the property where he died of tuberculosis. Alone. 



And then. 



The old man was gone. Disappeared seemingly into thin air. Which sent my dad spiraling. 



Was he hallucinating? Was that John’s ghost?



What the fuck just happened?



Okay, so you may be asking, why was that a mistake on my dad’s end? Well, because, being the curious little scoundrels we were, my sister and I made it our summer’s duty to find the grave site and dig up John. 



So that’s what we did. We would map out where the most likely sites for a burial plot would be and planned our digs when we knew our parents would be busy. 



Our first spot was next to the shed. The one where he died. God, I hated that thing. Even now I get the chills thinking about it. It was apparently part of the main house at one point, but the family literally severed it and hauled it to the very edge of the property after John began acting strangely, something they attributed to the TB. 



That first dig lasted probably about a week. We didn’t get very deep, although we had a few heart-racing moments when we’d hit a rock.



Another week meant another dig spot. We spent the better part of the summer trying to find that man, but our plan was foiled when our parents eventually found us covered in dirt, trying to explain away why their lawn looked like a family of moles had taken over.



But I'm pretty sure it was too late. I think we unleashed something out there. It was the beginning of the end. 



Things started relatively calm, for what they were. A door handle turning on its own. Heavy footsteps from upstairs that paced back and forth over the wood floor. A strange shadow that would stretch across the wall. But being 10 and 11, it spooked my sister and me nonetheless.



One night in particular has been burned into my brain far into adulthood. None of it makes sense, but my dad, my sister, and I all remember it, clear as day. It was a stormy night. The thunder rattled the windows in our tiny bedroom that my sister and I shared. She had the top bunk, and I was stuck with the bottom. 



Our parents convinced us it was "just a storm" and that there was nothing to worry about. Plus, we had our nightlight, which was actually a forty-watt bulb in the closet, and the door would stay cracked open. But I hated storms.



Between the violent flashes of lightning, I swear I saw little lights dancing on the ceiling. Like manic fireflies, flitting about every which way. I was just about to pull the covers over my face when I heard my sister whisper, "Do you see that too?" I wanted to cry. I wanted to disappear, but before I could fully process what those lights were, the power went out.



Our nightlight, our safety net, was gone. I ripped my covers off and bolted up the ladder to my sister's bed. We huddled together under the blanket, our bodies trembling. After a bit of back and forth on what to do, we did what any kid would do -- 



Scream. 



And we screamed our little hearts out for our parents, who were just down the hall in their bedroom. But they never answered. They never came. I screamed until my throat burned and my voice cracked, for what felt like hours, but I was just met with silence. 



We finally found the guts to run to their room. We bolted through their door, and my mom jolted. She might have even screamed herself. They were busy lighting candles, completely unbothered. I remember screaming at them, “Why didn’t you come for us? We were yelling for you!”



Our parents looked at us, their faces flooded with confusion, and my mom replied, “We didn’t hear anything.” I felt my body go numb. That house was silent, except for the sporadic thunder and pelting rain. There was no logical reason as to why they couldn’t hear our screams of bloody murder from ten feet away. 



Still, it was the middle of the night, and our parents wanted us back in bed. So, with candles in tow, we caravanned back to our room. When my dad opened the door, a pulsating red light cut through the darkness, blinding us -- it was our digital alarm clock, powered on and flashing. 



It didn’t have a battery. It shouldn’t have been on. But it was. And the numbers flashing turned our blood cold.



6:66. 



It flashed again. And again.



**6:66**



Tormenting us. Taunting us. Relentless.



The next day, everyone was eerily quiet. Maybe trying to figure out what happened the night before. Or trying to forget. My mom slept in, which was a bit unusual for her, but I didn’t blame her, all things considered. When she finally came downstairs, she looked…different. Sick. Except she wasn’t. 



Still, her skin had a pale yellow glow. Lines were forming around her eyes, ones that weren’t there just a day ago, and a silver streak blossomed in her hair. She blamed it on stress from work finally catching up with her. She blamed everything that happened after the clock incident on stress.



But I think that clock incident was the moment something moved into her body.



Things just got stranger, things that didn’t add up. She would lovingly serve dinner that she swore she cooked, but it would be raw, ice-cold, even bloody if it were meat. 



She would stand at the end of the driveway on days there was no school and just stare as dawn broke over the horizon, waiting for the bus to arrive, but it never did.



Once, a glass whipped across the kitchen and shattered against the wall, and my mom screamed, gripping her head as if her own skull was fracturing too.



And every time something unexplainable happened, the following day, she would look worse. 



My mom began saying that the man in the backyard was watching us. She wasn’t scared when she said it, but more matter-of-fact. Then, one day, she stopped saying much at all. When she did, it was as if her voice wasn’t hers, like something had infiltrated her voice box and was pulling the strings. 



My sister and I would watch her pace around the shed for hours. Same path every time. In fact, there was dirt where the grass used to be. At one point she got into a screaming match with John, at least that’s what I tell myself, her arms flailing. I remember being so worried when she came running to the house, blood pouring from her nose after that.



My dad took her to get checked out, and a doctor pulled him aside, explaining the scans didn’t make sense. The findings shouldn’t be possible for someone conscious, let alone alert, but he didn’t explain further. He wouldn’t.



My dad ended up calling a priest to perform an exorcism, but as soon as the priest showed up to the house, he turned a sickly shade of grey. He wouldn’t even step over the threshold. 



My dad’s friend knew a guy who “could help out,” but he barely made it in the driveway before he sped off, so when my mom demanded she move into the shed, my dad agreed. He was at the end of his rope and didn’t know what else to do.



One day, my sister and I were swinging. My mom had officially been living in the shed for a month, which was completely barricaded, except for a sort of serving hole at the front. My dad would bring her food and water, but that’s about all he could do. Because it’s all she would allow. My sister and I were forbidden to go to the shed alone. But on this day, I could feel my mom’s gaze locked on me.



And then I heard her voice, which sounded like death incarnate. She called to me. Her long, spindly fingers stretched through the cracks of the boards, gesturing to me to come over.



How could I ignore her? I slowly walked over to the shed, still cautious, but hopeful. I missed my mom. I was about a foot in front of it when she said, in her sweet voice that was on the verge of breaking, “There you are, my babies.”



“Mommy?” I gasped. My sister yanked my arm to pull me back.



“We don’t know if it’s her,” she said.



“Of course it’s me, silly. I just wanted to see your faces one more time.” My mom almost purred. 



“What does that mean?” I asked, even though I didn’t want to know.



“Why don’t you come closer, and I’ll show you,” my mom said.



And I did. I inched closer. When suddenly, my mom, who’d been hunched over, snapped straight up and yelled, “Turn around! Don’t come any closer!”



But her fingers were already curled around my arm.



Her soft voice trailed off and morphed into that sickly growl. “Should have listened to your mother!” she hissed, and she yanked me so hard my face collided with the shed, and I screamed.



My eyes were squeezed shut when she let go, and I fell backwards into my dad, who had a tiny bottle of holy water in his shaking hands. He pulled me close, and I just sobbed. I wanted to look away at what happened next, but it's as if I was being forced to watch. 



Through the cracks of the shed, my mom’s head bobbed every which way. Her body would contort in ways that the human body shouldn’t be capable of. She would bend, snap, contort, over and over. 



But she never made a sound. 



Only a low drone that seemed to come from underneath the shed was heard.



Then, a sadistic groan mixed with her soft voice escaped her chapped, bloody lips. “You’re not going to hurt them!” And she threw herself into the wall of the shed, collapsing in a heap.



I shrieked. My little legs tried to run to her, but my dad had a vice grip on me. My mom dragged herself to her feet and threw her shoulder into the wall again. And again. 



A sickly pop echoed out of the shed as the blade of her shoulder exploded through her skin, and she was on the ground again, in that same agonizing heap.



All was quiet for a minute, but then she dragged herself to her feet again, and a sticky black tar stretched from the soles. She lunged at the boarded-up entrance and clawed to get out, her nails splintering against the wood. A cacophony of screams and growls escaped the walls of the shed. Animalistic. Unnatural.



My dad couldn’t bear to watch his wife suffer a second more, but as he closed in on the shed, it erupted in flames.



No lighter. No fuel. Nothing. All four walls and roof engulfed.



I still hear her scream some nights, although it’s gotten quieter over the years. 



The moment she died, the house went silent. Truly silent for the first time in years. No footsteps, no lingering shadows. Nothing. And it stayed that way for nearly twenty years.



Then my dad remarried. His new wife and her two kids moved into that house with him about two years ago. 



He texted me the other day.



Said his stepdaughter has been asking “who the lady in the backyard is.”



I still haven’t texted him back. I’m not sure I will.



I honestly just don’t know what to do.
