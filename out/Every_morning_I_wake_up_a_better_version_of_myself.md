

Ok everyone listen used to like waking up early.

I’d make coffee, sit by the window, and enjoy the twenty quiet minutes before the day started. That was my ritual. It felt like the one part of my life that still belonged only to me.

Then the changes started.

The first one was small. I woke up and the dishes were already done. I live alone. I had left a plate and a mug in the sink the night before. I remembered doing it. But in the morning the sink was empty and the dish rack was full. Everything was dry. Everything was in the exact places I would have put them.

I told myself I must have cleaned up half-asleep and forgotten. It happens.

Two days later I woke up to a made bed. I never make the bed. I hate the feeling of tight sheets. But the corners were perfect, military-neat, the way my mother used to do it when I was a kid. There was even a slight crease down the middle of the duvet, something I have never once done in my adult life.

That morning I stood in the doorway of my bedroom for a long time, staring at the bed like it had betrayed me.

Then the notes began.

Not the dramatic kind. Just short, practical ones left on the kitchen counter in my own handwriting.

*Coffee’s already ground. Use the darker roast today.*

*Your blue shirt is cleaner than the gray one. Wear that.*

*You should text Sarah back. She’s been waiting.*

I don’t have a friend named Sarah. I checked my phone. There was a conversation thread with someone named Sarah that went back six weeks. Friendly, warm, slightly flirtatious on her end. I had apparently replied to every message in a tone that sounded like me but a little smoother, a little more confident. The last message from her was from the night before: “Can’t wait to see you tomorrow.”

I had never seen this woman in my life.

I called my sister. She answered on the second ring and immediately said, “Hey, did you and Sarah finally meet up? She seems really good for you.”

I told her I didn’t know any Sarah. There was a long pause.

“Okay… you’ve been talking about her for almost two months. You showed me pictures. You said she has that little scar above her left eyebrow and she makes you laugh more than anyone has in years. Are you feeling okay?”

I hung up.

That night I stayed awake as long as I could. I made a mess on purpose. Left clothes on the floor. Left the bed unmade. Left a half-eaten sandwich on the counter. I sat in the living room with every light on and watched the clock.

I must have dozed off around 3 a.m.

When I opened my eyes it was 6:12. The clothes were folded and put away. The bed was made. The sandwich was gone. There was a new note on the counter.

*You need rest. I cleaned up so you wouldn’t stress. Also, Sarah is coming over at 7. Be nice. She’s nervous.*

I spent the entire day in a kind of cold panic. I deleted the Sarah conversation. It reappeared an hour later with three new messages I had supposedly sent that morning while I was in the shower. The messages were charming. Thoughtful. Better than anything I would have written.

At 7 p.m. the doorbell rang.

She was real. Pretty in a quiet way. Scar above the left eyebrow, exactly as my sister had described. She hugged me like we already knew each other. She brought takeout from a place I “always order from.” She talked about things we had supposedly done together— a long walk in the park last month, a bad movie we’d both hated, the night I walked her home because she was scared of the dark.

I sat there and nodded and smiled and felt like I was watching someone else wear my face.

When she left she kissed me on the cheek and said, “I like this version of you better when you’re not so tired. Get some sleep, okay?”

I locked the door behind her and stood in the hallway for a long time.

That night I tried again. I stayed up. I drank coffee. I kept the lights on. I even set alarms every twenty minutes.

I still fell asleep.

In the morning the apartment was cleaner than I had left it. There was a new note.

*You’re fighting too hard. It’s starting to show. People notice when you’re not yourself.*

I looked in the mirror. Something about my face was slightly off. Not dramatically. Just… smoother. The tired lines around my eyes were softer. My posture looked better. I looked like a version of myself that had been getting more sleep and making better choices.

I looked like someone people would prefer.

Over the next week it accelerated.

My coworkers started commenting on how much more focused I’d become. My sister told me she was proud of me for “finally getting my life together.” Old friends I hadn’t spoken to in years reached out, referencing conversations I had never had. My bank account had a few hundred dollars more in it than it should have. A project at work that I had been behind on was suddenly finished and well-received. My name was on the email chain as the person who had stayed late to complete it.

I checked the timestamps. The work had been done between 2:14 a.m. and 4:47 a.m. while I was unconscious.

Last night I tried something different. I wrote a note of my own and left it on the counter before I forced myself to sleep.

*Stop. This is my life. Stay out of it.*

This morning the note was gone. In its place was a longer one, still in my handwriting, but neater.

*You’re exhausted. You’re lonely. You’re not doing a very good job of living this life. I’m just helping. The more you fight, the more people will notice something is wrong with you. And eventually they’ll stop preferring the old version. They already almost have.*

I looked around the apartment. Everything was perfect. The kind of perfect that feels like a hotel room staged for a photograph. There was a second coffee mug in the sink, still warm. The blue shirt was laid out on the bed. My phone had a calendar reminder for dinner with Sarah tonight.

I am writing this from the bathroom floor because it’s the only place that still feels like it belongs to me. I can hear the apartment settling the way it does when someone else is moving around in it, even though I am alone.

I am terrified of falling asleep again.

Because every morning I wake up a little less like the person who went to bed, and a little more like the person everyone else already likes better.

And I’m starting to forget which one of us is supposed to be writing this.
