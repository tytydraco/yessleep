“You must kill a dozen raccoons.”

The message was sloppily scrawled in colorful chalk on the sidewalk in front of my apartment building. It was a strange message, but I really didn’t think much of it. I figured it was some dumb inside joke among some kids in the neighborhood and I went on with my day. It rained the next night and washed away the message with it. 

A few days later I was walking to my car to head to work one morning and I noticed a piece of paper folded and left under my windshield wiper. I picked it up and unfolded it. Written in black marker was a now familiar phrase. “You must kill a dozen raccoons.”

I let out a small chuckle. At this point I was convinced someone was messing with me and this was a stupid joke. Afterall, my car was the only car in the parking lot that I could see with a note on it. 

It must’ve been one of my coworkers, I thought. After all, I didn't really know anyone in the city. I had moved there only about a year ago for work and I still hadn’t really made any friends outside of the office. For my old friends it was almost a two hour drive, none of them would've driven that distance just to leave a note on my car and write on the sidewalk days apart. 

But who from the office could it be? I’m not sure anyone from the office knows where I live. I’ve mentioned the neighborhood I live in to a few people.

There was an office party soon after for some old guy who was retiring. At the party I looked around at my suspects.

There’s Susanne. The most beautiful woman I've ever seen. She’s funny, she’s nice, she’s a great cook. She’s engaged to some guy named Jared. She’s a friend and I'm sure I’ve told her at least the neighborhood I live in. 

I walked up to her and smiled. “Cheers.” I said, raising my glass of ginger ale.

“Cheers.” She replied with a smile, raising her red solo cup of box wine. “You’re not drinking tonight?”

“No, no I gotta drive myself home.” I replied with a cool shrug.

“Makes sense. My boyfriend is driving me home, he’s here somewhere.” She said, looking around. 

I nearly threw up in my mouth. “You mean fiancé now right? I heard it from Daryl.”

“Yes!” she yelled back with a smile, quickly extending her hand out to show me the ring. It looked expensive. 

“Congratulations!” I said back with a fake smile. 

Then Jared appeared holding two white claws. “Babe you gotta see this guy, he’s doing this like, talking dog themed stand-up routine, it’s hilarious.” he said as he put an arm around her. “Oh hey Logan.” he said to me with a genuine smile on his face. 

“It’s Lewis.” I replied back. This guy could never remember my name. 

“Lewis, shit man that’s my bad bro.” he said, “Anyways babe, the talking dog thing, you gotta hear this.” he pulled her away.  
  
“Bye Lewis!” She yelled over her shoulder. I gave her a half hearted wave. I didn’t even get to ask her about the dozen raccoons thing. I’m almost positive it wasn’t her though.

My next suspect was Grampa. He was there when the company was founded. He knew the original owner. Now he was middle management. Dude had to be eighty years old. He was always chatty with me. I'm sure I must’ve let slip where I live to this guy.

And yes, he just went by Grampa. Not grandpa, never grandpa, just Grampa with an ‘m’. I think his real name’s Rodney I think, but nobody called him that. He was just Grampa. 

I saw him leaning over a table of deserts. “Hey Rodney.” I said.

“Don’t call me that kid.” he said in his grumpy old man voice as he lightly smacked me with a paper plate.

“I’m kidding around Grampa. How’ve you been?” I asked, grabbing a chocolate chip cookie. 

He let out a chuckle. “You know me, Lew, I’ve been good, bad, everything in between.”

“Yeah, cool.” I never knew what to say to this guy. “So, what do you think of raccoons?” I asked, trying to be subtle in my approach here. 

“Raccoons? I guess I don’t know. They’re funny lookin’ fellas, raccoons.” he replied, he sounded genuinely confused as to why I was asking.

“Interesting.” I said, and then I walked away before he could get another word out. Grampa may have gotten himself off the suspect list. Next was Daryl, the IT guy. He was always talking about guns. Dude was insufferable. I hoped to god it wasn't him because I’d never sleep again if he knew where I lived.

I saw Daryl chatting up Cindy. That was perfect, I could kill two birds with one stone. Cindy was my only suspect left besides Daryl. Cindy was for sure in love with me. She was always rattling off to me about some nonsense, asking about my life, and trying to get me to go bowling. I hate bowling. Cindy would have to be a really attractive woman to get me to set foot in a bowling alley, and she was not. 

I walked over to the two of them and waited for a casual time to worm my way into their conversation.

“Yeah, and it’s completely illegal in the United States, but I have two.” Said Daryl. I didn’t even want to know what he was talking about.

I let out a fake laugh. The two of them looked at me, Cindy letting out a short and quiet chuckle, Daryl responding with silence. “So, raccoons. What have they been up to lately?” I asked.

“The animal?” Daryl asked, looking puzzled.

“Maybe.” I responded, I didn’t want to reveal too much of my hand.

Cindy’s voice came next, “What have they been up to lately? What kind of question is that?” She said with a giggle. “Ralph has entered his watermelon era.”

Now it was me with a puzzled look on my face. “Ralph?” I asked.

“Yeah Ralph. Come on, I’ve told you all about him.” She said with a slight frown.

Shit. Who the fuck is Ralph? I wondered to myself. “Oh of course, Ralph.” I replied, not wanting to key her in on the fact that I rarely pay attention to what she’s saying.

Her frown quickly became a smile. “He’s just the sweetest little guy.” She said, she then took out her phone and held it up to me showing me a photo. It was a raccoon on a table with it's head buried in a watermelon. Cindy had a pet raccoon.

“He is just the sweetest.” I said back, trying to hide my newfound suspicion. Cindy was now suspect number one. I had to choose my next words carefully. I didn’t want her to get suspicious. “Imagine killing a dozen raccoons. Wouldn’t that be insane?” I asked.

I’m not sure what emotion came across her face, it was somewhere between disgust and sorrow. “What are you talking about?” She asked.

“Nothing, nothing. Never mind. Forget about it. Catch you later.” I said with a cool guy smile. I walked away after throwing up some finger guns in a last ditch effort to safe face. What I asked her was a really, really weird thing to say if she’s not the one behind the messages, and I was starting to think she was no longer a suspect.

In fact, I was starting to think that maybe the message was from nobody at work at all. Everyone I’m close with in the office seemed to have no idea why I was talking about raccoons. After all, maybe the whole thing was a coincidence. Maybe the note was put on the wrong car and this was all meant for someone else. Maybe it was just some poor dude having a schizophrenic episode leaving nonsense around and I overanalyzed it. Maybe it was nothing at all.

The office party started to wrap up early, as our office parties often seemed to do. I did a classic Irish exit. I got into my car and drove myself home. 

I got back to my apartment and parked in my spot. I reached into my pocket to check my phone but it wasn’t there. I flew into a panic right away. If my phone was still at the office my night was gonna be significantly worse. I frantically started searching my car, praying that it was lodged under a seat. Sure enough there it was.

I breathed a sigh of relief and I fished it out from underneath the seat. As soon as I did the screen lit up and I was startled. Apparently for the last 18 minutes, just under the length of my drive, I had been on a phone call with an unknown number. My first instinct was to hang up, however, right before my thumb hit the red button I felt like I faintly heard someone talk on the other end. I raised the phone to my ear and said nothing. 

There was a few moments of silence. I thought about saying something, but I was already kind of freaked out by the whole thing. The silence was broken by a voice on the other line. The voice was crackly like that of an elderly person. “You must kill a dozen raccoons.” 

I quickly pulled the phone from my ear and went to end the call, but whoever this was ended the call before I had the chance. At this point I was so freaked out. I rushed into my apartment building and into my apartment. I quickly locked the door behind me, even locking the little chain latch which I never do. 

I checked my call history hoping I could see the phone number of whoever this was. There wasn’t anything there. There was no 18 minute call with an unknown number. The only thing in my call history from that day was a missed call from the bank.

It didn’t make any sense. I saw the phone call, I saw the timer, I heard the voice, but it wasn’t there. Was I going crazy? Somebody had to be messing with me. 

I slept badly that night. My mind wouldn’t stop racing. This whole thing was starting to get too weird too fast. I woke up the next morning exhausted. It was a Saturday so I could’ve stayed in, but I was feeling too antsy and paranoid. I decided the best thing for me to do was get out of the house and head to my favorite coffee shop. 

I noticed nothing unusual as I left my apartment and headed down to my car. I got in and drove to the coffee shop no problem. Maybe I was just losing my mind and I never really got that phone call. I ordered myself some sort of cinnamon latte and sat down to wait for it. It was fourteen dollars. Fourteen dollars for a coffee. I looked at the receipt and my stomach sank. Printed at the bottom of the receipt was that same familiar message. “You must kill a dozen racoons.”

I walked back up to the cashier, now more angry than scared. Did someone at work have connections at this coffee shop or something? “Hey!” I yelled to get the attention of the workers. All five of them looked at me. They were all very young, none of them older than twenty. “What the hell is this?! You must kill a dozen racoons?! What is that?! Why is that on my receipt?! Are you harassing me?! Is one of you harassing me right now?! What’s going on?!” I may have gone overboard. Everybody in the packed coffee shop went silent and stared. 

None of the workers seemed to know what to say. It was the quietest that coffee shop had ever been. I could feel my face flush red with embarrassment. “I’m sorry.” I mumbled quietly, leaving the receipt on the desk and quickly leaving. I got into my car and started driving back to my apartment. 

I felt completely humiliated. This prank, or whatever this was, had made a fool of me in public. I could never go to my favorite coffee shop again.

While I was lost in thought a raccoon ran out into the road in front of my car. I tried to swerve but I was too late. There were two thuds, one when it went under my right front wheel, and another when it went under my right rear wheel. I didn’t have to glance in my rearview mirror to know it was dead.

Just my luck. Of course I'd actually end up killing a raccoon during this stupid thing. God damn it. The only silver lining was that whoever was doing this would have no idea I actually did kill a raccoon. There was nobody around who could've seen me do it. 

I got back to my apartment, parked in my spot, and headed inside. I breathed a sigh of relief as I locked the deadbolt and the chain latch behind me. I stood there in silence just breathing for a few moments to collect myself. 

After calming myself down I tossed two pieces of bread into the toaster. I opened the fridge and grabbed the butter. I got a whiff of a sour sort of smell as I did. I opened the butter and it looked fine. Then I held it up to my nose and gave it two quick sniffs. It smelt kind of off.

I put the lid back on the butter container and looked for an expiration date. When I flipped the container over and looked at the bottom a chill went down my spine and I felt my throat sink into my stomach. Stamped on the bottom of the container was a new message. “You must kill eleven raccoons.”
