Yes, my boyfriend is a vampire.

I know it sounds absurd, especially if you’re reading this on a random Tuesday night, but it’s the truth.

I met him in a town called Hollow Creek. My parents and I moved here six years ago for financial reasons, after my old pops got fired from his job at my middle school for what was probably the stupidest reason imaginable. I won't bother y'all with the details.

Anyway, I almost forgot to introduce myself.

My name is Zoe Green, and I met my boyfriend shortly after graduating high school. I'll be explaining how i met him further down the line.

My parents kicked me out almost immediately after I turned eighteen. Honestly, it was the best thing that ever happened to me.

You'll find out why eventually.

September 15.

I was enjoying a peaceful slumber in my new house, half of which my father had reluctantly helped me pay for, when I was suddenly jolted awake by loud, unpleasant banging on my front door.

"ZOOOOE!"

I groaned and buried my face in my pillow.

"ZOE! GET YOUR LAZY ASS UP!"

I knew exactly who it was.

April and Maddie.

Those two insufferable creatures had apparently decided that my front door needed to suffer for reasons I couldn't possibly understand.

I dragged myself out of bed, still half asleep, and stumbled downstairs.

"COMING!"

I swung the door open to find the two of them standing on my porch with the most irritating grins on their faces.

"Good morning, sleepyhead!" April greeted me.

"Morning, my ass. Do you two have any idea what time it is?"

Maddie glanced down at her phone. "Seven."

I stared at her. "Exactly. Seven in the fucking morning."

April crossed her arms. "We're doing you a favor, Zoe. Making sure you don't end up late on the first day of the new term."

I sighed, rubbing my eyes, and stepped aside to let them in. "Lord have mercy..."

I dragged my lazy ass back upstairs to get ready.

The three of us eventually made our way toward the campus.

Thankfully, it wasn't far from my house, so we decided to walk.

The weather was rather pleasant that morning. A thin layer of fog clung to the street, and cool breezes brushed against our faces, the kind of crisp autumn air that makes you want to stay outside for a while.

Unfortunately, I wasn't going to get much peace.

Maddie suddenly linked her arm with mine. "So, Zoe..."

I already knew that tone. "What?"

She grinned. "How's your little crush doing?"

I immediately rolled my eyes. "Can we not do this right now?"

April chuckled from my other side. "Come on, Zoe. You know we aren't going to let this go."

"I don't have a crush."

Maddie raised an eyebrow. "Really? Then why do you look like you've seen an angel every time Joseph Cole walks past you?"

I felt my cheeks warming up.

April glanced over at me, surprised. "Wait. Is it Joseph?"

I looked away, staring hard at the sidewalk. "Is it really that obvious? Like... ugh, can you blame me?"

Both of them burst into laughter.

Maddie squeezed my arm. "Girl, you look completely starstruck whenever he's around."

I wanted to disappear. Honestly, I probably looked like a peeled banana ready to be munched on whenever that man was nearby, and I wasn't even going to pretend otherwise.

April smiled. "To be fair, he is pretty handsome. And charming, too."

I playfully jabbed her shoulder. "Shut up!"

I sped ahead, leaving the two of them laughing behind me. We were getting close to the campus gates, and I was hoping the conversation would finally die down.

Unfortunately, I wasn't paying attention to where I was going.

I collided straight into something solid. Or rather, someone.

I stumbled backward, my heels catching on the pavement, but before I could hit the ground, a firm hand caught my wrist and steadied me.

"Are you alright?"

A soothing, measured voice reached my ears.

"Ugh! Man, what are you made of? You stupid BRICK—"

I froze. Slowly, I raised my head.

Emerald-green eyes stared back at me.

Joseph.

My face immediately turned bright pink. I wanted the ground to swallow me whole. I could only imagine my friends' faces behind me. The secondhand embarrassment of the moment was probably enough to make their entire week.

Joseph blinked twice before squinting slightly, studying my face. The intensity of his gaze made me feel exposed in... well, more ways than one.

"Are you Zoe Green? The girl who topped the midterm rankings last semester?" he asked, his voice carrying such genuine warmth that it practically melted my heart.

Of course, I was experiencing a different kind of melting. More of a complete cognitive meltdown, actually.

"Ye... yes, that's me."

I couldn't bring myself to say anything more. My brain was far too occupied with processing his face up close.

He smiled. "Well, I'm sorry again for bumping into you like this. Hopefully, we can get properly introduced next time."

I nodded dumbly. "Yeah... next time."

I hadn't even noticed that my other hand had somehow clamped onto his wrist.

He glanced down at my grip, then back up at me. "Do you mind?"

Man, things got awkward fast.

I looked down, gasped, and quickly yanked my hand back, taking a clumsy step away. "Right. Sorry."

He chuckled softly, turned around, and walked away, his black coat flaring slightly in the gentle breeze.

I stood there for a moment, completely frozen, until footsteps rushed up behind me.

Maddie practically threw her arms around my neck. "HE SAID HE LOOKS FORWARD TO SEEING YOU AGAIN!"

"Not now, Maddie... please."

April grabbed Maddie's arm, trying to peel her off me. I just wanted to erase the entire interaction from my memory, though I knew full well my friends were never going to let me live it down.

Hours later, after classes had ended, I was still replaying that encounter on a loop in my head. I couldn't help it. Every time I remembered his smile, my stomach did backflips.

I was walking down the main hallway with April and Maddie when someone called my name from behind.

"Zoe!"

I turned around. Joseph was walking toward me, a stack of textbooks tucked under one arm. My heart practically stopped.

"Hey," he greeted me with a small smile.

"Hi..." I was already struggling to form coherent sentences.

"Zoe, are you free tonight?"

I nearly fainted on the linoleum floor. "Yeah?"

He adjusted the books in his arms. "Would you be willing to help me study HRM?"

Human Resource Management. I knew what he meant, obviously, yet my brain decided the only appropriate response was—

"YES!"

I practically shouted it.

Joseph blinked, looking slightly amused before smiling. "Well, that's great."

Before I could embarrass myself further, April and Maddie rushed over, hooking their arms through mine.

"Zoe, can we borrow you for a second?" Maddie asked.

I looked between them. "What?"

"Just for a second," April insisted.

Joseph chuckled. "Sure. I'll see you later, Zoe."

I nodded, trying to play it cool. "Yeah. See you."

As he turned to walk away, I heard his voice, clear as day, echoing inside my skull.

I'll be coming by at 8 p.m.

I froze. It felt like cold fingers were scratching against the inside of my brain. Looking back on it now, knowing what I know, I actually love that weird, intrusive sensation. Kinky bastard.

At the time, though, I stared at the back of his retreating head in sheer bewilderment, genuinely wondering if I was losing my mind.

I shook my head to clear the static and turned to Maddie and April. "What do you guys want?"

Maddie leaned in, lowering her voice. "Zoe, do you know the old stories about the Chapel Ring? The ones the older folks use to scare us away from going into the woods?"

I pinched the bridge of my nose with an exhausted sigh. "Yes, Maddie. What about it?" I already knew where this was heading.

Maddie glanced at April before turning back to me with a wide, excited grin. She had always been a cryptic little nerd obsessed with local folklore.

"Well, we met three guys who said they'd guide us there safely without anyone noticing."

"Maddie, you can't be serious. Why would we venture into the woods with three complete strangers?" I was genuinely irritated by how reckless some of her ideas could be.

April stepped in before Maddie could argue. "Zoe, we just think it'll be fun. Aren't you at least a little curious to see what the Chapel Ring actually looks like?"

I stared at them, utterly dumbfounded. "Again, what about the three strangers?"

April rolled her eyes. "Don't worry about them. I worked with one of them at Mr. Brown's Bakery last summer. They're good guys."

It took a few minutes of relentless pestering, but eventually, I caved. "FINE! Fine. When?"

Maddie practically jumped in place, grabbing me by the shoulders. "Now, Zoe. They're waiting outside the campus gates."

Of course they were.

I accepted my fate and followed the two of them outside. Sure enough, standing just past the iron gates were three young men I'd never seen before.

"April, good to see you."

One of them, a tall, blonde-haired guy, stepped forward and shook April's hand.

"You too, Alex. I brought my friends, Zoe and Maddie."

Alex nodded politely toward Maddie and me, then gestured back toward his two companions. "The two behind me are Will and Michael."

Michael's eyes landed on me, and a slow grin spread across his face. "The red-haired one is Zoe, right?"

I'd already had my fill of these goobers, and we hadn't even left the pavement yet. "Bingo! Wanna clap for yourself?" I replied dryly.

Maddie and April shot me confused looks.

"What?" I muttered.

Maddie sighed and walked over to Alex. "Sorry about Zoe. She's a little out of it today. Anyway, which way are we going?"

Alex laughed it off. "It's fine. You girls just stay close behind us. We'll lead the way."

The walk into the woods started out surprisingly peaceful. There wasn't even a proper dirt trail, let alone a carved-out path to follow, which explained why we needed a guide. The forest floor was thick with tall, soft grass swaying in the cool breeze, surrounded by towering oak trees whose heavy branches knitted together overhead, blocking out most of the sky.

As we pushed deeper, though, the atmosphere shifted. The birds stopped chirping. The usual rustle of squirrels and woodland critters vanished entirely, leaving behind a heavy, suffocating quiet.

By the time we reached the clearing, twilight was bleeding into darkness.

"There it is, ladies," Alex announced, pointing ahead.

The Chapel Ring.

It got its name from the dozens of wedding bands and silver rings discovered scattered across its grounds over the decades, belonging to hikers and locals who had wandered into the woods and never come back.

Strange, unsolved cases. Over time, the townspeople had woven dark folklore around the clearing. The most famous tale was that of Iudex Caecus.

Old Latin for the Blind Judge.

According to local legend, it was the entity responsible for taking the missing people, leaving only their rings behind as a calling card. Why? The stories never bothered to explain that part.

I stopped at the edge of the clearing to take in the chapel itself. Its weathered wooden exterior had rotted to a near-black shade with age, and even from a distance, I could see the dull glint of a few stray rings resting near the warped steps of the front porch.

Maddie and April wandered off toward the back of the building to inspect the stained-glass windows, with Will and Michael trailing closely behind them.

I stayed near the front, hugging my arms against the sudden chill. I didn't notice I was completely alone with Alex until he spoke right behind me.

"It's beautiful, isn't it?"

I jumped slightly and spun around.

He stood a few paces away holding a lit oil lantern, its weak amber glow barely pushing back the encroaching shadows of the forest.

I crossed my arms and nodded stiffly, trying not to let my discomfort show. "Yeah, I guess. So many stories built around one old building."

Alex nodded slowly. I hated the way his eyes dragged over me, slow and calculating, like he was measuring me up.

After a beat, he shifted his gaze back toward the dark chapel. "Have you heard the story of Iudex Caecus?"

A cold knot formed in my stomach. Something felt deeply wrong.

I took a step back toward the tree line we had emerged from, while Alex casually strolled closer to the chapel steps.

"Alex, where are my friends?" I couldn't quite keep the tremor out of my voice.

From somewhere behind the chapel, a sharp, wet snap echoed through the silent woods, followed by a muffled gasp that was cut off almost instantly.

My breath caught in my throat.

Alex stopped, turned toward me, and tossed the lantern across the clearing. It landed in the tall grass near my feet with a soft thud, the flame flickering wildly inside the glass.

I crouched down with shaking hands to pick it up. When I stood back up, Alex was watching me with quiet amusement, as though I were a cornered animal.

"God... you're very pretty, Zoe," he murmured. "The freckles, the small nose, those blue eyes, that red hair. You really are stunning."

He sighed softly before turning his back to me, facing the darkened porch.

I swung the lantern toward the corner of the chapel where Maddie and April had disappeared, trying to pierce the gloom. "Alex... where are my friends?!"

Alex reached into his jeans pocket and pulled out a heavy, tarnished coin.

"They say the Blind Judge can only be summoned when a grave sin is committed," he said calmly. "And once it appears, the fate of everyone present is decided by a simple coin flip."

He paused, slowly rolling the metal disk across his knuckles.

"Could you imagine, Zoe? Just how terrifying that gamble must be? The creature possesses four arms."

He raised two fingers on each hand, demonstrating, before tucking the coin beneath his thumb.

"Two arms judge through justice, law, and fairness. The other two commit sin. Its very existence contradicts itself."

I backed away further, shivering uncontrollably, not just from the dropping temperature, but from pure, primal dread. "Alex, I'm calling emergency services right now if you don't bring my friends back."

All I could hear was the wind groaning through the branches overhead, casting long, skeletal shadows across the grass.

Something dripped onto my shoulder.

Warm. Thick.

Confused, I raised the lantern and looked up.

I still wish I hadn't. To this day, I wish I had never stepped foot in those woods.

Hanging from the thick limb of an ancient oak tree directly above me were the bodies of my two best friends.

Their headless corpses swayed gently in the night breeze.

My eyes went wide in absolute, paralyzing horror. A raw scream tore out of my throat.

I stumbled backward, my boots tangling in the grass, and hit the ground hard. The lantern rolled beside me, casting erratic beams of light across the clearing.

Alex hadn't moved.

As the lantern's glow swept over the chapel steps, my stomach heaved.

Will and Michael had been butchered, their limbs severed and arranged with sickening symmetry across the wooden porch.

Alex looked down at me, still holding the coin.

"The sin has been paid, Zoe. It's already here. All I need to do now is flip."

He flicked his thumb, sending the coin spinning into the air. He caught it smoothly and slapped it onto the back of his left hand.

I couldn't see which side had landed face up. Even if I could have, I wouldn't have known what it meant.

Alex looked up at me and smiled. His grin stretched far too wide, pulling at the corners of his mouth until his gums showed. It hurt just to look at.

"Hail, Iudex!"

The air behind Alex violently warped, folding inward like torn paper.

A towering, pitch-black silhouette materialized out of the darkness.

It spoke in a voice like grinding tectonic plates. "Sinners shall be punished with sinful hands."

Before Alex could even blink, one of the creature's lower, blood-slicked arms shot forward, clamped onto his raised hand, and violently ripped it clean off at the wrist.

The severed hand, still clutching the coin, dropped lifelessly into the grass. Alex didn't even have time to scream before collapsing backward into the dirt, clutching his spurting stump.

I couldn't move a single muscle. I couldn't even breathe. I was completely petrified.

This thing was real.

I had never truly believed such an abomination could exist outside of campfire stories. Yet there it loomed over me, its eyeless head tilting slightly to one side in grotesque curiosity. A small, round mouth sat in the middle of its otherwise featureless face.

Its upper two arms held a massive set of brass balance scales. Its lower two arms, drenched in fresh crimson, twitched eagerly at its sides.

Then, it began to hum.

The sound never rose or fell, just a flat, suffocating monotone that vibrated in my teeth and slowly chewed away at my sanity.

"Be judged... or be killed," the Blind Judge rasped.

With a flick of its upper fingers, it tossed a second coin toward me. It landed in the grass inches from my knee.

I snatched the lantern with a trembling hand and picked up the coin. A single hot tear rolled down my cheek and splashed onto the metal.

There were two symbols etched into its surface. One side depicted a balanced scale. The other bore a jagged Latin word I couldn't read.

"Flip... the coin."

I jolted violently. Its voice sounded as though it were whispering directly into my ear, raspy and dripping with malice.

I squeezed my eyes shut, took a ragged breath, and flicked the coin into the air.

"Lord... help me."

I didn't wait to see where it landed. The second it left my thumb, I scrambled to my feet and ran.

I tore blindly through the forest, slapping away low-hanging branches and leaping over exposed roots. All I could hear was the wind roaring in my ears and the frantic crunch of grass beneath my boots. The lantern swung wildly in my grip, throwing dizzying streaks of light across the trees.

That's when I heard it coming.

Heavy, thundering footsteps crashed through the underbrush behind me, snapping saplings in half. It was gaining fast.

"Zoe... Zoe... ZOEEE!"

It mocked my name, screaming it in a distorted echo of Alex's voice across the dark woods.

I was sobbing in pure despair as my lungs burned and my legs began to buckle. My toe caught on a hidden root, and I pitched forward, slamming face-first into the dirt. The lantern flew from my grip, skidding several yards ahead into the dark.

Pain flared across my cheekbone, though the sheer flood of adrenaline mostly numbed it.

I rolled onto my back, scrambling backward on my elbows.

Through the trees, a pair of hollow sockets glowed white in the darkness as the monstrosity charged straight toward me, all four arms thrashing wildly.

I couldn't even get my feet under me. I just stared up, frozen, as death barreled down on me.

"SINNER!"

It was ten feet away. Five feet.

Suddenly, a blur of black slammed into its side with the force of a freight train.

A deafening CRACK echoed through the woods as the impact sent the creature flying sideways. It plowed straight through three thick oak trees, splintering their trunks like matchsticks before crashing into the earth. The ground beneath me trembled from the impact.

I sniffled, violently shaking, staring at the dust settling in the moonlight. "W... what?"

Slowly, I pushed myself up onto unsteady legs. Something had just tackled that nightmare through solid wood. I didn't care what it was, I just needed to get out of there.

My head whipped around as I searched frantically for my light. "Where is it...? Come on, where did it go..."

Then, a calm, familiar voice spoke right behind me.

"Looking for this?"

Before I could even turn, a warm golden glow illuminated my shadow across the grass.

I spun around and gasped.

I couldn't believe who was standing in front of me, holding my lantern.

"J-... Joseph?"

He was covered in blood. His black coat was shredded, and his shirt hung in tatters across his chest.
