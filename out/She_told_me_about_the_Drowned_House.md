*Have you ever heard of the drowned house?*

The text lit up my phone. At the top of the screen read *3:01.*

Outside snow fluttered down, sparkling like stars in the night against the street lights. Another text pinged my phone.

*Come on, I know you’re awake.*

I should have pretended I was asleep. She wouldn’t have believed it. 

*What do you want?*

She texted back almost immediately.

*I want to show you something.*

I sigh and roll over in bed. My bedroom was bare, cold as the outdoors. Her purple sweatshirt still sat on the foot of my bed. Some spectre of  a warmer time. Another ping.

*Carter, please. I’m sorry. I miss you.*

I dug my palms into my eyes. I shouldn’t respond. She’ll take the hint.

*Please, I’m outside.*

Snow flurries stuck in my hair and clung to my pajama pants. Julia was out there, standing under the oak tree at the end of my parents drive way.

She was an awkwardly tall, skinny girl, almost at eye level with me. Her long auburn hair was not in a messy bun like normal but braided down her back how I like. She wore a beanie and a long coat that ran almost down to her ankles.

She smiled when she saw me. I blade turned in my gut. 

“Hey you,” she said.

“What do you want?” I mumbled.

“I wanted to see you.”

I raised my arms, “here I am.”

“And I wanted to show you something.”

Our parents were friends; Julia and I practically grew up together. Our folks always gave each other knowing smiles, little elbow nudges. They were so happy when we made it official. I never told them the truth when it ended, maybe I should have. They hate me. I hate me. I shouldn’t, but I do. 

Julia led me across town, trying and failing to make conversation. We stopped near the Scott Bridge, an old truss bridge that spanned the river cutting through town. 

“You really haven’t heard of the Drowned House?” Julia asked, leading me down a small foot trail that took us closer to the water. 

“Mm-mmm.”

“There’s an old urban legend around it, back when our parents were kids. My mom told me about it actually; she said it's haunted, but, if you’re lucky, there’s a ghost that grants wishes.”

“Cool.”

Icy water rushed past us mere inches from our feet. The water level was low, a dry autumn and a summer drought had done numbers on the river. Still, the current was strong enough to whisk someone away if we weren’t careful.

“It’s up here,” she said.

Why was I following her? What was there to gain? Closure? I had closure. I saw the truth with my own eyes.

In the darkness of the starless night a small island appeared in the middle of the river. No, not an island. The lines were too sharp; not the gentle curves of God but the straight cuts of man. It was a house.

“Ta-Da!” Julia said, doing jazz hands as if completing a magic trick.

A few boulders peeked out from beneath the grey water, convenient stepping stones to cross the current. They looked slippery, either from mud or water. Julia led the way, getting halfway across before waving for me to follow her. I did.

In the end, the Drowned House was nothing special. Just an old  single room brick building now covered in river mud. Likely built before the river changed course. The walls were thick and tall, well out of the water.  Julia took a seat on one and patted the space next to her.

“I wonder if we’ll see any ghosts,” she teased.

I didn’t like scary stuff. So why did I follow her?

I sat beside her. She smiled and stared down at the water. God she was beautiful. I confessed my feelings first. She blushed so hard. That day I was the happiest man in the world. I still felt that familiar warmth blossom in my chest looking at her. 

The wind picked up. A high pitch whistle blew through the Drowned House. 

“Ooooh, must be the ghost,” she teased, bumping my shoulder.

I smiled. I wanted to enjoy the moment. Forget what came before. Her hand rested on the wall. Slowly, it inched closer to mine. She reached out, hooking my pinky with hers. It was like being struck with lightning. I recoiled.

“What?” She asked.

“What the hell are you doing?” I asked.

“Nothing. I just thought…”

“What? You thought what? That you could lure me out here and we would–what?--make up?”

She blushed and turned away.

“Oh my god, you did didn’t you?” 

“Please, I miss you.”

I pinched the bridge of my nose and stifled a chuckle. It was the best I could do to not cry,

“Sure doesn’t feel like it,” I muttered.

“I made a bad choice, okay? I fucked up.”

“You didn’t fuck up, you fucked Logan for god’s sake.”

Tears rolled down my cheek. Cold against hot skin. 

I wasn’t supposed to find out. They both said the same thing. One time. Mistake. But seeing it with my own eyes, their bodies tangled together in the back seat of Logan’s Bronco. A deformed spider of human legs and arms and bodies. The shock in her eyes when they slipped from his face to me. 

She’d told me she wanted to wait till marriage.

What a fool I was.

“I told you, I fucked up!” She was crying too now.

“You know my parents’ barely talk to me? They think I broke your heart.”

“I’m sorry.”

“What would they think if I told them the truth?” I would never. But I wanted her to hurt, wanted her to panic.

Her eyes went wide, “No! You can’t!”

“God, I shouldn’t have come out here,” I stood up.

“Wait!” She grabbed my hand. She was so cold, fingers like ice. Another whistle of wind blew across my face.

“Julia, please let go.” I couldn’t look at her

“How can I fix this? Fix us? Please, just tell me!”

Don’t look at her. Don’t look at her.

“Goodbye, Julia.”

“Please, Carter! I wish I could be the monster you see me as! But I am not, I am just a person. A person who—” She started coughing, “A person who still loves–”

Julia started to gag. I turned only to see her clawing at her throat. 

“Julia?”

Her eyes were full of panic. Her hands moved from her throat, to her stomach, and back to her throat. I took a step forward but before I knew it, Julia fell. She had fallen backwards into the empty house. With a THUD she hit the ground and began convulsing in the mud.

“Julia!” 

I fumbled for my phone. There was little service but I still dialed 911. I couldn’t steal my eyes away from her as she seized.

*“911 what is your emergency.”*

Something slowly crawled up her throat. Fingers. Long, skinny fingers pulled their way out of her mouth, pulling her lips so far apart they began to split open.

*“911 what is your emergency?”*

I couldn’t breathe.

She grasped and pulled at her coat. Her stomach started to roll like something pushing and worming around beneath her flesh. Arms burst from her gut. Tearing free from her winter coat. Dripping blood, grasping and clawing at her, the mud, the walls. 

My phone slipped. Tumbling from my hand and splashing in the water below.

Julia wailed, but it was muffled and choked. The fingers grew and multiplied in her mouth. Snapping of bone and popping of muscle. More limbs tore free of her body. Fingers and arms and shoulders and legs and spines. 

Tears spilled down her cheeks. Her eyes, crazed and wild and scared, swirled in her skull. We locked eyes. Wind whistles in that split moment. A silent confession, a conversation not of words but of thought.

The truth struck me. Painful. Raw.

I still loved her.

They never found the Drowned House. Never found Julia. I don't think they'd want to. Not if they knew the truth. The truth I will take to my grave.

I am so sorry, Julia.
