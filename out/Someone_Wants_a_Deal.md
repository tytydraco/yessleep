I lived in a studio apartment in a small college town, surrounded on all sides by woods. I chose the school because I thought I wanted to live in the mountains, and I'd get to hike in the summers and ski in the winters and be happy. I'm now in my seventh year of the four-year program.

See, the problem is I don't have time to hike. I had to get an evening job at an office supply store just to survive, after my parents cut me off. Work until 9, lie awake in bed, startle up for morning classes in the dark. I thought I'd climb mountains. When?

As it stands the primary way I get to engage with these woods, is sitting on the back patio of the apartment complex, book in my lap, gazing longingly at the tree line. Sometimes I'm lucky and I see a Moose or something, just close enough to the edge to be visible.

The first time I saw him it was like seeing an animal. Like how sometimes hidden in the trees you gradually notice what you thought was rocks is a living thing. He came into focus, you might say.

Naked, covered in dirt. His face partially obscured by the leaves. It was hard to make out his expression.

I thought it was another hallucination, to be honest, but I went inside. Just in case.

That was in February.

In April, I think I saw him again. Someone rushed past the window, across from my bed, right as I woke up. No details discernible, except their nudity. How many people are running around these woods naked?

That second time was enough for me to tell Beth.

In the coffeeshop between mind-numbing class and work, she said, "Fuck that, Liz. Fuck that."

"Well, I'm not sure he's real. I'm not sure he's—"

"Liz." Beth was looking very serious now. Finger absently twirling hair. "I know you have hallucinations. When... when you're not getting enough sleep. Do you tell me *all* of them?"

"No..."

"But you're telling me about this." She adjusted. "It sounds... different. I don't know. Are you sure you're safe? Could be some creep."

"I'm safe," I said. Knowing even then it was a lie.

Later in April, I got back "home" very late after my evening shift because an old guy felt the need to yell at me about a printer instead of letting the store close. I opened the door into my room, and a shadow darted off.

I felt the breeze from the open window.

I looked out at the edge of the woods.

For a second, a silhouette.

Third strike; I moved in with Beth.

"I'm so sorry," I remember saying to her. "I don't feel safe. I... I think you were right."

"It's okay," she smiled as best she could. She offered her bed but I said I'd sleep on the floor. The only thing I've used my sleeping bag for in years.

I went all of May without seeing him.

But after the night of the intrusion, I wasn't brave enough to move back into my own apartment. Not for a while. And Beth was accommodating. But I was also worried for her. Because, living with her, seeing how she operated in private, she was almost like an automaton. Is everyone like that in the comfort of home? A sort of empty husk? I wish it was just me.

In June it wouldn't let me into my email account. My password suddenly didn't work. All of the alternate paths failed in one way or another. The labyrinth of customer service took about three weeks, before I could log in again. Three weeks without seeing him, at least.

But, sitting on a rock close to the edge of the trees, laptop in lap, finally logged back in, I saw so many emails. Between my parents and me.

I had made up with them. I was going by Larry again. They were happy, proud of me. Proud that I'd apparently gotten over this whole phase. In the emails, I was really playing into them. I had even sent them pictures. Selfies, from in my apartment.

Him.

A small shred of paper was blowing on the breeze, and swayed and landed gently on the edge of my knees. I reached around the laptop to grab it. Squinted at the scratchy handwriting.

"You can leave your phone. And your laptop. On the rock."

I thought that was the end. But I noticed writing on the back, too.

"You can have the woods now."

A chill.

I was certain he was watching me, I looked everywhere but I couldn't see him.

I got a phone call.

"Hello?" I said as normal as I could.

"Larry! So good to hear your voice, son. Listen, plane just landed, should be at your apartment in...."

I put myself on mute. Put the phone down.

Phone and laptop both I left on that rock. Beth made some kind of pasta tonight.

I told her, "I think I'm good to live alone again. I think... that weirdo gave up."

She looked worried. "Liz? Are you sure, girl? Maybe we should get the police involved. Should have from the start. I've been listening to this podcast...."

I listened to her tell me about some serial killer.

After dinner, I grabbed my bag I never unpacked, and we said our goodbyes. Like when I used to go home after coming over.

On the way out I passed by that rock. Both phone and laptop have been collected. I'm sure Mom and Dad are at Larry's place now, having a tearful reunion with their prodigal son.

These woods are drop-dead gorgeous. The mossy greens. The curling fog.

I know Beth tracks her phone. If I leave it here she'll find it. She and whoever reads this are all who need to know.

Mom, Dad? Your daughter's on her own now.
