I would like to start off this post with a letter to everyone, specifically the Brown family. I’m sorry. I never thought this would have happened; if I had, I would have stopped instantly. I am more than willing to do whatever you ask of me; I’ll do anything to make up for the sins and hardships I have put on your family and on the people who live here now. That being said, I will try to explain as best I can what happened to me and my best friend, Thomas, over the past week.



I never used to believe in Christianity or any religion, for that matter. I thought it was stupid to believe that there’s something out there judging your every move, some being that would send you to hell if you misstepped in its idea of good.



I realize now, of course, that my idea of God was incorrect, and I have begun to go to church, whether it's for my new sense of faith or for the need to feel protected by something holy. This is not a message for anyone to change their beliefs but rather to update you on mine.



Thomas was never a Christian either; he believed in some higher power, but not the Christian conception of it. Now that you understand our beliefs, I hope that this next part is understandable. I would never ask for forgiveness, but I am asking you to try to see where our thinking came from.

I moved up to a sitting position after lying down in the silence of our living room. I said, “Hey, I got an idea for our next business”

Thomas perked up from his side of the couch. “Is it something we can do this time?”



Thomas and I weren’t the smartest people in the world; I’ll admit, though, he was a whole lot smarter than me. I’ve always tried to be an influence, but nothing ever hit 100 likes on a video, and I never got enough attention. I tried a lot for clout, from reaction videos to pranking McDonald’s employees.



I’m self-aware enough to know that pranking fast food employees is the lowest fruit, but all I ever wanted was to be seen. Behind every video, though, was Thomas; we had been friends for about 6 years since freshman year of high school.



When we didn’t have a quick video idea, the two of us would try to come up with a business to sell. He was always thinking about practical things like a toothpaste that you can make at home, while I often followed trends, cryptocurrency or NFT’s (Which was one of the worst ones I’ve done, I’ll admit)



“Yeah, actually, this one's pretty easy, I think, and people would totally buy it.” I felt like the smartest person and the dumbest person in the world- who did not think of this before?



Thomas, still perked up, his back supported by a striped pillow, asked, “Well, what is it”



“Okay, hear me out: you those little bread things that Christians eat”



Thomas seemed intrigued but then gave a name to my thought: “They’re called Eucharist, but yeah”



“Okay, so what if we made a bunch of them and sold them, like, claim that they’re priest-approved or whatever, and say that they help you be closer to God”



Thomas was now sitting, doing what he always did when he was thinking, leaning forward with his fingers intertwined, as if that position were merging two puzzle pieces. 

Two cogs turned against each other as he circled his thumbs around, and his focus was burning a hole in the ground. It took him so long to respond that I just considered going back to my phone until he finally spoke.

“How are we going to make them believe that it’s priest-approved? There’s no way they would even buy it if they thought it was unholy”



I let out an exhale, as if letting out the breath helped my thought, as if breathing out turned some pinwheel inside my brain, which got it going, then it came to me.



“Think about it, though; there’s no way mega churches are good or holy, but a lot of people still go to them. I really don’t think it’s that important that it's holy, as long as they think it’s holy, which is really the only reason you would eat a Eucharist, right?”



Again, Thomas thought, but not as long as before, then looked up and asked, “So should we go to the store now?”



We looked up the recipe, and I was surprised that it was only two ingredients; Thomas wasn’t. I had water, obviously, and it didn’t need to be blessed, so that was covered. Unfortunately, I didn’t have flour; I had brownie mix, but we both realized the body of Christ probably wouldn’t be believable if it tasted like chocolate. I knew that we didn’t need much flour, though, so I offered to go myself, partly because even though Thomas and I did some dumb stuff, he wouldn’t like the fact that I was going to steal. 



Thomas and I weren’t the most well-off people in the world, and I didn’t want to spend tomorrow's breakfast on some powder. I also couldn’t tell Thomas because while we did some pretty dumb stuff, we never did anything illegal, at least; Thomas never did.



When I arrived at the store, I had come with a plastic bag and my pocketknife; I was just going to be in and out. Whether it was worry or the truth, it felt like everyone was watching me, a thousand eyes spectating me, judging me, but eventually everyone had left the baking aisle.

There were a lot of different brands, but I chose one that looked like no one had bought from it, since there wasn’t a single empty space. It had four knights riding a horse with a logo that read "4th seal whole wheat flour." I stabbed the bag and took as much as I could.



Even though no one was in sight, I still felt watched and judged, like my feet were burning, and the idea of money filled my mind. I quickly left, and the ‘being watched’ feeling had left me once I had the flour in my pocket, like no one cared once the deed had already been finished. I arrived home to Thomas working on a video of blue skies and basic text praising our soon-to-be product.

“I got the flour,” I said, heading to the kitchen and putting the bag on the table.



He headed over and looked at me. “Where’d you get this from? I thought you went to the store.”



“Well, I just visited Jen and asked her for some.” There wasn’t exactly a good way to justify the plastic bag.



“For you visited Jen for 35 minutes and didn’t call me over?” He looked skeptical



“Is it so bad? I wanted to stop by and talk for a little bit”



“No, sorry, I just didn’t think you’d be gone so long and not come back with a full bag”



We quickly made a batch of bread disks; I found an old cuff link from my junior homecoming that had a symbol good enough to pass for a sacred rune. After about 30 minutes, we had around 150 Eucharist, and we only had a problem with seven of them that wouldn’t accept the pattern we pressed into them.



One thing about the misshapen ones was that they were drawn together like magnets; it was as if the incorrect ones were grouping together so as not to be the only mistake. The same way humans find friends to fill in the gaps left by our imperfections is why I was such a good friend to Thomas; he completed what I was missing: humanity.



We wetted half a bundle of paper towels and stuck them in a sealed container to create the humidity our new money makers would need. I left the misshapen ones on the table. Thomas told me I could just toss them, but I felt some deep desire to keep them. Having them close made me feel powerful, knowing that I wasn’t being pulled into the bread, that I was being pulled into the bundle of mistakes made.



Such small fleeting moments can have such big impacts. It was late, and while making little bread doesn’t take that much effort, it was tedious, and Thomas and I got tired fast. We went to bed as fast as we made ten small slits in bread; I didn’t even brush my teeth that night. I was excited to see how the Eucharist would turn out, and I dreamt of riches, fame, and beautiful women. If we could get rich off this, I might be able to achieve this dream and become a real influencer.



“Mark, wake the hell up now,” A familiar voice called.



My eyes adjusted to the light, slowly fading in and out, as I saw and felt Thomas shaking me. Thomas was always an early bird and almost always the one who woke me up. His long brown hair was frizzled like a madman, and he wore an expression of mixed anger and confusion; I couldn’t tell which was more prominent.



“Yeah, what’s up?” My still waking voice is faint



“Come to the kitchen, now,” Thomas ordered



I’d never seen him so upset. In fact, I don’t think I’ve ever seen Thomas mad except right now. He was always calm, so I knew we had a problem. Still shirtless and in boxers, I ripped myself from the comfort of my bed and into the kitchen to see only one thing on our kitchen table, a loaf of sourdough bread.



“You woke me up for a piece of bread?”



Thomas ran his fingers under his eyes and squeezed at the root of his nose. “So you don’t know what happened then?”



Confused, I let out a “No?”



Thomas walked over to the fridge, pulled out the container holding the false Eucharist, and opened it. Nothing.



“They’re all gone.”



“Did someone break in?” I thought about our TV, Xbox, and camera



“No, well, yes? They didn’t take anything but the Eucharist, and they just left this bread here”



I grabbed a knife from the drawer. If we got free food, we might as well have some, I thought.



“Well, we can check it out later, but if they didn’t steal anything, then it’s not that much of a problem,” I said



Moving towards the loaf, I noticed it had symbols engraved on it. The symbol looked like it had been branded onto the bread with a hot iron, leaving a burned crust. The symbol had lines ending in arrows that pointed in multiple directions. A circle encapsulated all the lines and a triangle.



Thomas looked me straight in the eyes “Well I’m still worried that some- “



He was cut off by a scream, an ear-splitting scream not belonging to any specific creature, but a muddle of humans, horses, and a thousand creatures all yelled out at the same time.



As I cut into the bread, blood spilled off the top where it met the knife, and the muffled screams continued. The screams were coming from the bread. The small bits of blood then began to trace the markings engraved on the bread.  



Someone buried alive, trying to escape, is the best way to describe how the bread moved. It had millions of bugs inside it, trying to escape, squirming in every which way, its screaming continuing. The origins of where I cut were beginning to curve upward.



Thomas had one ear covered and ran over, trying to put his hand over the cut. Instantly, teeth on each side of the opening of the wound shot up. A geyser of blood, like a crimson fountain, rose from the bread, revealing a long red worm. A tongue wrapped around and squeezed Thomas’s hand.



“MARK, MARK, HELP! IT’S LICKING ME!” The screams that were once coming from the bread were replaced by Thomas’s shouting and grunting like a wild animal caught in a bear trap.



I ran as fast as I could toward him when whatever was licking him let go, and Thomas fell backward on the fridge. A voice not belonging to either of us spoke:

“Delicious, it’s been so since I’ve tasted the material world, thank you, thank you truly for the gift.” The mouth, which pointed towards our kitchen ceiling, belonged to the bread.



“What- what the hell, Mark? What kind of prank is this”

“It’s not me, I promise,” I said in dazed confusion about what I just watched.



“I can assure you, little lamb, that your friend is telling the truth. I am real, and the time has come; the new kingdom has come near. Believe the good news.” Its voice was soft and gentle, like a mother's, comforting.



“The time has come for what?” Thomas asked, I was glad he asked what I couldn’t, I was still in awe



“Well, my dear disciples, the second coming of me, the second giving of miracles and wishes.” The bread spoke like a harp, every word a different tune in an orchestrated piece telling us a story with little words.



Still shocked but too curious now not to ask, I quickly replied, “What do you mean by giving miracles? You don’t mean you’re really like Him… are you?”



“I would ask that you think about what you created and what I have become; you have created the body, and now I am the body. I now am able to prove to those worthy of me, such as yourselves, that I am who I say I am.” It was almost as if I could feel its voice caressing me.



“Wait, are you saying you're Jesus?” Thomas now began to stand up from the fridge



The bread chuckled like a toddler, as if what Thomas said was the funniest thing in the world. “If you wish to refer to me as that, then yes, but you may also call me by my true name, Malitiosus.”



Malitiosus then gave a bright grin, his human teeth a beautiful shade of untainted milk. I was in complete awe, while Thomas looked puzzled; we were both speechless.

“If you still have little faith in me, I am willing to prove my abilities if you so wish”



“Show us,” I said eagerly.



As I finished my sentence, I saw his grin appear again, his teeth so white that they reflected the ceiling light above him into small shards of light around the room. My finger was injected with warmth, gentle but noticeable.

As I inspected the cut the knife gave me, it seemed to stitch itself back together, my skin colliding like two cars head-on. 

A highway of heat went through me as the warmth moved from my fingers and coursed through my veins. The heat showed me that I had lived my whole life enveloped in cold, and now I knew the joy of being in the light.



Thomas ran over, stammering, his head turning left and right like he was about to cross a road, his focus switching from what was called Malitiosus and where my cut was: “H-how did you do that”



“Now you have seen my ability to make miracles happen.”

 He grinned again. “Would you like to hear how I can give you wishes?”



“Wishes? Like actual wishes?” I said, the warmth still pumping through my body



“Let me tell you how I may make your desires reality; firstly, you must know I will need something before each wish.” His tongue tracing his limbs made of dough.



Thomas’s focus was now only on him. “Well, what do you need then?”

“It will vary each day; today, I will need to feast on dough, so you will need more yeast for me to fulfill a wish.” 

Malitiosus paused, allowing us to digest the information. I was surprised by how he knew our yeast situation.

“Secondly, I may make only one wish happen at this current time, as I am still weak, but with each gift you give me, I will be able to give you more,” Malitiosus finished.



“Wait, so you can make any wish come true?” I asked.

“Yes, anything your heart desires, if I am given dough today”



Thomas and I met eyes. I couldn’t tell what he was feeling, but I knew what we both wanted: to see if he could really make wishes come true. Quickly, we headed to the store and went into the baking section, where I had just stolen from the day before. Our goal, from a quick scam to possibly talking to God, switched so fast.

“Thomas, do you realize what we just did?”



“Bring something way beyond what we even know how to explain into our home”



I scoffed, “No, dude, I think we recreated Jesus; you know how Christians say that the Eucharist is his body?”

“You think that we brought the second coming of Christ? You don’t think it was weird that it thought my hand was a snack?” Thomas raised his eyebrows



“Just think about it, the way he talked about ‘his second bringing of gifts’, his calling us his ‘disciples’, and you saw my finger”



“For now, we know nothing, not until we see a wish granted, and personally, I think a bread with a tongue is disgusting regardless of what it can do”



I turned back to the cooking aisle, looking for the flour. The flour bag I had stabbed was still on the rack, so I pushed it further back and grabbed a full one.

“Wait, how much dough do we even need?” Thomas asked, realizing that Malitiosus wasn’t very specific about how much we would need.



I shrugged. “We can always come back if we need more”



Thomas nodded hesitantly, and we walked to check out. I grabbed the 5lb bag of what would make our wishes come true from the back seat of Thomas’s car.

We arrived back at the house and opened the front door to see a dead frog lying in the entryway. I picked it up and threw it out of the house; it seemed to throw up all its little organs as it flew. We didn’t pay that much attention to it as it was the season when they and the snails became a little more active, and maybe it was just sick.           

As we entered the kitchen, we noticed two craters in Malitiosus’ body right above his mouth, where his eyes would be. I threw the bag of flour on the kitchen table and opened it up while Thomas grabbed a mixing bowl.



“So, um, Malitiosus, how much dough do you need?” Thomas struggled to say his name, trying to figure out exactly what to call him, and most likely pushing back from his distaste for the bread of life.



“That bag will suffice. Good work, my disciples; you have the hasty speed of a great horse and the unbreakable will of a lion”



Thomas looked at me, trying not to laugh. I think he thought it was funny that what I believed to be Christ sounded like an old grandpa complimenting us. I stared at Thomas blankly; the warmth returned to my body, and I didn’t appreciate my friend making fun of something I cherished.



We began making the dough as Malitiosus reminded us to add sugar, salt, and baking powder to help him. He also told us stories while we kneaded the dough, stories of his miracles and wishes granted while he gave them out to everyone while he was still in the mortal world.           



His most memorable story was about an old beggar who lost his wife and struggled to provide for his large family of fourteen children. An especially cold winter had struck one year, which covered the land and its hills in a sheet of white. This season brought a deathly cold in which every new morning the beggar would awake with either a daughter or son taken by its grasp. Malitiosus had been on a great expedition to show others his miracle, given that same year. He had roamed far in the snow-filled land when he heard a great sobbing that echoed through the hills.



Thirteen days of winter had passed when Malitiosus heard the sobbing and arrived at its origins. It was then that he saw the beggar and his last remaining child, a daughter.



Malitiosus spoke to the beggar about his miracles and told the man he could grant him riches of unfathomable quality, riches that could purchase a castle whose brick walls would combat the cold. In exchange, though, Malitiosus would need the beggar’s last daughter and child. The man asked why it took Him so long to come and why he must take his only daughter away, to which Malitiosus replied:



“Do not question the path of your savior, but question what I offer your family. You will receive riches and warmth that will protect you from the mortal cold, while I will give your daughter eternal heat in my kingdom so she will never know cold again.”



The girl ran into Malitiosus’ arms as she told her father, “She will see him again in the eternal warmth of the heavens.” The girl rose from the ground and disappeared to Malitiosus’ kingdom in a quick flash of flames. As quickly as his daughter rose, the beggar’s cup exploded in coins, pouring out and multiplying until a hill of gold surrounded the man.



 The beggar was eternally grateful to Malitiosus for his gifts and for the better life he had given his daughter. The beggar quickly used his new riches to build a great castle and kingdom that praised Jesus and the holy, for it was they who gave him the gift of his wealth. The beggar became a king, and his Christian kingdom was loved throughout the land, and when he died, he was brought to heaven for his praise of Christ.



“Did he ever see his daughter again?” Thomas asked



“No, no, he did not,” Malitiosus replied



“Why not?”



“Because the heavens are so large and the distance from his daughter is so great that, no matter how long in his eternal life he searches, he will never be able to find her there”



After a couple more stories, my belief that we would bring the Christian Lord back to life grew, and Thomas and I finished kneading the dough till it was finally a solid mass. Thomas asked me if we wanted to try making Eucharist again, but I told him it wouldn’t be necessary after my wish; he just nodded. Both of our hands holding the beige goopy slime, we walked to Malitiosus.



“So how do you feed on the dough?” I asked Malitiosus, looking around, as if I would notice something obvious

“Simply drop it into my mouth”  



Malitiosus opened his mouth, which now spread across the whole loaf as if he had unhinged his jaw. While his teeth were a perfect white and his tongue a soft scarlet hue, the inside of his mouth was the most beautiful part of him. It was a constant swirl of color and memories, as if just behind his teeth was everything I’ve ever loved and hated. The feeling it brought was an intense euphoria, jumping from memories of my mother hugging me to the time I got bullied, like watching my whole life on a reel-to-reel tape.



“Mark? You good?”



Thomas’ voice snapped me up, and out of new spiritual pleasure and frustration, I said nothing and dropped the dough into Malitiosus’ mouth. There was no thud or swallowing noise. 

I realized the vortex I had just stared into was truly endless, and I wanted my body to be surrounded by its colors. Thomas didn’t seem to see what I saw and simply dropped his share of dough into the now-empty darkness.



Beginning to pulsate, Malitiosus said, “Thank you, my children; you do me well, and now it is time your master returns the favor. Wish whatever you may crave, and I will make it come true”



I looked at Thomas, a silent agreement that I would be the first to make one of our many future wishes: “I want infinite money like the beggar in the story; I want my bank account to be endless”



Malitiosus licked his teeth. “It is done. I will need time, so once you sleep and awaken, my strength will have grown, and your wish will be fulfilled for the rest of the day. I will be dormant”



He then shut the holes which he used as eyes, and He, who was once talking to us, became a loaf of sourdough bread again, with the exception of a small outline where his mouth was.



Thomas glanced at me and the time on his phone. “Well, I guess we play the waiting game, and I have to get to work”



“Why are you going to work? We just wished for infinite money; we’ll never have to work again”



Thomas crossed his arms. “I’m just playing it safe; I’d rather us have some extra money than not have anything”



“Fair point, but I’m going to play video games all day then”



“Just don’t finish the campaign without me”



“It's way more boring with no one to play with, so don’t worry,” I joked.



Thomas gave a thumbs-up and walked out the door. I spent the rest of the day struggling to know what to do. I watched a couple of movies, scrolled on every social media platform I had, and when that got boring, I went on a walk.



The sun blinded me in white and returned all the color to the world as I adjusted to it. Imperfect, that’s the only thing I could think of when I saw the green of the trees and the grey of the sidewalk. Their colors are nowhere comparable to the colors I saw when looking in His mouth the first time.



I walked until I knew Thomas would be back, and I came home just moments before he did. The rest of the night was spent playing the Halo 3 campaign and eating an extra pizza from Thomas’ job. We then went to bed at around ten.



That night my dreams were more vivid than they’d ever been. The colors and warmth were all my subconscious mind could think about, their ever-expanding sights taking me away, and I needed to see more.



I traveled on a small boat through a sea of oil slicks that changed every time you looked at them for too long. The ocean was an infinite lenticular painting that only grew as you searched more. A giant wave came, its mass presenting a timeline of my life. I saw my beginning and end, my birth and my death.



I tried to focus on what was past my death; the image shifted and wrinkled, making it impossible to decipher anything, anything but the flames. 



The flames grew as the wave approached, and I stared deeper into them, each crackling from the fire like a gunshot. Then the wave loomed over me and my small vessel. It was beautiful and created by my king, Malitiosus.
