I’ll admit it. I’ve been on edge since my husband went missing. But I swear, there is something in my attic. And it’s not my fucking imagination, like my parents keep insisting.

It started five days after he went missing. I remember it because it was a Tuesday because that’s when a new episode of our show came out and I refused to watch him without it. I refused. I was sitting on our bed with a tub of ice cream and bawling my eyes out when I heard it.

*Thump.*

From directly over my head.

It sounded small. Like a bat, or a mouse. Too small to be a person. Unless that person was on all fours, frozen still, tapping the ground once with a single finger.

I froze and stared at the ceiling.

*There’s nothing there,* I told myself. *Just the house settling.*

But it happened again.

The very next night. Two thumps in a row. *Thu-thump.* From the same area right over our bed. It sounded heavier, this time. Like someone was knocking their knuckles into the floor.

*Knocking so I’d let them in.*

*Maybe Dad’s right. I’m just scaring myself out of my skin because of Julian being gone…* But to make myself feel better, I pulled up my security camera footage from the last week. That’s what was so confusing about Julian’s disappearance. We put in the effort to make sure the entire 360-degree view around our house was covered by camera, and there wasn’t a single blind spot. And still we missed a spot, and still I woke up one morning to find the bed empty and Julian gone, his car still parked in the driveway. The cameras had shown nothing that morning or the night before—he or whoever took him had been careful to stay in the blind spot on the west side of the house, leading into the forest.

My parents had said in so many words that he’d left of his own volition. But that was ridiculous. Julian would never do that to me. *Never.*

The camera footage showed nothing of note. Just me, coming and going in the driveway as I got groceries and ran errands.

I scrolled back to the footage of me, the night before he went missing. Sitting out on the back porch alone, nursing a whiskey in my right hand and a cigarette in my left. I’d watched those clips probably a hundred times by now. Three of them had tripped the motion sensors, catching me at various points into the wee hours, alone. I’d just found out I’d miscarried and instead of leaning on him, I pulled away like I always did, licking my wounds alone in the darkness with my vices.

But anyway. There was no evidence that anyone other than me had entered the house—except for the blind spot.

If someone knew about the blind spot, and had taken out Julian, and now had their sights set on me…

I bought a lock the next day. Went up on a ladder and drilled it into the pull-down attic door. Clicked the chain and made sure it was secure. Not like a bat or raccoon would have the weight to push the ladder down on their own. But just in case.

That’s when I noticed it.

Two little indentations on the carpet. Exactly where the ladder would go. And a thin film of dust laying on the top of the slate-gray carpet.

*The attic door was opened recently.*

There was no way. I backed away, chewing my lip. *There’s no one up there.* But I couldn’t shake the feeling that there was. I couldn’t stop picturing some strange man, naked and atrophied, on his hands and knees with his ear pressed to the floor listening to my every movement. Coming down in the middle of the night and stalking the hallways.

*Watching me sleep…*

Oh, God. I never locked my bedroom door.

*Fuck, fuck, fuck.*

I stared up at the chain lock I’d just installed. *If someone’s up there… will that really hold him in?* Probably not. Oh, no, no—

*THUMP!*

I jumped a foot in the air as another thump reverberated towards me, from the other end of the house. But it was louder here, right under the door, coming through the cracks. I could even hear a kind of… *wet* squelch following it.

*Oh fuck.*

*Call the police!*

I ran to my phone in my bedroom and dialed 911. As I did, more *thumps* sounded overhead. Louder and louder. *Oh, no, no.*

“Please come fast—there’s someone in my attic!”

By the time I hung up the thumps had stopped. I stood on the bed, the door locked, staring at the ceiling above me. *Please no. Don’t come out. Please.*

The clock on the nightstand read 8:02.

*Come fast. Please.*

My entire body shook as I stared at the ceiling, a foot above my nose.

*THUMP!*

The entire ceiling seemed to shake. I screamed and leapt off the bed, pulling myself under it, into the darkness.

Then a bang like the shot of a gun sounded from downstairs.

*“Police! Open up!”*

Clutching the knife, I slowly reached for the doorknob. I turned the lock and opened the door a crack, peering out. Nothing. The attic door stood untouched, the chain hanging from the ceiling in the darkness. I held my breath as I passed under it.

Nothing.

Relief washed through me as I let the police in. “He’s in the attic,” I breathed, my voice shaking. “Please—please.”

They carefully unlocked the door. Pulled down the stairs. A horrible smell washed over us. One of the officers gave the other a look, then started up the ladder. I heard his creaking steps above us—

“Oh, God—“

“What? What’s up there?” I asked.

No reply.

Something clicked into place. I ran towards the ladder and hoisted myself up. “Ma’am—“ the other officer started, but I was already pulling myself into the attic.

And then I saw him.

Illuminated in the beam of the officer’s flashlight.

Not someone who had broken in.

*Julian.*

Julian’s body. He’d been propped up against some boxes in a seating position, though it looked like he’d just fallen over, his torso limply laying on the floor. His skin was darkened with horrible decay, and pieces of his fingers and flesh littered the area around him.

Little pieces that had dropped to the floor as he decayed.

*Thump, thump, thump.*

And then I remembered. Little wisps of memories. Sitting on the back porch with my whiskey. Getting drunker and drunker. Going in and out as I argued with him, as he talked about his dream of starting a family, and maybe after four miscarriages it just wasn’t going to happen for us.

And that maybe we weren’t meant to be.

More whiskey. More cigs.

Nearly blacking out.

I thought it had been a dream. A really bad one. Asking him to get me something from the attic. Swinging the old lamp on his head. Hurting my hand in the process. Closing the attic door and retreating back to the porch, cigarette in my left hand, now. Staring up at the stars and slipping into a memory-less void.

Blacking out.

Waking up to an empty bed the next day.

Car still in the driveway. No evidence on the camera of him ever leaving.

Because he hadn’t.

At this very moment, the police, the coroner, everyone has left. At this very moment, they don’t have all the evidence in a row to arrest me. But it’s only a matter of time. Days, weeks, it doesn’t matter.

I’m more concerned about the thing in my attic.

You see—the thumps haven’t stopped. No. I hear them all day and night now. Flesh sloughing off a decaying body in the stuffy heat of the attic. Hitting the floor above my bed.

And every time I hear it…

It feels like a knife is slicing through part of my body. Cutting the flesh straight from the bone. My heart seizes, and a part of me goes numb.

It’s been less than a day, and there are ugly red marks all over my body. Deep slashes. The skin is numb, and it’s only a matter of time.

There’s not much of me left.

The red slashes have crawled up my torso, my arms. In a few hours, they’ll be at my neck.

Days or weeks, it doesn’t matter.

I’m sure I’ll be gone by then.
