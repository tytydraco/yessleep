Every few years I learn of another neurodevelopmental disorder that I very most likely have. It all started when I was six or seven when I was diagnosed with ADHD, though back then it was just called ADD. I had trouble with concentration, and my teachers were always getting after me for day dreaming. Due to my parents being poor, they couldn't afford to put me on stimulants. This resulted in a series of C's, D's, and F's all the way from grade school until I dropped out of high school when I turned 18. This of course led me to only being able to work minimum wage jobs, thus continuing the cycle of being poor.

I was also labeled from a young age as being ‘shy’. My parents never had to warn me not to talk to strangers, because that was the last thing I ever wanted to do. When the home phone would ring, my dad would occasionally try to get me to answer, but I always refused. This was always really frustrating for him. To this day, I pretend like I'm not home when someone comes to the door. After a while, the term ‘introvert’ was assigned to me. However, some time in my early twenties, I realized I was in fact an extrovert. Well, ‘that doesn't add up,’ I can hear you say. Some time in my late teens, I saw an ad for a prescription drug for a disorder called social anxiety. The way they described the disorder fit me perfectly to a T. Now how does one get by as a socially anxious extrovert? One doesn't. It's a fucking nightmare. I'm afraid of the one thing I want most out of life, social connection. 

As you might be able to guess, this leads to a lifetime of anxiety and depression. Two more disorders I can add to the list. More drugs I have to get from the doctor for those short periods of time when I have health insurance. In my mid to late thirties I learned that I might actually be autistic, which is where my social anxiety stems from. 

On top of all that, my family has a history of multiple other issues. My father's side has bipolar disorder, my mother's side has OCD, and I think I have a great grand parent who was considered straight up crazy. Now keep in mind, this was way back in the day before the words ‘crazy’ and ‘insane’ were taken out of the medical lexicon. So take that with a grain of salt. I haven't been officially diagnosed with OCD, BD, or Autism, but I show strong symptoms of all three. 

Now for learning disabilities. I strongly suspect I have dyscalculia, since I've always struggled with math, and I have a tendency to switch numbers around in my head. Recently I was reading a story I wrote when I was fourteen, and it looked like it was written by a ten year old. I did some research and found Dysgraphia: Impacts handwriting, spelling, and written expression. Messy writing? Check. Terrible spelling? Double check. Difficulty with getting my thoughts out onto paper? Do I really need a diagnosis? It's always been weird having good hearing while still not being able to understand anything anybody ever says to me. Oh what's that, there's something called Auditory Processing disorder? 

Knowing all that, I think it stands to reason why I would jump on the chance to get an implant that would improve my cognitive abilities. Oh, but I'm poor, how would I ever be able to afford something like that? First of all, that technology doesn't exist, at least it's not being mass produced. It's still in very early trials. The thing about trials is, they pay you. The next part is both a plus side while also being extremely scary. It's possible for this kind of technology to not only fail, but it could make you worse than you were before. It could create phantom pain, it can mess with your nervous system, or might even just kill you. What all this means is, beaucoup bucks. 

Considering all this, you need to keep in mind that there are some things worse than death.
