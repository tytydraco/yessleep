I don't really know where else to put this. I don't have anyone to call. My mom passed when I was a kid, and I'm an only child. The one person who might have understood this is the reason I'm sitting where I am right now.

I'm typing this out while it's fresh because I need a record in case I need it later. Cell signal out here is garbage. It's one bar that keeps flickering out. Bear with me if this posts in pieces or in duplicate.

My dad and I hadn't spoken in about ten years. I want to be upfront about that because I know how these posts usually go with the movie-style reconciliations and deathbed clarity. Not us. We were actually estranged. We didn't have each other's phone numbers.

A cousin I barely talk to messaged me on Facebook two days ago. Dad died. Heart attack. He was found alone in a rental house outside a little town called Corliss, about seven hours east of Seattle. He was sixty-one with no history of heart issues.

I got a call from a local lawyer the next morning since I'm the next of kin. Dad rented the house, but there was a detached workshop on the property that he owned outright due to some weird deed quirk. The bank wanted the workshop cleared out by this weekend so they could process the whole lot together. If I didn't claim the stuff inside, it was going straight to the landfill.

So I drove down yesterday. I checked into a local motel, and this morning I went out to the property with the keys the lawyer mailed me.

I need to explain my dad first, or none of this will make sense.

He was always a strange guy. He wasn't dangerous, just off in ways I didn't understand as a kid and hated as a teenager. He moved us three times before I was twelve. Always to different small towns. He never told my mom or me why.

He had rules that felt totally arbitrary. I wasn't allowed to let anyone take pictures inside our house. Not even a birthday party photo if the wall behind me was in frame. He walked the perimeter of the house every single night before bed, checking the same windows in the exact same order. If you asked him what he was doing, he'd just say he was making sure everything was where it was supposed to be.

If I moved my dresser or rearranged my room without telling him first, he got scared. I want to be specific about that because it took me until today to understand the difference. He didn't look like an annoyed parent. He looked like I had just put myself in immediate danger.

There was another thing I hadn't thought about in years. Every couple of months, he would wake me up in the middle of the night. He'd walk me through the house in the pitch black and make me point out where every piece of furniture was without turning on a light.

He called it a game when I was little. I thought it was fun. By the time I was ten, I understood it wasn't a game. He never smiled during it. He always checked his watch afterward like he was timing something. I never asked him what he was testing.

The last real blowup happened when I was fourteen. A friend from school came over and took a photo of my bedroom for a group project. Just my desk and my bed. They posted it in a shared class folder online. My dad found out somehow.

His face just dropped. He went totally pale. He made me stand in the kitchen while he called the school to get the photo taken down. When I asked him why it mattered so much, he grabbed my shoulders and looked me dead in the eyes. He said, "You don't understand what you just did."

He wouldn't explain it. I remember being more scared of him right then than I had ever been of anything.

For weeks afterward he barely slept. I'd hear him downstairs at two or three in the morning, walking the house over and over. Stopping in doorways like he was listening for something.

About a year later he told my mom he had to leave for our own good. He moved out to a wooded plot of land in rural Idaho and basically vanished off the grid. No internet. No smartphone. Just a P.O. box. I was fifteen. I didn't get an explanation.

The last time I actually heard his voice, I was twenty-one. He called from a number I didn't recognize. I almost didn't pick up. I'm glad I did, and I've hated myself for what I did next for seven years.

He said my name. He sounded so old. He said, "I need to tell you something about our family, about why I had to leave."

I cut him off. I said something cruel about how he didn't get to disappear for six years and then call to unload some excuse on me. I hung up on him.

He never called again. I never gave him my new address after I moved to Seattle. I told myself for years that was just how things go. I don't think that's true anymore.

I'm twenty-eight now. I live in a cheap one-bedroom apartment. I hadn't thought about him in a long time until the lawyer called.

The drive took six hours. Gas stations got further and further apart. Around hour five, I stopped being angry at him for disappearing and just felt sad about it. I told myself I was driving out to do a chore, sign some papers, throw junk in a dumpster, and go home.

I stopped for gas in the last town before the property. The old man behind the counter asked where I was headed. When I told him I was going to the place near the end of Corliss Creek Road, he gave me a weird look.

He said, "That's the guy who built the big steel barn out there, isn't it?"

I said yes. He just nodded and told me to be careful driving after dark out that way. He said the roads weren't lit and the deer came out heavy. I don't think he meant deer.

The dirt road to the property was rutted so badly I had to take it under ten miles an hour. Pine trees pressed in close on both sides. No other houses.

The house itself is a rotting single-wide trailer. I went inside first. I expected to find mail or dishes in the sink. It was completely empty. No furniture except a sleeping bag on the floor and a camp stove. No decorations. No mirrors.

The only weird thing was the door. The cheap metal door of the trailer had three heavy deadbolts installed on the inside. The outside of the door was covered in deep parallel scratches. The paint was entirely stripped off the bottom half of the metal like a large dog had been trying to dig its way in for years.

Behind the trailer in a dirt clearing is the workshop. It's a massive corrugated steel pole barn. It looks almost brand new compared to the trailer. No windows. Just a set of heavy sliding double doors at the front secured with three huge padlocks.

I brought bolt cutters from my trunk. It took me twenty minutes to snap all three locks off. I slid the heavy steel doors open on their tracks.

I thought I'd find old farm equipment or prepper supplies.

I walked in and turned on my phone flashlight. I'm not making this up.

Built inside the center of that empty steel warehouse is my apartment.

It's a freestanding structure built out of drywall and two-by-fours. There is no roof, just exposed wooden beams overhead. But the walls match the exact layout of my apartment in Seattle.

I found an industrial light switch mounted on the steel wall and flipped it. A row of harsh warehouse lights buzzed on. I walked through the front door of the drywall structure. I felt sick.

It's a perfect one-to-one replica of my living space. It actually has all my furniture in it too.

I'm sitting on a cheap dark grey sectional couch right now as I type this. It's the exact couch I have back home. The coffee table in front of me is the same scratched laminate table I bought off Craigslist three years ago.

I walked into the fake kitchen. The cabinets are painted the exact same ugly shade of mustard yellow mine are. The fridge is the same model. I opened the fridge door. It was unplugged and warm inside, but the shelves were stocked with empty cardboard boxes painted to look like the exact brand of milk and eggs I buy. There are three magnets on the door arranged in the exact triangle pattern I keep mine in.

There is an ugly orange coffee mug sitting on the fake kitchen counter. A coworker gave it to me as a joke gift five weeks ago. I never posted a picture of it anywhere. It's sitting on that counter right now. It has the exact same small chip on the handle I put there when I dropped it in my sink last month.

I went into the fake bedroom. My exact bed frame. The same grey comforter. The cheap wooden nightstand. He even hand-painted a yellow water stain on the ceiling right above the bed. It perfectly matches the leak damage from my upstairs neighbor that my landlord refuses to fix.

There are fake windows built into the drywall. Look through them, and you just see the corrugated steel wall of the barn. But he mounted LED panels behind the frames to match the exact amber color of the streetlamps outside my real apartment. He even ran a small fan behind the wall angled to blow a draft along the floor. It mimics the draft in my real living room perfectly.

I opened the hallway closet. There were actual clothes hanging inside. My sizes. They were secondhand versions he must have bought to match what I own. The tags were cut off. They were worn in at the elbows and knees to look lived in.

I stood in that fake living room and thought about the perimeter walks. The photo rule. Walking the dark house to name furniture. All of it started making a horrible kind of sense. Whatever this was, it started before I was born.

My dad had to break into my real apartment more than once to get this. He had to measure every wall, photograph every shelf, and track down the same rugs and books in the same order.

Behind the fake bedroom wall there is a small area my dad used as a workspace. A folding table with a lamp. A filing box.

Taped to the steel wall of the barn were dozens of sketches on graph paper. Hand-drawn floor plans of my apartment redrawn over and over with tiny measurement notes in the margins. In the corner of one sheet, he wrote a note to himself: closer this time. Still not enough weight in the room. Needs more of him in it, not just his furniture.

On the desk was a stack of spiral notebooks. Several of them, dated on the covers in his handwriting going back almost nine years. The most recent one has no end date.

I grabbed them, sat down on the fake couch, and started reading.

For the first notebook, I told myself a story that let me be angry instead of scared. A divorced dad who isolated himself in the woods and developed a sick fixation. I knew how to be angry at that.

That story held up for about two notebooks.

I'm typing out what's in them. I need this documented outside my own head.

March 14th, 2017

Found his new address through the county assessor records. Listed under the lease. Drove by twice this week, didn't get out of the car. Apartment is on the second floor, corner unit, two windows facing the parking lot.

April 2nd, 2017

Bought a pair of binoculars. Feel sick about this. Told myself I'd only watch long enough to know he's safe and then stop. I am already lying to myself about that.

April 19th, 2017

He has a routine. Home by six most weeknights. Blinds open until he showers. I'm writing all of this down and hating myself for it. I don't know how else to keep him safe if I don't know where he is.

Reading that made my stomach turn. My father watching me from a parked car with binoculars for over a year. I actually asked out loud, alone in this shed, what was wrong with him.

But the entries keep going. Somewhere in the third one, dated almost two years in, the tone changes.

August 6th, 2018

Saw something in the reflection of his window last night that wasn't him. I was three hundred feet away with binoculars, and I still felt it look back at me. I haven't slept.

August 9th, 2018

I need to stop telling myself this is about being a bad father making up for lost time. This isn't about me anymore. I think it found him. I think it's been circling for a while and I only just noticed.

August 22nd, 2018

I called my own father's old lawyer today. Asked him if he still had the copy of the letter. He did. He mailed it to the P.O. Box. I'm re-reading it for the first time in thirty years. I wish I had listened harder when I was young enough for it to matter.

I stopped reading. Nobody ever told me my grandfather disappeared. My mom always told me he died before I was born.

The next few pages weren't dated entries. My dad transcribed the entire letter his father sent him.

To my son.

I am writing this because I cannot stay in this house another night, and I am not brave enough to tell you this to your face. I am leaving. If I stay, it will learn the shape of you next.

There is a sickness in our bloodline. It is not a disease of the body. It took my uncle. It took my brother. Now it is taking me.

It does not hunt a person. It does not track your scent, and it does not look for your face. It has no eyes. It hunts the shape a life leaves behind.

The pattern is the same every time. A man in this family reaches an age where he has built a life he actually feels settled in. A real home. Not just a place he's staying. Once a place has been slept in and lived in enough that it holds the shape of him, the thing starts coming for it.

It finds people the way water finds the lowest point in a room. It seeks out the exact geometry of the space you've made your own. Where your feet have worn the floor. Where your body has worn the couch. Where the air moves differently because you've been breathing it for years.

It is a lock forming around your home. Once that lock clicks into place, it knows where you are. The only thing that can undo it is a stronger lock somewhere else.

Do not get comfortable, my son. Do not build a home. Keep moving. Make sure your things are never exactly where you left them. I am going to the woods now. I am hoping it follows my shape instead of yours.

I sat with that for a minute. I finally understood the perimeter walks. I understood the furniture rule. I understood the photo he made me take down. He wasn't controlling. He was terrified his whole life of something I never believed was real.

The journal goes back to my dad's notes.

May 12th, 2021

It found me again last night. I thought moving out here would confuse it. I thought the lack of structure in the trailer would break its memory. But I woke up at 3 AM, and I could hear it walking in the dirt outside.

It walks exactly seven paces. Then it turns. Then it walks four paces. Then it turns. It's walking the floor plan of the old house. Our old house. The one where my dad lived before he disappeared.

I remember being a boy and hiding at the top of the stairs when my dad tried to run from it. I heard the footsteps in the hallway. I heard the thing walking the floor plan in the pitch black without bumping a single wall. It navigated the dark perfectly. It knew exactly where his bed was.

August 4th, 2022

I tried rearranging the furniture in the trailer. Moved the camp stove in front of the door. Moved my sleeping bag to the opposite wall. It worked for a few months. When it came back, I heard it bump into the stove from the outside. I heard it dragging its hands along the thin metal door where my sleeping bag used to be. It got confused. It made a horrible clicking sound low in its throat. A sound like grinding teeth.

But it adapts. You can't outrun it forever. It followed my grandfather. It followed my father. It followed me. And now it knows my son is alive.

February 2nd, 2019

I finally called him. He picked up. I got as far as "I need to tell you something about our family, about why I had to leave" before he cut me off. I heard exactly how angry he still is. He hung up before I finished the sentence.

I don't blame him. I gave him every reason to stop listening to me. I just wish I'd found a way to say it faster so he wouldn't have hung up before the part that mattered.

I remembered that call. Hearing how much it cost him just to dial the number made the seven seconds I gave him on the phone feel like the worst thing I have ever done. I would give almost anything to go back and let him finish that sentence.

November 18th, 2023

I had to go to Seattle. I had to see where he lives.

I parked down the street from his building and watched him leave for work. I picked the lock on his front door. I know exactly what this is. If he ever finds out, he will hate me even more, and he'd be right to. But I have to know the exact dimensions of his cage if I want any chance of building a better one somewhere it can't reach him.

I spent six hours measuring every wall. Distance from the front door to the kitchen counter. Exact placement of his bed. Photographs of his couch, his rugs, his television stand.

If I can build something that carries that same shape more completely than his real apartment does, I can make it believe it's already found him.

March 3rd, 2024

The bank approved the loan for the shed. Ordered the steel. Started framing the drywall today.

It has to be perfect. If the couch sits two inches to the left, it will bump its knee and know it's a forgery. If the counter height is off by half an inch, it will feel the discrepancy in its hands. I've been practicing walking through the frame blindfolded, counting my steps, running my palms along the studs. It has to feel exactly like his apartment. The air has to move the same way.

I'm sleeping in here most nights now instead of the trailer. I want the place to smell like a person. A house that's never been lived in doesn't fool anything. I've started keeping my own routine in here. Coffee at the same counter every morning. Reading in the same corner every night. The way he would if this were really his.

June 21st, 2024

It came closer than it ever has tonight. I heard it enter the clearing around midnight. Instead of circling the trailer, it walked straight toward the barn. I stood at the tripwire threshold with my hand on the switch, ready to seal it in before it was finished. It stopped maybe ten feet from the doors. Just stood there. I could hear that clicking sound again.

I think it noticed the shed is starting to matter to it. I think it's starting to prefer it to the trailer. That should feel like good news. It doesn't. It means the design is working exactly the way I built it to, and I still have so much left to do before it's finished enough to actually hold.

January 9th, 2025

My chest hurts all the time now. My left arm goes numb when I lift the drywall panels. I don't think I have much time left.

I heard it outside the trailer last night. It pressed itself against the thin metal door. I could hear its hard fingers tracing the outline of the frame, slow, patient, the way you'd read braille. It's tired of playing with me. It's getting ready to stop waiting.

October 22nd, 2025

Went back to Seattle one more time. He bought a new coffee table since my last visit. Had to measure it, order the same model, ship it out here.

I also took one of his shirts from the laundry hamper. Sealed it in a plastic bag. I need the replica to smell like him. Every detail matters.

I've built a heavy steel locking mechanism on the outside of the barn doors. It is on a tripwire, so it can't just wander back out once it commits to going in.

The replica is finished now. It is a perfect clone of his apartment on Greenwood Avenue.

If I die before it comes, it won't have closure. It will move down the bloodline the way it always has. It will go to Seattle. It will go to my son. I failed him for most of both our lives. This is the one thing I intend to do right.

That was the second-to-last entry.

I don't even know how to process this. For years he'd been watching my life from the outside, parked down the street, keeping notes on my routine like it was a job. Twice that I can find, he actually broke into my apartment. But he spent his last years and all his money building this thing out here alone just to protect me. I gave up on him, and he was out here doing this.

The very last page is written in messy handwriting. The pen tore through the paper in a few places.

July 14th, 2026

My heart is failing. The pain is blinding.

I can hear it in the clearing. It knows I'm dying. It's pacing back and forth in the dirt outside. Seven paces. Turn. Four paces. Turn.

I'm going to leave the trailer. I'm going to walk out into the clearing and let it take me there instead. I've set the locks on the barn doors. The tripwire is active.

But I realized a flaw in the design too late to fix it.

It hunts by spatial memory, but the memory alone isn't what triggers it. If it comes here after I'm gone and finds the same layout sitting empty, it will recognize the shape. But a shape with nothing living inside it means nothing.

A trap doesn't spring on an empty room.

It hunts the space, but it needs the heat inside it. It needs the breathing.

The trap only works if the bait is inside.

That is the last thing he wrote.

I'm just sitting here on this fake couch in this fake apartment.

I keep thinking about the sketch on the wall behind the desk. Needs more of him in it, not just his furniture.

I thought that meant the shirt he stole. I don't think that is what it meant anymore. I think somewhere in the last two years of this, he started planning for the possibility that an object wouldn't be enough. He just stopped writing that part down because he couldn't make himself put it on paper.

I need to leave. I'm looking at my phone right now. I was going to call someone, but I don't even know what I would say.

It was so loud it made me jump.

The wind must have caught them. I probably didn't push them back far enough on the tracks. I just lost my single bar of cell service when the metal sealed. The Wi-Fi on my laptop is somehow picking up a faint open network from the landlord's house next door, so I'm trying to post this through the browser right now before that cuts out too.

Something is scratching at the wall.

It sounds like it's coming from right outside the fake bedroom, right where he painted that water stain. On the other side of the studs out in the dark part of the barn.

It's slow. It sounds like hands tracing the outline of the drywall.
