I'd been working as a law enforcement transcriptionist for a little over six years when the officer brought me the tape. I'd never seen him before, but that wasn't too unusual. There were a lot of new recruits and I didn't exactly have the best memory when it came to faces. He wore the customary uniform, which fit him rather well. Even as a straight man, I have to admit he was handsome, almost ridiculously so. Dark hair, a sharp jawline speckled with stubble, and an air of gravitas that was impossible to ignore.

He handed me a cassette tape.  
“I need this transcribed immediately,” he said in a smooth, inviting tone. “Don't put it through the usual queue.”  
With an eyebrow raised, I asked what the case was about. Instead of answering, he looked at the tape for a moment before looking back at me.  
“Just prepare yourself,” he said with a grimace. "It's quite intense.”  
I remember laughing a little because I assumed he was exaggerating. But he didn't laugh with me. Before I could ask for any more details, he disappeared back down the corridor.  
I've always been quite a curious person. It's what led me to this line of work. So, naturally, I immediately popped the tape in, pulled up Mark43 and began logging.

What follows is my transcription of that recording. I've removed the names and identifying information where necessary, but otherwise I've left it exactly as it was spoken. Be that for better or for worse.  
The transcript of this tape– or, more precisely, its contents– makes for difficult reading. The things I heard and witnessed that day remain beyond my understanding. It is for this reason that I decided to share the tape's contents. Perhaps one of you can make more sense of this disturbing tape than I.

\###

Tape Recorded Interview  
Patrick M./Detective B. Hauptman  
7.23.26

BH	This is Detective Hauptman, █████ Police Department. Today's date is Thursday, July 23, 2026. The time is at 11:23 A.M. This will be a taped conversation with the last name of █████, first of Patrick, P-A-T-R-I-C-K. Date of birth █████.

BH	So, Patrick, I've turned on a tape recorder so I can record our little conversation. I'm to understand you've been read your rights and– let me see here– you've declined to lawyer up. Is that correct?

PM	Correct.

BH	Okay. Well, let's get down to business then, eh?

PM	Alright.

BH	So, Patrick. I assume you know why you're here?

PM	Mhm.

BH	Why don't you go ahead and tell me?

PM	Because you think I killed–

PM	Can you speak up?

PM	Because you think I killed people. 

BH	The department suspects you, yes. But hey, don't shoot the messenger. (Chuckle)

BH	Let's start with… Isabella Moreno. How well did you know her?

PM	Quite well. 

BH	How's that?

PM	She lived down the street. We used to play when we were kids.

BH	And when did you last speak with her?

PM	Hm… December, January. Something like that.

BH	What did you talk about?

PM	Why would I remember that? It's been like seven months.

BH	Well, I'd think your last conversation might stick around longer. Especially with all that talk of art.

PM	What? 

BH	Art. You're an artist, right?

PM	Yeah– how did you–

BH	You take pride in your art, then?

PM	I– yeah, I guess I do.

BH	Every artist does, I suppose. But most are too scared to put their art out in the open. You have no such reservations, right?

PM	What do you mean?

BH	You like displaying your work publicly. In all its glory.

PM	I have no idea what you're–

BH	Really? So you don't look up to a certain… mentor?

PM	No. Look–

BH	You don't have to explain it.

PM	… What?

BH	I know.

PM	… The fuck're you– Know what?

BH	Why you did it.

PM	Then why am I here?

BH	Because they want to know if you did it.

PM	And?

BH	And… you did. 

PM	What– What's the point of this then?

BH	I wanted to meet you. In the flesh.

PM	I–

BH	I've seen your work. Your *real* work. Not those flimsy portraits in your apartment.

PM	What the hell're you talking about?

BH	You know. And I must admit, you are an artist at heart. Just not a very good one.

PM	Excuse me? What does that even mean?

BH	Her body. I mean, the way you arranged it. Chest splayed open, guts hanging around her neck. And the little message you left beside her. Very theatrical, I must say.

PM	I didn't—

BH	You spent three hours arranging her. Isn't that so, Patrick?

PM	No–

BH	You spent the longest on the fingers.

PM	Whatever you're doing won't—

BH	You wanted it to look natural. But you don't understand hands very well. (Chuckle)  
BH	Even in your shitty little paintings. It takes a special kind of anti-talent to make them look like the aftermath of a car wreck.

PM	Stop–

BH	C'mon, Patrick. You made the thing and now you don't want to discuss the composition?

PM	Shut up.

BH	I suppose it's to be expected. You don't care about the art. You don't even care whether you get caught. You just want to be seen.

PM	You're full of shit.

BH	You copied someone else's work because you wanted people to look at yours.

PM	I didn't copy anyone! (Something slams against the metal table)

BH	It's no use lying, Patrick. You read everything you could find about the man. Every little detail on how he displayed his victims. Hell, you even fooled the cops into thinking you were the real deal.

PM	You– You don't know anything about me.

BH	I know exactly what you are. A copy. You imitate the man, but you've never met him. And yet you've spent the last two years trying to make him proud. That's rather pathetic, Patrick.

PM	Stop–

BH	Isn't that what mommy always used to say?

PM	I said stop!

BH	(Mocking tone) The exact same rotten soul as your father! 

PM	What– How do you–

BH	She threw your paintings away, didn't she? You'd find them in the trash, sometimes even torn in half.

PM	Shut up! Shut the–

BH	Do you remember the blue one?

BH	Oh, c'mon Patrick. How could you forget? The one you painted when you were seventeen. The woman standing in the field.

PM	Stop– Stop talking about my paintings.

BH	Ah, you prefer to talk about your real art after all? (Chuckle)  
BH	I know you, Patrick. You started killing because painting wasn't enough anymore. You wanted to make something nobody could ignore.

PM	Please just—

BH	You wanted someone to look at it and understand you. But nobody did.

PM	Stop talking!

BH	Not your mother. Not your father. Not the police. Not even the man you copied. And now you're sitting here hoping I will.

PM	I–

BH	Well I do understand you. And I want you to feel how truly pathetic you are, Patrick.

PM	Please– (whimper)

BH	Patrick.

PM	Please just stop!

BH	Look at me.

PM	No! I– I won't.

BH	(Static crackles. Voice briefly distorts) Patrick, you slime. Look at me!

PM	I'll– I'll confess! I'll tell you everything you wanna know, please just–

BH	(Chuckle) Oh, Patty. You don't have to do that. I know what you did. 

PM	(Unintelligible)

BH	Chin up, champ. Smile, and look into my eyes.

(Pause)

PM	(Screaming, unintelligible).  
PM	WHAT– WHAT THE FUCK IS–

(Something slams against the metal table)

BH	(Voice warped) Come closer.

PM	(Unintelligible whines)

(Something drags across the metal table.)

BH	(Whispers something unintelligible)

PM	No– no it can't–

BH	(Slow, droning) Yes, Patty. You know she was.

PM	(Strangled cry. Sobs)

(Something slams against the metal table)

BH	There you go. Attaboy.

(Something slams against the metal table several times, with a small pause between each increment)

(The door whines open. The slamming continues.)

BH	Ah, good day gentlemen! 

(Frantic yelling. The slamming continues.)

BH	My eyes are up here, lads.

(Unintelligible cries. Thuds join the slamming.)

BH	This has been Detective Hauptman, █████ Police Department. Today's date is Thursday, July 23, 2026. 

(The slamming stops. The thudding continues.)

BH	I'm turning the tape recorder off again. The time is 11:30 A.M. This has been the confession and testimony of Patrick █████.

\###

When the tape stopped, I sat in silence for a moment. The shock of it all had rooted me firmly in place. Eventually, I forced myself out of my chair and scrambled towards the interrogation room.   
I honestly wish I hadn't. 

Patrick was still sitting at the table. He'd smashed his face into the metal table so many times that there was almost nothing recognizable left. His nose was almost completely crushed into his skull, which itself had been pulverized by the repeated impacts.   
The two officers who'd been monitoring the interrogation lay in a puddle of blood on the floor. The first officer had strangled the second with his own tie, while the second had beaten him across the head with a chair. There was no apparent reason for any of it. It looked like three men had simply gone mad at the same time.

In the weeks and months following, the department searched for Detective B. Hauptman. Of course, there was no officer by that name at our department. We still don't know how he forged or stole the badge and uniform. We've tried to identify him using the cameras he walked past, but these efforts yielded nothing. It seemed as though he'd wandered in, then walked off the face of the earth.  
I still have a copy of the cassette. I made it in case the original was ever destroyed. Still, I haven't mustered up the courage to play it again. The most I've managed is to re-read the transcript I made. I can't wrap my head around what happened. I suspect I never will. 

That brought me here. Perhaps sharing this will yield more results. So please, if you have any information on B. Hauptman, send it to me. I need to know who– or what– he was. More than that, I wish I knew how he knew so much about Patrick.
