I know this may sound weird, or even sad. I am deeply paranoid, and I have nobody to tell this to. I cannot allow myself to trust anyone I thought I knew.

It must have started around a month ago, or so I believe. My sister had just made dinner. I distinctly remember the smell of fried meat flooding our small apartment. It was a pleasant scent, masking the cigarette smoke that lingered in the air.

Everyone has their own peculiarities—I admit that I have plenty. One of mine was always sitting on the chair that was next to the window. She teased me constantly about it, but never questioned it.

So, when I saw her occupying my usual spot, I was a bit confused. I didn’t think much of it. Work had taken a toll on her that day, so I assumed she'd just forgotten.

“We're switching places today?” I asked absentmindedly, already sitting opposite her.

The look she gave me was one of confusion.

“What do you mean?” she asked, pausing mid-bite.

“I always sit there, that's all,” I pointed out.

The silence was only broken by the sound of my chewing. She remained still for at least another minute.

“Do you?”

We'd been roommates for years at that point. She certainly knew. I concluded it must have been a bad joke.

“Have it your way, then,” I replied with a sigh.

We brushed past it and didn't talk much for the remainder of the meal. 

I can't quite explain it, but I felt a sense of unease. I tried to drown it, but it was too strong to ignore.

I felt like she was staring at me. Persistently. I could imagine her unblinking eyes following my every move.

Frankly, I was too freaked out to return the look. I don't think I even washed the dishes that night; I simply went to bed.

It was small things at first. She stopped saying goodbye to me—something she always did. My clothes were left unfolded, even though the chores were shared.

I slowly started realizing that something was wrong. Our parents stopped calling out of the blue, even though they did so every other night.

One of the framed photos on my nightstand only contained my sister, even though we took it together.

In the span of three weeks, it felt as if I ceased to exist. My friends hadn't messaged me once, and my sister was home even less than before.

*Maybe she's seeing someone*, I kept telling myself, trying to rationalize what was happening to me.

It all went downhill about three days ago.

It could have been the anxiety or the growing paranoia, but I felt incredibly unwell that morning. So, I called in sick from work.

“I'm sorry, but who is this?”

I stared at my wall, dumbfounded.

“Violet…” I replied, unsure of how to react.

I had been working at that company for over five years. My boss was quite fond of me, for reasons I don't feel like expanding upon. It must have been a mistake, or a new secretary.

The woman on the other end said something I wasn't able to make out before she broke the news to me. It made my blood run cold.

“I think you have the wrong number. I'm sorry... we've never employed anyone named Violet. Have a nice day.”

The ground vanished under my feet. All I could do was keep my eyes glued to the wall, or else I'd have a breakdown. I checked the contact three times. Then I checked the company website. Same number.

I thought of calling my boss on his personal number, but I was frightened to do so. What if he'd forgotten about me too?

My mind tirelessly made up all sorts of excuses. A coordinated prank. Perhaps a cruel way to let me know I'm fired. At one point I even considered the possibility of it being a nightmare, and that I'd wake up to find everything back to normal.

The knock on my door interrupted my delirium.

“Violet, are you in there?”

It was my sister's voice. A wave of relief washed over me. She remembered me. If there was a logical explanation for whatever I was going through, I was certain that we'd figure it out together. Like we always did.

Right. My sister.

She was supposed to be at work. She had neglected me all that time, treating me as if I never existed. Was it a joke? Did she finally realize that she took it too far?

Every fiber of my being screamed at me to not open that door. I felt trapped and helpless for some unknown reason.

*It's my sister for fuck's sake*, I told myself.

I forced my weakened body to stand up and took slow steps toward the other side of my room. Just as my hand met the doorknob, a shiver ran down my spine.

Why was it so cold?

“Violet, open up,” she said softly.

If she hadn't said that, I probably would have. I know my sister better than I know myself. We're twins, after all.

That wasn't her voice. Or rather, that wasn't her tone. The gentleness was deeply unsettling. It sounded disingenuous, almost forced.

I slowly backed away from the door, trying as hard as I could to not make a sound.

*Knock.*

“Violet? Are you alright?”

That wasn't my sister.

I dropped my weight on the bed, hiding under the covers as I practically suffocated myself with my pillow.

The knocking became more persistent.

“Open the door, Vi. It's me; I'm worried.”

She sounded different from before. Impatient.

The knocking turned to banging, and the banging turned to kicking.

“Open the door!” she ordered.

I don't know who that was, but they sounded angry. All I could do was pray that they couldn't hear me hyperventilating.

“Open the fucking door!” they repeated, louder.

Raw, unadulterated fury radiated from them. It sounded as if my door would get destroyed any second.

Unable to control myself, I screamed at the top of my lungs.

“Get away from me! I'm not opening the door!”

My reaction might sound stupid given the situation, but I felt the primal urge to do that. Feeling threatened does that to a person. Almost as if I wanted to appear dangerous, even though I was far from it.

The breath caught in my throat when I heard them back away. I genuinely would have felt safer if they hadn't, because what happened next was far more terrifying.

I could hear them run around the apartment. The speed of the footsteps was irregular, too fast for any normal human. That wasn't all.

They were crawling. Cold sweat formed on my forehead as I imagined them clinging to the walls on all fours.

“Open the door!”

The voice was distorted and impossibly high-pitched. I frantically searched around for my phone as the being got quieter.

“Get away from me! I'm not opening the door!” I screamed, making my neck feel sore.

“That's alright,” they replied, the tone turning gentle once again.

“I'm already inside, Violet.”

I slowly turned my head, dropping my phone to the ground. It was my sister. Or rather, an attempt at looking like her.

The eyes had no warmth behind them, and they were slightly bigger than normal. The smile. Oh, God, the smile. It looked too wide for her face to accommodate.

Her jaw dislocated, opening her mouth impossibly wide to reveal a spiral of teeth that climbed deeper into her esophagus.

“How could I forget about you?” they asked, the voice coming from the space around them as they got closer. “There is barely anything left of you out there. I saved the main course for last.”

The room was so cold. My erratic breaths turned to thin fog.

When I opened my eyes again, I was surrounded by the fluorescent lights of the hospital. Someone told me I'd been unconscious for two days. They said my sister called the ambulance.

“How about my sister?” I asked, although I knew the answer to that.

“She's coming to visit tonight.”

My eyes widened and the beeping of the heart monitor accelerated. My sister doesn't remember who I am. Nobody does.

I don't know what to do. I know that there's not much you can recommend, but please, help me.
