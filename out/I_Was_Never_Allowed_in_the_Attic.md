The house I grew up in was a thin, old thing. I think it was a townhouse at some point, but everything around it was torn down. It was made up of two floors and an attic. My parent’s room was on the first floor, alongside the kitchen and first bathroom. My room was on the second floor, where there was a second bathroom and a short, carpeted hallway leading to a window that looked out over the street. There was a cozy window seat set up there; I used to spend hours curled up on it when I was small, devouring library books and watching the sun set. On the ceiling above the window was a square hatch, from which hung a worn brass chain with a heavy, wooden handle at the end. It could be pulled on to open the hatch and cause the ladder to unfold, giving someone access to the attic. The house was a bit drafty, and sometimes the chain would swing gently back and forth, seemingly on its own.  
I wasn’t allowed in the attic. My father said it was because it held all sorts of stuff that was dangerous for a child: exposed nails, rotting boards, mousetraps in the corners. But my parents spent a lot of time in the attic, usually after I went to bed. It wasn’t uncommon for me to see the ladder extended when I got up to go to the washroom in the middle of the night, or hear creaking footsteps above me as I drifted off to sleep. None of it bothered me because it had been happening for as long as I could remember. It was normal.  
When we moved out of that old house, it all felt very sudden, even at the time. As a kid any number with that many zeros registered as a lot. Looking back, I see just how cheaply they sold it for.  
The house we moved into is the one we still live in. It’s one floor, two bedrooms, one bathroom, and a small attic. The new attic can also be accessed through a square hatch in the ceiling, but you need to bring in an outside ladder to reach it. I’m still not allowed to go up there.  
Two police officers stopped by about a week ago and spoke to my parents for about an hour. They were speaking in hushed tones, so I couldn’t catch anything they said.  
Yesterday, I stumbled across a newspaper article about my old house. Apparently the current owners hadn’t bothered much with the attic until they noticed an odor coming from above the second floor. They went up to discover a mass that was later identified as thirty-seven bodies, all in various states of decay, naked and piled up on top of each other, old blood staining the boards and exposed fiberglass.  
I’m not sure what to do next. I’m laying in bed awake right now. I can hear someone moving around in the attic above me, all creaking footsteps and maybe something being dragged. It’s making it hard to fall asleep.
