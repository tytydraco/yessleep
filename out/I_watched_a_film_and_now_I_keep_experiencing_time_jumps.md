It started when I was 17. I was rubbish with people, got bullied all through school, had maybe two mates. When I finished I just stayed at my mum's and watched films and telly. All day, every day, curtains shut, Mum leaving cups of tea outside the door that I'd find cold hours later. The night I first saw 2001: A Space Odyssey I was glued to my seat. It did something to me, and looking back, that's the last night I remember properly.

The credits started rolling and I was just sat there, buzzing, trying to work out what the ending even meant, when Mum burst in, absolutely losing it. Said I wouldn't work, wouldn't go out, wouldn't do anything. I said I'd get a job soon, STOP going on at me. And she said, "You're 21 for goodness sake. It's time!"

I was 17. I'd been 17 literally a minute ago.

I ran to the bathroom and looked in the mirror and there was this scruffy, greasy bloke staring back at me. Four years. Gone in the blink of an eye.

I sat back down and it flashed, the telly was different. I was shouting for Mum and she wasn't there. In fact, it was a different place altogether. A council flat, with takeaway boxes, empty cans, a pile of letters from the dole office, and a dent in the sofa in the exact shape of me.

The phone rang and it was Mum. She was chuntering about how she's worried sick and how she couldn't keep sending me money out of her pension. I was lost for words. So I just said the only thing I could.

"I'll try, Mum. I love you."

"I love you too son. But you're 33 now. It's time."

Then I looked in the mirror. About 5 stone heavier. Balding. Stinking, like I'd been glued to that seat for a decade.

I was crushed. My young adult life just wiped out before my very eyes. I don't know what happened next. At this point I probably blinked.

But the next thing I knew, I was standing in a crematorium in a suit that barely fitted, holding an order of service. There was a photo of Mum on the front. She was smiling. The date put her at 77.

I was 49.

My Auntie was next to me and she told me Mum had rung me every Sunday for years and I never picked up. That she'd been in hospital for three weeks, and they'd left messages, and right up until the end she kept asking if I was coming. I didn't know she was ill. I didn't know any of it. The curtains started to close round the coffin and I could hear people behind me whispering about how long it was taking me to cry.

I didn't cry. That's the worst part. I stood there dry-eyed through the hymn, I only started howling in the taxi home, because then it went quiet and I could hear myself.

I got back to the flat. Complete squalor. A body that hardly worked. And I thought, it all started with that film. That one night. Everything since had been a blur with the occasional flash of daylight. So I tore the place apart looking for my old copy, I don't even know why, I think I wanted to watch it again and see if I could maybe stop this madness. I found it right at the back of a cupboard, a dusty tape with the sleeve falling apart. I put it on and sat through the whole thing with my heart going, waiting for something to happen.

When the credits started rolling, I let out a long breath and looked around the room.

The flat is unlivable, the telly is a huge flat thing on the wall, and the film isn't coming from any machine I know about. Just a loud metal box with a plastic typewriter thing to me.

I'm 76 now.

I'm too big to get out of this chair. My chest is tight and I get out of breath just typing this. I'm fairly sure the next jump will be the last one, and I've sort of made my peace with that, or I'm getting there.

I keep thinking about what I never did. I've never been on a date. I've never kissed anyone. I've never explored the world. I've never had a job. There's no wife, no kids, no mates, and I doubt anyone will come to my funeral.

Maybe I slipped through a gap in time. Or maybe I just chose this for myself.

I suggest you don't do either.

It's time.
