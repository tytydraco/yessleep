I think I knew that my ex would go missing. Okay, rephrase. I always sensed that one day he would get himself into a kind of trouble that even he couldn’t talk his way out of. And he could talk himself out of a lot. 

I don’t hate James. I didn’t wish this upon him. I did love him. There was a long period of time when we were so interconnected, you wouldn’t have been able to trace where my story started and his began. 

We were paranormal investigators. Ghost hunters. It feels worse saying it now, more childish and embarrassing. At one time, we had a modest YouTube following. People cared when we posted. Every time we traveled for a shoot, someone would stop us at baggage claim. We’d fish for our luggage on the carousel with at least one wide-eyed fan nearby trying to explain to their family who these random, sleep deprived losers were and why we were somehow very important. 

It was nice, for whatever it’s worth. 

The paranormal was how we met, at a fuckass occult enthusiast conference in Delaware. I was scarfing down a cold veggie omelette in a dreary Holiday Inn dining room when he asked if he could sit across from me. We sat in silence until James asked what talks I was excited about. Our lists matched perfectly. At the time, it felt like the most serendipitous thing in the world. Looking back, I wonder if I confused the feeling of dumb luck with damnation. 

I quit first. I didn’t want to. I wasn’t scared into retirement. It was mind-numbingly mundane. Traveling all the time was expensive, and so were cameras and permits and hiring people to help at locations if we wanted things to look polished. Neither of us had super cushy day jobs to endlessly fund a project that never really took off. Add on my medical bills from a series of accidents a couple of years ago and you have two people hemorrhaging money. 

James and I fought about it for months before I told him that I needed a break. That was the end of our relationship too, even if we didn’t break up for another three months. 

It’s been about a year since then. We thought we could stay in contact but that quickly fell apart. A few months ago I bumped into a mutual friend and they mentioned that James was seeing someone. A month later I heard he had started investigating again. Yesterday, his brother texted me that James has been missing for just under 48 hours. 

**Conner: Kelcey. Something happened to James. We haven’t seen or heard from him in a couple of days. Have you spoken to him recently??**

I’ve gotten the chills a lot in my life, lost my breath, felt my skin crawl and coil. I’ve heard the whining in my ears and felt my stomach drop more times than I could ever count. 

That’s how I felt when I read the first text. Your body forcibly lowered into frigid waters when you know something is *wrong*. That desperation to claw your way to the surface and feel your feet placed normally on the ground. 

But when I read the second message, I went hot all over. 

**Conner: He said he was going hiking in Cata Falls. His girlfriend said he’s been out there on his own.** 

My entire skin shrank two sizes and fit like a suffocating shell of hardened leather. My joints screamed at me, and my tongue felt thick and fluffy. It was immediate fire, an allergic reaction. Your body rejecting information it does not want, cannot accept.

My ribs ache, phantom claws digging into my sides and pulling me backwards. Again, further this time. No chance of escape. 

Cata Falls was the one place we swore we’d never go.

We used to laugh about it, smushed close on an air mattress in our first apartment together. We’d daydream about our trips, scheduling them for three-day weekends and saved days off. Old Alton Bridge in April. The Silent Movie Theatre in June. But not Cata Falls. Cata Falls was above our paygrade. Cata Falls was for A-list ghost hunters. Cata Falls was where you went if you wanted to disappear for real. 

Conner and I got on the phone so I could tell him everything I knew. I hadn’t spoken to James in seven or eight months. Cata Falls was something we had talked about, but we never planned to go there. The name referenced a pretty big swath of forest, but there were only a few trails that were interesting to paranormal researchers. I surprised myself with how much I remembered, but it didn’t feel like enough. 

I asked if James had mentioned what trails he’d be hiking. My right hand was shaking so badly I had it balled into a fist. Conner told me that the girlfriend didn’t ask for details because he always shared his location with her. Naturally, his phone was not currently findable.

I’m not above saying that this pissed me off. Who doesn’t get someone’s trail or campsite coordinates before they go off into the woods alone? Had she never seen a fucking movie? Did she just not care? 

I asked Conner about the girlfriend.

“Taya? She’s nice. I don’t know her super well. She’s very different from James.”

“Is there any way she was inv-”

Conner cut me off before I had to finish the thought. 

“Kelce, no. She’s, like, a normal person. Only into the paranormal around Halloween, doesn’t care about the ghost stuff, isn’t really into hiking. She’s a vet tech. Rides horses. She and James met at an arcade bar.” 

I could feel my pulse steady. I told Conner I’d drive out to his parents’ place the next day and tell them and the police everything I knew. He sounded relieved. 

I got ready for bed. I lit candles. I listened to a white noise playlist I made to help overpower the sound of hospital machinery when I was there for long stretches. 

At least I think I did. The details go fuzzy after the phone call. I tossed and turned, trying not to panic. Conner had sounded relieved when I told him that I would talk to the police. As if he was already planning on asking me to do that. 

I did not sleep. 

So I’m doing 90 on the freeway. I shouldn't be surprised that I am on the list of people the police need to talk to. I’m the ex-girlfriend, James and I didn’t have a great breakup, and I’m the one he did all his paranormal bullshit with. They need to talk to me because I know James really well. 

And I do. Well enough to know that he didn’t fall on this hike, that he didn’t meet a serial killer in the woods, that he didn’t drink from a stream without filtering his water and is now tucked away in a cave shitting himself from giardia. 

But I do know that with every passing second, he is running out of time. I can’t sit in his childhood home drinking his mom's favorite Coke Zero while some cop’s monotone voice asks me about my relationship to the “missing person.” 

*I was supposed to be with him.* 

I don’t go to his parents’ house. 

Conner mentioned that James and Taya were renting on the other side of the city near the post office and a dilapidated bowling alley I remember going to for birthday parties in elementary school. I pull onto the cross street, a one-way packed with apartment buildings and walk ups. It doesn’t take me long to find theirs. A homemade windchime hangs off the side of a brick duplex, dangling from a gnarled bit of rusty metal. It’s a protective measure, glass and wood and moss and other things we’d found while traveling. James hated it, but I insisted on basic wards. Always use protection. 

I knew it would be here. I insisted James keep it when we broke up. But seeing it shielding their house made my throat go dry. I didn’t realize how long I’d gone without crying. 

It only took me a couple of steps to launch myself up to the front door and only a couple of seconds after that to begin fervently hammering on the wood. I counted in my head, trying to reason how long it would take me to wake from a dead sleep, get out of bed, grab a weapon, and walk toward the uninvited sound. 

But it’s mere moments before the door swings open and I see a fully dressed woman with a tired look that ages her at least ten years. 

She takes one look at me and groans.

“Kelcey. Right?” 

“Hi, Taya. I’d say it’s nice to meet you but that’s pretty fucked up considering. I’m here because I need your help.”

Taya blinks slowly. Maybe she had been asleep, just sitting on the couch by the phone waiting for news. Never taking off her shoes in case she needed to dash outside. Her mouth twists. She’s chewing the inside of her lip. I wonder if she’s ever drawn blood by accident. 

“I’m guessing this has something to do with the ghosts.”

I want Taya to invite me in so that we can sit on her couch. I don’t want to have this conversation at the threshold of her duplex at 3:12 in the morning. I don’t want to have this conversation at all, but the universe has clearly stopped factoring in my opinion on things. But she doesn’t invite me in, just stays fixed in the doorway challenging me to continue.

“Cata Falls is fucked. James and I knew people that went missing there. A lot of the disappearances and accidents reported in the area in the last 20 years were ghost hunters or occult enthusiasts. Bad things happened to them, Taya. Car accidents, sudden paralysis, amnesia. James and I did a lot of really stupid shit, things I’m not proud of, but we said we would never go there. Cata was always off the list.” 

“I know. I know it’s a big deal. James talked about it a few times, mostly when we first started dating and he was telling me about ghost hunting. He’s a good storyteller.”

I nod. Common ground. 

“But how do I know you didn’t kill him?”

Okay, maybe less common ground than I thought. 

“Taya. If I hated him enough to kill him, I would have killed you in front of him first.” 

Taya’s arms fall away from her chest. She looks up at the ceiling, taking a hard earned break from looking at my face. 

“Fine. Give me a second to change my shoes.” 

Taya turns and disappears back into the house. I linger, suddenly compelled to push into their entryway and experience the life she and James built with each other. I hold my ribs on both sides with either hand, pressing into the scar tissue. 

I retreat, get back in the car. 

Taya slips into the passenger seat, closing the door quietly behind her. She places a backpack in front of her feet.  Every gesture is polite, conscious of how space and air do or do not move around her. James could be loud and a little bumbling; Taya is fluid and observant. She turns around as we drive away, looking into my backseat, seeing the duffle bags and camera cases I’d haphazardly yanked from my garage and stashed in the back. I couldn’t even remember what was in them. 

I think Taya is going to make a joke about me murdering her; it’s what I would do.

Instead, she looks up at another sigil hanging from my rearview mirror. 

“You really believe in this?”

Some sad, scared kid inside of me feels like she’s setting me up. Feigning interest only to crash into my meager confidence with full body malice. But her eyes are so earnest, nothing in them conjures falsehood. 

"As much as anybody." 

We finally turn into the park and I am heavy with guilt. Anticipation, not dread, is boiling in my stomach. Muscle memory has kicked in and I can feel the adrenaline flooding through my body.I’m suddenly worried that I look flushed. I vice grip the wheel so Taya doesn’t see my right hand trembling. 

She doesn’t ask more questions. She lets me go through the bins and pick and choose things in silence. I offer Taya a headlamp, but she pulls out James’s spare which is actually one of my old models. I shove extra batteries into my pack and lock the car before we set off. 

Neither of us speak for the first hour. I can hear Taya’s labored breathing, and my own. Both of us are tired, worn down, disoriented. We keep pace with each other, slowing if needed, never more than two steps away from each other at a time. 

I flinch every time I hear rustling in the brush. 

Taya is quick to comment, “You’re jumpy for a ghost hunter. You’re not used to this?”

“I’m used to fear, sure. I understand what it feels like for me. But it doesn’t lessen it. I just know what it is when it arrives. I’m not afraid of fear I guess.” 

But Taya isn’t listening to me anymore. She’s staring at a cairn up ahead. Unlike the usual rock stacks off to the side of a trail, this one is hulking, right in the center of the path. 

We approach slowly. There's a bit of red fabric peeking out. A red sweatshirt we’d both seen plenty of times before.

Taya runs before I can stop her. She flings rocks to the side until she uncovers what they've been hiding. The scrap of fabric has been stuffed with leaves, a crude person shape with twig arms. No eyes though.

“Is that supposed to be James?” Taya’s voice comes out as a squeak.

I don’t answer her, just grab her hand and shake the doll out of her grip. I pull her away and farther down the path. She wrenches out of my grip. 

“Kelcey, what was that? Did he do that?”

“No, James didn’t make that. It’s a warning to other investigators.” 

“A warning about what!” 

“That someone is already descending.”

At first, I was grateful that Taya hadn’t asked a lot of questions about what James was doing in this forest. It made the drive easier, made convincing her to come easier. I should have considered that it was too easy. Now, having to talk about all of this in the open feels like I’m putting an even more obvious target on my back. But I tell her. 

I explain that Cata Falls has a lot of technical areas, great for experienced hikers. But there are also unofficial maps. They depict a sequence of trails that, if you walk them in order, reveal a final section that will take you down to the threshold of hell. The veil is thin there, supposedly. 

I tell her that her boyfriend is trying to summon a demon. 

Taya does not take this well. She charges back the way we came, but immediately loses the path. I see what Conner meant when he said she wasn’t much for hiking. She has no sense of direction. 

When she finally slows down, it’s because she has prioritized sobbing over moving. I want to put my arms around her. I don’t. 

A chain has come untucked from her shirt. Silver. At the end is a small vial filled with red. It doesn’t fit her vibe, her energy. Too vampiric and gothic. 

She catches sight of it, tucks it into her shirt quickly, like it’s hot. 

“Is that blood?”

I don’t need to ask. James and I had necklaces just like those. Matching exchanges of blood. Violent promises. 

“James gave it to me. I know, not my *style*. But he was so excited. I try to leave it at home, make excuses about jewelry at work and stuff. But, today…”

Taya shakes her head. 

“I'm - I'm fine. Let's keep going.”

Relief hits me in a rush. 

I pull the map from my pocket. It’s an aggregate of different drawings from other investigators layered on top of each other. Unsurprisingly, it’s hard to get an accurate map of a cursed path. More diligent moderators are constantly finding them and taking them down for the safety of the community. But there are enough bits and pieces and screenshots floating around that you can assemble something if you’re desperate enough. 

We walk. Light filters in overhead, pressing through the crush of trees. Maybe 100 yards in front of us is a dead end, the path leading to a very small glen in front of a cliff face. 

I begin the approach. Slowly. But it doesn’t matter how light on my feet I am. Immediately, the ground undulates and bucks. The dirt moves like ocean water, pushed and pulled by some current from above. Or from below. Every step forward feels risky, like you’ll be swept to the ground. But you remain upright, swaying as you go. 

The trees mimic you, bending back and forth like they’re caught in an invisible gale, victim to torrential winds that you don’t feel at all. 

Taya looks at me, panicked. For a fleeting moment I think I should have been more honest. About the investigations. About the claws in the dark. About the months in the hospital. About James at the end of our relationship. The things I’ve never told anyone. 

But I cannot do this alone. 

So when the rockslide happens, the sheer cliff face trembling with perverse, unbridled excitement, I say nothing. When the rocks crash down upon us, the smallest pebbles to meteoric boulders, I don’t even scream. 

I grab Taya's hand, close my eyes, get buried.

*James, we’ll see you in hell.*
