It all started on a rainy Tuesday afternoon. I’ve been living in a small, somewhat isolated house near the edge of a forest close to Kassel for almost two years now. The garden is huge, overgrown, and full of old apple trees that I actually should have taken care of months ago. When the rain finally stopped around three in the afternoon, I put on my rubber boots to check around. In the back near the old elderberry bushes, right where the property line disappears in the thickest brush, something caught my eye.  
The wet ground had sunken in a circle about two feet wide. At first, I thought it was a badger burrow or maybe an old well whose wooden cover had rotted away over the decades. But when I pushed the thick weeds aside with my spade, my breath hitched. It wasn't a normal hole. The walls of the shaft were perfectly smooth, almost like driven into the ground by a giant drill, and they weren't made of firm dirt, but of a deep black, slightly wet material that didn't seem to reflect any light.  
I kneeled down in the wet mud and peered inside. Nothing. Just absolute, impenetrable darkness. I picked up a fist-sized pebble from the ground and dropped it right into the middle of the opening. I waited. One second. Two seconds. Five seconds. No noise. No thud on dirt, no splash of water. Nothing. The stone had simply vanished silently into the depth.  
As a skeptical, practical person, I couldn't let it go. The next evening, I bought two fifty-meter rolls of strong masonry rope and a heavy plumb weight at the hardware store. I wanted to know how deep this hollow space really was. I knotted the two ropes together, attached the weight to the end, and started lowering it slowly into the hole. The rope ran through my gloves meter by meter. Fifty meters. Seventy meters. A hundred meters. The weight pulled relentlessly further down, as if there was no bottom at all inside the earth.  
At around one hundred and twenty meters, the pull stopped sudden. I breathed out relieved. Finally, the lead had reached the ground. But when I wanted to tighten the rope to mark the exact depth, I felt something that froze the blood in my veins. It wasn't resistance from solid ground. It was a primitive, rhythmic pulling. Something on the other end of the fiber was picking up the weight. Tug. Tug. Tug. Like a regular heartbeat.  
Panic took over me. I started pulling the rope back up with both hands as fast as I could. The measure shrank, but the resistance got heavier from second to second, like I was fighting against a huge mass down there. When the knot where the two ropes were tied together came back to the surface, I heard it for the first time. A quiet, metallic scraping noise coming directly out of the black hole, followed by a wet, gurgling sound.  
With my last strength, I yanked the final meters of rope out and stumbled backwards onto the wet grass. The plumb weight slammed on the ground next to me. But it wasn't lead anymore. The heavy piece of metal had been cleanly cut off – the cut surface was melted smooth and was still glowing weakly in the dark. And on the silk rest of the rope stuck a thick, deep purple liquid that smelled like burnt hair.  
I ran into the house, locked all doors, and locked the windows. I spent the whole night trembling on the couch, holding my grandfather's shotgun tight. Outside, it stayed quiet. But when the first morning light came through the shutters today, I left the house with my heart pounding to cover the opening with heavy wooden boards. When I reached the elderberry bush, I almost had a stroke.  
The hole was gone. No disturbed soil, no drilling, nothing. Fresh green grass was growing right at that spot, like there was never anything there. I breathed out, convincing myself that the lack of sleep over the last weeks was playing tricks on me. But when I turned around to walk back to the house, my heart stopped. Right in the middle of my patio, directly in front of the sliding glass door to my living room, there is now a circular, perfectly smooth cutout in the concrete. And sticking out of the dark shaft is the top end of my cut rope.
