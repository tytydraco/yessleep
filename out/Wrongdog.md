*Bark!* 

"God damn it, why is Sadie outside?" 

I stand up from my bed, walk down the short hallway that leads into my living room. I glance at the neon glow of the oven clock as I pass through the kitchen. The numbers are too blurry to read. I decide that it doesn't matter what time it is. 

The linoleum sticks to my clammy feet as I make my way to the backdoor. The wind whistles through the worn seal between the door and its jamb. I hear rain pattering on the wooden boards of the deck. 

"Poor baby." 

I'm kicking myself for leaving her out as I swing the door open. She shuffles in from the dark, slow and awkward. I'm worried she might have hurt herself somehow. She's soaking wet. The water dripping off her splashes against my ankles. 

"Stay here, girl, I'll get you a towel," a foul odor reaches my nose, "and maybe a bath." 

I cross back through the kitchen, still too bleary eyed to read the time.  Through my living room and back into the bedroom. The adjoining bathroom is where I keep my towels. I choose one that's nice, but not too nice. I make my way back into the bedroom, and I stop there. 

Sitting in her bed, trembling and staring up at me with wide eyes, is Sadie. Perfectly dry and clean as can be. If you've got a dog, then you know how they can give you certain looks. The look Sadie is giving me says: 

*What have you done?*

A clatter of pans comes from the kitchen, followed by a loud retching. I rush back out. I'm expecting, hoping, it's a raccoon, or maybe a Coyote. What I'm not expecting, or hoping for is the six-foot tall border collie standing on its hind legs. It pulls its face from out of my pantry when I flip the lights on. Its pupils are bright yellow, nearly neon in the way they shine. Its irises are a shade of green that makes me want to puke. My eye is drawn down as a gob of bright orange bile drips from his drooping lips. 

I'm rooted to the spot. The saliva pools in large clumps that grow and shrink like marshmallows in the microwave. It's still looking at me when it starts retching again. 

Painful heaves writhe along its midsection. Black and white fur rippling visibly along with the flesh beneath them.  It opens its jaws wide, displaying rows of jagged teeth. 

A small shape, dark and covered with slime, surges up from its throat and out onto the floor. I can't recognize it for what it is, at first, but it becomes clear as the limbs begin to stir, and moreso as it begins to whine. A puppy. 

The dog ignores it, shoving its face violently back into my pantry and ripping into whatever it can find. I don't know what to do. Sadie walks gently over to stand beside me. I look around the kitchen, seeing more of the puppies lying all across the floor. The tar they're coated in holds them there tightly. 

After what feels like hours, but can't be more than minutes, it withdraws from the pantry. It stumbles over to the fridge, but doesn't seem to find anything there of interest. I watch, paralyzed as it continues rifling through every drawer of my kitchen. Finally, it turns to me and walks over on two legs. It vomits another puppy onto the floor. Then it looks at me, and the implication of a question fills the air. 

"Are you gonna eat that?" 

It takes a moment for me to realize it's referring to the puppy. It looks disgusting, a whining slimy ball of hair and bone and little else, yet some small part of me does want to eat it. I bend down and reach out to take it. Sadie bites me. Not roughly. Just enough to stop me, more like grabbing my hand with her teeth than a real bite. 

She looks at me one last time, then lowers her head and takes the puppy in her mouth. It pops loudly on the first chew, a flood of black tar slipping through Sadie's teeth. The dog-thing cocks its head. It seems pleased, and heads toward the door. Sadie follows after it, scarfing down its babies until there are none left. 

The dog fumbles at the doorknob with its strange paws and lets itself out into the night. Sadie walks sluggishly to her bed. She seems to be okay, but the whole time her eyes stay fixed on the floor. 

She didn't get up much after that. She mostly just sat there in bed for days, growing all the while. She'd almost doubled in size by the time I caught her looming over my bedside. She was standing there on two legs, staring at me. Her eyes looked so... sad.

 I screamed bloody murder when I saw her, and she ran off. 

I miss her, I truly do, but I can't deny. The sight of Sadie's empty bed, and the sound of wind rushing through the backdoor, came as a relief.
