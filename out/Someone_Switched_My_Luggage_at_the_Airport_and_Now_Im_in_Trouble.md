Hi Reddit, 

I need help. I’m not sure what to do. Something scary is happening and I think I’m being followed. 

About two weeks ago, I had the most traumatic and horrifying night of my life. 

I was returning from an international work trip, and had finally landed at my home airport. It was a red-eye flight, and we landed at about 3:25am. Thankfully, the airport wasn’t too crowded, as there were only a few other flights connecting at that time. 

After I deplaned, I rolled my carryon to the luggage carousels to grab my checked bag. Unfortunately, there was a bit of a delay, and the luggage didn’t start appearing till after 4am. I was exhausted, and standing near the end of the carousel. There weren’t many other people there, so as soon as I saw my black Luggex come around the bend, I grabbed it. 

I started hauling my bags toward the exit. TSA agents stood at the gates and were directing people through, while waving the occasional person off to a different line. I prayed the agent would wave me through. It was nearing 5am, and I was desperate to get home and get a few hours of sleep. 

As I approached the gate, I slowed to a crawl as I followed the man ahead of me. He had dark hair and kept impatiently checking his watch. The TSA agent waved him through, and he exited the gate.  

“Please follow the line to your right,“ the TSA agent said to me, blocking my exit and pointing towards the right. I groaned internally. 

“What for? I can show you my passport if that helps?” 

“Sorry, ma’am. We’re doing mandatory secondary screenings and you have been randomly selected.”

The man who had gone through ahead of me had turned around and was watching the interaction. He looked like he was hoping for a scene. I sighed, and got a better grip on my belongings, before wheeling my bags down the aisle on the right. 

I followed the railing, and came to another TSA agent, who was standing beside a long table. 

“Good morning. You’ve been randomly selected for a secondary screening. Participation is mandatory. If you have any concerns, you can ask to speak with a supervisor. Please place your luggage on the table,” she recited in a bored tone.She pursed her lips. 

I sighed and heaved my check bag on the table first, then threw my carryon beside her. 

“Is this your own luggage?” The agent asked. 

“Yes,” I replied. 

“Did you pack your own bags, and were they ever out of your sight?” 

“Yeah, I packed it myself.” I patted the check bag. “This one was checked, but I grabbed it right off the carousel.”

The TSA nodded. She started with my carryon bag and quickly unzipped it. I blushed as it opened and revealed my dirty clothes, including some underwear I had thrown right on top.

She removed all the clothes, then emptied the pockets, and ran her gloved hand along the inside seams of the bag.

“You can repack on that side of the table,” the agent said, as she slid my belongings over. 

I quickly shoved my dirty underwear into an inner pocket, then started grabbing a handful of other stuff.  

“What the fuck!” The agent gasped. I looked up at her, to see her staring inside my Luggex. The agent held the lid half-opened and it blocked my view. But her face was pale and drawn, and her lips suddenly looked bright red. 

“What?” I asked, my stomach twisting in a strange way. I started to move towards the luggage, but the agent’s head snapped up. 

“Get on the ground, now!” She screamed. There were a few other pedestrians in the adjacent lines and they looked over. At the agent’s shout, other TSA agents came sprinting over. It all happened so quickly. 

My agent lunged towards me when I froze in place, and as she moved, she dropped the lid of my Luggex open. In the moment between her lunging towards me, and tackling me to the ground, I saw what caused her horror. 

The bag appeared to be lined with plastic. Body parts rested in a pile of oozing fluid. A hand stuck out at an awkward angle, limp and grey. 

The agent’s body landed on mine and I barely had time to cry out, before someone else flipped me over. 

People were screaming, and my body was jerked around. The agents were yelling instructions at me, but my brain felt like it was shutting down. My limbs wouldn’t react and I couldn’t comprehend what I saw. 

My arms were forced behind my back, my shoulder threatening to pop. A loud, “you’re under arrest,” rang out in the chaos. Handcuffs were snagged around my wrists. 

My right cheek was smushed against the dirty floor, and a pebble was pressing in the delicate skin near my eye. I could see people’s legs running around, and as I twisted my eyes as far up as they could go, I could see that grey hand in my luggage, reaching towards the sky. 

Very quickly, I ended up detained in an interview room at the airport. I felt shockingly calm, as everyone ran around in shambles around me. Hours passed as I sat in that room, handcuffed to the table. 

I stared at the clock on the wall. When it ticked its way to 9:32am, the door opened and an officer walked in. 

“Good morning, Eloise,” the officer said as he took a seat across from me. He looked kind, with bright blue eyes, greying hair and a stiff moustache. 

“It’s Ellie,” I responded, though my nickname at this point probably didn’t matter. 

“Oh, sorry. Ellie.”  The officer cleared his throat. “I’m Detective Carson. I’m sure you can understand that I have a few questions about your luggage.” 

“It’s not mine,” I responded, numbly, staring at my hands. The handcuffs cut into my wrists and large splotchy bruises had appeared. I yanked on them slightly, and the cuffs rubbed into my raw skin. 

Detective Carson frowned at my movement. He leaned over the table, and produced a small key, opening the cuffs. 

I let out a soft cry, that turned into a choking cough as I pulled my hands towards my body, and held them close. I rubbed my wrists and tears prickled in my eyes. 

“It’s not mine,” I whimpered, and the floodgates opened. My chest tightened, and my breath came out in heaving gasps. Tears ran down my face, and I began to hyperventilate. The shock of what happened suddenly set in.

Detective Carson reached once more across the table, and laid a gentle hand on my clenched fists. 

“Ellie,” he said, staring at me. “We know it’s not yours. We found your actual luggage.” 

I let out a strangled sniffle, and aggressively wiped my nose. 

Detective Carson got up and walked to a row of counters on the opposite side of the room. Opening one revealed a mini fridge, and he removed a bottle of water. He fetched a box of Kleenexes as well, and brought both over to me. 

I wiped my nose with a soggy tissue and stared at him with my sore eyes. “Can you tell me what happened?” I asked. 

The detective nodded, “After you were detained, and the police were called, we did an initial search. We found an identical piece of black luggage, which had a piece of your ID in it.”

Carson scratched his chin before continuing. “The luggage with the remains had your luggage tag on it. The luggage with your ID in it had a blank luggage tag. We believe someone switched the tags.”

I let out a wavering sigh of relief. They knew it wasn’t mine. 

Carson went on, “I need you to recount the events leading up to the luggage collection.”

I nodded, and rubbed my wrists again. I took a small sip of the icy water, and it soothed my throat. “I don’t know how much to tell. After I got off the flight, I just went straight to the luggage area.”

“I was standing near the end of the carousel, and when I saw my bag, I just grabbed it. I didn’t think to check my tag.”

The detective nodded. “Are there any other details you remember?”

I closed my eyes and tried to recount every step I took. But in my exhaustion, I couldn’t draw forward any other details. I opened my eyes and shook my head at Carson. 

“That’s okay. I’m sure it’s been a stressful night.” Detective Carson stood up. “We are not detaining you, and you are free to return home. You must remain available, should we have any other questions for you.”

The detective reached into his pocket and pulled out a card. “If you think of anything, no matter how mundane, please reach out to us. Tracy will confirm your contact information, then you may go home.”

The rest of the night after that was not nearly as important, and to be perfectly honest, it was a bit of a blur. 

I took a taxi home, and brought all my real luggage inside, throwing it in my entryway. I locked my doors and crawled into bed. My alarm clock flashed bright numbers at me. It was almost noon. 

I passed out nearly as soon as my head hit the pillow. 

Around 8:30pm, I briefly woke long enough to glance at the clock and see the time. I thought I heard a noise outside, but my mind bled my dreams and reality into one. 

I woke the next morning at the crack of dawn. I pulled myself out of bed, feeling an ache and pain in every bone. I moaned softly. 

I decided to call my brother over. The events of the past day were flooding me, and I needed to get it off my chest. He arrived within the hour.

“Hey, your front door was unlocked,” Steven called, as he pushed his way through my front door. 

I frowned. “I’m pretty sure I locked it last night, but I was so exhausted, I honestly can’t remember.”

“Long flight?” He asked cheerfully, running his hand through his thick brown hair. People often said we looked a lot alike, with our straight noses, brown hair, and fair complexions. Steven was nearly a foot taller than me though, at 6’3”. 

I waved him to the couch and handed him a cup of freshly brewed coffee. “You have no idea,” I said. 

Steven frowned. 

“Sit down,” I said. “This is a long story.”

I told Steven in great detail the events of last night. By the end, I was shaking all over again, and my eyes burned, but I didn’t cry. 

Steven sat quietly. “What the actual fuck?” He finally exclaimed. 

“I know,” I whispered as I fingered a hole in my threadbare couch. 

“And Ellie!” He jumped up and stared at me, “you didn’t lock your fucking door last night! What a dangerous situation!”

“I thought I did,” I responded quietly. “I mean, it’s not really a dangerous situation. I’m not at the airport anymore.”

“I don’t care about the airport!” Steven started pacing. “Some fucking maniac chopped up a bunch of people and stuffed em into a luggage bag. They PURPOSEFULLY SWITCHED YOUR NAME TAG. Why?”

I winced at Steven’s words. I hadn’t thought that deeply into the situation. “I don’t know,” I responded honestly. 

Steven stared at me with a piercing gaze. “They wanted you to carry the bag out. Or, somebody. But the point is, you were gonna take the bag out, and then they were gonna take it back from you.”

I tightened my grip on my coffee mug and my knuckles turned white. I suddenly remembered the noise I heard last night and knew with a gutting feeling, that it was not part of my dream. I said so to Steven. 

“Jesus Christ El. Call the police. I’m gonna look around.” Steven stalked out of the house as I phoned the police, my finger trembling. 

As I hung up the phone, Steven returned inside, his face pale. 

“What’s wrong?” I twisted my fingers. 

“El, there’s a footprint outside your window.”

“What?” My stomach clenched and I nervously twisted my hair.

Steven led me outside and I stared in horror at the imprint of a shoe tread, freshly pressed into the ground, facing my bedroom window. 

A few hours later, the police had come and gone, stating there was nothing they could do tonight. They suggested if I heard or saw anything else, to call them back, but otherwise make sure my doors and windows were locked. 

Steven stayed with me the rest of the weekend, but come Monday, he had to go to work. There were no other sounds or anything to scare me. 

Monday was a relatively normal day at work. I gave a report on my work trip, socialized with my coworkers and collected details for my next project. I stayed late, catching up on emails. And partially because I didn’t want to go home just yet. 

Finally, after dusk, I decided to pack up my belongings and leave. I made my way out of the office building, and as I closed the door behind me, I heard a soft scrape from around the corner. It sounded like a footstep. 

I froze and urged my breathing to slow. I turned my head and concentrated on where I thought I heard the sound. Nothing else happened. 

I looked around the parking lot and saw someone exit another one of the buildings across the lot. I turned from my office, and hurried in the direction I saw the person. 

A louder scrape sounded behind me and I broke into a run. Heavy footsteps sounded behind me and I dove behind a car. I crouched low, trying to control my panting breath. 

There was silence for a long moment. Then, a loud crack pierced the air. A soft thump followed, and more silence. 

I continued breathing quietly and waited what felt like forever. After a long ten minutes, no other sounds appeared. I remained low, and crept to the next car, peering over its hood. 

There was no one around. It appeared as though the other person had left too. I let out a gentle sigh, and rose upright, continuing to scan the area. 

As I glanced around, my gaze fell upon a mound a few cars away. My heart sunk as I crept a few cars closer. My hand trailed across their hoods and helped guide me through the dark lot.

A woman lay on the ground, blood pooling underneath her. She had long dark hair, and looked to be about my height. A bullet wound pierced her chest. Her eyes stared blankly at the sky. 

I leaned over the car and my vomit splattered on the ground beside me. 

When I recovered, I made my second call to the police in nearly as many days. The police arrived shortly after. The woman’s body had been removed, and Steven stood beside me, hugging my shoulders. 

“I’m sorry, Ellie,” he whispered, in an attempt to comfort me. I felt numb and just nodded against him. 

“Do you want to go home?” He asked, pushing me out to arm’s reach, and holding my shoulders. I nodded again. 

Steven took me home and slept on the couch. We both took a few days off work and Steven stayed with me. 

On Thursday, Steven and I were sitting at the breakfast table nursing coffees when my phone rang. I glanced at it, and saw a private number. I looked at Steven and he shrugged. 

After a moment’s hesitation, I answered. 

“Good morning, is this Eloise?” A familiar voice questioned. 

“Yah, this is she.”

“Hi Ellie. You may remember me. I’m Detective Carson, who was investigating the incident at the airport last week.”

I shared a startled look with Steven, who waved at me to continue talking. 

“Uh, yes. Hi, Detective. How can I help you?”

“Well actually, I just had an update on the investigation, and thought you would appreciate knowing. We have arrested a suspect and it doesn’t look like we will have anymore questions for you.” 

I let loose a relieved sigh and Steven rubbed his face and gave me a thumbs up. 

“Oh, well thank you so much for telling me, Detective Carson. I appreciate that.” I exchanged goodbyes with the detective and hung up, turning my attention back to Steven. 

“Oh, thank God,” I said to him, dropping my head into my arms. 

“Sounds like your nightmare is over,” Steven said with a grin. 

“And my therapy is about to begin,” I retorted, lifting my head back up. I gave a terse smile. 

Steven laughed. He ended up spending a few more hours with me before heading back to his own house. 

For the first time in almost two weeks, everything seemed like it’d be okay. After having Steven take up so much space in my house the last little bit, it seemed suddenly empty after he left. I made a meal for one, and felt like I could finally breathe. 

I did mean what I said to Steven about therapy. After dinner, I sat on the couch with my laptop, and scrolled through my benefits page. Surely, seeing a bag full of body parts, being falsely detained, then witnessing a murder has a lingering effect on a person. The thought was darkly humorous and I snorted quietly.   
The night remained uneventful, and in sweet boredom, I eventually made my way to bed. I lay propped up against my pillow, and doom scrolled for about an hour. 

A scratch on my window had me bolting upright, and slamming my laptop shut. I clenched my sheets and peered across my bedroom, heart hammering. A flash of a person’s silhouette flickered across the dark glass, before disappearing.

Steven is back on my couch. It’s nearly 2am, and he is sleeping quietly. I’m awake. I can’t sleep and am writing this out.

So, tell me, Reddit. If the murderer was arrested, who was skulking outside my window?
