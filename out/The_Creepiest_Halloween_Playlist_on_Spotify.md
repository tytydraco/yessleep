Halle and I had only been living in Brunswick, Georgia for a few months. We moved here from Tallahassee over the summer and honestly, we missed home. But hey, the area had grown on us. We liked the beaches and the culture. But one place we hadn’t checked out yet was Jekyll Island. Jekyll had a mystique to it to say the least. It had history given that it once served as a vacation destination for many of America’s wealthy elites during The Gilded Age. The opportunity to visit said island arose when my coworker-turned-buddy Robert invited us over to his family’s vacation home right there on Jekyll for a Halloween party.

Now, Halle and I *loved* Halloween. This would be our first one away from Tallahassee so hey, this would be something different given it would also serve as our first ever visit to Jekyll. So on the evening of October 31st, we dressed in our costumes: I was Count Dracula, Halle as Dr. John Hammond from *Jurassic Park*.

Around six-thirty, Halle and I made the drive from Brunswick. The last week had brought about a cool front with a chilling breeze that I knew would only increase once we reached Jekyll. We made it pretty far while discussing our favorite movie in the *Halloween* franchise besides John Carpenter’s original (she was set on 1981’s immediate sequel while I preferred the 2018 version). With our windows rolled down, I actually felt the autumn air transition from cool to outright cold as I made the turn on to the causeway that led to the island. In addition to the breeze, we could hear the roar of the waves from that rough but beautiful Atlantic Ocean. Driving down the causeway all I saw surrounding us was endless marshland and a fading sun.

“Put on some music,” Halle remarked.

“Music?” I said to her with a smile. “Let me guess-”

She gave me a light punch on the arm. “Halloween music, Peter. Come on.”

“I got it.” I was tempted to pull up my own playlist from YouTube but I settled on Spotify. Instantly, a hundred Halloween playlists greeted me. I stole a glance back at the two-lane blacktop while we crept along at around thirty miles an hour. “Alright, I got one.”

“Sweet,” she remarked in her deep voice.

So I pressed play on the random playlist. I didn’t even look at it long enough to see what the exact title was other than a few key words I noted such as ‘Spooky Szn’ and ‘2026 creepiest playlist’. I didn’t know who or what uploaded it. I only even glanced at the thumbnail that showed an AI generated image featuring Frankenstein’s Monster, Dracula, The Wolf Man, and other Universal horror monsters seated around a bonfire. It would’ve been a cool cover for a 1960s Halloween album if the AI hadn’t made it look so glossy.

“Let’s hear it, Dracula,” Halle said.

I reached over and adjusted the volume knob. We were hit by the familiar but eerie guitar riffs of Blue Oyster Cult’s ‘(Don’t Fear) The Reaper’. I heard Halle’s guffaw of laughter.

“That’s an obvious one,” she said. With a smirk, she placed a hand on my shoulder and leaned in a little closer so her laughter would sound even louder (at least, that was how I interpreted the gesture). “You probably just picked the most basic playlist.”

“I mean… it’s my favorite Halloween song,” I teased her.

“Yeah, yeah, Dracula.”

“Hey, that’s *Count* Dracula,” I teased her further.

She just shook her head, although she did it with a smile. I felt even better when she started singing along to the chorus with me. This was four minutes of bliss complete with head banging, Halle on the air drums, and me ripping some air guitar during the solo (while keeping at least one hand on the wheel during the drive on this seemingly-endless causeway).

“Man, I so need a drink when we get there,” Halle said during the song’s fadeout.

Laughing, I caressed her shoulder. I stole a glance toward her at first until the surrounding marshland drew my attention. “For sure,” I replied. I heard Halle groan and didn’t need to guess why when I too noticed something a tad too commercial out here in this landscape of gorgeous scenery.  
“Ugh, that’s so ugly,” she said.

“I agree,” I said seconds after laying eyes on that eyesore of a billboard. On it was the grinning face of a bearded old guy giving me the thumbs up signal. I could only assume he was Cameron Hawkins given that name and the phrase ‘The Best Personal Injury Lawyer in The Golden Isles’ were the only words plastered across this monstrosity. Yeah, it was corny but I found it more disheartening to see such crass commercialism starting to creep on to Jekyll Island of all places. But hey, as Halle and I talked, we both agreed we wouldn’t let the occasional billboard tarnish our expectations on what would be our first experience here.

“Okay, so what’s next,” Halle said while playfully running her hands together. Her smile electrified me. Hell, it excited me. So much so that I heard nothing except our collective heartbeats during the very few seconds between the end of ‘(Don’t Fear) The Reaper’ and whatever awaited us next on this playlist-

 Only it wasn’t a song but an ad. There was a faux rap song that quickly transitioned into an awful jingle for ‘The Best Personal Injury Lawyer in The Golden Isles’. I cracked up even before Cameron Hawkins and his upbeat voice introduced himself.

“Wow…” Halle said with a grin. She stole a glance behind us and I knew she was looking back at the billboard that was the only one out here on the causeway. “That’s kinda strange,” she said before facing me. “I mean we just saw his ad.”

“Yeah, he has a few in Brunswick.”

“It’s just weird timing.” She shrugged and rubbed her faux white beard in playful contemplation. “Coincidence, I think not…”

As we laughed, we were greeted by the next song on the playlist: Warren Zevon’s ‘Werewolves of London’. The *ahh-hoo*s made us both crack up. Halle snapped her fingers. “Another obvious one.”

“I know, I know…” I played along.  
She shook her head. “You got to do better, Peter. I’m disappointed given our collective horror knowledge.”

I shrugged in return. “It’s supposed to be the ultimate Halloween playlist.”

“We could’ve done better.” She slouched back in her seat. I gave the steering wheel a light pat.

Now I saw where the marsh was giving way to civilization… or at least the closest thing to civilization here on Jekyll. I saw a sign advertising the historic district and then we saw a gas station called Zachary’s on the left. “I do like this one,” I said. Once Halle looked over at me, I gave my own *ahh-hoo* to match the chorus.

“I mean it’s good but overplayed on these playlists,” she commented. I followed her gaze up ahead as we neared a very random stop sign. For the first time, I noticed just how quiet Jekyll really was. I saw no other cars on the road. Hell, I didn’t even see any at Zachary’s either. I caught another chill. I instinctively turned on my headlights given how much the sun had lowered throughout this drive. “Next, they’ll probably play ‘Thriller,’” she quipped.

“Yep.” I came to a stop. Now I could make out a few more buildings up ahead. Not to mention I could make out a parking lot that led down to what was likely always a quiet beach. “Maybe Robert will play some deeper cuts. He’ll, uh, let us choose a few songs.” I quickly sent a text message to Robert to say we were almost there.

“Oh, is there karaoke?”

I cruised ahead. “I think he mentioned it. He’s always singing at Little Zook’s anyway.”

“Oh, you’re right.”

Once ‘Werewolves of London’ faded away, a brief silence let us hear the wind roaring from the ocean. Now that we were closer to the buildings, I could make them out more clearly. I saw a sign proclaiming that this collection of bars and boutiques was Beach Village. It was definitely cute. The Tortuga Jack’s bar particularly caught my eye given it had a miniature tiki hut out front. The tiki bar even had fake cobwebs hanging all over it. Before I could comment to Halle, yet another ad disrupted our playlist. Both of us groaned in unison.

“Every damn song there’s an ad, man,” Halle complained.

I was about to respond when the sounds of fast waves and even faster cars played over the sounds of generic surf rock. For an advertisement, the music wasn’t too bad. I had just cracked a grin when a radio announcer with a baritone voice started pushing the positives for Jekyll Island’s number one gas station: Zachary’s. Before I caught yet another chill, I felt Halle snatch my arm in a death grip. I didn’t need to turn to see her wide open eyes locked in on me. Uneasy, wide open eyes.

“Okay, now that’s weird,” she said.

I nodded. My unease made me cling tighter to the wheel. Not even the cute caricatures of the sun, the sand, or of cocktails painted on the windows of Beach Village’s storefronts nor all of the glowing jack o’lanterns on display could comfort me from what I couldn’t say for sure but just *sensed* was more than a mere coincidence. “I mean,” I tried to reason, “maybe these are popular ads.”

“It’s still weird.” Halle collapsed back in her seat and folded her arms. “It just proves these fucking apps trace our every move.”

“Well, I can’t argue with that.” I glanced at my phone in hopes of getting a reply from Robert. Instead, all I saw was Spotify transitioning us from the Zachary’s commercial to the next song on the playlist: the eerie *A Nightmare on Elm Street* theme by Charles Bernstein. Needless to say, this one didn’t help my nerves any.

“Have you heard from him?” Halle asked as if reading my mind.

“Naw.” I made another turn that took us down Beachview Drive. From here, we had a pretty good view of the ocean… or more or less what was a mysterious abyss in the impenetrable darkness. There was no full moon nor stars to illuminate the murky depths of the Atlantic. Even the lights in the parking lots for the beaches seemed dim. Hell, the only way that I knew we were near a beach were the crashing waves we heard even over the *Elm Street* theme. “I think he said we go down Beachview a little. He’s on Hollybourne Road.”

“Yeah.” Halle ran her hand rhythmically through that beard in what I knew was a nervous tic. “The Freddy Krueger music isn’t helping, man.”

“It damn sure isn’t.”

The theme abruptly ended to give way to a hoppy synthesizer beat reminiscent of a carnival ride. I didn’t have to glance at the playlist to know that this wasn’t a song…

“For the cheapest drinks on Jekyll, come down to Tortuga Jack’s!” proclaimed a woman’s glowing voice.

“What the fuck, man!” Halle said before she glanced back toward Beach Village. Back toward where we had just passed Tortuga Jack’s moments earlier. “How’s it following us!”

But then the chills went away, or at least faded somewhat, when I saw a green sign up ahead: Hollybourne Road. I showed a weak smile. I could even see lights on that road glowing from the inside of several pretty houses.

“Alright, there it is!” Halle celebrated. “Now we’re cooking.”

“Yeah, let me call him.” I made the turn just as ‘Spiders & Snakes’ by Jim Stafford started playing. I proceeded to call Robert-

*But the call didn’t go through.* It didn’t ring once. “Shit…” I muttered.

“What’s wrong?” said Halle.

I struggled to keep my eyes on the road given the rising adrenaline. The rising dread that matched the swampy beat of Stafford’s country hit. A dread that also only accelerated when I saw where my last text to Robert apparently didn’t send. “This doesn’t make sense.”

“I don’t have any service,” Halle said.

Those were words I didn’t want to hear. I now saw where I had no service either.

*I don’t like spiders and snakes* sang Jim…

Yet the playlist kept going. I looked around our surroundings even as Halle dug her fingers into my shoulder. She leaned in closer to me, the force of her movement stretching her seatbelt as far as possible. “Peter, where’s his house!” she said.

The beach houses all looked the same. The garages were all closed even when the lights in each of the homes were on. The Halloween decorations that ranged from tall skeletons to a Michael Myers figurine to a collection of swinging ghosts would’ve normally amused me but not when I was already this fucking scared. “It’s a party,” I stammered, “so there’s gotta be like cars and shit.”

Then both of us saw it: a brick house at the very end of a cul-de-sac. There were several vehicles parked out front and the front door was wide open. Not to mention there were plenty of Halloween decorations here including a fake cemetery and a beach chair with a sign that stated it was reserved for The Invisible Man. Okay, so I did grin at that one.

“Pull in!” Halle said and patted me on the shoulder for good measure.

I started to pull into the driveway but swerved to the side slightly *after I saw two people standing right there by the road*. I stopped just a few feet away from them. They stood still and didn’t say a word. I had no idea what their age was or anything else given one of them was dressed as a spider and one was dressed as a snake. They each wore a psychedelic mask. Neither of them said a word.

Jim Stafford’s ‘Spiders & Snakes’ now sounded amplified. Hell, I heard it overpower our car speakers. I caught a chill when I traced the *louder* version toward the front door of Robert’s house. This ‘Spiders & Snakes’ aligned with ours. He was playing the same goddamn playlist…

Before I could act on what I knew was the right instincts and drive out of here, Halle pointed me back toward the two partygoers standing nearby. Their spider and snake masks didn’t move just as their bodies didn’t. But I knew those two were looking right at us…

“Just go!” Halle said to me.

“You don’t have to tell me twice,” I replied. Both of us looked back toward the house: but emerging from the darkness was Dr. Frankenstein himself. A man in a white coat, pale make-up, and hair in complete disarray. It was Robert. He wore a costume but his fear was genuine. He swung his arms in the air and while I couldn’t hear him over the music, I knew he was begging us for help.

It was only a millisecond later that Halle and I saw why: *more figures emerged from the house*. Someone or something in costumes. We saw Freddy Krueger and a tall werewolf stop on the front porch. The werewolf even wore a Union Jack coat to complete the Werewolf of London outfit. They stood still like the Spider and Snake people… *They too were watching us*.

Halle and I exchanged uneasy eye contact. My sweaty hands gripped the wheel and my foot started to hover off the brake pedal. Yet the closer Robert got to us, the more I heard his shrill scream:

*“Peter, wait!” he cried into the night. “Please!”*

“Goddammit!” I heard a conflicted Halle say. When she looked back toward the window on her side, I did the same-

*Just in time to see Robert slam his hands against the glass*. Blood-stained hands. His big eyes pleaded for help as much as his cracking voice did.

“Get me out of here, man! Please!” he cried.

“Shit, get in the backseat!” I yelled at him. I stole a glance back at the Spider and Snake and neither one of them had moved an inch.

“Yes, hurry!” said Halle.

Robert started to do what we said. He reached over toward the backseat-

*When a scythe smashed through his head and pinned him right into the window on Halle’s side*. The edge of a blade tailor-made for Poe’s ‘Pit and the Pendulum’ shot through Robert’s forehead and pierced through the window to leave all of the glass shaking and on the verge of shattering. Halle and I looked on in shock and fright. Robert’s blood was all over us. Through the many cracks of that window, we could still make out my friend’s face forever frozen in fear while the scythe was lodged into his head like a hurled javelin.

But it was what I saw behind him that didn’t so much shock as terrify us. *A Grim Reaper was holding the scythe handle*. I couldn’t see his face under the cloak’s black hood and I damn sure didn’t want to. The sheer power of ‘Spiders & Snakes’ made that window shake more. And when it finally collapsed, Halle and I would be at the mercy of the Grim Reaper and what I now realized were literal recreations of all of the characters we’d heard on this Spotify playlist from hell.

Halle made sure we weren’t going to wait on them. She snatched my arm. “Peter, go!”

As the Grim Reaper struggled to pull his scythe back out, I hit the gas pedal! I did a hard swerve in the cul-de-sac. I heard Halle cry out. I heard Robert’s corpse collapse against our car while his body stayed pinned to the window. I heard ‘Spiders & Snakes’ continue on what had to be an endless loop. And worst of all, I heard *every single one* of those costumed characters come charging after us.

“Go faster!” Halle cried. She kept looking on behind us. I kept hearing all of the costumed killers and their footsteps even over the Halloween music. But I did what I was told: I floored it.
