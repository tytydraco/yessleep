We pulled into Peattie's a little after 9 p.m. on Saturday. Being at Peattie's that late was not strange in itself: Peattie's Pawn 'Til Dawn didn't even open until 8 p.m. in the winter and 10 p.m. in the summer and remained open—as the name suggests—"'til dawn". But we were there to rob the place, even though we all knew that you don't fuck with Peattie's. We didn't know why at the time, but it was just something that was known: a truism, a superstition, a myth like "don't swim after eating" or "don't feed a fever": 

Don't fuck with Peattie's.

After that night, we all knew why. Except for Don. He probably didn't have the time to think about it.

We were at Peattie's to get Don's ring. Why Peattie had Don's ring and why we needed to get it back so bad is a long story, and I need to keep this short because I'm not sure how much longer they'll let me keep my phone. Cops have been in and out of my hospital room with lots of questions, and none of them have liked my answers, I can tell.

It was me, Don, and Gale. The plan was: Don and I would go in, convince Peattie to part with the ring, and get out. Not just out of the pawn shop, but out of town for good. We'd all had enough of that town anyway, but we were not about to stick around and find out what happened to those who fucked with Peattie. I thought we could get the thing with just me and Don, but Don insisted on Gale as driver. Gale had driven for a few serious crews out of Federal Way, and had a reputation for being fast and smart, and Don insisted that if things went bad, we would need a driver with both those qualities. And things went bad.

Don brought his big scary gun, his Taurus Raging Judge—everyone seemed to listen to Don when he held that thing in their face. But Peattie wouldn't listen. Don was reasonable, told Peattie there was no need for anyone's pawn shop to get busted up or burn to the ground, so long as the owner of that shop would just hand over the little old gold ring with the big red stone. Don said that's all we needed and we'd be out of Peattie's hair—which, this last bit was an unnecessary jab at Peattie, who didn't have much hair to speak of, and was known to be self-conscious about it. But Peattie just smiled at us and kept shrugging like he had no idea what ring we were talking about. And so Don reached over the counter and grabbed Peattie by the collar of his Hawaiian shirt and stuck the Judge in his face and told him to stop fucking around and hand over the ring please and thank you.

And this is where my brain gets fuzzy and weird, because next thing I know Don and I are taking off our masks. Peattie asked us to take off our masks, and at the time, as I'm thinking back to it, it seemed like the thing to do, like I *wanted* to take off my mask. And then Peattie was on the other side of the counter all of a sudden, next to Don. And then he went for Don's neck. *Bit* Don in the neck, and the bite was so big that when Peattie pulled away, I could see there was barely any neck left, and Don's head wobbled and then flipped *upside down* with not enough neck to support the weight of his head. And God there was so much blood then. Don was still standing somehow with his head hanging down like that and the blood was spraying out like his body was a fire hydrant.

Wherever Don is now, I hope he can forgive me, because I got the hell out of there. Gale was waiting in the alley with the car idling and one back door of the car open on the passenger side and I dove in and told him to drive, and boy did he drive. He put the car in gear and I heard the tires scream and claw at the wet asphalt as he accelerated out of the alley and through the parking lot. I must have been screaming too because Gale kept looking back at me in the rearview. And then I caught part of my own reflection in the mirror and I looked like prom night Carrie. I tried to wipe some of Don's blood off my face with the hem of my shirt but the shirt was already so saturated with blood that it couldn't absorb any more, so I must have just smeared it around my face a bit.

Gale asked me where Don was, but I didn't answer. I could hear myself chanting "ohfuckohfuckohfuck" not knowing what else to say or do, just trying to keep my mind filled with something other than the image of Don and Peattie and Don's fucked up neck.

"Don's gone," I told him finally, and he looked back at me, and I could see he was sorry for me. He knew what Don meant to me.

And then I saw the silver Honda Civic stopped at the red light just ahead and my eyes went wide and I yelled at Gale to watch the road. He just turned the wheel slightly, almost casually as though he had seen it the whole time, and had always planned to avoid it at the last second. In fact, I knew he had seen it. Probably had clocked it 5 car lengths away or more, when the light was still yellow, and he knew it would stop just about when and where it did stop. This was Gale's "thing", what made him a legend with every crew north of Portland. He could see several moves ahead: see the cars in all lanes in all directions, and the intersections, which ones had stop signs and which had yield signs, and where the traffic lights were and how far away and which color, so that he could judge based on the color and timing of lights and number of cars at each part of the intersection and know exactly when to turn where to avoid this or that car and cut right between them like an alpine skier.

"Jesus Christ!" I yelled at him, even though I knew all this, knew he was a pro—it still scared the shit out of me the way he drove. We shot past the Civic, through the red light, and I could feel us decelerate slightly as a large pickup coming from our left passed just in front of us, and then felt the acceleration as we passed a foot or so in front of a police car entering the intersection from the right.

I heard Gale mutter, "Shit," when he saw the cop. I looked at him and could see the gears turning in his head and I knew—we both knew—this was a crucial moment where we had to be fast and smart or we were fucked. But guys like Gale live for moments like this, they pray for them. He glanced at the rearview mirror, and I looked back too and saw the cop swerve to the right and point his headlights towards us, and then get T-boned by a car that had been following too close behind the cop.

And then we were really going fast and I realized I still wasn't wearing my seatbelt so I scrambled to find it as I felt the speed trying to push me back into the seat and heard the V6 struggling to meet Gale's demands. Did I mention we were in a 2009 Kia Spectra of all cars? It was like an ego thing of Gale's. Anyone with a fast car could go fast, but he liked to challenge himself with boring, underperforming cars: compact Kias, PT Cruisers, old Priuses. Plus they didn't stand out: it was easy to disappear in a car that looked like everyone else's. All this I learned from Gale who wouldn't shut up about stuff like this if you got him going.

The siren sounded behind us and the night was lit in red and blue flashes. We had a narrow window now. We had caught the cop off guard, and plus the guy would be recovering from the shock of the hit from the other car. It would take the cop a moment to get up to full speed, but not much more than that.

And then I saw the opening—Gale would have seen it long before. Heavy cross traffic ahead at the next intersection moving east and west. A gap in the traffic was coming, one or two car lengths, and if he could time it right, he could slip right in. And he timed it right. He accelerated as we approached the intersection—another red light—moved around a short line of stopped cars, more acceleration, and then an abrupt change in direction and velocity as he pulled the parking brake and slid parallel to the cross traffic and just through the two-car gap. Once through, he disengaged the brake and accelerated again and was off and heading west now, driving on the shoulder until we reached the head of the pack, and then quickly cutting across the eastbound lane into the parking lot of a small strip mall. He rolled into a parking spot, killed the lights, put the car in park and took his foot off the brake. The brake lights went dark, and we both looked back just in time to see the cop fly by going west, oblivious, sirens wailing.

The cars that had pulled over to the shoulder to let the cop past moved on and for a moment it was quiet, and Gale sat there like a stone gargoyle, but I was breathing hard and for the first time I was conscious of the blood smell. I'd never smelled blood like that but knew that's what it was. What else could it be? The parking lot was dark except for a pair of shoebox lights in the center of the lot, and Gale had parked well away from them.

Gale glanced at me in the rearview and asked me if I was OK. I looked back at him and caught sight of my own reflection again and could see that I was crying now—when had I started crying?—the tears drawing little parallel tracks through the blood. My face looked like a very realistic watercolor painting.

"What the hell happened?" he asked.

And I tried to tell him, tried to form the images and then translate the images into words, but I was having a bad time of it.

"I...he...Don, he...we..." I mumbled, stuttered, couldn't get anything out but pronouns.

And then Gale looked past me, like he had seen something.

Gale reached down and turned the key in the ignition, his eyes still looking back over my shoulder, and the car came alive again. I followed his gaze and turned and saw a shadow stretching long, past the shoebox lights towards the car. I followed the black line of the shadow past the lights to a dark figure walking out of the road and into the parking lot. The shadow was all wrong though. The figure hadn't even passed under the lights yet but was nevertheless casting its shadow out from the darkness, into the light, and then back out again into the darkness on the other side.

Gale put the car in gear, backed out of the space, and pointed the car back towards the east-west road. The figure emerged from the darkness and into the light of the shoeboxes. A short stocky man materialized with a "U" of black hair on top of his head and a large bald patch in the center of the "U". The bald patch of pale skin looked wet and reflected the lamplight. The man was covered in blood too, even more than I was from the looks of it—which was saying something. I forgot how to breathe and speak for a moment, and looked to Gale hoping he would see in my eyes that we needed to go and now. Gale squinted past me.

"Is that...Peattie?" Gale asked. I tried to say "yes" but what came out of my throat was a terrible, high-pitched, choked, screeching sound that I've made mental note to never make again. The sound made Gale flinch, but he got the message.

The engine sounded real mad as we flew across the empty parking lot, and as we reached the road, Gale looked quickly to his left towards the light, and I looked too, but the figure and the shadow were gone. And then we faced the road again and there the guy was, and this close I was sure it was Peattie, and the son of a bitch was smiling like a madman and was so close that I could see his teeth and—had Peattie always had so many long fucking teeth?

I said a silent prayer for Gale to run him down. But at the last second Peattie took an impossibly wide step to the side, disappearing into darkness, and the car careened past him and onto the road going east this time.

As we approached the edge of town finally, Gale tried to follow the speed limit. Finally, I was able to compose myself enough to connect nouns to verbs and explain what had happened. It came spilling out of me in fact, uncontrollably, word after word, and Gale kept glancing back at me in the mirror, like he was reading my lips as I spoke, like he needed both the visual and audio inputs in order to make sense of what I was saying. Finally I finished, and I felt dizzy, and my whole face felt pinched from the blood which had mostly dried by then. 

We turned onto a small two-lane highway headed away from town, and neither of us spoke for a while. The highway wound through evergreens on either side, the trees parting every now and then to reveal pond, or farmhouse, or open field. The fog was as thick as it always was out there that time of night.

A shadow flew out of the darkness towards us and then disappeared above us—a large owl or some other massive night bird.

"I'm sorry," Gale said finally. He started to say something else, the tone reassuring, but he only got out a word or two before the driver's side window exploded inwards and something shot in through the window, pushing Gale's head backwards against the seatback as it squeezed past him. The black mass landed in the passenger seat next to Gale, and quickly sat up and it was Peattie.

Gale said "Hi, Peattie," casually, like they were just passing each other in the grocery store. Then Gale started to say "Wh-" but was cut off when Peattie shoved some sharp object up through the soft spot under Gale's jawbone and into his mouth.

Gale let go of the wheel and looked over to Peattie who was looking back at him and smiling. Peattie pulled the sharp thing out, which turned out just to be Peattie's hand in a karate chop shape. The hand was all red now, and Gale stared at it confused.

I was in hysterics in the back seat, keening and pleading and trying to get my door open as the car started to fishtail on the dark, empty highway, casting headlights back and forth across the fog and the road and illuminating the trees on either side. Finally I managed to get the door open and tried to jump from the car but was held back by the seatbelt. And then Peattie was there in the back seat with me and I could see the back of Gale's head slumped down on the center console, his ear resting in the cup holder.

Peattie was helping me with my seatbelt and I didn't understand what was happening, and the headlights got brighter as they reflected off of the trunk of a large tree just before impact.

I was airborne for a while after that. It felt like dreams I've had of flying, and I seemed to fly so far, so slowly. During my flight, I saw a large black shape soar past and ahead of me and out of sight.

Then everything was black for a moment, and then finally bright again but with pain this time, not light. I tried to get up but couldn't. I saw the lights from the car and it was so far away and hugging a tree very tightly. I laid my head down on dirt and wet grass and tried in vain not to hurt so badly. 

Peattie climbed on top of me. I tried to push him off but I couldn't move my arms. His head disappeared from my line of sight and I felt him licking me then, could hear the sound of him licking me like a dog lapping up water, and could feel the sting of his tongue in wounds I hadn't gotten the chance to inspect yet.

"Don't worry," Peattie said to me between licks. "Don't worry." And I found it so easy not to worry anymore, about Don or Gale or the car or the ring or Peattie. I smiled to myself and laughed then, I don't know why. It hurt so bad to laugh but I couldn't help myself. I laughed and laughed and I could hear the lapping sounds growing louder as the laughing pushed the blood from my broken body. And Peattie started laughing too between drinks and then my laughs sounded like I was gurgling mouthwash, and before long the sound sputtered out and I couldn't laugh anymore. And all that remained was the sound of Peattie, and of a stream off in the distance with at least one frog out there making frog sounds, and the clicking of the car engine as it cooled in the night air. And then black and then hospital bed.

And impossibly: I feel fine. I feel great actually. Just hungry and shaky and a little cold. When I first woke up I thought I must have been out for weeks to have made such a full recovery, but my phone says that the whole bad time at Peattie's was last night. And now the cops are back with another more serious, federal-looking guy, so time to wrap this up before they take my phone away, or take me away. They tell me they can't find Don or Gale, or Peattie. 

Gale: if you are out there, if you see this, you know where I'll be. If they let me go, you know where to find me. Find me, Gale.
