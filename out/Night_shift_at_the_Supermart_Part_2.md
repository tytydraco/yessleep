I have been working at the Supermart ever since graduating high school. I didn’t really have a plan for what I wanted to do with the rest of my life (still don’t if I’m honest), but I needed cash, and to get cash you need a job.

I checked the jobs listed in the local paper and saw that the store was hiring, so I called the owner over the phone and told him I wanted the night shift at the Supermart.

“You want the night shift at the Supermart?” he asked.

“Yeah”, I replied. “That’s what I just said.”

“You worked this kind of job before?”

“No. But I’m a quick learner.”

“Can you work weekends?”

“Sure.”

“Do you have a criminal record?”

I didn’t see any reason to lie. “I spent four months in juvie after I punched a teacher in the face for trying to catch a feel of one of the other students.”

“Have you ever seen a dead body?”

There was a long pause. Still not sure why that would be on the questionnaire, but at the time I didn’t bother to ask. Again, I didn’t see a reason to lie.

“I have.”

“Alright. You are hired. Your first shift starts tonight. I'll call ahead and inform the others that you are coming. They will sort you out. 8 pm sharp. Don’t be late.”

Then he hung up and that was it. My first job.

Two years later and I’m still here. Still working the register. Still cleaning the aisles and restocking shelves with cheap brands you won’t find in any other American store. I’m serious, ever heard of *Clown Crunch*? Of course not. It’s dry, tasteless cereal, nothing strange there, but the mascot on the box was the creepiest clown I ever saw. Except for Ronald McDonald, of course. Not paedophile scary, but at least *serial* killer scary (get it?).

I really don’t get it, what sort of corporate jackass looked at this box and said, “Perfect! Kids love clowns! This will sell like crack cocaine at a kid’s birthday party where the children really like crack cocaine!”

(Look. I’m a store clerk, not a poet. Don’t judge me for my lack of imagination here).

I was cleaning up one off the black pools when the payphone rang, the one in the far corner of the store. I placed the yellow CAUTION: WET FLOOR-sign near what was left of the stain before walking over and picking it up.

“Supermart”, I answered. “Are you sure you called the right number?”

“It’s me.”

“Oh.” I recognised his voice. “Sorry, Boss. What can I do for you?”

“I want you to lock up the store for tonight. Stay until your shift ends, but don’t let any customers in. You got it? Not even one.”

“How come?”

“It’s going to be one of those nights. I can feel it. Something's on its way and I don’t want it to get inside.”

I should have asked what he meant by it, but for some reason the question didn’t formulate in my head properly at the time.

“I’ll get it done”, I said.

“Good.” Click, and he was gone.

I grabbed the keys from the box underneath the register. Supermart has two exits: one in the front for customers, one in the back for deliveries. I locked the front doors before walking to storage.

I mentioned that we keep some strange things in there. Look around and you will find the usual stacks of ramen noodles, cans of soup and toilet paper lie in any store, but in Supermart you might also find a couple of bags with graveyard dirt, an old duck costume or maybe even the unsold CD: s of the rock band THE UNHOLY VIRGINS (I tried listening to their CD once, but the subliminal messages, demanding me to kill babies and drink their blood, didn’t sit quite right to me).

We also have a couple of old posters advertising a food truck: *Chopmans Grill and BBQ on wheels! Sponsored by Supermart.* Then there is the box full of snow globes; the inside showing cute snowmen in different costumes. We don’t sell them for some reason, not even during the holidays. Boss don’t allow it. I locked the backdoor and headed back. My head finally began to wonder, what the hell did he mean by “it”? I didn’t get the chance to think much about it, however.

Someone was standing in one of the aisles when I walked out.

He was a big man dressed in a white butcher's apron, holding a monstrous chainsaw in his strong grip. His leathery mask was strange combination of that of Jason's, Michael Myers' and Leatherface's. With stitches and a skull like appearance. Dark, cold eyes met mine.

“Ah”, the man said, delighted. Very Freddy Krueger like. “Fresh meat!”

He pulled the cord and the chainsaw roared. The man rushed me, grinning through his makeshift mask.

He ran past the yellow caution sign and slipped on the black stain.

“Oh, shit!” was his last words before he tumbled forward to the floor and the chainsaw went right through his torso. Blood and pieces of intestines gushed forward from his stomach. The man screamed for a few seconds and then died.

So that happened.

I looked at the bloody mess and thought to myself, *I just cleaned that. You couldn’t kill yourself anywhere else, you fucking idiot?*

Don’t think I’m heartless, a man did just die in front of me, yeah, but it’s hard to feel anything for someone who clearly was planning on turning you into ground beef. Who was he even? Where did he even come from? I know I locked the front door.

I walked past the corpse, making sure I didn’t get any of his guts on my new shoes, and towards the front. One of the glass doors had been shattered. One mystery solved at least. I made sure no one had touched the cash register, before turning around and heading over to the payphone. I dropped a few coins in and pressed the three buttons for emergency service.

“911, what’s your emergency?”

I didn’t get a chance to respond. My attention was drawn away by a loud sound above me. I looked up at the ceiling. Lights above flickering for a second.

“Hello?” asked the operator.

“Sorry”, I said. “Wrong number.”

I hung up, stepped away from the phone and listened. There was no mistake. Something was moving on top of the Supermart. Something very big and very heavy. Something sniffing the air. Looking for something. My eyes fell on the body of the dead intruder, and thoughts of wolves following the scent of blood filled my head.

I can’t say what sort of impulse led me to do what I did next, only that it seemed like the most logical thing at the time. I grabbed the dead man’s legs and pulled him over to the broken glass.

I waited. Soon I heard the sounds again, the sniffing, then something quickly moving. My legs trembled as I slowly backed away from the shattered glass door. My heart beating fast like a drum on caffeine. And then I saw it. A big, furry hand with long, sharp claws – a hand big enough to hold a full-grown man in its grip like he was a children’s toy.

It leaned down, and in, and picked up the corpse, pulling it away and disappearing from sight above the store.

It ate the body; I could hear the sound off teeth biting into flesh overhead, ripping it apart. The sound of bones cracking. Drops of blood dripping down from the mouth of the thing now devouring the man, sounding a bit like rain. This went on for nearly a minute, before silence took their place. A few seconds later, a loud sound near the back of the store, and then silence again. I waited, but nothing happened. Whatever that thing was, it was gone now.

I took a 5-minute break before getting back to work.

I cleaned up the blood and returned the sign to the closet. From the storage I grabbed a few pieces of cardboard and some duct tape, which I placed over the shattered glass door so as not to let the cold in. On one of the cardboards I wrote: CLOSED FOR REPAIR with a red marker and placed it on the window. The chainsaw got added to storage.

The night passed and sunlight soon shone through the window. I awaked and found myself lying on the floor inside the Supermart toilet. A kitchen knife from aisle 4 clenched in my hands. Not sure if that night had been real or not. It was Monday, and therefore delivery day. Which meant I had to get up and shovel away the snow from that night. I didn’t really want to, but work is work, and you don’t get much of a say regarding these things. Unless you want to lose your job.

Well outside I saw big footprints in the snow, leading away into the forest. Still, the delivery truck would be there within the hour. So I really didn’t have time to look much closer at them. Soon the footprints were gone, and I got ready to leave.

I left a note to Paul and Emma, the usual guys working the day shift, regarding the broken glass (result of some drunk, I wrote) and lifted a candy bar before heading home.

Just another night shift at the Supermart.
