 I don't have the energy to defend myself anymore. I just need to finish this, because last night I finally understood what that place actually is, and I need someone, anyone, to know before I stop being sure of things again.

I went back to the gym alone, like I said I would. I didn't tell my parents. I didn't tell Tommy. I just went.

The door was open before I even touched it this time. Like it was tired of waiting for me to knock.

I went down. The classroom was exactly the same, except this time both desks were empty. No dust disturbed. Clean, like nobody had sat there in years.

That's when I understood. Neither of those things upstairs, the boy who looked like Tommy, the girl who looked like me, they were never down here waiting. They were already gone. Already out. Already living our lives while the real us sat frozen somewhere, watching through our own eyes like passengers in a car we no longer drove.

I turned around to run and the chalkboard was covered edge to edge in handwriting. Not two hundred lines this time. Thousands. My name. Tommy's name. Over and over, in handwriting that slowly changed line by line, starting as messy kid scrawl and ending, near the bottom, in handwriting that looked exactly like my mother's.

That's when I understood the second thing, and this is the part I need you to actually sit with.

This didn't start with us.

My mom went to this same school. She told us that once, years ago, at dinner, like it was a boring fact about her childhood. I never thought about it again until last night, staring at her handwriting on a chalkboard three floors below a gym that shouldn't have three floors below it.

Whatever is down there doesn't want children. It wants a family. One at a time. Generation after generation. It takes the real ones down, quiet and patient, and it sends something wearing their face back up into the sunlight to keep the family going, to keep the house warm, to keep the story looking normal from the outside, until it's time to reach for the next small kid who wanders too close to a grey door with no handle.

I ran. I didn't look back. I got home and I stood in the kitchen doorway watching my mom make dinner, humming like she always does, and for the first time in my life I looked at her and I did not feel safe. I felt like I was looking at a very good copy of someone I used to trust completely.

I asked her, as calm as I could, "Mom, did anything strange ever happen at your old school?"

She stopped humming. She didn't turn around. She just said, very softly, "Everyone has a door like that, sweetheart. Most people are just lucky enough to never find theirs."

Then she turned around and smiled at me, and it was her smile, her actual smile, the one I've known my whole life.

But her eyes didn't smile with it. Not even a little.

I'm writing this from my room with the door locked, and I can hear Tommy laughing downstairs with her, easy and happy, like nothing in this house has ever been wrong. And some small, cowardly part of me wants to just go down there and laugh with them too. Pretend I never went back a second time. Pretend I don't know.

But I keep thinking about that empty desk with no dust on it.

I keep thinking, if the version of me that ran out of that classroom the first time already wasn't the real one...

then who exactly has been writing all of this the whole time.

And why does my hand feel colder with every sentence.
