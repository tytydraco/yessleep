Deep within the Italian peninsula, there is a national park in the Apennine Mountains. There are many, actually. So many. But I wanted to talk to you about one in particular.

There are a lot of noteworthy things about this one. A grand woodland, growing all over the lazier side of the mountains. The edges of it reach many rural villages and small towns nestled between the valley and the higher peaks, like a green ocean washing the shores of different continents. Maybe it’s not *that* big, but it’s big. Trust me. It is home to many hiking trails, some easier than others, that lead to quiet spots in the woods and breathtaking mountain vistas. On the side of the road leading up to the denser forest areas, you may see the townsfolk walking dogs, pushing strollers, jogging or sitting at one of the picnic tables. There are also white brick barbecues that are fairly popular as a spot for a day out on the weekend with the family. On top of all that, I work there.

During my last year in high school I started working as a volunteer and I would later be promoted to junior ranger, which really just means I helped the guides babysit the people coming to visit during organized tours of the area on our side of the park. Yes, our side. The place is big, so we have different groups of rangers working from the various little towns around it. If we fast forward a bit to these days, now I’m officially head of “Night Security”, a department that, if you’d like a headcount, has a grand total of one member: me.

I don’t want to make it sound like I am the only one doing all the heavy lifting, because really, there isn’t much to do. I work (and sleep and so on) in a little brick shack in the woods, close to the threshold, where the tarmac road ends and the “wilder” side of the park begins. I have some cameras to check that look at the road and some other spot leading up here and I have to make sure people don’t venture deeper than my post at night. And usually not much happens. If things get messy with some reckless visitors, I can always call my fellow rangers and they can back me up after a whopping 10-minute drive from town.

You might be asking yourself: Why? Why pay a guy to just live in the woods and check some cameras. Well, I am getting there. The thing is, a couple of drunk or high students looking for God knows what up here at night is not the messiest thing that can happen here. Far from it. As I mentioned, my post sits close to the end of the road that leads here from town. It is very important that people don’t get deeper than here at night, when it’s harder to search even on a known path. Camping in our woods is highly discouraged and forbidden in any place that isn’t visible from the mapped trails. 

You may want to try it. Don’t. When you go rock climbing or cave diving or some other extreme sport, you go knowing the risks. Here, they don’t tell you the risks. I don’t blame them, because I really wouldn’t know how to explain it. But I will try anyway.

Ripping out the band-aid: people here go missing. That is a reality of most national parks, I know, but not like this. I said it’s hard to explain, and I meant it. Walking here, surrounded by nature, you might see or hear something. It may even seem familiar. Probably out of place. Don’t get near it. I don’t know if there are places like this out in the world, but please listen to my advice. If you are out in nature and you see something that seems weirdly placed, maybe something you recognize, go away. Go home. If you get curious, you might find yourself lost.

I once read a book. A sci-fi novel. In it, they talked about higher dimensions. You probably heard this one already. If you were to plunge your finger into a two dimensional world, its inhabitants would only see the contours of your finger, lacking any depth. A pinkish circle getting bigger. Then, as you take it out, smaller, until it disappears. In that book, people find three dimensional objects that take them to a higher world. A fourth dimension, where every aspect, inside and out, of everything in their world is laid bare before them. 

Maybe, out there, a giant four dimensional creature is taking a nap. A nap longer than the life of our universe. While asleep, it may have moved and accidentally nudged our little world. Maybe with an arm or a knee. Maybe with some other ungodly limb or organ. And its higher world body is now penetrating our planet, right here at my park. And we only get to see aspects of that new realm, dumbed down in a way our minds can see. And here, maybe, just maybe, something can see us from there. And it can see us whole, in every aspect. Inside and out.

I don’t think I painted a clear image of the things that can happen here. Sorry, I did say it was hard. 

To be honest, this place has been almost the entirety of my world for a long time. I may have given the impression that this thing is a warning, but it isn’t. Not entirely, at least. I just felt the need to talk about it. To share it with someone who didn’t already decide that this is a place that you don’t want to be alone in and nothing else. 

I am getting over myself with all these ramblings and personal stuff. I’m sure you want to hear some story and not my sappy feelings about my weird woods job. And I want to tell a story, because I think that explains everything I’ve been trying to say better.

I was still in high school and I was just a volunteer here, as I already mentioned. It was June and I was walking on a trail with a trash bag. Plastic orange gloves making my hands sweat profusely. I was on garbage duty. Exciting stuff.

It was a popular spot. An easy to reach path that circled a hillside clearing before taking a slow turn. Green grass year around and white dolomite rocks poking out of the ground here and there. The sight was really nice too. You could see far into the valley, where cultivated fields and orchards turned the land into a patchwork of squares coloured in all kinds of shades of yellow and green. That is all to say that there was a lot of trash to clean up there.

I was paired with this older twenty-something-year-old girl I’m going to call Sara. She was way more excited about being here on a morning summer day collecting cigarette buds and chewed up gums than I was. She was the kind of person that could keep up a whole conversation on her own. In the couple of hours I knew her, I learned a whole lot about her grandparent’s lovely home here in the countryside and her passion for riding on horseback. She loved her first year in college but she often felt homesick and hoped to catch up with her high school buddies over the summer.

What I gathered from all of that is that she was a shallow rich girl who liked to talk to herself. To be fair, I was the kind of seventeen years old who took himself way too seriously. Which is a gracious way to say I was a jerk. Thinking back, I’m sure Sara was nice enough, if not a bit too extroverted.

At some point I started to tune her out, which came back to bite me in the ass when I realized she was asking *me* a question now.

“Huh? What was that?”

She repeated the question to me. I think she didn’t fully realize I was not listening to her before.

“I asked if you’re thinking about college yet. Are you?”

“Uhm… look, let’s just keep cleaning up, alright?”

As I said, I was a jerk. 

“Okay, Jesus.”

That kind of killed the conversation. 

Embarrassed by the sudden change in tone, I tried to smoothly get a bit of distance by walking away from the beaten path, towards the clearing. From there, I had a better view of how the trail looked after taking a slow turn further ahead. In the distance, something caught my eye.

Standing out against the blur of white and brown bark and green leaves was a red car, parked at the treeline. It was an eyesore.

“How did that get there?”

I said out loud, catching Sara’s attention. She froze mid-step while approaching me, following my gaze.

“What the fuck…”

She seemed immediately weirded out. And I don’t blame her. It was really eerie. The car was immaculate. The red paint perfectly reflected the sun. Polished metal handles. Not a smudge on the windshield. It was a nice car too. Vintage and all. 

Shaking off the initial surprise, I left the trash bag on the ground and started walking towards it. Sara’s eyes were on me now.

“Wait… what are you doing?”

“I mean, it’s just a car, right?”

I think that, on a level, Sara felt responsible for my safety, being the oldest of the pair. Reluctantly, she carefully left the trash bag next to mine and joined me in quick steps.

“It’s a cool car too…”

Sara didn’t respond. As I looked at the car from the front, noting the rolled down window of the driver’s seat, she circled it at a distance. Her walk was cut short: while looking at the rear, she let out a yelp. Her eyes fixed on the back window, wide and scared.

“Seriously, fuck this! Let’s just go, okay?”

Her voice trembled. 

“Why are you acting like this? It’s just a weird car!”

Sara quickly looked at me, but immediately, her eyes darted back to the window. She struggled to articulate the next words, probably because it made the impossibility before her eyes feel more real.

“My dad used to own a car that looked exactly like this one and…”

She pointed at something on the glass.

“I think I put that on, when I was like four.”

On the bottom right of the glass, there was a glittery sticker of a pink pony. 

“Please man, let’s just go.”

But there was no way I would have left after that.

You see, at the time I knew something was going on around here already. I did not know half of what I know now, and I wonder if even that is a relevant fraction of all of the things this park is. Anyway, I was aware of it. And I really wanted to know more.

Listen, I’m not proud of it. Not one bit. I thought poking around here could somehow lead me to… shit, I don’t even know. I was a dumb teenager in over his head.

I reached into the car from the rolled down window and opened it. Sara’s eyes snapped on me, and this time they didn’t look away. When I sat at the driver’s seat, she almost screamed at me in a higher pitch than usual.

“Hey, stop that! This isn’t funny.”

We agreed on that one. I didn’t find it funny. My hands trembled as I fumbled with the glove compartment. At that moment, I saw something above my head that I could swear wasn’t there before, when I was looking from outside. Dangling from the rear view mirror, there was a red camera strap. 

I felt goosebumps forming on my arms and up to my neck. Thinking back, that was probably how Sara was feeling. I used to wear that strap to keep my old polaroid camera around my neck. And I mean I used to wear exactly the one dangling before my eyes.

Suddenly, something moved in the mirror. I quickly turned to the backseats and I found myself blinded by a flash of light. There was a mechanical sound, I’m sure of it now. Back then, all I could hear was my heartbeat thundering in my ears.

Then, the car moved. And I don’t mean it was starting to move. The engine coming to life and all that. No. What I mean is that it was like the car was already going at high speed. 

The sudden acceleration slammed me against the seat. I opened my eyes, but everything still felt blurry and vague. I think I remember it being night outside all of a sudden. I was screaming.

Something grabbed me and a sharp pain made me gasp as my hip hit something hard. Then, the world turned upside down as I fell with my back on the ground. Bolting upwards, still scared out of my mind, I noticed it was sunny again. I was on the grass and Sara was looking down at me, with a terrified expression.

She had yanked me out of the car from the driver seat window. 

“What… did you…?”

I mumbled, before getting interrupted.

“You started to freak out in the car. I thought you were pranking me at first but then… that scream, Jesus. What…”

But the question was cut short. I think she realized mid-sentence that she didn’t want to know. After that, we silently gathered our trash bags and left. 

I don’t know how that story ended for poor Sara, or if it even continued. I can tell you how it did for me. I came back there the next day.

Look, I told you already that I was so obsessed with this stuff back then. I couldn’t sleep over it. Sure, I was scared shitless, but at the same time I wanted to see. Like in a car accident. 

But it wasn’t there anymore. All that was left in the place it was parked, half buried in the dirt, upright, was a polaroid picture. It was taken from the backseat and the image was blurry. I was in it. Eyes shut. Face brightly illuminated by the flash. Startled and confused. 

Looking at it, I squinted at something. It was in the rear view mirror, hardly visible due to the white light. I could vaguely see the camera. What caught my attention were the fingers holding it. So many fingers. Long and curled around it like the legs of a spider. Some of them bent in the air and crowned the camera like the petals of a flower.

Despite my obsession. My drive to all the creepy things going on in these woods, I couldn’t stomach keeping the picture with me for even a moment. I crumbled it and threw it as far as I could. Trash for somebody else to gather.

Well, this wraps it up I think. The sun is rising right now here, that is my cue to get to bed. Oh, I did not mention it. My name's Alex. 

Remember: be careful in the woods. Don’t be an idiot, if something stands out to you when you are on trail, don’t approach it. If it’s on the trail, turn around and leave. It might have been there intentionally. Until the next one, stay put.
