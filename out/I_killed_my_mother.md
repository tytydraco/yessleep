I've never told anyone this before. Not a therapist, not a friend, nobody. I'm telling strangers on the internet because I don't think I can carry it by myself anymore, and because I need someone to tell me if what's been happening to me since is real or if I've finally lost my mind the way I always feared I would.

I'll say it plainly, because there's no gentle way to say it. Three years ago, I killed my mother.

I'm not going to ask you to feel sorry for me. I'm just going to tell you what happened, and let you decide that for yourself.

My mother was not a good mother. I'm choosing my words carefully here, because the truth is uglier than that sentence makes it sound, and some of it I still can't write down even now. She was gone for days at a time throughout my whole childhood, sometimes weeks. I raised myself more than she ever raised me. When she was home, she wasn't really present — not in any way that mattered. I won't go into the specifics of what those years were like. I'll just say that by the time I was an adult, I understood two things clearly. I understood that I had survived something most people never have to survive. And I understood that some part of me had been quietly planning, for a very long time, exactly what I would do about it.

I found her again when I was twenty-six. She was living in a small apartment across town, older, sicker, alone. She didn't recognize me at first when I showed up at her door. When she finally did, she didn't apologize. She didn't cry. She just asked me if I had any money.

I went back three more times after that. Each time I told myself I was going to confront her properly, say everything I'd rehearsed in my head for years. Each time I left without saying any of it, because some old, small part of me still wanted her to say something first. She never did.

The fourth time, I didn't leave.

I'm not going to describe what happened that night. I've spent three years trying to convince myself it doesn't count if the world never technically found out. Nobody ever came looking for her. Nobody ever asked questions. As far as anyone else knows, she simply disappeared, the same way she always used to disappear when I was a kid, except this time for good.

I told myself that was justice. For a while, I actually believed it.

The first sign that something was wrong came about six months later. I started smelling her perfume in my apartment, faint but unmistakable, always at night, always gone by the time I turned on the lights to check. I told myself it was memory playing tricks, the mind doing what minds do with guilt it hasn't fully processed.

Then I started finding her sleeping pills. The specific brand she always used, the ones I remembered from childhood sitting on her nightstand during the rare weeks she was actually home. I'd find one or two scattered somewhere in my apartment every few weeks, on my bathroom counter, on my kitchen table, places I knew for certain I'd cleaned the day before. I don't buy that brand. I've never bought that brand. I checked my receipts obsessively for months trying to prove myself wrong.

I couldn't.

About a year in, I started hearing her voice. Not constantly, not even often, but always at the worst possible moments — right as I was falling asleep, right as I stepped out of the shower, right as I was alone in an elevator. Always the same handful of words, said in the exact flat, uninterested tone she used to use on me as a kid, like I was an inconvenience she hadn't asked for and didn't especially want.

"You always needed too much from me."

I started seeing a therapist around that time, though obviously I never told her the real reason why. I let her believe it was ordinary grief, ordinary complicated-family-history stuff. She told me some version of what I already suspected — that guilt manifests in strange ways, that the mind can create very convincing experiences when it's trying to process something it can't fully face. I wanted so badly to believe that was all this was.

I don't believe that anymore. Not after last month.

Last month, I finally worked up the nerve to go back to her old apartment, the one she'd been living in when I last saw her. I don't fully know why. I told myself I wanted closure. I think, if I'm honest, some part of me wanted to see if anyone had moved in yet  some way of confirming, once and for all, that this was really over.

The building was still there. The apartment was empty, unrented, the landlord told me, because nobody who moves in ever stays more than a few weeks. He said it like it was a mildly annoying fact about the property rather than something worth investigating further. When I asked why, he just shrugged and said tenants always complain about "someone crying in the walls at night" before breaking their lease early.

He let me in to look around, thinking I might be interested in renting it myself. The apartment was bare, nothing left of her in it at all, except one thing. Sitting in the exact center of the bedroom floor, in the spot where I know for a fact I left her that final night, was a small pile of her sleeping pills. Arranged carefully. Like someone had taken the time to count out exactly enough for a full dose.

I asked the landlord if he'd put them there. He looked at me like I was speaking a foreign language and said the apartment had been completely empty since the last tenant moved out in a panic two months ago.

I've been having the same dream every night since that visit. I'm standing in my mother's old apartment, except in the dream it's not empty — she's sitting on the floor exactly where I found those pills, looking up at me with an expression I never once saw on her face while she was alive. Not anger. Not even sadness. Something closer to satisfaction, like she's finally gotten something she'd been trying to get from me for years and never managed while she was still breathing.

In the dream, she always says the same thing before I wake up.

"You finally gave me all your attention. It only took killing me to get it."

I keep waking up with the taste of her perfume in the back of my throat, so strong I have to rinse my mouth out before I can fall back asleep. Last night, for the first time, I woke up and the taste didn't go away, even after I brushed my teeth twice.

It's still there right now, as I'm typing this.

I don't know what I did that night three years ago. I thought I ended something. I'm starting to understand I might have started something instead  something that finally has all of my attention, exactly the way she always wanted, except now I can't get free of it no matter how hard I try, and some sick, quiet part of me wonders if that was the whole plan from the very beginning.

If I stop posting after this, I need someone to know I tried to make it stop. I really did try.
