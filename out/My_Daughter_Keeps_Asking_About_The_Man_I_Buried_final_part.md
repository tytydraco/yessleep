I know it’s been a while since my last post and I’m sorry. Things have been…rough.

I wish I could tell you things have gotten better since finding out his name, but if I’m going to be honest, knowing who Michael is has only made everything worse. Not for Olivia thankfully, but still. Actually I think that’s probably the part that bothers me the most.

She had gotten used to him.

When all of this started, she would tell me whenever she saw him. She’d point him out while we were driving or mention something he said. It was like she wanted me to know and be involved. Now she doesn’t bother to say anything about it unless I ask her directly. There are times where I’d hear her laughing from her bedroom and when I ask what’s so funny, all she would tell me was,

“Michael told me a joke.”

I could hear her in her room or in the living room recounting her day like she was talking to an old friend. I watched her playing in the backyard one afternoon. She was sitting on the grass with her dolls lined up in front of her, talking to an empty spot beside her. I went outside and almost immediately, she stopped talking. It felt like I was interrupting.

One night, I put dinner on the table and Olivia stared at it for a moment.

“Everything alright, baby?” I asked.

“Where’s Michael’s plate?”

I just looked at her.

“What?”

“You didn't make him one.”

“Michael doesn't eat with us.”

She frowned.

“He does sometimes.”

I didn't know what the hell I was supposed to say to that. It had been just Olivia and I for years, but now I felt like a third wheel in my own home.

 

Then, it stopped happening to just Olivia. I started to hear things around the house. I would be lying in bed and hear the sound of the floorboards in the hallway creaking, long after I knew Olivia was asleep. Then, I started to hear keys in the lock. The sound of cabinets opening and closing when I knew I was the only person at the house.

Then things started moving.

Make no mistake, I’m not claiming I saw a plate float across the kitchen. It was simple things. I would place my keys on the hook near the door and when I came back later to get them, they would be on the table instead. More than once,  I woke up to my coffee cup already out and on the counter, like it had just been used. One day, I came into the kitchen to a single chair pulled out from the table. When I asked Olivia about it, she simply said,

“Michael was sitting there.”

I stopped sleeping much after that.

Every noise woke me. I would turn every time Olivia looked over my shoulder, only to see absolutely nothing behind me. I started to check her room multiple times a night. I even put more locks on the doors.

After getting Olivia in bed one night, I took a long shower. I heard footsteps in the hall with the tale tell creaky board about halfway down it. Then I didn’t hear anything else. I about jumped out of my skin when I pulled back the curtain. The steam revealed one word written on the bathroom mirror.

Jason

I yelled for Olivia.

She came running into the bathroom and stared at me like I'd lost my mind.

“Did you do this?”

She looked at the mirror and denied it.

“Olivia, don't lie to me.”

Her face changed immediately.

“I'm not!”

“Then who did?”

She looked toward the doorway but didn’t answer me. She didn’t have to.

I dropped Olivia off at my parents’ house that night. I sat in complete silence as I drove back to my house. When I arrived, I threw the truck in park and stormed inside.

“MICHAEL!” I yelled.

Silence.

“WHAT THE HELL DO YOU WANT?”

Silence.

I started walking through the house, continuing my rant.

“If you want something, you talk to ME! Do you understand? Leave my daughter out of this!”

Nothing.

Just empty, endless silence.

“It was just an accident,” I said, falling to my knees. “It was just an accident.”

For the next few hours, I sat in the kitchen, whiskey in hand as I was assaulted by a flood of emotion. That night replayed in my head a thousand times. I thought about his son. His wife. All the things he wouldn’t get to do.

I spent the rest of the night talking to an empty kitchen. I talked about how scared I was that night. The amount of panic I felt and the guilt that followed. I must have said it was an accident a million times. Eventually, I started to talk about Olivia and how much she meant to me. How scared I was of losing her. I just wanted to keep her safe. I had to keep her safe.

I don't know what I expected to happen. Maybe I thought he'd answer me. Maybe I thought saying everything out loud would make him leave. Instead, morning came and I was still sitting there alone.

I poured a cup of coffee and changed. I didn’t want to pick up Olivia in the same clothes I was in when I dropped her off. My parents already were worried. It was impossible to miss the bags under my eyes. I was a mess.

“You doing okay, son?” my dad asked. “You’re not looking so good.”

“Yeah. I’m fine.”

“You know you can always talk to me.”

“I’m fine, dad. It’s…I just have a lot going on. I’ll be okay.”

He said that it may be a good idea if Olivia stayed there for another night. I declined. I didn’t want to be apart from her anymore. The ride home was uneventful. The only sound was the music coming from the radio.

We sat in the driveway for at least ten minutes when I pulled in. I didn’t want to go inside. I begrudgingly carried her bag inside. The key felt heavy as I pushed it into the lock. It was like the house was pushing against me. Then the door opened.

Nothing.

Silence.

Olivia just ran to kitchen and asked me if she could have juice. She hugged me as I gave her a glass, then went to play in the living room. Hours went by. Nothing moved. No sounds. Just Olivia and I. I finally felt a bit of relief as I lay in the couch.

Olivia’s voice ripped that away from me.

“I missed you too.”

I didn’t move.

“Is he here?”

She nodded.

“Did he hear me last night.”

She didn’t answer. Her eyes moved towards the empty space beside her.

“Olivia?”

“Can I just play, daddy?”

My blood boiled. I jumped off the couch, threw my glass and walked to my room. I knew I wasn’t mad at her. It wasn’t her fault. None of this was her fault.

I could hear her laugh as I lay in bed. Then words I couldn’t quite make out. I tried to drown it out. I held a pillow over my face as I let out a muted scream. She continued to play for the rest of the night. When bedtime came, I helped her get ready and tucked her in like normal. After getting her in bed, I brought the bottle of whisky to the room with me. Eventually I heard another creak in the hallway. I opened my door to check and I saw nothing.

Then another.

Still nothing.

This continued all night. I stared at a shell of a person in the mirror the next morning. I was irritable. I kept losing track of what I was doing. A splitting headache was a constant reminder of my choices. My dad was right. I needed help. I could barely function.

I fixed the strongest pot of coffee I could and told Olivia to pack a bag. Afterwards we got into the truck and started back to my parents’ house. I just needed another night or two. My mind continued to drift as I drove.

I looked back at Olivia in the mirror. She was just sitting quietly, smiling and looking out the window. She looked so much like her mother.

I don’t know how long I had been staring when she asked me, “Daddy, are you alright?”

“I’m fine, baby.”

I rubbed my eyes, trying to wipe the tears away without her noticing. That would be the last thing I needed at that point. I didn’t have much longer until we would make it to my parents’ house. That’s when I heard a man’s voice, whisper in my ear.

“Jason.”

I turned around wildly, looking into the back seat.

Nothing.

When I turned back around, the truck was already drifting across the center line. I jerked the wheel.

Olivia screamed as the tires squealed against the pavement. Then everything went black.

I came to disoriented, my head resting on the steering wheel, the horn blaring. The windshield was shattered. A red stream of blood poured from my forehead and into my eye. My whole body ached. I tried to grab the steering wheel and turn when a wave of pain radiated up my left arm.

“Olivia?” I asked, turning around.

She was still in her seat. Her head lay against the door. She was unconscious. Her face was cut from glass, but she appeared to be breathing.

“OLIVIA!” I yelled, as I ripped my seatbelt off and crawled into the back seat. My eyes filled with tears.

“Baby, please, please. I’m sorry baby. I’m sorry.”

I continued to sob uncontrollably. “Not like this, please. I’m sorry.”

I held her tight with my right arm; tears streamed like a raging river. I desperately searched for my phone. I gasped in pain as I searched. My left arm was broken but I wouldn’t put her down. Not now. She was going to stay with me. After finding it, I pushed my door open and dropped to the ground with her still cradled. I dialed and told the operator what happened. My vision and consciousness started to fade right as I saw the lights cresting over the hill.

I woke up in the hospital to a nurse standing over me. I started to sit up in a panic as she placed a hand on my chest. “It’s okay,” she said, “She’s alright and she’s right here,” as she pointed over to her.

“Hi, daddy,” Olivia said, a cup of ice cream in her hand.

I dropped my head back onto my pillow as I started to cry again. She was okay. She was here and safe.

“Daddy, why are you crying?” she asked.

“I’m okay now, baby. I was just worried.”

She climbed into bed with me. Aside from a couple of stitches and a bruise on her forehead, she was fine. The nurse assured me she would be okay. She then left the room as I held Olivia tightly in my arms. She fell asleep curled up next to me, her head on my chest. As she lay there, I just kept staring at her. For the first time in weeks I had felt clarity.

Looking at the stitches on her forehead, I realized I had been telling myself the same thing for ten years.

It was an accident.

And it WAS an accident.

But burying Michael wasn't.

Keeping quiet wasn't.

Letting Michael's family spend years wondering what happened wasn't.

I don’t know how long I sat there holding her. Eventually, Olivia fell asleep against my chest. I watched her breathe for a while, her little hand resting against my hospital gown.

I had almost killed her.

Not Michael.

Me.

Now I sit here, her still in my arms. I know what I have to do. I’m going to cherish what little time I have left with her. I guess in a way, I've already confessed.

I've told all of you what I did. I've told you things I've never told another person in my life. Maybe that's why I started writing these posts in the first place. I think some part of me knew this was where it had to end. But telling a bunch of strangers on the internet isn't the same as taking responsibility.

Olivia is still asleep beside me. My parents are on their way here now. When they get here, I'm going to ask them to take her home with them. After that, I'm going to press the button beside my bed.

I'm going to ask the nurse to call the police, and I'm going to tell them everything. I don't know exactly what's going to happen to me. I don't know how much time I'm going to lose with Olivia. That's the part that scares me the most.

But Michael had a son too.

His family deserves to know what happened to him.

I owe them that much.

I owe Michael that much.

So, I guess this is goodbye for now.
