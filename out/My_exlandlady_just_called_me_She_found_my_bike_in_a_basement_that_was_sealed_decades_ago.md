“Here are your keys, and enjoy your new home!” the landlady, Sarah, said. “If you need anything, call me.”

She turned, ready to leave.

“Wait,” I called after her. “I completely forgot to ask. Is there a basement or some storage around where I could put my bike?”

“Unfortunately, no. You’ll have to keep it inside the apartment.”

It was a shame, but at least I'd get some extra exercise. 

One day I came home, feeling like my body weighed a ton and my mind could barely hold a thought. I still decided to bike, even though I probably should have skipped it. I got a quarter of the way when my thighs felt like they were on fire and my head started to spin. I got off the bike and walked it back into the building, stopping every few minutes to rest.

When I saw the flight of stairs, my heart almost skipped a beat. I leaned down on the bike, panting hard.

“Hi,” a soothing, friendly voice called.

I looked up. An old lady in her home clothes stood there, smiling.

“Hello.”

“You’re the new neighbor from floor four.”

“Yeah,” I said, catching my breath.

“I’m Sally. I live on floor three. We haven’t had a chance to meet.”

She extended her hand. I wiped mine and extended my hand too.

“Carl.”

“You don’t look too happy about your bike ride, Carl.”

“No. It’s that I have to take it up the stairs now.”

She furrowed her eyebrows.

“You don’t have a space in the basement?”

“Basement?”

“Yes.”

“My landlady told me there is no basement.”

Sally stared at me, even more confused.

“It’s right here.”

She stepped back a few paces and pointed at a wall. Bars stood before a staircase that led into a dark hall. 

I stared at it for a second before speaking.

“Oh, weird. Why would she lie?”

“Do you want to put your bike down?”

“I don’t have a unit.”

“That’s okay. You can put it in mine.”

“Well, I don’t know.”

“Unless you want to carry it up,” she said, chuckling.

“You sure you don’t mind?”

“Of course not.”

She stared down the stairs.

“Okay.”

She unlocked the bars and went in.

I followed.

The basement was damp and dark. The light was on, but the old lightbulbs barely gave off any light. The basement seemed small at first, but after a minute, we had already taken a few turns.

“Where did you move from, Carl?”

“Mooresville.”

“That’s far away, but I’m sure you go there and visit often.”

“How did you know I was from there?”

“The accent. You all talk the same. So do you visit your Mother often?”

Sweat rolled down my back.

“No. She’s recently passed away.”

“But you did before that?”

“Not as much as I would have liked to.”

Sally didn’t answer. The pipes now rattled. The air grew thinner. 

After a long silence, Sally spoke again: “But why?”

“What?”

“Why didn’t you come more often?”

“I was busy. Life got in the way.”

“Too busy for your own Mother?”

The bike chain echoed. My head started to spin.

“It…it just happened.”

“Happens? How do you think she felt? Poor Mary, all alone.”

“How do you know her name?”

“Does that matter?” Sally’s voice grew deeper. “She was alone, scared.”

I quickly looked over my shoulder. The dark, empty hall stared at me.

“Don’t look there. Look at what you caused.”

She stood by the wall, next to a room behind a heavy iron bar where bright yellow light shone.

“Look!”

I swallowed and leaned on my bike. My heart sank into my stomach. It was my Mother’s room in the nursing home. She sat there, staring out the window. One of the workers stood beside her.

“Let’s go to bed, Mary,” the caretaker said.

“Where is Carl? He said he’d come by today,” my Mother said.

“He didn’t come. Let’s go lie down.”

She touched my Mother’s shoulder.

“No. He will come, he will!” My Mother pushed her away and stood up from her chair.

“Nurses?!” the caretaker called, and two large men ran into the room. The light then went out.

Tears rolled down my cheeks. I looked away. Cold shot through me. Sally’s face had now turned into my Mother’s, but it was twisted in pain and agony.

“You left me alone!” Sally screamed in my Mother’s voice.

“I…I’m sorry.”

“No, you’re not.” 

My whole body shook. I quickly turned around and ran.

“You’re leaving me again?!” echoed behind me, followed by loud footsteps.

I ran faster, but it was harder to speed up with the bike. I threw it on the ground and ran again.

“Don’t leave me again!” Sally called in my mom’s perfect voice, but I didn’t dare turn back.

I ran out of the basement, up the stairs, and right into my apartment. I sat on the ground for hours, shaking, looking at the wall. I left that apartment faster than I’d moved into it.

Months had passed, and somehow I had convinced myself it wasn’t real.

Until my old landlady called me.

“Carl? This is Sarah. You rented one of my apartments a long time ago.”

“Yeah. Why?”

“They reopened the basement.”

“What?” My voice shook. “I thought it didn’t have a basement.”

“It was sealed off decades ago.”

Silence.

“They found a bike down there.”

I couldn’t breathe.
