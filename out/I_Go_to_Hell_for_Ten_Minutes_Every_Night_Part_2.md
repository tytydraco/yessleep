[**\[Part 1\]**](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1xqykdb/i_go_to_hell_for_ten_minutes_every_night/)

Wow, this got a lot more attention than I thought it would. The comments that I received were helpful and I’m thankful for the support. These last two days have been pretty terrible—I haven’t slept much and when I do sleep my dreams are all about her.

So believe me when I tell you that your kind words meant a lot to me.

I need to catch you all up, because so much has happened. Right off the bat I noticed that I messed up. As I rolled back from my desk after writing my first post it hit me. I forgot about her phone!

We were naked over there because we had slept that way. That meant that her clothes, purse, and phone were all in my apartment. I mean, I saw them that first day and I put them away, but I didn’t mess with anything. That means that it was turned on and pinging from my place for three full days.

Well, it’s off now and I even pulled the SIM like they do in the movies. There were a lot of notifications, and I don’t know if they can track it here or anything like that. I’m absolutely terrible with technology. It’s got me worried, though, because that cop came back by to see me.

It was at lunchtime the day before yesterday. I’ve been using my sick days, so I was at home. My laptop was open on the kitchen counter and I was having a chat with an expert (more on him in a minute). That’s when the detective knocked on my door.

I looked through the peep hole and saw him out there. He’s an older guy, maybe in his late fifties, with a shaved head and a well-groomed beard. He was wearing a tan suit with a brown leather coat.

“Hey, you are still alive?” he rasped. “Talked to your boss yesterday and she said you were feeling puny. Good to see you up and about.”

“Yeah,” I said. “It’s been a rough few days. You might not want to get too close to me unless you like running to the bathroom.”

“I think I’ll risk it. Mind if I come in?”

I stepped aside so he could enter, and Detective Shugart took a moment to turn and look around. As he turned back towards me I saw his eyes linger on my computer. They flicked back my way and he shot me a toothy grin.

“I like your place, Will. Plenty of space for a young man on his own. What are you, twenty-three?”

“Twenty-four for a couple of months now. Can I help you with something, Sir?”

His smile only broadened. “Well, I hope so. I wanted to talk to you about our last conversation. You remember, a few days ago. I only ask because, looking back on it, it seems like you might have a condition.”

When I didn’t reply he just went on.

“Yeah, you told me it had been a week since you’d seen the girl. You know, Maia? Pretty little thing. She’s your girlfriend, right Will?” He pulled a picture of her out of his pocket and held it up to me. “This girl, right here.”

“I mean, we… we’ve gone out a few times.” I remember that I stuttered.

“A few, that’s good Will,” he said. “Her mom confirmed that. She said it was three times now, with the last one being four days ago. That’s less than a week, Will. So you can imagine my concern for your uh… faculties.”

I couldn’t think of what to say and I just stared at him. I knew that it made me look guilty as hell, but I thought more stammering would be even worse. The cop was good at what he did and he had me rattled. The way he kept saying my name took me right back to high school and every talking to I ever got. I couldn’t stay silent forever, though. I had to say something to get him to back off.

“I should have told you the truth, officer,” I said, trying to sound as dumb and immature as I figured he already thought I was. “I’m sorry, but I’m worried out of my mind about her and I was scared because I knew it would make me a suspect. If you were so busy investigating me I was afraid that it might keep you from looking for her.”

Shugart’s smile slowly faded as I explained myself and his expression transformed into one of concern. He nodded at me and placed a hand on my shoulder.

“I can understand that, Will,” he said. “But you’ve got to be honest with me. See, I can always tell when someone lies to me. I wouldn’t be good at my job if I couldn’t. Your lie made you suspicious, son, and that gets my hair up. A thing like that slows an investigation for an old bloodhound like me. You made me waste a whole day tracking your trail, Will. If you’d been honest I could have used that time looking elsewhere.”

I swallowed hard and let my eyes widen for him. I hoped I wasn’t overplaying it. I could see him watching everything, measuring every tic. When I thought it had been long enough I asked him where they were in the investigation.

“It’s coming along. There’s plenty of us working on it, so don’t worry. You didn’t slow us much. Since we’re being truthful now, Will, why don’t you take me through what really happened that night so I can mark you off and move on to the next thing.”

We went over it then, the whole night. I told him about meeting her outside of her work and how we spent a couple of hours walking and talking at the park. He asked me if she had mentioned leaving town and I told him she hadn’t. I went on, telling him about our visit to the noodle place she likes and I even gave him our exact orders. He wrote down everything, asking me for specific details here and there.

I didn’t lie until the very end. I knew that there could be witnesses at any of those places, and I wasn’t going to take the risk. I admitted that she had come to my apartment that night, but I told him that she left shortly after eleven.

I expected him to grill me then, but he didn’t. He just made a few more notes and gave me his card. I’m not dumb enough to think I’m off the hook, but it was good to get him out of there. As soon as he left I typed it up as well as I could remember what we said. Some of it was for this, but mostly I don’t want to forget the details about what I’ve told him.

I hope it’s the healthy type of paranoia.

Oh, and don’t bother looking up Detective Shugart. It’s not really him. I didn’t even use my own name here. It’s too big of a risk with the investigation and everything else.

For the next few hours I continued my research. I read up a bit on spirit guardians like someone suggested, but it just led me down a rabbit hole that gave me very few answers. I had found a book the night before, though. I won’t mention the title here, but it’s about something called the Perditious Cluster. It’s not a well-known thing and I haven’t been able to find much about it anywhere.

What caught my eye was his description of a dark afterlife shaped as a group of thousands of different hells. He compared it to a pomegranate, saying that the skin is like the barrier between it and our world and the spaces between each aril, or seed if you are a normal person, are the borders. He even describes a few of them, and I’ve been to every one.

All of that was super intriguing, but what convinced me to send him a message was what I read in the last chapter of the book. He met a man who visited the hells, on multiple occasions.

Well, that was enough to make me email the guy shortly before the detective showed up. I told him a little about my thing and how it works and asked him if we could talk. That evening he got back to me and offered to meet. I’m supposed to have lunch with him tomorrow.

It got me thinking about the person I talked to on AOL all those years ago. I mean, I don’t think there’s any way to pull any of that up, but there could be something on my old PC. If I have time before the meeting tomorrow I’ll swing by my parents’ house and grab it. Just thinking of myself doing a deep dive on my old hard drive is laughable, but I can cross that bridge when I get to it.

The hell that night was one I like to call the Dalí Hell, after the old artist who painted the creepy clocks. It was one of the ones that always bothered me, because everything there is in a continuous state of semi-solidity. I can touch objects when I’m over there, but not the people or spirits or whatever they are. They can’t touch me either. That means that every step in Dalí Hell sends ripples across the ground that cause the trees and buildings and things to warm and drip into each other.

The tormented souls that exist there make my skin crawl. They suffer in a constant, shifting existence. If they bump into one-another their masses will bleed together like the stuff in a lava lamp. I watched one woman there who turned to look at me and I guess she moved too fast, because her left eye slid over her nose and merged with the other one. I almost threw up. Her eyes were a double-yolk egg that blinked at me.

My ten minute visit couldn’t end quickly enough after seeing that.

Not a lot happened yesterday, at least until last night. That morning I got a call from my boss. She told me that the police had come by and spoken to her and the people in HR, and afterwards they had a meeting about it. They basically decided that it might be best if I took a leave of absence until I’m in a better situation to return to work. With everything that’s going on I was happy to agree with it, even though my savings leave a lot to be desired.

Maia’s sister also texted me a few times. I had met Mina before and she was always pretty nice to me. Her messages skipped all of that entirely, and I understood. She’s worried about Maia, just like me, and she doesn’t know what I know. Hell, how could I tell her?

She asked me if I knew anything or if I’d heard from her. When I told her I didn’t and I hadn’t, she didn’t text for a while. Later that evening she sent me one more, a short one. It said, “Well the cops think you do.”

That left me spiraling, wondering just how bad I had fucked things up with the detective. I haven’t been able to think about that part of it—what would happen to me. My mind is too fuzzy for that, dominated by the image of her stuck over there. She doesn’t even understand what’s happened to her and every bit of it comes back to me and my carelessness. I can’t worry about going to jail when she’s going through that. A prison sentence would be like a tropical vacation by comparison.

Whatever happens to me is okay as long as it brings her back to the people who love her.

Speaking of guilt, I need to tell you about last night. When I flipped over I instantly knew I was in the right place. I was back in the black not-water stuff that surrounds the marble island. I could even see it there, ahead of me. It brought a rush of energy flowing into, like I had access to my next three winds, nevermind the second one. I started swimming as hard as I could, willing myself to be faster. I needed to get there quickly enough to have time to search for her.

I never made it to the stone, though. As I drew closer to it I heard what sounded like a sharp burst of wind and something struck me hard from the side. It sent me tumbling and sliding across that oily surf and when I finally skidded to a stop I was even farther away than where I started. I screamed in anguish and looked for the thing that hit me.

It was on me before I even saw it.

A cloud of darkness separated itself from the void and grabbed hold of me. We spun together like a vortex in that murky sea. I saw that its shape was vaguely human even though I couldn’t discern its features. I could see its eyes though. They were filled with hate or hunger, and they shone with a bruised violet light within that shadowy mist.

Somehow I was able to squirm free of the thing and I started to flee from it. I could feel the tingling in my joints that always heralds my departure. It struck one more time before I vanished and I felt white heat on my left forearm as its claws slashed me. A moment later I was lying bleeding on my carpet as I stared up at the clock on my nightstand.

It read 1:30 am.

Something over there touched me last night, and I was there for twelve minutes.
