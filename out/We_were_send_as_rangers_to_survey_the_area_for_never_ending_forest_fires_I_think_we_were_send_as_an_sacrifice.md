**We were sent there as rangers to survey the area for the never-ending forest fires. I think we were sent there as sacrifices.**

I never knew what I wanted to be in life. Growing up in a small town near Haddonfield, I was surrounded by quiet suburban neighborhoods. For years, I lacked any kind of motivation and picked up wacky jobs to earn a buck during summer breaks. All that changed in the eleventh grade, when my uncle came to visit.

Uncle Robert, or Big Robert, as he was known in the family, had always been an outlier. While the rest of his generation pursued jobs like engineering and politics, he was steadfast in his desire to become a park ranger. And he was going to take his job very seriously.

While most park rangers were young adults who wanted to escape societal pressure by sipping hot chocolate on the balcony of a lookout tower, Big Robert wanted a never-ending challenge. And the park gave him one. From constantly fixing the generator under the tower to getting called in to investigate smoke, there was always something to do. Most of the time, it was caused by dumb teens screwing around with blazing sticks or forgetting to tend to their kindling, but sometimes it was something way more bizarre.

“Are you in?” Robert's voice came from the other end of the line. “The pay isn't great, but if you want a break, well... it doesn't get better than this.”

Without a second of hesitation, I agreed. A few days later, Robert drove me out to the site.

The tower was massive, its frame leaning over a big patch of withered grass. On each corner stood two massive stage lights, used for surveying the surroundings at night. It was magnificent.

Robert opened his mouth to speak.

“Okay, the others will arrive in about an hour.”

I looked at him, confused. He hadn't mentioned that I was going to have co-workers.

“Wait, what co-workers?”

“Did you really think you could manage the tower alone?” he responded in a belittling tone.

“Don't worry. I looked into their backgrounds beforehand. Anthony and Sophie. Anthony is around twenty and graduated with a degree in botany and zoology. Sophie, meanwhile, is an electrical engineer. I think they'll be of big help, especially with the stuff going on around here.”

We made our way up the stairs, the fresh air of a thousand pines enveloping our lungs. As I reached the last stair, I peered inside. The whole room was decorated with Christmas lights hanging around the walls, while a large oak table sat on the far side. On top of it were countless electronic devices that emitted radio frequencies.

“Umm, what are those?” I asked curiously.

“Those are your gateway to the outside. Without them, you're stranded here, miles of wood and dirt on every side.”

Uncle Robert was suddenly interrupted by a call and rushed downstairs.

“Come on, they're here.”

I couldn't hide my curiosity. After all, Robert had hyped them up as my escape from boredom out here.

A blue Toyota Corolla pulled up, its roof covered with packed suitcases of all sizes.

Two people stepped out, dressed in park ranger uniforms.

Anthony and Sophie, I thought to myself.

Anthony was a guy around 5'9", with a durag on his head and a mustache on his upper lip. Sophie was a nerdy-looking girl with big, square glasses, brown curly hair, and a freckled, smug-looking face.

This was going to be interesting.

Uncle Robert spoke up first.

“Well, I'm going to get going. You all better behave.”

And with that, he walked off into the forest, whistling.

After a few seconds of silence, Anthony spoke up.

“Wassup? You Mike?”

“Yeah,” I answered.

He extended his hand, and I shook it.

“Nice meeting you. This place looks dope. Very rustic.”

“Yeah, I like it as well,” said Sophie. “Nice meeting you guys.”

Mike got to know everyone, and they spent the rest of the morning and afternoon unloading things in the tower.

Evening slowly befell the forest, casting a golden glow over the countless pine trees. In the distance, a light tower switched its lights on and off in Morse code. Robert switched their lights in return.

“What are they saying?” Sophie asked while stepping outside with a warm cup of hot chocolate.

“Nothing much. There will be a thunderstorm tonight. Strange wildlife activity has also been reported in the area. Probably riled up by the increased pressure in the atmosphere.”

“Anthony, what do you think?”

“I think something is going on,” Anthony responded somberly.

“I suggest we take a little expedition tonight. Who is coming with me?”

“I am. Let's take the rifle!” Anthony responded with excitement, grabbing the flashlight.

“Hold on, you two,” said Robert, stepping in front of the door.

“Rule of the rangers: either we're all coming, or no one is.”

“Let's split into teams. Me and you, Michael, will go towards the fire in the woods. Anthony and Sophie, you pay a visit to the nearby lookout tower. You got that?”

“Yes, sir,” they all responded in unison, half-jokingly.

The teams split up, sharing two walkie-talkies.

“Okay. We're going to be Team Alpha,” said Robert. “You two will be Beta. Don't use them unless you've got something important to say.”

And with that, Team Alpha and Team Beta parted ways, one heading toward the big fire and the other toward the nearby tower.

Anthony and Sophie slowly made their way through the foliage, the silence periodically shattered by a branch cracking under the weight of Anthony's boots.

“Anthony... Are you shivering?” she asked, smiling.

“N-no, I'm not scared or anything. I'm just cold.”

“Do you want me to hug you? Maybe you'll get warmer,” Sophie said with a nerdy chuckle.

Anthony looked at the ground, playing with his mustache.

“I'm a bit worried. I don't think splitting up was a good idea. Plus, I doubt this rifle is going to do much with all the rust on it.”

“I'm sorry,” Sophie slowly responded, playing with her belt. “I've kind of been a jerk with you, provoking you and stuff. The thing is, Robert and Mike make such a great team. I just felt left behind. And with you being all nonchalant, I wanted to lighten the mood, y'know? After all, what was I supposed to do? Give you a smooch?”

Anthony blushed a bit and looked ahead.

“I suppose we do make a great team.”

Before Anthony could say another word, Sophie leaned in and gave him a peck on the cheek.

He was left frozen for a second, but he was soon jolted awake by a guttural scream.

“What the fuck is that?” she shouted toward the direction of the noise, then quickly began following it.

Anthony, left completely shocked, lost track of his co-worker.

Sophie pushed the foliage aside and discovered a deer on the ground, wailing.

On its torso was a large open wound of exposed flesh and intestines. However, they appeared to be moving, writhing and squelching like rabid ribbon worms. The deer's eyes were beginning to overflow with blood as it screamed louder and louder.

Anthony finally caught up and looked in horror at the scene.

“Hell nah! What in the world is that? Sophie, we gotta go! That deer is straight out of The Thing.”

“N-no, Anthony! Wait!”

“Look at the worms. They appear to be made out of the same material as the deer was. Polymorphic in structure, they seem to resemble carbon polymers, using them to imitate everything around them.”

“SOPHIE! Stop with the sci-fi shit! Let's go!”

As if triggered by the noise, the deer let out a final wail and exploded, sending bits of its intestines flying like shrapnel and smashing into Sophie's face and neck.

She collapsed on the ground as the worms burrowed into her flesh, making her convulse. Her eyes went bloodshot, and her teeth began falling out as the veins in her face and arms bulged.

Anthony crouched down next to Sophie, tears streaming down his face.

“N-no... What the fuck... This is not real...”

He got up and dialed Robert on the walkie-talkie.

“Yo, old man... S-Sophie is gone. The forest took her. Please help us... Help her.”

He leaned down next to her twitching body and collapsed in tears.

Rob and Michael ran like rabid dogs through the forest. Trees rushed past Michael's vision, blurring into an ever-changing overgrowth of foliage. The forest seemed to twist and shift, like it was trying to kick the parasites out.

As they reached the clearing, they spotted Anthony standing near the ground, as tendrils of flesh twisted around his body, slowly pulling him into the soil beneath.

Michael tried to reach his co-worker, but Rob's iron grip held him back.

“Mike, he's far gone. We've gotta go. NOW.”

The two ran back for what felt like hours until they reached the staircase of the tower.

“Michael, what's wrong?!”

“Robert... They're dead. Was this all your plan?” he snapped back.

“Bring us here as a fucking offering for your little science project???”

Robert sat in silence for a second and then opened his mouth.

“A few months ago, my previous colleague and I were out exploring the wilderness when we discovered an unmarked cave. Inside sat an organism never documented by science before. It was like a tumor of tendrils, writhing and twitching. It was something, all right.

“We would go there every day, take samples, and document its properties while also bringing the thing dead carcasses. Eventually, we halted our research and came back into the city when winter came. I was sure the biomass was too weak to survive temperatures below zero.

“But it did.

“It hid in the belly of a bear, festering and nurturing itself from the heat. When I returned, I wasn't alone. I brought my nephew and his friends.

“On the first night, I went back and discovered it was still alive and bigger than ever, having spread through underground tunnels.

“I decided then, with sadness, that your lives and the fate of the forest were too important for my little science project, so I burned it. I burned the living hell out of it.

“I-It should have died.

“But... this thing... defies all logic.

“It escaped by burrowing underground and infecting everything in its sight. I had no idea. If I had, I would have never brought you here, like meat to the slaughterhouse.”

Michael sat in silence and then slowly wrapped his arms around Robert, pulling him into a hug.

“Let's go end this thing, John Carpenter style.”

The two loaded about eight drums of gasoline into the back of Robert's truck and, after saying goodbye to the watchtower they'd called home for the last few days, set out toward the cave.

As they drove, the quiet forest was dead silent.

“Where are the creatures?” Michael asked.

“Back to their lair. The cave. I've seen that before. After a big catch, they retreat to trigger some sort of accelerated evolution. My guess is they're an amalgamation of DNA from everything they've consumed.”

The two parked the truck outside and unloaded the drums, setting them at the entrance.

“What's the plan?”

“This is what we're going to do. Take a tank and rub gasoline all over your body. The things attack by smell, and the petrol will cover your scent.”

“How does that make any sense? The gas is biological material.”

Rob snapped back while dipping himself in the dark liquid.

“It just does! With all its biomass, it's still no match for human ingenuity.”

The two slowly pushed the barrel into the cave, and Michael was left with his mouth wide open.

The walls were covered in bioluminescent biomass that glowed in all the colors of the rainbow, almost like an ever-changing fractal. The tendrils were twisted into floral patterns adorning the walls, and all of it came from a small black cube.

“You ready?” asked Robert.

“Been ready for years.”

“Go be happy and forget about this place. And always remember something. You don't need to know what you're going to do with your whole life, Mike. Just don't waste it waiting for something to give you a reason. Find something, or someone, you care about, and when the time comes, have the courage to stand for it.”

Michael closed his eyes, trying to keep tears from falling, and quickly ran out of the cave. He got into the car and drove away, speeding down the highway as the morning sun finally revealed itself and spoiled the forest, casting the horrors of the night aside.

Behind him, a large explosion erupted, blasting his windshield and making him stop the car with a screeching halt.

Taking a moment to step out, he looked up at the horizon, watching it grow even more luxurious, as though the tumor that had been feeding on its grounds was finally free.

Robert sighed and looked back at the car.

“Man, thank God break's over.”
