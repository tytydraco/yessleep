This happened a while ago. I was 15 at the time.. My mom, my dad and I moved into a small house at the edge of town. I don’t know where dad found the place, and I never will. We used to live in California, but Dad got a job in a small town in Oregon, so we moved.

I won’t describe my family much, but I will say a few things now. I loved my family, especially my Mom. I loved my Dad and he loved me, but he was always very distant. For the first 15 years of my life we moved all around California, Dad going from job to job. This was the first, and last, time we moved to a different state.

Before I get to the house, I should probably talk about the town. We only lived there for a year, but most of that time was wonderful. That was mainly the reason why I haven’t left Oregon. I left the town, but I never left Oregon.

It was a very ordinary small town, nothing remarkable. Pretty much everyone knew everyone. There were odd things, odd people, and some would say the town had a dark history. I won’t deny that it did, but this isn’t about what happened in the town’s past. It's about what happened in the last year of my life there.

I quickly made a few friends. Tim is the most notable, and the only one still alive. He moved a month before I left. He was 17 at the time, and lived there his whole life. When I arrived, Tim lived with his parents and sister. When he moved, he only had his sister. This isn’t Tim’s story, but maybe he’ll tell you some other time. He has a lot of stories.

When I was still there, me, Tim, and the others would sit by the lake at night and share spooky stories and urban legends. Tim loved to tell stories. The others didn’t believe many of them, but I did. Tim doesn’t tell stories if he didn’t believe them. Many even happened to him. Again, maybe he’ll tell them sometime.

The first urban legend Tim ever told me is the one most important to our story. Because It had to do with the house.

The house my family lived in was small, but I didn’t hate it. But it always made me uneasy when I stepped inside. At the top of the house, attached to the roof, was a long silk rope that extended into the sky, as far as the eye can see. According to legend, to Tim, the rope had been there as long as the house. The house was apparently never officially built. It just appeared one day. I don’t know if I believe that part, but I wouldn't be surprised if it is. Tim did though.

On our first day there, me and my father got up onto the roof. I brought my pocket knife, I don't remember why exactly, maybe to cut the rope. The oddest thing was that the rope wasn’t tied to anything, it was just stuck to the roof of the house. 

I moved closer to the rope, so I could cut it. My father gripped my wrists, and threw my knife off the roof. “Don’t! Don’t touch it.” I didn’t, and I never went up onto the roof again.

For the next year, nothing happened. Occasionally I had to chase some kids away from trying to climb it. They never got far. Sometimes, Dad would sit on the roof at night. I think he was just looking at the rope. He never touched it.

Five days before I left, my father told me something that made me worry.

“I’m going to climb the rope today.” Those were the last words he ever said.

The next few minutes were a panic. I don’t know why mom and I were panicking, but we were. We tried to talk to him, to say it was a bad idea. But he didn’t stop, I don't even think he heard us. Like he was in a trance, my father climbed onto the roof and gripped the rope.

The house began to shake, like someone was trying to pull it up. At last, the roof gave way, tearing away from the house. That was the last I saw of him, my dad being carried away, chunks of roof falling onto the now ruined house. He didn’t look scared, he didn’t look like anything.

Eventually, I couldn’t see any of the roof anymore.

Mom and I just stood there, on the yard, looking at our ruined house. Soon, the roof fell back down, so did my dad’s remains. Not much of him was left, just blood and a few flaps of skin.

Mom wanted to leave as soon as we could. I couldn’t blame her, I did too. Over the next few days, we packed up as much as we could into the car. Then the rope came back down.

Now, attached to it was a hand. My father’s hand. I knew it was his hand because it had his ring.

At first it didn’t move, it didn’t do anything. Mom called the police. What else could she do? Officers came to investigate, and that's when it moved.

It gripped the closest officer by the face and pulled the poor man into the sky. Soon, the officer’s skin and blood came raining down on us. The rope came down quickly this time, now it was above us.

It gripped my mother by the face and pulled her kicking and screaming into the sky. Then, what happened to my dad and the officer happened to her. Blood and skin.

I wasn’t there when the last officer was taken. I was already halfway across town. I didn’t see what happened next. 

According to one of my friends, the hand disappeared that day, up into the sky. It hasn’t come back.

There wasn’t much to bury, and I couldn’t bring myself to go back. So I buried their photos.
