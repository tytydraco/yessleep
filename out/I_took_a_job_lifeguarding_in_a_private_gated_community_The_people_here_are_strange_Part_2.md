Viktoria and I stood on a pier in Cape Cod at the end of a long summer together. Life was perfect in a way it hadn’t been in a long, long time.

Her hair was golden like the sunset. Her grey cotton dress hugged her body from head to toe as though she’d just stepped out of the water. I leaned in, taking her face in my hand as her hands wrapped around my back, fingernails sending shockwaves down my skin.

But as her fingernails grazed my skin, the sound was just…wrong.

Like metal scraping something.

I blinked, and the pale light of dawn streamed in under the blinds as I realized I was hearing metal on concrete. Something was being dragged across the pool deck.

How long had I been out? *Shit.*

I sprang up and threw open the door to the outside as a thousand nightmarish scenarios ran through my head, mostly involving me being shown the door and told to pack my things.

I blinked again as I realized that “something” was the lifeguard stand, which had somehow been ripped from its foundation and was now teetering on the edge of the deep end. There was a small splash as something - presumably whatever had pulled it, and I was more than happy not knowing whatever that might be - slipped back under the surface. I took in my surroundings.

The pool deck was in complete and total disarray. Several chairs and tables were toppled over. An umbrella stuck up out of the pool like it was doing a handstand. I had my work cut out for me, but if there was any upside to this, it was that the pool wasn’t totally in ruins.

I was nearly done getting everything back in order when I heard a voice behind me, not unlike a parent in a teen movie when they get home and see the house wrecked from a party.

“What on earth has happened here?!”

Vitaly. *Shit.*

I turned toward him as he strode towards me.

“Uhh…storm last night. Kinda messed stuff up.”

“I was at the Boston Red Sox game. Clear sky all night. Do not bullshit me.”

As shit as the Red Sox were that year, that didn’t really seem like whatever administrative tasks he claimed to have had. Still, I also couldn’t call him out either.

“I’m sorry... it was a long day. Church freaked me out a little bit and so I was emotionally exhausted as well.”

I saw a flicker of sympathy across his face, and I had a feeling church had been on his radar but he had forgotten to mention it yesterday. He took one look at the security camera in the foreground, then started wildly gesturing at me while keeping his voice down, as though he was performing for the camera.

“Alright…I let you off the hook this one time since you did volunteer for this and it does appear that I forgot to inform you of the schedule change for church. So that is on me. But this is your only warning. This cannot happen again. You understand?”

Relief flooded through me. “Yes, absolutely…..thank you so much.”

“Yes. Now…help me finish fixing up the rest of the pool, then go home and get some rest. You will not participate in the straw drawing tonight.” That was more than fine with me.

I went home. Slept for about six hours. When I got up, the other three wanted to know everything about the night shift. I just told them it was easy. Nothing much happened. It wasn’t that I didn’t want to tell them about the church meeting. I myself still wasn’t sure whether I had been hearing things or not and wanted nothing more than to tell them everything. But something kept me from doing so.

We went back earlier in the afternoon for our shift that day. Got there about 20 minutes early to a very pleased Vitaly. He clapped his hands together in satisfaction as he saw us approaching. “Now THAT is more like it!”

The deck looked…normal. As though nothing had happened to it the following night. I mean, it hadn’t been that hard to put everything back. Vitaly had made me wait before hopping in the deep end to get the few chairs and umbrellas that had wound up in there. Wanted to make sure the “cleaning system was off.”

I couldn’t help but notice, though, that the guard stand was right back in its place. Firmly cemented, as though nothing had ripped it out of its foundation the night before. I wanted to ask how they had managed to get it back in that quickly but decided not to. Figured some things were better left unanswered, and the answer would be much simpler than I expected. Assuming he called some guys in as soon as I left, they could have picked it up with some serious effort, put in some quick drying cement, and no one would be the wiser.

Viktoria had the night shift that night, followed by Ricky, then Cody. I noticed a pattern the mornings after - they would arrive home, and the rest of us would want to know all about it. Anything crazy happen? You would see the forced reluctance in their eyes, but they just cheerfully waved it off. I had to give Ricky and Cody a look that second morning, one that said “don’t press it” without actually saying it, and that seemed to do the trick.

Lord knows what happened on Cody’s overnight. He let out the most forced sigh I had ever heard before he even walked in the door. “WOW. Easiest fuckin shift ever!” We knew he was bullshitting us, because he looked like he’d been through a war. We also knew not to ask.

Vitaly took Friday and Saturday night. Saturday was the regularly scheduled church service, he said, and he needed to have a word with Pastor Patterson beforehand. He gave me a look right before saying this, mostly telling me he didn’t entirely trust me to not fall asleep again on the shift, but that was fine with me.

Sunday night was “scheduled maintenance” before the pool would begin normal operating hours since school was now out. The other five guards had orientation that Monday, and would begin working throughout the week, replacing one of us each shift.

They were all fine. But the four of us had already bonded, and we kept gravitating toward each other.

About a month in, maybe mid-June, Ricky and I had a night shift together. The church was having a baptism that night. Ricky had been voluntold for the shift to get back in Vitaly’s good graces after, hungover one Sunday morning, he had grabbed the wrong shirt for work. Viktoria was pissed about something or other, so I jumped at the opportunity.

By this point, I was no stranger to the services, because my night shifts just so happened to land on them half the time. I figured Vitaly thought I was best accustomed to handling them once I had proven I wouldn't fall asleep, and I felt a weird pride in that.

So, once we got our dinner heated up and were situated in the guard office, I propped the front door open, pulled down the front and back blinds, and shut the door to the guard office. I looked over. Ricky was watching me, a mixture of curiosity and confusion on his face.

“Aren’t we supposed to leave the blinds open for night shift?”

“Oh…yeah, but Pastor Patterson likes them closed. Says his parishioners are shy. Something like that.”

I tried to avoid elaborating as much as possible. I’d learned over time that certain things were left…unsaid.

He nodded, though uncertainly. “Gotcha.”

We settled in. I kept a curious eye on Ricky. I wasn’t sure if he’d worked a church night yet, and it was sort of like watching a horror movie with someone, and you knew a jump scare was coming and they didn’t.

As if on cue, the chanting started a few hours later. Slowly, at first. I could never be sure where exactly it started.

Ricky raised an eyebrow slightly. “…you hear that, dude.?” he asked.

I wasn’t sure how, or if I even should react. Didn’t want to gaslight him but also wasn’t sure how he’d react if I validated him. So I played dumb. Tried to look confused. “Hear what?”

“That weird…moaning noise over there. Like over by the clubhouse. But now it’s getting closer?”

The chanting was louder now. Like they were coming outside. I tried to picture a bunch of guys in hooded robes processing through a men’s locker room and it wasn’t nearly as funny as I thought it would be.

“Dude….it’s like, right outside now… what the fuck is going on?!”

Ricky started to get up, almost certainly to try and raise the blinds and get a look. I grabbed his hand and pulled him back down. He no longer looked confused. He now looked genuinely frightened, and I could tell the chanting was already starting to get to him. I remembered that first night. Lord help me if the keening started.

“Dude. You gotta sit down. Please.”

He ripped his hand out of mine. I could see that he was panicking now and trying and failing to hide it.

“Get your hands off me, man! WHAT THE FUCK IS GOING ON HERE?!” he screamed, and I hoped with everything I had that something outside hadn’t heard us. I could almost hear it in my mind -sudden, unbearable silence. Followed by slow, steadily increasing knocking on the window from the outside. Then the doorknob would shake, as if someone was trying to get in. Slowly, at first. Then the entire door shaking, as if someone was trying to break in.

As if to answer his question, the chanting was replaced by a low hum. Something I hadn’t heard before. It wasn’t from a machine, but nor was it from a human. This was something entirely unnatural. And, while I couldn’t tell specifically, it seemed to be coming straight from the pool.

*The baptism.*

Ricky began to cry. He looked at me, tears streaming down his face, sobbing.

“Dude…what is going on here… WHY WON'T YOU ANSWER ANY OF MY QUESTIONS?!”

I looked at him, at a loss for what to do.

“I’m sorry man…my guess is as good as yours… I’ve never dealt with this before.”

He grimaced. Scowled at me with puffy, red-rimmed eyes.

“That’s just not fucking good enough.”

With that, he sprang for the blinds.

I’m not sure how close he got to actually pulling them. Or if he ever got a hand on them to begin with. But it seemed to me like he was just about to pull them, exposing us to whatever horrors lay right beyond the glass, when I grabbed him by the waist and pulled him down.

We rolled around on the floor, Ricky trying to get me off him, me trying to hold him down. I think he was ready to fully rip the blinds off the windows at this point, not just pull them up.

I’m not sure why I didn’t think of it until then, but I felt like a total dumbass when I did.

Out of the corner of my eye, I saw my headphones and phone lying on the office desk. Managed to extract myself, pin Ricky down with my knee and barely, just barely stretch out enough to grab my headphones and shove them onto his head.

The chanting had returned by this point and was nearly overwhelming. It was almost in my head. I grabbed my phone and found the first white noise playlist I could. Some “rain on pavement” thing. Turned my phone to max volume and hit play.

Up until this point, Ricky had been in such a frenzy that I doubt he had been aware of the headphones until now, because a look of confusion filled his face. He put his hands up and touched the headband and earpieces.

The plan had worked. He could no longer hear the chanting. Me, I could still hear it fine. It was awful, don’t get me wrong, but like I said, I had worked a couple services by now and had more or less gotten used to it.

Ricky took a deep breath. Flopped over onto his back. Put his hands on his face and started to silently cry.

I sat down in a chair. Put my feet up on the guard desk. Didn’t close my eyes, but I stared up at the fluorescent ceiling lights until spots appeared in my vision. Tried to mentally drown out the chanting. Did some thinking, about nothing much in particular.

I don’t want to say “when I came to” because I never fell asleep, but you get what I mean. When I popped back to reality, I guess, my phone and headphones were on the desk in front of me. The chanting was gone. I could hear sniffling. Looked behind me to see Ricky sitting in the corner. Knees scrunched in front of him. Eyes red and puffy. Looked like he had been through hell, which he probably had been mentally. Why he wasn’t in the chair was beyond me. Maybe thought I was upset with him.

He looked up at me. Sniffled.

“Man…I’m so sorry… I don’t know what came over me.”

“It’s all good buddy. Happens to the best of us.”

He let out a choked laugh. “You tried to tear the windows down because you couldn’t handle a little noise?”

I snorted.

“Well…not quite that.”

“I… thank you for putting the headphones on me. No one’s ever done anything that nice for me before.”

I refrained from telling him that it had felt pretty life or death to me.

“Hey…anytime. Figured you could use a little distraction.”

“Understatement of the fucking century right there.”

We laughed, harder this time.

“I ever tell you about my parents?” he asked, throwing me for a loop.

“I’m sorry?”

“My parents. Why I’m living at the house for the summer when my parents live five minutes away, on the other side of the neighborhood.”

“You’ve mentioned it in passing. Something about how you were held back a year. Parents booted you for the summer.”

“Got it.” He took a deep breath before continuing.

“I never knew my dad. He left when my mom was pregnant with me. Claimed he wasn’t ready to raise a kid. Was just my mom and me for years. She really did try her best, I’ll give her that. But I’ve seen pictures of him. It’s like they took him and hit copy and paste. My mom also didn’t really have a support system. She had moved across the country after a rough childhood herself - she named me after the doctor that delivered me. No, I’m not joking.”

I think he could see I thought he was joking there.

“Anyhow, she tried her best, but there was always some distance between us because my dad had fucked her over so badly. Like, don’t get me wrong. She was a great mom. I had a great childhood. It’s just… sometimes, she would look at me, and I don’t think she realized I was aware, but I think all she could see in those moments were the guy that left her high and dry with a newborn. So it was just us for years. I could tell we were struggling at times, but she did her best to hide it."

"When I was in 8th grade, my football coach had this end of season summer cookout. She came out of her bedroom when it was time to go, and she looked put together in a way I had never seen before. Not that she usually looked frumpy or anything, but she was always tired from working, you know? She had on this long, summery blue dress I had never seen before. Her hair was straightened. She had even thrown some makeup on. The entire cookout I could see his eyes were on her. Thank God he didn’t have a wife or kids, that would have been a scandal.

"But yeah. They both disappeared near the end for like 30 minutes. I was playing kickball with some other kids and at some point, she comes up and is like it’s time to go. Her hair was kinda messy. Makeup is smudged in places. But she was glowing in a way I’d never seen before. He started coming by more and it didn’t take a genius to know what was going on. "

"He proposed at Christmas. No joke. They married 6 months later. He’s been an amazing dad. Don’t get me wrong about that. But they had a baby maybe like a year ago. My mom dotes on that kid in a way she never ever did with me. Bums me out a bit, but again, she was never outright, like, a total bitch growing up, so I can’t complain, you know?”

"Right around the time Benny - that’s my half-brother’s name - turned 1, we moved here because our growing family supposedly needed more space. That was six months ago. Right after that, my dad caught wind that the country club would house lifeguards in the house we’re living in. I guess he coaches Craft’s grandson now."

"Since I was already planning on lifeguarding this summer, and I’ve been pretty independent my whole life on account of having a single mom for 14 years, they asked if I would mind living in the lifeguard house this summer. Give them some more space with the baby, whatever that means. They said they would still come by the pool to see me, check in on me and stuff, and whatnot. They haven’t come by once. Not a peep from them either. Asked my mom if we could call once, just to catch up since, you know, it’s been a fucking month since I last talked to her…said she was tired with the baby, and she’d text when she woke back up. That was a week ago. That… that’s kind of sucked. Not gonna lie.”

He swallowed a lump in his throat. Wiped a hand across his face. I put a hand on his back.

“Jesus, man…. I’m sorry.”

“It’s all good…just life, you know?”

I nodded. Not much else I could do. Then, he looked at me again.

“Do you notice anything?”

“Like…?”

“The blinds are open.”

“Right. They’re supposed to be. The chanting stopped like an hour ago.”

“Right…. but did you open them? Because I didn’t.”

We looked out onto the dark, unlit pool deck. Nothing was out of place. No deck furniture was sticking out of the water. Lifeguard stand was in place. According to my phone it was about 75 degrees outside.

And yet, unbeknownst to us before, a chill suddenly filled the room.

We went about the rest of the early morning as normal. Once it had lightened up a bit outside and we were more at ease, we decided to try and earn some brownie points by getting everything set up for the day ourselves.

Vitaly arrived. Winked at me as he took in the spotless pool deck, and then Ricky’s rather bedraggled appearance.

“Eventful night?”

Ricky couldn’t stop himself.

“Fuck you.”

Where another boss might have been, well, pissed, Vitaly just chuckled.

“I’ll give you that one. I’m sorry. I know from Luke that those... services can be a bit overwhelming the first time. How can I remedy this for you.”

“Never give me a church night again. Please.”

He sighed. “That I cannot promise. But please. Get some rest. Take this and get yourself some breakfast. Get Luke some too. No coffee. You need sleep today.”

He handed Ricky the gold credit card he had handed me the first night.

“Do not tell anyone else about this. I will know if you do.”

He walked off, in the direction of what I assumed was the pump room.

Ricky did not use the card to buy himself breakfast. He left it on the desk, where Vitaly would see it. When we got home, he went into his room and shut the door. He had the day off, which was good, because he did not emerge until dinner almost 12 hours later.

There was one day in, probably early July, that I remember vividly.

It was just Cody and I that day. Vitaly had planned an outing to that evening’s Red Sox game. Six guards had gone, including Ricky and Viktoria.

I remember it particularly because Viktoria, who I don’t think was familiar with how casual baseball games are, had spent the entire morning and early afternoon shopping for an outfit for the game. She had finally come out in a figure hugging, red and white striped maxi dress. Her hair was down in waves. She looked breathtaking, although I kept this to myself.

Unfortunately for her, what Cody chose not to keep to himself was that she looked more like she was going to a polo match than a baseball game, and that earned him a hearty “fuck you!” and a middle finger that we noticed had a tattoo on it, a swallow, as she flipped her hair and strode out the door.

The day went by uneventfully. Cody had volunteered to cook that night, but then decided he didn’t feel like cooking, so he invited me over for dinner at his parents’ house.

Cody’s house was not what I expected, so let me leave it like this: he had the personality of someone who lived in a picture and signs house, you know? Family pictures everywhere, and “Live, Laugh, Love” signs where there weren’t. Little kids running around. Very relaxed and homely. Cluttered in a way that everything made sense.

What I did not expect was a house that looked like it had been plucked from Architectural Digest. His parents looked like they had strolled out of the L.L. Bean summer catalog. And, not that there’s anything wrong with that whatsoever. I just didn’t expect it.

“Hey there, bud.” His dad.

“Dinner in 30, Code.” His mom.

I nodded at both as we passed - neither really seemed inclined to shake hands.

Instead of heading to his room, we went straight through the back of the house, and out into an enormous, very well-manicured backyard that looked out onto the forests bordering the edge of the neighborhood. Headed to a treehouse in the back.

We climbed up the ladder, and, as we entered, he made for a toy box in the back, pulled out a small package taped under the lid.

Took out a small cigarette, lit it, took a hit.

“Fuck, man. Been waiting all day for one of these. You want a hit?”

“Sure. You good?”

He let out a small cough as he absorbed the smoke into his lungs. Waved off a cloud of smoke and sprayed what looked like a bottle of Febreze in the air.

“More or less.”

He handed me the joint. I got a hit.

Instant warmth, followed by an instant coughing fit. I felt a cloud descend over me. It had been a minute since I’d done this.

“So…remember when I told you Craft had paid my tuition?” “Right.”

“Yeah, so I failed out. Couldn’t cut it. And they’re gonna go to pay for the fall - albeit with Craft’s money - and they’re gonna find out, and I’ll be in deep, deep shit.”

“And you haven’t told them, why?” I knew it was a stupid question before I asked. Luckily, Cody was mellowed out now, so he took it in stride.

“Well, for starters - I am - or I was, now - a third-generation student there. Grandfather went there. Then my parents met there. Both of my brothers went there. So, not sure how well it would be taken if I told them I’d failed out.”

“Can’t you, like, just…take a semester off, get your grades back up, and go back?”

He laughed, a hint of bitterness on his tone.

“Yeah… fat fucking chance of that happening. I hated school, man. Hated it. All I did was party. I’ll be honest, I was that kid in high school that didn’t have to try. Then I went to college. Rudest reality check I’ve ever had.”

“And your parents never checked? Asked about anything?”

“Like I said, I had my shit together enough in high school that they didn’t feel the need to. I avoided that dreaded letter home by the skin of my teeth. I don’t even remember most of the spring semester. They did send a letter home then. I just made sure they didn’t see it.”

“Are you gonna tell them?”

The look he gave me told me he was done with the questions. “Fuck if I know, man. I’m just skating by, one day at a time.”

Dinner - lamb chops and some sort of summer salad - was incredible. As were the homemade Long Island Ice Teas they made us. The family dog, Otto, also seemed to very much like the lamb chops, because he kept climbing onto my lap and trying to get at them.

Tension sucked the air out of the room, though, when Cody’s brother, Austin, made the ill-advised decision to bring up the check from Craft.

Austin worked in private equity-something in Boston and had come for dinner, for which I was beginning to think Cody had invited me to for moral support.

“So…” he said. “This check is a…scholarship?”

“Yes, it is.” said Maureen, Cody’s mom.

“And this is for… what? Having the highest grades out of all the lifeguards? Something like that?”

“It’s for service to the community. Your brother has been lifeguarding since he was 14. You know that.”

“Oh, right. Sorry… wish I’d have known, maybe I’d have…tried a little harder. So that’s all he did.”

“I saved a guard last year. Pulled up to open the pool. Craft was there. One of the foreign guards was lying on the edge of the woods. He’d had a seizure and fallen and messed up his face pretty bad. Not sure if he survived. But I got the award for that. Now, please. Shut the fuck up.”

“CODY BROOKS WARREN!!” his mom.

“… Sorry.” he said, still glowering.

“Sorry, brother.” Austin said, smirking. “Didn’t mean to hit a nerve like that.”

Cody didn’t say anything back.

“Guess who got a date with Vitalyyyyyyy!” she announced in a very singsong voice.

The day of the date arrived – a Saturday - three weeks later because of a road trip for the Red Sox. Vitaly had scored suite tickets for the first game back, and he wanted to treat Viktoria.

It was gorgeous. Not a cloud in the sky. And, since the four of us all had the morning shift together for the first time in seemingly forever, Cody, Ricky, and I decided to grill that night while Viktoria and Vitaly were out doing their thing.

One of the things I remember most that day was that the pool was…empty. The weather was perfect for the pool. Mid 80s. People were absolutely out and about. The playground next door was popping. Kids were out on their bikes. Being near the end of July, it seemed like you’d want every last bit of pool you could get before it closed in a month. And yet I could have sworn I saw multiple people in bathing suits walk by the pool entrance… and then just walk right past.

We were all sitting on the deck in the deep end, legs in the water, just shooting the shit, when Ricky asked the question that I think had been hanging over us since the beginning of summer.

“So… what the fuck’s up with the deep end?”

You could tell Cody had begun to smoke before he came to work. Not a lot. I think the stress of his college situation had gotten to him more, and he was doing it to relax a bit.

“Alright…y’all didn’t hear this from me… I heard there’s some Lovecraft shit in the deep end. It gets so hazy because sometimes there’s no bottom and they let it out at night. That’s why we have the night watch.”

You’d have thought from Viktoria's reaction that that Cody had insulted her to her face. Because she punched his arm. Hard.

“Don’t say shit like that, Cody! Ever again!”

She glared at him. He looked at her, shocked.

“Alright…Jesus. Sorry Vik. Didn’t mean to hit such a nerve there.”

She looked back at him, face hard, before softening again.

“…it’s okay. And I’m sorry too. I don’t know why I reacted like that. I think I’m just nervous about tonight.”

I think she could tell I was about to say something, because she put a finger up.

“And, no, I can’t just call it off. I’ve been throwing myself at him this entire summer. What do I even say? Sorry! Cold feet?”

We heard a knock in the back just then.

“Helloooo! Anyone there?”

Our first patron of the day. An hour before our shift ended. Go figure.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

As it turned out, Viktoria didn’t need to come up with an excuse to cancel the date, because Vitaly gave her one.

We were in the kitchen getting everything we’d need for the night. Cody was grabbing beers from the cooler. I was grabbing the food from the fridge. Ricky was in the family room getting the speaker, so he had a front row seat to it all.

The front door thundered open with a loud bang that sounded like a bomb going off. I doubt a SWAT team could have opened it with more force than it had been.

Viktoria stood there. Soaked from head to toe. Her hair was slicked down her back, soaking wet. Her dress - the same one she’d worn to the Red Sox game - was also soaked.

I might have noticed how she looked had she not looked ready to commit murder. But I kept that to myself.

Ricky was right there to chime in, though. “What happened here? I’ll grab a towel.”

Followed by Cody. “I’ll grab another burger or two!” I saw the barest of grins crack her face.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

After drying off and opening a drink, she gave us the full story of what happened as we ate dinner in the backyard, seated around a small bonfire:

After getting off, she had gone to the women’s room to get ready for the date. “I love you guys, but that shit is like a 5-star resort.”

After getting dressed, she realized she had forgotten her makeup and decided to walk across the street to grab it.

Instead, she was greeted by screams as she exited the locker room.

Vitaly, who was supposed to be watching the pool, had gone to use the bathroom. That was enough time for a kid to - go figure - find himself struggling in the deep end. Viktoria, not even considering for a moment that she was wearing a floor length dress, had sprinted across the deck and dove headfirst into the pool, making it over to the kid in under 5 seconds. After carrying the kid to the ladder and rolling him onto the deck, she’d gotten out and stormed over to the guard office.

Vitaly had left the bathroom and walked right into Viktoria, who had loudly and profanely told him off right then and there. He’d begged her not to tell Craft. She’d said she would think about it. She had no intention of doing so but wanted to let him sweat.

“And now, I’m here.” she said, midbite. “And, quite frankly, I prefer this.”

“You do?” said Cody. As though she hadn’t spent the entire summer up to that point hanging out with us.

“…yes?” She said, with a raised eyebrow. “Have I… told you guys about my family back home?”

Our faces told her she hadn’t, and that she might have phrased the question in a way that painted her family in a…less than favorable light.

“My family back home is…very traditional. I was never really allowed to do…boy-ish things very much. I’m using this job to help pay for law school back home because my family wouldn’t pay for it. You get the idea”

“Getting to let loose and be one of the boys this summer has been the best experience of my life. I know I’m pretty much the same age as you guys and not like an old maid or anything, but it’s been…an amazing time. And I’m truly going to miss you guys when I go back home in a month.”

She wiped her hand across her face. I resisted the urge to put a hand on her back.

None of us were like, blinking away tears or anything, but I could tell Cody and Ricky were as touched as I was.

“And Cody… I’m sorry again for punching you today. That was a very stupid joke, but it still was not okay for me to do that.” 

He nodded. “All good…. but who said I was joking?” 

Viktoria laughed and punched him again. “Stop!”

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

We spent the rest of the evening outside, long after the sun went down. Drinking, playing games, listening to music.

We went to bed. Cody was in no shape to drive, so he took the couch in the living room. I lay in bed that night, trying in vain to fall asleep, but something was nagging me.

Around, I’d say,  1a.m., as I finally drifted off, I realized what it was.

After Cody denied it being a joke, we all laughed.

Except for Cody.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I had a nightmare that night.

A horrible one, in which I had laid in bed, unable to breathe or move, as hooded shadows moved throughout the house.

One came into my room. Stood over my bed. At some point, the moonlight shifted just enough to show part of a face.

The face of Craft Dunn.

I woke up around 5am to a splitting headache and knocking on my door.

It was Ricky.

“Yo…did you see Cody leave last night?”

“No... why?”

“He’s not here… but his stuff still is… and his car is still out front.”

We got a text that morning from Vitaly that Cody had fallen ill and would be out for the week. We didn’t believe him at all, but at this point, not asking was second nature.

A week passed. I texted him that Sunday.

“Hey bro - how’s it goin”

“Hey! Still super sick. Not sure when I’ll be back. Sorry!”

I might have believed it. But Cody texted in all lowercase. I had never seen him use punctuation.

This was not something he would ever write.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

Vitaly came by the house three days after I texted him. Asked us to sit down.

“I’ve been asked to inform you all that Cody will not be returning this summer due to a family emergency. He has requested privacy, so please do not reach out to him.”

He looked around.

“Where are Cody’s things? I’ve been asked to retrieve those as well.”

I pointed to the backpack against the wall. Right where he had left it.

“Right there. But I can take it back. No- “

“I will take it back. You will not. You will not contact Cody again. Is that understood?” he snapped.

*What. The. Fuck?*

“… yes?”

“Good.”

He walked over, picked it up, and quickly walked out without another word. 

Right past Cody’s car, which was still parked outside, without so much as acknowledging it.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

The first Saturday of August, I woke up and decided to start my day off with a run. This wasn’t unusual - I ran almost every morning.

What was unusual was that, after about 10 minutes, I found myself standing across the street from Cody’s house. I don’t know if it was subconscious, or something guided me there, but there I was.

There was a giant truck in the driveway, with a moving company’s logo on the side. His mom stood outside on the front stoop.

I figured this was my last chance to maybe get some closure, so I ran over.

“Hey…Mrs. Warren!”

“Oh! Hello! You are…?”

“Luke - the friend from work that Cody brought over that night? Last month?”

“Oh…yes! Did you need something?”

I could tell she wanted nothing more than for me to leave and never come back.

“Yeah…uh… I just…really enjoyed hanging out with Cody this summer. He left…rather abruptly and I wanted to make sure he was okay? We were never told what happened.”

“Oh! Right…. He…. decided to leave for college a little earlier this year. Found a great place close to campus that opened early. Got a job. With him gone…we…just felt the house was a little quieter and decided it was time to downsize to something smaller.”

She had been avoiding eye contact the entire time, but I noticed something. As she had said he was gone, her eyes had gotten a little…moist…..As though filling with tears.

“Well… let him know I wished him luck this year! And that I hope to see him around at some point again!”

“Yes, absolutely dear! Will do!”

At that moment, just as I was turning around to leave, the dog - Otto - chose that moment to turn around and scamper out through the door.

“Otto!” she cried. “Get back here!”

The dog leaped into my arms. I guess it remembered me from a month ago.

As I sat there holding and petting it, a shadow approached us. I looked up.

Craft Dunn. In the flesh.

He was shorter, stockier than I expected. He wore a golf shirt and slacks. Was tan.

He approached Ms. Warren and handed something to her. A check.

“Hi, Maureen. On behalf of Dunn Dwellings, LLC, I would like to thank you and your family for all the memories over the last 20 years. You will be missed by the community.”

She took the check without looking at him or saying anything in response. Silently walked back inside.

I took the opportunity to silently set the puppy back down and walk down the yard, before setting back off on a brisk jog. If Craft noticed me, he didn’t say anything.

A lot of things went through my mind on that 10-minute jog. Mainly Cody’s joke that first day, about Craft and the shotgun. Then his comment at dinner about the award. And then his comment that night around the fire. Tried not to notice how well the two stories fit together.

I thought of the check Craft had handed to his mom. Of her telling me he was back at school… and then him telling me he had failed out.

The way she had seemed on the brink of tears as she described him being “gone”. The way she refused to look at or speak to Craft.

Then, I realized something that nearly stopped me in my tracks in the middle of the road.

Cody had disappeared on a Saturday night.
