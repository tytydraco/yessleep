I’m alive and I don’t know how. I should be dead. I wish I were after what I’ve seen and heard, but somebody’s gotta tell the world what happened even if the world won’t believe it.  

It started with that damn UFO conspiracy podcast called Truth Nuke. I can’t remember when I started listening to it, but once I had, I couldn’t stop. I just really wanted to believe and the “Shepherd” as the host liked to style himself, would tell me whatever I wanted to hear. Things like the aliens are already among us and that the government is covering it all up. The usual bait for UFO nuts like me. But his last episode was a bit more than bait…  

"Hello real world, or those of you still claiming to live in it. It's your good Shepherd here. As always I'm here to help keep you sheep away from the wolves. Welcome one and all to TRUTH NUKE!"

A dramatic bomb sound effect rattled the car’s speakers.

I was driving, which meant we had to listen to whatever I wanted and what I wanted was of course, Truth Nuke. 

Jason tried to turn the volume down two notches. I turned it back up. 

"Today's truth nuke is a big one. After years of cover-ups and close encounters, we are on the eve of first contact. NASA claims a signal picked up from across the galaxy is nothing but a dying star. Well… Dying stars don't speak."

Then the Shepherd played the signal and I can’t tell you how much I fucking loved this sound. I knew it wasn’t supposed to. It was supposed to sound scary and all that, but it made me feel excited. It came through the car’s crappy speakers like something between a frequency and a voice. It was oscillating, layered, and almost rhythmic. I could feel it in my molars. 

“Encoded in this signal are coordinates that tell us where these aliens plan on landing once they arrive on planet earth. I’ve cracked the code, can you?” 

I glanced over at Jason. The only thing he was cracking was his pipe. The sweet, skunky smell filled the car before we'd even hit the interstate.

"C'mon man, you got to smoke that right now?"

He exhaled slowly toward the cracked window. "Why not, let's get this weekend started early! Besides, it's just open road ahead of us, what's the worst that could happen."

"We're not going to a fucking party man! We're going to make history."

"I don't think failed hypotheses make the history books."

"The operative word is failed. And there's no failure to be had today. I guarantee that."

Jason gave me the look he always gives me. It’s one that's equal parts fond of me and completely exhausted by my bullshit. "C'mon man. Why are you so convinced that this is the alien landing zone? Oh shit! Maybe cuz you've actually been an alien all along. Would explain why you're so fucking weird sometimes."

"First of all, the weird one in this car right now is the man who brought a microwave to the woods."

“You brought a generator." Jason interjected.

"Secondly” I cracked on, “how can you not be excited about this? We are close to the moment of definitive proof we're not alone, that there's more to the universe than just us. Think about what we could learn from them, all that they could teach us."

"We're not doing so bad ourselves that we need their help."

I wanted to laugh. "Are you serious? The world's on fire, the economy's in the toilet, and we still don't have girlfriends! Hell no, we need their help ASAP."

Jason scoffed, "Speak for yourself. I don't need ET to get me a girlfriend."

"What if ET wants to be your girlfriend?"

He groaned at my joke. "Oh God. Now I know where your imagination is going with this."

"My imagination is that the aliens help level us up as a species."

"This isn't fucking Star Trek man, be real. If we want a roof over our heads after we graduate, we're gonna be designing missiles for the army or something. At best we're gonna be launching rockets for Billionaires, not working alongside aliens to build flying saucers."

"If money's all you think about, then how about this. We discover aliens, we reap the rewards. Any choice of research fellowship, grants! Fuck! We could even win a Nobel prize!"

Jason was quiet for a moment. Then said with a deep sigh, "I get it. That's all gonna happen in your mind, cause in your mind it's definitely aliens. But for me, this is likely just a relaxing weekend where I can get high in the peace of nature and stare at the stars, ok? If ET does show up and is DTF like you're hoping, I'll send them your way."

I let it go and turned up the volume of the signal. I swear it got louder as we drove deeper into the country, like it was getting stronger the closer we got. Maybe it was.

The coordinates took us off the highway, down a fire road, and into the woods as the afternoon light came sideways through the pines. We parked, popped the trunk, and started pulling out gear. I was running through the checklist in my head when Jason suddenly went still.

"Uh, Steve. I don't think we're the only ones here."

 "Ha ha. Let me guess, the aliens are right behind me?" I said with a chuckle.

"Nah, man. I'm not joking. It looks like we're not the only crackpots out here this weekend."

I finally looked up. About a hundred yards through the trees, there was a camper. Parked crooked between two oaks, surrounded by what looked like antenna arrays, a tripod-mounted dish, and a folding table stacked with equipment. Someone had been here a while.

"That's not too shocking. The Truth Nuke has a lot of fans. Should we, uh, go say hi or something?"

"Not so fast. Random guy out here in the middle of nowhere. Could be trouble? Then again, we're out here. Sooner or later he's gonna notice us."

"IDENTIFY YOURSELVES ASSHOLES OR I WILL BLOW YOU OFF THE FACE OF THIS FUCKING EARTH!"

They had noticed us.

The voice came through a megaphone that was loud enough to send birds out of the treetops.

"Looks like we're gonna have to introduce ourselves," I said.

"We're friendly!" Jason called out.

"WHAT!? Get closer."

Jason looked at me. I looked at Jason. We walked toward the camper.

The man standing in front of it was built like someone who'd done a lot of push-ups and nothing else to stay in shape. He had tactical cargo pants, a faded army cap, and a ten thousand yard stare. 

"Now," he said, lowering the megaphone. "Who're you motherfuckers and why are you out here?"

I spoke before Jason could. "We're just two postgrad students, here to do some camping."

He kept staring. I could see him working through something internally. "You two here about the aliens too, huh?"

I shot Jason a look. He rolled his eyes in the most restrained way possible.

"You listen to the Truth Nuke podcast too?" I asked.

"Oh fuck yeah I do. The Shepherd is my lord and savior... other than Jesus Christ of course."

"Of course," I agreed.

"What's your name?" Jason asked.

"Lonny."

I put out my hand. "It's nice to meet you, Lonny. I'm Steve and this is Jason. We came out here, just like you, to witness history today."

"Today?" Lonny shook his head. "More like tomorrow. You better sit the fuck down cause by The Shepherd’s newest calculations, aliens aren't gonna be here for another 13 hours or so."

"Wow, really?"

"Yup. That's even after factoring in light speed."

I felt something loosen in my chest. Thirteen hours. We had time. "Well, I guess we got time then to hang out."

"You guys wanna start a fire?"

"Sure, we're just gonna go back and grab some of our stuff first."

Lonny pointed two fingers at his eyes, then at us. "Be quick. I'm keeping my eyes on you two. I'm not convinced yet that you both aren't aliens."

Jason laughed. We both did,  assuming he was joking.

He wasn't joking.

We walked back to the car and Jason turned to me with an expression I'd been expecting since we left the apartment.

"Just our fucking luck."

"What do you mean? If he's here then we're at the right spot."

"Aliens come down and see that guy, they're gonna assume there's no intelligent life to be found on Earth."

"He seems a bit odd, I'll give you that."

"He seems like he might cut us open in our sleep to see if we're really human or not."

I didn't have a clean answer to that. "Well, let's hope that doesn't happen."

Jason was already rifling through the trunk. He came up with the tire iron and tucked it under his arm with a practiced casualness that suggested he'd already decided to hold onto it.

"We should have a safe word or something in case something does go wrong."

"Go wrong? What do you mean?"

"This guy snaps and shoots us both thinking we're alien spies or something."

I looked back toward the camper. Lonny had disappeared around the far side of it. "Ok, ok, I can see how you'd think that could happen so... how about... three rapid blinks."

"What, no, what if we're separated."

"I don't know. Shout a codeword or phrase?"

Jason was quiet for a second. "I'll tell you the stars are out when it's time to get the fuck out of dodge."

I looked up at the sky, still pale and wide open above the tree line. Full of stars we couldn't see yet.

"Sounds like a plan."

Lonny's campsite turned out to be better equipped than ours. He had a folding table, a mounted antenna, three separate coolers, and a gun rack. He set up near his fire pit while Jason and I established a polite distance and pitched our tent. We reconvened when Lonny called us over for a fire.

He was still suspicious about the coordinates.

"Why over there?" he asked. “Why not right here next to me.”

"It's where the GPS said the exact coordinates would be."

"Your GPS told you that?" He laughed, "Well your GPS is lying! The real spot would be getting blacked out harder than your moms were the nights you two chuckle fucks were conceived."

I checked my phone. No bars. I held it up in different directions and still got nothing. I looked at Jason and he was checking too. 

“We don’t have reception.” I said after admitting defeat.  

Lonny laughed again, genuinely delighted this time.

"All right, I take it back. This is the right spot. You guys should stay close."

Before either of us could respond, Lonny walked to the edge of the clearing, unzipped, and started relieving himself while staring up at the sky and raising his middle finger at it.

"Prove me right motherfuckers, I dare you!"

Jason put a hand on my shoulder. "A whole weekend alone with this guy," he whispered. "If we end up dead out here, it's your fault."

He gave me two firm pats and walked off to finish setting up his tent.

Lonny came back. He stared out at the trees for a moment, something flickering across his face. "This woods out here, they remind me of the mountains of Afghanistan… Makes me want to get my gun out."

"Oh yeah, did you serve?" I asked, very carefully. 

Lonny didn’t respond. He wandered out of sight and not long after the crack of his rifle rang through the trees. He shot it in irregular intervals while Jason and I quietly assembled our campsite. At some point the shots stopped, and a few minutes later Lonny came crashing back through the brush holding a dead bird by the feet. 

"YES! I still got it!" He held it up like a trophy. "You guys want some nice, tasty government spy drone?"

By dusk, we were sitting around the fire with Lonny's bird rotating on a spit and Truth Nuke playing through a portable speaker.

"Truth Nuke! They may already be among us and waiting on orders to attack! They may have even replaced those around you with doubles..."

Lonny glanced at us over the fire, "You guys wanna beer?"

"Yeah, sure," I said.

"I'm not trying to get twisted right now," Jason replied.

"I insist." Lonny pressed. 

"Why?" Jason asked bewildered. 

"To prove you're not a goddamn alien!” Lonny’s voice fell as fast as it rose, “If you aren't, a little beer won't hurt ya, will it?"

Jason and I exchanged a look and both took a beer.

Lonny smiled and pulled out two full six-packs.

As the night went on, the fire burned low and cans accumulated. Lonny drank faster than either of us, and as the night came on, the particular gravity of his worldview began to fully reveal itself.

"Okay, truth nuke of my own here," he said, leaning forward. "If aliens are real, then the signal we're getting is from a series of modulators on the mothership transmitting frequencies that only those aligned with their chakras can understand."

Jason laughed, which was brave of him. "So tell me then, for my own protection, how do we align with our chakras?"

"The answer to your question is peyote."

"Peyote?"

"Yeah, peyote!"

"Oh okay," I said, nodding drunkenly like a bobble head. "That… that makes sense."

What followed was twenty uninterrupted minutes about the U.S. government experimenting on quadriplegic Russian children, Sasquatches from the Jetelpian star system, the Younger Dryas impact event as a lizard-people land grab, Hollywood blood orgies, surveillance pigeons, and the pyramids. He tied it all together with a kind of conspiratorial confidence I had to admire on a purely structural level, even as individual pieces of it gave me genuine concern about the next twelve hours of my life.

When he finally finished, there was a long silence.

"Wow," is all I could say.

Later, while Lonny rummaged through his truck cab looking for more ammunition.  We could hear him banging around and muttering curses. While Lonny was distracted, Jason grabbed my arm and pulled me to the far edge of the firelight.

"We need to get the fuck out of here asap."

"Ok, he's really drunk."

"He's a bit more than that, Steve."

"Stop yelling, I need to think!” We heard Lonny shout from the back of his truck, “Sorry, if you heard that. That was not about you guys, I'm talking to myself!"

"You're all good!" I called back, with a friendly wave that I hoped read as casual and not terrified.

Jason stared at me. "Steve. The stars are fucking out right now."

I looked at him, then back at the truck. Lonny was still in there, still cursing. "Ok," I said quietly. "He might get angry if we try to leave right now. Let's just wait until he passes out, ok?"

Jason looked like he wanted to argue, but then the truck door slammed and Lonny came back to the fire, hands empty.

"We're out of bullets."

"Darn… you shot them all?"

I noticed something on the ground near the cooler. A single bullet, lying in the dirt. I looked at it and said nothing. 

"Yeah, we're completely defenseless out here now."

"It's a good thing there's no one but us around," Jason said.

Lonny stared out into the dark. "That's what you think."

A silence settled over the campsite, "Do you hear that?"

We sat and listened. There was nothing… not crickets…  not wind… not the distant sound of traffic that I hadn't even realized I'd been hearing until it was gone.

"I don't like it," Lonny said.

"What?"

"The quiet."

I thought fast. "Maybe we put on some music. It'll keep the animals away too."

Lonny looked at me. For the first time all night, he smiled without any menace in it.

I played Beethoven's Fifth through the portable speaker. It was the most inoffensive song I had downloaded. 

Lonny set his empty gun down on the cooler and then stood up. "All right, we don't have guns anymore... so I'm gonna have to teach you betas how to fight like alphas."

He lined us up in front of the fire like a gym teacher. “I am fluent in all 150 martial arts, so watch closely.” His demonstration consisted primarily of miming repeated strikes to the groin.

"You're gonna teach us to fight in just one night?" Jason asked dubiously. 

"It can be done if the master is a boss like me."

"I don't fight," Jason said.

Lonny stopped like he just cursed God. "What…"

"I said I don't fight."

"Those who don't fight, don't survive! You want to survive, Jason?" Lonny was getting a bit intense, but Jason kept his cool.

"Is ET gonna throw hands?" He joked, trying to defuse the situation.

"They might. More than two if they got em."

Jason shrugged, "Right. Well, I don't believe aliens would have traveled all this way just to start a fist fight."

"You'd be surprised by the things those big-headed fucks come all the way down here for."

I tried to bring it back to something constructive. "I think they just want to share their knowledge."

"You also believe in sunshines and rainbows?"

"Uh, technically yeah, those do exist."

Lonny shook his head like I'd failed a test. He sat back down by the fire, and his whole register shifted, "Let me teach you something about why aliens don't reach out, and if they did, we should be worried. It's called the Dark Forest theory."

Jason groaned softly beside me.

"You've heard of it?" Lonny asked.

"Yeah, and it's based on stupid caveman logic."

"Exactly! That's what we all are at our core! Stupid cavemen! Even the aliens too."

I hadn't heard of the dark forest before and I said so. Lonny looked at me again like I'd told him something wrong. Jason looked genuinely shocked.

“I’m surprised Steve. For an alien guy like you, I’d think a theory like this would be right up your alley.” 

“What is it?” I asked again eagerly. 

Jason began to explain,"It's a hypothesis that many alien civilizations exist throughout the universe, but they're all hostile and hiding from each other, because they all fear being destroyed by another equally hostile and hidden alien civilization."

Lonny leaned forward and added, "Think of it this way. You're a hunter in a dark forest and you hear something move in the bushes next to you. It could be something harmless like a squirrel, or it could be a fucking mountain lion waiting to pounce. You don't know. You could fire your gun in the hopes it might scare it away, but you're not the only hunter in these dark woods, I mean,  you can't be. It's too damn big. So there's gotta be other hunters out there, other hunters who are just as hungry and just as afraid as you are. Do you want those hunters knowing where you are? Where your camp is? How big a gun you got?"

"Maybe if we can work together," I said.

"Work together? You sure have a rosy picture of them aliens."

"He thinks they'll come down like Jesus and solve everything." Jason chimed in. 

"They would though! They could advance us eons, settle our petty squabbles, show us a better example of civilization."

"You assume they'd be better than us?"

"Well, they'd have to be to achieve light-speed travel on their own."

Lonny scoffed, "I don't know. We fucking split the atom just to nuke the Japanese into submission. What makes you think aliens aren't so similarly motivated?"

I didn't have an answer for that. Neither did Jason, though he looked like he wished he'd said it first.

"Let me tell you both a story," Lonny began. "It's not one I like to tell, but I think you both need to hear it." He pointed at me. "Especially you. Cause it's an abduction story."

Jason glanced at me sidelong upon hearing that.

"Yeah, that's right. I am an alien abduction survivor."

"Survivor? Do some not come back?" I asked bewildered. 

"Yup."

"How do we know they were abducted then?"

"We don't."

"Oh."

Lonny closed his eyes and took a slow, deep breath. When he opened them again, something had changed in his face and he began, "I was in basic training when it happened. I don't remember what I was exactly doing at the time, but I do remember seeing a bright light above my head. I don't remember anything after that... until I woke up in a dark room."

He hesitated, "There were shadows everywhere. That's why I don't remember what they looked like, the aliens I mean. There was so much darkness. But I could hear them laughing, though. They laughed and laughed as they..."

What followed was deeply, specifically uncomfortable to listen to. I will spare the details, but it was clear Lonny had endured a horrific assault at the hands of the “aliens.” When Lonny finished, there was a silence heavier than a boulder in the air.

Then Jason burst out laughing. I can't fully blame him as what Lonny described may have sounded a bit ridiculous if you can’t read between the lines. But Lonny's face went rigid.

"This is funny to you!"

"Jason," I said, disappointed.

"I'm sorry, I'm sorry! it just caught me off guard is all."

"It caught you off guard?! I'm sorry my abduction caught you off guard! It caught me pretty off guard too when it happened to me!"

"My apologies," Jason managed. "Please continue."

I looked at Jason with everything I had. He got himself under control.

And then I made the mistake of saying, with complete sincerity to Lonny, "I'm so sorry."

Jason lost it again. Something about my earnestness must have done it, I don't know. But it set him off, which set Lonny off, and suddenly Lonny was across the fire and on top of Jason and I was in the middle of all of it, physically separating them. 

"Stop! Stop! Stop!"

Jason had the tire iron raised. "Get the fuck back! Get the fuck back!"

"Only an alien would react like that! Only an alien!"

"Stay the fuck away from me!"

"Lonny stop!"

Lonny stopped. His breathing was ragged. He looked at Jason, then at me, then at some point in the middle distance. Then he turned and walked into the dark without another word.

Jason turned to me. I turned to Jason.

"Fuck."

"Look, Steve," Jason said, when the silence had held long enough to feel certain. "I think you're a dumb ass, but you're still my roommate, so I'm gonna give you one last chance to come to your senses and get the fuck out of here with me."

"Ok." I glanced toward the tent. "Let me grab my stuff first."

"Be quick about it."

He started walking toward the car. I ducked into the tent and started gathering my things by flashlight, moving fast. The night had gone very quiet again. I could hear the podcast still playing faintly from the car, The Shepherd was wrapping up.

"The signal is growing stronger and stronger. We are only moments away from a close encounter of every kind. For those many fans who answered my call to arms, I know you'll understand what has to be done next... Your sacrifices will not be in vain."

I grabbed my bag and came out of the tent.

Jason was gone.

The car was running. The headlights were on. But Jason was not in or near it.

I looked around the campsite, the empty beer cans, the dying fire, Lonny's gun sitting on the cooler. "Jason? I... is this a joke? It isn't funny... Jason?... Lonny?"

I turned around.

There was a trail of blood leading off into the dark.

I stood there for a moment. I don't know how long. My flashlight beam trembled in my hand. I didn't know what else to do, so I followed it.

The blood trail led me deeper into the woods, away from the campsite, away from the car, away from everything. The Shepherd’s laughter echoed distantly behind me. My flashlight cut through the trees. The signal, the one NASA said was a dying star was audible now, humming from somewhere nearby, from the air itself, resonant and layered.

The flashlight found a pair of feet.

I stopped as I stared at the badly bludgeoned corpse of Jason.  I stood there and shook knowing I couldn't do anything else.

"They're here..."

I spun around.

Lonny was fifteen feet away, blood soaked into his clothes and skin, face turned upward. He looked perfectly calm.

"We better run while we still can. The light's coming."

The signal rose in pitch and volume, filling the woods and pressing in on me. I ran and Lonny ran after.

I could hear him crashing through brush, closing ground on me fast. He caught me at the tree line, hands on my throat, and I did the only thing I remembered from his demonstration, I struck him in the groin as hard as I could and he went down fast. I ran for the campsite after that.

I could hear him getting back up slowly.

When I got to the fire, I saw the bullet. The one I'd noticed earlier, sitting in the dirt by the cooler. Still there. I looked at Lonny's rifle on the cooler.

My hands were moving before I'd made a decision.

"Stay back!"

Lonny came out of the dark, slowing when he saw the gun pointed at him.

"You shoot me," he said, "they'll know where you are hunter. They'll all know."

He lunged and I fired. 

I don't remember falling. I remember the gun going off and then the ground and then looking up and not being sure how I got horizontal. Lonny was beside me somewhere…  I could hear him breathing. We'd both hit the ground and I'd hurt him and he had hurt me. Neither of us was going anywhere.

Above us, was a light.

Not a campfire. Not a flashlight. Not the moon. A large light, vast, silent, and growing brighter. Lonny saw it. I saw it. We lay there in the dirt and couldn't look away from it.

The signal reached a pitch that wasn't a sound anymore so much as a pressure, filling everything, filling my chest. The light kept getting brighter. I found myself looking at the car speakers where the Shepherd's voice was emanating from, "See." His voice was changing, even as he spoke. There was something underneath it, something that had always been underneath it. "We didn't need to do anything. Humans are their own worst enemies."

And then he laughed and laughed, and laughed as his voice slid into something else. It was the same laugh Lonny had described in the dark room, the same laugh that had played at the edges of the signal all weekend, strange and harmonic and deeply, unmistakably, not human.

The light was white.

Then it was everything.

The first thing I was aware of was cold.

It was not the damp chill of the woods at 2 a.m. This was an unnatural cold like a freezer. I couldn't move, but I wasn’t restrained either. I was lying flat on something hard and narrow and there were lights above me, bright and sourceless, the kind that cast no shadows of their own but seemed instead to pull shadows toward them.

And at the edges of the room, there were shapes.

I couldn't make them out clearly. My vision kept sliding off them, like trying to read words with dyslexia. They were tall, at least I thought they were tall, and they moved in ways that didn't quite resolve into anything I could name. There were instruments. Gleaming things arranged on surfaces I couldn't see directly, tools that caught the light and threw it back in colors that had no business belonging to metal.

Something was being done to me. I understood that much. I couldn't feel pain, which was either a relief or the most frightening thing that had ever happened to me.

The shapes moved around me with a quiet, coordinated efficiency. Not hurried. Not curious. They had the energy of people completing a task. They were focused, indifferent, and nearly done with me. I tried to turn my head and found I could, barely, just enough to catch the edge of an instrument catching the light. Long and slender. I couldn't tell if it was a scalpel or something with no earthly name at all.

One of the shapes paused near my head. I felt its attention the way you feel a change in air pressure. It looked at me, or did something that functioned like looking, and I had the distinct, cellular certainty that whatever was happening to me, it had been planned long before I drove down that fire road.

The lights got brighter again and a sound, somewhere between a beep and a tone, pulsed steadily. 

I closed my eyes and when I opened them next I was in a hospital bed.

It was like a switch flipped and I was in a hospital now.

A nurse came in shortly after. Then a doctor after that. Then I noticed two police officers who stayed in the doorway with their arms crossed and their faces arranged into expressions of dour professionalism.

The story, as I pieced it together from what they told me is that I had been found on the shoulder of a rural county road at approximately 5:15 a.m., unresponsive, by a truck driver who called 911. I had lacerations consistent with a physical altercation and I had severe bruising around my throat. The blood on my clothes,  of which there was a lot, did not match my blood type.

Jason was missing. His phone was found in the cupholder of my car, which had been located at the trailhead with the driver's side door open and the engine off. There was no sign of him.

There was no sign of Lonny, either. No record of anyone by that name having been at those coordinates. There was no vehicle registration, no campsite permit, nothing. The police had sent someone out there and they found our tent, our gear, what was left of our fire pit, a lot of spent bullet casings, and a significant amount of blood that was still being analyzed. They found no evidence of a third party. No tire tracks beyond mine. No abandoned camper. No antenna equipment. No gun rack. Nothing to suggest anyone named Lonny had ever existed, let alone spent a weekend in those woods with us.

I am currently a person of interest in Jason's disappearance.

I have been told not to leave the state.

I'm writing this from my apartment. It's been eleven days since the hospital discharged me. I still can't sleep through the night. When I do sleep, I hear the signal and I wake up with my heart going and my hands already halfway out in front of me like I'm reaching for something that isn't there.

I know how this looks. I know exactly how this looks. A man goes into the woods chasing a conspiracy podcast. His friend disappears. He turns up covered in someone else's blood with no memory and a story about a man nobody can confirm existed, a light in the sky, and shapes standing over him in a cold operating room.

I know.

But here is what I can't stop thinking about. Lonny told us his story with the dark room and the laughter. What they did to him was inhuman and Jason laughed. I didn't laugh, but I sat there and let it go. I wanted so badly to believe in the good version. The version where they arrive in peace and raise us up and solve the things we cannot solve ourselves. I wanted the universe to be fundamentally generous.

I don't know what's generous about any of this.

I don't know what happened to Jason. I don't know what happened to me in those woods or in that cold room. If the cold room was even real. If the shapes were real. I don't know if Lonny was a man or something wearing the shape of one, or something I invented to survive an experience that had no framework for surviving. I don't know whose blood was on my clothes.

What I know is this: the Truth Nuke podcast is gone. The Shepherd's archive was pulled the same weekend we were in those woods. Every episode, every account, every trace was wiped. If you search for it now, you'll find a Reddit thread full of people who swear they remember it, and a moderator note saying the topic has been locked due to "coordinated inauthentic activity." The signal itself, the one NASA classified as stellar interference, I can still hear it. Not from a speaker. Not from a recording. I hear it the way you hear tinnitus. It’s constant… Just beneath everything.

I'm not asking you to believe me. I'm barely asking myself.

I'm posting this because Jason is missing, and the people looking for him have already decided what happened, and I need someone who was in those woods that weekend, or who heard the signal, or who knows something about who or what Lonny was, to reach out.

I need to know what I was part of.

I need to know what happened to my friend.

If you know anything, please. I can’t take the signal for much longer.
