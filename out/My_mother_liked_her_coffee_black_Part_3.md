The plumber stood up, looked at me, and shrugged.

“Yup. Looks about right to me. Nothing wrong here.”

I knew at this point to expect it. My heart still sank.

I faked a smile and shook his hand.

“Hey… all you can ask for, right?”

“Absolutely.”

I signed a check, handed it to him, and he left.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

It had been two weeks since my mom’s death. One since the fight with my dad.

His buddies had taken him on a golfing trip out of state for 10 days, so we hadn’t really gotten a chance to talk since then.

That was more than fine with me - him being out of town, that is - because I had come up with a plan for that night that I knew would have him thinking I’d gone crazy. If he didn’t think that already.

After work, I was going to sit there, all night, and watch the coffee maker.

No frills. A couple Red Bulls, me, a good book, and the coffee maker from sundown to sunup. Would it - or whatever was running it - start with me there?

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

The answer, as I drove to work the next morning, completely and utterly exhausted - was no.

I sat there for 11 hours, waiting for something, anything that might clue me into what was going on.

The dripping of coffee.

A voice or a cold draft.

Even a full body apparition of my mother walking in and slapping me across the face would be welcome at that point.

But no. Nothing. *Nada.*

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

And it had cost me.

This time, in the form of my job.

I’d gotten stiffed on a tip by a family that was in as bad a mood as I was. Had seen the dad lecturing my manager and chewed him out in a voice that sounded so much like my dad the other week that it scared me.

She’d told me not to come back.

I had grabbed my stuff. Stormed out the door. Got in my car.

As if he knew I’d just been fired, I had a voicemail from Rick.

“We know where you live. Don’t forget that.”

That scene in a movie where the dude gets into his car and unleashes a string of profanities while he beats the shit out of his steering wheel?

That’s how I felt right about then. And I was too tired to even do that.

So I just drove home, silently fuming.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

The first thing I did when I got home was look for something to smash.

It’s a shame I didn’t smash the first thing I saw, because my eyes landed on the coffee maker almost immediately.

Probably would’ve saved me a whole lot of pain if I did.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

Like clockwork, I woke up late that night to the sound of the coffee machine starting. The slow, constant drip.

I was so tired, it didn’t make a difference. I fell back asleep almost instantly.

20 minutes later, I woke up again to the sound of the coffee machine starting. Again.

And the slow drip sang me to sleep like a lullaby. Again.

Only to wake me up again 20 minutes later.

I didn’t even know if it was actually making coffee. I was so tired, from the previous night and from being woken up repeatedly, that I couldn’t even muster the energy to get out of bed and go downstairs and check.  

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I don’t remember falling asleep last night.

I blinked, and suddenly bright rays of sun were peeking through the blinds.

And the smell of fresh coffee was drifting through the room.

The source was a fresh cup of coffee on my nightstand.

I knew my father hadn’t made it.

Not because his car wasn’t in the driveway, although that was the more logical answer.

I knew my father hadn’t made it because there was a trail of brown footprints leading out of the room. Footprints the size of a woman’s foot.

Footprints leading to the coffee machine, which by now was gathering dust due to disuse.

I knew what could be in a cup of coffee. That was more than enough reason for me to not drink it.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

My father arrived home a few days later. I asked for a second to chat. Had some things we needed to discuss.

“Hey - about that whole thing with the insurance papers - I’m sorry. It wasn’t cool of me.”

He looked at me, obviously confused.

“I have no idea what you’re talking about.”

“Are… are you bullshitting me?”

“Why would I be bullshitting you?”

I was speechless.

“…. Never mind.”

He nodded. Slowly. Like he was worried I was going insane. 

But I had one more question for him.

“Oh… and have you used the coffee maker recently?”

Again, the raised eyebrow. The slightly confused, concerned look.

“Why do you ask?”

“I… I swear I’ve heard the coffee machine going every night. A steady dripping noise. That’s why I called the plumber. I thought it might be a leak or something.”

He looked at me. His face was grave and bore none of his usual humor.

“Bud… I haven’t touched that thing since your mom died.”
