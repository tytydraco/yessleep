Ever since I was a kid, my parents have told me that I have extreme paranoia. It started as early as I can remember; I always felt like… I wasn't alone. 

The house I lived in was a nice one. It was an early-modern, single-story house that seemed to be built around the early 90s. The outside was painted dark green and white, with windows facing every direction. My room sat facing the backyard. I liked it that way; it made me feel safe from weirdos peering into my room from the public street. Inside, the house was a typical middle-class home. 3-bed, 3-bath. 

I loved my room; as a kid, my room was filled with all sorts of trinkets and toys from who knows where. My prized possession was an old TV that sat atop a dresser, with a PlayStation plugged into it. The only problem, undoubtedly, was my closet. The house we lived in was old, which sadly meant a lot of my belongings, including the closet, were broken. 

The closet itself was an old white wooden closet with blinds ever so slightly open. The darkness in the closet always seemed to peer at me, a black emptiness that felt like it was coming toward me, trying to absorb and hide whatever lay behind. I always felt watched. Whatever the reason, I tried my best to cover the cracks in the closet, but it always returned to its original state. It felt like the closet was watching me, and whatever I did to hide its eyes… it didn't work. 

A brisk, unknown cold always emanated from the closet. An unfamiliar gust of wind came every time it was opened. My room became increasingly colder than the rest of the house. With the cold came a scent of cigarettes, the same ones my mom used to smoke. She loved the chocolate-flavored ones. 

“Don’t do these when you’re older.” She said, holding the white stick of fire between her ring and middle finger. “It's not good for you.”

I always wondered: if it was so bad, why did she do it? I never asked, regardless.

The fear of being watched followed me throughout my entire childhood. Sleeping became a chore, or… more like a challenge. The more I thought about sleeping, the more my mind raced. My go-to was someone watching me sleep through my window. Or the stereotypical man under my bed. Sometimes I lay facing the wall, trying to sleep, and then heard faint, almost robotic clicks. Then a shiver ran down my spine like liquid metal. Something…or someone opened the closet, footsteps… a pattern like a speeding heartbeat. Then… nothing. Paranoia took over me once more. I never turned around. Not even once. 

The feeling of being watched grew stronger and stronger, like a sniper's laser always pointed at me, but I seemed to be the only target, and the sniper's intent was always a mystery. Whatever the reason, I didn't want to find out. 

One night, my friend Mitch came over, hopefully to take my mind off the “man” in the closet, as I called him. I don't really know why; looking back, I called him “the man,” but in my adolescent brain, it made more sense than a closet staring at me. Naming the feeling made me feel more… sane. 

“Don't stay up too late!” My mom's voice echoed throughout the house. 

“We won't!” Mitch and I yelled back. We walked back to my room after grabbing a couple of snacks from the kitchen. We hugged the snacks like robbers making a break for it, acting as if dropping them would reduce the pot of money. 

Mitch and I were surprisingly similar in personality and physical appearance. We always joked that we were “brothers from another mother”; I wish we were brothers; it would make my no-friends situation at school a lot better. Honestly, without Mitch, I’d be pretty much alone. We both had dark brown, curly hair and glasses. At times, it felt like staring into a mirror. Sometimes at school, Mitch and I would swap name tags just to confuse the teachers. It was kind of stupid, but we thought it was hilarious. 

Mitch and I stayed up late playing video games until close to midnight. It was about time we headed to bed before my mom found out we were still awake. We were in the middle of playing Halo 3 on the old PlayStation when, suddenly, the hair on my arms and the back of my neck stood up like twigs glued to my body. A chill ran through my body with every heartbeat. Sweat beaded as I sat there motionless. I was being watched. 

“Are you feeling okay?” Mitch asked, slightly amused. “You look like you've seen a ghost?”

“Yeah… I- I'm fine.” I said, wiping away the sweat and trying to think of something else. 

I couldn't tell him. He'd think I was insane. Everyone else already does. Looking back now, this decision might be the biggest regret of my life. 

It was best to ignore the feeling running down my spine; maybe it would leave if I did. Maybe whatever was hiding in between the slits of the closet door would leave me alone. 

—

Seconds felt like minutes; minutes felt like hours. My body grew weaker, my muscles tiring from how long I'd been tensing every muscle. It's just in my head, I repeated like a broken record. There isn’t a “man” in the closet. It's not real. It's no-

Silence.

After what felt like an eternity, the hair on my arms and neck fell back down, and my muscles relaxed like a slowly deflating balloon. The “man” disappeared behind the closet door. Whatever it was, the feeling stuck with me like a bad habit. The unease and fatigue weighed on me like a secret I needed to get out. 

An hour or two later, we were both ready for bed. Almost instinctively, I said I would take the top bunk. The top bunk was much less comfortable than the bottom bunk, where I usually slept, and I knew I probably wouldn't be able to sleep anyway. So I let Mitch take it for the night. I lay down, facing the wall. The warm, heavy throw blanket made me feel safe, but I couldn’t shake the overwhelming fear. What happens if-

“What’s your mom making for breakfast tomorrow?” Mitch said, interrupting my inner dialogue

“Uh… she usually makes cinnamon rolls on Saturdays.” I was slightly taken aback. 

“Are you hungry?” 

“Just a little,” Mitch said, the words slightly drowned out by his yawns.

I didn't reply, and before I knew it, Mitch was out cold. 

I was alone again, alone in my thoughts of fear and dread. My mind started running sprints, so much so that I was physically exhausted. My therapist told me a way to relax in times like this. It was simple, but it helped me a lot during my panic attacks. Take a deep breath in and think of 3 things that you can see, smell, and hear. I took a deep breath in and out, sighing. Silence.

 I began scanning the wall in front of me; I couldn't turn around to face whatever was behind me. I saw the Spider-Man poster I got from the book fair that year, an old teddy bear from when I was a child, and another stuffed giraffe with its head slightly torn—poor guy.

I began to smell the room, but all I could smell was the fabric softener on my bedsheets. No help.

I began to listen. A small ticking sound was coming from my Spider-Man clock on my wall, just above the closet. Okay, at least Spider-Man was here to save the day if needed. Before closing my eyes, I listened more closely. Then came a faint *click,* followed shortly by a wooden door opening. Heavy footsteps… heels dragging across the floor, muffled by my carpet, but footsteps nonetheless. As they inched closer, the hair on my arms and neck stood up. Another sense hit me like a train. Cigarette smoke. It smelled like the cigarettes my mom used to smoke when I was a kid. A chocolate scent, ever present, grew stronger with every inching step.

It was my mom, or at least I think it was. Probably coming in to check on whether Mitch and I were asleep, so I put on my best acting job. I couldn't have my mom see me awake; she might have known we stayed up way past our allotted time. I felt a sense of calm. My mom was in the room; Mitch was here. Soon enough, that acting job became pretty real; I fell asleep. 

After a few minutes of silence. 

*BANG*

The sound of someone or something kicking or shifting the bed jolted me awake. A sudden movement. Muffled moaning or screaming. Maybe Mitch is moving around in his sleep? I sat up and scanned the room. Silence. I listened closer, searching for anything that might hint at what was happening. Then, after a few seconds, I heard breathing. Heavy and wet. Predator-like. Rationally, I thought Mitch was having a nightmare. However, the paranoid part of my brain overwhelmed my thoughts of a monster, an unknown person. 

I began to lie back down. Facing the wall, I felt dread wash over me like a tidal wave. I tried to shake it off, but it stuck. Mitch is just a heavy sleeper. I began telling myself over and over. Just try to go back to sleep. A few minutes of silence followed. Only my heartbeat remained. Almost coming out of my chest. I held my breath and waited. 

bdum-bdum-bdum-budm

Then, faint footsteps came back. Heavier than last time. Quicker than last time. Almost a sprint. The quicker they moved, the farther the noise got. The wooden door creaked ever so slightly. Before I could even react. Silence followed once more. Dread grew as the night went on, finally, after what felt like hours. Exhaustion took control. I passed out.

—

I woke to the smell of breakfast, cinnamon rolls and bacon, a smell that could make Porky Pig fly. I sat up in bed and thought about last night. I felt a sense of calm. Just a bad episode, I told myself. I didn't realize the horrors that lay beneath me. 

“Mitch,” I said, voice slightly altered as I stretched, “Man, you had a nightmare last night or s—”

As I climbed down the ladder, I expected to see Mitch. There was nothing. Nothing but a slightly wrinkled piece of paper. 



*Hey, Mom, I've decided to run away. Please don't try to come after me. This is what I wanted. Please respect my decision.*   
		  
*Love,*  
*Braydon*



My heart nearly dropped to my stomach. It was a letter I wrote for my mom. For obvious reasons, the letter made my blood run cold, sending a wave from my head all the way down to my toes. Memories of last night rushed back. As I turned to run to my mom, I noticed something. 

At the bottom right of the note, something was written that sent fear through my entire body. 

That's not how you spell my name. 

—

Police were alerted to the disappearance after Mitch’s mom confirmed that she never picked him up. Over the next couple of days, my paranoia seemed to vanish. I couldn’t tell whether it was because my mind was elsewhere or because the “man” was gone. Either way, I wasn't feeling watched. It felt empty. It felt wrong, like a weird case of Stockholm syndrome. Something was wrong.

A couple of days later, red and blue flashed throughout my entire house, and sirens wailed as loud as you can imagine. My mind raced, and my vision and thoughts grew foggy.

The cops began searching my room. I watched as they searched for clues, anything that could explain what might have happened to Mitch. I stood there in the middle of the room. They finally searched my closet. A cold shiver rushed over me, not from fear, but from the brisk air rushing in. 

Confused, they searched inside the closet, and what they found made my blood run cold. Behind an old poster propped against the back wall of my closet was a hole. It led to a small crawlspace. Inside, the room fell silent. Inside the hole was a sleeping bag, a dark red bag, its colors muffled by dust and dirt. Next to it was an old pillow from my childhood. An old Cat in the Hat pillow. 

I was frozen solid, fear flowing through my body like a river of dread. My skin began to prickle, like ants crawling all over me. In a room with at least 5 different people, it was dead silent. The only sound was the slow *tick, tick, tick* of the Spider-Man clock. Next to the makeshift bed in my closet. Was a Camera—an old Canon PowerShot A530.

—

Nothing ever came up after the investigation. Mitch and the intruder were never caught and most likely never would be. I’d like to imagine that Mitch was still alive, out there somewhere. But deep down I knew that was just hopeful delusion. My mom and I didn't have the funds to pack up and leave. We thought it would be cheaper to plug and cover the whole crawl space. Only physically covering up the mental and physical trauma of what happened that night.

Over the years, I never fully recovered from Mitch, and I don't think I ever will. On sleepless nights, I find myself searching his name on missing persons lists, news articles, anything that can give me closure. Every night ends the same way. Nothing. Nothing but returning dread and guilt. 

This incident happened over a decade ago. I might never find out what happened to my friend, and honestly, I don't want to. I don't want to and don't think I ever will. 

Everyone I know has seemingly moved on from this, everyone… but me now.

 I find myself wondering what would have happened if Mitch took the top bunk that night. What if Mitch never came over that night? But what-ifs don’t matter anymore.

I'm sorry, Mitch. I'm sorry that you decided to become friends with me that day in elementary school. I'm sorry I couldn't save you from the monsters in my closet. 

The horrors that you went through, a mere couple of feet away from help, haunt me. Simply put, my actions are why Mitch isn't here anymore, and  I don't think I'll be able to forgive myself for that. 

I've graduated from high school now. Sitting in my apartment just outside the college campus, no bigger than the average living room. It was the first place I've ever lived other than my childhood home. Writing this, I'm hoping someone, anyone, knows information about the case or just anything. I hope it can find me. Part of me doesn't want answers; part of me needs closure so I can try to move on, but I can't. Not anytime soon, at least. 

Thinking back on the case, on the incident. The therapy sessions, talks with my mom, everything. It was all for… nothing. 

I was never paranoid, making up things in my head—the “man” in my closet. Staring at me every day, the feeling of being watched for as long as I could remember wasn't an illusion. A trick in my mind. Whoever came to check on Mitch and me all those years ago. Standing a mere 2-3 feet away from me was the man who had been stalking me for almost 12 years. 

My paranoia was real.

The man in my closet was real.

Sometimes, on restless nights, I feel that same feeling from all those years ago. The same feeling of hearing something that isn't there. The same feeling of someone staring at me. This time, I always got up to check everywhere I could. Nothing. 

I’ve grown a bad habit throughout the years. Always face the wall when you sleep. The thought of whatever lies behind has scared me ever since. Sometimes though. When my mind starts to run in circles, I still hear the faint clicks, sounds of breathing, wet and heavy.

Right outside my apartment window.
