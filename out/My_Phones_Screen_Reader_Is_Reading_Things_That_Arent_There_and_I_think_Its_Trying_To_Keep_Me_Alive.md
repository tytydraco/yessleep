I’m completely blind, so my phone’s screen reader—VoiceOver—is basically my primary connection to the outside world. If you’ve never used one, it’s a robotic, monotone synthetic voice that reads out whatever your finger touches on the glass, plus incoming notifications, battery percentages, and UI buttons.  
Because I rely on it for everything, I know every quirk of the software. I know the exact cadence of "Samantha" (the default voice I use), I know what it sounds like when an app crashes, and I know how it reads corrupted text or weird formatting.  
About three weeks ago, Samantha started reading things that weren’t there.  
The first time it happened, I was standing at a crosswalk on my way home from work, waiting for the audible walk signal. My phone was locked in my coat pocket, connected to my bone-conduction headphones. Suddenly, without any chime or notification buzz, Samantha’s flat, synthetic voice cut into my podcast.  
*"Step back. Two paces. Now."*  
I froze. It didn't sound like a text message—there was no "Message from..." prefix, no app name, just raw text being spoken directly through the accessibility engine. Confused, I took two steps backward off the curb just as a massive delivery truck blew through the red light, its side mirror clipping the exact space where my head had been a second earlier. The wind from it knocked my hood back.  
I pulled my phone out, swiped through my notification center, checked my recent texts, and even looked at the VoiceOver caption panel history. Nothing. No messages, no alerts, no background apps open. I chalked it up to some bizarre, one-in-a-million Bluetooth interference or a stray broadcast signal, though my hands were shaking the rest of the walk home.  
Four days later, it happened again.  
I was in my kitchen, boiling water for pasta. I had my phone sitting face-down on the counter while I reached into the upper cabinet for a colander. Out of nowhere, the phone’s external speaker blared at full volume—even though I had it set to silent.  
*"Drop. Cover your face."*  
I didn't even think. After the delivery truck, my body reacted before my brain could catch up. I dropped to my knees behind the kitchen island and threw my arms over my head.  
Less than two seconds later, the pressurized glass lid on my cheap stockpot shattered like a grenade. Boiling water and shards of tempered glass sprayed across the upper cabinets and the exact spot at chest-height where I’d been standing.  
I sat on the linoleum floor, breathing hard, listening to the hiss of water hitting the hot burner. When I finally reached up and grabbed my phone off the counter, the screen was completely asleep. When I woke it up and swiped across the lock screen, VoiceOver cheerfully announced: *"No notifications. 6:42 PM. Battery 84 percent."*  
That’s when I realized it wasn't a glitch. Something was hijacking the accessibility API to talk to me.  
I tried testing it. I opened a blank note, typed *"Who are you?"* using dictation, and waited. Nothing happened. I checked my phone’s diagnostics, ran a malware scan with a friend’s help, and even did a full factory reset over the weekend.  
I thought the reset wiped whatever it was, because I went ten full days without a single weird vocalization. I actually started to feel a little disappointed, like I’d accidentally deleted a guardian angel.  
Until tonight.  
It’s currently 2:15 AM. I live alone in a second-floor apartment. My bedroom door is closed, and my phone is sitting on my nightstand, plugged into the charger.  
About ten minutes ago, I woke up to the sound of my front door deadbolt clicking open.  
I didn't hear a key in the lock. Just the heavy, metallic *clack* of the bolt sliding back, followed by the soft creak of the front door swinging inward.  
I went completely rigid under my blankets, my heart hammering so loud I could feel it in my teeth. I reached out slowly, my fingers brushing the edge of my nightstand, trying to grab my phone so I could use Siri to call 911 without making noise.  
Before my fingers even touched the glass, the phone’s speaker clicked on. Samantha’s voice filled the quiet room—not at full volume this time, but lowered to the absolute minimum setting, barely a synthetic whisper.  
*"Do not touch the screen. Do not call 911."*  
My hand hovered an inch above the phone, trembling. Out in the living room, I heard the distinct, heavy scuff of a boot against my hardwood floor. Someone was inside. Slowly, deliberately, footsteps began moving down the short hallway toward my bedroom.  
I pulled my hand back, holding my breath, praying the glowing screen wouldn't give me away—until I remembered my screen brightness is always set to zero to save battery. To anyone looking in, the room was pitch black.  
The footsteps stopped right outside my bedroom door.  
My phone whispered again, the cadence strangely rapid, like the speech rate had been cranked up to 80%.  
*"He has a knife. He thinks you are asleep. Breathe through your mouth, slow and shallow. Do not move."*  
I forced my jaw open just enough to let air slip silently in and out, fighting the urge to hyperventilate. Through the thin hollow-core door, I heard the brass doorknob slowly turn. The latch clicked free. The door eased open, and I felt the subtle shift in air pressure as someone stepped into the room.  
For what felt like an hour, nobody moved. I could smell damp wool and stale cigarette smoke standing just a few feet from the foot of my bed. I kept my eyes closed, my body completely limp, listening to the faint, steady rhythm of my own fake sleep breathing.  
Then, the floorboard near the doorway creaked. The air shifted again. The door pulled shut—softly, carefully—and the footsteps retreated down the hall. A moment later, the front door clicked closed.  
I waited a full two minutes, shaking so hard my teeth were chattering, before I finally reached out and grabbed my phone to call the police.  
As soon as my thumb touched the glass to wake the screen, VoiceOver didn't read the time. It didn't read the battery percentage. It didn't read my emergency contacts.  
Instead, Samantha’s flat, emotionless voice spoke one last sentence before the screen locked itself completely:  
*"He didn't leave. He's in the closet. Keep pretending to sleep."*
