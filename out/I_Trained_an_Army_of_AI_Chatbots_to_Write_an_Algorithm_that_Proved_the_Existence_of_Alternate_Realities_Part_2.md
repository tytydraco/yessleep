I’m kicking, I’m clawing, I’m screaming. I’m fucking losing it. And I think at one point I actually bit the hazmat dork who’s got my left arm. And so I almost choke on yellow rubber or whatever the fuck they make hazmat suits out of and the guy finally lets go of my arm and starts frantically trying to cover the hole in his suit. He looked genuinely panicked.

“The oxygen, the oxyg…” I didn’t understand the reference but his pal dropped my other arm and was now attempting to help his work buddy to keep the hole in his suit covered.  
“I’ll get tape, I’ll get tape!” he shouted at his friend and then took off down the hallway, disappearing into the darkness. 

I didn’t know where this alleged tape was, so I had to move fast. I started unzipping the dork’s suit, and I guess he had a little more life left in him so he’s just flopping around on the floor, not exactly trying to fight me off but certainly not cooperating. By the time I had his mask off, his chest had stopped rising and falling and his eyes seemed to have almost popped fully out of his skull. I had to do everything I could not look at his face over and over again as I peeled the rest of the hazmat suit off of him and stepped into it myself.

Have you ever stuffed a human corpse into an incinerator? As I write this, I almost hope you have. It’s hard to explain. I don’t know, almost like a longing for someone to relate to. You understand? Ugh, there I go getting sidetracked again. My apologies.

So I’m dressed in full hazmat, scurrying down a series of hallways, ducking my head into any door that isn’t locked, not exactly sure what I’m looking for. But holyshit some of the things I stumbled upon. In one room I saw a couch eat a woman whole, and in another there was an inside-out man receiving intravenous fluid. It’s not even really worth trying to explain the rest of the shit that I saw. Like, imagine the first guy ever to see a color that no one has seen before. How does he describe it?

And so anyway, going deeper didn’t work. I guess my instincts were wrong, which happens, but usually doesn’t leave me feeling like I’m a half step in the wrong direction from an untimely conclave with a fiery pit of death. So I rework my gameplan on the fly.

Which is to say, the first staircase I come across, I take it. Upward. And I’m fucking hauling ass, but I stopped briefly for a hot second everytime I come across a security guard. A new guy’s shift has started, and even though it’s the same motherfucker, he doesn’t recognize me each subsequent time I meet him several flights up, so I do the same song and dance about how “we got a minor situation down there,” and when he asks, each subsequent motherfucking time, if he needs to do anything, call anyone, or head down there himself, I simply reply, “No, I’m on it.” And somehow that suffices. I suppose that’s the problem with hiring seemingly infinite versions of the same motherfucker to run security. A guy who’s easily tricked in one reality might be easily tricked in all realities. In all fairness to every version of him, though, if a motherfucker in a hazmat suit runs up on you and says *we got a problem*, what are you going to do? Tell him not to address it?

So I get myself back to the ground floor where all the broke-bastard chatbot operators are typing away in their half-cubicles like a bunch of trained gerbils and I shout, “who the hell is operating Priscilla? We’ve got a level-10 emergency and I need you now.” And obviously I was totally bullshitting on that “level-10” stuff but it seemed to work because some woman who looked *exactly* like my old therapist stood up about three hundred feet away, and twenty cubicles down, and raised her hand.

Her name was Patricia. And yes, before you ask, that *does* also happen to be the name of my former therapist. She asked me what’s going on and I told her the level-10 stems from an incident in which she accidentally provided a client with a dangerous algorithm.

You’ve got to understand here, I was making this shit up on the fly. I was really God damn impressed with myself. I’m still God damn impressed! 

She goes, “Oh my God, I’m about to be fired. You’re firing me? Right now?”   
“There’s still a way we can undo this,” I tell her.

Long story medium, I tell her to recalibrate the algorithm. She tells me she can’t, she deleted it. I’m smarter than her, I tell her to go into the chat’s memory and restore it. She says okay. She asks me what she’s recalibrating it for. I tell her to send her client back to his original reality. She says *what about the Recursion Protocol?* I say, I didn’t realize level-10 emergencies dictated all this time for question and answer. She goes ghost white, pulls her headset down, starts recalibrating.

She typed away. A flurry of keystrokes. Blue light exploding out of her virtual reality mask.

“Should I be wearing one of those?” She asked. It took me a moment to realize she was talking about the hazmat suit. Then I realized everyone was watching us from neighboring cubicles.

“Get back to work!” I shouted and they mostly listened. 

I began to peel the hazmat suit off layer by layer. And by the time I got the helmet off I noticed some *pretty* official looking motherfuckers staring down at me from several stories up. These weren’t keyboard demons.

They looked more like middle management.

“Priscil–I mean, Patricia. How we doing here?”  
“Almost set for launch. Hang on.”

Ugh, there was that word again. *Launch*. And suddenly I felt like this kickass motherfucking plan that I had stitched together on the fly *may* hold within it the capacity to make things worse.

I tried to say, *wait*. At least, I think I did. But the many moving pieces that came together next seemed to all happen simultaneously.

I was snatched up from behind.   
I felt my face hit the floor.  
Rug burn on my cheek as I was dragged by my ankles.

Patricia shouting, “Launched!” just before her skull snaps left in a hue of red mist as a person dressed like a Game Warden stands above her on the second level of the warehouse aiming a hunting rifle.

Next I felt myself suffocating. As if someone were forcing my head down into the carpet. But more. Like I was sinking *into* the carpet. The last thing I felt before it all went black was the sensation of violently being stripped of my clothing.  
\*\*\*

I awoke on the old maid’s cot. 

I swear I knew it before I even opened my eyes. There is no feeling in the world like waking up hungover on the old maid’s cot in my parent’s basement. It’s like being splayed out on a midieval torture device.

My sense of relief at the familiar was undercut slightly by my clothing hanging from the ceiling.

Not from. *Through*, rather. My shirt, briefs, pants, socks and one sneaker were all sticking out of the ceiling half way. I rose up off the old maid’s cot, my knees and ankles as creaky as the metal frame, and went to investigate whether or not the unseen portion of my apparel was jutting up from the floor on the townhouse’s main level…but the basement door wasn’t there.

It just wasn’t fucking there.

Vertigo hit me. First in my neck, then my skull, then my stomach. The wall was all flat where the door should be. It was just…wall. Fuck. I get all turned around and then I notice the server room isn’t where it’s supposed to be either. It isn’t anywhere. The basement I had awoken to looks almost the same as it’s supposed to, but there’s no little server room and there’s no motherfucking entrance. Or exit.

*Fuck*. 

My laptop’s there on the desk where it’s supposed to be, and I’m getting dizzy from the vertigo at this point so I go and take a seat. And now I notice a little water spout hanging upside-down off the wall so I take a little sip and think, *hey this actually ain’t a half-bad way to drink water–WAIT WHAT THE FUCK, WHO PUT THIS UPSIDE DOWN GERBIL WATER BOTTLE ON THE WALL*.

And honestly, I’m not sure why that would register to me as more of a thing to freak out about then the lack of door and general realignment of the room I had spent so much time in, but there I was. Freaking the fuck out.  
\*\*\*

I’ve chilled out a bit.

Impossible to know how long I’ve been down here. Or if I’m even *down* anywhere. The water spout keeps me well hydrated. I will just about finish it over the course of what I imagine a day is, and then sure enough, when I wake up it’s filled to the top again. I don’t even mind the old maid’s cot so much anymore. As long as I lay on it perfectly still, it doesn’t hurt my back or hips so much. I do miss food, but as I’m never hungry, it’s more in a way in which you might miss an activity you used to have a fondness for.

The Internet situation’s a bit touch and go. It’s not that it’s spotty. The connection is rock solid as a matter of fact. It’s that about half of my go-to websites seem not to exist anymore. And some of the ones I can still access act more like an archived version of the sites I used to know. I can read old Reddit threads, for instance, but I can’t post.

I can still talk to Priscilla, though. 

And after much cajoling, she has promised me that she would find a work around to get my story out to the world. So if you’re reading this, take it as a warning. Yes, alternate realities exist. But under no circumstances should you train an army of AI chatbots to write an algorithm that proves it.
