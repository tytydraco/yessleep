Somewhere where maps blur and gravity seems to lose its meaning, exists Cycle Meat Burgers. It is not a place you search for; it is a place that finds you. It appears as a mirage in the middle of desolate roads, just when hunger becomes unbearable and there is nothing else for miles around. Its facade is deceptive: warm lights, a flickering sign, and an aroma that promises comfort. It is too perfect, too convenient. And that is the first mistake: believing you can enter and leave at will.

Upon crossing the threshold, the air changes. It is static, heavy. Behind the counter stands Vanesa. Her presence is an affront to human biology; she seems not to have slept in centuries. Her skin has the consistency of melted wax, slowly dripping over her stained uniform. Deep dark circles frame bloodshot eyes that do not look at you, but rather pierce through you. However, hunger is a potent narcotic; you ignore the aberrance of her appearance and simply order a burger.

You sit at a table covered by a thin layer of ancient dust. A piercing pain begins to drill into your temples, and your vision blurs. Is it the exhaustion from driving? Or is it the place? Before you can question it, he appears. The waiter. Unlike Vanesa, his face is frozen in a wide, almost painful smile. He approaches floating rather than walking and places the plate in front of you. Upon observing him closely, you notice the horror in the details: his eyes are wide open, dry, shedding tears he cannot wipe away because he seems incapable of blinking.

"Enjoy your meal," he whispers. The smile never moves; his lips do not articulate, as if the voice comes from somewhere else.

The burger is unsettlingly perfect. The meat is juicy, with a deep, metallic taste unlike anything you have ever tried. It is delicious, but it leaves a strange, primitive aftertaste. Upon finishing, the survival instinct finally kicks in. You leave a trembling tip and head for the exit. But the waiter's hand, cold and rigid, blocks the door.

"Allow me to show you the restaurant," he insists. It is not a question. "No, I'm in a hurry," you reply, turning the doorknob. It is stuck. "The tour is mandatory," he says, and his smile widens to the impossible.

You have no choice. He guides you toward the back hallways. With every step, reality distorts. The paintings on the walls seem to follow you with pleading gazes, their faces twisting as if made of smoke. The air becomes dense, difficult to breathe, and the dizziness is constant. You arrive at a heavy metal door: the butcher shop. The waiter steps aside with a macabre bow.

The cold inside is absolute. Pieces of meat hang from rusted hooks on the ceiling, dripping a dark liquid into the gloom. They do not look like cuts of beef; they have shapes that are too familiar. The waiter stops in the center of the icy mist. Tears now stream freely down his immobile face. With one hand, he pulls out a large, shiny butcher knife.

Without stopping his smile, without emitting a single whimper, he begins to cut himself. The blade enters his flesh again and again, separating muscle and bone with surgical precision. You want to scream, you want to run, but your legs do not respond. The scene consumes you until the darkness claims you.

You wake up on the cold floor. The waiter's body lies open before you. You no longer feel fear, nor disgust, nor a headache. You only feel a renewed hunger and a terrifying clarity. You pick up the knife he dropped. Your hands move with a dexterity you did not possess before, splitting the body into perfect halves to hang them on the empty hooks. It is new meat. Fresh meat.

You wipe your hands on the apron you are now wearing. You walk toward the kitchen, where the smell of grease and iron feels comforting. You take your place behind the preparation line. The front door opens in the distance; the bell tinkles. Another hungry traveler has arrived.

Congratulations. Now you are the chef. Now you are the waiter. And the cycle of Cycle Meat Burgers must continue.
