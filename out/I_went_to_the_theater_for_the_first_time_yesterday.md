It wasn’t until I stumbled into a bank job and was miraculously selected for a two-month temporary rotation in the city that things changed. My colleagues begged me: Make the most of these two months! That theater district stays lit day and night, don't you want to squander a little time there?



To be honest, as a corporate novice about to possess my very first taste of savings, I starved for a life I had never known. Did I love theater? I had never seen it. I had no right to an opinion. I was simply drawn to the sheer novelty of it, the thrill of using money I had earned with my own hands to buy a ticket into the unknown.



The day of the show—yesterday, to be exact—the weather was so radiant it felt like a personal favor. I changed into a dress I bought specifically for the occasion and carefully done my makeup. My friends joked that people who frequented the theater must be obscenely happy.



That evening, when I finally stood on the street illuminated by towering production posters and neon lights, my heart hammered against my ribs like a trapped bird. The sheer, magnificent illusion of it all was something no online video could ever truly capture.



Watching the crowds stream past me, a sudden realization hit: I had arrived so terribly late to this world, yet I was overwhelmingly grateful. Since I was here now, it could never be too late.



I had purchased a seat in the Upper Circle. It was high, but perfectly centered. My eyesight had always been flawless, peering down from the high above, the Stalls below were a shifting sea of humanity.



There were couples locked in elegant embraces and solitary gentlemen in fine tailoring. A sense of quiet, absolute privilege radiated from them. Their effortless belonging reminded me of my graduation night—the evening my mother and I had tried so hard to dress up, visiting a flower shop to pick out a bouquet. It was the only memory from my impoverished past that had ever brushed against the edge of "dignity."



In a theater, the quality of your seatmate is a game of pure chance, much like picking a bag of popcorn at the cinema. But the man who took the seat next to mine looked as though he had stepped straight from the pages of a high-fashion editorial.



He was alone. His hair was a stark blonde, his skin possessed a nearly translucent pallor, and his features were handsome in a cold, detached sort of way. He wore a perfectly tailored dark suit. I couldn't help but quietly speculate: Did he take the tube here? Or was he wealthy enough to simply hail a cab?



As he settled in, a gust of crisp, cool cologne washed over me. Suddenly self-conscious, I nervously nudged my umbrella and handbag further into the shared space behind the seats. When I realized my sudden retreat might seem rude, I stole a glance at him through my eyelashes. Mercifully, his face bore no trace of disdain or annoyance.



Around us, the audience was still murmuring, excitedly debating whether tonight's cast featured something something. I couldn't decipher the terms. To my other side, an elderly woman let out a sharp, smirk when she overheard a group of young ladies discussing a cast change or something behind us.



The girls seemed to notice, and their voices quickly withered away. Yet, the man to my left remained entirely indifferent to the subtle currents of social friction around him. Was he a foreigner? A tourist? Or was he, like me, just an outsider who had wandered into this place?



I tried to peek at his phone screen. Seeing nothing of note, I gave up. He was merely scrolling through social media, checking the weather forecast, and skimming a few work emails. I swear I had no intention of invading his privacy, I only wanted to confirm if he, too, was hiding a flutter of uncontrollable excitement beneath his skin.



Then, in the absolute fraction of a second before the house lights died and the theater plunged into total silence, I heard it.



It was a sharp, distinct sound of shifting bone—a sickening click-clack. It sounded exactly like someone violently cracking their knuckles or forcefully wrenching their neck. Was he just clenching his fists in the dark? Borrowing the final, dying glow from the stage, I glanced over. His hands were resting flat against his knees, completely motionless.



The pre-show announcement boomed through the speakers. I checked my phone one last time, ensuring the silent mode was on, and slipped it away.



It was a world-famous production. I won’t name it here—protecting one’s privacy these days is as difficult as trying not to breathe—but I had prepared for it. I had Googled the synopsis, watched the trailers, and memorized the official clips on YouTube. Yet, nothing could have prepared me for the actual impact of live theater.



When the orchestra swelled, my chest vibrated. The music, the rhythm, the piercing clarity of the actors' vocals—it resonated within my very bones.



By the second number, a powerful solo, the stage was bathed in shifting, dramatic hues.



Then, out of the corner of my eye, I saw him press his palms hard against his face. More wet, grinding cracks muffled under the music.



Then, his torso began to shudder. His upper body was vibrating with terrifying velocity, a frenzied tremor taking place in less than a second.



The seat to his left was still empty. No one else seemed to notice.



As the next few songs played, The snapping sound grew frequent, rhythmic, as if every joint in his body was coming undone. Yet, theater etiquette dictated I remain frozen in my designated seat.



Then, the shaking escalated into a violent, thrashing frenzy, as though some invisible, monstrous hand in the dark had grabbed him by the spine and was shaking him like a ragdoll. His internal clicking began to sync perfectly with the thumping bass of the orchestra.



Unable to bear it, I snapped my head toward him.



My breath caught in my throat. In the cross-cutting shafts of the stage lights, his face was gone. In its place was a horrific, hollow, wrinkled expanse of translucent skin stretched over bulbous, glassy spheres. His neck twisted on its axis with the sickening, mechanical rotation of a rusty screw.



I blinked hard, and when I opened them, his pale face was perfectly intact.



Suddenly, a small beam of light pierced the darkness. A theater staff was guiding an well dressed woman down our row. She wore a sleek black dress, and her makeup was artistic that made my own efforts look childish.



She took the empty seat beside the twitching man. Sensing my gaze, she turned and offered me a warm, gentle smile—a reassuring look that seemed to say, It’s alright, dear. I wondered if she could tell it was my first time.



Then came the final crescendo before the intermission. The stage dimmed to near-total blackness, save for a single, blinding spotlight focused entirely on the leading lady’s tragic solo.



Under the cover of that deep shadow, the wet, snapping sounds next to me reached a maddening peak. The man was violently vibrating, his limbs a blur.



I looked over again.



This time, the elegant woman had placed her hand firmly upon the man’s chest. But there was no fabric there. His chest had split open to reveal a pulsing, exposed mass—a heart that glowed with a sickening, bioluminescent green light. With an expression of utter, chilling indifference, the woman squeezed the glowing mass.



Pop.



It deflated like a punctured balloon.



Within seconds, the man’s entire form collapsed inward, his bones dissolving, his flesh shrinking until he was nothing more than a limp, empty sheet of skin. The woman calmly rolled the skin up, tucked it neatly between the pages of her theater program, and slid it into her designer handbag.



A second later, the curtain fell. The house lights flooded the theater for intermission.



My hands were shaking violently. I turned to the group of young women sitting directly behind us. “Did you… did you just see that? The man who was sitting right here?”



They looked at me, their expressions blank and bewildered. “What man? That seat has been empty the entire night.”



“Never?” I stammered, my voice rising in panic. They began to exchange uneasy glances, looking at me as if I were losing my mind.



Before I could spiral into a full panic attack, the elegant woman in the black dress appeared at my side, gently taking my arm and leading me out into the grand, brightly lit lobby.



“It’s your first time at the theater, isn’t it?” she asked, her voice smooth and comforting.



My face flushed crimson. “Yes… it is.”



“Oh, don’t worry at all,” she said with a soft, melodic laugh. “Everyone has a unique experience their first time. Honestly, sometimes I wish I could lose my memory every night, just to experience that magical, overwhelming freshness all over again.”



She leaned in slightly, confidential and warm. “You know, large theaters often employ hidden cast members. They mingle with the audience in the upper tiers, staging surreal, immersive performances for a select few. It’s called immersive theater—creating the illusion that the audience is witnessing something extraordinary and secret.”



Of course! I wasn’t losing my mind, and I wasn’t cursed. I had been chosen. I was part of the magic.



I couldn’t believe my luck. To think I had been sitting in the Upper Circle, complaining about my seat, only to be granted a bespoke, VIP immersive experience!



My earlier frustrations felt incredibly ignorant and ungrateful.



The rest of the night passed in a beautiful, theatrical blur, but the details of the second half don't even matter.



I’m already planning to book tickets for the exact same show next week. I just wanted to share this here to brag a little—I hope all of you get just as lucky the next time you step into the theater!
