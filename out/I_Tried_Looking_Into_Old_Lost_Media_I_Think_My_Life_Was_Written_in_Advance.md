Okay, I don’t really know where else to post this. Fandom message boards seem wildly inappropriate, self-help forums would probably see me getting locked up, and there’s no way I can tell my wife about this. So, here I am, hoping this one corner of the internet won’t just label me some paranoid lunatic. I’m trying to be as rational as I can about what I’ve found, so just try to keep an open mind, that’s all I ask. 

If you’re familiar with internet culture from the mid- to late 2000s through the early 2010s, you’re likely familiar with the concept of a creepypasta. These were frequently shared, internet born horror stories that perpetuated on the web and early YouTube like modern boogeyman stories, often told in the hopes of having a good scare. Long time heavyweights like the Slenderman, Jeff the Killer, and the Rake all originate from this specific time on the internet, and while some have mostly been forgotten, others were revolutionary for the horror genre. For me, many of these stories were my earliest introductions into the world of horror, and I’d be willing to say that was the case for a lot of kids like me. I can’t even begin to tell you how many late nights I’d stay up on my phone or on the YouTube app of my gaming console just to hear one more supposedly true hitchhiking story, or a famous horror story courtesy of the World Wide Web.

Sorry, this is all probably getting a little dry for you, I always did have a problem with hyper fixations. I promise there is a reason I’m telling you all of this. 

To try getting to the point, one of the more common, and arguably popular genres of the early internet creepypasta was the “Lost Episode” fad. These were often fictional, hyper gory, and admittedly poorly written fictions that detailed missing episodes of Spongebob or the Simpsons that got shockingly dark and disturbing for what those shows were at the time. These usually relied on suddenly detailed characters, a lot of blood and guts, or pure shock value to get a rise out of young kids. I think these were my absolute least favorite creepypastas, even when they were popular. I certainly wasn’t ahead of the curve or anything, I just thought they were kind of dumb, no disrespect to the actual authors or anything. 

Over time, I grew up and mostly forgot about these classic online legends. I graduated high school, met the love of my life, and married her. We have two kids now, who are probably, no, scratch that, ARE the best things that have ever happened to me. Neither one is any older than three right now, but already they’re developing their own unique tastes, personalities, and interests. 

I swear something about being a parent reawakened my old fascination for the stuff I enjoyed as a boy. Seeing my girls discover their love for colorful toys or popular bands like the Wiggles just made me think back to my old late night sessions with spooky stories. Maybe I just wanted some connection to my  childhood, or their happy screams at seeing their own cartoons made me wanna appreciate the old stories I used to love. Whatever the case, I couldn’t get the idea out of my head. 

Before long, I was looking up all the old tales on my phone and listening to them while I worked or changed diapers. Most of them were way worse than I remembered, and I admit I laughed at how silly or tryhard some of those seemingly ancient pastas were. There were certainly diamonds in the rough, and those were the ones I gravitated towards for a little while.  About a year ago though, I stumbled on that old collection of lost episode stories, and while they were just as bad, if not worse than I remembered, a part of me did start to wonder.

You see, in my mind, the idea of a lost episode in it of itself isn’t exactly the most outlandish concept. I mean, I can recall a number of movies and tv shows that were filmed, produced, scored, and even given release dates, only to be pulled and scrubbed from any existing database. Heck, if you squint at it hard enough, *Nosferatu,* one of the most famous horror icons in the space, kinda counts as lost media. Sure, it survived to the modern day, but historically, every copy of the movie was ordered to be destroyed due to a court ruling that it infringed copyright. If it weren’t for a few hidden copies or overseas preservations, we as a population would probably have forgotten it even existed decades ago. 

 So, I wondered, what if there are similar stories for old cartoons? What if there actually were some creepy lost episodes for those old shows I grew up watching? Surely there were far stranger things to look into, right? 

To the surprise of absolutely no one, the initial research I delved into was pretty definitively not scary. Stuff like the missing ninety-something classic Doctor Who episodes, or the two scrapped endings to The Thing. Both of these had simple, completely mundane reasonings for their erasure; studio heads just wiping their archives in the case of Doctor Who, and John Carpenter hating the confirmed ending of MacReady surviving/being confirmed as a Thing. No blood, no gore, no secret curses. Well, technically there WAS gore for The Thing, but you know what I mean.  

My first real hit didn’t actually set off any alarm bells. Thanks to it being tied to classic internet creepypasta, I decided to look a little bit into SpongeBob. Now I’m fully aware that *Squidward’s Suicide* is a classic in the online space, but it’s pure fiction. I wasn’t trying to prove its validity, I was just curious to see if there was any *actual* forgotten media for the show, scrolling through dozens of interviews, behind the scenes videos, and online forums. 

What I eventually came up with was an article detailing the remnants of an old episode that was storyboarded, but ultimately scrapped called “Cruising Blues”. According to the article, this episode would have featured Squidward receiving an invitation to go on a cruise, only to find SpongeBob and Patrick also invited. The episode would have focused on Squidward trying and failing to relax as the classic pair found themselves in more and more ridiculous scenarios, usually at Squidward’s expense. The storyboards I found had the guy with a cake splattered over him, getting domed by a shuffle board puck, and screaming as his tentacles got pulled into the propeller blades of the boat (I know that sounds brutal, but it was cartoony and had no blood) before the final illustration had Patrick and SpongeBob happily walking off the boat. Meanwhile, Squidward stood bitterly off to the side in a multitude of casts and holding crutches. 

On the surface, this episode sounded completely harmless, so I wasn’t really sure what would have caused such a cancellation. However, the article didn’t stop at just the synopsis, going to an interview with an anonymous animator who claimed to have worked on the episode. 

Here’s an abbreviated transcript with the interviewer (Neither had provided names in the article, probably for anonymity) - 

*Interviewer - So what caused you to stop production on the episode?* 

*Animator - Well, there were a few reasons. By this point in the series, we’d kinda done the* “*Squidward torture*” *thing to death. A lot of our team was suffering pretty severe burnout from the concept and didn’t really wanna make ANOTHER episode about it. Execs had a bit of a problem with the propeller blade scene, thinking that a character getting sucked into it while screaming was too intense for little kids, which, eh, I kinda get? But the main reason was what happened to the lead storyboard artist for the episode.* 

*Interviewer - And what was that?* 

*Animator - Apparently he’d gone on a fishing trip with his family around the time the first drafts were finalized, he’d been working with the writer for the episode for… I wanna say two weeks by that point? Anyway, he ended up having a bad boating accident and got cut up pretty bad by his boat's propellers.* 

*Interviewer - Oh…*

*Animator - Yeah, kinda felt like poor taste at that point.*

I’ll admit, my stomach did kinda form knots when I read that. 

“That’s… that’s a messed up coincidence…” I thought, my hands curling into fists as I did. But that’s all it was, I told myself. Coincidence. A messed up, really unfortunate coincidence. Assuming it was even true. It’s not like this was an official statement, just a random internet article. It could be made up, right? 

I didn’t follow up on that thought, though. After I finished reading that interview, I heard my wife calling for help with the girls, and I kinda just decided I was done looking into it for the day. 

My wife, the perceptive woman that she is, could immediately tell that something was bothering me as we changed the kids. 

“Everything alright, honey?” She asked as she helped our oldest to her feet, who squealed happily and hugged her mother. Looking over at her, I considered telling her for a split moment, but thought better of it. It was a sad story, absolutely, but not really something that she would need to know, let alone be interested in. Besides, what wife wants to hear that her husband got freaked out by an old wives’ tale online? Still, I didn’t wanna lie to her, so instead, I just shrugged and said;

“Just read a creepy story online, it shook me a bit. I’m okay.” I reassured her, lifting my youngest in the air as she gave me a big smile. My wife didn’t seem too happy with my response, but left it there. 

I should have just it alone myself, taken that as my evidence that creepy lost media exists and been done with it. But… over the next few days I kept hearing snippets of that interview in my head. Picturing a man falling out of his boat and getting sliced open. Those storyboards. 

What were the odds? 

I want to say I tried to put it behind me, to just focus on my work and my family. But no, I didn’t even give that token level of resistance. Before long I was back online, looking into more lost media, trying to find that animator’s name, trying to find some kind of a connection. I wasn’t erratic or manic, I don’t think. I still paid attention to my wife, helped with the kids, everything I was supposed to and wanted to. But my wife did notice I seemed on edge. 

Every time she asked what was wrong, I just told her I was stressed, which again, isn’t a lie. When she offered explanations of work or being tired, I accepted them. Work had been busy, and I hadn’t really been sleeping well thanks to late night sessions. I didn’t want her off my back or anything, I just… wanted to find some kind of evidence one way or the other. 

I found it a week later. 

While looking through information on other Nickelodeon programming, I found a video on some news website I can’t remember the name of about *Hey Arnold!*. You know, that old show about the kid with a football head? 

The video went into a few specifics, from what inspired the show to the writing process, classic behind the scenes stuff. Honestly I was about to click off of it entirely when one of the storyboard artists came on screen. 

Here’s another rough transcript -

*Artist* *- Yeah there was this one episode that, I think it was around season 2, they were gonna play around with the timeline of the show. This guy (Abernathy) wrote this episode where Arnold and his buddies were gonna be sixteen and… the whole idea was that Arnold really wanted his driver’s license. Like all the kids had their licenses at this point and Arnold really wanted to get his so he wasn’t left out, you know?*

(This part of the interview started showing more story boards and rough illustrations)  

 *And he would have really struggled, you know, hitting cones, grandpa shaking his head while he backed up too far, stuff like that, and Arnold would just get so frustrated like, ‘Why can’t I get this?’ You know?* 

*Then towards the end there’s this scene where he’s taking his test, and some guy runs a red light, almost hits Arnold and his instructor. Now of course Arnold stops in time, he gets freaked out, instructor is breathing hard, and then they just drive back to the school. Then the instructor tells him, you know, “Hey kid, I can’t pass you cause we didn’t finish the test but you did good, good job out there.’*

*And it was supposed to, you know the end was he eventually realizes oh, you know, I’ll get it when I’m okay, I don’t need to kill myself just to get this thing, I’ll get it when I’m ready and I’ll get there.* 

The video then cuts to a different guy, rubbing his chin and looking up. 

*Animator - Yeah that episode kinda fell through cause we were, we kinda were thinking “Will a timeline episode even work? We haven’t done that before, what if the kids hate it or get comfused?” But then the writer of the episode, Abernathy, comes in telling us that one of the guys he was talking to about the episode just got into a wreck.* 

*So we’re asking ‘oh, is he okay, is he okay?’ and… we find out later that, some guy had, and I swear it’s true, some guy had run a red light, slammed into his driver’s side door. So uh… yeah we kinda took that as a sign that maybe the episode wasn’t meant to be.* 

“Crap…” I thought. That made two instances now, and not just two instances, two hyper specific, niche instances of a script literally coming to life. My heart was pounding as I rubbed the back of my head, trying to take deep breaths to calm myself down. I just stared at my screen, blankly watching as the documentary continued on about… I don’t  even remember what. What was I even supposed to do with this information? 

That’s when I realized; Abernathy. I had a name for the writer. Just search up the writer, I thought, nip this in the head so you can be done with it. Easy, I thought. I’d get some kinda proof this was all just weird happenstance, and I’d be done. Taking a deep breath, I opened a new tab, and typed his name into the search bar. It took some digging, filtering through dozens of pages and “more fitting” results before I eventually found what I was looking for. 

What I read next… I think it almost broke me. 

Apparently, according to his credits, not only was this Abernathy guy also responsible for that first SpongeBob episode, he had written THREE other scripts for popular kids’ shows on Nickelodeon, each one of them having their own bizarre tragedy. 

*Rugrats* having Tommy’s dad fall downstairs and needing to go to the hospital? The lead animator takes a fall and needs stitches in his head. *Invader Zim* has an episode where the little alien gets choked out by his rival? Sound technician gets mugged by a masked creep and almost dies. 

There’s an old saying that something happening once is just randomness, and twice is coincidence, but a third time is a pattern. Four major accidents, all of them tied to something that was supposed to happen in these episodes, and all of them written by the SAME guy. If three was a pattern, what the heck was four supposed to be, let alone FIVE?

The fifth script might be the one that freaked me out the most… 

This one was apparently never fully taken out of the rough draft phase, there weren’t even any story boards I could find on it. Instead, the hypothetical script was supposedly for a show called *Aaah! Real Monsters!*. Said episode was so early into production, that apparently it never even got past the full brainstorming stage. The only proof of its  existence were Abernathy’s notes on the subject. 

Said notes outlined the basic plot as follows; a kid begins suspecting one of the main monsters for stalking him and trying to scare him, leading him to start feverishly looking into the world of monsters, with the main trio having to desperately try avoiding him. Said kid’s name? 

Miller, my name. 

My. Name. 

This had to be a joke… it had to be, it absolutely had to be. An elaborate prank by someone, but who? 

“That’s ridiculous…” I thought, none of my friends were pranksters, and none of my family, let alone for something that would take this much effort. But what other option was there? That this Abernathy guy was actually psychic? Somehow knew my EXACT name and that I’d go digging down some rabbit hole? No, no no that was impossible, it had to be. And yet… here I was. Digging into it. 

My head was swimming with thoughts I could barely process, my heart beating so fast that I thought it was going to explode. How could I explain this? How could I rationalize this?

I couldn’t. 

More in a daze than anything, I looked at the last bit of text on the page, I don’t know why. Maybe I was too stunned to realize what I was doing, maybe I subconsciously wanted to find some justification. Whatever the reason, I wish I’d just closed the page. 

The last paragraph was an explanation from Abernathy himself, explaining why the episode never went anywhere, and why he chose to give up writing all together. 

To quote the man…

*Abernathy - The show was cancelled around the time I began drafting the episode, that’s the main reason it never saw a proper script. Looking back, I’m almost elated that it never saw the light of day. Considering how disastrously my future projects would end up going, I can only wonder what would have happened to whatever poor fool had been tied to that script.* 

*I plan to give up writing entirely by the end of this year, though, not before I write up one last snippet where that Miller kid lives a happy life. No other details, no big dramatic moments, just a happy life. I only hope that it doesn't get twisted for whatever poor soul I’m writing about.*
