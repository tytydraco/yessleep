Something really strange is going on with my wife, and it’s kind of hard to explain.

It all started a couple of months ago with the kids. Usually,  she tends to be really moody in the morning unless she’s had her coffee. I end up being the one who gets them ready for school and makes them breakfast, and I’ll usually take them to school too.

Well, that Monday, I woke up a bit late, and she was nowhere to be found. Admittedly, I did freak out a little, but after throwing the bedsheets off, and running downstairs to look for her, I found her with the kids having breakfast, laughing and . . . well, I hate to say this, but actually happy for once.

That wasn’t a one-time thing either! I’ve been sleeping in a lot more, and she’s taken a lot more of the childcare responsibilities.

Then, a few weeks after that, something else weird happened. You see, she got laid off recently and has been struggling to find work. Honestly, that’s mostly why she’s been a bit depressed and not taking care of the kids or herself. 

Of course, I don’t hold it against her, the market has been really tough. But, shit, one day I come home to flowers on the kitchen counter and a big cake. I’m entirely confused until she pops out of the bathroom with a massive smile, telling me to congratulate her for getting a job as a manager in an office, of all things. 

The crazy thing is that she’s doing amazingly well for somebody who was just in a deep depression. Happy, taking care of the kids, we get a lot more alone time, if you know what I mean *cough*.

But then the really weird thing happened: her mother died suddenly. We’ve always had boundary issues with her, and if you can believe it, she even wore “off-white” to our wedding. I don’t want to say she’s a fucking bitch, but truly those are the most appropriate words for her tombstone.

Of course, the wifey was sad, but she got over it after a few days, and the big benefit is that we inherited her mom’s house, which is massive!

Really, even during the move she’s been a total champ: she found the movers, helped pack most of the things, and even explained to the kids all the new things that would be going on. They were so psyched since they’d each finally get their own room, we’ve been talking about an upgrade for ages.

What’s been a little weird, though, is that for the past two weeks in the new house, I often wake up in the middle of the night to her standing next to the bed. She’s not looking at me or anything, but she’s just completely and unnaturally still, like she’s been frozen in time. Also, come to think of it, she was really gung-ho on moving to her mom’s place.

Even more worrying is that I often see her sneaking into the basement. She’s made it entirely off-limits, but . . . well, I guess I couldn’t let sleeping dogs lie.

One day when she was out of the house, curiosity got the better of me. After making sure that the house was empty, I went to the basement door, and shimmied it open. I used my phone’s flashlight to see because it was incredibly dark and the only light switch I could see at the top of the stairs was ripped out.

I only took a couple of steps before I started hearing muffled sounds of something moving and labored breathing. 

I was going to stop and turn around, but the curiosity was just eating at me too much, and I continued down the stairs. The sounds of a shifting body got louder and louder, and, despite my best attempts to not do something stupid, I started moving closer, and closer to it. 

Finally, I found another door I’d never seen before, even after having come down to this basement dozens of times since I’ve been married. The sounds of moaning and shuffling were nearly deafening in this quiet place.

I reached out and put my hand on the door handle, fully expecting to be jumped, but nothing happened. I just steeled myself and opened it, shining the light directly into whatever thing was inside there.

It was my wife. Bound with rope and her mouth covered in duct tape. Her eyes looked up at me suddenly, and I could see relief in her expression, maybe even a muted smile behind the tape. She tried speaking to me, but only muffled sounds came out of her mouth.

I backed up, from shock, or fear, or . . . I don’t know. Something.

She started scooting towards me, her eyes pleading. 

And I . . . I couldn’t do it. 

I closed the door.

The last thing I saw was the moment of realization in her eyes, and a single streaming tear.

She started banging up against the door with her body, at least, that’s what it sounded like. But by that time, I was already halfway up the stairs. 

By the point I had reached the top, I pretended not to hear or notice anything happening down there. I stepped out, closed the basement door, and went on with my life.

It’s been several days since, and I’ve been living with whoever this thing is, and I’ve never been happier! The kids are healthy and happy, the sex has never been better, and she’s doing amazing in her career.

And sure, I do sometimes hear weird muffled screams and dragging sounds from the basement, but I’m sure that’s nothing.

I don’t really know who or what this thing is pretending to be my wife.

But the truth is, I prefer her this way.
