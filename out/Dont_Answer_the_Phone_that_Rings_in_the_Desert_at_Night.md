Endless road lay ahead and behind. Days burned into nights that smeared into another round of endless nothing. Above stars were pin pricks piercing the veil of a sunset that might have once took my breath away, but at some point the brazen colors had lost their luster in my eyes – perhaps at the same time the cracked asphalt and faded white lines had done the same.

Along that damned road my drifting mind caught on the form of a glowing neon diner sign. After so many meals alone in them, the homey feel they tried to evoke wasn’t much more than a spit to the face. This one, though, it was reminiscent of an old movie and I couldn’t help but long for the meager nostalgia it offered. Gravel crunched under my tires as I pulled alongside the long building with it’s foggy windows that spilled yellow light onto crumbling sidewalk. As though in a trace, I pushed my way inside to the sound of a bell ringing over the door. My hand ran along the cracked faux leather of booths before landing on the chipped Formica countertop of the bar top, rings burned across the surface from years of condensing glasses. The stool had long lost its cushion, flat and complaining as I sat down. Automatically I sat my briefcase beside me, not that it did me any good in there, but old habits died hard.

“I’ll be with you in just a moment,” a plump waitress called from where she was clearing a table in a vintage apron tied far too tight.  

Through an opening I could see the sweat drenched fry cook flipping burgers. Each sizzle added to the greasy fog that formed a permanent cloud over the kitchen. His eyes cut to me as I sat stiffly, picking at the sticky laminated menu.  

“What’ll you be having?” the waitress asked as she leaned a little too close.

“Ah, the number five,” I muttered.

“One of my favorites! Do you want to upgrade that drink to a chocolate shake? It’s our specialty.”

“Sure,” I said with a flat smile. “Sounds great.”

She hummed to herself and took the menu before heading back to yell my order to the cook. After days of stale gas station sandwiches, a burger and shake felt like an unimaginable luxury – which at that point, it was. Just like the cheap motel rooms had once been before they turned to truck stop showers and empty parking lots. My fingers tapped anxiously on the counter as I waited, watching the window where sunset hadn’t lingered, simply gone on its way.

“You enjoy now, and let me know if there’s anything else I can get ya’,” the waitress said cheerfully as she slid the meal in front of me.

And yet small comforts turned to ash when soured by guilt. I tasted nothing as I forced myself to eat in that asynchronized diner with its colorful posters and checkered tile floor. All the cold shake did was make my teeth ache.

“What brings you round these parts?” the waitress asked as leaned over the counter.

“Traveling for work,” I offered nonchalantly.

“Not a lot of that passing through here anymore,” she frowned. “Most stay on the interstate.”

“Highways have more character,” I told her, because it was true, but the reality was interstates were for people who had a destination to aim for.

That made her brighten. “Well, you picked the right one. Which way you headed?”

I vaguely pointed the direction I’d been driving for days.

“Beautiful desert out there. Make sure you fill up, and when you pass the phone booth don’t stop. Best to let it keep ringing.”

That made me pause, but she moved on like there’d been nothing strange about the statement. When she brought me the ticket on a curling piece of receipt paper, I fished around in my pockets for the last crumpled dollars I had and still came up short. She offered a pitying smile and declared the shake on the house.

Outside the night offered no relief from the hot suck of air coming off the sand. But, I left the windows down anyway, letting the heat suffocate me. The weak light of my headlights barely illuminated the path ahead, though there was nothing to see anyway. It was all always just more of the same.

That was, until a lone streetlight illuminated a phone booth. Despite her warning, I slowed. I’d never seen one in real life, and it was like another set piece from movies I’d grown bitter accepting were prettied up reflections of what we pretended reality could be.

I parked.

And sat, only staring.

Faded blue panels surrounded the hookup, with a painted white phone symbol that was unmistakable even in the orangish artificial light. Old weeds broke through the cement around each corner, and even older cigarette butts were littered around them.

The phone didn’t ring, though.

No, it didn’t ring – but it did call to me.

Not for any mystical reason. I’d long stopped believing those existed. Oh no, it was because this was an unknown number, one I could call her from and she might actually answer.

She’d hang up the second she recognized my voice, though, and then block that number too. But maybe, just maybe, I’d get a chance to rest my case. Idly I spun the wedding ring on my finger – a false flag, one that I used on potential clients all the time.

“You see, I know a thing or two about commitment and how scary it can be to take that step. But it pays off, and you’re left with a legacy that lasts…”

I dug around in my car cupholders for a few quarters then stood in front of the phone with the tasteless memory of dinner still on my tongue. She’d offered a small life. A boring life. One with an old house that always needed some repair and simple meals most nights, but a deluge of small comforts that came day by day. No grand vacations, maybe a hotel a few towns over once or twice a year. A nice dinner on anniversaries at the upscale restaurant in the city. Neighbors who waived, a plush couch with worn cushions, the same tv shows on repeat until they were but a familiar background.

Maybe another life to fill the spare bedroom. One that brought more responsibility and strained our wallets to the last cent but filled the void enough that we could pretend there was a point in it all.

A small life, with little moments of joy that were supposed to be enough to make up for the monotony.

Her number was muscle memory – and it rang, and rang, and rang.

Voicemail.

I shouldn’t have, but I did.

“Hey. It’s uh, it's me. I know you said to stop calling, but I miss you, you know. It would be great to hear your voice again. This is for us, so I can give you the life of your dreams. All it takes is one client who believes in me. Don’t give up yet because–”

I stopped. Because it was what I refused to accept while she had long ago, after crying that I’d wasted years of her life she could never get back.

"Because it could be tomorrow.”

The click of the receiver was swallowed by miles of open desert around me. Sage brush that barely moved in the wind. Distant rock formations that were black shadows against an inky sky. So many stars that the whirls of purple galaxies broke through the atmosphere. And there I stood, in a pool of manufactured light next to a plastic phone that hadn’t even made connection.

My briefcase sat in the dirt. I never even thought about it anymore, just carried it with me from place to place. Scuffed at the bottom, clasps still shining from clacking open and closed. Always lighter but never heavier.

I sighed, picking it up and heading back towards the car. Dust powdered my worn leather shoes as I tracked across the dirt path, only to be stopped dead in my tracks by a harsh ring.

Slowly I turned.

The ringing continued, shocking against the quiet night.

It had to be her.

I rushed back, but my hand came up short right before grabbing it.

“Just let it ring,” were the words I remembered, or at least the gist of them.

But that was ridiculous. It was just *her*. She’d gotten my message and was calling me back. All those lonely nights and she’d finally seen what I’d been trying to tell her. As long as I kept moving forward there was hope. If I’d stayed all we’d ever have been was so small, with never a chance to be anything more.

“Hello?” I said breathlessly.

A crackle like static from an old tv.

“Hello?” I repeated. “Are you there?”

The static shifted, as though gathering itself, but words never broke.

“Is anybody out there?” I asked, resentment slicing through my voice.

“Right here,” another said quietly, with a shushing quality like the pixels of sound were working out how to come together.

I almost said her name, but it died in the same breath. Her voice I knew, in every shape, and this was not it.

“Who is this?”

The static swelled and quelled again before settling on a simple question. “When are you?”

“I’m sorry. What did you say?”

Another rush. “When. When are you?”

My eyebrows came together, but I lifted my watch and read the filigree of numbers.

“Just before midnight. Here, anyway. In this time zone.”

“Time zone,” the voice seemed to smile with how it picked up.

“Can I help you with something?” I asked, my customer service side keeping me neutral despite the prickle of annoyance along my neck.

“Tell me about when you are,” it demanded.

“Don’t you mean where?” I asked.

“Where doesn’t matter. Just when.”

“That doesn’t make sense,” I tried to explain. “It’s all the same, even if the clock reads different. But I guess if it helps you to know, it’s dark here and the moon is full."

“I see it,” the voice said wistfully, starting to hold more weight in its youthful, feminine tone.

“You see it? Where are you?” I spun around, but all that existed was more and more desert.

“I see the full moon and every phase in between.”

“That’s not how it works,” I shook my head, eyes still darting through the shadows looking for someone, or something.

“It must be nice to not see it all at once.”

“Sure,” I laughed nervously, “but it’s always the same things over and over again anyway.”

“You don’t have to follow it,” the voice offered, the sizzling effect almost gone as though it were at full attention.

I should have hung up. This stranger on a strange phone in a strange place – what good could come from that? But I was intrigued, and for so long the conversations had all turned the same circles. This was something *new*.

“Oh yea? Tell me about that then.”

A musical trickle like a giggle. “Okay. Step outside the booth. Look straight up.”

“The cord’s not that long.”

“Try it.”

I took one large step, and the coil stretched just enough. My head craned up into a cloudless sky.

“Alright. What now?”

“See the stars.”

“Yes, I see them,” I agreed like talking to a child – which maybe I was.

“No, *see* them.”

“What, do you want me to start naming constellations? There’s the Big Dipper, Orion’s Belt… ah, I don’t remember the others.”

“Follow one.”

“The North Star?”

“Sure, any of them really,” the voice laughed. “See where it takes you.”

“I have a GPS that’s more accurate. Nobody travels by stars anymore.”

“Give it a try anyway.”

I looked at my car – a car that had taken me so many places, but none that had stuck. Miles were eaten under tires without ever amounting to anything. What were a few steps after stars? It’s not like I had anywhere else to be.

“Alright. I’ll tell you what. I’ll walk until the end of this cord. Then, I’m going to hang up and be on my way.”

“Deal,” the voice warped as it agreed.  

Goosebumps peppered my skin, but I shook it off. The deeper night sank on the desert, the more chill it brought. Sometimes it was downright cold. Or perhaps, that was just from the shock of losing the beat of the sun.

“Walking,” I said with falsetto.

“Keep looking up. You can switch stars, just keep watching them.”

“I don’t know much about night navigation, but I don’t think that’s how it works.”

“Where doesn’t matter, just when. Remember that.”

“How old are you?” I asked, my tone dropping. This had to be a kid prank calling.

“Many and none,” the voice said cheerfully. “Though once something measurable by your standards.”

“My standards are years, and I’m starting to think you don’t have enough to be talking on the phone to a stranger at night.”

“Seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, months, years, decades… millenniums. None of them mean much.”

“There is a huge difference,” I said, pulling the cord that continued to stretch. When I looked back, there were yards between me and the booth. “But I can agree the years feel short and long at the same time.”

So many of them lost to the road. One more, just one more. That would make them all worth it. Her smile at the beginning hadn’t show the marks of time as she’s waived goodbye, but lines had been added every year without anything to show in return. Had it really been that long since I stayed still?

“Starlight is so special, traveling in lightyears. Those are measured in distance, you know. Not time. It’s a beautiful thing, traveling by them. You are moving forward by looking back.”

“I guess… I guess that’s true,” I said, giving the cord another yank and it just kept stretching. Maybe I should have stopped looking up and instead looked back, because now the booth was just a rectangle in the distance.

“They are born, and live, and die without us ever seeing them as they are in the present, since you only see *when*, and it’s traveled far past that. But do you want to know what I see?” the voice became an excited whisper.

“How long is this damn cord?” I dismissed as I pulled and pulled but it just kept going and going.  

“I see it all. But I miss *moments*. Moments have potential. When you see it all… all you see is all you get. I miss the mystery of what’s next.”

“Yea, well, it never amounts to much anyway,” I grumbled, stopping and turning now that the booth was fully gone and all that tied me to that spot was a taunt black cord endlessly feeding.

“You move to reach tomorrow,” the statement almost sounded like a question.

“That’s how time works, yes.”

“When are you?”

Glass shattered in my mind. I was standing, in a desert, with an impossibly long cord and a stranger on the phone. This wasn’t just stupid, it was dangerous, and I needed to turn around right away.

But as I lowered the phone, I was in the stars. No, the stars were with me. Sky had fallen around me, starlight striking the ground in beams. Reaching out a hand, the circles of light brightened on my palms, and I followed them up, and up, and up. Far into the sky. Small variations on the lines, like flickering flames that had frozen in place. A million streaks, a million moments, a disco across sands that warped with each blink. One moment there, the next not, one second the sand a boulder, the next a granule. The aura of galaxies fell, colorful smokes drifting over sand grains whispering that they, too, were once stardust, and would be again.

And it was loud.

Loud silence, like the sounds were inside my mind but not outside. Sounds of the earth forming and falling apart while starlight struck it over and over again. I clenched that phone to my ear as if that could ground me.

My days passed behind my eyes. So many forgotten moments flashing – and the days ahead the same. Back and forth, a rolodex of all I was and would be – and it was dim compared to the universe exploding ad infimum around me.

“Sell it to me,” the voice seemed to goad. “That peace of mind. A moment that lasts, that means something. Why should I invest in yours?”

“Is this all there is?” I asked, watching my life bounce back and forth without ever catching.

“You wanted more?” the voice grew hard.

“It’s… not enough,” I said towards an existence that was just moment after moment after moment of moving towards something greater that never came.

A sigh, frequency cracking. Stardust auroras fading back to dark blue, then black. Sand sweeping into smooth scapes, individual granules lost in the sea.

I walked, eyes still up as beams of light receded ever so slowly back to pinpricks.

“Tell me again when you are?” the voice asked, sounding wistful as it grew further away until I had to strain to hear it.

And the words I said, they came from somewhere I didn’t recognize – but dammit if I’d never felt something so true in all my life.

“Where the hourglass comes together and a stream of sand passes from before to after.”

“What happened to the grain?” it asked, so quiet I was almost certain I’d made it up as I stood in front of the receiver, looking through the grimy slats of the booth.

“It’s all the same,” I said, this time my voice a broken whisper as the phone clicked back place.

Behind the wheel of my car, it was daylight. And then it was night. A comfy bad, the back of a car. A smooth hand, then wrinkled. Smiles rising and falling, tears wetting and drying. Seasons of spring in bloom while the skeletons of winter still stood. No more moments, it all simply was and sometimes, when I looked up at the sky, I would ask myself *when* I was.

And in those brief moments, my life would still and lay out before me.

So, so small, after all.
