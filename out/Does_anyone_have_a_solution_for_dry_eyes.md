I’ve tried everything. Damp cloths. Lubricating drops. Boiling the kettle until the room is a sauna. Nothing works. My eyes are so damn dry.


It all started with that damn box. I never should have bought it at the flea market.


I just thought it was neat - a charming little tchotchke. Metallic, brushed brass, and no bigger than a small brick. On the front were two deep glass lenses; on the back, two eyes carved into the metal. A rectangular shutter button sat on top. The only writing was stamped on the bottom in a raised, winding font:


**The Buskin Box.**


The woman at the stall couldn't tell me much. A customer had traded it for an antique Sheffield penknife two weeks prior. She only remembered that the guy looked utterly exhausted - "like he hadn’t slept in weeks." She took the twenty bucks I offered for it, I tossed it into my tote, and I went home.


It was later that I took a closer look and realised what I had on my hands: The Buskin Box was a vintage viewing toy, like a View Master or a stereoscope.


I lifted it to my face and peered into the deep black lenses.


The image inside was dim. As my eyes adjusted, I made out a man standing in front of heavy red velvet curtains, like the backdrop of an old vaudeville stage. He was facing away from me, wearing a buttoned white shirt with his pomaded hair slicked back.


I pressed the shutter button.


**Flash.**


A blinding light burned the scene onto my retinas as a piercing, high-pitched note shrieked in my ears - the kind of sudden pressure you feel when an airplane cabin drops.


Nausea hit me like a wave, and I dropped the box. Then, just as quickly as it came, the sickness vanished.


I picked up the box and looked back into the lenses. Nothing. Complete darkness. I guess I broke the light mechanism when it hit the floor - twenty bucks down the drain.


When I blinked, the afterimage lingered. You know how staring at the sun leaves a floating burnt spot in your vision? It was like that.


Except it didn't fade.


Every time I blinked, the image refreshed. All afternoon, the ghost of that skinny man and his red curtains floated in front of my vision. By nightfall, I had a killer headache, so I called it early and went to bed.


Lying in the dark, the image grew unnaturally vivid. I found myself inspecting the scene behind my eyelids: the crimson drapes, the spotlight, the man’s silhouette. It felt so tangible I could almost reach out and touch it.


Then I noticed something impossible. The man was turning.


Earlier, I had only seen the back of his head. Now, he had rotated just enough for me to catch the profile of a long nose and the edge of a wet, bloodshot eye.


I figured I was exhausted and forced myself to sleep.


I don't remember what I dreamed, but I woke up at dawn screaming, soaked in sweat.


My vision was still wrecked. Every blink brought up the picture, locking me in a feverish haze for the whole of the morning. I was unable to function, so I went back to bed, closed the bedroom curtains, and tried to sleep it off.


That’s when I noticed the image had shifted again.


The man had turned completely. He was staring straight at me.


He looked like something out of a Norman Rockwell painting - slicked hair, white apron, buttoned collar. But his expression was pure malice. His eyes were rimmed red, dripping wet, and filled with a horrifying mix of agony and rage. It was an unmistakable look that screamed: *HOW DARE YOU LOOK AT ME?*


I blinked twice, trying to clear my sight.


**He moved.**


It was an inch, but it was real. His face contorted into deeper fury and he was closer to me than he had been the moment before. 


Panic set in. I sprinted to the bathroom, splashing cold water onto my face, blinking rapidly to wash the hallucination away.


That was a mistake.


He moved with every single blink. Frame by frame. A sick, living flipbook.


Blink. Move. Blink. Move.


He was raising his arm, reaching toward me. I could feel hatred radiating off him like heat from a fire. He despised being seen, and he wanted to destroy me for looking.


I ran into the living room, snatched the box off the floor, and searched desperately for an off switch, a latch, instructions - anything. 


But there was nothing. Just those three stamped words: The Buskin Box.


In a rage, I slammed it against the hardwood.


The moment it impacted, a white-hot spike of agony shot through my frontal lobe. I collapsed, trembling and sweating as the pain ebbed away.


When I looked behind my eyes again, he was right there. "Mr. Buskin" was leaning forward, his clubbed fingers inches from my face.


I had fifteen, maybe twenty blinks left before he touched me. And I knew - with the absolute certainty of instinct - that if he touched me, I was dead.


I had to ration my blinks. Keep my eyes open. Buy time.


But the harder you try not to blink, the faster your body forces you to. I burned through six blinks in three minutes. His teeth set into a snarl; his hand strained forward.


Hysteria threatened to grip me. So I tried the opposite: I clamped my eyes shut. If I didn't blink, he couldn't move.


I slammed my eyelids down and pressed my knuckles into my sockets. But in the dark, his hatred was amplified. Malice, disgust, and loathing washed over me so intensely my skin felt like it was peeling off in a boiling pot. I couldn't bear it.


I opened my eyes. I was out of time.


I stumbled into the kitchen and yanked open the drawer, pulling out a dull chef's knife.


*Blink.*


He was closer.


*Oh god. Okay. Keep it simple. Pinch the skin. Drag the blade. Pinch. Drag. Pinch. Then drag.*


I grabbed my left eyelid, pinched the skin tight, and brought the metal up.


I began to slice. Halfway through, I realized the screaming echoing in the kitchen was coming from my own mouth. 


I threw the wet scrap of flesh into the sink. I remember thinking how small it looked. Barely anything at all.


*Blink*. **Fuck!**


Right eye next. My fingers were slick with blood, making it impossible to grip the eyelid. I started laughing- or maybe crying; I couldn't tell anymore.


If I couldn’t pinch it, I would have to slice directly. I pointed the tip of the blade at the base of my right brow.


I counted to three. I held my breath.


And I pushed the knife around my eye.


That was three hours ago. I think. I might have lost time.


It’s hard to type with blood dripping onto the keys, and the screen is a blur, but I haven't blinked once. 


I know if I do, Mr. Buskin will be there. Ready to get me.


But goddamn. My eyes are *so* dry.
