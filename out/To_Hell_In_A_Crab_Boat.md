I can still remember the sound of my sister humming, as our Mom tied our Dad to the pier behind our house.  The marsh grass rustled in the wind, almost humming along with her.

“Connie…damnit. I said with gusto, girl….”

Mom nodded and obliged to Dad’s request, making the ropes tighter.  He laid on his back with four ropes strung from each foot and hand to a wood pylon.

Their serene calmness and acceptance of the matter is only now strange to me.  Dad even giggled as her hair brushed his face when she leaned over him.

“You still teasin’ and ticklin a man straight to the end…”

Mom, slapped his chest playfully.

“You bad…”, but then she leaned in for a real kiss, one bursting with true love, albeit troubled, before coming back up. “Thank you, our King…”

“You live…you find solace in Mother’s mercy, in her newfound fruit.”

She didn’t cry, she just smiled proudly. “We will.”

Just then…

The tidal clock began to ring.

Dad shouted to us, “Your fortune cometh, my angels. Live in light.”

“Yes, Daddy.” “Yes, Pa”, we both squeaked out.

“COMMENCE!”, he bellowed. 

Our Mom stood up and walked back over to us.  We held each other and swayed as we sang…just as we practiced.

*“Mother drink from our wine;*  
*We’re gonna let it shine;*  
*Mother drink from our wine;*  
*We’re gonna let it shine*;”…

WHEN…

The water began to rise.

From a foot beneath the pier, steadily, it came up an inch…and then another…and another.

We continued….

*“Mother take this man of swine;*  
*He scorched your Earth time and time;*  
*Please take this man of swine;”….*

Dad kept the same grin, the same easy, content breathing as the water rose over the wooden planks of wood, up the sides of his body and, finally, over his face.

*“He scorched your Earth time and time;*  
*Time and time, time and time, take this swine;”*

And with that, the water at our knees on the pier, we trudged back up to our risen house and had a dinner of potatoes and beans. The only food that could grow.  Then we took turns reading from Mother Natures hymnal*.*

Ten years later, I miss my father and we are no closer to appeasing the Queen. If you think me false because your water, wherever you sit, has not risen…I fear for the eye that has not opened.  

I can tell you from experience that Her wrath is resistant to any promise, sacrifice or act of fortune.  I asked my Mother just this morning if she thought Father’s sacrifice was for nought, and she simply scoffed and said, “We still livin’, ain’t we? Man has done wrong by Her and she will take every last one.”

“Why does she toys with us though? Why not create a wave or fire to end it in one fail swoop?”, I asked.

“I do not bother myself with such quandaries, those that provide little salt to our means.”

I am starting to doubt my family’s path, but I have to keep my family alive, as long as I can. My teenage sister, my aging mother. 

We live in this Crab Boat now, awaiting the great rise of the tides that will eventually come.  I am the captain now and we will persist.
