Before I started keeping records, little things had already been happening. Not dramatic enough to convince me something impossible was happening. Things I'd normally attribute to bad memory, coincidence, tiredness, misunderstanding, etc.

Everything became increasingly difficult to explain over roughly a year.

The scariest thing by far that happened to me before I began recording evidence was when I was going to bed. Before I fell asleep, I got tucked in and watched YouTube as normal. I looked up from my phone and I promise you I saw someone staring me down in the corner.

I saw them clearly.

My instinct was to freeze, so I pretended I didn't see them until I could come up with a plan. After all, if they've been there this long, they'll be there a little longer. I just lay there and continued scrolling, my heart racing.

I've seen enough Forensic Files episodes to know sick people learn your patterns and wait in your closet all day until you're home.

I wasn't going to assume I was seeing things.

Finally I had a plan. It felt like it took me minutes, but it was probably a few split seconds. I keep my golf putter close enough to my bed, slightly off the path I'd need to reach the closet. So the plan I came up with was to roll off the right side of my bed, and in one motion grab the putter and charge back at the closet and smash with all my might.

I was ready to execute my plan. I kept my eye on the phone and held still. And in one shot of ancient fight-or-flight adrenaline I jolted off the right side of my bed, grabbed the putter faster than I could blink, charged the closet like a scared and angry animal, and swung that putter with all the force I could muster in my entire life and directed it at this creep.

Nothing.

I swung again. Clothes and drywall. Nothing else.

As you might have guessed, there was no one there.

I don't think I've ever moved so fast or used so much force. I was genuinely ready to kill the person and deal with the legal consequences.

I can't even sit for more than a minute without looking over my shoulder when I think about it. This happened in like late 2024, November or early December.

That was absolutely the first bizarre thing that's ever happened to me in my life. I'm the first one to come up with rationalist explanations. And believe me, for months I was convinced I was probably half asleep and hallucinated someone standing there.

I remember thinking it might have been sleep paralysis, but obviously I wasn't paralyzed. I got out of bed and swung a golf club at it.

So for months that's basically how I treated it. Something weird happened when I was half asleep and my brain filled in a person that wasn't there. I didn't start keeping records or anything after that.

There was nothing to record.

But then little things kept happening. Sometimes I'd think I saw a person or a shadow and when I'd look again there'd be nothing there.

I almost asked him if I knew him but I didn't.

Nothing I could prove, and honestly nothing that strange individually. Every time there was a reasonable explanation.

It was only way later, after enough things had happened, that I started documenting them. At first it was mostly because I wanted to be able to check myself. If I remembered something one way, I wanted something I could go back to and see if that's actually what happened.

So I started collecting pieces.

It had been months since I last hallucinated (or whatever you want to call it) about an unwelcome visitor. This time was different. I woke up from a deep sleep and again, that horrifying presence. Standing there, staring at me, like it could get me, yet I couldn't get it. Just silently looking at me across the room.

By this time I was just about as scared as I was the first time it happened, but ever so slightly wiser. So I pulled out our "rational friend" ChatGPT and asked it what it thought. Plus I thought doing so accomplished the "pretend you don't see the intruder" method that I've so clumsily developed.

This time it was a very clear figure standing in the entranceway to my apartment, a section I can vaguely see from my bed.

The photo was far too dark to make sense. It was just grain. Of course ChatGPT couldn't decipher it. Just abyss, and the red light of the motion sensor.

The motion sensor light was probably caused by me.

Fast forward a few months. It was an entirely normal night in the apartment. I heard a massive "bang" sound coming from my wall. Not a person, but a mechanical type of sound.

I rushed around the apartment trying to find out what it could possibly be. I was really frantic, not scared per se because it didn't sound like a person, just that if it's a mechanical issue I need to find it ASAP.

As I was searching for the source of the sound, perhaps within 30 seconds, I heard another horrendous sound. The sound of metal crushing and tires screeching and a woman yelling as loud as she could, seemingly to get the attention of the entire neighbourhood.

And for good reason.

Very soon after, blue and red lights reflected off the glass of the apartment buildings, sirens blaring. But I had my own issue to deal with.

What was this extremely loud bang from my apartment?

There was absolutely no visual evidence to suggest what the sound might be, so I called building maintenance. They sent their 24-hour crew very soon, and in fact they were ready to call me anyway.

The system detected a pressure spike in the piping running along my unit, and my unit was the point to the burst or whatever it was. They came into the unit that night, opened up the drywall, took a look and closed it back up.

I never had another issue.

But again, I've never heard such a pipe burst, and I've never been around a car crash of this magnitude, and yet they happen within 30 seconds of each other in my life?

How and why?

I keep searching for an explanation.

Yes.

The timing of the accident was impeccable.

10:42 PM.

The same minute the pipe burst occurred.

10:42 PM.

That's the pipe burst.

How strange.

Why would the crash and pipe burst happen simultaneously?

Why would the driver flee on foot if there were no reports that the vehicle was stolen and no serious injuries occurred?

The pipe is easily explained. The crash is easily explained. The figures in the corner are easily explained. The people staring me down are easily explained.

Everything is so easily explainable, yet nothing makes sense.
