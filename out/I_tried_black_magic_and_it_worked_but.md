I don't know how to explain what my life has been like, so I'll just say it plainly. Nothing good has happened to me in years. I'm not exaggerating for effect. Every year something worse happens than the year before, until eventually I stopped waiting for it to get better and just started waiting for whatever was coming next.

About eight months ago I noticed I couldn't feel things anymore. Not sadness, not happiness, nothing. I'd sit in my apartment — I live alone, my parents live far away and depend on the money I send them every month — and I'd just stare at the wall, feeling absolutely nothing, and some small part of me almost missed being sad, because at least sad was something.

That's the state I was in when I found the ritual online. I'm not going to explain what it involved. I'll just say I was desperate enough to try anything that promised to make me feel like a person again, even for a second.

It worked. Just not the way I wanted.

The first thing I noticed was the cold spot by my bedroom door. It never went away after that night, no matter the season. Then I started hearing three distinct sounds at three different times, every single day, like clockwork.

The first always came in the early morning, around 4 a.m. A woman's voice, humming, slow and mournful, from somewhere inside my closet. I never opened that closet again after the second time I heard it, not because I was scared exactly — remember, I couldn't really feel scared anymore — but because some old animal part of my body still knew to avoid it, even if my mind didn't react the way it should have.

I started calling her the Mourner. She never spoke words, only hummed, and the tune always sounded like it was building toward something, some final note that never came, over and over, every single morning, endlessly unfinished.

The second came at midday, always while I was trying to eat. A child's laughter, high and delighted, coming from directly behind my chair. Every time I turned around, nothing was there. But small things started disappearing after the laughter — a spoon, a single sock, the second half of whatever I was eating, gone from my plate the moment I looked away.

I called this one the Thief. It never seemed to want anything valuable. It just wanted to take small pieces of my life, one at a time, like a child pulling wings off something slowly, delighted by how little it took to make me feel like I owned less of my own home every single day.

The third came at night, right as I tried to sleep. A man's voice, calm, reasonable, always saying the exact same six words into my ear right as I drifted off.

"You should stop paying for this."

I understood after the third night that it meant the money I sent my parents. I didn't need to ask what it wanted me to stop doing instead.

I called this one the Convincer, because unlike the others, it wasn't trying to scare me or take from me. It was trying to talk me into something, patiently, every single night, the same six words, never louder, never angrier, just endlessly, calmly reasonable.

The three of them together were bad enough. But about two months in, I understood there was something else living in my house with them. Something none of the three ever went near. I only ever caught it out of the corner of my eye — tall, still, standing at the far end of whatever room I was in, never approaching, just watching, like a landlord checking in on tenants doing exactly the work he'd assigned them.

I went to five different priests. Two churches. An exorcist my coworker found online who charged me almost everything I had left after sending my parents their share that month. Every single one of them did their ritual, their prayers, their blessed water and salt, and every single one of them left my house looking pale and shaken, telling me something I didn't want to hear.

The last one, an old priest who'd clearly seen more than the others, told me the truth plainly before he left. He said the three spirits weren't the problem. They were symptoms. Whatever I'd opened during that ritual had let something much older into my house, something that didn't need to scare me or steal from me or convince me of anything, because it wasn't interested in my choices at all.

It was just waiting. Patiently. For the part of me that still felt anything to finish dying on its own.

I've thought about ending things more than once since all this started. I won't pretend otherwise. But I can't. Not because I'm strong, and not because I found some reason to hope things will get better. It's because my parents depend on the money I send every month, and if I stop, I don't know what happens to them. So every single day I get up, and I go to work, and I feel nothing the entire time, and I come home to a house with three spirits and something else standing quietly in the corner of every room, and I keep going, because stopping isn't a door I'm allowed to walk through.

I thought that was the worst part of my situation. I was wrong.

Last night, I couldn't sleep, so I finally went back and reread the instructions for the ritual I did eight months ago, the ones I'd been too afraid to look at again since that first night. And near the very end, in a section I must have skimmed past in a hurry back then, desperate to feel something, anything, I found a line I don't think I truly understood the first time I read it.

It said the ritual doesn't call spirits to torment you.

It calls them to feed on whatever emotion you're the most starved of. It draws them toward the exact shape of what's missing in you, and it keeps them there until that emptiness is completely full again, one way or another.

I've spent eight months thinking three spirits and something worse have been torturing me for no reason.

I don't think that's true anymore.

I think they were sent because I couldn't feel anything, and somewhere in that house is something that's been very patiently, very carefully, teaching me how to feel again fear, loss, dread, grief, one lesson at a time, exactly in the order I stopped being able to feel them naturally.

And for the first time in eight months, lying awake in the dark last night, I felt something I hadn't felt in a very long time.

I felt afraid of what happens when the lessons are finished.
