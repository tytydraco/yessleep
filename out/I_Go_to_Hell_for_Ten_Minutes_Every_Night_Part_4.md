[**\[Part 3\]**](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1xv5ick/i_go_to_hell_for_ten_minutes_every_night_part_3/)

I spent all day watching Sarah and doing very little else. I did check out my old PC. It’s like ten or twelve years old and still works okay, but it’s definitely on the slower side. I found the old chat logs from back when I was fifteen or so. The guy went by an odd username, **1over137** (plus a few more digits), and he would only ever talk to me over old AOL dinosaur chat. He’s the one who taught me a lot about the different hells.

I remember being really scared of them during that first year. I’d spend all day dreading the 1:18 am flip, and I’m not ashamed to admit that I pissed myself on more than one occasion. 137 is the one that made me realize I was safe. Sure, it all looked scary, but nothing could touch me, and there weren’t any demons there to guard the tormented. They didn’t need them—the hells could mind themselves.

It meant a lot to me, and I started to think of 137 as the older brother I never had. He was someone who understood what I went through. It wasn’t exactly the same for him—he was never really clear on how his thing worked—but we had walked a lot of the same paths. I never got to meet him in real life, and he had already ghosted me well before the hospital incident. I wasn’t mad about it, though; he and Sarah are the ones who got me through the rough patches.

They knew about each other. I rarely held anything back from either of them, except maybe the embarrassing stuff. I didn’t think they wanted me to describe the physiological effects of the Orgy Hell on a horny fifteen-year-old. Let’s just say I never needed the full ten minutes when it sent me there. God, I’m blushing about it even now.

You asked for it, people. I’m in a sharing mood after what happened last night.

But yeah, I told them all about each other, and the three of us figured out what I had to do and how I could do it. The rules I came to follow, and later break, came from those conversations. So, to sum it up, 137 was very important to me, and now more than ever I really needed his advice.

Oh, and Sarah is a motherfucking genius. If she lives, I’m totally going to gross her out with a big, wet sloppy kiss. She found his email!

There was a notepad opened on the desktop when I woke up the computer. Typical Sarah shit. The first line said “remember when Will thought it was Lover 137.” It was interesting, because I could visual her sitting there, a devilish grin spread across her face, while she planned out how to sneak that into the conversation. Everybody needs a friend like her.

After her lame joke, there was a lot of code and similar crap that made my eyes go ultra hazy. Below that there was buried treasure. The last line she had typed was an old hotmail address, and it had the word valid written next to it.

I seized on it immediately. If I could get in touch with 137, he might know what to do or how I could help Sarah. Something. Whatever it was, I’d have an ally again, and I could really use another one. So I made a new gmail and sent him a message. He responded a few hours later, and we set up a meeting for Saturday morning on a weird messenger app he told me about. I followed his instructions and got it set up right away.

Friday night was the Charred Hell. That’s what I’ve always called it; I guess I prefer snappy one-word names for them. It’s awful, like so many of them, a forest of trees under a gray sky with heavy piles of ash that weigh down their limbs and bury their feet. The petitioners—that’s what 137 called them—are stuck to those trunks, and their bellies are open and stuffed with coals. Their thirsty moans are absolutely heartbreaking.

I walked along the forest trail that night holding the piece of marble in a death grip. I wanted to use it, but I was scared to. If I died, there would be no one to take care of Sarah, and I could already tell she was getting worse when I checked on her before I left. It was painful to me because I felt like I was choosing her over Maia again.

“She’d want you to,” I muttered to reassure myself.

Sarah would laugh at me for overthinking it. She always said that internal speculation is a doomed expedition. But what if she was doomed, or Maia? Not to mention me.

And what did she know?

I had almost made up my mind to just go ahead and flip over to the Marble Hell anyway. I guess I was in the mood to take a chance and tempt the devil. Apparently, the latter had already happened.

The purple-eyed phantom popped in down the trail from me, and its shriek drowned out everything else. I turned and ran, checking the clock as my feet pounded away from the terror behind me. 1:32 meant I had a little less than two minutes to go if my estimations were correct. I could do it; I just had to put some distance between us.

I left the path and ran among the trees, twisting and turning in my attempt to get away from it. I tried not to look back, but I’m human and those claws are sharp. It was getting further away, but I didn’t know how long I could keep up that pace.

When I came around the base of a particularly massive tree, I saw a huge pile of ash behind it and I dove into it immediately. I had just oriented myself, making sure I was fully covered, when I heard my pursuer go screeching past me. The ash stung my skin, and it was particularly agonizing for my injured arm, but I lay still and counted the seconds. I had to be getting close.

When the tingling began, I smiled out of pure relief and was rewarded with a mouthful of the disgusting charred powder. I coughed, and the phantom was on me in an instant. It shoved the ash away with a swipe of its claws, and I knew it had me. I wasn’t getting away this time. You’d think I was scared, but you’d be wrong. I was pissed.

I can’t take credit for it, because it was against every rational thought I had, but I lunged for the thing. If it was going to kill me I wasn’t going to make it easy. I don’t know how it worked, maybe I caught it off guard, but I actually knocked it onto its back and started wailing on it. My fists mostly passed through its vaporous form, but there was something solid inside and I felt it when I connected. That’s how I ended up with my hands locked around its throat.

It’s how I saw what it showed me.

I was fading away but I absolutely saw it. The Island. Maia was looking right at me and I heard her when she said, “Hurry, Will. I can’t last much longer.” And then I was back in the driver’s seat of Sarah’s car.

I didn’t sleep much that night. It’s becoming a bit of a habit for me. When I wasn’t losing my mind over seeing Maia and the Island, through the phantom I was standing over Sarah. She was bad, and I wanted to take her, to get her some help, but she shook her head. I had already put every blanket and all of the towels in the tub with her, but she still shivered like she was freezing. Between that and the wet cough she had developed, I didn’t think she had long. She finally fell asleep at around four, and I took a nap as well. I had to be up at 7:30 for my chat with 137.

Sarah was still sleeping when I logged into the app. Less than a minute later, I got my first message from him. I won’t bore you too much with how it started. We caught up for a few minutes—or I did, he’s still cagey as hell. I told him about everything that’s happened. I even shared links to my posts. It turns out he had already read them, and even stranger than that, he was the one who sent me the text about the police.

>**\[ME\]:** How? How’d you know they were coming?

>**\[137\]:** Can’t tell you yet. Let’s just say I have my sources.

>**\[ME\]:** You’ve got to give me something. I think she’s going to die.

>**\[137\]:** You’d be in more danger if I told you. They’re watching you close, but I’ll tell you everything soon. I’m not jerking you around, man, these people are dangerous.

>**\[ME\]:** What about Sarah? Is there anything I can do?

>**\[137\]:** You can call an ambulance or drive her yourself. It sounds like shock or sepsis. You can’t play with it.

>**\[137\]:** Talk again soon. Take care of your friend.

He was right. Of course he was, and I was a fool for waiting too long to do what needed to be done. I knew when I loaded her into the car that I was making the correct decision. She was so hot to the touch that I actually checked my hands for blisters. I locked our room up and slung a fishtail of gravel as I sped out of the parking lot. We pulled up to the hospital an hour later.

“Help!” I screamed, and a pair of nurses came out with a gurney. I helped them get her on it, and we had just finished when a young doctor came walking up. She asked me what had happened, and I told her I found Sarah that way. She looked down at my bandaged arm and then raised her eyes back to mine. I could tell by the incredulous look on her face that I was in trouble.

“Just help her, hurry,” I begged.

Say what you want about our healthcare system. The people in that ER were awesome yesterday. They took her back without another word. I was too exhausted to argue when the doctor told me to wait in the lobby, and I didn’t say anything at all when the security guard came to collect me. I was going to jail and I knew it. I figured I’d make it easy. I dug Shugart’s card out of my wallet and tossed it to the guy.

A few minutes later, a nurse came into the little room where they had stashed me. She had a medical kit with her and asked me if she could see my arm. A doctor arrived right after that, and they cleaned it out and sewed it shut. For a wonder, the stitches actually stayed in.

I could tell that they wanted to ask me about it, but neither of them did. Maybe they had seen Sarah and thought it better not to know.

I saw Shugart walk in. He glanced over at me and nodded at the guard, but he didn’t come over yet. Instead, he stood there for a few minutes, talking to the doctor. She handed him something, and I heard him thank her before he came over to me.

“It’s good to hear from you, Will,” he said. “You had us worried.”

“Yeah, about that—”

“Save it, kid,” he chuckled, but there was no humor in it. “Stand up and turn around for me. Place your hands behind your back.”

I did it, and I felt the cold steel of his handcuffs close around my wrists as he read me my rights in that gruff, two-packs-a-day voice. The two of them walked me out to a black sedan and tucked me in the backseat. Shugart reached inside and slid the belt across me, clicking it in place. After he closed the door, he turned and shook the security guy’s hand before walking around to climb into the driver’s seat.

“What, no backup?”

“Nah,” he said. “I wasn’t too worried about you. Plus, Ernie said you’re the one who gave him my number. Sounded like you wanted to talk.”

He turned around to look at me. “I’m a sucker for a good conversation.”

We didn’t really say much on the way into the station. It was mostly light chit-chat, and he kept it steered away from anything serious. I could tell he was working me. He’d probably handled hundreds of interrogations before me, and I’d bet you he talked to every one of them just like he was talking to me. He was almost pleasant and seemed genuinely good-natured. Shugart wanted me to think that he was open-minded and truly concerned for me.

He would have been happy to keep me thinking it all the way up until the judge sentenced me.

When we got there, he led me in through the back of the station. It wasn’t like what I’d seen on TV. There was no crowd of officers there to cheer and pat him on the back. I didn’t see anybody else in cuffs or anything like that. Shugart just led me in front of him down an empty hall and into a room with a table in the middle and a chair on either side of it.

“Get you something to drink before we start?”

He left and came back a few minutes later with a cup of coffee and a bottle of water. Shugart took a sip and set down the mug before walking over to me. He released one of my hands and hooked the empty cuff to a metal ring on the table.

“I’m giving you back one of your hands. Don’t make me regret it.”

He slid my drink over before opening up a thick file and saying the time, date, and both of our full names out loud.

“You don’t mind if we record this, do you?” he asked, and I shook my head as I took a drink.

“You’ve gotta say it, Will,” he told me.

“Yeah, might as well.”

“Good,” he said. “You don’t have anything to hide.”

I didn’t say anything to that. Honestly, I couldn’t think of what to say about any of it. If I told the truth, he wouldn’t believe it, and if I lied to him he’d know. I could see him studying me with his bright green eyes, and he was smiling a little, like he knew a secret and he was dying to share it. After a few minutes of silence and several awkward sips, he went into it.

“For the record, what time did your girlfriend leave your house that night?”

“She didn’t,” I told him. I was tired of lying, and I’ve always been terrible at it.

“She didn’t?” He cocked an eyebrow. “Well, where’d she go, Will?”

“You wouldn’t believe me if I told you.”

“You should try me,” he said. “You might’ve noticed that I’m no spring chicken, Will. I’ve seen a lot in my time. You’d be amazed at what I’m willing to consider.”

That’s when it hit me, and I said, “I’ll tell you at 1:15, and not a minute before then.”

“It’s 1:45 now, bub.”

“I mean the next one.”

Shugart agreed to play along, but I don’t think he believed me. He still had a weird smile on his face when he took the cuffs off of me through the door of the holding cell. I didn’t say anything else to him. I just walked over to the cot and laid back on it. I was asleep a few minutes later. It felt like I had only been out for a few minutes when I heard a hard rapping on the cell door. It yanked me out of a strange and scary dream where I was being chased by something through a forest of heavy chains across a dark space. When I got up, I was cuffed again and led back to the interrogation room.

Shugart came in a minute later, and I asked him for the time. He held up his phone and smiled apologetically. It was 1:17 am. I could feel it starting to happen.

“Don’t touch me,” I said. “I’ll be back in about seventeen minutes.”

He saw the change and lunged over the table at me, but I was gone before he touched me. I emerged under water and quickly kicked my legs to break the surface. The salt stung my eyes and the glare of the bright sun above me elevated it to blindness. When I heard the wails of the bloated, desperately treading damned, I thought I knew where I was. As my vision slowly returned to me, I saw the nearby island of linked arms and bobbing heads and I was sure of it.

I had landed in the Drowning Hell.

I began kicking my legs softly and stroking with my arms to keep my head above water. As I did so, I noticed that the bracelet of the handcuffs was still attached to my wrist. Two silver links trailed behind it as it glided across the surface of the sea. I was on edge, like I could feel what was coming, and I was terrified because I wasn’t able to run. I knew from experience that I couldn’t climb onto the mass of tormented souls in front of me. My hands would just pass right through them like they weren’t there. It was all part of the no touching rule.

So when the phantom came up from below me and grabbed my ankle there was really nothing I could do but take a deep breath as it yanked me under. It was okay, though, I wanted to get in close again. I kicked hard when he tried to shift his grip and dove after him. The claws swiped lazily through the water and I brought my arm up to block them. They snagged the bracelet, barely scratching my palm and I launched myself at the thing’s exposed shoulder.

As I closed my hands on him I felt his silent scream and I focused as hard as I could on the Marble Isle and my memories of Maia. We flipped in an instant.

The two of us splashed down on the stone along with a few thousand gallons of salty brine. I heard Maia scream from nearby as the phantom leapt for me, and I barely managed to get out of the way. I landed awkwardly on my right hand and felt the serrated metal of the slashed cuff bite into my wrist. When he lunged at me again, I yanked off the severed bracelet and drove it into the creature’s body with everything I had. He staggered back away from me and collapsed to the ground. His hands closed tightly over the spot where I had struck.

I raced over to Maia and pulled her into a tight hug. I think I said about a hundred things with that first breath. She was excited too, and her thin arms squeezed me right back. I could feel that we had only seconds remaining and I started to tell her to get ready, but she interrupted me.

“Look at him, Will,” she said, and turned me towards him.

The last thing I saw before we left that hell for a different kind of chaos was the body of an old man. He was breathing heavy and clutching the piece of metal that I had driven into him. I barely saw his face, but it was familiar. I’d seen him before somewhere.

The next thing I knew, the two of us were lying in the floor of the interrogation room. I had done it. I had brought her back. I turned to kiss her, to tell her she was safe now, but the sound of gunfire stopped me. When Shugart came through the door with his pistol held up, I lifted my hands and jerked my head in Maia’s direction.

“She’s alive,” I said. “See, I didn’t do anything to her.”

“Later,” he said, and as he walked over to us, he kept one eye on the door. “I’ve got to get the two of you out of here. A bunch of people in masks have come for you, and they mean business. They’ve killed a lot of good cops.”

“What?”

“Can you walk?” he asked us. “We’ve really got to go.”

He snuck us out of there, and he did it well. I never saw any of the perps, as he calls them. We drove away in his car and back to the motel where Sarah and I were staying. It seemed as good a place as any.

Somewhere in there I told him everything, and while I don’t think he believes all of it, he at least accepts it. Maybe Shugart does have an open mind.

I think Maia is still in shock. She hasn’t said a lot since I got her out, and I can’t leave the room or she’ll try to follow me. That’s no good either, because half of her left leg has been turned to marble, and so is the sole of her right foot.  I pray to god that getting her away from there is enough to reverse the spread.  I guess I’ll find out tomorrow.

I’ll leave it there. Catch you on the next one.
