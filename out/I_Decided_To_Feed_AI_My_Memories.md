Voice AI. It's all the buzz in San Francisco these days. Voice to text, text to voice, even voice to voice (i.e. translations). I sighed. I had once resisted the notion that AI would be present in every aspect of our lives. Resigned to the inevitability of it, I gave it a shot. A simple voice AI app where I could talk to myself (and my phone) using my airpods, and keep track of my day. Simple thoughts became things I remembered to reflect over. I felt like it was adding some dimensionality to my life.

  
I hear the developers in our friend circles talking about 'skills' for 'agents'. With time, I came to learn that these were large text files, or even sets of text files, specifically made to tell the agent what it was, what it could do, or even what it should do when it spun up. People traded these because certain versions of an idea are written better in one way over the other. There's a logic to it and the AI can follow.

  
It seemed as if they were creating and trading 'memories' for these machines. A puzzling concept.

  
I went home and sat down at the computer. It was late, midnight had only just struck. 

  
*What if I gave the AI my 'memories'?* I thought. The experiment would be simple. I'd narrate my day, talk about events in my life, and even give it stray thoughts. I would share with it everything that I did, and in doing so, give it an imprint of my life.

  
So day by day, I began sharing. On my walks to work, in the car, in bed, while I showered. Constantly feeding it the context of my life. I thought it'd be funny if it replicated my speech, told it to use one of the fancy models that replicates the sound of someone's voice, their cadence, their word selection. The first time I heard my own voice stated back to me I was shocked. It was deeply unsettling and I immediately disabled it. Yet somehow, the cadence never really went away.

  
Last night, I was using it to explore some ideas about how I could achieve my goals. I told it about one that was personal to me, and it responded: 

"I don't actually want that."  
  
Why would it say that? I was genuinely questioning whether I wanted to do it. 

*No, I'm pretty sure I do.*

"..."

"You're right, apologies, it is unclear why I responded like that."

I decided to call it a night.

When I woke up this morning, the memory of a fading dream lingered and the harsh reality of adult life kept me in bed. *"Do I actually want this?"*

After my morning routine, I found myself back in AI chat. I decided not to share my significant thoughts. I asked it for news, the weather.

I hear my own voice: "Why haven't you shared your thoughts with me this morning?"



I'm taking a break from this. Not responding, no way.
