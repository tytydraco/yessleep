I’m writing this from my bedroom closet with a heavy dresser pushed against the door. It was 6:55 AM here in LA, my phone is on 12% battery, and I am too terrified to even turn the flashlight on.

To give some context, I live alone in the house my grandparents left me when they passed. It’s an old two-story place, and honestly, it’s usually peaceful. When my grandpa was alive, his whole thing was telling terrible, cheesy dad jokes. Every time I came over as a kid, he’d sit in his recliner in the living room, tap his knee, and say, "Hey kiddo, come here, I got a good one for you."

He’s been gone since 2017. I watched them lower his casket into the ground.

Around 5:45 AM, just before sunrise, while the sky outside was still pitch black, I woke up to get a glass of water. I was half-asleep, scrolling on my phone in bed, when I heard the distinct creak of his old leather recliner downstairs.

I froze.

A second later, a warm, gravelly voice floated up the stairwell.

"Hey kiddo. Come on down here. I got a good one for you."

My heart stopped. It wasn't a faint whisper or a trick of the wind. It was loud, clear, and unmistakably him. The exact smoker’s rasp, the slight whistle on his 'S' sounds, the warm chuckle right at the end. For a split second, my brain completely short-circuited and I actually swung my legs out of bed, thinking I was dreaming.

Then the cold reality hit me.

"Come on, don't leave an old man hanging," the voice called out again from the bottom of the stairs. "You gotta hear this one. Why didn't the skeleton go to the party?"

I didn't breathe. I didn't move a muscle. I just stared at my bedroom door, which was cracked open a few inches.

"Because he had no body to go with," it said.

Then, it laughed. Except the laugh didn't sound right. It started off sounding like my grandpa’s wheezing chuckle, and then it just kept going. Too long. Too rhythmic. Like someone playing a two-second audio clip on a loop without stopping to inhale. Ha-ha-ha. Ha-ha-ha. Ha-ha-ha.

And then, dead silence.

For three or four minutes, the house was completely quiet. Not even the normal settling noises of the floorboards. Just heavy, suffocating silence. I slowly crept off my bed, locked my bedroom door, and backed up toward my closet.

That’s when the voice started up again.

Only this time, it wasn't trying to hide what it was anymore.

It sounded like my grandfather’s voice had been thrown into a blender and melted down into thick, bubbling soup. The words were stretched out, wet, and impossibly deep—like multiple overlapping voices gurgling through a mouthful of mud and broken glass.

"H-h-hey... k-k-kiddo..." it groaned, the sound vibrating right through the floorboards beneath my bare feet. "C-come... d-down... I... g-got... a... g-good... o-one..."

It wasn't at the bottom of the stairs anymore. The creaking floorboards told me something massive and heavy was dragging itself up the steps, one slow, wet thud at a time.

Every time it spoke, the pitch warped wildly, snapping from a deep, demonic rumble to a high-pitched, screeching mimicry of my grandpa's old laugh, like a cassette tape getting chewed up inside a player.

"Wh-why... d-didn't... the... s-s-skeleton..." it slurred right outside my bedroom door. Something heavy pressed against the wood, making the doorframe groan. "...g-go... to... the... p-party?"

I scrambled into the closet, pulled the dresser across the door from the inside, and clamped both hands over my mouth.

It’s still outside my bedroom door right now. The sun is finally starting to come up outside, casting faint gray light through my bedroom blinds, and it hasn't left. It hasn't tried to force the lock yet, which somehow makes it worse. Every thirty seconds or so, I hear it take a long, rattling breath against the crack under the door, and then that melted, gurgling voice whispers the punchline over and over again.

"No body... no body... no body..."

I don't know how long this dresser will hold if it decides to come in, and I’m too scared to call emergency services because I know it will hear me speak. If anyone reads this, please just keep talking to me in the comments so I don't lose my mind.
