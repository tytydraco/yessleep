Who killed the dog?

This has been a discussion for many years in my neighbourhood. The story goes back a long way, beginning when a newlywed couple moved in, bringing with them a Jack Russell that was barely past puppyhood.

I suppose nobody told them the house's history. I mean, who was there to tell them?

They seemed to settle in nicely at first, even attending the local church service every Sunday. They were quiet. There was not much gossip to be said about them, nor did either of them ever do anything to frown upon. Maybe they kept to themselves a bit too much and were seen as slightly antisocial, but in a place like this, that is always preferable to the opposite.

But it didn’t remain that way for long. It never does in that house.

The first incident happened just shy of their fourth month.

The police sirens started wailing at around 10 p.m. Evelyn, the wife, had stepped outside—maybe to throw away some trash or let the dog out to do its business; that part is up for speculation. But when she turned around to go back inside, she saw what had been left on her front door.

Crudely smeared across the wood was a massive 'X' in an unidentifiable dark liquid. It was still fresh, dripping under the porch light. The neighbourhood watched the flashing red and blue lights from behind their curtains and manicured lawns, but not one person went over to offer help. Even the police seemed reluctant to step too close to inspect it, quickly writing it off as teenagers playing a prank. It was October, after all.

This was their first warning. But how could they have known?

The second incident happened just over a week later. It was much worse.

The young couple were out in their garden, planting shallots and garlic. We guess maybe they were planning to harvest and pickle them for the following summer—we can only speculate. They did end up growing, by the way, and they tasted delicious.

It was foggy that day. It was easy for someone to slip in through the garden gate, and even easier to sneak up behind them.

I suppose the couple were frightened when they turned around to see three masked figures standing there, and I suppose they were even more frightened when three buckets of blood were dumped right over their heads as they knelt before their garden bed. Maybe that is why the vegetables ended up tasting as they did.

The police were called yet again. They promised to keep an eye out and left a patrol car down the street all night. The couple was told to just wash away the mess with a garden hose, since it "could only be animal blood." The officers even promised to check in with the local butcher to see if any strange orders had been placed.

It was quite funny, though, how much they both resembled Stephen King's *Carrie* in those moments.

I’m not sure why they stayed after that. Maybe they hoped these were just stupid pranks from stupid kids, with Halloween approaching.

Needless to say, nobody in the neighbourhood asked them about it.

The third incident was two days before Halloween, on October 29th.

Although it's not the finale, this one is always my favourite.

They had both turned from being slightly antisocial to complete recluses after the blood incident, apart from quick trips outside to take the dog out or make silent runs to the local grocery store.

This one had to be more calculated. We couldn't scare them off before the ceremonial day, but we hadn't yet harvested enough energy. They needed to feel fear again.

It happened while they were sleeping. It was quite easy.

Through the basement window, up the basement stairs with the spare key, and right up the landing to the master bedroom.

Two things were needed for the harvest: their spit and their terror. Petrified spit works best. It is much easier to collect it while they sleep, but it’s far less potent. The stakes were high this close to Halloween, and we couldn't risk a weak harvest.

We swapped the inside of their mouths with cotton swabs, moving silently in the dark. Once we were satisfied with the amount, we lightly sprinkled ashes from the last occupants across their faces. They had to breathe it in—that part is crucial. Then, the chant had to be whispered directly into their ears. It’s hard to project properly while wearing the bear mask, but it's necessary.

And then came the fear.

We left the house first—the risk of them waking up inside was too high—but we made sure to leave their bedroom window cracked open.

Once we were back out in the garden, we began the death chant. It was beautiful. The bear-wearers throwing their deep, unearthly voices into the fog; the death whistles piercing from every single direction in the neighbourhood; the lighting of the past occupants' clothing on fire.

They both rushed to the window and looked out at the same time. I still savour the look on their faces.

The police were called, of course. They swept the area, and a patrol car was left overnight once more. They apologised profusely. They told the couple they were so very sorry, whispered promises that hopefully this would all end after Halloween, and assured them that they were doing everything they could. They reminded them that this is usually a safe neighbourhood.

October 31st.

All was set to plan. We noticed the couple didn’t decorate for Halloween this year—spoilsports.

Nobody had seen them since the chanting. No surprise there. But we knew they were still inside. No more police had been called, and no taxis had arrived. Which was good, because the whole thing becomes terribly rushed once they try to run.

We waited until nightfall. No doubt they were still awake. The basement was fully set up, our symbol painted onto the floor once more: three interlaced triangles inside a square. Eight candles.

11 p.m. It was as good a time as ever.

Five of us crept up the basement stairs, our faces and bodies unidentifiable, hidden beneath heavy cloaks and masks. The basement door was already slightly ajar.

Inside the house, it was pitch black. And entirely silent.

The house was empty, upstairs and down. Every single piece of furniture was flipped on its head. Food was scattered across the floor and smeared all up the walls. The carpet had been violently ripped from its staples.

The house was empty except for one thing.

Laid to rest upstairs, only the dog remained. It was tucked neatly into the bed, almost as if it were asleep.

On the bedside tables sat two severed fingers, completely drained of blood, still wearing their gold wedding rings.

We never found out what got to them before us. To complete the harvest, we had to sacrifice one of our own. Crops grew well that year, nonetheless. And to keep ourselves safe, it was in our best interest.

I've been told since I was a child that we are keeping something away with these rituals. Maybe this time it just couldn't wait. Since then, I sometimes think I hear people screaming from the woods. But I never speak about it. Nobody does.

But all these years later, the question still stands. To kill the dog too? Not even we could be so heartless.

Whatever got there before us, I hope I never have to witness.
