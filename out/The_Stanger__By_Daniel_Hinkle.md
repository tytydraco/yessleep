# Prologue: The Night Emily Ran

The wind clawed at Emily’s jacket as she sprinted down the empty street, her breath sharp and uneven. Her mother’s slap still burned on her cheek, but the words hurt worse.

>

She didn’t look back.  
She just ran.

The town was silent too silent as if the night itself was listening.

That’s when she heard it.

A soft, trembling voice.

>

Emily turned.

An old woman stood beneath a flickering streetlamp, her silhouette fragile, her smile warm. Her eyes, however, didn’t match the smile they were too bright, too focused.

She extended a gloved hand.  
  
“Come. You shouldn’t be out here. I’ll take care of you.”

>Emily hesitated… then nodded.

# 🌘 Chapter 1: The House That Watches

The woman’s home sat crooked at the end of a narrow lane, its windows dark and reflective like eyes.

Inside, the air smelled of cinnamon and old wood.  
Warm. Safe.  
Almost too safe.



Emily noticed the pictures first.

Dozens of framed photos lined the walls children of all ages, smiling beside the old woman. Some photos were faded. Some were crisp and new.

But none of the children were here.

>

The woman stirred a pot of stew, humming a tune that sounded like a lullaby played backward.

>

Her voice wavered on the last word.

Emily felt a chill crawl up her spine.

# 🌒 Chapter 2: The Room of Whispers

The woman led Emily to a bedroom decorated with soft blankets and plush toys. It looked perfect  too perfect, like a room staged for a photograph.

The bed was enormous, covered in quilts.

Emily sat down.

The mattress shifted beneath her weight… uneven… lumpy… almost alive.  
  
“It’s uncomfortable,” Emily whispered.

The woman’s smile twitched.

>

That night, Emily lay awake, staring at the ceiling.  
The house creaked.  
Something brushed against the underside of the mattress.

And then

A whisper.

So faint she wasn’t sure she heard it.

>

# 🌑 Chapter 3: The Midnight Ritual

Emily jolted awake.

The old woman stood beside her bed, silhouetted by the hallway light.  
In her hand: a long, thin knife.

Her voice was soft.

>

Emily’s heart hammered.

>

The woman’s smile stretched too wide.

>

She left the room, humming that strange backward lullaby.

Emily didn’t sleep again.

# 🌘 Chapter 4: The Frame With Her Name

Morning brought a brittle sunlight.

The woman announced she was going shopping.

>

The moment the door shut, Emily moved.

She searched the hallway again the photos, the frames, the smiling children.

That’s when she saw it.

An empty frame.

Her name engraved at the bottom.

**Emily Rose Carter.**

Her full name.

Her breath caught.

She ran to the bedroom, grabbed scissors, and sliced open the mattress.

Bones spilled out.

Small.  
Brittle.  
Hundreds of them.

And beneath the bones, carved into the wooden frame of the bed:

>

# 🌒 Chapter 5: The Children’s Revenge

The front door creaked open.

>

Footsteps approached.

The old woman appeared in the doorway, her smile gone, her eyes hollow.

>

She raised the knife.

Emily backed into the mirror behind her.

The woman lunged

But froze.

Her reflection didn’t match her movements.

In the mirror, dozens of ghostly children stood behind her, their faces pale, their eyes glowing with sorrow.

They reached out.

Hands pressed against the glass.

The woman screamed as the children’s reflections wrapped around her, pulling her backward into the mirror’s surface.

Her eyes burst into flame.

Her body twisted, contorted, and vanished into the darkness inside the mirror.

The glass cracked but didn’t break.

A whisper filled the room.

>

# 🌕 Epilogue: Home Again

Emily ran.

She didn’t stop until she reached her house.  
Her mother burst into tears, hugging her tightly.

>

Emily cried too not from fear, but from relief.

She never ran away again.

But sometimes, late at night, she swears she hears a faint lullaby…

Coming from the mirror.

**The End**
