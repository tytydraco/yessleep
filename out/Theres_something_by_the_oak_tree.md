It had been a strange week.

December had been unseasonably calm, the usual biting winds and relentless rain nowhere to be found. Stranger still, the overgrown path leading into the grove beside our school had cleared, despite having been left to the elements since summer.

I asked the other teachers about it, but none of them had an explanation.

Still, it meant my class could return to the woods.

Every class had its own extracurricular activity. Some played sports, others drew or read. My class preferred the woods.

Whenever the weather allowed, we would venture down to the grove, photographing the local wildlife and adding our findings to a shared binder.

At least, that was what we had been doing until Wednesday.

Flicking through our binder, I came across a strange photo. It must have been a critter of some kind, but it was obscured by the trunk of a particularly large oat tree.

Discarding the photo, I thought little more of it. However, the following day, as I collected every student's pictures, I found another one. Almost exactly the same angle, capturing the same tree and the same dark shape.

I questioned the class. Part of me expected someone to own up to their harmless prank, but a pair of boys spoke up almost in unison.

“That’s the monster.”

A little taken aback, I still assumed it was a prank. I almost expected the usual eyeroll and an insistence that they were lying again from a particular child.

Strangely, she had a vacant look on her face. Two other children sitting by our classroom window wore the same expression. They were gazing out towards the woods, seemingly oblivious to our conversation, at least until another pupil chimed in.

“Yeah, I’ve seen it too. It was really tall.”

“No it wasn’t, it was crouching behind a tree. And it had pointy ears.”

Before long, the class had devolved into a frenzy of descriptions, fuelling the paranoia of those who had yet to witness the so-called monster.

One throughline held on. They all saw it behind the old oak, and it had never moved.

Obviously, I didn’t believe them, but stupidly I humoured their point. An oversight.

The moment I acted as though I believed them, panic spread like wildfire. If their rational teacher corroborated their experiences, even in jest, then the sightings must have been real.

I tried to calm the situation, but I'd already tipped the scales. Most couldn't bring themselves to look at the photographs. Some were afraid of what they might see; others couldn't bear to look at it again.

Hoping they'd change their tune, I encouraged them to follow me down to the grove. I was certain that seeing the woods in daylight would put their minds at ease.

A small group who hadn't been affected by the stories reluctantly followed, almost luring the fearful majority along with them.

The grove seemed almost too inviting. Birds chirped, unseen critters scampered through the leaf litter, and the day was far too bright.

Wanting to put their minds at ease, I refrained from inviting them to venture any further.  Simply being there was enough.

Then someone spotted something.

A shifting shadow, or maybe a shape by the oak.

Whatever it was had them turning tail and running back to our classroom. Once one fled, soon they all saw the vague outline.

I had to try and quell the rumour, at least from the source.

So, I went in.

Locking the kids in my classroom, I ventured back down, keeping an eye on them as I descended. Their eager little faces, plaster to the glass as they begged for my safe return from the depths of their monster’s lair.

Maybe their fears had sparked a little of my own.

Not enough to break the smiling mask I shot back at them, but enough to light the match. I'd seen the photographs, and I couldn't make anything out, but the children were adamant that they depicted something.

That thought ran through my mind as I closed the gate behind me and strode in alone. Again, the day was beautiful, streams of light piercing the canopy as a selection of birds chirped a symphony above.

Walking deeper, I glanced at my surroundings. Even with the wood being so small, the isolation was real. From my position you couldn’t see a single man-made item, let alone hear one.

Then I felt it. The same sensation the children had described. The back of my neck prickled, and I became acutely aware of the space behind me.

I swivelled in place, taking in every ich of the pine, birch and ash trees, until my eyes landed on a particularly large oak tree.

Just the sight of it had me turning away, averting my eyes as my mind conjured up a shape, peaking from behind its aged marron trunk.

As I blinked at the floor, questions whirled in my mind. Had I actually seen something?

Then the photographs and the binder came together in my mind. There was something I'd been too focused on to notice before.

The woods were alive, echoing back the sounds of a thriving ecosystem, but on my walk, I’d not seen a thing.

No rustling in the canopy, no fluttering wing nor scampering fuzzy shape. I hadn't been alone. I was sure of that now.

The woods seemed to shut off, the light dimmed, a breeze picked up, and every sound dissipated as if someone had flicked a switch.

Then the leaflitter crunched.

I knew immediately which direction it had come from, but I forced my eyes down to the floor.

Another crunch.

Every instinct screamed at me to look. To give a shape to the thing my pupils had witnessed.

Another crunch.

It couldn't have been more than a metre away. A shadow crept into the edge of my vision, but I kept my eyes on the ground.

I had a choice, listen to my primal fight or flight instinct and look, or…

I took a step forward, charting the path I’d followed into those woods. Then another, and another. Each one as painful as the last, as I forced myself to return to the safety I’d left them in.

With every step, it followed. Matching my stride almost perfectly, but with an audible delay. I swear I could hear it breath or at least let out the sounds any animate creature would as it moved.

Occasionally I’d stop, listening out for its footfall a second behind my own. When I stopped it did too. I even caught myself stumbling, kicking a root as I trained every sense I had on my pursuer. So, it did the same.

Regardless, I pressed on, keeping a slow relaxed pace. Whether it knew I could perceive it or not, I could feel it burning a hole in the back of my head.

A light in that dark tunnel, the blue paint of the gate came into view.

Almost home free, I tried to keep from breaking into a sprint, but my next couple of steps were too hasty.

I knew for sure in that moment that it understood my motive.          

The wind had grown wild, yet somehow, through the noise, I heard an echo.

Words that hadn’t left my mouth, but could have been my own. I don’t know if it was in my head or spoken aloud, but those words mocked my bravado.

“Nearly there.”

The words cut deep. It was using the same reassurance I'd offered my class, turning it against me in one final attempt to break my resolve.

Somehow, I manged to hold firm. Perhaps it was the thought of their beaming faces, just glad I’d returned, I don’t know.

Somehow, I closed the gate with no physical scares and strode up the playground path. Finally safe, embraced by the once worried faces of my pupils, I couldn’t stop myself from turning to glace, even for a millisecond, back onto the grove.

There was something by the oak.

Again, I placed that smiling mask on as I informed my class of what I seen.

Nothing.

They took my word for it. I'd taught them better than to accept things blindly, but I couldn't bring myself to tell them the truth.

Fortunately, my reassurance seemed to settle their nerves, at least momentarily.

From then on, I refrained from taking them close to the gate, focusing on the insects and birds within our schools’ grounds.

Unfortunately, there would be times when a kid or two would stray too far, though I was always on hand to shut down the images they thought they’d seen.

“It’s just a shadow, believe me, there’s noting there.”

I know differently.

 

Their fears aren’t unfounded.

 

But I will hold my nerve.

 

For their sakes.

 

Because I know.

 

There is something by the oak tree.
