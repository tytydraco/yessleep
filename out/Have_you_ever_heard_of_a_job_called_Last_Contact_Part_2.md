It’s been several months since my original post regarding my strange job.

A lot has happened.

I am no longer a trainee at Last Contact; it’s been a month or so since I finished the training. Nate, my trainer, has remained on the night shift ever since management determined that the night shift needed more staff due to a higher call volume.

Thanks to this job, I have saved enough money to move out of my parents' home and into my own apartment across town. It’s nice being on my own; having my space and privacy has been my dream for a while, and thanks to Last Contact, it's now a reality. I had hoped that the noises coming from the closet in my parents' house would stop after I moved out. But it hasn’t, the scratching followed me to the closet in my new apartment. I remember the first night I heard the scratching in the closet. I cautiously opened the door, hoping it was just a trapped mouse. But when I opened the door, the scratching stopped, and the closet was empty. Seeing nothing unusual, I shut the closet and climbed back into bed. But the scratching returned, only this time it was coming from the top of the door.

Every morning since then, loud scratching came from the closet; it would start at the floor and move its way up to the top of the doorframe. I was disappointed to discover the scratching had moved with me. One night, I mentioned all this to Nate, and he simply shrugged and said

“Sounds like you got an attachment”

I looked at him, confused

“An attachment?”

“Yeah, it happens every now and then.”

“Well, how do I get rid of it?”

He sipped his coffee and replied

“Not sure, sorry.”

The only solution I have found is noise-cancelling headphones.

Another big change is that we now have official uniforms! One night at the start of the shift, Nate and I entered the building and were greeted by a large, worn cardboard box sitting on the floor. On top of it was a short note that read:

“These uniforms are now mandatory; do not allow anyone in the building who isn’t wearing the uniform, even if you think you know them. Signed Management”

We opened the box and pulled out dark blue polos with light purple sleeves. On the left breast pocket was an embroidered telephone receiver and the words ‘Last Contact’. Nate rolled his eyes and sarcastically said

“Great, now we have to dress like a bowling team.”

The last big change since my last post is that we have a new coworker, or rather, we’ve had a few new coworkers. The first was a tall, thin man named James. One night, James answered a call that changed him. He went pale and stared unblinkingly. Nate asked him what happened, and all he would say was

“I know her.”

I was sitting in the break room, reading, while James sat across the room, staring out the wall of windows at the deep darkness beyond the train tracks. I just happened to glance up from my book and noticed James wasn’t at the table anymore, but was now standing mere inches from the window, staring unblinkingly. A moment later, he loudly said

“Okay, I’m coming”

Then he turned, and before I knew it he exited by the side door, and wildly ran across the trains and disappeared into the darkness. He was never seen again.  

The next trainee we got was a short man named Charles. Now, Charles had the bright idea to take the banking information he gathered on calls and use them. According to the police report, he had gone to several banks with the account information of several recently deceased people. The police still don’t know exactly what happened to him; all that can really be said is someone, or something, didn’t appreciate what he was doing. He disappeared from a bank vault after trying to access a safety deposit box that wasn’t his. All that remained in the vault was his severed arm. The skin of the arm had been completely removed, leaving a bloody mess.

After Charles, management became much more careful about who they hired. And after some time, they brought on Marie, she’s still here, next week she’ll finish training. She’s college age, pretty, and a bit shy. She takes this job seriously and follows the rules. She’s become a good friend and, like Nate, is a bright spot during my nightly shifts.

I must admit that since my last post, I had settled into the routine of the job, and in some ways, I had become pretty desensitized to the macabre nature of my work. But things are changing, and after the week I’ve had, I worry about what the future holds.

Monday night began like any other. I pulled into the parking lot, the train had just passed, and the echo of its whistle lingered in the empty night. Both Nate and Marie were already there. Marie sat in her car while Nate leaned against the building smoking a cigarette. We all entered together as we always did. It had rained a lot over the weekend, and two buckets full of dirty rainwater cluttered the floor.

“Did you have a good weekend?” I asked Marie as we walked the long hallway to our stations

She gave a quick shrug

“It was ok, finals are coming, and I’m a bit behind,” she said with a tired voice

Unlike me, Marie was doing something with her life. She was going to be a doctor one day, while I would be stuck here or at some other dead-end job forever.

I smiled and said

“Well, I know you’re busy, but maybe if you get a free moment later this week, we could grab a coffee?”

She stared at me for what felt like forever. My stomach dropped. Then she smiled.

“I’d love to,” she declared, “would Wednesday afternoon work for you?”

I grinned like a madman,

“Yeah! That would be perfect!”

Her eyes sparkled, and I could tell she was blushing

“Let me give you my phone number so you can call me.”

Now I was blushing

“That sounds good” was all I could say

She wrote her number on a piece of paper and handed it to me. Our eyes met as she did, and I could feel my heart beating faster

“Hey, guys?” Nate’s voice broke my trance. I hadn’t realized he had been watching us from the doorway of his station.

“let’s get to work,” his monotone voice declared, “we've got a long night ahead of us.”

I nodded awkwardly, and Marie quickly pushed some hair out of her face before hurrying by me down the hall to Nate’s station. Before she entered, she looked back and smiled at me. I opened the door to the closet-sized workstation and collapsed into the creaky office chair before releasing the breath I had been holding. My hands shook, and my heart beat fast. I had wanted to ask Marie out for weeks now, and I had told myself to let the moment arise naturally, but I never thought I wouldn’t recognize the moment when it came, and now that it was gone, it felt like a massive weight had been removed from my shoulders. And like a child waiting for Christmas, I finally had something to look forward to, and Wednesday couldn’t come soon enough.

About twenty minutes later, I received my first call of the night.

“Hello, my name is Chris, what’s yours?” I made the decision a while back that it was best to keep using a fake name.

Static poured out of the phone for a moment before a weak voice answered

“I’m Anna. What’s going on? It’s so dark.”

“Hello Anna, this is your last contact. I’m sorry to have to tell you, but you have died.”

The phone went silent

“No. No, that can’t be right, I was just on a date, and we had gone back to his place.”

I shuddered as my mind filled in the gaps of what happened.

“I’m sorry, Anna, but I’m afraid it’s true, we don’t have much time, but do you have any banking information or passwords that need to be passed onto you’re family?”

She shared her bank information and a final ‘I love you’ to her family, though they would never hear it. At exactly 60 seconds, the phone clicked, and the line went dead. I entered the information Anna had given me into the computer and tried my best to forget what I had just heard. Over the next couple of hours, I got only two more calls, one from a suicidal teen and the other from a biker hit by a drunk driver. It had been pretty quiet for a while, and I was about to head to the break room to stretch my legs, when the phone rang again.

“Hello my name is Chris, what’s yours?”

I heard a low chuckle before the phone said

“Hey Chris, it’s John, when are you going to tell me your real name?”

I rolled my eyes and said

“John, I don’t know how you’re doing this, but you've got to stop.”

A loud laugh broke out

“I don’t have to do anything, ‘Chris’ I’ll keep calling until I find out who you are and where you are.”

“Goodbye, John.” I hung up the phone.

From everything I could find, John had died 102 years ago, hit by the train that still goes by. For whatever reason he keeps calling. He shouldn’t be able to keep calling us; management is quite confused about how it's even happening. But no one wants to think about what will happen if he learns where and who we are.

I sighed as I stood and walked to the break room. Both Nate and Marie were already there. Marie sat at a table, working on a cup of coffee. And Nate was across the room, reading the newspaper. I plopped down next to Marie, who greeted me with a warm smile. Her eyes still sparkled when she looked at me.

“How’s the shift been?” she asked

“Not bad, only four calls, and the last was John.”

She rolled her eyes

“Yeah, he was my first call, doesn’t quit, huh?”

“I guess not, but at least we can just hang up.”

She nodded.

After several minutes of small talk, Nate walked over and joined us at the table.

“Good shift so far?” he said as he looked at me

“Yeah,” I replied, “same old same old.”

He nodded in approval.

The three of us sat there chatting for over an hour. Typically, the time between midnight and two is pretty quiet; around three, things pick up. We talked about the weather, and movies we liked, Marie told us some of what she was learning in school, while Nate told us an embarrassing story from ten years ago. As we stood to return to our stations, I looked at my two coworkers and realized I finally had something I hadn’t had since middle school: friends.

Returning to my closet, I sat down again in my chair and worked on that week’s book. Since working this job, I have been averaging a book a week. About 30 minutes later, the phone rang. I picked it up and began my normal spiel

“Hello, my name is..”

The caller cut me off and only said

“Thank you Will.” The line went dead as the caller hung up

A chill went up my spine, this was a new one for me, but I remembered the rule:

*If the caller thanks you before you have helped them, end your shift immediately and go home by a different route than usual.*

I quickly rose to my feet, and briskly walked down the hall to Nate’s room, both Nate and Marie looked up at me as I entered

“They only said ‘thank you’ and hung up” I declared

Nate’s gaze grew darker, and his jaw clenched before he said

“Ok, we follow the rule, pack it up and head home, take a different route, and if I were you two, I would drive around for awhile just to be safe.”

Marie stood from the desk and gathered her things, as we switched off the light and headed for the door, Nate said

“don’t worry the company will pay you for a full shift.”

We exited the building, and as we reached the parking lot from all around us we heard a dull monotone whistle. It almost sounded like the Disney ‘It’s a Small World’ but the tone and pitch were way off. We couldn’t see where it was coming from. Nate went pale when he heard it and through gritted teeth he whispered

“Leave. Now.”

We didn’t linger.

I spent the next hour making random turns until I wasn't even sure where I was anymore, when I felt comfortable, I returned to my apartment. That morning after my shift was the first and only time in months that there was no scratching on my closet door.

I didn’t sleep well that day. The lack of scratching on the closet unnerved me, and when I saw Marie, I could tell she didn’t sleep much either.

“Rough night?” I asked, though I already knew the answer

She nodded, “Yeah,” she whispered, almost as if she didn’t mean for the words to escape her lips

“Every time I was about to drift off, I jolted awake; it felt like someone was watching me from the corner of the room.”

I didn’t know what to say, so I said

“Yeah, me too.”

“What happened last night, Will? What was whistling in the parking lot?”

“I don’t know,” I replied, “But I have a feeling that it's worse than anything else we watch out for.”

She swallowed hard as she stared at the ground before nodding to herself

“Where’s Nate?”

Nate was late. Since I’ve worked here, Nate’s never been late; in fact, I’ve never arrived earlier than Nate. But that night he was late. For ten minutes, Marie and I stood outside the worn door. We could go in; I had a key, but we never did without Nate.

In the distance, we saw headlights moving down the road towards the parking lot. As the vehicle grew closer, it was clear that the driver wasn’t sober; they swerved all over the road, slowed down, and sped up erratically. The car pulled into the parking lot and took up two parking spaces. It was Nate’s car.

Nate stepped out of the car, barely able to keep on his feet, and stumbled his way over to us. He was drunk, and it was clear he had been crying. When he reached us, he stared at us for a moment before releasing a heavy sigh and mumbling

“Let’s go to work.”

With that, he grabbed the door handle and awkwardly pulled it open. Marie and I quickly exchanged glances. We had never seen Nate like this. We followed him as he stumbled his way down the hallway. He made it to his office, and even before we entered the room, we could hear him sobbing.

Nate was a miserable mess of tears. I stood just inside the doorway, while the kindhearted Marie knelt by the crying man's side. We watched in silence as he shed his tears after his eyes ran dry. Marie meekly asked

“Are you ok, Nate?”

He clenched his jaw, and slowly answered

“Not really, kiddo.”

“Please tell us what’s wrong. We want to help.”

Nate stared at the floor. He seemed embarrassed. He was silent for a long while before cautiously speaking.

“5 years ago, my wife and three-year-old daughter were in an accident. The driver was drunk and t-boned my family.” His lips quivered as if he was going to cry again, but he continued

“I was just about to finish up my second contract here, and I didn’t plan to do any more. I was working second shift then and had just gotten off when I got the word that my daughter had been killed, and my wife was in critical condition in the hospital.”

I ran my hand through my hair, and Marie put a hand to her mouth. Nate continued

“The doctors said there was nothing they could do; they wanted to pull the plug. I knew in my heart it was the right thing to do.”

He swallowed back tears.

“In the hospital I was visited by one of the upper management. I don’t know his name, he told me how sorry he was for my loss. And he told me that I was a valued employee. In a whispered tone he told me that the company was now offering life extensions for family of employees who are on their third contract.”

He paused to regain composure

“He offered me a fresh contract with the added promise of one year of extended life for my wife for every year I worked. I had just lost my daughter, and I couldn’t bear the idea of losing my wife, so I signed it.”

“And to the doctor's amazement, my wife seemed to recover. Before long they were sending her home, but something was wrong. It was like she was a corpse trapped in a living body. Though her heart beats and her lungs fill with air, she reeks of death, and it didn’t take long for her body to decay. Now she’s nothing more than a hollow husk, long expired but unable to die. Her eyes beg me to let her go, but I can’t lose her.”

I stared at the floor in shock. Marie looked like she was going to start crying herself. Despite summoning all my courage, all I could manage was a low question

“Nate, what was that thing humming in the parking lot last night?”

A moment of soberness could be seen in Nate’s eyes as he answered

“I don’t know, but the last time I heard that song, it was being sung by my daughter.” With that, he started crying again.

Marie stood up and whispered to me that she was going to find some tissues before passing me into the hallway.

A moment later, the phone on the desk in front of Nate rang. He stared at it before reaching his hand towards it

“Nate, I don’t think..” but it was too late, he picked up the phone and, through tears, said

“Hello?”

The voice on the other end was loud enough for me to hear it.

“Hello, I’m John. What’s your name?”

To this day, I don’t know if it was because Nate was drunk or simply because he wanted to be done, but he replied blankly

“My name’s Nate.”

It was as if time had stopped; every single light in the building instantly turned off, and the place was completely quiet. All background noise was gone, the a/c, the hum of the lights and computers, even the crickets outside. We were in perfect darkness and perfect silence.

“I’m sorry,” Nate said

Suddenly, outside roared with the sound of the train. Though looking out the window revealed no sign that the train was passing. After the sound of the train passed, it was replaced by a quiet tapping on the window.

I whispered as softly as I could

“Nate, we need to get out of here”

The tapping grew louder, and a voice followed. It was low, yet energetic. It sounded like it’s source didn’t understand tone and inflection

“Nate? I found you! Nate? Can you come out? Please? Come out.”

Nate stood to his feet, but stared at the silhouette hiding behind the blinds.

“Nate? Are you in there? Please come out? I’ll come in if you don’t.”

Nate looked at me and said

“I’m sorry, Will.”

The voice replied

“Who’s Will?”

Nate pushed me into the hallway and slammed the door. I heard the lock click, locking Nate in the tiny room. The next noise I heard was the sound of the window opening

“Thank you, Nate.”

The sounds I heard made vomit rise in my throat. It sounded like someone trying to sip chili through a straw, with the sharp snapping of bones mixed in. The door handle rattled a bit as if a toddler was trying to figure out how it worked. I wasted no time making my escape. My first thought was to make a run for my car, but then a wave of dread overcame me. Marie. She was still here; I had to find her. I ran to the break room, which was just as dark as the hallway. I whispered as loudly as I could

“Marie? Are you here?”

A moment later, a gentle voice answered

“Will? I’m over here.”

I followed the voice and found Marie hiding under a table.

“Are you ok?” I knelt down beside her

She wasn’t looking at me, but rather past me to the wall of windows

“There’s someone out there.”

Slowly, I turned my head to see the dark outline of a figure, it looked to be the shape of a man, roughly six feet tall, only its arms were far too long. They nearly reached the ground. The figure tilted its head to the side and slowly raised one arm and with a single finger tapped on the glass

“Will? Is there a Will in there? Will please out, please?” It was the same voice that spoke to Nate.

I tried to get Marie to follow me by pulling on her arm, but she wouldn’t budge from beneath the table, in a barely audible whisper, I said to her

“Marie, I need you to move.”

Instantly, the figure responded

“Marie? Who’s Marie?”

She gasped and I pulled her to her feet

“Come on!” I said as I dragged her by the hand into the hallway. I ushered her into the janitor's closet as we heard the loud smash of the window shattering.

We huddled together and held our breath as we heard loud, unhurried footsteps work their way down the hall. A low, gentle tap resounded off the door.

“Hello Will?”

“Hello Marie?”

“Are you in there?”

The tapping grew faster and louder, before long, it was pounding, but the voice never changed.

“May I come in?”

“Will? Please come out”

“Marie? Please come out”

For hours, it repeated the same lines, all while tapping nonstop. As dawn drew near the thing grew more desperate and began to repeat

“I know your name.”

“I know your name”

“I know your name”

In the last hour of the night, it became angry, and we heard it leave the door and begin smashing and destroying things throughout the building. At 6:59, it went silent. It was silent for half an hour. Then at 7:30, we heard the main door open and heard human voices react in surprise to the damage around them, and at 7:35, I cautiously opened the closet while Marie gripped my arm, and we met the day shift in the midst of a completely ransacked building.

I don’t know if or when I’ll write again. Marie and I have been transferred. A man from upper management came to take our story. He told us the company valued us as employees, but we couldn’t keep working at this branch. John knew our names and knew where to find us. He told us that a Last Contact branch in a big city across the country needed some experienced night shift workers, and he had prepared them for our arrival. I was to be a supervisor there, and Marie would receive a bonus after completing her training. He informed us everything was in order and movers would be sent to our homes within a day. Marie looked at him and asked

“Do we have a choice?”

He smiled and replied

“Not really.”
