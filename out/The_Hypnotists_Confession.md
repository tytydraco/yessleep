For most of my life, I have had a good opinion about my moral character: I decided to be a psychologist because I saw the world getting sicker and sicker, and felt like I should dedicate my life in order to cure those sicknesses that live and contaminate the human soul. The last few years have made me realize, though, how deeply selfish of a creature I actually am, and if Hell exists, I am certain there is a special place reserved for me there.

Before I start my story, I have a few disclaimers to make: this confession is not a plea of atonement, I recognize that what I did (and still do) is wrong, I have just decided that my guilt over my actions is far superseded by the love I feel towards the only person who can actually make this life look meaningful to me.

When I was really young, I met my first wife when her family moved away to the city where I lived with my parents. We studied in the same school, and for most of our lives, we were nothing more than friends, though I have always felt something for her. Those feelings of mine were only reciprocated when we got to college together, and we both decided to study psychology.

Something happened in those moments where she would ask me for help studying for the exams, and like a moonflower, her love for me bloomed in the sleepless nights we shared at my dorm. 

By the end of college, we decided to get married, and I was the happiest man alive, feeling like no one else could ever be as lucky as I was for holding her hand and opening the door to share my entire life with her. This was taken from me without ceremony, and without a chance to say goodbye.

After two years of living together, we both had stable jobs working in the field, but whilst I worked as a family therapist, she worked in rehabilitation of criminals. I never liked how dangerous her job was, but she was a brave (although stubborn) woman, and I would not have married her if she was any other way. 

It was a Tuesday, and we ate breakfast together, as usual. I usually would go to work hours before the sessions started so I could get my office in order, but my wife asked me to stay for a little bit. I said I had to go, and I wish to this day that I had made an exception for her, just once.

She never came back home. One of the officers committed mistakes whilst retaining a violent prisoner, and she attacked my wife for something that was said during the session. They tried to help my wife, but nothing could be done: she was stabbed with a sharpened toothbrush in the stomach multiple times. I hated the prisoner like I have never hated someone in my life, thinking of her as the scourge of Earth for killing someone who was only trying to help her. In retrospect, it would be hypocritical of me to still consider her that.

After my wife's death, I spent years living in what felt like a mental purgatory, as if the world lost its colours. My interest for anything other than my grief completely vanished from my inner life, and bitter nostalgia became the only thing that would make me leave the bedroom in the morning, in a strange way, I became addicted to the pain, as if I recognize that it was the only feeling my wife left me when she parted this world, and I had to cherish it, otherwise I would forget her, and this was worse than death.

The pain never left, but after some time, I learned to still function, and from the outside, people would think I had gotten over her loss. Truth is, I was living like an automaton, letting the people around me guide the difficult decisions, whilst I simply survived by engaging in the habits that did not take me much though. 

Due to my friend’s insistence, I even started dating, but never once I found a woman that could substitute her, I found something else, though, I found my second wife.

She was also married in the past, but she divorced her ex-husband after he cheated on her with one of his students. She was a teacher, and had a young son from her previous relationship. She was very different from my first wife, but maybe that was what made me like her: my mind could open a new space for a new person, instead of comparing her to a person she did not even resemble personality wise.

We got married after seven years of dating, and I took her son and raised him as if he was mine. The grief for my first wife stayed there, but my second wife showed me happiness I forgot whilst in my state of depression years before. I did not love her as much as I loved my first wife, but I still felt very strongly for her, and I was (still am) thankful for the times she gave me. Which is why the decision I took was not an easy one.

One day, my stepson had a sleepover party with his friends at our house, the house where I once lived with my first wife. He and his friends did a lot of the stupid challenges that teenagers like him usually do, and one of those challenges was using an Ouija board in order to communicate with a spirit. They did, and when the spirit talking through the board presented themselves by my first wife's name. My stepson asked some questions to the spirit, but after a while, he felt sick, like one of his friends was controlling the board and making fun of my first wife’s death, and he decided to cut contact with them.

He told what happened, and me and my wife supported his decision, but part of me, the childlike part that is devoid of logic and lives of pure emotion, thought: what if? What if they actually talked to my dead wife? 

I tried to dismiss this notion, but once an idea is planted, it is not easy to take it back, and after some days of being slowly possessed by that idea, I surrendered, and decided to run the experiment.

I waited until I was alone at home, and I attempted to contact my wife with the Ouija board. She answered, and I felt as if I was the same young boy meeting her for the first time at class. We shared the entire night together, and it was in that moment that I realized that I had forgotten what true joy felt.

Still, there was the barrier of two worlds separating us, and this made the conversation bittersweet for both of us. I could not feel her touch, and she could not hear my heart beating faster whilst her head was pressed against my chest.

Still, we continued talking for months, and I will admit, even though I did not do anything physical, I was cheating on my second wife, emotionally speaking. I became cold towards her and my stepson as the ghost of my late wife took their place, or in better terms, regained the place that was once hers anyway.

The separation became unbearable for the both of us, and as I grew distant emotionally towards my wife and stepson, fighting became commonplace in our household, and they wept for the loss of the stepfather and husband that I once was. I did not care, all that I could think of was her, I had part of her back, but the separation still hurted us both.

This would come to an end when, on a starry night just like the ones where we used to study together at college, we devised a plan to eliminate that separation.

As a psychologist specialized in hypnotherapy, I have learned the same lesson over and over again: the human mind is extremely suggestible. Most people pretend to actually understand what that understanding actually reveals about us, but the truth is that just a few actually comprehend and deal with the fact that what constitutes a person is something very fragile, and a feel stimuli placed on strategic points is enough to make someone break, so you can rebuild however you want.

Spirits can possess living things, but only when the thing in question has a weaker will, or is currently in a state where her will is temporarily dormant (like deep sleep). I inverted every single teaching my psychology teacher gave me, and descended from surgeon into butcher.

It started with small commentaries: I started comparing my second wife to her friends, making her feel insecure about our relationship, but never stating anything out loud. Then, I started randomly deciding to be cold or warmer towards her, feeding the insecurity that I made take root. I started lying about things that happened in the past, and made her doubt her own mind. I started doing hypnotic sessions on her whilst she sleeps, making her more and more susceptible to control as time went by, and after a couple of months, I succeeded: my second wife was constantly afraid of going against my will, and she slowly became a shell of the person she was, a shell in which a new spirit could fit.

One night, after we both felt like it was time, my late wife took the body of my second wife. It fitted her like a glove. Sometimes my second wife still manifests in short periods during the day, but the night is always the moment in which she is the weakest, and when I can share the stars with her without fearing interference. 

My stepson left our house when he saw the way I was treating his mother, and I do feel bad for the boy: I was, at the end of the day, his father figure, and to see someone you trusted slowly becoming a monster… That does things to you, and if I could get what I wanted without harming others, I swear I would, but we do not live in a perfect world after all.

I should get going, my wife is calling my name, she wants to see the star with me, like we did when we procrastinated in my college dorm.
