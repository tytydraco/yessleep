So, I'm a VTuber who has been trying to play old survival horror games for the past few months, and I've started off with the usuals of the classic RE games, *Rule of Rose*, *Siren, Fatal Frame, Haunting Ground*, etc. Well, a chatter told me about this game called *Little Fairy*, and since my model is of a fairy, I thought it was funny enough to learn more about it. Apparently, it was the final part of the "dog-and-girl in spooky place" game in the holy trinity, as that chatter so kindly put it.

Well, I can't find any record of it anywhere.

Game stores don't know what I'm talking about, Google is useless, and none of my friends know anything about this thing.

Normally, I'd think it's a prank, except this chatter literally sent me the game. It was in a small, pretty purple box, and there was a letter on top of a USB stick. I opened the letter up, and it read:

*"Thank you, Ophelia, for being interested this game. I've had it rotting on my shelf for far too long, and I hope it plays for you just fine. It's the only one that exists. I'm sure you'll do the right thing about it. I have no other way to repent.*

9:09 P. M.

Okay, so I just got through a massive chunk of the game, and I think writing it down will help me process it. First, I was caught by a witch in the kitchen, and she shrunk me down to two inches tall. From then on, it took me two and a half hours to even progress, but I quickly began to figure out how the game's puzzles are laid out. I met Hansel and Gretel (also called the Hungry Prince and the Gluttonous Princess), and they acted as my companions for the next section of the kitchen.

I think I like this game now. Don't get me wrong, the problems are still rampant. It's buggy beyond belief, some dialogue isn't even spoken out loud, Gretel just straight-up vanishes during a conversation at some point, and the witch's boss battle took an hour and a half because of the horrendous hit boxes and that she'd literally shrug off the knife she's supposedly vulnerable to.

However, the story I'm seeing so far is intriguing. I think it's using fairytales as ways to explain how children grow up, if that makes sense. Hansel and Gretel cling to each other, never fully leaving the other's side, and Lilith is stubborn and tries to do things on her own, even if it's easier to have help.
