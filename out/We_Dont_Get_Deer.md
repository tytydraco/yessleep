Every day, I pass by the same woods on my way to work. They’re the kind of woods where the trees are too dense to see past more than a couple dozen feet. They’re pressed right against the highway, so it ends up being really claustrophobic, but the city does a decent job at making sure that the branches don’t brush against the cars too bad. I drive a beat up old pickup truck–where the paint on the different parts don’t even match, y’know? 

Anyway, I’ve been driving by these woods every day for going on three years now. On the way home, the shadows between the trees are the kind of thing that you’ll spook yourself with if you linger on them too long. You start seeing stuff. Like how you see the silhouette of a tree in the window, and you’re so tired, you end up freaking yourself out about it. You turn on the light, it’s nothing, you go back to bed. Same thing with those trees–those woods. You stare out into the shadows long enough, you imagine ‘em moving. You get curious enough, maybe you get out, shine your flashlight, and you realize it was just the moonlight catching on the branches. As such, I do my level best not to look in that direction. It’s to my right, and there’s not enough room for bikers or pedestrians to walk that way anyway. So, I put on a podcast, or I put on an album, and I keep to myself.

Then, probably two, three days ago, I’m heading home, and I see it outta the corner of my eye this time. I knew there was *something* there. Just the right movements, but a flash of color too. A deep, earthy brown. Now, in my logical brain, I know that’s probably a deer. Now, we don’t get deer out here, what with the cold, but it’s still a sane assumption. Maybe one got lost. Still. It’s unusual enough that I can’t help but look over occasionally. And it’s nothing. It’s the same shadows as always. But, just like always, I imagine I’m seeing more than what’s there. I’m seeing movement. But I’m three, four miles out at this point. Whatever it was that I saw–if I saw anything at all–it’s long behind me. But, god help me, I gotta clear my head this time. I end up slowing the car down.

And as the truck comes to a stop, I dig into my glove box, rifling through shit until I find the damn flashlight. Its batteries are damn near out, but it’s working well enough for this. I double-check it to make sure the bulb’s not out, just in case. Then, I push my door, kicking it a little to pry it open. I take my keys, and I stuff ‘em in my pocket. I slam the door closed, and I look back out into the woods and all them moving shadows. 

I turn the flashlight on, and I watch it flicker, then turn off. I curse at it under my breath. I start fiddling with it. I manage to get it to flick on again. Then, it seems to die out on me. I know most everybody would just use their phone flashlight, but them things never did seem bright enough for these purposes. Besides, it’s army issue. It’s a good flashlight. Just old, is all. I smack the side of it, and this time, the batteries seem to be fully dead. I scoff, and I look out into the shadows that dance about in the moonlight. Now, I’m getting the heebie-jeebies. I dunno why, just one of those things where you can just feel something ain’t right. I shake it off best I can, and I head back to the truck. 

I open the door that special way you gotta open some of these old trucks. Meaning, I kicked it, and I pushed my shoulder against it, and only then did I even bother with the handle. I pulled it open, and I climbed inside. For a split-second, I thought on leaving the door open. Then, with them heebie-jeebies in full effect, I closed it. Pure paranoia, I’ll admit. I turned away from the closed door, and I started digging for batteries. My hands, shaking. It’s nonsensical, and I knew that. Probably part of what’s got me so frustrated there, I think. I know it’s dumb. Then, I hear something.

A quiet, sharp creak. I froze solid, I swear to god. It was from right behind me. Then, just as I was starting to react, there's a thunk from the same direction. Metal on metal, complete with rust and cold.

I spin around to see somebody standing at my fucking door. So close they coulda put their damn chin on my roof, I mean it. I jumped, and I ain’t gonna lie, I yelped like a little girl. Heart’s going a kajillion times a second. 

Person leans down, and with the lights from the car, and the light from just behind me, I can see it’s a state trooper. He’s got his brown uniform on, and that hat they all wear. His hand comes back. It was the door handle what made that thunk. He’d tried to open the door. I mean it when I say I had no earthly idea what to do about that. He takes a short step back. Slow-like. He puts his hands up. He’s got something in one of them hands, but I can’t see what it is. Something small. 

And yeah, he’s got me spooked, but then he gives me a smile. It ain’t no horror movie smile, don’t get excited. Just a kind, warm, apologetic smile that he wears as he calls through the door, “Thought it was abandoned, sorry about that.”

I give him a little nod, and I reply, “You done scared the hell outta me.”

“Apologies!” The fella tips his hat, and he lifts whatever that was in his hand up a little. “You dropped it.”

“Oh,” I say. Now, I dunno about y’all, but I never been one to try to make a scene with the police. I figure, best thing to do, logically, is to open the door, take whatsoever it is he’s handing me, and move on. But, I don’t figure myself for a stupid man, and it ain’t normal for an officer of the law to just go and try to open a door like that. So, I hesitate.

Thankfully, he seems to understand. He gives me a little awkward laugh. He sets whatever it is down on the ground just outside the car. He gives me another tip of his cap. And he turns and walks towards his car. He walks slow-like. Relaxed. I watch him walk towards his car. Now, I tell you, this part of town is *silent*. Dead silent. I didn’t hear that car at *all.* He stops at the door He reaches forward. He turns the handle. And he gets in the car. Then, the motor starts. It starts with a roar loud enough to scare me a little. Then, it dies to a purr. Then, to nothing. And only then, does it start to move. It crawls forward, moving maybe ten miles an hour along a freeway marked sixty. He drives past me. He’s looking straight ahead. His windows are a little tinted, so I ain’t able to tell if there’s anything else in there. Then, I see something. A flash of brown. And as he finally passes me, I see he’s got the carcass of a deer on the back of his car. It’s thumping around as he hits potholes and shit. It leaks little bits of blood. And eventually, the car’s gone. And he’s gone. And that there deer’s gone too. 

I feel like I’m able to breathe again for the first time since he’d shown up. I look out towards the woods, and I don’t even feel like there’s nothing to be afraid of that way now, but I still wanna check. I look around, looking for deathly silent rangers or whatever, but there’s none to be found. I have to choose between seeing what it was I dropped and just getting home. With no small amount of consternation, I eventually choose to see them woods. Get that confirmation and hope that shakes off those heebie-jeebies.

So, I pop open the door again, kicking it just like before. And I reach down, feeling on the ground for whatever it was before finding batteries. Fresh ones, new-like. I ain’t have anything like that in the truck. I look around, feeling them heebie-jeebies yet again. I push through. See, I’m just about done now, and I’d like to put this outta my head and go home. Not seeing the other batteries nowhere, I decide to just flick these in. And I do, and sure enough, flashlight works just fine. Army-issue, I told ya. 

I step out the car, keeping my eyes and ears on full alert. And I shine the flashlight towards the woods to find… nothing. Big ole nothing. Not even no deer. ‘Course there wasn’t no deer. Just a bunch of sticks and twigs and whatnot. Shadows moving around. Maybe a bug, I don’t know. I just let out a little sigh of relief, and I walked back ‘round the door. I opened it again, kicking it, putting my shoulder against it, then finally pulling open the door handle. I flung the door open, and...

An engine roared to life.

I turned to see the trooper from before, comin’ back my way. I started to give a wave when he tore ass past me, going ninety easy. His engine was so damn loud, I covered my ears just to put up with it. I looked out and saw that goddamn deer again, hanging off of the back of that truck, dead just like before. Looked more rotted than it did last time, but it seemed that might have just been me getting a better look at it. I dunno. I just thought one thing.

Fuck all this.

I get back in my truck, I slam the door closed, and I just go as fast as that little truck’s gonna let me. I’m breathing hard. I’m sweating. My whole damn body’s shaking. I just ain’t built for weirdness, I think. I have to remember to slow down from eighty by the time I get to town again, just in case I get pulled over by any weird ass staties. I live on a backroad, even in the backroad town, but it ain’t hard to get to. I’m getting home late, but I know the only being what cares is my dog. So, I got in, and I took some sleeping pills to put it outta my mind. I fed the dog, I fed myself, and I went to bed.

And then, this morning, I’m hearing barking. And I don’t normally make much of barking. Lucy’s one to bark at the breeze. Still, it wakes me up, and so I get up, and I shush her as I go to check and show here there ain’t nothing to be worried about. And I open the door. On the other side, I see that every window on my truck’s broken. Every one. The mirrors hanging. And the door’s opened. There’s a unfamiliar boot mark in the door. And just in front of that door, there’s a deer, eating my grass. Looking right at me. And what I tell you? We don't get deer so much 'round here.
