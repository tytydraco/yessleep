My nightmare began a few months ago.

I was sitting in a bar with my laptop open in front of me, and there were barely two sentences on the screen. I was sipping my drink when a strange voice pulled me out of my thoughts.

“Hey, Tim. Writing still isn’t going very well, huh?”

I frowned and looked up to see who was talking to me. A man who looked barely over twenty was standing in front of me. I looked him over from head to toe. He had short, curly blond hair, dark eyes, and an amused smile playing on his lips. He was wearing a blue long-sleeved shirt, black pants, and dress shoes. He was carrying a bag, which he placed on the table next to me.

“Do we know each other?” I asked, a little more rudely than I intended.

The guy smiled.

“I know you. And now you know me too. My name is Logan, and I have an offer for you.”

“Oh, for God’s sake. Not this,” I said desperately, rubbing my face. “I don’t want to buy anything.”

Logan burst out laughing.

“Okay. In that case, I’ll give you something for free.”

He sat down across from me.

I looked at him irritably and said, “Okay, let’s get this over with. What is it?”

He pulled his chair closer, glanced at my laptop, and began.

“I’ll make you one of the most famous horror writers in the world. You’ll write plenty of books. You’ll never run out of ideas. You’ll be swimming in money, just like you always wanted.”

I started laughing.

“So you’re basically going to give me what I want most? And you don’t want anything in return? My soul, perhaps?”

But Logan wasn’t laughing. He was completely serious.

“Not today. Sometimes I ask for a soul, sometimes for something else, but I promised you that yours would be free.”

“And you want me to seal the deal with blood, Faust?” I said between another fit of laughter.

He laughed too, but there was nothing funny about it. A chill ran down my spine, and my laughter immediately stopped.

“No. All I need is for us to shake hands. I’m just a merchant. There’s only one condition.”

“What condition?”

Logan stared into my eyes.

“You must never stop writing.”

“Whenever you have an idea and feel the urge to write, you can’t stop. Ever.”

I downed my shot and asked, “And what happens if I stop?”

Logan stood up from the table.

“You don’t want to find out.”

“You can still change your mind,” Logan said.

I thought about it for a moment and then told him I would take the deal. He held out his hand, and a strange feeling passed through me, one I couldn’t quite put into words. Now I know what it felt like. It felt as if something inside me had died.

After we shook hands, Logan turned around and walked away. Just before he stepped through the bar’s door, he called out to me.

“Don’t forget, Tim. Don’t stop writing.”

I sat there for a while longer and then went home. It started that very night. One idea after another. I finished a book within a few weeks. It was a huge success. Then came another one, and another.

My bank account was filled with more money than I had ever dreamed of. My dream had come true, but I started to feel exhausted. The urge to write and all those ideas… I barely had time for anything else.

So I decided to screw it and take a few days off.

The next day, I was walking down the stairs when I saw a woman in a white shirt walking through the hallway. Her face was horribly disfigured. She looked at me and started screaming.

I started screaming too and immediately ran back upstairs to my room. I shut the door and locked it. I heard a strange rasping sound behind me.

Slowly, I turned around. A few feet away from me, something horrible was standing in the corner. It had claws, a wide, grotesque mouth full of enormous teeth, and bulging eyes.

My heart was pounding, and I was shaking all over. I screamed like a madman until my voice gave out completely.

I knew what was standing there.

It was a character from my book.

So was the woman.

Logan’s voice echoed in my head.

“You must never stop writing.”

The creature pointed toward the laptop.

“Okay, okay,” I stammered.

I sat down and started writing. I thought they would disappear, but they didn’t. I encountered them everywhere in the house. At first, I would wake up terrified and screaming, but eventually, I got used to them. You can get used to anything.

But I was already so tired.

“Screw you!” I screamed one day. “I’m not writing anymore.”

There were more and more of them. They wandered around the house, but I didn’t care. The woman, the creature, the torso that crawled around my apartment… But the longer I refused to write, the more nervous they became.

It all came to a head the day they attacked me.

They threw themselves at me and started scratching and beating me. They bit off several of my fingers. I had no choice but to start writing again.

But I couldn’t keep it up for long.

“Fine. Then kill me,” I said resignedly.

For a few days, everything was quiet. I was lying on the couch watching TV when the doorbell rang. I went to the door, and my brother Mark was standing there.

He wanted to come inside, but I told him no, that we could meet somewhere else. He was already leaving when something grabbed me by the legs and dragged me back inside.

Mark ran after me, and then…

Some of them held me down so I couldn’t move, while the others lunged at Mark. He had absolutely no idea what was happening. He desperately screamed for help and called my name.

I broke down crying and begged them to leave him alone. I promised I would start writing again, just as long as they let him go, but they didn’t.

They started tearing him apart.

It took an eternity before Mark’s screams finally stopped, and then they ate him. They left nothing behind. Nothing at all.

I threw up and then just sat there, staring at the spot where Mark had been moments before.

The disfigured woman shuffled toward me. Mark’s blood was still dripping from her mouth. When she spoke, her voice sounded like a creaking door.

“Next tiiime, there will be others…”

I knew what she meant.

I have children, parents, friends. So many people I care about.

I’ve been writing ever since. Constantly.

The urge is getting stronger and stronger. But finally, I have a glimmer of hope.

I found an online discussion where a woman was talking about a merchant. Her description of him matches Logan. Apparently, he came to their town in a caravan and sold wishes, and hers came true.

I messaged her and asked if he was still there. She said he was.

I’m going there tomorrow.

For the first time in a long while, I felt fear again, twisting my stomach. What is he? What is he capable of? I don’t know, but I have to find out.

I hope he’ll still be there when I arrive. If he isn’t, I’ll never stop looking for him.

I need him to stop this.
