I grew up in a relatively small town in Idaho. The name isn't very relevant. Not that I remember it or ever cared to learn it anyhow. My parents were the only two adults in the whole town and they made most of their money by farming and selling potatoes. That’s how they provided for all their children.

Their children made up the entire rest of the town's population. I was one of the lucky ones who got to live with them before I moved out. The vast majority of my brothers and sisters were relegated to the other homes of the town where they would receive just enough care to not die like clothes and food and whatnot. Though I'm reluctant to call them brothers and sisters since given their abnormally large quantity they didn't quite feel that way. For the most part we treated each other just like any neighbor's kids despite coming from the same two people.

Only one of them ever did feel like my brother. Jerome was about my age and was the only other kid who lived in our parents’ house when I did. We did pretty much everything together. Our town was surrounded by a pretty dense forest which we'd go out and explore every day during the summer. We'd run around and play games and all that nice stuff and come home each night panting with sweat stains down our shirts.

There was another kid in town our age. He's the only other kid I really remember and the only one besides Jerome and I who's actually relevant to this story. His name was Andrew. He was smaller than us. He was skinny and short and his clothes were always just a little baggy. Not many kids hung out with him I'm sure. I only realize this now though. I never noticed him back when I was a kid. 

Anyway I believe this was the reason he began skulking around the forest one summer whenever me and Jerome would run around. We tried our best to ignore him even when he'd join in on our games without permission. I still remember when he really ticked me off for the last time. Though I guess everything that followed wasn't his fault at all. I guess it wasn't even his fault I was so mad. I wish I only knew that then. I maybe wouldn't be here right now.

I was playing hide and seek with Jerome. I was the seeker this round and I had just found him hiding inside a hollow light that had fallen over. I got on top of the log and peered into the opening with my face upside down and said:

“Found you! Now it's your turn to be the seeker.”

“No fair!” he said as he crawled out. “You looked. I knew it.”

“Did not.”

“Did too!”

“Hey. What's that thing on your neck?”

“What thing? Are you messing with me?”

“No. I swear. There's a bug on your neck. Here.”

I wasn’t lying to him. There was a centipede curled around his neck. Its dark shell appeared all sleek and shiny. I reached over and plucked it off and showed it to him as it hung from between my fingers and squirmed. It jostled all hundred of its legs. They moved in waves on either side.

“See?” I said.

He gagged when saw it. “Gross.” He started patting all over his body to make sure there weren't any other bugs on him. I giggled at the sight.

It was then that Andrew perked his head up from behind a nearby stone. I was letting the centipede back into the log. It looked back at me before scuttling away when he said:

“What about me guys? You can't finish before you find all the hiders. You still gotta find me. Come on.”

I glanced at him without turning my head and looked back without saying anything. Jerome turned to him.

“You're not hidden anymore Andrew. It doesn't matter now.”

“Oh.”

“...”

“That was a pretty smart way to catch me though.”

“How?” Said Jerome.

“You know. He pretended like you were done so that I'd come out. Pretty smart.”

“Wow. That is really smart. I never thought of that. Good job Micheal.”

He patted me on the shoulder. I continued to ignore Andrew. I hadn't thought of it that way at all. It wasn't intentional.

“But you'll find me normally next round.”

“I dunno. That might be too good a strategy to pass up.”

“Well I won't fall for it next time then.”

And he didn't fall for it. Jerome found me first and when he did he told me it was my turn to be seeker. Then he let his silence hold as I turned around to face the nearest tree trunk. He gripped my shoulder as I was putting my hands to my eyes and he whispered:

“No. Not actually. I'm just trying to get Andrew to come out. Just wait.”

We both stood there in silence for a moment. After a while Jerome gave up. He really wasn't falling for it. 

We found him an hour later. He climbed up a tree and sat on its branch and hid up there. We never thought to look up until we saw it was getting late. I looked up at the setting sun nestled between orange clouds. Its golden beams shone through the cracks between branches and there he was smiling at me. He waved. That really tilted me.

It was my turn to be seeker then. Andrew protested insisting it was actually his but I just turned around and started counting till he shut up and walked away.

That was the last round we played that night. I found Jerome and told him it was his turn now and that the round was over. I knew if we were in earshot of Andrew he wouldn't fall for it anyway. I whispered to Jerome that I wasn't trying anything. We were actually done. It was too late for another round and we ought to've headed home then. And so we did. We went home without even looking for Andrew.

That night after supper after we had both put on our pajamas and were sent to bed I snuck into Jerome's room. He was lying in his bed already with his eyes closed.

“Jerome?”

He said nothing.

“I know you're awake. I just saw you come in here.”

“You don't know that,” he said with his eyes still closed. “You don't know how fast I fall asleep.”

“People don't talk when they're asleep Jerome.”

“Yeah they do. Haven't you heard the stories?”

“They don't have conversations with awake people.” (He put a finger up and opened his mouth to speak) “At least not ones that make sense.”

“Fine,” he said and opened his eyes and sat up in his bed. “What is it?”

“It's about Andrew.”

“What about ‘im? You think he's alright? Think he's made it home?”

I put a finger to his lip. “Shush. They might hear you.”

“Sorry.”

“You're fine. Now listen here.”

I leaned into his ear and whispered to him about how much of a nuisance Andrew was and what I planned for us to do to him. It was a fool proof plan as far as I saw it. We were to enact it the following day.

That next morning we ate breakfast and ran off to the forest's edge as usual. Andrew was there. Or he arrived there just then. He crawled up from between the bushes. His eyes were red and his face was dirty. His shirt was torn with tiny little slits in its sides and leaves were resting in his messy hair. He smiled when he saw us.

“Hey guys. Woof. I oughta tell you about the adventure I just had. It was like one of those spooky stories Dad tells us around Halloween.” He paused. He looked up and put a hand to his forehead to shade his eyes. “Is that the sun? Is it morning already?”

“Yep,” said Jerome.

“Aw man.” He pouted and let his arms dangle. “I missed supper.” 

“Don't worry Andrew,” I said. “I'm sure you won't miss it tonight.”

I smiled at him and put a hand over his shoulder. It was all artificial.

“Okay. But you guys have to let me know when you're going home this time. Even if the game’s still going.”

“Sure.”

“Promise?”

“Promise.”

“Jerome?”

“I also promise.”

“Okay. So what are we playing today?”

“Good question,” I said. “We've come up with a new game to play. It doesn't have a name yet but we think it'll be real fun.”

“Really? How do you play?”

“You have to crawl through tunnels.”

“Tunnels?”

“Yeah. Underground tunnels.”

“are they pretend underground tunnels or real?”

“Oh. They're real. Here. Let us show you.”

We brought him to a spot behind our house. Well it was more like I brought him. Jerome sort of sulked a bit behind us for some reason.

The backyard of our house was where our parents kept their potato farm. It was a pretty big farm at least as we (the kids) were all aware. It was the biggest open section in the whole town.

“Are we digging tunnels in the farm?” Andrew asked nervously. “I'm not so sure about this guys.”

“No Andrew,” I replied. “We're not ruining the farm.”

That was true. What we were really looking for was this door in the ground that was right at the back door of the house. Ordinarily I'm sure now it would lead to a bunker but I wasn't aware of what a bunker was back then and when I asked my parents they just said:

“It's where you came from.”

I wasn't very satisfied with that answer but every time I asked it was the same so I kind of just gave up at a point. Anyway we weren't allowed in there. Plus it was locked. Fortunately I had snuck into our parents’ bedroom while they were asleep and stole the key the night before. They didn't even hide it. It was right on the dresser. We stopped right before it and Andrew started straight at the wall.

“Here we are. The tunnel's right down there,” I said gesturing downward.

“Oh wow. I didn't know you guys had tunnels under your house!” 

No one did. Me and Jerome (and Mom and Dad) were the only ones who knew this door even existed. 

“Yep. Now just let me open it up and we can get started.”

I pulled the key out of my pocket and started finagling the lock. As I let the chain fall from the the handles to the dirt and swung the door open I heard Andrew say:

“Wait. What do we do once we're down in the tunnels?”

“There's no time to explain now,” I said with a hand on his back as we stood over the dark hole. “The tunnels are open. You've got to get in quick before they close.”

I shoved him. He fell forward into the hole. We heard his screams echo. Quickly I shut the doors over him. He started banging on them as I refastened the lock and chain. 

“Let me out!” He said as he pounded the doors. “Come on guys! This isn't funny! Let me out already!”

He started crying after a little bit. Then there was this little noise like the crack of a whip. Only it was softer. And with that the pounding stopped. He buzzed like a mosquito. It faded quickly and he finally went quiet. 

“Hah!” I said. “That oughta show him. You ready Jerome?”

“Yeah.” I didn't realize it then but he seemed quite solemn.

We went and played in the woods as usual. We came home as though nothing happened. I for one felt free in a way. It was relieving to be done with that nuisance. We resumed this behavior the next day and the day after. I forgot about Andrew in that time. This is why I was confused when Mom burst into my room one night.

“Micheal,” she said sternly. “Come to the living room.”

I could tell I had done something wrong by the tone of her voice. So in my pajamas I scrambled towards the living room. Jerome was on the couch staring at his lap and twiddling his thumbs. Dad was there too. Mom spawned behind me and put a hand to my shoulder.

“Have a seat Micheal.”

I sat next to Jerome. Dad began.

“Your brother told us that you and him pushed Andrew into that spot behind our house. Is this true Micheal?”

“Yes,” I said. There was no point in lying now.

“And when was this?”

“Um. Two days ago.”

“Christ. Tell me you let him out.”

“...”

“Tell me you let him out Micheal. You let him out. Didn't you?”

“...”

“...”

“No,” I whispered finally.

“Oh dear.” He put his hand to his mouth. I saw it twist into an awful grimace. He looked down and tried not to cry.

“Why'd you do it?” Our mother said.

“...”

“Why?”

“Well,” I stammered. “Well. Well he was annoying.”

No one said anything. Our mother frowned. Her whole face went stern. After a moment she said:

“Go and get him.”

“What?”

“Go let him out. You and Jerome. Now.”

I ran into my room and came out with the key. Jerome brought a flashlight from his own. We scrambled back to that set of doors. They faced up at us in the light of the moon. It felt like my whole stomach was twisting in me. My knees wobbled as I stared down. I'm sure Jerome felt the same way.

Finally I mustered up the courage and unlocked the doors. The chain slinked to the dirt and lay there curled like a snake. I split them apart and opened them slowly. Their hinges squeaked.

It was pitch black in there. There were wooden steps coming from the dark and ending at the entrance. Jerome went in first. I followed him.

When we reached the bottom the floor felt like a tangle of vines. They were dry and stiff and papery in texture. We both felt it shift under our feet.

“Andrew?” Jerome said. “We've come back to get you. We're sorry about earlier. Promise.”

Silence.

“Are you asleep down here?” I said. “Come on Andrew. Get up so we can go. It stinks down here.”

Silence again. The floor shifted under our feet once more.

Finally Jerome clicked his flashlight on. He pointed it at the floor. In a dim circle of white light we saw what it was now. They were like tendrils. They were stiff though like moderately thick tree branches. They were white like teeth and they were what had been shifting under us. They made up the entire floor.

Under Jerome's light they started to gesticulate like crazy. They twisted inward first. Then they started to sprout upward. They reached from the ground growing like bamboo stocks. And the whole time they made this low creaking sound. Underneath there was this soft cooing like that of a newborn. I felt something rhythmic beating like a heart under my heel. I realized soon there were multiple sources of pulsing.

We both froze. Jerome continued to scan the room. Under his light stocks began stretching from everywhere across the floor. There was this cacophony of cracks and fizzles as they stretched towards the light. We were in a forest of ghost trees before long.

He stopped the flashlight at a corner. There was a bundle of stocks in the shape of a boy. They had grown into a set of clothes that looked a lot like Andrew's. The clothes were dirty. There was a baggy white button up and contorted brown pants. The sprouts stretched out of them in the shape of arms and legs. They sprouted in bundles down the pants legs where they had become intertwined with the floor. Tiny little tendrils came up out of the spots between buttons of the shirt and little tears in its sides. The stocks making up the form were all pink. The chest was rising and falling steadily under the shirt pushing those little spouts up and down.

At the top there was the round shape of a head. Under the light the arms and legs started to extend. Some of the sprouts grew outward like tree branches. And the ones making up the head twisted inwards as they grew.

Something rustled in the head. A hole opened up in the spouts revealing a fleck of white. It was something round and mushy. It had thin red veins running through its moist surface. The hole opened wider as the thing rolled around in its newly formed socket until it stopped revealing a pupil and hazel iris. The same color as Andrew's eyes.

The second we were caught in its sight the sprouts went crazy. The creaking sound multiplied. It got all jumbled. The pulsing under our feet reacted the same way. It was more like a pounding now. And it sounded like something was crying underneath it all. 

They (the sprouts) began to grow rapidly. Even faster than they had before. They shot straight at us. And from the ground I saw stubby little arms rise up and squirm their fat little fingers about. They started reaching towards us. They were pink and fleshy and moist. We scrambled for the exit. I got on the steps first as I was closest and Jerome followed right behind me.

I came out of the hole first and turned back and through the darkness saw Jerome rushing up the ladder. He kept the flashlight in his hand as he clung to the wooden board keeping the steps in line. He was on all fours and climbing them like a ladder. 

He reached his hand out for me. I froze. I could see the sprouts stretching through the darkness at his feet. Before I could move one wrapped around each of his ankles. They pulled him down into the darkness. He dropped his flashlight and it fell into the hole. His scream echoed and faded.

The sprouts continued climbing. They were reaching for me. I turned and I ran. I ran right through the potato farm and the forest and didn't stop until I came out the other end the next morning. I never looked back.

I'm not sure what exactly happened after that. The months since are such a blur in my mind. I can only recall the big swoops.

I wound up sleeping in the streets. I didn't know where to go from there. Even if my parents would take me back I couldn't find my way home. I started looking for another one (another home). I would ask any adult I found if they'd be my mom or my dad. Eventually I wound up going through the foster system. I've found a new family since then because of that. They've treated me fine.

I'm in high school now. I've grown up to be shorter and skinnier than the other kids. I thought I might try to befriend some of them. I haven't made many for a while. It didn't go over so well.

So now I'm here. I'm in the janitor's closet. The lights are off and the door won't open. I think they broke the handle from the other side. The bell rang and everyone left hours ago. It's just me here now. 

I'm told it's an awful thing to go with your sins still hanging in your brain. I don't know if I'll die in here but I don't think I'll be able to say much when I get out either. It's like I’ve got tendrils on my back. I can feel them rub against the fabric as they shift under my shirt.

I'm sorry Andrew. I'm sorry Jerome. But they're not reading this. They never will. Isn't that funny? Maybe it still means something. I sure hope it does.
