This happened several years ago back in Uni, but recently I read a Reddit story about a guy self-inducing sleep paralysis, so I thought I’d share here as well.

Back when this happened, I shared a small two bedroom apartment with a very good friend I will be calling “Carlie” for this post. The apartment was very poorly designed in a way that made it difficult to stay out of each other’s way completely (example, the only bathroom was inside Carlie’s bedroom) and we got along great, so we did almost everything together in the day to day. Because of this, we kept our bedroom doors unlocked and it was not unusual for us to just enter without knocking (important for later). 

So, it’s a long weekend and Carlie is out of town visiting her boyfriend’s family. I have the whole apartment for myself. I don’t remember what I spend the evening doing, but I do remember having an uneasy feeling going to bed. Having all that space for myself when there was on the regular one or two more people (boyfriend stayed often as well) was unnerving. 

But I put those feelings aside, turned the lights off and did my best to fall asleep. It must have started when I was just beginning to doze off, not quite sure. But next thing I know is I heard the front door of the apartment opening and heard Carlie’s voice, speaking super loud and energetic. It gave me the impression she was on the phone with someone. Could not discern what she was saying though, my mind was too busy wondering why she was back. I remember thinking “I thought she was gone the whole weekend”. 

Her conversation quieted down and few seconds later I felt the door to my room bursting open and the lights turned on, as Carlie came in screaming “OP! You cannot imagine the day I just had!” I say I felt all this, because I still had my eyes closed. So I could only perceive her presence and sense the light now on behind my eyelids. She continued talking, but I was just wondering in the back of my head. “How does she know I am awake if I still have my eyes closed?” And thinking I should open my eyes to say hi properly and hear what had happened to her. But they felt so so heavy, I was not able to open them at all. Instead of listening to what Carlie was saying (she went on a monologue unbothered by my lack of reaction), I focused all my efforts into opening my eyes. 

And the moment I did, the light in the room went off. The door was closed and Carlie was no longer there. Instead of her, there was a tall man standing at the foot of my bed, staring hard and silent at me. Expressionless, except for a hint of anger in his very much completely black eyes. 

I wanted to scream but I couldn’t. I wanted to move but I was not able either. All I could do was breathe fast. My breathing went faster and faster, as I was in this bizarre staring contest with the man, and doing all I could to move. Minutes went by. He did not move a muscle either. 

Finally I was able to move my hand. A single spasm, and with it, the man vanished too. I was able to move quickly right after that, and never have I ever ran to the light switch as fast. After that, I just sat in my bed and cried. I did not sleep that night. I eventually turned the lights on every room and verified neither Carlie nor the man were in the apartment. 

In the following days I learned what sleep paralysis is, by sharing this event with friends (including Carlie of course) and doing some intense reading in google on the subject after someone mentioned it. I concluded that is what must have happened to me that night, although nothing even remotely similar had never happened to me, nor has happened since. Except now, every once in a while, when I close my eyes to sleep, I can feel the stare of that man behind my eyelids, and his presence by the foot of my bed. And when it happens, these feelings are gone the moment I open my eyes. Nobody is ever there. This might too be a type of sleep paralysis (?)

And after years, I’ve grown used to the feeling of being stared at while falling asleep. I pay it no mind these days.
