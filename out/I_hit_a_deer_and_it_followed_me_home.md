Hello everyone, apologies if this is the wrong subreddit. I’m trying to locate an animal rescue or something in rural Washington. You see, I did a pretty shitty thing last Thursday and I’m feeling pretty bad about it. Especially since it’s been following me around. Okay, let me just explain and then maybe let me know if anyone can help in the replies. I’d really appreciate it.

 So my name is Justin. Just to get introductions out of the way. I live in a really nice area in the countryside, somewhat near Mead. I think Spokane is the nearest major city here. It’s out of the way, it’s real peaceful. The only problem is it’s damn hard to get any help or services around here because we’re miles away from most civilization. Anyway, I work graveyard half the time. I commute into the city or wherever my services are needed, since I work security. I was driving home in my pickup after a pretty long and draining twelve hour shift. Some stupid event that literally took all day and went into the night. It was after 2 AM. I was exhausted. That’s probably why my eyes were blurry and I almost drove off the road twice.

 I was two, maybe three, miles from home when I saw a few deer loitering in the trees next to the road. Not an uncommon sight, mind you. I even feed the herds around here whenever I can spare the extra cash for corn or other feeds. They’re beautiful animals. I just don’t tell them I find them really tasty too when I hand-feed them an apple. (I don’t want any PETA people in my replies saying I’m evil for eating venison, I appreciate deer as a part of our natural ecosystem. They just so happen to be lower than me on the food chain. Not my fault.) I didn’t see any deer on the actual asphalt, mind you. I even slowed my pace for a bit to make sure I could swerve or brake in case I did see one.

 I was so close to home, though, and my eyelids were getting heavy thinking about my bed. The feather-soft pillows and insulated comforter were calling my name. My eyes might have actually closed for a few seconds, but only that. I didn’t doze off or anything. I was still driving straight and steady on the road when they opened again. But then I saw a blur in my headlights and felt an almighty *‘thud’* against the front of my truck. I feel sick to my stomach just thinking about it, really.

  It was a horrible scene. There was blood splattered on the grill, chunks of fur caught on the metal. I pulled over as soon as I realized what had happened, but I still had to walk a few feet back to the impact site after. I thanked God that I had my phone charged so I could use the flashlight. Blood drops and pieces of flesh littered that part of the road, leading into a grassy ditch. I swallowed hard, knowing what I’d find, partially-hidden by that grass. And there it was.

 Surprisingly, it was still breathing. But barely. A gash, stretching down its ribs and towards its belly, was still oozing blood. The bulge of wet organs, swollen with bruising, glistened between torn skin and exposed bone. There was already a sizable pool of dark liquid below it. My flashlight beam moved to its face, where I saw its muzzle was parted as it heaved. Thick, red liquid flowed from between its teeth. Nostrils flared as it wheezed out small, high-pitched noises. The thing that took me aback was the eyes. It was looking right at me. Its eyes were so expressive, I could see the whites and the pupils as small as pinpoints in the light. I’d seen deer eyes up close before, they were always brown and always pretty boring. But these eyes? They were gorgeous. When talking about a bloody, dying deer anyway. They just had this air of emotion that hit me right in the chest. There was an almost iridescent, glowing effect to them as they reacted to the flashlight. I took a long, hard look at the helpless creature below me and felt a sudden, deep grief.

  I’m man enough to say I cried and wasn’t ashamed. I bawled as I stroked the deer’s head and muzzle, and it let me.

 “You’re alright, pretty thing.” I cooed, trying to comfort it. “I got you, everything’s alright.” I thought maybe if I could lift it into my truck, I could drive it to the nearest vet? But I didn’t even know where the nearest vet was. And a vet wasn’t likely to be open at this hour, let alone be able to take a deer. The breathing got more labored and a thick, dark film escaped its mouth as it let out hacking sounds.

 “Oh, beautiful-” Tears stung my eyes as I realized what was happening, “Just close your eyes. It’s okay, it’ll be over soon. It’ll be over.” The deer’s eye trained right on my face as its entire body jerked. Its eyelid twitched, shutting halfway before it emitted a cry that made me sob. The abdomen heaved once more, blood continuing to flow from the wound. It then fell still, and everything was silent. My hand ran over its head again, rubbing its ears. I heard that brain activity continued for a few seconds after death. If that was true, I didn’t want its last experience to be cold and dark. I stroked over its left ear, feeling the soft, velvety touch that few people get to experience. A round notch was etched into it. I wondered what had caused that.

 Don’t get me wrong. I understand animals naturally die all the time. What’s another deer gone, when there are whole herds of them wandering the woods? Sue me, I’m a bleeding heart for animals. My dogs Lucy and Chucky can attest to that. I shed a few tears over a dead, mangled squirrel they brought me last year. This one particularly hurt because I was to blame. If I had been looking closer to the area around the road, I wouldn’t have hit this deer. It would still be alive, frolicking in the woods with its family.

 In the end, there was nothing for me to do now. I understood that. I wiped my eyes, climbed into my truck and re-started the short stretch home. As sad as I was, I thought that would be the end of it. Turns out I was wrong.

  I woke up around 11 AM the following day. Coffee and some eggs were my goal after I pulled myself from the covers. Lucy and Chucky were all excited to go outside before their breakfast. And I wasn’t about to clean up golden retriever poop from my rug. So I let them out. When I went to grab a spatula from the drying rack by the sink, I glanced out the window. Lucy was doing her business by the porch, nothing unusual there. Chucky was just sniffing by the barren patch that I had planned to make a garden but hadn’t yet. I cracked a smile, feeling content in that moment. My eyes trailed upwards towards the forest that borders my backyard. It leads down to a little creek, really nice to have in the hot months. I didn’t feel a need to fence in my entire yard, as my dogs are well-trained and never leave the general area without me. That made it possible for a lot of wildlife to make its way into our space, though. So I wasn’t surprised when I spotted the shape of a deer standing in the foliage, just within the line of trees. I figured one of the dogs would see it soon, chase it off and come bounding back to the house. But they hadn’t reacted yet. I shrugged, figuring they’d come inside soon enough, and started on my breakfast.

  It wasn’t long before I heard scratching at the back door. Both of my dogs came tumbling inside, running towards their full food bowls. Sipping my coffee, I looked out the door’s window as I locked it. I licked my lips as I realized the deer was still standing in that same spot. Not moving. Now that was something I didn’t see a lot. I squinted at it, trying to figure out if my eyes were just playing tricks. Was it just a weirdly shaped bush? Or maybe a hunter’s decoy? Walking over to my dining area, I peered out the bigger window to see a better view of the woods. Strangely, it looked like the deer had moved. But only its head. I froze for a moment, processing what I was looking at. It was a deer alright. Its body hadn’t moved an inch from where I first saw it, but its head was following me. I could tell because when I moved, it moved slightly. It was keeping its eyes on me. How on earth could a deer tell I was there so far away, through a wall, no less? Safe to say it freaked me out a bit. That superstitious, supernatural-fearing part of myself had the idea that the deer herd wanted revenge after last night. But I knew that was ridiculous. Most likely it was just dazed or something. I placed my coffee cup down on my table, wondering if I should go out there and check on it or something. The trauma of last night still lingered in my mind, and if there was something I could do for this deer, I’d want to try. But as soon as my mug hit the table with a small clink, the deer tilted its head. It bent at an awkward angle, almost like there was something wrong with its neck. Then, it simply twisted itself towards the trees and bounded away, vanishing into the foliage. I was relieved at first. As I watched the silhouette disappear between the trunks, I paused. There, on the deer’s left ear, was a round notch. It was barely noticeable, but it was there. Or maybe I was just going insane. I thought I was. There was no way that deer from last night had lived. I saw it take its last breath, left its battered body in a ditch. So, using my common sense, I shook it off as a deer with a similar scar. Maybe there was a farm or animal tracking thing around here that used tags and they were removed or ripped off. Two deer could have the same scar. That wasn’t very weird. I’d seen a few bucks with arrows sticking out of their sides or chests before, and those weren’t supernatural. A notched ear was nothing.

  The deer was back when I got home from work. Late again, but only around 12 AM. I cracked open a beer in the kitchen, light pouring out from the window into the backyard. My eyes caught it completely by chance. Just enough light, really only a twinkle, managed to touch the trees at the edge of the yard. It was still enough to reflect off of unblinking, iridescent eyes in the dark. I blinked and rubbed my eyes, trying to see if they were just playing tricks on me. They weren’t. A deer, and I bet it was the same deer, stood in the same spot as before. Staring straight at me. A chill worked its way down my spine, though I was reluctant to allow it to affect me any further. Deer are deer. Woods are their home. As much as I was freaked out after the events of yesterday, I wasn’t going to allow myself to become paranoid over the local wildlife. What was next, thinking birds were microphones to accompany the ever-watching deer? No, I’m not a conspiracy nut, thanks. I closed the curtains and continued my night.

   I thought ignoring it would solve the problem. It hasn’t. If anything, the problem has gotten worse. Way worse. It’s been several days, almost a week now, and the deer hasn’t stopped showing up. The same deer, I know that for sure now. The notch is unmistakable on its ear. Always the same size, place and shape. At first, it stuck to the area by the trees. That was fine. It wasn’t close enough to alert the dogs, in fact they didn’t seem to care about it at all. It didn’t seem to mind them, either. It just watched me. Kept its eyes on me, no matter where I was. Whether I was on the porch or in the house moving room to room. It was always looking at me. I tried to approach it twice, both times it was gone before I even got a few feet away from my house. Turned tail, dashed back into the woods. I never get a complete, full look at it. But last night, something else happened that really has me alarmed.

  It was my day off so I’d been lazing about the house most of the time. Catching up on some shows, drinking some beers and snacking to my heart's content. The pups were also happy about me staying home, even if my circadian rhythm had been fucked up long ago and we didn’t really have the same sleep schedule anymore. We took a lot of naps. I also hadn’t seen the deer. The woods were empty. Looking back, it was actually pretty weird. I don’t remember even seeing any squirrels or birds in the backyard. It was a pretty uneventful day. So, it was probably like 10:30 PM. I had decided that my kitchen trash really stunk and needed to be taken out. Wrapped in only my boxers and a robe, I carried the bag out into the driveway. It was a peaceful evening, the temperature was just warm enough I didn’t need a jacket, and the crickets were chirping in the grass. I felt an air of pure calm settle over me, the kind that comes when all is quiet and you have no real worries in that moment. Tossing the garbage can lid back on top of the container, I paused for a moment. Suddenly, I felt something strange. You know how when you think you’re alone, and you feel your body get alert without knowing why? The hair on your skin starts to stand up, or your muscles tense without warning. It felt like that. Behind me, I heard a quiet ‘*clack*’.

  I spun around, not really thinking about what I’d find, only concentrated on that feeling. My first thought was ‘*Fuck*.’ The sight in front of me made my stomach drop to the soles of my feet. It was the deer. I’m not talking about *a* deer, I’m talking about *THE* deer. It was the deer I hit. It had to be. The light from my porch and the moon weren’t a great source, but I know what I saw. The notched ear was plain as day. Its body was still visibly broken and twisted in places, like one of its front legs sat at an awkward angle. I never noticed that before, not when it was tucked away in the bushes watching me. The gash in its side was still present, even if the dried blood had now caked with dirt and leaves and little twigs with thorns. I could still make out the shapes of organs and intestines sticking out from its flesh. Honestly, the worst part was the face. It was like the muscle had sunk away, leaving the skin stretched in some places and sagging inwards in others. Its tongue had bloated and was blotched with shades of blue and black, sticking from its mouth like a fat, dead worm. Those eyes were the same, though. Large, expressive, bright. Staring at me with a deep intensity that I didn’t- couldn’t- understand. We stood there, staring at each other for a few moments. Honestly it felt like a lot longer. It was only a few feet away, close enough to touch. Not that I wanted to. I was horrified. My body was frozen, but my stomach was churning. I had done this. The broken leg took a step towards me. Another ‘*clack*’ against the pavement woke me from my terrified stupor. I watched a dark, thick substance dribble from between its lips, its muzzle pushing towards me at an alarming pace. I flinched away, and the moment I moved, it did too. But instead of lunging towards me, it flung itself backwards. A gross noise came from its body as it hit the ground. As soon as it hit, though, it was scrambling back to its feet. I’m not sure how it moved so fast. With injuries like that, that deer should really have been dead or at least unable to move. Just like it was in the ditch last week. Now, though, it was bounding away, into the woods, at a pace I’d never seen even the healthiest deer manage. I ran inside, locking myself into the safety of my well-lit house.

  That’s where I am now, and I’m honestly feeling pretty guilty about the whole thing. After thinking it over, I’m pretty sure the deer is really badly injured. It’s clearly dazed, confused and really not doing well, if the blood and exposed guts was any indication. The whole fiasco I’ve experienced this week is surely a sign of brain damage, and it has a broken leg. I would try to help it out myself, but I don’t know the first thing about veterinary medicine. I also don’t have a gun of my own, even though I probably should since I live out in the sticks. If I see it again, I guess I’ll update. I don’t know, I feel pretty bad for it. Maybe it’s been trying to ask for help all this time, and all I’ve done is ignore it and think it’s creepy. If someone around here has expertise in dealing with deer medical emergencies, locate this one as soon as possible. I think it really needs help.
