I’ve been trying to write this for four months, but each time I decide to start writing, I wind up shaking so hard it becomes difficult to type. But I need to get this out of my mind. I need it to be real and be out there.

I am writing this from my room at the \[————\] Mental Institute. I know exactly how I got here; I remember the twenty-fifth of June as clearly as if it was yesterday. I have told my story over two dozen times to doctors and psychologists and psychiatrists and anyone else who asks, but they don’t believe me, so I’ve stopped talking altogether. Why waste my time when my words fall on deaf ears?

Five months ago, I bought a house in the Mon Valley. It was not my first choice of place to live, but the house was cheap. I worked from home, anyway. The man who sold it to me, Luke, explained that the house belonged to his mother, but she passed about a year ago and he’d been sitting on the house unable to decide what to do with it. Luke looked to be in his mid-forties and told me he lived with his family out in Pittsburgh, and if I happened to find anything of his that the movers failed to find, I should give him a call. He was a nice man.

As I was moving in, I decided to put some of my boxes of winter clothes up in the attic. The attic was an odd shape as the house was quite old and the floor plan was dotted with small rooms and closets. After putting some of my stuff into the main room, I decided to explore what else was up there. There was a door that led into a smaller storage space, and within that space was one of those small doors. In truth, I don’t know the purpose of them; the house I grew up in was modern and didn’t have an attic. I pulled the chain to turn on the overhead, then knelt down and tried the handle. The door creaked open with a billow of dust, and I coughed as it coated my lungs.

Inside the small space was a cardboard box labeled in black lettering “Our Friend Nick”. I pulled the box towards me and slid off the lid. Inside was a messy collection of photographs and a notebook. Immediately, I recognized one of the men in the top photo: it was Luke, but he looked about twenty years younger. Each photo was dated on the back and said who was in the picture. Some went as early as the mid-90s. I grabbed a photograph of Luke and another guy and turned it around.

*Luke & Nick, November 5th, 2009*

Once I knew which man was Nick, I saw him in almost every photo. There was one of him sitting on a couch with a girl (labeled as Danielle) lying beside him, one of him picking up another guy (labeled as Joey) and carrying him like a princess. He looked like a fun guy to hang out with; he always had a sort-of-goofy smile on his face. Another thing I noticed about the pictures is that he was often drawing on any piece of paper he could get his hands on. In one picture, he shows his drawing off to the camera and it’s a sketch of a naked woman in a lewd position. If you ignored the subject of his drawing, he was actually quite talented.

At the bottom of the box, folded in on itself, was a newspaper clipping. As I unfolded it, it became very clear to me why this box of happy memories was hidden away. It was an obituary for Nick. According to the paper, he went missing the night of the twenty-fourth of June, 2010, and was found a day later in the woods. They said it was a drug overdose. I felt a wave of sadness rush over me. I didn’t know the guy, but after looking through the photos, I almost felt like I did. He was only thirty, only two years older than me.

I put the lid back on the box and took it downstairs with me. I wasn’t sure what to do with it. Luke told me to call him if I found anything of his, but I still hesitated. Nick’s death obviously hit him pretty hard considering where he stashed the box. It was getting late by this point, so I put the box on my kitchen table and planned to sleep on the decision. But before I left the room, I turned back and went over to the box. I took off the lid and grabbed the notebook that I had neglected to look at.

I changed into my pajamas and sat down on my bed. I pulled the elastic band off the cover of the notebook and opened it to the first page. It looked like a journal, but the handwriting was very difficult to read. The dates of the entries jumped from month to month, until I landed on an entry from June of 2010.

*We’re all (illegible) worried about him. Me, Joey, and Danielle met up without him to talk about it. Danielle said he was getting angrier, and one night almost hit her. She said that (illegible) she tells him he doesn’t need to take more than one pill, he tells her, “It’s the only way I get any sleep.” Joey asked her if Nick had had any bad episodes recently, and she said that she’s gotten so used to it (illegible) that it was difficult to decide which episode was bad or not. I told her to keep doing what the doctors said, to lock the bedroom door and windows and to carefully wake Nick up if he got restless. Me and Joey (illegible) that we’d talk to Nick and promised Danielle that we wouldn’t mention this meeting to him.*

Sleep didn’t help with the decision. As I drank my morning tea and turned on my laptop to start work, my eyes kept wandering over to the box. I tried to put myself into Luke’s shoes. It’s been over a decade at this point; would he still be so distraught over Nick’s death? I didn’t know; I haven’t lost any close friends. The only death I’ve experienced was my grandfather, whom I barely knew. The box became so distracting that I put it in the closet next to the bathroom.

I was finally able to focus. At twelve, I gave myself time to make lunch. While standing at my fridge, trying to decide what to eat, I heard a knock at my door. I’m not the sociable type, so I let some time pass in hopes that whoever it was would go away. But then another knock came, one more rapid than before. I shut the fridge and slowly moved through the kitchen and into the foyer. I went up on my tiptoes and looked out of the front door window.

Standing on my porch was a tall man looking to be about thirty and had short spiky brown hair and pale skin. I knew his face instantly.

It was Nick.

My hand hesitated on the doorknob. I wouldn’t have answered the door if Nick hadn’t seen me and made eye contact. He smiled that sort-of-goofy smile I had seen in so many photographs. I heard his voice through the door.

“Hello! Excuse me!” He called. My hand trembled, but I opened the door.

His clothes had gone out of fashion a decade ago, and his shoes were covered in mud. He smelled faintly like cigarettes and had a tattoo of a skeleton on his right forearm. Pictures flashed in my mind: Nick sitting in a white lawn chair with a cigarette hanging out of his mouth; Nick proudly showing off his fresh ink to the camera.

“Who are you?” I asked, but I already knew the answer.

“My name is Nick,” He replied. “I don’t know how I got here. Can you help me?”

I just stared at him.

“You can’t be,” was all I was able to say. He leaned his hand on the doorframe.

“What do you want, my ID?” He laughed. I nodded instantly, and he shrugged and fished around in his pants pocket. “Look, my friend’s mom lives here. Can I come inside and use the phone?”

I didn’t give him an answer, but instead snatched up the ID he had produced from his wallet. It was expired by over a decade. His birth year was 1980. As I scanned his ID, he cocked his head.

“Hello, did you hear me?” He asked. I handed his ID back to him.

“What year is it?” I asked him bluntly. He laughed, but there was a hint of annoyance in his tone.

“I don’t have time for riddles; my girlfriend is probably worried sick about me.”

“What year is it?”

The man sighed.

“It’s 2010. Can I use your phone now?”

I stood my ground.

“This really isn’t funny,” I told him, ignoring the goosebumps forming on my arms. “It’s actually very disrespectful.”

He was getting impatient. He looked past me into the house, but when he didn’t see what he was looking for, he leaned over to look into the window.

“Look, can you just tell Barbara that Nick is here and he had another sleepwalking incident, and she should call Danielle? Please?”

He didn’t sound like he was lying. He looked exactly like Nick; he knew Danielle’s name. If it wasn’t for the obvious, I would have believed him.

“Barbara doesn’t live here anymore,” I said. “She died about a year ago. Luke sold me this house. I can call him, if you’d like.”

Nick’s brows furrowed.

“No, that’s not right. What are you talking about? I just saw her the other day, and she was fine!”

“She’s not here. I live here now.”

Nick started to pace around the small porch. His eyes were frantic, his mouth twisted into a deep frown. I wasn’t sure what to do.

“I can call Luke,” I told him. He stopped pacing and nodded. I decided to let him inside. He was a skinny guy and I felt that if he tried anything that I could take him. If things got really bad, I had my gun in my nightstand.

Nick sat at the dining room table. I went into my bedroom and fetched the paper with Luke’s number from my desk. The line rang a few times, then someone answered.

“Hello?”

“Luke?” I asked, keeping my voice low.

“Yes, who is this?”

“This is Lucy.”

“Oh, Lucy! How’s the house?”

“Uh, the house is fine. Um, someone is here. He asked for Barbara.”

“Oh shit. Did he give his name or anything?”

I paused, pressing my lips tightly together.

“Hello?” Luke said.

“It’s Nick,” I replied very quickly, my words suddenly flowing from my mouth. “I mean, he looks like Nick. He asked for Barbara to call Danielle. He said he had a sleepwalking incident. He thinks it's 2010. I don’t know what to do.” I was so focused on getting the words out that I hadn’t noticed I was crying.

Luke didn’t answer right away; he just listened silently as I sobbed.

“Lucy, I’m about ten minutes away,” He finally spoke. “I’ll be over as fast as I can. Keep him busy; don’t make him angry.”

“What?” I got no reply other than the call ending.

I sat on my bed for a second, trying to control my breathing. I wiped the tears from my eyes and took a deep breath.

I left the bedroom and returned to the kitchen. Nick was still at the dining room table, but now he was hunched over, and his left hand moved quickly. As I approached, I saw that he was drawing. It was a woman with horns and a forked tongue extended between her pointer and middle finger. It was in the same style as the drawing from the photograph.

“Luke will be about ten minutes,” I said, keeping my distance from him. “Would you like some water?”

“Sure,” he replied, “I hope you don’t mind, but I took my shoes off.”

“Oh, that’s fine.” I handed him a glass of water. “I like your drawing.”

Nick smiled.

“Thanks. Girls don’t usually like my stuff.”

I shrugged and looked away. We sat in awkward silence until Nick cleared his throat.

“Uh, can I ask why you asked me what year it was?” He rested his head on his hand.

I’m not a good liar.

“No reason,” I said, keeping my eyes moving. Nick stared at me with narrow eyes.

“No, really,” He pressed.

I tried to think of something.

“Just making sure you weren’t crazy.” I crossed my arms tightly across my chest. “I’ve had some real nutjobs knock on my door.”

Nick slowly nodded.

“I see,” He said, pointing his finger and swirling it next to his head. “Well, I’m not crazy. Trust me.”

I wished I could trust him. I wished that I were witnessing a miracle, that somehow Nick had escaped death and returned to life as if nothing had happened the night he went missing.

“Can I ask you a question now?” I asked.

“Sure.”

“Sorry if this seems weird, but what’s the last thing you remember before you wound up on my porch?”

Nick waved his hand.

“That’s not weird; I’m so used to that question by now. Well, not that exact question. I sleepwalk, a lot. Okay, uh, last thing I remember. I think I was in my living room. My girlfriend was sick, so I was sleeping on the couch. I remember turning off the TV, then taking some of my meds. And, uh, then I was standing in front of your house.”

“Your girlfriend Danielle?”

“Yeah, do you know her?”

“No, I don’t”

“Oh, shame.”

The awkward silence returned, but then I saw a car pull into the driveway. Luke exited the car and hurried up the steps to the porch. I walked over to the door and opened it before he even had a chance to knock. He looked pale as a ghost; his lip trembled.

“Lucy,” He started, “Is he still here?”

“Yes,” I said, letting him inside.

Luke stomped through the foyer and reared up like he was going to do something, but he stopped dead in his tracks as his eyes landed on Nick.

“Whoa, Luke!” Nick exclaimed, “What’s up with you? You look like shit, man.”

Luke just stood there. I slinked past him and stood by the kitchen island. I wanted an explanation.

Luke suddenly ran up to Nick and grabbed him by the collar and lifted him out of the chair.

“Who the hell are you, and what do you think you’re doing?” He yelled into Nick’s face.

“Hey, chill out, it was just another incident,” Nick explained.

“Who are you?”

I almost plugged my ears with how loud Luke yelled.

“I’m your friend.” Nick’s voice was innocent and soft. Luke slid his hands from Nick’s collar to his shoulders.

“Nick,” Luke whispered, then pulled the other man into a tight hug.

“Seriously, dude, what’s up with you?” Nick asked, his voice muffled.

“The years have been rough.”

Nick looked at me, confused.

“Did you let Danielle know? I’m sure she’s worried.”

Luke let go of Nick.

“Danielle’s gone,” Luke said.

“What?”

“After they found your body, she couldn’t take it. You meant the world to her.”

Nick took a step back, his eyes darting from Luke to me.

“What the hell are you talking about?” He asked. Luke didn’t answer. “What the fuck do you mean, Luke? ‘Found my body ’? I’m right here! This isn’t funny. Danielle’s sick; I promised I would take care of her.”

Luke tried to hold on to Nick, but he pushed his friend’s hands away. He turned his attention to me.

“Are you in on this?” He growled, pointing a finger at me. “What the hell, dude? What’s wrong with you?”

He started towards me, but Luke moved in front of me.

“We’re not messing with you,” He tried. “Nick, just sit back down, and I can explain.”

As Luke tried to grab Nick, Nick swung around and stabbed Luke in the arm with his pencil.

“Get away from me!” Nick screamed, but Luke still tried to grab him. I backed away, feeling lightheaded at the sight of blood trailing down Luke’s arm.

“Nick, please,” Luke pleaded, “Let me explain.”

Nick clutched his head, then placed his hands out in front of him. I couldn’t discern the emotion in his eyes. Without a word, he grabbed Luke’s throat and squeezed. Luke flailed around, trying to make Nick let go. But he didn’t.

I ran into my bedroom and shut the door and locked it. I grabbed the gun from my nightstand and held it in my sweaty hands and fumbled for my phone. I heard thuds and grunting coming from the kitchen, all intermixed with a noise that didn’t sound human. My eyes welled with tears, then my stomach lurched.

My phone was in the kitchen.

A loud crash echoed through the house. I held back the urge to vomit. My breathing quickened. Sweat rolled down my back. I never took my eyes off the door.

“Hey, you in there?” I heard Nick’s voice just outside. He tried the handle. I shut my eyes, begging for it to be over.

“Hey, you in there?” He repeated, “Who was that man? That wasn’t Luke. Why are you doing this to me?” He started pounding on the door; his voice grew louder. “You hear me? Where’s Barbara? Where’s Luke? Where’s Danielle?”

I stifled back sobs as Nick screamed. He gave up forming words and just wailed. Swirling through his voice was something terrible, like an animal on the verge of death. I was afraid to move. As he pounded on the door, I heard metallic creaking and the snapping of wood. His voice grew louder.

“Danielle!” He screamed, sounding as if he was tearing his vocal cords, “Danielle!”

The wood around the doorknob splintered. Then, there was a sharp sound and the door flew open. I didn’t hesitate. I pointed my gun and shot.

Nick fell to the floor, blood pooling around his head. I ran past him, heading straight to the door.

I just kept running. My vision was blurry with tears. I don’t really remember what happened after that. I think I passed out. I was taken to a hospital.

I told the police what happened. They explained over and over that they only found one body in my house. They tried to pin it on me. I kept trying to explain that it wasn’t me; it was Nick!

He was there, I swear to God, he was there. I shot him.

I’ve been here ever since. I’ve been playing nice with them recently so they’d get me what I want. I’m nowhere close to being let out, but they’ve loosened their grip on me.

I got a letter the other day; I think it’s the reason I’ve been able to write this all down. It was sent by a man named Joey. It was a sketchbook. I could tell by the style that it was Nick’s. But there weren’t any ladies with forked tongues within the pages. They were odd drawings: bodies with limbs protruding from places they don’t belong, trees bent and twisted into symbols I didn’t understand, eyes falling to the sky like rain and splattering onto the ground.

I recognize the scenes. Ever since the incident, I’ve started sleepwalking. I don’t sleep much anymore. I don’t want to be in that hellish place and hear those garbled voices screaming at me.

I’m afraid that one day I’ll wake up and be standing outside my house.
