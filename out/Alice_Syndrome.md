My Hell began on our sixteenth birthday. My older twin sister, Alouette, had been writing in her journal like she did every day, humming some song beneath her breath. It was storming outside, and we were alone in the living room. She had wanted to watch a movie, *Saw IV* I think it was? The smaller details escape me, I fear. I was watching it, mostly bored because she hadn't shown me the previous films before, and I didn't care enough to ask. It was nearly midnight, but I was doing my best to be a good sister and stay awake so she could have someone to talk to.

"Hey, Arte," she said during a lull in the movie, "is that a rabbit outside?"

I had turned to look at that...fucking *thing*. Frowning, I told her, much too calmly, "No. It's....Okay, I don't know what it is, but it's not a rabbit, dumbass."

"It looks scared. I'm gonna see if I can get it inside." With that, Alouette was off the couch and heading to the door. I stole another look at it and tilted my head, trying to understand whatever it was. Maybe it was only because I was five seconds from passing out, but it just looked off. It was so white that just a brief glimpse hurt my eyes, and its ears looked more like scraps of flesh that hung by the sides of its misshapen, rabbitoid head. Teeth, bucked and curving outwards, caught the light from its fur, and I realize now that they were chipped and broken at the tips. It was bipedal, its legs nothing but twisted bones, but it was hunched over as if to make us think it wasn't.

"H-Hey, don't be an idiot!" I called after her. "It's probably gonna bite you! It might have rabies!"

She just laughed and went outside.

I don't know why I didn't stop her. To this very day, I have no idea why I didn't get up and drag her back to the couch and shut the curtains. I know I seem like an asshole of a sister, but please, I've had to live knowing I could have done something, a*nything*, to stop this Hell from beginning.

Nowadays, the mental image of the creature makes me lose sleep, but back then, I just felt almost nothing towards it for those seconds. It was only when she shut the door and walked off the edge of the patio did I bolt upright and book it after her, ripping the door open and going outside. I remember my stomach twisting and my heart spiking so much I wondered if I'd have a heart attack. My mouth had gone dry, and I was practically running around my backyard like a racehorse just trying to find her. All I found where the holes our dog Ace dug over the years. I called Alouette's name over and over, growing louder and louder when she wouldn't reply.
