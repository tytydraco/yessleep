“Is this some sort of fucking joke?” He said before grabbing my collar and pinning me to the edge of the house.  
  
“What the fuck is your problem, dude? Let me go!” I shoved him just hard enough for his grip to give out. 

“You waste *my* fucking time? Two hours of work for some prank or something? JOHNATHON, PUSH THE DIRT BACK IN!” he yelled, calling to his colleague who was operating a front loader.

“What are you talking about? WHAT ARE YOU DOING? I already paid you guys! Don't push that dirt back in!” I yelled back. Too little, too late. It took two hours to dig about a fifth of what I needed. I estimated about 10 cubic yards was pushed back into the hole within 5 minutes. All that work, gone.

“We are *leaving,* and if you call my company again, I will cave your fucking head in. *Do you understand me?*” He said, sticking his rigid, callused finger on my sternum. I backed off; he had 3 inches of height and about 80 pounds of weight on me. Honestly, I was surprised I was able to push off his initial collar grab. 

They backed their equipment out, leaving only the remnants of disturbed topsoil and tire tracks. 

“HEY, WHAT DID YOU SEE? DON’T LEAVE! WHAT ABOUT MY MONEY?” I yelled back, only to be met with a middle finger and a wad of cash being thrown out of his truck.

I’ve been trying to dig a hole to install an in-ground swimming pool for some time now. I foolishly thought I could start the digging process by hand; the shovel I purchased barely even scratched the ground before it simply stopped wanting to move. I really wanted this pool to be done by winter; I had plans to install a pretty beefy heater and a nice glass enclosure for it. Unfortunately, fall in Montana this year was far colder than usual.   
  
The first contractor I hired took all the precautions before digging. He got the plans for the house, made calls to the city, and called the local internet providers. No sewers, no foundations, no expensive fiber optic cables; it was a perfect place to put a pool. I negotiated a fairly reasonable price, though there was an upcharge for the time constraint and worse-than-usual dirt conditions. He worked solo, using a small front loader that was similar to the one the second contractor used. He made good progress on his first day and told me he’d be able to finish within the next two days. Unfortunately, I was visiting family in Vermont, so I was out of town after his first day. I paid him upfront and told him to call me if he needed anything. 

When I got back from the trip, I expected to see a pool-sized hole in my backyard- how foolish. I walked through my back gate and saw only disturbed topsoil and a small envelope filled with the cash I paid him on the table. The envelope had pencil writing on the back, letting me know: *do not call me again, do not contact my work, stay away from me.* 

As you know, the second time did not go any smoother. At least the first guy gave me part of the payment back. After the second attempt, I was less worried about getting the pool set up and more curious about what kept driving them away. I went through the permits and housing plans more than three times; there should be absolutely nothing there. Maybe a sewer line setup that wasn’t properly documented? I feel like they would have just told me I had a pipeline there. 

I was going to give it one more shot; I really didn’t have the extra funds or experience to rent my own toploader, so I ended up going back to Craigslist. *Please come dig a pool in my yard. Contact me for details.* 

To anyone who contacted me, I decided it would be best to send them a quick paragraph. *Hi! Thank you for your interest in helping me. I am looking for someone who either owns or knows how to use a front loader. My address is \[----------\], and the price is up for negotiation. Before you accept, I would like you to know that 2 contractors have started digging but randomly quit the job midway. I do not know why this is happening, but at this point I am desperate to know. If you accept this job, I will be hovering over you while you work to see what is going on. I know this is strange, but that's why the price is negotiable.* 

What started as 4 initial messages resulted in 3 ghosts and one acceptance. Two days later, Michael showed up at my house. He had brought his skid steer, which was smaller than the last two machines but able to be towed via a trailer.  Michael seemed nice and was surprisingly understanding of my situation. He requested half upfront and half after, which I was happy to do. 

Work began that day, with Michael setting up outlines and stakes around the would-be hole. He managed to dig the first 2 feet of the pool before we decided to call it a day. Only worked about 6 hours, but it was already 6 hours out of my weekend I wouldn’t get back. The novelty of seeing a big machine tear the ground up only lasts for the first two hours; the rest of it is just the agitating sound of the hydraulics.   
  
Day 2, we decided to start much earlier in the day and try to knock the rest of it out. We both had other jobs during the week and wanted to be done with this side project. The rest of the digging was much faster after getting past the initial permafrost. What was done in an hour before was cut to about 30-40 minutes. I patiently sat in my lawn chair, careful not to get too close to the edge, while Michael continued to carve into the ground. I was confident he had made more progress than the second contractor had. 

At 6 hours in, I was getting ready to start preparing us a late lunch when I heard the sound of the top loader's bucket hit something metallic. Being 40 feet away, I was immediately able to tell that whatever he had hit was *solid* metal.  

“YEAH, SOMETHING'S HERE. GRAB A SHOVEL,” He said over the engine.

I grabbed my recently bought shovel and hopped into the pit, now about 6 feet deep at the end. 

“I would keep using the skid steer, but the first contact already dented part of the bucket.” He said, turning the machine off. 

I walked around him, studying where the end of the bucket met the dirt. A rusty pipe? I looked closer; definitely rusty, but the shape resembled more of a small arch. I dug around it, occasionally hitting the shovel end against the metal. It wasn’t long before I could make out the shape. A human hand. Definitely strange, but perhaps an old, hopefully valuable, statue lies in my backyard. 

Michael joined the dig, using his hands to more delicately remove dirt from the surrounding area.

“Here, found the face.” he pointed to the obvious shape of a nose and lips. He proceeded to use his hands to brush off the remaining dirt. 

“What the fuck… what, how did you do this?” He said, voice trembling. 

I wasn’t going to let this opportunity slip this time. I ran over to look at what he had uncovered. Right below Michael, sitting just in front of his knees, was a perfect replica statue of himself. Even with the caked rust, it was undeniably his face. Michael stood up. “I don’t know what the fuck your problem is, but this is twisted. Look, I am going to take my shit and leave. I am going to call someone right now so I am not left alone with you. Stay in the pit while I leave.”



I wrote this today because I need help. I swear to god I did not make that statue, nor did I bury it. Please, if you have the equipment, come to my backyard and help me dig.
