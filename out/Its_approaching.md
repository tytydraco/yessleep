I think it all started about 6 months ago. That's my best guess anyway. It's the earliest I remember but honestly, it could've been sooner.

The only reason the ‘first time’ sticks out to me is because I’d had a rough day at work. When I got home and got changed after work, I found a use by date sticker stuck to the inside of my work shirt. 

Jen laughed. 

“I’ll need a new man soon”

 I couldn’t even guess where I’d picked it up. I balled it up between my fingers and flung it into the bin, never to be thought of again. 

A week later, there was another one. This time in the lint catcher of the tumble dryer. 

 USE BY  07/08/26.

I didn't think much of it, slightly curious what shop I was going into that were using these, truthfully, pretty useless stickers. 

I travel all over for work, so I don't really have a local supermarket. I’d stop somewhere different most evenings and grab whatever I needed. The big chains didn't use flimsy orange stickers, so I figured it had to be one of the smaller independent shops.  

The next few turned up in boring places. Stuck to a t-shirt. Another on a trouser leg.

I didn't bother keeping count until around number 10.

 This one stuck out to me. It wasn't until late into the evening, after a change of work clothes and a night of watching tele with my girlfriend. I had a shower before bed. As I rinsided shampoo from my hair, I opened my eyes and saw an orange label sliding towards the drain.  Almost instinctively I stopped it with my foot, ending the shower immediately. 

I bent down and picked up the soggy, but still intact and sticky label. I stuck it to the bathroom mirror. I had to, this was by far the strangest one. 

Where the hell did it come from? Surely my girlfriend would've noticed it stuck to my hair. It's not like you can miss them. 

It wasn't until I had 5 stuck on the mirror that she brought it up. 

“It's bad enough you keep showing me them. Do you really need to decorate the bathroom as well?”.

But I did need to. I had to keep count. If they keep turning up, then eventually I'll see the pattern.  

I started keeping a note on my phone. Every shop. Every town. Every day I worked. I knew there had to be a cross over somewhere. 

On a Friday, after working a particularly filthy site, I did what I always did. Tapped my boots together, shook them out and left them outside by the back door. 

Maybe that was my mistake. 

Come Monday morning I picked them up and gave them the same tap and shake. A label fell out. 

07/08/26

On my way to put the label on the mirror, Jen was getting up. I told her where I'd found it. I finally asked her outright. Was it some sort of joke?

“Obviously it's just been there since friday, you're over thinking it”. 

I checked my work boots every morning after that. Then my pockets. Then my lunch bag. It only took a couple of minutes. 

Plus I found one almost every other day. 

Then every day.

I was late for work a couple of times, but I didn't mind. I'd rather miss out on the morning small talk and shit coffee. By then, a couple of the younger contractors had started taking the piss out of my thinning hair. I almost replied. Instead I found myself wondering if there’d be another label in my van.

I'd mentioned the labels to a couple of the other guys to see if they'd found anything. Maybe it was from a tool. But it just gave them more ammunition against me. 

When I went home I tried complaining to Jen, she’d always listened about the work site politics. But for some reason she seemed disinterested in what I had to say. At one point she even agreed with them. Apparently she'd noticed it too. 

“Not just at the front,” she said. “All over”.  

Somehow we ended up arguing about the labels again. She said I talked about them more than I talked to her. 

By the end of it I had gone to bed, scrolling my notes page on my phone.

I'd stopped writing where I'd found them by then. 

There wasn't enough room on the notes page anymore.  
 

I left her downstairs on the phone to her mum. 

That morning I expected to roll over and open my eyes to an angry Jen. 

Instead, on the pillow next to me. A label. I held it between my thumb and finger tip. I examined it more than I had any of the others. It felt different. Too close. 

07/08/26

I could hear Jen making coffee downstairs. I didn't go down.

I went into the bathroom, she was right, the mirror was full. The same date arranged perfectly to face me. My face glowed orange reflecting the colour.

 I stuck it on the toilet shelf. 

It wasn't long before most of the ceramics were covered in orange labels. 

Jen stopped commenting on them.

Almost every day I found at least one little orange reminder. 

I say almost. There was one unfortunate morning when I arrived to work just in time for stale coffee and stale jokes. 

One of the younger pricks spotted it before I did. I felt his grubby fingers pull it off me. It must've blended into my hi vis.

“Look, it’s how long his hair has left”.

I watched his thumb rub across the date. Sweat and dirt dulled the orange, the edges beginning to curl. 

The next thing I remember, 3 people were pulling me off him.

Looking back, I think they were already looking for reasons to get rid of me. Work had slowed down and there were contractors everywhere. The fight just gave them a good one. 

They had fair grounds to get rid of me, but for some reason the manager felt it necessary to mention my grubbyness and my lateness.

Still he gave me 2 weeks notice and basically put me on grunt work. For the next 2 weeks I’d drive around and survey previous sites. Basically barely check they're clean and suitable.

 I should've been worried. He probably saw that as some sort of punishment.

For me it meant a chance to revisit some of my previous shops. Some I hadn't been to since before I started the notes page on my phone. I'd finally have the time to figure out where the labels are coming from. 

Sure the list was long but I basically had 2 weeks paid to myself. 

I'd kept that morning's worn label in my wallet. 

The last 2 weeks flew by. Not because I was enjoying myself. 

But I finally had time to look. 

I spent those 2 weeks revisiting shops and long finished work sites, hoping to find a label that wasn't already stuck to me. 

By the time I got home and Jen was there I had no time to argue with her. When I did see her I managed to appease her, I promised that after the 2 weeks were up, we'd go have dinner and drinks. I'd show her that nothing had changed. I'm still her guy. 

She wanted one more promise. Go to the doctor. Apparently she was starting to worry. She said my skin was turning yellow. 

Probably from all the stress she was causing me, all the coming and going. Always wanting to argue. I couldn't concentrate. 

All I needed was 2 weeks. 

Thursday, 2 days till it's over. The boss was practically letting me do what I wanted. I was having a look around an area I’d worked away in 6 months ago. There was so much work we stayed there weekends.

 I passed a closed shop. A flash of orange caught my eye . I cupped my hands on the window to get a better look inside. There was a roll of orange labels behind the till. Same colour. Same size. Same date. I needed to get in there, get a closer look.

It wasn't long before someone thought I needed to know the shop was closed. As if I couldn't read the sign.

CLOSED  
ON HOLIDAY BACK SATURDAY  
PAPER ROUNDS WILL GO ON AS USUAL

It was a 4 hour drive here and a four hour drive back. But maybe they'd know where the labels come from, I was sure with a bit of research I could find out who they supply near me. 

Friday dragged.

Every couple of minutes I’d check the clock.

By the time I got home I had already decided what time I was leaving in the morning. 

Jen seemed happier that Saturday morning. I told her work needed me in for a few hours. There'd been a problem with yesterday's paperwork. 

“Maybe dinner and drinks could just be drinks?”. I said. 

I set off to the shop. When I got there, I walked in expecting every shelf to be covered in orange labels.

None. 

I walked straight to the counter.

No roll. 

Just an empty space. 

I asked him about the labels. Then I demanded to see inside his stock room.  

For a moment he just stared at me. 

“What labels?”

“Orange ones. Use by stickers”. 

“I saw them”

“Mate… I’ve owned this shop 12 years”

He didn't know what I was talking about. 

No help at all.

Eventually he asked me to leave. 

I looked behind the till into the empty space one last time before leaving. 

I sat in the van for nearly 20 minutes before turning the key. 

I knew I would have to be back for drinks with Jen eventually.

When I got home she was gone. Most of her clothes. Her toiletries. 

Anything that mattered to her. 

She left behind a bitter note, something about not caring anymore, that I'd let myself go. Bad breath and baggy eyes. My greasy hair. 

I lost interest halfway through. 

I folded it without finishing it.

The label on the back of the note was much more interesting. 

That morning I woke up choking.  I sat bolt upright, coughing and retching into my lap.

 There, floating in a puddle of bile and spit, besides one of my teeth.  
Floating perfectly. 

07/08/26

I still had 3 weeks to wait then. I smeared the sticker to the fridge door, fluids ran down from it. 

Over the next 3 days it got worse.

More teeth. More vomiting. More labels. 

Wednesday evening, I felt a scratch on my eye. I already knew what it was.  

The label wasn't the shock.

My skin was yellow, blotched with bruises. My eyes were bloodshot and sunken. I had barely any teeth.

It took me a moment to feel brave enough to get close to the mirror. 

I lifted my eye lid, under it I could see the corner of the label. I thought about leaving it. 

Slowly, hoping I didn't rip it, I pulled at the corner, thick strands of mucus followed it. As it revealed itself. Number by number. 

07/08/26

I stuck it to the fridge door. I could feel my eye throb as the orange seemed to burn into my vision.

Even when I shut my eyes the numbers were there.  

After that they never stopped. More labels, multiples everyday. In clothes, under anything I moved.  

They were coming out of me.

I have no where to stick them.

Reminding me.
