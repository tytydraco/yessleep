I live in a shitty pre-war building in Chicago, the kind where you hear your neighbor cough through the wall. My neighbor is a guy named Daniel. Every single night, for the past few months, I hear his sewing machine going. It's this little whirring sound, feed the fabric, pump the pedal, snip. *Whir-pump-snip. Whir-pump-snip.* Like a metronome that never runs down. I honestly thought it was kind of pleasant at first. Background noise. Better than hearing the guy upstairs beat his cat, anyway.

So the dolls. They started showing up on Daniel's fire escape about six weeks ago. First there was one. Little rag doll, maybe a foot tall, pink cotton face, no features. I laughed when I saw it. Figured his girlfriend was into some weird craft shit or whatever. Then there was a second one. Then a third. Now there's like a dozen of them lined up on that fire escape, all facing the alley, all with those blank pink faces, and I swear to God they've started looking less like crafts and more like they're **waiting for something**.

I ran into Daniel in the hallway a while back and asked about them. Just small talk, you know, ha-ha what's with the lot of puppets. He didn't laugh. He stood there holding his mail and said, "*I make them every night. It takes three hours. The pattern never changes.*"

I asked why they don't have faces.

"*They get their faces later,*" he said. Then he just went inside and closed the door.

I didn't think about it after that. Life goes on. The machine kept going at night and I kept sleeping fine, mostly.

Then Mrs. Alvarez stopped coming out. She's the retired teacher on three, feeds the pigeons every morning like clockwork, always wears this old lavender cardigan. One Tuesday she just didn't show up on the sidewalk. Nobody really noticed except me, and I only noticed because the pigeons were losing their minds on the ledge.

That night the machine ran way later than usual. Like until four. And when I looked out the window the next morning, there was a new doll on the fire escape. Same blank face, same cotton body, but it was wearing a little knitted cardigan, the same washed-out lavender color as the one Mrs. Alvarez wears every day.

*Coincidence, I told myself. People throw out clothes all the time. It's a big old building with a basement full of junk. Whatever.*

But then I started counting the dolls, I don't know why. Nine, ten, eleven, thirteen. And the machine sounded faster, like it was *chewing its way through the wall*.

I stopped looking at the fire escape. I stopped using the bedroom window entirely. I slept on the couch for a few nights with the TV on, which is a thing I haven't done since I was a kid, and honestly it felt fine. It felt normal. Nothing weird happens when you keep your blinds shut and pretend your neighbor isn't up all night sewing human-sized toys.

Then this morning I found a package on my doormat. Brown paper, no stamp, no name, just my apartment number. I figured someone dropped off the mail for the wrong unit. I almost left it there.

I opened it.

Inside was a doll. Finished, proper, dressed in a miniature version of my navy coat — I know it's my coat because it has the same stupid stain on the sleeve from when I spilled coffee on myself in the car last winter. And the hair, they gave it my hair, down to the stupid little cowlick at the temple that I've been made fun of for my entire life.

There was a note tucked into the collar. Small handwriting, like a doctor's:

***Your skin is next. I need one more for the pattern.***

I should probably call the cops. I know I should. But instead I sat on the floor of my hallway holding this creepy doll for about ten minutes, and then I did something really dumb. I cut the doll open. Just to see.

There was a piece of fabric inside the chest. A cuff. Same material as the shirt I'm wearing right now. I don't have any idea how that's possible. I'd been wearing this shirt for two days, it never left my sight. Whatever.

And underneath that, a folded-up piece of yellowed paper with a bunch of names and dates written on it. I recognized a few of them. *Mrs. Alvarez wasn't even at the bottom.*

I counted the names. There are sixteen.

***I'm the seventeenth on the list.***

The machine started up again about twenty minutes ago. It's going faster than I've ever heard it, and it sounds like it's *counting instead of just sewing*.

I looked out the window just now. All of the dolls are facing my side of the building.

**Their faces aren't blank anymore.**
