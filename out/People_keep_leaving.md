It's hard to say exactly when it started. The first time I noticed was Friday, maybe? Could've been Thursday, but it's been so subtle. Each day seems like a natural progression from the last, and maybe that's the worst part. But, I'm getting ahead of myself.

I am a new graduate and I landed a job in a small office a couple of months ago. Everybody is really tight knit; they've been working with each-other for years now, leave early on Fridays to go golfing with each-other... The whole nine yards. They're all very personable, great to work with, and it's overall a very relaxed work environment. Relaxed to the point that, despite being pitched to me as an in-person only sort of job, it is a fairly normal occurrence for my co-workers to take one or two days from home, and nobody really says anything about it. Because I'm new to the office, I've done my best to show up in-person every day to present as a diligent worker, which has led me to kind-of develop a sense of who I might expect to see on a given day.

Which is why I was surprised to see only six people at work on Friday. To further explain: we have maybe seventeen or eighteen people in our office. Having twelve on any given day is about the norm. The only time *six* people show up is if people are trying to stretch out a holiday weekend. It was unusual enough that the guy I share a space with, we'll call him C, bothered asking me if he'd forgotten a day off. But still, it wasn't *so* out of the norm that anybody else made serious comment on it.

Saturday was when I really started to take notice. See, I live in a fairly large apartment complex, and my apartment overlooks both our parking lot, the parking lot of a grocery store next-door, *and* a pretty major roadway. I *hate* both of those parking lots. They are *always* filled to the brim -- to the point where people regularly park illegally in the handicap spaces -- and people start flying through them in frustration when they can't find spots. Dense and dangerous, my dad would say. But, on Saturday morning, when I woke up and looked out the window, maybe half the spaces were empty.

That wasn't really what tipped me off either. As a matter of fact, I was really happy to see it! I was planning to make a trip to the hardware store for some stuff to hang posters and *really* wasn't looking forward to playing duck-duck-goose when I got home. Granted, I wasn't particularly excited for the drive as a whole. The way to that specific store tends to be pretty congested, especially on the weekends, and I am not the most patient person to be put behind the wheel. Lo and behold, I make it to the store and back, *into a very convenient parking spot*, in record time.

It was by this point that I had taken conscious notice. I wouldn't really say I was *concerned*, but I was to the point where I had to start rationalizing. It's a weekend, Summer is over but it's still kind-of warm... Maybe people are trying to fit in one last beach trip? I mean, it's possible. I didn't see a lot of east-bound traffic (I'm situated three or four hours from the east coast), but maybe most people drove over on Friday. *Sure, that checks out.*

A few things happened by chance that finally made me suspicious. They are a bit disjointed, but bear with me. Firstly, I have been keeping my windows open. The weather has gotten a lot nicer lately, and I like to get fresh air when I can. Secondly, I stayed up a bit later than I otherwise would have. A childhood friend of mine recently moved pretty far west, he called me, and I lost track of time. Thirdly, a bit of rain was supposed to come down deep in the evening.

So, the rain comes, and I tell my buddy to hold on while I go to close the windows and my balcony door. And as I go to pull one of the windows shut, I catch sight of the parking lot again. It's not *empty*, but I have never, ever seen so many parking spaces free. I will reiterate: it is like the fucking Thunderdome down there. Frequently, two cars will enter, and neither will leave (but an ambulance might also enter). Even more disconcerting was the lack of street noise. I didn't notice it right away -- presence is much easier to notice than absence -- but if not for the light drizzle, it'd be dead quiet.

It is never quiet. *Never*. Perhaps the single biggest gripe I have with this apartment is that it is facing the street, and the street does not sleep. Car alarms will go off and stay active all through the night. Assholes will redline their engines down the pike just to make sure that everyone knows how big their dick is. Occasionally, half-drunk kids will pull through with their windows down blasting the shittiest hypercore punk-hop ever devised. And yet, now that it was gone, I was deeply uncomfortable.

I tried explaining this to my friend, but he didn't seem all that interested. I get it, it's a very easy thing to explain away, it was very late on my end, and it is really hard to understand that *the vibe* is off if you are not accustomed to *the vibe*. I probably sounded like a paranoid lunatic -- and I would gladly take a world where that truly is the case over the one I now seem to be living in.

See, it's Sunday afternoon now. I can count on one hand the number of cars I've seen on the pike. And I've been watching. The parking lot below me has maybe twenty percent of its capacity filled. I went to the grocery store and it's practically empty. I spoke with the two cashiers and they told me that they haven't seen anything like it since the lockdowns. But it's not like people are just *disappearing*. Cars are gone, and cars don't move on their own. People drive cars, so they've got to be *going somewhere*. Like, did I just miss a memo or something? Where is everybody?

I don't get out much, I'll admit. I don't go to bars or coffee shops or anything like that. And I do my best to avoid the news -- it's all just so bad these days, like a machine made to drive people insane. But, I mean... I guess I'll go looking? There's no way that so many people just decide to skedaddle and nobody says anything about it, right? I don't know.

If you've been experiencing the same thing, please, tell me. And, even better, if you're one of the people about to get up and go to *wherever it is* that everyone seems to be going, clue me in? I don't want to be the only guy to die to some crazy anthrax-prion carpet bombing attempt just because nobody bothered to tell me it was coming. One way or another I'm going in to work tomorrow. I guess we'll see who else shows up. If anything of note happens, I'll be sure to keep you guys posted.
