[***Part I*** ](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1xw5aej/the_silence_between_breaths/)***- Job Opportunity*** 

*Knock Knock Knock*

Three hollow knocks echoed throughout my apartment. My eyes shifted to the door almost on their own. The mail slot slowly opened, and an envelope appeared, then quickly fell to the floor. Hollow footsteps passed by, and the shadow of a man in a hat appeared, cast against my slightly closed blinds, behind the TV. 

Lifting myself slowly, I shuffled over to the envelope, which lay, conveniently, face up. Exhaustion and disappointment washed over me. Stamped on the top of the envelope in bright red letters:

# Eviction Notice

I should have known. 

I picked up the letter and threw it onto the side table next to the coat rack at the front door. After I got fired from my job at a local coffee shop, making ends meet has been a hassle enough, let alone paying rent for my apartment. However, if I could go back in time and throw that iced coffee on that man again, I'd do it again every time. 

"It's not my fault he was a dick," I said under my breath, a habit I've had for as long as I can remember. Before my mom passed, she always had to remind me to keep my thoughts to myself. Now that she's gone, it's gotten harder to remember—words vomiting out of my mouth before I could grab the trash can.

Plopping back onto the couch, I scoured the internet for jobs, anything that could get me off the chopping block of eviction. I always ended up on Indeed, scrolling through the same 20 jobs in my area, looking at the same applications that had been ghosted for who knows how long. Great job, Caleb; you're about to be officially homeless, left to go live back at your deadbeat dad's place. 

I turned the TV back on, trying to get my mind off the fact that I might be homeless in a couple of weeks. Static, the low buzz and crackle of the TV crawling into my brain and itching my brain like a small family of maggots. A sound I've grown to hate more than nails on a chalkboard, nasty. I quickly switched off the TV, threw the remote away, and sat there… motionless. 

Drowning in thought, I went back to searching for anything. I needed money, and I needed it quick. 

—

After a while of doomscrolling, I found myself on 5Miles. A place where people can make a quick buck doing jobs for others, mostly landscaping and other random jobs on occasion. 

Entering my location, within a -mile radius. A job opportunity stuck out like a neon-lit billboard:

# Need Dog Sitter for Weekend Vacation, URGENTLY

Almost instinctively, I applied for the interview and waited. 

After about 30 minutes, I began dozing off on the couch. I glanced at the clock on the wall in the hallway, between my bathroom and bedroom. 

**10:43**

Walking into my room, I began getting ready for bed. I plugged my phone into the outlet behind the nightstand. 

I began to lie down. Silence. 

*Bzz…Bzz*

I sat up slightly and rubbed my eyes. My phone's white light illuminated the pitch-black room. I picked up the phone and squinted. Eyes adjusting: 

5Miles

2 Missed Messages

—

The next morning, I was dressed and headed towards the stranger's home. About a 30-40 minute drive, which made me feel a little better. It wasn't too far in case of emergency.

The drive was relaxing and isolated. Trees lined both sides, bending over the road like a monster's ribcage. 

"In 1 mile, take a left on \_\_\_ street." The voice glitched, as if the road we turned onto wasn't public on Google Maps. Slightly concerned, I continued forward. 

"In 100 feet, your destination will be on the left." 

"Thanks, Google," I said, slightly under my breath. 

As I turned, my car dipped slightly onto the makeshift gravel driveway. As I turned the corner, the house came into view—a beautiful 2-story vintage home, surrounded by trees on all sides. An old, rotting wooden playset stood towards the far end of the front yard near the tree line. The exterior lights kicked on as I drove onto the paved driveway. The closer I got, the more ragged and rotten the house became. Like it aged the closer you got. Nevertheless, the house was gorgeous.

I kicked my feet out onto the driveway and scanned the area around me. A beautiful, isolated home, with houses slightly hidden from view by a thick line of trees. I checked my messages from last night:

"Can you get here tomorrow at 2 pm for an interview?"

"We can discuss payment and house rules before my departure."

I checked the time on my phone. 

**1:58**

Walking up the wooden, creaking stairs, I fixed my hair and knocked. Silence. I began checking my surroundings, double-checking that I had the right house. 

*Bzz…*

1 Missed Message 

Clicking on the notification, I read:

*"Hello Caleb, sorry for the inconvenience. I had to leave early due to family issues. The key to the house is underneath the doormat. Please make yourself feel at home. I recorded an old videotape that explains everything you need to do while I'm gone. Please complete all work with care. Does 2 thousand for the weekend sound good?"*

Slightly creeped out, I replied, "*That sounds great. Have a good trip*."

I tucked my phone back into my pocket, flicked up the doormat, and, sure enough, A silver key lay beneath. I clicked it in and opened the door. Silence. Eerie, unearthly silence. Despite the job offer being labeled "Dog sitter," there was nothing else here, or so I thought. I set my keys on the table next to the front door. I flicked on the lights and walked past the stairs; I noticed old landscape paintings of the house and family photos. One in particular caught my eye like a fish with a barbed hook—an old family photo of a mother and son, strikingly similar to my mom and me. Memories flow through me like a river. 

Before I started crying, at a random person's family photos. I took a deep breath and turned towards the kitchen. It was a beautiful 1980s-style kitchen with wooden cabinets and cupboards from head to toe and white tile flooring. I scanned the room for the video recording mentioned in the text. 

Peering over the half-wall that led into the living room, I spotted it. A VHS tape sat in the middle of the wooden living room table. I started toward the table when something made me stop in my tracks, like liquid metal seeping through my body and hardening in an instant. To my right, down the hall, a head was peering at me from around the corner. As I turned to look, all I saw were the strands of straight black hair vanishing around the corner. Silence.

"It's just the dog," I said, thoughts leaving my mouth without permission. 

Taking a deep breath, I figured it was probably just the tail of whatever dog was in the house I was looking after. It's probably just a shy one; I'll let it warm up to me. Taking a deep breath, I focused on the task at hand. I just need to watch this video. 

After another couple of seconds, the heat in my body slowly turned the metal back into liquid. I began to move once more. I picked up the VHS and looked at the side. Written in Sharpie: 

# House Rules

I walked over to the VHS player beneath the old TV and pushed the recording in. The TV turned on, flickering with black-and-white dots. Static, the crackling, fuzzy sound pierced my ears. Crawling into my brain once more. Maggots. Then-

"Hello there." An unknown voice called out as the video came to a woman sitting on the couch.

"If you are watching this, you have been chosen to watch over the house for a couple of days. By now you have probably gotten all the basic rules of just keeping the house tidy and all the boring stuff from the interview."

"I did not actually," I said, words slipping once more. 

"If, for some reason, the interview never happened. I will leave a note with basic instructions on the fridge. But this video is what to do about the… animal." Her demeanor slightly altered into a more serious tone. "If you couldn't tell already, she's very shy and often doesn't come out during the day, even for me. I've learned that he only comes out during the night. You must stay awake during this time. She's grown a liking to… trying to run away if she thinks that they're alone."

The woman then fell dead silent, breaking her train of thought. She turned her head to listen. Silence. Silence, at least what I could hear. A couple of uninterrupted seconds go by before-

"Anyway. As I said, you must stay awake during the night. The importance of this cannot be overstated. Secondly, there's a bowl in the first-floor laundry room. Feed her before sunset; if she gets hungry, she becomes increasingly violent. Finally, the most important rule: once the sun goes down, you must stay absolutely silent. No matter what, don't talk or run; even breathing can be too loud. I recommend setting alarms so you're never late and finding a place to stay far away from her. If you get too close, being silent is almost impossible." She checked her watch and looked out the window, then continued. "Alongside the note on the fridge, I will leave these 3 rules. Follow them, and the money is yours. I'll add an extra tip for good service. Feel free to sleep in any bed you like, and help yourself to any food left in the fridge. Thank you again. Goodbye."

Before I could even think, the TV shut off abruptly. Silence

"What the fuck even was that?" I stood and surveyed the room. Nothing

What does she mean, be silent and stay away from the dog? All of my instincts were telling me to run, head back, and leave this crazy house all alone. But I stayed. A decision I would later regret for the rest of my life. The money could help me get back on my feet.

I stood up slowly and walked back to the kitchen, keeping a close eye on the hallway. Nothing. I found the note. It hung on the fridge with an old ladybug magnet. 

I pulled my jacket sleeve back and checked my watch. 

**4:32**

Less than an hour till sunset. 

—

I sat on the couch, lost in thought. Silence. Deathly silence. Lifting my sleeve and checking the time: 

**5:02**

I looked out the window; the sun was slowly falling over the distant horizon. Instead of walking out of that house and getting away from whatever lay in front of me, I walked through the hallway, barely lit by the dim hallway light. Peering my head into every room. Nothing

I started to wonder if the figure I saw was all in my head. Freaking myself out, like when you think you see a shadow run by in your peripheral vision. Checking the time again:

**5:05**

5 minutes; I need to move so I can feed this dog before sunset. Whatever that means, I don't wanna find out. I scrambled through the hallway, peering into every room. Nothing.

**5:07**

I scrambled into the last room; it was the laundry room. Finally. A silver food bowl lay on the floor, its inside smudged from previous use. I scrambled to look around for food, anything to feed it. At the very top of the shelf was a bag of dog food. I grabbed it and poured it as fast as I could. I put the bag down and sighed. Checking the time:

**5:09**

Just in time. I began walking out to the hallway, finding the final inch of sunlight before it lay to rest beyond the horizon. 

**5:10**

I walked back toward the living room. I peered into each room as I walked. Suddenly, a sound sent a shiver down my core. Cold enough to re-harden the liquid metal inside my body. Frozen. Behind me, only a few doors down, a slow but loud creak signaled a wooden door opening. Knocking the door against the wall with a hard *thump* 

Don't make a sound. I thought. I controlled my breathing to make less noise. Then —wet smacking, palms against the hardwood floor—heavy, unearthly breathing. Joints and bones popping and cracking with every inching step. I turned, faced towards the creature that lay feet behind me. Covering my mouth, I almost screamed. It was a person, unnaturally skinny, with long dark hair covering most of her body. Crawling on all fours as if she were a spider. She sniffed and panted like a dog, wet and heavy. She scanned the area with her head. I noticed something that shook me to my core. 

Her eyes. Her eyes were sewn shut with stitches. Pulled so tight you could almost see her eyes move. Blood covered around her stitches like it was done unprofessionally. I couldn't breathe, couldn't move, couldn't think. Don't make a sound. She slowly inched further into the laundry room. Her ragged, torn clothes snagged on every corner. She was far enough away. I could breathe, barely, but enough not to make a sound. 

Turning back to the hallway in front of me, I controlled my breathing.

"Fuck” I said. Thoughts leaving my mouth without permission.

Suddenly, with a halt. The creature stopped in its tracks, its breathing growing heavier. Predator-like. 

The creature… it heard me.
