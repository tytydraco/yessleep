I’ve been a journalist for 10 years. Always traveling through Eastern Europe, visiting small towns and villages, looking through the archives, hoping to find something interesting from the past.

Well, today, I found something that I must share with the internet. This is the most disturbing thing I’ve ever read.

It’s a letter from August of 1436, written by a bishop called Kálmán. I tried my best to translate it from latin.

*“To the Most Holy and Blessed Father in Christ and Sovereign Pontiff, by Divine Providence shepherd of the Universal Church, your humble and unworthy servant Kálmán, by the grace of God and the Apostolic See Bishop of Veszprém, offers obedience and kisses of the sacred feet.*

*I’m writing to Your Holiness from a small village named Folt, part of the duchy of Veszprém, and the kingdom of Hungary. I was called here by a young man, and your humble servant, Chaplain Nicholas. I regret to say that he’s the only person who’s still living inside the local church after the tragedy that happened here, resulting in the death of the local priest, Lord Lajos, my dear friend, who died protecting the people of Folt, as he always did.*

*According to Nicholas, these events commenced two weeks ago, when a peasant family residing near the forest at the edge of Folt was murdered. The master of the household, Ferenc, was discovered in his chamber, suspended upside down. A wound had been pierced into his chest, and his heart was missing. His wife and three children were likewise slain, each bearing deep lacerations upon their necks.*

*None knew what had occurred. Their neighbor, an aged woman named Sára, reported that she observed a man of tall stature entering Ferenc's house at nightfall. When she knocked upon the door the following morning to collect the cheese she had requested, no one answered. She returned thrice more during the day, until at last she peered through a rear window and beheld the horrific scene.*

*Following these atrocities, the villagers of Folt united to arrange their burial. Lord Lajos announced an inquiry to apprehend the murderer, declaring that whosoever committed such deeds served the Devil and would face divine and temporal justice.*

*He commanded the erection of a wooden cross, ten feet in height, upon Ferenc’s farm, and painted a white cross upon the door. This act appears to have enraged the murderer, for on the subsequent morning, the entire farm was consumed by fire, together with every structure and the cross itself, which had been inverted. As it had required three men to erect that cross, we surmised the culprit was either possessed of extraordinary strength or assisted by accomplices.*  
*Thereafter, the men of Folt established night watches to guard the village. Lacking proper weaponry, they armed themselves with pitchforks, sickles, hammers, and other implements.*

*After several days, Chaplain Nicholas, Lord Lajos, and two devout men who had volunteered to guard the church were conversing near the altar. Having no other living witnesses, I relay that which Nicholas communicated in his testament.*

*A man entered the church, and the four turned to face him. Nicholas described this individual as a monstrous and unholy creature, resembling the Adversary himself. The man was half-naked, clad only in footwear and leather trousers. A scabbard hung from his girdle, containing a sword. His eyes were dark and malevolent, and his grin was broad, stretching nearly to his ears and filled with small, sharp teeth.*

*His hair was dark, his skin of a reddish-brown hue, and two structures protruded from his back. Wings, perhaps, though Nicholas noted they appeared hideous, akin neither to those of a bird nor a bat, but something monstrous between the two.*

*One of the guards, a valiant young man, charged toward him with his pitchfork. The tall beast, without strain or hesitation, seized the pitchfork, wrested it from the poor man's grasp, and cast it aside. He then drew his sword from its sheath and ran it through the young man's heart. He kicked the body from his blade, casting it across the sanctuary where it struck the wall with great force.*

*The second guard held a sickle and prepared to strike, but the creature was swift, reaching out and plunging its claws into the man's eyes. Drawing them forth, it seized the guard’s lower jaw and tore it from his head. The guard fell to the stones, screaming until death took him.*

*As he advanced past Nicholas, the tall man struck him with his talons, severing poor Nicholas's throat. The young man collapsed, watching the horror from the floor. He remains unable to speak, nor shall he ever speak again, for his throat was irreparably damaged, leaving him able to communicate only through writing.*

*Lord Lajos grabbed his cross necklace, and pointed at the tall man, praying to God to protect his house.*

*The tall creature was filled with wrath. According to Nicholas, his countenance changed, and he instantly swung his blade, severing the hand of Lord Lajos at the wrist as it held the cross.*

*Lord Lajos cried out in agony. The tall man returned his sword to its sheath, stepped past Lajos, inverted the great crucifix behind the altar, and seized Lord Lajos, pinning the unfortunate priest upside down upon the cross by driving the sickle and pitchfork through his arms.*  
*Nicholas lost consciousness shortly thereafter, wherefore the subsequent events remain unknown to us. The villagers discovered them only on the following morning. The creature presumably believed the young chaplain would succumb to his hemorrhage, thus leaving him behind. Poor Nicholas was spared only to lose his voice.*

*I arrived here this day, and regrettably, Nicholas's account was confirmed to be true. I have examined the remains. Poor Lord Lajos…*

*Though my heart aches for the young curate Nicholas, I have nonetheless placed him under guard and in chains within the sacristy. For he alone drew breath while the altar ran red, and his tongue has been taken by the blade, leaving only his trembling hand upon parchment to tell of this demon. God forgive me, Holy Father, but until your Commissioner arrives, I cannot know whether Nicholas is Christ’s lamb who witnessed hell, or the wolf who opened the latch.*

*I address Your Holiness to entreat your aid, for whatever entity wrought this destruction was no creation of God.*

*Your humble servant,*  
*Bishop Kálmán”*
