It’s a rainy Thursday evening in January. I’m driving back home from work on the back roads, the country roads. I take this route as it allows me to stay away from the larger trucks and lorries that frequent the bypass. This way may be slower but it’s definitely more scenic. You get a long stretch of nothing, just pot-holed road with hedgerows, and occasionally a small quaint village. I’ve driven this road a lot. I’ve been driving it for years. I know the twists and turns, where to slow down, what pot-holes will decimate your car tyres. So tonight’s downpour doesn’t faze me so much. I’ll drive this road like I do any night, carefully and comfortably. 

I turn on the radio and tune into the local stations. They’re all currently playing adverts. I switch the radio off. I turn my focus to the road.

I take a small right around a concealed bend and see a tractor in the road. A John Deere model, the classic green colour. Its yellow-ish lights declare its presence in the road. I slow down immediately, taking my foot off the gas, and give the tractor plenty of space. Yes, tractors are slow, and I guess it can be frustrating to be stuck behind one, but the farmers grow our food so all we can do is give them the respect they deserve and give them some space on the road. I whisper to myself “only a fool breaks the two second rule”. A mnemonic my old driving instructor used to tell me to help me remember how much distance to give other drivers. It’s been stuck in my head ever since. 

After five or ten minutes the tractor signals left and turns off into a field through an opening in the hedgerow. I pick up speed again until I hit the speed limit.

I swerved past some road kill. Pheasant, maybe. Usually there are lots of pheasants around here. They are beautiful birds but bred to be a bit simple, easier to shoot that way. 

A blinding white glare reflects into my eyes from my rear-view mirror. For a split second I feel like a heavenly angel has visited me. “Do not be afraid” it says. “I have come to aid you” it continued. It’s not an angelic being at all. A white Audi speeds around the corner with its full-beam headlights turned on. I can feel my eyes squinting and squirming as they try to adjust to their new light source. But within a few seconds the driver turns their full-beams off. It happens. It’s always difficult to know when there is another car around the corner especially in this weather. Your senses are dulled.

It's a white Audi convertible with a black fabric soft top. I cannot tell the make of the model, I’m not a car-person. They are quite close to my rear bumper. The driver in the Audi speeds up on every straight bit of road only to slam the brakes on a bend. “Only a fool breaks the two second rule” I whisper. This continues for about a mile. I don’t let it get to me. The driver clearly wants to overtake me but I am not going to break the speed limit. 

I come to the stretch of the road where the national speed drops to 30mph. It’s due to there being a primary school nearby. Yes it’s late in the evening, no one will be at the school, but speed limits are there for a reason. It’s the moral principle of the thing. But clearly the Audi driver behind me hasn’t taken too kindly to me slowing down. A shuddering roar is let out behind me as the Audi goes speeding past. They barely leave a half a metre distance between them and I. I hastily lurch my car to the left in order to avoid a collision. The stupidity of that driver. The fool. Absolutely no patience whatsoever. And we are driving past a school too. I observe the Audi boom off into the distance and amateurishly drift around the corner. It’s not like this is the first time this has happened to me. You do get impatient drivers from time to time. Disrespectful drivers. Disobedient drivers. I try to shrug it off. I turn on the radio station. Still adverts. Just my luck. For a moment my foot pressed harder on the accelerator than I intended. I eased off immediately and blamed the adrenaline. 

I come to a set of traffic lights at a junction that leads into the city centre, where I live. I notice the Audi up ahead. Three cars between them and I. The traffic lights switch to green. I turn left for home. At least, I mean to. My hands turn the wheel right. I was now following the Audi. What am I doing? I’ve never done this before. It all seems automatic. My muscles are moving themselves, controlling the steering wheel and the pressure on the accelerator, not me. I’m a trapped passenger in my own car, my own body. 

The Audi pulls up to a small cluster of houses and reverses onto a driveway. I instinctively park about five houses down to remain out of sight, I’m concealed enough here. I turn my car engine off and watch a man step out of their Audi. He is dressed in a white button shirt and black formal trousers. The man enters his house through the front door and one-by-one, like daffodils popping out in spring, I see the house lights turn on. I turn on the car radio and wait. More adverts.

I check my phone to see if I have any messages. One new message from my landlord asking me when’s the best time to come round for the yearly inspection. The time on my phone reads 11:28pm. The last light in the house I'm watching turns off.

I reached for the ignition. If I drove away now, none of this would happen. My hand stayed wrapped around the key, but the rest of my body climbed out of the car. I notice the house has a right-side passage so I enter there. I come to a wooden door that separates me from what I assume to be the garden. Surely there must be a padlock on this thing? I stand on my tiptoes and reach over the wooden door and start to feel around. I feel a metal bolt, unhinge it and pull. The wooden door opens. No padlock. Is the Audi driver inviting me in? Maybe he’s just overconfident or maybe it’s a naïve feeling of safety from living in this neighbourhood. 

I walk through to the garden and notice some glass doors at the back of the house that leads into the kitchen. These doors must be locked. They have to be. Who leaves their back door unlocked at night? Without another thought, I reach over and pull down on one of the glass door handles. I feel no resistance in the handle and open the door. The arrogance of this person must bleed out of him. I can almost smell it wafting out of their house. A putrid arrogance that is inviting me in, like a freshly baked apple pie baked by one of those witches in those Grimm-like fairy tales. I step into the kitchen through the open glass door.
