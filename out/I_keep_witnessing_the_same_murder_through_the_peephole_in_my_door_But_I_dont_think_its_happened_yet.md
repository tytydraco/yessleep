Something strange has been happening the last few nights and I feel like maybe I'm losing it.

It started three nights ago. I ordered some food late night, and when I got the text that the delivery guy had left it in the lobby of my building, I checked the peephole of my apartment door before going out. Being a woman in a big city, it's become habit. I've heard too many horror stories of people lurking outside doors, waiting for someone to walk out so they can rob them. Or worse.

The first thing I noticed was how dark it was in the hallway. There was a soft reddish glow, like emergency lights, which was weird because the hallways of my building are brightly lit with harsh overhead lights. They're somewhat off-putting since it's one of those pre-war buildings; one of the only things I hate about living here, besides the ancient elevator that feels like it takes ages to creep up. But when you live on the 8th floor in a city with a ton of walkups, you don't complain about how fast the elevator moves.

The next thing I noticed made my blood run cold. A figure was crouched down in front of the apartment door across the hall. They wore a black hoodie and looked like they were trying to pick the lock of the door using only the light of a flashlight.

I called my landlord Carl immediately. As much as my first instinct was to dial 911, I have only been living here a few weeks so I kept telling myself I was overreacting.

It was strange - the dark hall, the hoodie pulled up, the way they seemed to be looking around to make sure no one was coming. But for all I knew, the neighbors had locked themselves out and this person was just the locksmith.

Carl answered and I apologized for the late hour, but he shrugged it off, saying he'd been dealing with a plumbing emergency anyway. I told him what I was seeing and he told me it didn't make sense but that he was on his way.

I got off the phone and put my eye back to the peephole, watching while I waited. I heard a muffled ringtone - plink-plink-plinkplink-plink-plink--

The figure jerked at the sound and reached into his pocket to silence the phone before taking it out and looking at it. Just as he answered-

KNOCK KNOCK!

I about jumped out of my skin and stepped back, freaked out, completely having forgotten I was waiting on Carl when I heard his voice.

"Sam? It's Carl."

I opened the door to a baffled look from Carl. My own face instantly mirrored his confusion.

The hallway was brightly lit- the lights clearly working just fine. There was no one crouched in the hall and no sign that anyone had just been trying to break into the unit across the hall.

"Did you... see them?" I asked.

Carl shook his head and looked around.

"Just now?"

"I was watching them literally when you knocked."

I didn't see how it was possible that he hadn't seen them. And yet, even I knew that somehow what I'd been seeing couldn't have possibly been real.

I repeated what I'd seen, but Carl's confusion quickly turned into concern, even though he did his best to hide it. He told me even if I had seen someone, it was unlikely they'd be trying to break into the door across the hall because it didn't lead anywhere. Well, technically it did - but it had been sealed off from the inside. After the girl who was living there had moved out, the couple next door had bought the unit and merged the two apartments together to make room for the baby they had been expecting. Rather than have two entrances, they blocked off the one across from me.

I realized in the three weeks since I moved in, I'd never seen anyone coming or going - it didn't even have an apartment number on it - and now I knew why.

I realized my food was still waiting in the lobby downstairs, so I apologized to Carl, who shrugged and chalked it up to being new to an old building.

"The place creeps me out sometimes too. Always the closed off doors that spark the imagination the most," he said, but I could tell he was wondering who the hell he'd leased the place to.

On my way downstairs I was so lost in my own head, I nearly slammed right into my hot neighbor as he stepped off the elevator with groceries in both hands. I muttered an apology and kept going as he greeted Carl and they made small talk, but I made a mental note that we were both night owls so I might have something to bring up next time we ended up on the elevator together.

\*\*\*  
By the following night, I'd all but forgotten the entire thing. Work had been crazy lately, so I had planned on getting to bed earlier than usual.

As I brushed my teeth and turned off the lights in the living room, the front door caught my eye. Specifically the peephole. It was one of the things that I found most charming when I first looked at the place.

Like I said, the building was pre-war, and the apartment doors had these vintage brass peepholes. Most doors have fish eye peepholes these days - but back in the day before fish eye lenses were a thing, the peepholes were moveable. Since the lens only showed you what was directly in front of them, you'd move them up and down and side to side in order to check the area outside your door.

I almost didn't look but curiosity got the best of me. I crept up to it like it might burn me, took a deep breath, and pressed my eye against the peephole.

There it was. A brightly lit, totally normal looking hallway and my neighbors' extra door. I moved the peephole around to check the coast was completely clear, laughing at myself before double checking my lock and heading to bed.

Still, now that the previous night's event was back in my head, I couldn't quit thinking about it. How could I have possibly imagined what I saw? It was too vivid. I've never had an experience like that, not even on the occasional plant medicine I used to enjoy back in college.

I must have gotten up six or seven times to check the peephole before I finally convinced myself I'd imagined the entire thing and settled into bed for real. I was on the cusp of sleep when a thought occurred to me.

Maybe it only happened at a specific time?

I checked my phone. The food had been delivered the night before at 11:17, which meant I'd probably looked through the peephole around 11:20. It was 11:21. So much for that early night of sleep.

I got up, promising myself I'd check one final time and then I'd never think about it again.

I pressed my eye against the peephole, expecting to see the normal hallway again. A chill ran down my spine, the hair on my arms stood straight up.

The hallway was dark, that eerie red glow emanating from somewhere. The figure was back at the door with their flashlight, working on the lock. This time I noticed a loud hum coming from somewhere down the hall.

I heard the same muffled ringtone - plink-plink-plinkplink-plink-plink-- The figure reacted the same way as the night before, jerking a hand to silence it.

I instinctively pushed away from the door. Holy shit. I wasn't crazy. This was real somehow. I kneeled down with my ear to the floor. I could just make out a sliver of light creeping under the door crack. But it didn't look like the light of a flashlight.

I stood and put my eye to the peephole again.

The figure was on the phone speaking in a hushed whisper but I knew for sure now it was a man.

"...you think this won't come back and bite you too, you're crazy. Get over here now and help me with her fucking body."

My mouth went dry. What body. What was he about to do?

I checked under the door again - the sliver of light telling me whatever I was seeing through the peephole couldn't be real. But I had to know for sure.

I steeled myself and gripped the doorknob. Then I yanked it open.

Harsh bright light flooded my apartment from the hallway. The un-numbered door across the way untouched.

I slammed it shut and pressed my eye against the peephole. The man had the door cracked now, and shoved it open, the chain splintering the door frame as it ripped free of the wood.

A woman screamed as the man entered. It was pitch black in the other apartment but moonlight poured through the window on the other side of the apartment and I caught the glimmer of something sharp and shiny in the man's hand.

The adrenaline that flooded my body overwhelmed every rational thought I had. Even though I'd just established whatever I was seeing wasn't what was outside my door, I couldn't help it. I had to do something.

I threw open my door and ran over. I began pounding on the neighbors' door. 

I guess I thought maybe I could somehow stop whatever evil was occurring. That the figure might hear me and stop.

I heard the sound of a door opening as the husband stormed out, rubbing the sleep from his eyes.

"Are you out of your mind?" he said. "Our baby is asleep."

"Someone was breaking in. They were trying to kill her. The woman-"

His look of anger changed to one of disturbance. "If this is some kind of joke, it's not funny."

By this point other neighbors were opening their doors to see what the commotion was about.

As the adrenaline faded and my mind cleared, I realized how insane I must look to them. I stuttered out some apology about how I was mistaken and went back into my apartment. I didn't get much sleep that night.

It felt like I had only just nodded off when a knock on my door awoke me. The apartment was bright with daylight. I hurried to the door to find Carl standing there.

"Hey so the Pattersons told me about last night," he said.

"I know I know, it was a misunderstanding."

"Was it?"

I looked at the clock and winced as I realized I'd overslept.

"Umm, can we talk about this later? I have to get to work."

"As long as you promise not to bother them again, we can just forget about the whole thing."

"Yeah ok," I said, knowing forgetting it was far easier said than done.

\*\*\*

The rest of the day was uneventful for the most part. I was a space cadet at work, zoning out in a meeting when it was my turn to present, sending my boss the wrong document - twice. But I couldn't help it. All I could think about was that I needed to figure out what was going on and fast. Before I completely lost it.

If what I was seeing wasn't literally outside my door, then what was it? Or when? Carl had said there was a girl who lived across the hall. Was it possible she didn't move out like he said? What if she'd been murdered and somehow I was witnessing her final moments over and over again? I was seeing the same thing happen repeatedly, right?

That became my best guess as to what was happening, as crazy as it sounds. That my peephole was somehow functioning as a glimpse into the past. I felt like I owed it to the woman I'd heard scream. I decided I owed it to her to figure out who attacked her. Starting with a better timeline of events.

That night I set up my phone's camera to record myself. I knew for sure whatever happened started around 11:20, so I set it up at 11:10 and waited with my eye pressed to the door.

My plan was to dictate everything I saw. Maybe I'd find some clue as to who the woman was or who the figure was or when this all took place.

The hallway looked normal at first, just like the night before. Then at 11:18 the lights flickered. Moments later, they went completely out.

The hallway got eerily silent. It was pitch black. After a few seconds I heard a buzzing sound and then a loud clank and that same hum I'd heard the night before as the darkness softened just a bit in a red glow.

At 11:19 the hooded figure walked up and I let out a tiny shriek as they seemed to look right at me. I couldn't make out their face in the darkness, only a soft red reflection in their dark eyes.

As they crouched down, I struggled to track them - the hallway was too dark. Then they turned on a tiny flashlight. The light illuminated them just enough for me to watch their back as they went to work on the door. From what I could tell, they had the flashlight between their teeth to use both hands to work on the lock. At one point, the flashlight must have slipped, and as the beam of light angled up, I caught sight of a number on the door.

18.

I realized I'd had it wrong. When was only half of the mystery. But now I knew where.

Even after three weeks, I'd already met Jess several times. She was a sweet mousy actress who lived alone in apartment 18 seven floors below me. I don't know if our postal worker was dyslexic or simply always in a hurry because we were constantly getting each other's mail since I was in Apartment 81. We kept saying we should make plans to grab a drink and get to know each other.

I still didn't know when, but I realized it couldn't have been in the past because I'd just seen Jess that morning as I raced out the door for work.

At 11:23 I heard the muffled ringtone - plink-plink-plinkplink-plink-plink--

I watched him jerk and let out a tight hiss as he silenced it before removing it to answer.

"Where the fuck are you? I said to get over here as fast as you can-- No I'm not fucking joking. She knows about Veronica-- I don't fucking know how-- No of course I didn't tell anyone-- I'm taking care of it. Just get the fuck over here. If you think this won't come back and bite you too, you're crazy. Get over here now and help me with her fucking body."

By 11:25 he'd picked the lock. He shoved against the door, the chain splintering the door frame as Jess screamed.

Despite the dark apartment, I could see their shapes silhouetted in the light from outside the window. I saw the gleam of light on the knife.

And when I heard the first sickening sound of blade piercing her flesh, I screamed. My own voice matching hers.

My stomach sank as I realized that while this wasn't in the past, there was no way to know whether it was happening right now.

I scrambled to my phone and dialed 911.

\*\*\*

The cops got there fast. A patrol must have been in the area because by the time I had given up on waiting for the elevator and raced down seven flights of stairs, they were already inside with Carl trailing behind them.

I stood outside Jess's door, my heart thudding in my chest. Carl eyed me warily.

Jess's door was closed, and I started to get nervous as I looked at the door frame for signs of forced entry.

The two officers banged on the door, ready to burst in if need be. A few seconds later it swung open and a terrified Jess stood there, soaked in blood.

One of them pushed past her with his gun raised.

"Where is the intruder?"

The second cop grabbed Jess, pulling her to the safety of the hall while checking her for wounds.

Jess's terror turned to confusion as she asked, "What intruder?"

The officer looking Jess over called out to his partner, "It's fake."

\*\*\*

They still made sure to search the apartment but found no signs anyone had been inside but Jess.

As everyone settled down, Jess explained she works nights at a haunted house nearby for the months leading up to Halloween.

I tried showing the cops the footage on my phone but all they saw was a woman standing at the door, looking through the peephole, recounting something only she could see. The more I tried to explain myself, the more they looked at me like I was insane.

Carl apologized profusely to the officers for wasting their time and to Jess for the frightening intrusion. He told them about my outburst the night before, and the police suggested I had seen Jess in her "costume" and the combination of stress had manifested as some hallucination. I have to admit that was about as polite a way as they could have put it all things considered. I suspected what they were actually thinking was "batshit". They probably had a good laugh about it later.

As they left, Carl refused to even make eye contact with me. Jess seemed a little more sympathetic, probably because she knew what it was like to live alone in a city as a woman. But I could tell she wasn't thinking about grabbing drinks with me anytime soon anymore.

\*\*\*

I've lost all credibility with anyone who will listen to me, which is why I decided to write the events of the last few days down. Because either they will fade away, nothing more than a haunting memory, or they will come true and I need to know I wasn't crazy. To be honest, I hope I am.

I have decided I won't go home tonight. I'm meeting up with a friend after work. I hope this is the end of the whole saga.

\*\*\*

Oh fuck. Fuck fuck fuck.

I was so wrong.

About all of it.

But especially about it being the end.

I don't have much time to write this. I've been adding to this post in my notes whenever I've had a second tonight. 

I really was planning to avoid going home tonight.

I wish I'd stuck to the plan.

I met my friend after work. Told her about the last few days. She thought I was just stressed too. Said the cops' explanation made sense. My mind playing tricks on me.

The lights flickered in the bar. An alert on my phone said a record heat wave was stressing the power grid and causing rolling blackouts. 

Suddenly the lights went completely out. As the bar erupted in cheers, the emergency lights turned on and cast an eerie glow over the bar. 

I felt sick as I realized tonight was the night. 

I booked it out of there.

I couldn't get the image of Jess being murdered out of my head.

If no one else believed me, then there was no one else who could protect her.

I tried calling Carl even though I knew he was over the whole thing, but my phone couldn't get through. Too many people making calls at once.

Half the blocks between the bar and my building were already dark. Police sirens chirped from all directions as people took advantage of the situation.

I got back to my building a little before eleven, relieved that the lights were still on. 

Fried nerves had me convinced I needed to carry a knife, so I’d stuffed one in my bag that morning before work. I went straight to Jess’s floor, planning to stay until 11:25. In retrospect, pacing outside the apartment of a woman I’d just told the police was going to be stabbed while clutching a knife was about the worst thing I could have done. Jess spotted me and called Carl. He showed up a few minutes later, carefully took the knife from me and marched me downstairs. 

Carl told me Jess was terrified of me and that if I went near her again he’d call the police himself. He said he’d let me out of my lease without penalty because the building clearly wasn’t good for me. He mentioned he’d thought “fixing the whole door thing” would put an end to this, but we were clearly past that. 

I begged him to listen to me but he was done with the whole thing. He told me to get some sleep and pack my shit first thing in the morning. 

He started to walk away. 

"What about Veronica?" I asked. 

That stopped him. 

He admitted a Veronica had lived in the building recently, but insisted she’d simply skipped out owing rent. 

“How could I have heard her name if what I saw wasn’t real?” 

“I don’t know how you heard her name, Sam.” 

I was shouting by then. “He killed her and now he’s going to kill Jess! You have to listen to me. He killed Veronica!” 

Carl ignored me as he walked away. I stormed towards the elevator, colliding into my hot neighbor as he walked in the building entrance. 

He steadied me and I felt embarrassed. I didn’t know how much he’d heard - probably enough to think I was insane. 

We got on the elevator together. I was so in my head about what I was going to do - how I was going to stop this that I barely registered his question. 

"So uh, how do you know Veronica?" he asked.

"Huh? I uhh. She used to live here right? What was she like?"

"She was kind of a bitch. Did Jess tell you about her? What did she say"

I looked at him. He was smiling at me but only with his mouth. His eyes were prying.

"Oh, no... I heard someone else--"

\-plink-plink-plinkplink-plink-plink--

His hand jerked to his pocket as he silenced his phone.

"Stupid fucking phone. Just got it and I swear I keep turning it to silent."

My mouth went dry as I noticed his black hoodie, finally recognizing that same tone in his voice that I’d heard as I watched him through my peephole the last few nights. 

The elevator lurched to a stop at our floor. 

He put his arm over the door and waited for me to squeeze past him. 

I walked slowly to my place as the lights in the hallway flickered, eyeing the stairs at the end of the hall as he kept pace a few feet behind me.

I stopped at my door. He passed by, stopping at his own apartment a few doors past mine, blocking my path to the stairs. His keys clinked as he stalled, waiting for me to go inside my place first. 

I considered turning back to the elevator, but I heard it hiss as it went down. It would take too long to call back up, and he could easily follow me if I changed course all of a sudden now. 

I wondered if I somehow shifted things. *Did I tip him off? Would he change plans, wait for a better opportunity to kill Jess?* 

I was so intent on getting into my place and figuring out how I was going to stop him from hurting Jess that it wasn't until I walked inside and started to close my door that Carl's words came back to me.

He said he thought “fixing the door” would help me but I’d had no idea what he meant. 

Until now. 

Carl must have thought if I couldn't see the door through my peephole, I'd stop "imagining" things.

So today while I was at work he'd covered it up. With a floor to ceiling mirror.

When I'd looked through the peephole and seen the murder, I'd never noticed the lock he was picking was on the wrong side of the door.

I locked myself in and peered through the peephole.

The door I'd been watching him pick wasn't Apartment 18.

It was 81 in reverse.

My apartment, reflected in the mirror.

The lights flickered and went out.

An eerie silence filled the building before the emergency lights hummed to life, casting a red glow over the hallway.

I tried texting Carl, but it won’t go through. The network is still overloaded.

I called the police.

They're on the way, but they warned me that all patrol units are busy so it might take a minute. They told me to go somewhere safe.

But there is nowhere safe.

And I don't think they'll make it soon enough.

I spent what little time I had left typing up the rest of this so there’s at least a record of what happened somewhere. 

If the future is set in stone, I have maybe a minute left to live.

If not, I will update.

But if you don't hear from me, keep an eye on the news.

Who knows how many people go missing in this city without anyone ever hearing about it.

But if you do, and there's a number to call, tell them about this post.
