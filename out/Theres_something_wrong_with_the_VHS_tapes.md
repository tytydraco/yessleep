I took the job because who wouldn't? Twenty-five bucks an hour in this dead-end part of the world? I could live like a king. Or at least like someone who could finally make his minimum student loan payments and have a little extra cash for DoorDash.   
  
The job was posted online. Some random forum. I can't find the post anymore. I can't find any evidence it ever existed. But it was there, I swear. Short and sweet: *Vintage Video Store Seeks Cashier for Night Shift. $25 an hour. Discretion is a must.* There was a phone number to call.   
  
The number is no longer in service, whatever that means.

There wasn't an interview. I called the number, and a man with a shaky, raspy voice asked if I wanted the job. I said the first thing that came to mind, which was "yes."

"Are you sure?" he asked.

Again, I said yes.

"Discretion is a must," he repeated.

He gave me the details, and I thought it had to be a joke. I was to show up every night at midnight. Not a minute before or after. The guy said there could be trouble if I got the timing wrong. The store would be empty, the previous clerk having already left. We were never supposed to meet. I'd go inside, stand behind the counter, and answer the phone if it rang. It didn't matter if I could understand what the caller was saying. I just had to answer. And from there I was just supposed to hang around.

The videos, he explained, were special. They weren't known movies but rare collectibles. The only customers would be people who had arranged to come and had already paid. They would enter, select their movie, and leave. My job, mainly, was to make sure that no one entered who wasn't supposed to be there.

"How can I tell?" I asked.

"You'll know who a real customer is," the man said. "They know their way around the store."

He warned me not to wander the back shelves. Said it was bigger back there than it looked, and finding things could take longer than I could imagine. He also said I could pop a video or two into the VCR to pass the time, but he didn't recommend it. After all this, he asked if I still wanted the job.

I'm broke. I'm hopeless. I said yes.

I never met the owner. He said they already had my information, and the money started getting deposited into my bank account after my first shift.

This is how I found myself at Timeless Video.

The store was in what I thought was an abandoned strip mall outside of town. Seriously, the light poles in the parking lot were broken and falling over. Grass pushed through the cracked pavement. Storefronts were boarded up, windows broken, graffiti covering the walls. Every entrance was dark except for one.

Timeless Video. It looked like a local video store, the kind that was all the rage in the '90s. VHS tapes packed the shelves and display cases, all illuminated by flickering fluorescent lights. There was no one there, just like the owner had said.

A bell rang as I entered. The sound echoed throughout the store, longer than I thought it should have. I stood in the main room, but the store continued off to the right, extending into other rooms, each packed with shelves and tapes.

I walked over to the nearest display rack. No pictures. No advertisements. Just VHS tapes slipped into generic boxes, like they'd been recorded by someone at home. A white sticker labeled each video, but there was no clear rhyme, reason, or theme.

The first said "Jeanette Wilson, 1937." The next, "Death (N.D.)." The was written in a series of characters I didn't recognize. In fact, most of the shelf was labeled in a language I'd never seen before. The writing looked less like letters and more like symbols.

I stepped behind the counter. There was a VCR hooked up to an old CRT television playing static. An old rotary phone sat beside it. There was no cash register. A door labeled "BASEMENT - DO NOT ENTER" was the only other thing behind the counter.

The man on the phone had told me never to go inside, but that sometimes I could expect someone to come out of the basement. He said they would be delivering videos.

The first night was boring. No one came. Why would they? It was the middle of the night on a weekday. I wanted to investigate the shelves or even watch one of the videos, but I was too skeeved out to wander around.

The only thing that happened was the phone call. It came three hours into my shift, stirring me from the edge of sleep. I fumbled for the phone, lifted the receiver, and said hello.

There was noise on the other end. That's the best way to describe it. Like hissing or a garbled choking sound. Maybe someone's voice deeply distorted by static. But there was a rhythm to it, a cadence that suggested it was intentional.

I kept saying hello, then told them I couldn't hear what they were saying. They continued, gurgling and whispering, the sound rising in volume. And almost, just for a second, I thought I could understand them. I felt like I'd always known them. Like they'd always been there, right behind my eyeballs, watching and listening and waiting for me to notice. The sound grew louder. Someone on the other end screamed. Then the line went dead.

I shook my head and set the receiver back in its cradle. I thought the call had lasted a minute or two. But when I looked at the clock, twenty-seven minutes had passed. That didn't make sense. I'd been awake the whole time. At least, I thought I had. Maybe I'd drifted off after the call ended. That was the only explanation I could come up with.

Nothing else happened the first night. I went home and slept, perhaps more deeply than I ever had in my life.

The next day, I had a direct deposit in my bank account for the hours worked.

So I went back the next night and met my first customer.

He came in twenty minutes after my shift began. Tall, unusually so, but hunched over like he had a medical condition. The man wore an oversized trench coat even though it was July, and an old-fashioned hat was pulled forward to hide his face. He wore a handkerchief or something similar over the lower half of his face, and his skin was...off. Clammy. Gray. Malnourished. I wondered if the guy was dying.

I hadn't seen a car pull up, so it seemed like the dude had just stepped out of the night.

"H-hello, w-welcome to Timeless Video," I said, feeling like an idiot. No one had told me to say this, but I didn't know what else to do.

The man raised a gloved hand and waved me off.

"Nice weather we're having, right, friend?" he said in a stilted, gasping voice, like he could barely speak.

He marched to the right and immediately found the shelf he was looking for. He snatched a tape off the shelf and furiously tucked it into his trench coat, wheezing or laughing as he did.

"Can I..." I began, but the man was already moving.

He turned, kept his head down, and hurried toward the front of the store.

"Yes, they watch," he said before stepping out into the night.

"What the fuck?" I muttered.

On one hand, it was creepy. On the other, I was getting the hang of the job.

The only other thing that happened that night was another phone call. The voice on the other end was a woman's this time, and she said a single word in a singsong tone before hanging up.

"For-ev-er."

The money was deposited into my account the next day.

Day three brought no customers, but I thought I heard sounds coming from the basement. Rattling. Thumping. Some type of groan, plumbing or otherwise. It grew so loud I thought I could feel the floor vibrating.

A few hours into my shift, I took a stroll.

I walked back through the racks and into another room. The VHS labels were mostly the same. "Past Lover's Thoughts (Ongoing)," "Your Summer 1993," "John Aiken (1913–1944)," as well as the nonsense language. The vast majority of the tapes were labeled with those strange symbols.

I took a turn, and the shelves seemed closer together. I kept walking, took another turn, and they appeared closer still. I was surprised I'd gone this far back and taken so many turns. The store hadn't seemed this large. Another turn, and the shelves were so close together I almost had to walk sideways. I passed a rack I swear I'd seen earlier. I remembered one VHS in particular, labeled "Been Here (Ongoing)."

I kept going.

One minute turned to two. Then five. Then ten. I tried to trace my way back, but everything looked the same. Time started feeling funny, too. I felt as if I'd already been here. Or that I'd never left. Some of the rows had dead ends now, and on my way back, something strange happened.

I heard a noise, a shuffling sound like someone else was walking among the rows.

I called out, "Welcome to Timeless Video" because I'm an idiot and could think of nothing else. I asked who was there, but no one answered. Finally, I heard a rattling sound coming from a nearby shelf. I turned just as a VHS tape fell to the floor with a clatter.

I walked over and picked it up. The label said "Present (N.D.)." I held it in my hand. The tape weighed more than it should have. It also had a pull to it.

I *wanted* to watch it.

I *had* to.

So I found my way back.

It took another twenty minutes or so. The shelves and pathways kept changing. I laughed. It was like they were playing a game with me.

But I knew the shelves, and the shelves knew me. It really was like I'd been there for years.

I popped the VHS into the VCR and an image filled the staticky screen. 

It was me. This was no hyperbole. The image on the screen was me, standing behind the counter, watching myself on the television screen. 

"Pfft," I said. It had to be a gag. Security footage perhaps. I glanced around but could see no camera. Hidden then. 

But...how was it triggered by the video?

I clicked fast forward and nothing happened. I simply kept seeing myself holding the button and watching the television. After I let it go I was surprised to see that fifteen minutes had gone by. 

That couldn't be right. 

I shook my head and ejected the video. I placed it back in its case and put it on the counter. I walked over and grabbed a tape randomly, this one labeled with the strange characters, and popped it into the VHS. 

Darkness filled the screen. A blur or blotch. Like a shadow consuming. It had to be a glitch. An error from the damaged tape but...

It moved. 

The black blob, it moved, and shifted, and it was just patchy enough with two centered spots of white that were like...

Eyes. 

They blinked. The shadow-thing watched. It grew. Moving closer. Closer. Closer. Taking up more of the screen. 

"What?" I mumbled. 

The sound. Low and piercing, a hum like I had never heard before. It came from the video. It came from the inside of my mind. It sounded like the hum of the machinery at the center of everything. This thought just came, fully formed into my mind, dropped off like a gift. 

The thing watched and moved closer. The television shifted as it thumped against the glass. 

I slapped at the VCR, ejecting the tape. I stuffed it in its case before throwing it toward the shelves. 

It took me awhile to steady my breathing and calm myself. I was just getting creeped out, that was all. The first video was some type of gag and the second, just a strange horror film, something old and foreign. Yeah, that was it. 

Time seemed to pass slowly and I couldn't help it. Part of me felt I *had to* watch more. It was my video store after all. I had a duty, in some ways. 

The next tape was called Alexander, June 11th 2004 10:01-11:57pm. The images on the screen were simply flashes of colors. Bright bursts like lightning or fireworks. The audio was what sounded like a teenage boy narrating stream of conscious thoughts. Literally, the audio just rambled, from him talking about what Jessica thought of him, to the argument with mom, to tryouts and back to Jessica, and the shame about his sexual thoughts about her, and if he wanted to get another round of gaming in before he went to sleep...

It just went on like this. Thoughts and bursts of colors, no pattern ever the same. 

"What the fuck?" I whispered before shutting this one off, mostly out of confusion and boredom. 

The next day the money was deposited to my account. And I've kept going to the job. And going. And going. And going. More customers have come. They don't say much. The voices on the other end of the phone have become somewhat familiar, even comforting. And the videos, I think I've actually found a few gems. 

My shift starts in a few hours. I'll provide an update. The only thing that continues to bother me is the scratching that comes from the other side of the basement door. That and the chanting on one of the videos, the one that makes the television bleed. But for twenty-five an hour...the job is pretty chill...and they need me...

The store has always needed me. 

I'll report back soon.
