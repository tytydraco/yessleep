“Ooh, watch out!” Jackie warned me, sprawling an arm out in front of my chest, stopping me in my tracks.

At the same time, a small black cat jumped out from some bushes in front of us. The animal let out a nasty hiss as it scurried across the sidewalk to the other side of the street, disappearing somewhere beneath the underbrush and into the night.

Wrapping her arms tightly around mine and pulling me close, my girlfriend turned to stare at me with a deadpan expression, “You’re going to die now, Brian.”

I let out a breath and chuckled, playfully shrugging her off. “I thought a black cat crossing your path meant bad luck, not death?”

Jackie dropped the creepy act and smiled up at me with her big brown eyes and alluring dimples, “Actually, I think it’s both. Something about them laying on sick people’s beds in medieval times meant they were going to die or whatever. The bad luck thing is because of witches.”

I nodded in understanding of her fun fact, taking her hand in mine, interlacing our fingers tightly together. “Oh, I see.”

“Eh, who cares? It’s all a bunch of malarkey anyways,” she said with a shrug, waving it off.

As the two of us continued on with our nightly stroll, I couldn’t help but notice my heart was still pounding in my chest. My nerves were shot and I was still a bit frightened from the sudden encounter- fresh goosebumps still lingered on my skin. I tossed a glance back behind my shoulder, somewhat expecting to see the cat again. 

I gulped hard, subconsciously squeezing Jackie’s hand. A pit formed at the bottom of my stomach, leaving me feeling an impending sense of doom. 

“Yeah… malarkey.”

When we returned from our walk, Jackie and I were greeted with an owl perched on the roof of our porch, sitting above the front door. It was massive, its feathers a deep shade of brown with white and grey specks all over. It perked up with our arrival, standing on its legs and twisting its head sideways, gazing down at us with curious wide yellow eyes.

It was an odd sight. I usually never saw great  horned owls in this part of town. Maybe hear them hoot once or twice early in the morning, but that’s about it.

“Awww, look at him,” Jackie cooed, gushing over the creature, “he’s so cute!”

“He better not poop up there,” I warned, giving it a side eye. Seemingly understanding me and taking the hint, the bird of prey fluttered its wings and flew away.

“Dang it! He took off before I could snap a picture,” Jackie pouted.

“Oh well,” I said while sticking my key in and unlocking the front door, hurriedly stepping inside, trying my best to ignore the unsettled feeling in my gut.

\*\*\*

The sound of a sharp breath drew my attention off the morning paper and my breakfast of French toast, bacon, and eggs over to Jackie.

She had paused mid-step in the middle of the dining room, sucking her tongue between her teeth. Her long brown hair was tied up in a high ponytail. She wore a cropped purple tank top and a simple pair of black leggings. She held her white running shoes in her hand with her gym bag slung over her shoulder.

“Huh. That’s odd.”

“What is?” I asked, folding the newspaper down and setting it aside on the table. I leaned back in my chair, enjoying the comfort of my pajamas (a baggy old t-shirt and basketball shorts), running a hand through my short blonde hair.

“The grandfather clock, Brian. Don’t you hear it? It stopped ticking.”

I took a moment and looked over the mahogany tall-case clock. It looked completely normal despite the thin layer of dust covering it. I glanced at the clockface and examined it further. The time was stuck at 7:06 am. The clock had only stopped a couple minutes before. That’s probably why I hadn’t fully registered that the rhythmic ticking had stopped until Jackie had pointed it out.

I grabbed my newspaper and unfolded it, going back to reading the sports section. “Sucks. Must be broken. The thing was old, it was bound to happen at some point.”

Jackie grimaced, lamenting over it. That grandfather clock had belonged to her dad and he passed it down to her when we moved in together. Her grandfather had passed it down to him many years before. “I hear they can be really expensive to fix. Like a couple hundred to a couple thousand.” 

“Yay, another expense to save up for!” I cheered, sarcastically. In all reality, I knew how much the clock meant to her and would more than likely end up getting it fixed up and detailed for her birthday coming up.

“Well, I’m off babe,” Jackie said, rolling her eyes. “I’ll tinker around with it when I get home from work later.”

I sent her off with an air kiss as she waved at me, “Bye, love you. Have a good day.”

“Love you, bye,” Jackie replied, taking her leave.

Once she was gone and I was all alone in our house, I cleared my throat and peeked above the newspaper, eyeing the clock suspiciously. Something about it just felt *off.*

\*\*\* 

“What are you doing?”

I jumped back from my computer, almost shrieking like a little girl after Jackie whispered in my ear. I hadn’t heard her come home for the evening.

She laughed to herself as I calmed down, just tickled that she got the jump on me. Jackie opened her mouth to say something, but was interrupted by someone knocking on the door. The knocks were short, evenly spaced out, and came in a single set of threes. Soft, yet punctual.

“I’ll get it,” she informed me, taking off before I could even do anything.

“Who was that?” I asked when Jackie returned shortly after with a confused expression on her face.

She answered with a shrug, “Nobody.”

“Nobody?”

“Yeah,” she informed me, “When I answered the door there was no one out there. Must’ve been an Amazon delivery person that realized they had the wrong address. Anyway, going back to my previous question before I was so rudely interrupted: What are you up to? What is all this?”

Jackie was referring to the mess I had made at the kitchen table. My laptop was open with a small stack of papers spread around it. They were a couple articles and pictures I had printed out. She then motioned to the notepad and pen I still held in my hand. “Brian, you’re taking notes,” Jackie scoffed. “What are we, back in high school?”

“Something about what you said last night,” I said, setting my notes down to look at her, “with the black cat? It intrigued me. I just started so there isn’t much, but I’ve been researching death omens.”

“Death omens,” Jackie repeated slowly, as if feeling the words out on her tongue, “*right.”*

“The owl. Last night,” I pointed out, frenziedly grabbing her wrist- stopping her from walking away and pleading my case. “It’s thought that if an owl is seen perched on the roof or above someone’s door they’re about to die.”

“You know the term dumb blonde doesn’t apply only to women, right? This is stupid,” She groaned, shaking her head.

“Jackie, I’m being serious,” I said with a sigh, breaking out my puppy-dog eyes. “Hear me out,” I begged.

She crossed her arms into her chest as she sat down next to me. With a huff, she breathed, “Okay. Only because I can’t say no to those blue eyes. Or those freckles. Continue.”

I searched through the papers on the table, grabbing a snip-bit from an old journal I found online, handing it over for her to see. The text surrounded an old-timey picture of a grandfather clock. “A clock stops without reason (that is, it breaks) or a broken clock suddenly starts up again,” I read aloud from my notes.

Jackie still didn’t look convinced, “Brian-”

“The grandfather clock broke this morning, babe. Right after the black cat and the owl from last night. All three of those are omens, signs death is coming. I mean, what are the chances of those things happening together in the span of a day and a half?”

Gently, Jackie slid her hand over to my laptop and closed it. With her thumb and forefinger, she grabbed my chin and tilted it slightly down until we made eye contact, “You’re getting yourself worked up over nothing. Everything is fine. It’s all a coincidence, Brian.”

“Okay, but,” I scrambled through the organized chaos of my research, “did you know cracked bread is one? While baking, if the load cracked at the top this was said to be a death omen in the USA.” 

I slid my pen down one of the pages in my notebook looking for more examples, turning it around and tapping the end against the pad where the next omen was circled in red pen.  “Doppelgangers?” She asked, raising an eyebrow in disbelief. But I knew Jackie. There was a hidden undertone to her words. Her interest was piqued. 

At the risk of sounding like a crazed conspiracy theorist, I replied, “Yes. In German folklore a doppelganger is the idea of a spectral double of a living person. If you were to see an exact copy of yourself or someone else, it was a sign that death was imminent.”

Desperate to keep her attention, I frantically flipped through the pages of my notes until I came across another omen, The Black Ambulance. “The origins date back to the 1980s in Czechoslovakia, Romania, and other countries in Eastern Europe,” I explained to Jackie, her skin increasingly going pale. “Rumors spread of an unmarked black ambulance with tinted windows roaming the street at night. If you were to see it, you would never be seen again. Abducted and killed for your organs to be sold on the black market.”

I reached across the table and threw down the photo of an insidious insect in front of us. “O-or in the Victorian era when someone was gravely ill, they would hold quiet vigils at night next to the person's bedside called the “death watch.” Because it was so quiet in the home, the ambient noises amplified. Sometimes soft taps could be heard ominously echoing throughout the halls, often in sequences of three or more. These taps eerily sounded like a tock clicking, and if you heard them, that meant time was ticking down to the sick person’s final moments. That’s where the Deathwatch Beetle gets its name. The taps? It’s their mating call. They’re butting their heads against the wood.”

I chuckled nervously at her lack of response. “Those last ones were just for fun.”

“Interesting,” was all she could say. Jackie was no longer looking at me. Her gaze was far off in the distance, staring at something I couldn’t see.

I took her hands and held them in mine, letting the quiet settle between us. This gesture seemed to bring Jackie back to me and she gave me her full attention. With tears stinging  my eyes, I asked, “but how long have these signs been following me and I’m only just now realizing? How much longer do I have left?”

She didn’t say anything, just caressed her thumb over my knuckles.

“I’m scared, Jacks. I don’t want to leave you.”

“Y-you’re not having another episode are you?” she asked after a minute, flush with concern. Her tone was soft and quiet. Like she always got when she tip-toed around the elephant in the room. Treating my Bipolar I disorder as if I were a ticking time bomb about to go off any minute.

I released my grasp and stared down at my feet and the floor, shaking my head disappointedly. “No.”

It had been years since my last one, Jackie knew that. It almost ended our relationship. I’d accused her of cheating on me and things got… out of hand from there. I've been taking my medication religiously ever since. 

“It’s just that when you get manic, paranoia is the first sign for you and-”

“I’m not paranoid!” I snapped. Guilt instantly seeped into my stomach and I apologized for my outburst. 

“Alright,” she conceded, putting her hands up defensively. “ Let’s just leave it alone for the rest of the night. Before you get nightmares and can’t sleep.” 

She started to clean up my research, compiling it neatly underneath my notebook and computer as I sat there in my chair, looking despondent. As she tidied up, she began humming a tune to herself. After she tucked my stuff away in a cabinet, she pulled out her phone and played the song, setting the device on the table next to me. She smirked as I looked up at her, fighting the urge to smile, still determined to be a sourpuss. Waving me off, she started singing along with the lyrics. Then she started dancing around. They were small, groovy movements at first, but then she got more into it, swinging her hips and jostling her shoulders. Jackie looked silly, but was beautiful when she was like that. Free and totally herself.

She giggled as the song ended, spinning to look at me. Slowly she walked over, holding her hand out for me to grab, inviting me to dance along as one of our favorite songs began to play. She tilted her head and looked at me expectantly.

With a sigh and roll of my eyes, I relented and gave her my hand, allowing her to metaphorically pull me out of my funk.

We migrated over to the living room where there was more space for us to goof around. Together Jackie and I jumped and leaped, twirled and swayed, all while keeping time as the genre of music changed with the shuffling of her playlist. We went from rockstars to classical composers and everything else in between. It was just us. Brian, Jackie, and the music. It was a nice and tender moment between us. At the time I wished it could last forever.

Though I smiled and looked happy on the outside, a haze of dread still encircled me. Wrapping itself around me like a blanket, suffocating me. In my core, something still felt wrong. I tried my hardest to stuff the feelings down, lock them away never to be seen again. *Maybe Jackie was right,* I told myself. It was all in my head. There’s nothing there. No patterns, no signs, no omens.

As I twirled Jackie into a dip, a loud thud came from the other side of the room, followed by the sound of shattering glass. I quickly returned Jackie to an upright position before the two of us crept over to investigate.  It appeared that a picture frame had fallen off the wall suddenly and without reason. She knelt down on the ground, carefully sweeping away broken pieces of splintered wood and shards of glass with her hand, picking up the picture that had been inside. She frowned as she looked at it. It was a photograph of Jackie and I on our prom night.

“Is now a bad time to point out that’s an omen too?” I asked sheepishly, rubbing my hand at the nape of my neck.

That comment earned me the stink eye from Jackie.

\*\*\*

Despite my best efforts, Jackie insisted we still went on our evening walk. She was convinced I’d become agoraphobic right then and there if I didn’t wander around the neighborhood with her that instant. 

So, there we found ourselves, walking down a road that could be classified as a liminal space. The street was quiet, everyone asleep in their homes. The only noise for miles being the street lights overhead, buzzing softly as they drenched everything in a low orange hue. Jackie and I had stopped for a moment to stargaze. 

“Don’t. Move.” She said to me suddenly through gritted teeth.

I froze in response, unable to see what she saw. What was it? Was I about to die?

Slowly and with precision, Jackie lifted her pointer finger over my shoulder. I flinched as she got close to my face, having no idea what was going on. 

Then she smiled, which worsened my confusion. Was she happy I was about to meet my maker? She pulled her hand back, revealing a rather large but beautiful monarch butterfly perched on her finger. 

“Little guy was hanging out on your shoulder,” she said, carefully stroking its head. 

“Don’t you think that’s weird?” I asked her, watching as the critter batted its wings and fluttered away. On any other day I’d have been just as mesmerized as Jackie, but I knew better.

“It’s a butterfly, flying around. It’s what they do.”

“But at night?” I prompted, shooting her a look.

She rolled her eyes, knowing exactly where I was going with this.

“That’s it, time to head back,” she announced before I could get a word in edgewise. She pivoted on her heel and started marching back to the house, declaring, "It's bedtime.”

I didn’t argue. There was nowhere else I wanted to be other than the comfort and safety of my own home. 

\*\*\*

Jackie and I called off work the next morning. She called it a “mental health day” but I knew it was really a “keep an eye on Brian so he doesn’t lose his shit day.” She said that wasn’t true, but then watched me like a hawk as I took my meds. She thought I didn’t notice, but I did. I always do.

After whipping us up a nice breakfast, we geared up for yet another outing. Jacks was insistent we go on another walk. When I brought up how the last two wound up, Jackie rationalized if we changed our location and did something different it would go better. 

I let her drag me along to the park in hopes that was true.

I didn’t particularly like the idea of roller skating when Jackie pointed out the rental booth she spotted as we pulled into the parking lot. It could be dangerous. What if I fell and hit my head? I could go out Final Destination style. That thought alone was terrifying and made me itch. But I loved her, and despite my fear, I wanted to make her feel better so I went along with it. 

“We should think about making our wills,” I brought up after one of the wheels in my skate caught an uneven step on the path, causing me to crash and fall on my ass pretty hard. At that moment I cursed myself for being cheap, wishing I had splurged to get all the arm and knee pads to go along with the helmet and shoes. In retrospect that would have been the safer option.

Jackie laughed at me before skating over and leaning down, offering me a hand. “Last Will and Testaments already? What a flirt! We’re not even married!” She joked.

She dragged me back onto my two feet where I stumbled trying to maintain my balance. I tripped into her arms and Jackie caught me before I could fall again. Looking up at her with a dopey grin and lovesick eyes, I proposed, “We could be.”

Jackie blushed and got flustered like a school girl, pushing me away. She then hurriedly rolled away, making me chase after her. Our whole relationship, that's what I did. From the first time I saw her sitting in front of me in class in the fourth grade all the way to when I won her back after our last break up. I was happy doing it and never wanted to stop.

“I’m not ready yet,” She called over her shoulder, bringing me back to the present. “But one day. I think that would be very nice.”

We explored the park for a little while after that. Eventually, we came across a community garden with a bench in the center. Deciding it was the perfect spot for a water break, Jackie and I sat down to catch our breaths.

“What kind of flowers?” She asked out of the blue, breathless from the big gulp of water she’d just taken.

“Hmm?” I mumbled, breathing through the burn in my chest and lungs from exercising.

“What kind of flowers would you want at your wedding?” She reiterated.

In typical guy fashion, I responded with a nonchalant, “I don’t really care.” A beat passed before I looked down at her. Jackie was just beaming to tell me hers, making me think she had thought this over a hundred times before. “What kind of flowers do you want?”

“I’d want purple peonies. They’re just such a romantic flower,” she gushed, dreamily. She then almost fell out of her seat, pointing at some that were planted in the garden. “Look there’s even some right there! I can envision them now, being staple center pieces with the decor.”

I didn’t want to ruin the moment, but that didn’t set right with me. Those peonies were the only flowers in the garden. Now, I’m no flower guy, but even I could tell that those shouldn't have been in bloom in early Fall. That was another omen of death if I ever saw one.

I held my tongue, realizing it probably wouldn’t be the smartest move to bring up kicking the bucket to the love of my life while she was currently daydreaming about our wedding. 

If I was going to die, I wanted her to have some nice memories of the two of us spending time together before I passed.

\*\*\*

One eventful afternoon of avoiding the grim reaper while roller skating later, Jackie and I returned home in hopes of having a quiet evening. That dream was unfortunately cut short by a pair of scissors. 

While attempting to cut a couple pieces of bacon in half  for BLT sandwiches, I dropped the kitchen scissors- which landed on the wood floor upright with a point sunk into the floor. 

The sound around me was drowned out by a loud ringing in my ears. I paused, mouth agape, staring at the menacing sight. The omens. They were ramping up and increasing in frequency. I was perpetually stuck feeling a strong sense of foreboding. Whatever was going to happen, I feared it was going to come to fruition soon. That had been the most violent omen yet. 

Was it a sign from the universe that my end would be a painful one?

Jackie quickly noticed me and my panic. I was really glad I hadn’t brought anything up earlier as she started to take pity on me rather than get annoyed like usual, gently guiding me away from the scissors and sitting me down at the table. We went through some breathing exercises together before she went back into the kitchen. With a hard tug, she managed to free the tool from the floor and tossed it in the sink. The smell of burnt bacon permeated the air as she shut the stove off.

“Why don’t we go out for dinner and have a date night,” she offered, wringing her hands together, waiting for my response. “A change of scenery and some good food might make you feel better, babe,” she added.

I thought about objecting at first and getting takeout instead, but my mind went back to the scissors. I shuddered, a chill crawling down my spine. Perhaps I wasn't as safe at home as I thought. 

With a nod, I agreed, letting her choose the restaurant.

We went to a local steakhouse downtown. Jackie had gotten all dolled up for me. She curled her hair and wore my favorite outfit: a red noodle-strap silk dress with a black shawl paired with matching stiletto heels. To me it was a classy and timeless look. She gave off Marylin Monroe vibes, which I was very much into. Just like my girlfriend I cleaned up nice too. I gelled my hair back and wore black dress slacks and a white button up. I felt like a finance bro, but at least if I died, I’d go out in style. 

I ordered an eight ounce steak with some alfredo pasta on the side. Jackie ordered the surf and turf. Dinner went fine, normal even. We talked about nothing and everything all at once. It was amazing and genuinely took my mind off things. It was a bit of a reprieve from the last three days in all honesty.

After I paid the check, Jackie brought up the idea of walking downtown for a little bit, conveniently ending the route at her favorite ice cream parlor. I had to admit, it was the perfect way to end the night. And who can say no to ice cream?

The sun was just beginning to set when we left, leaving the sky a mix of light blues, oranges, purples, and pinks. 

I thought nothing of it at first when we first entered the restaurant, but out of the corner of my eye, I noticed a crow across the street. It was innocently picking at some crumbs near a trash can. That same crow was still there almost two hours later in the same spot. I swear it turned to look at me when Jackie and I exited the establishment.

My thoughts started racing again as terror flooded my veins. I tried my best to keep it together as we walked around town. Jackie dragged me into almost every department store we came across, window shopping with glee. Which admittedly, was a very welcomed distraction.

However, after exiting every business, it seemed another crow joined the flock. 

First it was the one across the street. Then it was him and his buddy down the road, but still in sight, pecking at the sidewalk looking for worms. When Jackie and I were done perusing around a book store, there were three of them lined up on the building’s roof. Their beady, soulless eyes following my every movement. They cawed and squawked to each other as we walked away, seemingly laughing at me, foretelling of the grim future that awaits.

I held my girl close to me as we kept going, the end nearly in sight. The ice cream parlor was just at the end of the road.

“The crows,” I said, coming to a stop. There were about five or six of them perched on the shops sign and loitering on the ground near some picnic tables. They had me surrounded.

“We should get a cone and give them some,” Jackie suggested, giddy with the idea. “Or a nickel! I hear they make good friends if you give them something shiny. They remember faces too.”

I couldn’t pussyfoot around it any longer. The signs, everything, it was all beginning to be too much to bear. 

“I think they’re stalking me,” I admitted.

Jackie scoffed, playfully hitting my arm. “Brian, that’s not funny.” The smile cracked on her lips faltered when she realized I was being dead serious.

“It's another omen. In Irish and Celtic tradition, crows following or watching you was often viewed as a sign of approaching bad luck or death.”

“Brian, stop,” she demanded, taking a shaky step back. “You're really scaring me now, you know.”

“You’re telling me!” I screamed, reaching my breaking point. “I’ve been seeing the signs for days, Jackie. When will you believe me? When I’m finally dead and six feet under in a grave? This is torture! Madness! I just want it to end!” I grabbed at the sides of my hair, overcome with frustration. “When will I know peace?!”

For the first time in a long while, I saw Jackie break down and cry. “Oh for God’s Sake, you’re not going to die!” She yelled at me, face turning red with anger. Though there was a slight bit of worry that settled in her features as well. “Snap out of it! You need help, Brian. Mental help. Either you go see someone or we’re breaking up and I’ll have you committed, myself.”

I hesitated to say anything, just stared at her. I shook my head, kicking an imaginary rock while pressing my lips into a thin line, unable to meet her gaze as I shoved my hands in my pockets. I couldn’t do the one thing she asked of me. 

I was right, I know I was. 

“You really are crazy,” she said, dragging her hand through her hair, her voice cracking as she choked back tears. Something in her broke then. “I’m done.” And just like that, Jackie stormed off, continuously wiping off her cheeks as she sobbed.

Like usual, I chased after her. I hated seeing her all emotional and worked up like that. It physically pained me knowing I was the cause of her turmoil. We couldn’t end. Not like that. 

“Jackie, wait!” I called out to her catching up, grabbing her wrist. She came to a stop at the edge of the sidewalk.

She sniffled, tugging herself out of my grasp. “I don’t want to be near you right now.”

“Please, I’m sorry,” I pleaded, grabbing her hand with both of mine, never wanting to let go.

She yanked herself free again, using the momentum to take a step forward and further from me, “Just go away-”

I can’t really describe what happened next other than feeling a big gust of wind, Jackie being there one second, then gone the next.

The doctors say it was so traumatic to witness that my mind went into a catatonic state to protect itself. I came out of it a day later.

At the same time we had been fighting, there had been a drunk driver barrelling down the road. When Jackie took a step off that sidewalk, it was right into the front of that car. 

She died immediately upon impact and pronounced right after EMS was on scene.   
   
It was then that I realized I had it wrong this whole time…

For every omen, she had been standing there, right next to me. The warnings hadn’t been meant for me, but for her.

My only solace now is knowing that I’m next. 

I keep telling anyone that will listen, trying to warn them, but that doesn’t do much good in a psych ward. They’re transferring me to a mental institution for round the clock monitoring soon. I can only laugh at the thought. I know better. Even a locked padded cell can’t stop death.

As the end draws near, I can only ask; Please, stay vigilant out there. Be aware of your surroundings. You never know if you might be next up on Death’s Docket. But trust me, there will be signs. 

You just need to know how to look out for them.
