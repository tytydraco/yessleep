When I was a kid, I didn’t have the best home life. My parents were always busy and when they weren’t, they seemed to never have much interest in spending time with me. They were never abusive, just emotionally unavailable. So eventually I just stopped trying to get their attention and made peace with how it was. 

The summers I spent at my grandparents house was where I felt the happiest and the most at home. They lived out in the country on a large piece of land, filled with woods and fields. I’d spend my days wandering the game trails and exploring the property with my dog, Rusty, always finding something new and interesting. Like the old stone wall in the middle of the woods that stretched across a small creek, or the rotting remains of an old wooden shed long forgotten. Then, on cooler nights, I would put up my small tent in the yard and sleep under the stars. My grandparents were the best and kindest people I ever knew. Any time I got hurt or felt bad about something, Grandma was there to pick me up and fix me a meal. Grandpa was an amazing storyteller, many late nights he stayed up telling the tales of hero’s, cowboys, and frontier times. They gave me free range of the entire property and the house. Except for one place. I was never allowed to go in or near the basement. Even straying too close to the basement door was discouraged.

“There is nothing down there for you.” My grandmother would say.

On the rare occasion I got up the courage to ask my grandfather about it, he only smiled and said, “We keep a lot of old things in the basement.”

“Like old tools or something?” I asked.

He nodded. “Yes. It isn’t safe to go down there, Tommy. Besides, you have the whole property as your playground.”

And for years I was satisfied with that answer, happy enough to leave the basement alone. Until I wasn’t.

By the time I was fourteen, I had explored the property in its entirety. Seen all it had to offer. I found though, that there was a nagging curiosity that pulled at my mind. One that I had come back to more than once. What was in the basement?

One night after supper, fried chicken and Mac n cheese. I waited up in my tent until late into the night, long after the lights in the house had gone out. I got up and made for the tent zipper, waking Rusty as I stepped across the crackling plastic of the tent floor. The red heeler sat up and looked at me sideways as if to ask what I was doing. 

“Stay here, boy.” I said patting his head. 

He let out a soft woof, as I climbed out of the tent and made my way to the house. 

The old wooden door creaked as I pushed it open. I paused in the doorway, listening for signs of movement from upstairs. After about a minute of silence, I let out the breath I had been holding and made my way through the front room to the kitchen. Grandpa always kept the basement locked with a heavy padlock. If I wanted to see what was down there, I would need his keys.

I knew that sometimes he took his keys to his room with him, but luckily they were hanging in their place on the key hooks by the back door. I reached up and took the keys, doing my best to prevent them from jangling, then made my way over to the basement door. 

Wood creaked from somewhere within the old farm house as I reached for the lock. I ran through half of the keys on the ring, before the lock finally snapped open audibly. My heart pounded with anticipation as I turned the old iron knob and pulled open the door. The hinges squeaked with disuse and again I held my breath, waiting to see the lights flicking on from upstairs. 

With a sigh of relief, I stepped through the dark doorway, flicking on the old Maglite I used when camping out in the yard. The old wooden steps seemed to go down far deeper than they should have. I kept watching for a pull string for the lights, but there was only thick layers of ancient looking cobwebs illuminated in the dim yellow of my old flashlight. The smell of dirt any musty wood rot filled my nose as I made my way down.

After what felt like 20 yards of rickety steps, I found myself standing on a dirt floor. I shined the flashlight around to see dirt walls and roof. There was no basement, just a rough hole dug into the ground. And there at the back of the hole was a wall. The back wall was not made of dirt but stacked cinder blocks, and in the center of the wall stood another locked wooden door.

 This one was different, heavy chains ran across the door to large steel bolts drilled into the wall and the door itself had heavy padlocks at the top and bottom.

I stepped up to the wall, grandpas keys in my hand.  Examining the chained door, wondering what my grandparents could be hiding down here, when I heard the sound of movement. Only it wasn’t  coming from upstairs. It was from behind the door. I stepped back, shining my light on the door. Were they keeping something in there? Some kind of animal? And then I heard it speak.

“Hello?” The voice sounded small and pained. Muffled by the heavy door. 

I began to back away, goosebumps rising on my arms.

“Please, I don’t want to be here anymore. Just let me go home.” they said with a sob.

My heart pounded as I asked. “Who are you?” 

The voice was silent for a solid minute before it spoke again.

“You’re not the ones who put me here. How did you get down here?”

“I… I was curious. I’m not supposed to…”

“Can you let me out?” asked the voice.

“I, I probably shouldn’t.” I said backing away. 

They let out another sob, “Please, I just want to go home.” 

I noticed that there was a sliding peep hole on the door. Curiosity itched at the back of my mind, as I stepped back up to the door.

“Who put you down here?” I asked. “And why?”

I reached up and slid the peep hole open, trying to see in, but the hole was too small to use my light and look through very well. I the faint rays of light that managed to shine into the locked room I could make out a figure, crouched there in the dark.

“Please let me out.” It sobbed. “I want to go home.” 

“I can’t see you.” I said trying to shift my light. 

In the gloom, the figures head rose slightly. “You want to see me?” it asked as it slowly stood to its full height. I couldn’t see it clearly but its rail thin frame seemed to have to hunch over as to not hit the roof. Suddenly it broke into a dead sprint at the door. 

I fell back as it slammed against the heavy wood and began screaming, “Let me out! Let me out! Let me out!” The once small voice, huge and filled with rage.

The door shook as I climbed to my feet and turned away. I ran up the stairs as fast as I could. When I got to the basement door, I did my best to close it as quietly as I could and snap the padlock back into place. 

I ran back out of the house to my tent and climbed into my sleeping bag. Rusty, seeing my panic, curled up next to me, licking the tears from my face. I turned back to look out the mesh tent window at the house to see my grandparents bedroom light turn on, followed by the rest of the house lights. They had heard me. I turned away, pretending to be asleep. I heard the front door opened and waited for one of them to call out to me, but they never did. After a moment I turned back to see grandpa standing in the front porch light, staring at my tent. I turned back and pinched my eyes shut and didn’t sleep at all.

Eventually, the sun rose and I heard the door open again. “Tommy, come eat breakfast!” grandma called.

I left my tent and went inside, waiting to be scolded for what I had done. But the scolding never came. Grandma smiled as she sat a large plate of pancakes in front of me. I smiled back and dug in. Maybe they didn’t know I had gone down there. Maybe I just imagined the whole thing. 

“Linda, have you seen my keys?” asked grandpa as he stepped into the kitchen.

My heart skipped a beat. The keys. I forgot to put them back.

Grandma shook her head, “No, did you leave them in your pants again? Check the laundry.” 

He nodded and headed off to check.

After a moment, he called out, “No, I can’t find them anywhere.” 

“That man.” Said grandma shaking her head, she then called out, “Well, I’m sure they will turn up.” She then went to start washing the dishes. 

I hurriedly finished my pancakes and stood, preparing to leave the room and patting my pants pockets for the keys. “oh no.” I thought, hurrying out of the kitchen.

“Finished already?” called grandma behind me. 

“Yeah, thanks grandma. They were great.” I said over my shoulder as I pushed through the screen door.

I searched the tent over and over, but the keys were gone. “Where are they?” I muttered over and over. Then it hit me, could I have dropped them last night? Were they down there, in the basement? I felt sick just thinking about it. 

I stepped out of the tent and saw Rusty standing on the grass. He was staring at the house, his head cocked to one side as if listening. I stepped over to him and put my hand on his back. I guess the sudden touch startled him because he turned, snapping and growling before realizing it was me. 

“Whoa, easy boy. It’s just me.” I said pulling my hand back. 

He wined and lowered his head, nuzzling against me as if to say “I’m sorry.” I petted him and whispered to him that he was a good boy.

“Thomas.” Grandpa called. 

I stood, feeling my heart pounding. They only ever called me Thomas when I was in trouble

He was standing next to his pickup in the driveway and motioned me over. As I approached, he said, “Get in. Let’s go for a drive.”

I numbly walked around the truck and climbed into the passenger seat. Grandpa got in and started the truck, eyeing the house.

We drove in silence out to the pasture. He didn’t speak or look at me. The old suspension of the farm truck squeaked as we drove over bumps and through ruts. Eventually we came to a stop overlooking a wheat meadow. Grandpa killed the truck and stared through the window for a moment in silence.

Without turning to me, he said, “I’m gonna ask you a question now. And whatever your answer, I’m going to trust you.” He turned to face me, “Okay?” 

I nodded, “Yes.”

His pale blue eyes started into mine for a moment, “Did you go into the basement last night?” 

My heart was pounding and I felt like I was going to cry. I thought about lying to him but I couldn’t. “Yes.” I said, my voice trembling.

He closed his eyes and dropped his head for a moment. When he looked back, there was anger in them. 

“Grandpa?” 

But he held up his hand stopping me, “What did you see?” 

I swallowed, “There were stairs, a lot of them and another door. A… a locked door. With someone behind it.”

He leaned in close, his eyes drilling into mine, his next words were spoken slowly and with great care, “Did you speak to it?” 

I nodded, feeling tears building up in my eyes, “Y…Yes.”

He closed his eyes and clenched his teeth, “Okay, Tommy. It’s okay. Just tell me everything.” 

I told him.

He listened and nodded, “But you didn’t open the door? You didn’t let it out?” 

“No.” I shook my head, “But I almost did. I thought it was someone in trouble.” 

He nodded. “I know. It’s okay, the important thing is that you didn’t.” 

I sniffed, “What is it?” 

He sighed, and looked out over the meadow. “I don’t know. But it came from the woods.” He nodded to a thicket of trees on the other side of the meadow, “From those trees, right there.” 

“Years ago I was out hunting in this very spot.  Sat right here as the sun sat, no deer in sight. But then I heard leaves crunching just ahead in the trees. I sat up and readied my rifle. Thinking this was it, here comes a big one.”  He turned to face me, “And then your grandma walked out of the woods. She stood there in the dusk light, looking around until she saw me. I almost believed it was her. It looked just like her, moved like her, sounded like her when she called out to me. I would have sworn it was her, only she was sitting right next to me in the deer stand.” He shook his head, “Back then she liked to go with me every once in a while. Neither of us has been hunting since.”

“What happened?” I asked. 

“I shot it.” He said bluntly. “I didn’t know what it was, but I knew what it was trying to be.” He shook his head before continuing. “The thing fell to the ground and we left. Thinking it was over. It wasn’t until the next night when I realized, it wasn’t. She… It was calling my name out by the fence, “Bill, I’m hurt.” Or “Honey I need you.” We tried to ignore it, tried to kill it, tried everything but it just kept coming back.” 

“What did you do?” I asked.

He laughed, “The only thing I could think of, trapped it.”

“How?” 

He sighed, “I dug the tunnel in the basement and I let it inside. Let it believe I fell for it’s deception. I called her, it, to come in and see what I had been working on. Led it down to the basement and shut the door behind it.” 

“That worked?” I asked.

“Yeah.” He said with a chuckle. “It screamed and raged, banging on the door when it realized, but as long as we keep it locked up down  there ,it can’t hurt us.”

“What does it want?” I asked. 

He thought for a moment, “You know what a cuckoo bird is, right?” 

I nodded.

“I think maybe it’s something similar. I don’t know for sure, but I think it wants to take the place of someone.”

I swallowed, “Like grandma?” 

“Yeah.” He nodded, “But I’m not gonna let that happen.” He said turning to face me, “Now, where are my keys.” 

“I think I dropped them last night.” I said.

His face fell, “Where?”

“I… I think…” I stuttered.

“Dammit , where, Tommy?!” He shouted.

“In the basement, I think.” I finally said.

“Oh my God.” He breathed.

Grandpa started the he truck and slammed it into drive, throwing dirt and grass as he peeled out and headed back for the house. 

“What’s wrong?” I asked. “I thought it couldn’t get out.”

He didn’t answer until we slid to a halt back at the house. 

“Thomas, take Rusty and get away from here.” He said, flinging the door open.

“What? Where do we go?”

He shook his head, “Go to the woods, to the old shed. I’ll come get you when it’s safe.” 

I nodded and whistled for Rusty to come. Grandpa was heading for the house as we headed for the trees.

15 minutes later, we arrived at the shed, Rusty was running all over, sniffing and peeing on everything he could. We had been out to the old shed in a quite a while and he had lots of territory to remark.

We sat there waiting for most of the day. I wondered what happened at the house. Could that thing have gotten out? Were they okay? I felt sick worrying about them. About what was happening.

Eventually I heard the sound of grandpas truck coming down one of the trails. I stood and made my way out to see the truck coming along, grandma behind the wheel. 

She smiled when she saw me, “Come along, Thomas. Dinner is almost done.” 

I slowly stepped forward. “Where’s grandpa? I thought he was gonna come get me.” 

She smiled again, “Oh he had to run to the store to pick something up, he should be back soon.” 

I nodded and headed for the truck, “Okay.” 

Rusty suddenly came up behind me and started barking at the truck, I turned to face him, “What is it, boy?” 

He continued barking and growling, and bearing his teeth. I turned back to grandma who was still smiling. “Silly dog.” She said, looking at him, “I visited with the neighbors earlier, he probably smells their cat on me.” 

I nodded and climbed into the truck. But Rusty refused to get in, he just kept barking and snarling at grandma. 

Grandma drove us back to the house with my dog following at a distance. 

“Grandpa mentioned some stuff earlier.” I said as we drove. “Stuff about the basement.” 

Grandma sighed, “Is he telling you stories again?” 

I shook my head, “No, he seemed serious. He said you guys saw something strange a long time ago. And that you had it locked up in the basement.”  

She pulled the truck to a stop in the driveway. “I don’t  want to talk about that anymore.” She said, getting out. “Now come on, dinner is waiting.”

I climbed out of the truck to see Rusty sitting out in the yard. No matter how I called to him, he wouldn’t come any closer. Eventually, I turned and followed grandma into the house, leaving him outside.

As I stepped into the house, I immediately caught a whiff of burning meat. Stepping into the kitchen, I saw the dinner she had laid out. I think it was supposed to be meatloaf but it had been burnt so badly it looked like a piece of charred tree bark. 

“Sit down.” She said pointing to the dining room table. As I sat, my eyes tracked along the wall to the basement door. The wide open basement door. 

My heart pounded as she lay the plate of food on the table in front of me. She then sat down and smiled wide at me, waiting for me to eat. 

“I’m… not that hungry.” I said with a glance down at the plate.

She sat there, unblinking as a fly landed on her open eyeball. Her smile never wavering. “But you love my cooking.” She said after a moment. 

I nodded, “I do.” My voice trembled, “I’m just not hung…”

She slammed her hand on the table and screamed, “Eat the fucking food!”  Her face contorted in rage.

Tears rolled down my cheeks as I reached for the fork. 

Rusty began barking outside again. Grandmas smile slammed back into place, “My goodness, did you forget to feed that dog again?” 

I tried to stand, “Yes, I’ll go…” 

But she stood and slammed her own fork down through my hand, pinning it to the dining room table.

I gasped in the sudden pain as blood oozed up from around the utensil. 

“You stay here and eat.” She said still smiling. “I’ll take care of the dog.” 

Tears of pain and shock and fear rolled down my face as she turned and walked out the back door. 

Rusty barked like a feral beast outside and I realized now was my chance to get away. I didn’t know what happened to Grandpa but right then there was nothing for me to do but get out of there. I reached to the fork and felt a shock of pain as I grasped it. With all the effort I could manage, I gave it a solid yank. I tried not to scream, I tried. But the pain was more than I could bear and I screamed anyway. Rusty let out even more furious barks and growls, then my dog let out one pain filled yelp before falling silent. 

Silent sobs racked my chest as I reached for the truck keys on the counter. Then the back door creaked open. I turned to see the imposter, standing in the doorway. Her fingernails coated in wet blood. 

“Rusty isn’t feeling well.” She said, still smiling. “Did you eat?” she asked. 

I nodded shakily, “It was good. Can I have more?” 

She smiled all the wider and stepped up to me, placing one bloody hand on my cheek, “Of course you can, you’re a growing boy. And I’m going to take care of you, forever.” 

When she stepped over to the stove and began filling another plate, I ran for the door. I pushed through it, slamming it back against the house and running for the truck. I leapt inside and fumbled the keys with my injured hand. Looking back to the house I saw the door open and the imposter step outside, looking around for where I had gone. 

When her eyes locked on me she tilted her head, just like Rusty had. She suddenly dropped to all fours and ran at the truck, her limbs contorting and bending at extra joints like a huge spider. 

The engine roared to life just as she kept into the hood and started beating on the windshield. “Stay! Stay! She screamed, over and over in a voice that sounded like too many voices scrambled up together. I floored the truck in reverse, then spun the wheel, flinging her off and turning the truck around before slamming it into drive and tearing off down the road. 

I watched in the rearview mirror as I drove. She stood and looking like nothing in the world was wrong, turned and headed back to the house.

When I finally made it home, neither of my parents were there. I called the police and told them that an intruder was in my grandparents house and that I had gotten away. They said they would send a car out and let me know what they found. Then I called my parents and told them the same thing. 

They found both of my grandparents in the basement. They never told me how they died, but we had closed casket funerals. They never found the intruder. The police and my parents didn’t believe me about the imposter. Said something about traumatic memories or something, I don’t know. I know what I saw though, and I know what I heard in the following nights. What I still hear, just outside my bedroom window. She wants to come inside. Maybe I should dig a tunnel.
