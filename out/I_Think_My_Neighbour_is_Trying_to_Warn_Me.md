I moved into this house three months ago.

The first thing I noticed was the porch light across the street.

It was always on.

Day or night.

Rain or shine.

Even in the middle of the afternoon, when the sunlight made the bulb look completely pointless.

The house belonged to an old man named Mr. Vale.

At least, that's what I thought.

I never actually met him.

I just knew his name.

I faintly remembered the previous owner mentioning him while giving me the keys.

“Nice guy,” she had said. “Keeps to himself.”

Then, almost as an afterthought:

“Don't knock on his door.”

I laughed.

She didn't.

“Seriously. If you see him outside, just wave.”

I thought it was an old-person quirk.

Until my first night there.

At around 2:13 AM, I woke up because someone was knocking on my bedroom window.

Three slow knocks.

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

I froze.

My bedroom was on the second floor.

There was no balcony.

No tree.

Nothing that could possibly reach the window.

I pulled the curtain aside.

Nobody was there.

But across the street, beneath the glowing porch light, stood Mr. Vale.

He was staring directly at my bedroom window.

I couldn't see his face clearly.

He simply stood there.

Completely still.

I stepped away from the window.

When I looked again, he was gone.

The porch light remained on.

I convinced myself I'd imagined it.

Until the next morning.

There were three fingerprints on the outside of my bedroom window.

On the second floor.

The knocking happened again the following night.

2:13 AM.

Three knocks.

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

I didn't look outside.

I stayed in bed until sunrise.

When I finally got up, I noticed something underneath my front door.

A piece of paper.

There were only six words written on it.

**DON'T ANSWER WHEN HE KNOCKS.**

I immediately thought it was a prank.

Some neighborhood kid.

Maybe the previous owner had left it as a joke.

I put the note in my wallet.

I don't know why.

I just felt like I needed to keep it.

That night, at exactly 2:13 AM—

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

I didn't move.

Then came a fourth knock.

Not on my window.

On my bedroom door.

*Knock.*

I stared at it.

Another knock.

*Knock.*

Then a voice.

“Are you awake?”

It was Mr. Vale.

I knew his voice.

I couldn't explain how.

I'd never spoken to him.

The voice came again.

“I'm sorry.”

Silence.

Then:

“I think it knows you're here.”

My bedroom door slowly creaked open.

I hadn't touched it.

The hallway beyond was completely dark.

At the far end, I could see my front door.

It was open.

I ran.

I didn't stop until I was sitting in my car three streets away.

I called the police.

Two officers came with me.

They searched the house.

Nothing.

No footprints.

No broken windows.

No sign anyone had entered.

One officer checked the second-floor window.

“Probably an animal,” he said.

I pointed across the street.

“What about Mr. Vale?”

Both officers went quiet.

The older officer looked at me.

“Who's Mr. Vale?”

I laughed.

Then I realized he wasn't joking.

“Your neighbor,” I said.

The officer looked across the street.

Then back at me.

“There isn't anyone living there.”

He walked across the street.

The house was empty.

Completely empty.

The officer checked the property records.

The house had been vacant for eleven years.

There was no current owner named Vale.

No resident.

Nothing.

I remember arguing with him.

I remember telling him about the porch light.

I remember telling him about the man standing beneath it.

I remember him looking at me with an expression I couldn't understand.

Concern, maybe.

He told me to get some sleep.

I didn't.

The next morning, I searched for Elias Vale.

There wasn't much.

A few old property records.

A missing-person report from 2014.

Nothing else.

No photographs.

No interviews.

No neighbors talking about him.

No newspaper article about the porch light.

Nothing.

I searched for hours.

Eventually, I found the original property records.

The house had been empty since 2014.

But I couldn't remember what the house looked like before that.

I could only remember the light.

And the man beneath it.

That night, I packed a bag.

I was done.

Whatever was happening in that house, I wanted no part of it.

I was halfway through packing when my bedroom door creaked open.

I stopped.

The hallway was empty.

Then I noticed my bedroom window.

It was open.

I knew I'd locked it.

I walked toward it.

Three fingerprints were visible on the glass.

On the inside.

I backed away.

My phone rang.

Unknown number.

I answered.

There was silence.

Then Vale's voice.

“Whatever you do…”

A pause.

“…don't look outside.”

I stared at the curtains.

Something was moving behind them.

“Who are you?” I whispered.

His voice became quieter.

“I'm trying to keep you alive.”

The call ended.

I grabbed my bag and ran.

I've been staying in a hotel ever since.

It's been four days.

I haven't told anyone where I am.

I haven't even told my family.

Last night, at exactly 2:13 AM, someone knocked on my hotel window.

Three times.

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

I'm on the seventh floor.

There is no balcony.

I didn't look.

I didn't move.

Then my phone lit up.

Unknown number.

One message.

**KEEP THE LIGHT ON.**

I stared at it.

My bedside lamp was already on.

Then I looked through the curtains.

Across the street was a house.

The same house.

The porch light was glowing beneath the darkness.

And underneath it stood Vale.

He raised his hand.

Not waving.

Pointing.

At me.

I felt a strange calm come over me.

For the first time in days, I wasn't afraid.

Because Vale was there.

He was watching.

He was warning me.

He was keeping me safe.

Then the knocking stopped.

The porch light went out.

Vale disappeared.

I waited.

Nothing happened.

I slowly opened the curtains.

There was no house across the street.

No street.

Nothing.

Just the city far below.

My room was silent.

I reached into my pocket.

I don't know why.

Maybe I needed proof.

My wallet.

I opened it.

Cards.

Receipt.

Some cash.

I searched every compartment.

Nothing.

I checked again.

Still nothing.

The piece of paper.

The note.

**DON'T ANSWER WHEN HE KNOCKS.**

It wasn't there.

I emptied my wallet onto the bed.

Nothing.

I checked the floor.

Nothing.

I searched my bag.

Nothing.

It had never been there.

Frantically, I opened upp my call logs. I checked them. No unknown number. 

There never had been a call.

I swiped open my messages. No message saying "Keep the light on."

And suddenly I couldn't remember putting the little paper note in my wallet.

I couldn't remember where I'd found it.

I couldn't remember the previous owner giving me the keys.

I couldn't remember her warning me about Vale.

I couldn't remember anyone ever telling me his name.

The police officer's question came back to me.

*Who's Mr. Vale?*

I looked toward the window.

There was no porch light.

There never had been.

There was no house across the street.

There was no Mr. Vale.

He had never existed.

But I finally understood why I'd seen him.

Why he'd always appeared when something was wrong.

Why he always knew what to say.

Why he was always standing beneath that light.

My mind had needed someone to warn me.

Someone I would trust.

Someone who could tell me what I was too afraid to realize myself.

Vale wasn't my neighbor.

He wasn't a ghost.

He wasn't protecting me.

He was my subconscious mind.

The knocking started again.

Three slow taps.

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

I stared at the bedroom door.

The handle began to turn.

Then a voice from the hallway whispered:

“Are you awake?”

A funny, rasping sort of voice, otherwordly.

And this time, there was no porch light outside.

Because there had never been one.

And there was nothing left in my mind to warn me.
