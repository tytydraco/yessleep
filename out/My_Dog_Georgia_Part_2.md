Honey Pestilence Dew

I’m sure many of you assumed that I had a mental breakdown after reading my second entry. I wouldn’t blame you for thinking that. I have a hard job! I cry a lot, I sleep poorly, and hell, sometimes I hear and see things that don’t make sense. It’s a little crazy but it’s  nothing I can’t handle. Though, the concern that you all expressed did prompt me to reach out to friends for a second opinion. 

I told Davis everything during our shared shift. He read through my posts methodically with a perfect poker face. When he finished reading he looked over at me as if to gauge whether or not I was joking. I think he was a little disappointed when he realized I was serious. 

“I think you need to take some time off.” He muttered, handing my phone back to me. I groaned and tried not to roll my eyes too aggressively. I knew he wasn’t a believer in ghosts or the supernatural but I thought he knew me well enough not to think I was going crazy. He knows my little quirks don’t escalate into madness. I’m just a little awkward. 

“Look, I don’t know what you want me to say.” I started. “I know I’m not “having a break down” and that Georgia is most assuredly a dog, but I also know that I’ve been seeing some weird stuff. Or, I guess that Georgia is doing weird stuff. I just wanted a second opinion, not whatever this is.” I gestured wildly at him.

“You’ve been seeing warped versions of what’s been happening to you on duty. That’s PTSD. Or something…I don’t know.” Davis finished eating his prepackaged chicken and rice and began driving to a call for injured wildlife. As he drove, he half heartedly rambled off some lecture on mental health that I almost completely tuned out. Something about keeping your mind right. I felt like a kid forced to listen to my dad calmly explain why he chose to hit me for acting up. 

“Iiiii-don’t-BREAKING NEWS-like deals?-ThAt!” I fiddled with the radio in a huff after a solid twenty minutes of the silent treatment. I attempted to replicate Georgia’s trick from when we first met. It was much harder to do than I realized. Which only furthered my belief that Georgia was supernatural and I was super fine. 

Davis, though used to my eccentricities, just looked at me like a mother looks at their child after being handed a dead frog. Unamused and a little annoyed.

“Yep. I’d say you’re doin’ just fine.” He muttered to himself. 

“I am.” I reassured him before sulking through the remainder of our ten hours together. 

Davis knew something was up but he thought something was up for the wrong reason. He thought I was weaker than the other officers. Too soft. A bleeding heart. That’s what he liked to call me. I tried to take it in stride but I knew it was an insult. If I was a bleeding heart then he was a heart surrounded by a stainless steel brick wall and turrets. Nothing ever bothered him. I doubt he would have even flinched if Georgia transformed into something heinous in front of him. 

So, naturally, I invited Davis over to walk our dogs together, several times, but Georgia never shifted. She even started getting friendly with him. Excited to see him and his dogs, Barley and Basil. She only ever shifted or made weird sounds when we were alone together. It was frustrating to say the least. So frustrating, that one night, while making dinner, I looked down at all three of the dogs that were looking up at me and asked. 

“Georgia, can you speak English?” She cocked her head when she heard the name she had gotten used to. 

“No?” I asked again. At this point I was just talking to the pasta as it boiled. Part of me thought it was fruitless but part of me really wanted the validation of knowing I wasn’t losing it. I wanted concrete proof that Georgia was abnormal and I wanted to be able to show other people that that was the case. It’s not just me. It’s her. 

“EiiI CaN.” While I was mid thought Georgia had placed both paws on the counter top and stared up at me with her mouth closed. The voice sounded automated. Like a scrambling of syllables loosely strung together. I kept my eyes fixed on her as I tried to calmly fashion a response. 

“And you can, uh, morph yourself?” She didn’t say anything. She didn’t move. So I kept talking. I knelt down to her eye level and got closer. 

“Like a skinwalker, I guess. Are you a skinwalker?” I asked flatly, blinking but maintaining eye contact with my dog. Georgia opened her mouth and began to pant. She hopped off the counter and sat amongst her siblings, closing her mouth again. Like a dog waiting for a treat. 

At this point I was on all fours, petting Georgia with a bit more intensity than I usually do. 

“I know you can. I don’t mind. I don’t want you to think you’re in trouble. I mean it’s a little weird when I catch you yawning with too wide a mouth or licking your paws while they’re spindly little fingers but I’m not upset. Just tell me! I just want to know what you are!” I pleaded with her. I held her head in my hands like I was ready to embrace her with a kiss. It didn’t seem to matter how much I begged though, she remained silent. 

Eventually I gave up and looked back at my pasta, which was now boiling over. I turned off the heat and completed the remaining pasta steps. Once dinner was finished and I had gotten tired of the silence that had loomed in the air like a heavy fog I caught myself fixating on the wall in front of me. The mantle above the broken fireplace was lined with wooden boxes of various sizes. Each one was labeled with a different name in bronze. Honey Dew, Bella, Eebie, Yubaba, Apollo, I could keep going but for my own sake I won’t. They were all memorials to my previous pets, fosters, or just dogs from work that I knew died horribly. I had a couple hundred at this point. Most of them were stacked along the mantle but some were in between unread books on the bookcase. Only one was on my night stand. Or maybe two. Who even knows anymore? 

Now, I’m not big into the supernatural. The only reason I know what skin walkers are is because I used to live up north with a lot of deer. At the time I was living with my mastiff, Honey Dew, in a trailer in the middle of heavily wooded mountains. I was scared of the deer and their glowing fluorescent yellow eyes.They stood like statues just along the tree line and stared out at the house like stars that had fallen too close to the earth. Though, they still burned as bright as they did up in space. Dew would scare off the deer when they got too close. We had a good thing going when we walked together at night. Dew was one of my closest companions. She made me feel safe even when my old boyfriend tried hard to make my nights more eerie. 

He told me stories about the deer. Stories to make me keep Dew on a leash beside me at all times. He taught me about skin walkers. He told me that they would sneak around the woods and peel off the skins of unsuspecting prey animals. Then parade around the world as a wolf in sheep's clothing. Deer freaked me out more after hearing all that. 

I say all of this to explain why I thought that breaking open Honey Dew’s urn was a good idea. I didn't know the logistics of how skinwalkers selected their bodies. I guess at that moment I was hoping their powers would mold them to whatever remains they could find. Like how snails find their shells and latch on. It doesn’t have to be a shell. It can be something shell shaped instead. So by that logic, it didn’t have to be peeled skin, it could be any kind of bodily remains. 

That’s what I told myself when I poured Dew’s ashes out on top of Georgia as she laid on the carpet. I crouched down like a kid under a Christmas tree and waited eagerly for something to happen. I was half excited to see if my theory was right and half anxiously hoping I wouldn’t have to vacuum up Honey Dew. Georgia didn’t shake the ashes off; she just sat there, looking up at me as if to ask why I did that. To that question, I had no real answer. I kept waiting, kept staring. I wanted her to start glitching. 

Eventually, she did. 

At first she got smaller. Till eventually there was just a neat pile of ashes left. There was no bone, no fight, no noise, just the slow withering away that comes with aging. Then I heard whining coming from the pile. I laid on my side, getting eye to eye with the pile. Sitting before me was what I can only imagine Dew must have looked like when she was first born. Jellied, gasping for air, whining for milk. Then she kept growing. Her weeping eyes began to fully form into the black inky pools I used to love not thinking much about while she was alive but I now wistfully remember fondly. Then, two pink and bloodied orbs protruded from her tear ducts. I had forgotten about her cherry eyes. 

Dew came to the shelter after her owner tried and failed to put her down himself. He gave her a good whack on the head with a shovel. He did all of this because of her eyes. Cherry eyes are fairly common for dogs with saggy eye lids like basset hounds and mastiffs. Dew was victim to them before she even turned a year old. Her owner thought it was a sign from God. He thought she was evil. He called her devil dog. I fell in love with how much she loved and trusted people even after being treated like garbage by the person she was meant to trust the most. 

It took a few surgical procedures to fix her eyes. I was tired of people asking what was wrong with her. I had forgotten about it until now. Now that I was watching Dew slowly fully form before my eyes. Not as slow as she had when she was alive. But still relatively slow. I laid out on the floor and marvelled at how methodically these wiry lines of pink viscera that emerged from Dew’s ears were suturing down her blood lacquered cherries. Once they were pushed back and her body finalized the process of coming into itself she laid limp on the carpet. I knew I had to show Davis. 

I dialed his number and listened to the phone ring, patting Honey Dew’s scarred head. She must have been tired from all the growing. After all, she just had to pick up all the fragments of skull and tissue that had exploded onto the floor seconds ago and place everything neatly back into place. She did a good job though! She was methodical. Honey Dew looked and snored just like she used to. I see now why Georgia didn’t transform often. The process must be exhausting. 

I heard Davis’s phone ringing just beyond my front door. Weird. I couldn’t tell you why he was at my house so late but I was glad he was here when I needed him. Now I could prove to him that Georgia was magic. That nothing was wrong with me. I opened the door to find him in a black coat holding a plastic bag full of store bought tomato basil soup, my favorite. He had been grabbing for his phone before I answered the door. I must have startled him because he jumped a little when he saw me. 

“Perfect timing!” I shouted maybe a little too enthusiastically. That was a mistake. Davis hated when I was overly chipper. 

“Yea, sorry, I brought dinner. You can just take it and I’ll go if you’re busy.” 

“No, shut up, I’m not busy! I actually have great news! You have to see this. I’ve been trying to show you for a hot minute now!” I grabbed his arm and pulled him inside to the living room. I waved my arms out to show him Dew, like a magician does after they complete a trick. Honey Dew was sprawled out in the same spot as before. She was unmoving as the other two dogs sniffed at her. She lifted her head ever so slightly to see who I brought in, but she didn’t bark. She was visibly a little lethargic but she looked good given the circumstances. I waited with baited breath for Davis to say something. I tried to gauge his reaction to the transformation. I waited for a gasp or a soft wow. I wasn’t waiting for what he actually said. 

“Where did you get this dog from?” He asked bluntly. 

“Honey Dew? She died years ago. But look! Georgia just turned into her! She looks just like her. It's crazy, actually, how spot on her mimicry is. It’s these little pink things. Like uhh 3d printing? But fleshy. So, I guess, maybe she’s just made of this material that can turn into whatever. ” I would have kept rattling on but Davis was not having any of it. He grabbed my shoulders and gave me one quick shake to bring me back to the present.  

“Where’s Georgia?” He boomed, trying to be calm but his anger came through regardless. 

“Right there. What do you mean?” 

“Martinez. That’s not Georgia. I don’t know where you got that dog from–”

“Honey Dew is my dog! She died before I met you!”

He waved his hand as he shushed me. 

“I don’t know where you got this dog from, but, I know it isn’t Georgia. I just need you to remember where she was last so we can get her and then we can worry about microchipping this dog.” 

“Microchipping! Yes! I don’t think even Georgia knows how to change her microchip!” This was genius. I was so glad Davis said something cause I would have never thought of it otherwise. I went to turn away from him and find my spare chip reader but he gripped his fingers into my shoulder blades harder, locking me in place. 

“Martinez, stop! Look at me. Focus on just me right now. I’m talking to you.” Davis was steadfast in his desire to keep me from proving Georgia’s powers. 

“You have a sick dog laying on your floor. Okay?” He waited for me to nod.

“She isn’t sick, she just transformed. It took a lot out of her-” Davis cut me off. 

“Your dog, Georgia, is not here in your house. Where did you leave your dog, Martinez? We need to find her.” He spoke loud and slow, enunciating each word as if to put a period behind them one by one. It was infuriating. He was infantilizing me in my own  home. Talking down to me like I was a petulant child. I could feel my face get red. I knew tears were welling up in my eyes. My one friend was treating me like an idiot and he just wasn’t listening to me. I know I shouldn’t have but I hit him. I slapped him square in the face as hard as I could. He didn’t yell after it happened, just stood in stunned silence for a moment as if he was processing what his next move should be. He let go of my shoulders and walked towards the door. 

“Fine!” His tone was low but shaking, like he had to convince himself not to yell. “Figure it out on your own. I’m not helping you anymore Isabella.” Davis set the bag of soup down on the doormat. Then he opened the front door and shut it without looking back. I didn’t move, I just stood in the same spot trembling. I tried to stop myself from crying but I couldn’t do it.
