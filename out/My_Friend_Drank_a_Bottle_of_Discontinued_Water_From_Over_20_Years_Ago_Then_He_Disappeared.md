The story begins seven years ago.

I don’t know if there is anyone here who has a friend like mine—someone with an almost pathological obsession with water.

My friend Avery loved drinking water.

The first time Avery introduced me to his hobby, we had known each other for less than a month.

I still remember that day. It was summer, and the air conditioner in our dormitory had broken down. On my way back, I stopped by the convenience store downstairs and bought two bottles of chilled mineral water.

Originally, I only bought them to press against my skin to cool myself down. After all, our dorm had a water dispenser, so there was no reason to buy bottled water outside.

But when I handed one bottle to Avery, his reaction surprised me. It was the first time since we met that he looked at me properly.

“You like this brand too? Good taste. A smart choice.”

I froze for a moment.

“What?”

He didn’t answer. He quietly twisted open his own bottle, took a small sip, then closed his eyes as if savoring some expensive wine.

“The mouthfeel is slightly firm. The aftertaste comes quickly. The calcium content is a little high, but this year’s batch is softer than last year’s.”

At the time, I thought he probably had some kind of psychological condition, maybe high-functioning autism or something similar.

Later, I realized he was simply a “water sommelier”—and the strangest person I had ever met in my life.

After we became closer, I learned that Avery was actually somewhat famous in certain communities and niche liquid-tasting forums.

Most people probably think “water tasting” is nothing more than some pseudo-scientific hobby invented by pretentious middle-class people trying to appear refined.

But Avery was different.

He had an almost abnormal sense of taste. He claimed he could accurately distinguish the subtle differences between dozens of brands of mineral water sold on the market.

At least, he firmly believed he could.

Regardless of whether it was real, his strange obsession never affected me. It was just drinking water. Compared to the alcoholics living next door, it was practically harmless. At least on the surface, it sounded like a healthy hobby.

The four years we spent together in university were actually quite pleasant.

And I have to admit, Avery was genuinely intelligent.

He was the only student in the entire chemical engineering department to graduate with a perfect GPA. After graduation, he immediately joined a major company as a chemical engineering analyst. I heard his salary was quite impressive.

Even then, we still kept in touch—not too close, but never completely distant.

Although, perhaps that was because I was probably his only friend.

I was glad Avery hadn’t turned into some basement-dwelling eccentric.

We would occasionally meet up. Every time, Avery would excitedly talk about the water he had recently tasted, while I nodded along and enjoyed the expensive whiskey that I could never afford myself.

Of course, Avery always paid.

Until one day, he suddenly sent me a message.

“Do you know about tasteless water?”

Before I could finish typing my reply, Avery had already forwarded me a post.

It came from the same liquid enthusiast forum I had seen on his phone before.

When I clicked on it, there were already more than two hundred replies.

The original poster claimed that many years ago, he had once drunk a bottle of water with an incredible flavor. He vaguely remembered the brand being called “Taste-less.”

Recently, he suddenly wanted to drink it again, but no matter how hard he searched, he couldn’t find any information about the brand.

Someone below replied, claiming that it had been a brand only circulated privately among a small number of clinics and rehabilitation centers in the late 1990s.

The manufacturer had gone bankrupt after only three years, and all production lines and records had since been destroyed.

Aside from that, most of the comments questioned whether the brand had ever existed at all, suggesting that the original poster had simply fabricated the story for attention.

I hadn’t even finished reading the comments when the post suddenly disappeared.

When I returned to the chat interface, a new message was already waiting.

“Do you think tasteless water was real?”

“No way, he deleted the post. Maybe it was just a joke.”

“It doesn’t matter. I saved screenshots.”

He sent me several pictures.

Nothing useful had been missed. The word “rehabilitation center” in the comments had even been circled in red.

“Do you think this could be a breakthrough point in the investigation?”

“No…”

“I just found several private rehabilitation centers that have already closed down. They all claimed to use some kind of ‘natural therapy.’ Interesting, right?”

“Avery, don’t do this.”

“Relax. Nothing will happen. I’m just going to take a look.”

“We’re still friends, right, Ave? :D”

“I’ll contact you later.”

But he never did.

After that, Avery disappeared for about half a year.

During that time, I tried every possible way to contact him, but none of them worked.

Until one day, Avery suddenly appeared outside my office and told me he had something good to show me.

He took me to the basement he was renting.

The room was filled with a heavy smell of mold and some indescribable odor that reminded me of something rotten.

If I hadn’t been certain that our friendship was unbreakable, I would never have stepped into such a strange place.

Avery told me that during the months he had disappeared, he had spent all his savings searching for tasteless water.

He had even quit his job and moved into this basement, surviving on temporary work just to cover his daily expenses.

At the center of the old wooden table sat a brown glass bottle.

It was tightly sealed, the opening covered with something resembling black resin.

There was no commercial label on the bottle.

Only a line of handwritten ink remained on the surface. The writing had faded with age, but I could still make out a date:

1998.04.12

Avery gently touched the bottle, his movements almost disgustingly tender.

“An old collector kept it as a specimen from some kind of psychiatric treatment. I spent everything I had to get this bottle.”

That’s right.

My friend spent all of his savings on a bottle of expired water that was more than twenty years old.

And he was completely satisfied with it.

Maybe he really did have some kind of high-functioning autism.

Avery carefully cut through the seal with a knife and pried open the cap.

There was no sound of escaping gas.

Not even the slightest movement of air.

He took out two spotless glass cups, and like someone performing an ancient ritual, slowly poured the liquid from the bottle into them.

“Drink.”

He looked at me.

There was an urgent pressure hidden in his eyes.

“Me too?”

He looked at me strangely, as if he couldn’t understand why I would ask such a question.

“We’re friends.”

Fine.

For a friend.

A glass of twenty-year-old expired water.

Besides, it had been stored in glass, so there shouldn’t be any issues like plastic decomposition.

We raised our glasses at the same time and drank.

The liquid slid across my tongue and flowed down my throat.

Honestly, I didn’t feel anything special.

It was just water.

Slightly cooler than room temperature. Plain. Without any flavor.

If I had to describe something different, there was perhaps a faint sweetness lingering deep at the base of my tongue after swallowing.

Or maybe it was simply an illusion caused by saliva.

“How is it?”

“It’s… pretty smooth?”

I carefully chose my words, trying to make him feel that his money had been worth it.

“Maybe there’s a slight aftertaste? The calcium, zinc, and magnesium levels are probably quite good too…”

Avery suddenly laughed while watching me struggle to come up with an answer.

“Don’t be so nervous. It’s just water.”

I let out a sigh of relief.

“Exactly. Just water.”

Avery shook the empty glass in his hand, his voice filled with slight disappointment.

“Although it really is tasteless, there’s nothing particularly special about it.”

“My three million dollars were probably wasted.”

Three million?

That was slightly less than I expected.

I guessed he probably still had some savings left.

Apparently, Avery hadn’t completely lost himself.

“How have you been lately? Did I cause you trouble?”

His expression was normal as he naturally started chatting.

I had been worried he might break down emotionally, but now it seemed like he was doing fine.

“I’m the same as always. But next time, don’t disappear without saying anything. We’re friends, you know…”

After that, we talked about all kinds of meaningless things.

How was business going?

Fine. Not great, but not terrible.

You look exhausted. You should rest more.

Everyone looks like that these days.

Do you remember that trip we took in college?

Of course. It was a great trip, wasn’t it?

Most of the time, I was the one talking.

Looking back now, I think I was trying to drag Avery back into the normal world through these ordinary little things.

At least, I wanted his mind to stop being filled only with the subtle differences between dozens of types of mineral water.

Avery barely spoke.

He only nodded occasionally, constantly holding that empty glass in his hand.

Our roles had somehow reversed.

Just as I was enthusiastically complaining about my strange coworkers at the office, Avery suddenly interrupted me.

“After talking for so long, I’m getting a little thirsty.”

“Really? Then let’s go somewhere and get a drink. I still have a lot to tell you.”

“No, no need. It’s too much trouble…”

“There aren’t any bars nearby anyway. We’d have to walk quite far…”

“I mean, you can keep talking. I’ll just drink something casually.”

His eyes drifted away, and he lowered his head slightly.

Following his gaze, I looked at the empty glass in my hand.

There was still about one-third of the liquid left.

“I only need to drink a little more of this…”

“I’m really, really thirsty…”

At that moment, I saw something in Avery’s eyes.

A primitive greed.

Something almost animalistic.

Avery was a person with mild obsessive cleanliness.

Normally, even if someone accidentally touched his cup, he would frown and wash it immediately.

But now, he was staring directly at the leftover water in my glass.

Water that might have already mixed with my saliva.

A powerful sense of unease crawled up my spine like an ill-fitting sweater scratching against my skin.

I hesitated.

Then I handed him the glass.

Avery almost snatched it from my hand.

He didn’t even wait until the rim reached his lips before he desperately poured the remaining water into his mouth.

He greedily sucked at the bottom of the glass.

Even his tongue traced along the inner surface, trying to collect every remaining drop.

“Are you okay? God, you must have been really thirsty. If it’s not enough, I can go outside and buy you some water—”

“Ha…”

Avery let out a satisfied breath.

It sounded as if he had just finished drinking an ice-cold beer.

He wiped the water from the corner of his mouth and apologized for his behavior.

“Nothing. I just suddenly felt… extremely thirsty.”

“I’m fine now.”

Avery seemed not to notice my expression.

He lowered his head and slowly sucked the remaining moisture from his fingertips.

The faint wet sound echoed through the empty basement.

“Ave?”

“What?”

Avery looked up.

I realized he was staring at my lips.

“We’re still friends, right?”

“Of course. Why would you ask that?”

“…Nothing. Just asking.”

That was our last conversation.

After that day, Avery disappeared again.

He never came back.

His phone number became disconnected.

The basement was emptied.

His account on the forum was never updated again.

I tried to report him missing, but the police told me that an adult leaving voluntarily did not qualify as a missing person case.

That was when I realized something.

Avery had never once mentioned his family to me.

I knew nothing about him.
