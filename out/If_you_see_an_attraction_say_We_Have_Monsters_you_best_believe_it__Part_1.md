A couple days ago, I was visited in the hospital by a detective who wanted me to write him an account of what had happened the night prior. Spoiler alert: He didn’t believe me. No one does…but reading likewise stories on this subreddit gave me the idea to post my story here too. I’m hoping that at least someone will take me seriously so that maybe someday the man responsible for all of this will be served justice. For privacy reasons I will be referring to myself as Kat from this point forward.

3 YOUNG ADULTS FOUND DEAD AT NEW LOCAL ATTRACTION: BEAST-MASTER BARNABY’S WE HAVE MONSTERS

With one eye covered by bandages, the other crept open like an old door to read the local paper that had just been handed to me. That was the title of the article, and those ‘3 young adults’ were my friends.

“I need you to tell me everything,” A male voice spoke with gentle sorrow holding back an eager demand. “And I mean everything.”

My eyes filled with tears of incomprehensible fear, loss, regret, anger and - 

“Ma’am, I know this isn’t going to be easy, but we need to know everything we can so that the one who did this to your friends is caught.” He put his hand on my shoulder prompting me to toss the paper and jolt upright, wrapping my arms around him.

“It- It’s not fair. W-we just thought it was a ride!” Ugly crying into his chest I held on tight, not wanting to let go as if his shirt was theirs. “He said we’d be the first. First to ride it. How were we supposed to know it was real?”

Accepting the hug the detective slowly dropped his hand to my shoulder before lightly grabbing both of them to separate my body from clinging to his. Reaching for a paper and pencil, I could tell he had talked with my parents already.

“Kat, your mother said you loved to write as if it were a second language. If you think writing it down while I give you space will be best, you’re welcome to do so” Pencil and paper in separate hands, held out towards me. Taking them, I began to write as the past repeated itself in the form of graphite.

We were in my car, driving down the highway on our way back from an urban exploration and talking amongst ourselves excitedly. My passenger, Tyler, sat there quietly before pointing out a sign that wasn’t there that morning on our way up. Tyler and I weren’t the talkative types so when one of us talked the rest listened.

“Beast-Master Barnaby’s: We Have Monsters? Was that there before?”

The three in the back stopped yapping and fought to look out the right side window.

“Move bitch, I’m trying to see.” Snapped Abigail.

“Who are you calling ‘bitch’? I was here first?” Said Sally.

“Oh shit! We gotta do that.” Teddy said eagerly.

I remember being torn between spontaneously going to this new mysterious attraction or just going home. It wasn’t that late or anything, but unlike Teddy my life wasn’t all just thrill seeking even though that’s what we always all came together to do.

Sensing my unwillingness, Teddy switched to persuasion mode. “Oh come on Kat. We gotta at least check it out, right? Plus it said coming soon so it’s not like we’ll even get to participate in anything. Well, unless y’all down to explore the construction site?”

“Hey! Hell no. We agreed on no construction zones.” Abigail chimed in. “They’re unpredictable, unfinished, and probably have security.”

“Alright smart ass. I remember the damn agreement. But we should at least check it out, no?”

“Oh I’m not saying we shouldn’t, but if it’s still being built that’s where we stop. Got it?”

We had almost explored every known haunted house, abandoned school and store in the area ever since we graduated from high school. The idea of possibly being the first to see what this new attraction could be, enticed me. So in my soft tone that cut through their irritatingly loud one, I gave my answer.

“I suppose we could check it out.”

“Hell yeah.”

“Let’s gooo.”

“Sweet.”

“Ok.”

Seeing another sign up ahead with a new gravel road branching off the road we slowed down onto the shoulder and pulled in. Right away we saw two huge metal gates that surprisingly opened to welcome our car in, causing us to stop immediately.

“I thought you said it was coming soon?” I turned to the three in the back.

“I think it did.”

“It did, moron. I saw it.” Snapped Abigail.

“Abby, can you not?” Pleaded Sally.

“What? He’s an idiot. I KNOW what I saw. Y’all should know at this point I’m never wrong.”

“Hehe. You were wrong about the ice on that pond last winter though.”

“Ugh. It’s called a hyperbole you dunce. You’d know that if you actually passed English.”

“Hey I did pass-“

“By a point!”

A small, maybe 5’6” man with a cane emerged from the gates raising his hat towards our halted car. With Sally between the bicker in the backseat, and Tyler turned to enjoy the show, I had to plead for their attention.

“Guys?”

Turning immediately to match my gaze, Tyler gave his reaction. “I didn’t know magicians were native to these woods?”

“Why do we put up with your constant attitude again?” Teddy gave his final punch before turning to see the man in the road. “What the hell?”

Unwilling to possibly lose an argument to Teddy of all people, Abigail just kept nagging. “My attitude? What about your recklessness? You do dangerous stuff all the time and expect us to follow your lead. How the hell-“

“Abby, politely shut up and look.” Sally lightly turned Abigail’s cheek to see what we were all looking at.

“Hello my friends! Are you here to partake in the thrill of a lifetime?” Said the man coming towards our vehicle.

Lowering my window, I crept the car closer to him till he was peering in. “We, um, saw the sign and-“

“Is it open?!” Interrupted Teddy.

“Woh! An eager one I see.” Said the strange man. “Not quite yet. However, I was just looking for volunteers to be the first to try the new trolley system. Do any of you wish to partake?”

Raising both his hands in victory, Teddy sat back saying, “Let’s fucking go.”

Looking at everyone else’s faces in search of agreement, I got four pairs of eyes all excitedly staring back at mine. Turning back to the magician I gave our answer. “Yeah… we’d be down.” An agreement I would greatly come to regret.

Grinning ear to ear he gestured us out of the car and through the big metal gates that grinded closed behind us. Each of us looking up, down, and around the inner walls of this new place we would soon come to find out was our tomb, we took in all of the bright colors that gave this zoo structured confinement a circus feel. Approaching a big red trolley like one pulled straight out of England, the magician gestured us into it as the doors swung open. One by one, we all climbed inside.

Looking out the window we saw the magician pull a walkie talkie out from inside his purple suit jacket. His voice cracked over the speakers in the trolley, and throughout the whole park that was just passed a new set of gates that the tracks led through.

“My name… is Beast-Master Barnaby. And boy oh boy do we sure have monsters! Just beyond these gates lies creatures you may or may not have heard of before.” The gate ahead slowly opened as the trolley jolted alive and crept up to speed. “I pride myself in my collection of these majestic creatures. They are everything to me. They are my children. They are my world. Join me, as I entice each of you to them so they may get to know you better, and likewise to you.”

“Well that’s an unpleasant way of putting it.” Tyler chimed, giving us all a giggle as the magician gave his closing statement.

“Please stay seated inside the trolley, unless forced not to. Some of them have already eaten today.”

The trolley passed through the gate as it slowly closed behind us. I- I’m sorry. Just remembering the thud of them closing still haunts me. We had no idea what we were getting into, but I digress. You have to know what happened to them. But more importantly… you have got to believe me.

We were stunned after entering this new section of the park. It’s like the forest that was once here had been cleared overnight to make for wide, open areas presumably for where these ‘monsters’ were confined. Some areas had tall, metal fenced walls that surely kept massive creatures in. Other areas were just wooden fencing that could easily be vaulted over. As we took in the scenery, Barnaby’s voice returned over the speakers.

“Peculiar isn’t it? So many different places that seem as if they’d been here for ages, yet out of place with the land you know. Almost as if they were just magically dropped here, no?”

“I thought magicians weren’t supposed to reveal their secrets.” Tyler sarcastically added.

We all laughed before freezing from the response we were not expecting to be given.

“I could tell you everything, and anything my boy. It’s not like you’ll be alive to tell the tale.”

“What the hell? You’re listening?” Abigail snapped at the speakers.

“How else am I supposed to get their feedback?”

“You mean your.” She corrected.

The man just giggled to himself before introducing us to the first of his abominations.

“If you all look to your left, past the wooden fence, you’ll see a tree line of Douglas-fir and Western Hemlocks. Hiding in their shadows is my most iconic creature of all! The legendary Sasquatch.”

We all looked at each other with pressed lips, not knowing whether to laugh or give in to the fantasy mindset and enjoy ourselves. Making our way over to the open windows of the left side of the trolley, narrowed our eyes searching the shadows for any sign of the towering hairy creature. Stunned, Abigail nerded out as she asked Barnaby how he possibly planted the trees so quickly seeing as those trees don’t grow around here. He went on with his informative chit chat about the mythical Bigfoot which must’ve bored Teddy.

“Hey look! Old man’s not lying I see two glowing eyes out there. That a guy in a suit or something? Or just two lights on a post?”

“You flatter me with your attempt to comprehend what you see.” Barnaby responded.

“Yeah whatever. I ain’t sitting in this tin can just to see a bunch of distant lights for however long this ride is.” Putting his hands on each side of his open window, Teddy hoisted himself out of the trolley. “Hey! Carney!” He yelled to the eyes. They blinked in response.

Tyler bolted towards the eyes in an attempt to scare the worker back. He did this every time we ever did a haunted carnival house. It always took us out of the experience; made us less afraid. This time was different. As the trolley kept moving on, and no pushback was given by the voice over the speakers, we waited for Teddy to come running back to the trolley grinning his stupid grin. Instead, we heard a loud roar followed by an unfamiliar scream of terror, though we could make it out to be Teddy’s.

“Old Sassy isn’t much of a carnivore, but that doesn’t mean he likes it when foolish people run up to him like that.” The voice broke our confused silence.

“Did.. did the worker actually get Teddy? He might need a raise.” Said Sally optimistically.

“I’ll see to it he gets extra blueberries.” Mocked Barnaby.

More of Teddy’s screams could be heard as we watched the trees sway against the backdrop of the setting sun. With no wind that night, we all felt the same nervousness tingle our stomachs.

“That’s uh… that’s pretty impressive I guess.” Sally said with her a clear sense of worry on her breath. “Are we going to stop for him?”

“Silly girl. I told you all to stay in the trolley unless ordered otherwise. His fate is his own doing.”

“Fate?! Enough of the act. This isn’t funny anymore. Give our friend a break. He’s an idiot!” Abigail cried.

Barnaby ignored our pleads as he directed us to look out the right window. Disinterestingly, we also just glanced over to see the next of his freaks staring us down from along a rock wall.

“This little guy is often referred to as the Dover Demon. Lucky for you he does not feed off of flesh. Unlucky for you his gaze is more than enough to clench his appetite.”

On a stone wall of rocks right along the track, a child sized, alien looking creature on all fours stares us down. As our eyes met his, we struggled to look away. It was as if our heads were being held by a set of hands, forcing us to follow his turning head that only went out of view once the trolley slowly passed by. Once out of the trance, we rubbed our sore necks that felt the tension of our struggle to turn away.

“Alright. What the hell is going on?” Sally shivered.

“Something’s not right.” Abigail added.

“Little shit.” Murmured Tyler.

As the trolley kept rolling, we saw several things that we tried to explain but simply could not. Barnaby had gone silent besides the occasional info dump on the origins of the creatures and how he came to ‘owning’ them, letting our minds drift off into guttural confusion. The worry over what could have come of Teddy only grew more fierce, yet none of us spoke a word more of it for we did not want to face the possibility that something unnatural had happened to him.

After coming face to face with a group of frogs whose appendages matched more of our own, we entered into a new area. Two metal doors swung open, making an opening in a massive stone wall we had no idea meant the intended end of our journey was just beyond them. The metal doors slammed behind us as the trolley came to a halt.

“Why did we stop? Wh-where are all the fences keeping in those- those things?” Abigail cried to Branaby, not expecting an answer at this point.

“The walls separate them from the rest. My children in here suffer a most sensationable hunger that I fear may consume the rest. This is your stop.”

Panic began the sink in as we bargained for our lives. We knew this wasn’t an act anymore. We knew something terrible must’ve happened to Teddy. We just wanted to leave so we pleaded and to a man that surely sat behind a screen, watching us scramble around our cage that was about to be opened like a bunch of mice about to be fed to a snake.

 “But the tracks keep going! I-I want to see, please! We’re interested I swear!” Abigail cried.

Sally just held her head in her hands and paced back and forth, while Tyler and I looked around for something, anything! Anything that could possibly help us but we knew there was no use. We were in a trap of Barnaby’s making. This was always how it was supposed to go, we just didn’t listen.

A massive thud that shook the cart got all of our undivided attention. Out the right window, a massive disfigured silhouette waltzed our way. As it got closer, we could start to make out its features, and lack thereof. It used its front two massive hands like gorillas with its small feet supporting it from behind it. It lacked any sign of a head and as it got closer and it was lit up but the few lights the trolley produced we saw a small crooked mouth filled with a series of mismatched teeth, and two beady red eyes non symmetrically placed at the corners of its mouth. All these features, if you could even call them facial, were just indented onto its torso as it peered into the trolley. One massive hand raising to grab the top of the roof as it started shaking it violently. Gurgling sounds echoed through our enclosure as we just stood there and cried. 

“This is my curious friend, the Grafton Monster. He doesn’t like it when you scream,” the troller started to tilt mechanically as the left walls slided apart. “So please be good little snacks as he tears you limb from limb.”

We tried to buck up and grab onto whatever we could but the tilt was too much and the beast was slowly making its way around towards the opening. If we waited here, we’d be done for sure.

“We need to run!” I cried to the rest. “If we stay here we’ll die!”

Everyone just shook in fear, not wanting to be out there. We still fought the truth that this trolley was never a safe, confined place like we had thought. It was no more than a silver serving bell and we had to accept that now or never. One by one, we all let go and slid onto the grass outside the trolley. The monster peered around the side of the tilted trolley, looking down at us. We jolted to our feet and ran as fast as we could, but the monster gave no chase. Cries we had thought had been right beside us, lagged behind as we realized Abigail never got out of the trolley. Her cries intensified as the beast now blocked her only way out. 

We slowed our run and turned to watch as one of its massive hands grabbed Abby by the leg. And yanked her out of her cage. Dangling her upside down and looking into her terrified face, it let out a gurgled growl before slamming her like a ragdoll against the ground. I pray that’s what killed her, because after her screaming had stopped it took both of its hands and tore her legs from her body. Raising her open torso to its narrow mouth, we heard it gargle and suck her innards out of her before discarding her floppy, hollow remains.

We just stood there in utter disbelief, crying silent screams. I dropped to my knees, squeezing the earth between my fingers. Why? Why us? How could we have known? What could we have done? Tyler knelt beside me and put his hands on my shoulders urging me to stand but I could not. Looking up at the beast, its mouth covered in blood, it gave us a satisfied glance as it turned back the way in which it came.

“We have to go. We can’t stay here.” Tyler begged.

Sally stood there, swaying in a trance. We all grieved differently in that moment. How were we supposed to know that out there, even the slightest of things meant our survival? With me on the ground, and Tyler beside me, Sally was left standing vulnerable in the open field. Before we even got a chance to finish mourning the loss of Abigail, a thunderous noise like a jet came swooping down and grabbed her off her feet.

Not even fully comprehending what was happening we turned to see she was grabbed by two massive talons and was being lifted away by a humongous bird. Its posture resembled that of a vulture or condor, but its long blacker-than-night feathers made it obvious that this was no bird we’d ever seen.

As her screams faded into the sky, and our silent mourning became screams of abandonment, the devil returned to mock our mourning as if it was just another part of his show.

“Sorry. I forgot to mention that. These fields are stalked by the most fearsome scavenger to soar these skies. With the powers of Mother Nature herself, your friend has surely already succumbed to the shocking grasp of the Giant Thunderbird.”

“YOU MONSTER!” I screamed.

“W-we have to go. We can’t stay here. We have to keep moving.” Tyler pleaded more. “NOW Kat!”

I got to my feet as Tyler half holding me urged me toward the treeline in the direction the bird headed off towards. We had nowhere to go; nowhere we knew was safe for us to be. Cover was our only option.

Beneath the trees I cried to Tyler that we had to do something. In my mind I fought for hope that Sally could still be alive. Abigail, oh poor Abigail, knew all about nature and birds. I remembered a presentation she had given about scavengers, and how birds would take their prey to their nest for their young to feast on.

“No. We have to get out of here. You heard what he said! She’s probably already gone.” Tyler tried to reason. 

“We have to try. I-it took her up that mountain right? Even if- even if she- we’d still get an overview of the place and maybe find a way out.”

“Or we follow the treeline along the tracks and see where they lead. Surely they loop back around.”

Almost on que, the distant trolley tilted back to its upright position and moved quickly down the tracks towards the other end of the field.

“I say we go that-“ Tyler was interrupted by a familiar voice that started us half to death.

“Guys? Is that you?” A mangled Teddy limped towards us, crying in disbelief. “Oh my God! It’s you! It’s actually you!”

He fell into our arms as we raced towards him. His blood from his open wounds stained our clothes.

“Where’s Abby and Sally?”

Tyler and I exchanged looks at each other that Teddy picked up on immediately, prompting him to embrace us once more. There we held each other under the moonlight. Not knowing we were being watched.

It was at this time that the detective came back into the room to check on me. Looking over what I had written, some things clicked while others clearly confused him.

“I’m not done yet.” I croaked. “There’s still a lot that happens.”

“I can see that… but you wrote here that, including you, five individuals were present. Only three bodies have been recovered. Where’s the fourth? His mother has reported him missing, and we’re hoping you might be able to assist us in finding out where he is.”

He noticed his question started to make me break down again. Continuing to read, he reached the more ‘fantastical’ parts of the story. I could see it on his face. He didn't believe me.

“Kat. This is serious, not a writing project. Is it possible these are- metaphors? The bodies show clear signs of animalistic tendencies. Though there are some anomalies, surely you aren’t trying to tell me it’s because of-” he looked back down at the paper. “Fucking, Bigfoot?!”

I bowed my head, not meeting his stare of disappointment. He slapped the paper back down on the fold out table I’d been writing on and stormed out of the room. With tears and anger in my eyes, I picked back up the pencil and continued to write.

This is all so difficult… I feel so alone. But their stories must be told. I’ll upload again soon. Till then, if you see an attraction off of your local street or highway that wasn’t there the day before, don’t pull up to it. Don’t enter any gates. And don’t trust the magician that welcomes you inside. It’s not an amusement park ride, it’s a feeding station.
