I have been going to that boxing gym for four years. Four years of spitting blood in those damn rings.

Four days ago was the fourth anniversary of my girlfriend Sarah's disappearance. On the morning of **November 8th**, I stormed out of the house after we had a fight. When I came back, she was gone. At first, I figured she had just gone to her parents' place, but hours passed with no word, so I eventually called the police.

Months went by and the cops were completely in the dark. No leads, no sightings. It was like she had vanished into thin air, like she had never existed at all. I blamed myself for years. *"Maybe if we hadn't argued..."*

I would have given my life just to tell her *"I love you"* one more time.

Last night, around 8 p.m., I finished my boxing session and drove home in my new car. I had bought it just a few weeks earlier. It cost me a fortune, but it is as quiet as an acoustic chamber and synced to every device I own. More than anything, I bought it because on the stretch of highway I take to get to the gym, I can set it on autopilot and just zone out.

I got home no later than 9 p.m., walked in, and set my keys down. Then I froze. The living room was completely trashed. Someone had broken in.

"Siri, call 911," I said, practically yelling out of panic.

I walked into the kitchen and found the window wide open. It struck me as weird that the alarm hadn't gone off. When the cops arrived, I closed the front door behind me and followed the two officers through the rooms.

I felt embarrassed when they walked into my bedroom. It was a total mess, though I knew for a fact I was the one who had left it like that that morning.

"It's possible the burglars heard you pull up and took off. Put together a list of anything that's missing," the younger officer told me as he started up his cruiser to leave.

So I did.

They had smashed a glass coffee table, yet the TV was still mounted on the wall and my consoles were right where I left them. It felt off. *"Maybe they hit the wrong house,"* I thought.

That was when something caught my eye: a wallet lying on the floor. It wasn't mine.

When I opened it, my blood ran cold. It was Sarah's. *"This is impossible,"* I thought. *"What kind of sick joke is this?"*

I shot up and reached for my phone, only to spot Sarah's jacket draped over the couch. A black jacket with gold buttons. I picked it up. It still smelled like her perfume, as if she had taken it off just minutes ago.

I patted my jeans pocket, realizing my phone was gone.

Then I caught a strange scent in the air, like the smell of food cooking on the stove.

I stared at the kitchen door, shoved it open, and my brain completely short-circuited.

Sarah was standing there, calmly making dinner.

"Babe, you had me worried sick. Have you cooled off?" she said, smiling at me.

She didn't look like four years had passed at all. Her hair was still short, and she hadn't aged a single day.

"You... we need to call the police," I stammered.

"Honey, have you been drinking?" Sarah replied, her face twisting with concern.

"You're not real! You disappeared four years ago. I was the one who called the cops to report YOU missing!"

"Missing? You're the one who's been missing for four days," she said, looking even more worried.

"The living room is wrecked! I left it spotless this morning! Stop bullshitting me!"

"Babe, you tore the living room apart after our fight, don't you remember? I left it like that on purpose. I wanted you to see that you need to talk to a therapist about these anger issues," she told me. Her expression had gone neutral. Way too neutral.

I bolted out of the house, desperate to find a phone. "What the fuck," I yelled.

My new car was gone. Parked in its spot was my old one. I saw someone walking a dog heading in my direction, and then I heard a voice call out to me. It was my elderly neighbor. Nothing unusual about that, except the man had died of a heart attack two years ago.

"Welcome back, young man. Don't scare us like that again. Life is good here, remember that."

I didn't answer him and ran back inside. Sarah was standing near the entryway. "Do I need to call an ambulance, or are you going to calm down?"

"Give me your phone," I yelled.

She handed it over, and I froze at the lock screen: ***November 12, 2026***. It was completely impossible. I thought I had to be dreaming.

I sprinted into my bedroom and found it spotless. I threw open my closet and ripped everything out, desperate to find something to prove I hadn't lost my mind. Every piece of clothing I had bought over the last few years was gone. At the very bottom of a drawer, I found only my old iPhone with the cracked screen, the one I had replaced two years ago. With shaking hands, I shoved it into my pocket.

Sarah wrapped her arms around me from behind. "Can you please tell me what's going on?"

"This morning was **November 12, 2030**. Maybe you should tell me what's going on."

Sarah gave me a soft smile. "Babe, maybe you should get some sleep. You'll feel better in the morning."

She was treating me like I was crazy. And all night long, I almost convinced myself that I really was.

I was right on the verge of giving in when, half an hour ago, an idea hit me. I went down to the basement. Walking past the old mirror hanging near the stairs, I stopped dead in my tracks. My body had changed. The muscle and broad shoulders I had built over four years of boxing were gone, along with the scar tissue on my knuckles. I was skinny and lean again, looking the exact same way I did four years ago.

Heart pounding in my throat, I walked over to the shelves in the back and pulled down the box I was looking for. I opened it, and I honestly don't know whether to feel relieved or terrified.

Inside were the copies of the missing person flyer Sarah's family and I had printed to put up around town four years ago. We had twenty copies made and I had only kept two spares, yet all twenty of them were sitting right there, untouched, as if I had never hung a single one. Her photo seemed to stare straight back at me:

Sarah Miller, age 30, missing since **November 8, 2026**.  
Height: approx. 5'6"  
Build: Slender  
Hair: Short, brown  
Last seen wearing: Black jacket with gold buttons, black designer crossbody bag.

Those flyers weren't the only things in there, though. At the bottom of the box, I found the prayer card from my neighbor's funeral, with the year of his death printed right on it: 2028.

And directly underneath that one was another card. It had a picture of my sister, her date of birth, and the year of her death: 2027.

I don't know where the last four years of my life went. I don't know what happened to Sarah, either. All I know is that today is **November 12, 2026**, and I know the exact year my sister is going to die.
