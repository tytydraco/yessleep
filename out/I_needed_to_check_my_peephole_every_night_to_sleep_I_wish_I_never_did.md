I couldn’t sleep without first checking my peephole every night. Some nights, I needed to check it three to four times before I could shake off the paranoia enough to rest… For the longest time I lived blissfully unaware of the forces surrounding me that could change my life at any moment. I felt safe. I hardly remember any of that now. The need to constantly guard my doors and windows dominated my life and mine alone. The only other person in my life, my girlfriend, never shared my fear. Their friends thought that I was a paranoid mess but they accepted me until they didn’t have to anymore. Everyone around me made me feel crazy but I know now that I’m not. There was always someone on the other side of the door.

I only ever lived with my parents and in a dorm before I eventually moved into Modernity Inn Apartments alone. They’re pretty basic one bedroom apartments, with white popcorn walls and beige carpet. Mine was on the first floor of the eight story complex, on the side of the building where the sun rarely touched. Hedges obscured the windows and I rarely ever heard my neighbors on all sides. If not for the occasional bouts of loud arguments some nights or other tenants I would pass in the morning on my way to work, you would’ve thought the place was dead. I assumed not many people lived there despite the ample space but maybe the complex was just full of other agoraphobic loners like me…    

Honestly, I enjoyed being a recluse for most of my life and only broke that continuity for my partner, June. She was really the only person I needed. All of my interaction with the outside was either filtered through the necessity of working to pay my rent or outings with June and their friends. Or just with June… We had been together for close to four years by that point. June was the kind of bright eyed person that was hard not to love. They were beautiful: their adorable round face and body was toned in supple pecan skin, topped with a lovely mane of black, wavy curls.

Aside from their alt look and style, they also had an infectious laugh that somehow always got you to join in too. Only someone like June could get me to break out of my shell from time to time. I could sit in a group of people and not hyperventilate. I could forget about how dangerous the city was at night. For a few moments, I could feel like a normal person as long as I was at her hip.   
About a month into my stay at the new apartment, June and I came home from a night out bar-hopping with her friends. She was about ready to knock out, given that it was almost three in the morning. She stumbled and slinked into the bedsheets, beckoning me to join them. 

“Come curl up with me…” she said, muffled through the blanket. 

Normally, I’d have lunged into the bed to join her, but I had been waiting *all day* to get high. I couldn’t go to sleep before I did. As such, I assured her I’d be there soon and just continued on in the kitchen. I went to grab the eighth I left in the car, Youtube playing loudly on my phone without a care in the world. But as I swung open the door, I was met with a frightened looking woman standing right in front of the doorway. She stood barefoot in yellow pajamas, her sallow face gaunt and confused, partially shrouded by braided, frizzy hair. I think I mirrored her confusion, still processing what was happening as she begged, whispering to me, to call the police, looking back down the breezeway between each plea.

She seemed so mortified, she could barely get a word out. Repeatedly mouthing the same pleas, she tried to come inside, propping her foot against the door. I was taken aback, panicking at what to do. I assured her I was calling the police, which I did end up doing, but I had to close the door. After that, I stood at the peephole watching her walk off into the parking lot, shortly followed by a lumbering, shirtless man. He trailed after her in only his boxers and slippers; I couldn’t see his face through the moppy blond hair obscuring it. Shortly after calling 9-1-1, an officer was sent out and stayed in the parking lot for maybe two hours or so but left without arresting or confronting anyone from what I could tell.

That was it. For whatever reason, that encounter really fucked me up more than it probably should have. I didn’t get any sort of followup on that incident aside from the fact I think I saw the same (I assume) couple walking to their car a few weeks after. They looked fine, if it even was them… Nonetheless, I felt changed. The moment was so abrupt and serious and I was scared of the possibility of being in the middle of an abuse case or something. I worried about June being thrown in the middle of that too and felt too freaked out to let the lady in. But honestly, what really stuck with me was the fact that she was standing there before I even opened the door. I didn’t hear her walk up, she didn’t knock, nothing for god knows how long. And her face… Her eyes pierced so harshly into mine when I opened the door, it was like she was watching me through it.

After that night, I couldn’t shake the feeling of being watched, or of someone waiting outside my door in the night without me knowing. It probably sounds ridiculous to fixate on that part of the interaction, but I did anyway. I felt so vulnerable. There were many nights after that incident where I could hardly sleep. I mean, to sit in this apartment felt like laying bare in the middle of a savannah, surrounded by hungry lions. Even leaving the house for any reason felt that way.  No matter what else I did, the sense of imminent danger followed me everywhere if I didn’t watch my back. Only obsessing over my safety and self medication would eventually ease my fears. So from that point on, I needed to look outside and check the locks. Every day. Anything from the freakish murmur of tenants passing by my door to rustling in the leaves would set me off, prompting me to get up and look again. Even the few times I actually *left* the house, I had to relock like four to five times to move on with my day, to be sure nothing sneaks in and waits for me. Same for my car.

Needless to say, I’ve been exhausted and paranoid beyond belief. Everyday has been shit, more so now. Keep in mind, I never actually had my car or house broken into, even in the shady areas I lived in before this apartment. In spite of that however, I carried on my new routine every day for over a year or so until the night that led me to what I am now.  

I remember June moved in with me about a month before that day. We were settling into domestic life together for some time and it felt like my fears were actually beginning to subside. In place of the anxiousness over my own vulnerability however, I began worrying a lot more for June. Therefore, I kept on checking, every night, even more so than before. It was a Tuesday evening last May. I had just gotten home from another grueling day at the office when June kissed me hello and we started on dinner. We got done eating, lounging about when she decided to get up and start getting ready for sleep. After looking through the door and windows to the dark parking lot, I laid back on the couch to rest my eyes before I would join her.

That didn’t last long before the loud wailing of a woman and child outside shot me wide awake. The sound of screaming zoomed from down the breezeway, out into the parking lot by the time I got to the door. With my vision blurred a hazy pink, I peeped through the glass and locked the deadbolt. It seemed that dawn was just about breaking now and while I couldn’t see exactly how far they ran, I could hear their screaming choke to a violent stop just past the corner, replaced by a loud mechanical clunking and whirring. Just then, my attention to whatever was happening was broken by the lock handle, still in my hands, budging towards the opening side. Right below my vision on the outside of the door were now two large figures hunched over the door knob. Despite the sunlight pouring in, I couldn’t see any features through their impossibly black silhouettes burning a deep hole in my eyesight. They were both twitching and shaking like they were trying to contain laughter. Panic overcame me and I tried pounding my other hand against the door to scare them off but my fist never landed on the wood. I looked at my hand for just a second, confused, before turning back to the door. I glanced outside again and my heart was ready to burst from my chest. There was nothing. There was no outside. Just black. The glass wasn’t there either. I felt cold air gust from the hole into my eye. And then I was right back on the couch. 

Somewhat disoriented from what just happened, I sprung up to lock the door. I checked outside and everything was totally… normal. The minute hand on our clock had only moved about 10 minutes from when I stretched out on the couch.  My heart was still pounding but I figured whatever the fuck just happened was over. June called out from the bathroom “Everything good Nick?”, in response to the sound of me tripping over myself. I tried to shrug off how unnerved I was and told her about my dream but I guess she could tell I wasn’t alright. We laid down for a bit and she held me until she fell asleep. Slipping out from under her, I ran to check the lock again. I stayed awake for another hour, just smoking and scrolling anxiously. I must’ve looked outside five or six times before eventually trudging over to our room and collapsing on the bed.  

The bedroom was pitch black when I woke up. It was about a quarter to three in the morning when I decided to get up to piss. First, I peeped through the glass of the front door for good measure, relieved to see an empty breezeway. Now on the toilet, for some reason, the distant sound of dripping caught my attention. It was such a mundane thing to even notice or care about but I kept listening. There was a drip followed by a few seconds of total silence and then another. Drip. Drip drip. I was mildly fixated until I realized after a few drips that the sound seemed like it was a bit louder than the last. I think I sat there for half an hour in the dim blue nightlight, intently concentrated on the approaching sound. Eventually, whatever was leaking now seemed like it was just down the hallway.

The normally busy I-12 was nonexistent. Silence buzzed so deafeningly loud between the drops that I felt my eardrums were going to pop. The dripping shambled closer towards my door as I realized that the drops sounded more like wet globs hitting the ground. It was heavy and slow. After a few moments, the source of the dripping stopped moving. Whatever it was stood tightly in front of my door as blobs of liquid were clearing splashing against the metal kickplate. I thought maybe I was just tweaking from my lingering high but ultimately, there was no way I could just go back to sleep normally after that. Even if I was just tripping, I needed to calm down before I could catch a wink of sleep, and that nagging anxiety of danger could only be dismissed one way. It didn’t matter that the door had a deadbolt lock. It didn’t matter that I had checked it seven times that night. My compulsion required submission. So, before I went back to our room, I tip-toed as lightly as I could towards the total darkness of the door, and looked through the tiny, bright lens.

The image of what was there burnt into my brain. Even being so close to the door, the outside was dead silent. Within the fisheye view of the concrete hallway was a mass of glistening white standing tall right in front of the door. I was confused trying to understand what I was looking at until I realized the mass was actually a person, emaciated and seemingly naked. They stood so close and so tall against the door that I could only see from the middle of their chest downwards. The white covering their entire form was glistening, wadded and clumped against matted body hair all over them, like it wore too many layers of grease paint sprinkled with loose dirt and debris. They were sopping wet in this disgusting cream; loose clumps of it slid down its legs and arms, falling and forming a large blob of white on the concrete. Down there, I could see small, similar piles and white footsteps trailing up to them further down the breezeway. The only part of this figure that wasn’t covered in white seemed to be black nails crowned by dark red blotches poking out from its curled fingers and toes.

My stomach sank and I was so choked up with fear that somehow, I resisted the urge to scream. I wanted to just lock myself in the bedroom with June and call 9-1-1, so as lightly as I approached the door before, I started backing away. I swear I didn’t make a single sound, I didn’t even breath, but then,

*Knock, knock, knock, knock, knock…* 

The knocking sent painful spikes of fear directly into my gut. It froze me. I didn’t know what to do. I wanted to throw up. And every second that passed just standing there was an eternity. My thoughts were racing; who or what the fuck is standing outside of my door? What is wrong with them? What could they possibly want with me? Maybe he just happened to knock right then but didn’t actually hear me? I considered continuing to back away but was too terrified to try. I couldn’t decide what to do so I stared at the blackness I stood in, just praying to hear them walk away. I got no such relief. Creeping out from the top of the door frame came the sound of gravel rattling against metal, cutting through the silence like scissors. Somehow, the scraping of rusted scrap was a voice, panging against my eardrums. In its mutilated tone, it spoke, scratching the inside of my skull as it gurgled out its awful question:

*“PLEASE OPEN THE DOOR… HAVEN’T YOU BEEN EXPECTING ME?”*

Those words crawled right through my skin. Reality broke for me. The moment felt so surreal and impossible, I think my mind delayed any response to what I just heard expecting that I would wake up at any second. That futile hope broke as the immediate, physical dread of the situation smacked back into my body. Every ounce of fear in me compelled me to continue sneaking back to the bedroom but as soon I did, the locked doorknob rattled with force. Whoever this was tried to turn the knob and when it didn’t open, they began beating on the door, hard. The pounding of fists was so heavy, I could feel the absolute rage in the force behind each bang against the solid wood.

Harmonies of rolling gravel hummed outside among the pounding. I panicked, leaping to secure the window too and reached for the sliding pane’s lock. Suddenly it was like a storm was raging outside. The window was now rattling from the crowd of beating, scratching fervor that had gradually encased the apartment, inexplicably spreading from the door to the window, to the ceiling, and walls. This was impossible but it didn’t matter. At any moment, something was going to break or snap and the merciless riot outside would pour in and rip us apart limb from limb. I grabbed a kitchen knife and fled to protect June but the bedroom door was held locked shut. I desperately pushed against it, trying the knob, until I realized that among the harrowing chaos, relentless knocking banged against the bedroom door from the other side of it too. They were inside now.

Even writing this now makes me dizzy. It doesn’t make any sense but I know what happened to me was real. I was completely surrounded in my own home and for all I knew at that moment, June was dead. There was nothing to save or be saved by, so I just slid under the breakfast bar to shield myself in complete anguish. The rumbling waves of enraged knocking surrounded me entirely as I squeezed into the corner of the granite and the wall, gripping the knife. I held my eyes closed so tightly hoping to just die quickly from whatever was about to finally burst through the walls. Wood splintered as I felt death was just a second away when suddenly the banging stopped.

There was nothing. The incessant riot outside disappeared almost instantly and silence rang again. The apartment was deathly still. But then there was a click. No, two clicks. The front and bedroom doors swung open slowly. Snowlike footsteps on the carpet skulked intently out of the otherwise static bedroom. Whatever approached only made it to the intersection between the kitchen and the living room before I blacked out from the fear. I fainted before I could even see if it was June or whatever had broken in. When I awoke sometime after with cuts on my arms, the front door was wide open. Not much time seemed to pass since it was still dark out. June was nowhere to be found. I even ran outside looking for her but nothing. All that was left was still drying footsteps from our bed to the front door and stains of white on the welcome mat. I never saw June again. 

It feels like I’ve viewed the aftermath since that night through a distant, foggy window. After I woke up, I eventually gathered myself enough to call the police and try to explain in pieces what happened. After an officer arrived, I guess the cuts along with my story did not make me look good because they brought me to the station. Thankfully they only held me for a few days before dropping their suspicions. From what I could gather through my bouts of lucidity, security cameras showed a woman who matched June’s description leaving my apartment. She lumbered all the way to the other side of the complex, beyond the I-12 service road, and into the canal, from which she was never seen coming out of. It didn’t sound like they could track her from there either so for now, that’s where the case stands.. I think that was what did it for me. 

Somehow they didn’t think I was showing enough signs of psychotic behavior to make admission into a ward necessary. My experience ever since begs to differ. For the last couple of weeks I’ve been trying to hold on to a tattered piece of normal life, hoping that some day June will come back or that at least I might recover and live again. I don’t know how I’ve filled the hours of each day as it just all blurs together, leaving only a few clear moments of my life before and of course, that night.

For some reason I thought I could confide in June’s friends, my only other friends, with what happened. Likewise, none of them believed my story and dropped me like a hot iron. They think I hurt her. I’ve been exhausted, rotting in my apartment, ostracized by my only friends, now my job... All that’s left is the knocking. It has kept me from getting hardly any sleep, unwavering in its return every night around three in the morning. Everytime I’ve opened the door hoping to see June, only to be met with no one, not even a glimpse of someone down the breezeway.

Weeks have felt like a surreal, sluggish haze but today has been especially strange. I fear something horrible is about to happen. One of the housing agents came in this morning unannounced without saying a word to me. They quickly surveyed the apartment without so much as a glance in my direction and quickly scuttled out the front door, horrified, after seeing my bedroom. Outside has been dead silent since they left. I haven’t heard any traffic. There’s been no passing neighbors, nothing, except for the knocking droning against the hardwood door. I have nothing left for me like this. At some point I will have to answer and the fear that’s overtaken me will be vindicated in whatever gruesome fashion awaits me. I would have just done so hours ago, if not for my hesitation at what’s there. The stench is rancid and I see familiar curly hair sticking out from the peephole.
