I was about to fall asleep when I heard it.



A cracking sound. Coming from the wall on the abandoned side of the house. Someone was breaking through it. Slowly. Deliberately. I could hear the plaster crumbling, and tiny bits of dust were falling from the cracks. I don't remember what that material is called — the old drywall stuff.



I looked toward the window. It was around 1 AM. The power was out all over the district because of the rain. We have weak electrical lines here. The window had no curtains — I never bought them. Outside, I could see the yard with its old swing set and the tall metal fence that separates my house from the street. The moon wasn't visible.



Someone had been breaking that wall for almost two hours already. Earlier, I'd heard footsteps in that same area, but I tried to ignore them. Now I turned my face toward the broken wall because I was too scared to look at the window. I kept imagining that something with white eyes — a shadow — would appear there.



And just as I pictured it — the breaking stopped.



I didn't even notice when. I had gotten used to the sound over those two hours. Then I heard someone climbing over my metal fence. A loud, banging sound — like kicking the metal bars. Then silence.



I lay there, frozen. Too scared to move. Every muscle told me to stay still. Then — four knocks on the window.



I reached under my pillow and grabbed my phone. My hands were shaking. I started dialing emergency services — not because I was injured, but because I couldn't breathe. The fear was crushing my chest.



Then I felt it. Someone was watching me. A heavy, cold gaze on my back.



Suddenly, the lights came back on. I jumped out of bed and ran to the bathroom. I locked the door behind me — but I forgot my phone. The light flickered, went dark for a second, then came back on. I was relieved. Then it went out again — and stayed off.



Complete darkness.



I felt around the door for the lock. When I found it, I tried to slide it open. But the door started moving on its own — opening by itself. I couldn't see anything. There was a wall right in front of the bathroom, but I couldn't even see the windows anymore. I pushed the door closed, but it kept swinging open. I had no strength left.



I let it open.



I ran back to my room and shut the door. It was now 1:47 AM. I checked the clock on the wall — lit by moonlight coming through another window. I realized that someone was already inside the house. The door wasn't opening by itself — someone else was opening it.



I stared out the window. And I saw it.



A black figure standing in the yard. It had arms and legs. About 1.80 meters tall. Its head was shaped like a triangle — the bottom point was the neck, and the two top points looked like ears. No eyes. No nose. No mouth. It tilted its head and stared right at me.



Then I heard a voice from behind my room door. A man's voice.



"Open the door. The ambulance is here."



I quickly slid under the bed and covered every gap with a blanket. I pressed my back against the floor. Then I froze.



I remembered — I didn't call the ambulance. I never dialed. I had the phone, but I never finished the call. They were lying to me. It was a trap.



My hands started shaking uncontrollably. They started kicking my door — the same force as the wall breaking earlier. And then I remembered — the black figure saw me hide under the bed. They knew exactly where I was.



I knew there were two of them. I knew I had to run.



I crawled out from under the bed. They were kicking harder now. I grabbed my brother's hammer from his toolbox — he was away for work. I smashed the window. As I broke the glass, I heard a scream from the other side of the door — not human. It was so loud that other windows started shaking.
