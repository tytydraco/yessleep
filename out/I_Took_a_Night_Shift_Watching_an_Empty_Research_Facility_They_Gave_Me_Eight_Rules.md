I don't work there anymore.

And, no, before anyone asks, I can't tell you where it is; I signed an NDA. Although after what  
happened, that's probably the least important reason.

To provide some context, I'm a struggling writer. I worked at a coffee shop by day and was too exhausted to write by night, so I ended up not writing at all. These past months, I'd been  
searching for watch jobs that don't require too much moving around. I know, I know, it seems like too much to ask for, I knew that even while searching. But, as luck would have it, I found a flyer for a job as a night watch at an empty research facility. As far as I'd known at the time of applying, the facility had last been used since summer of 2016, a decade ago. I didn’t know what for. I still kind of don’t.

The job paid absurdly well, and all I had to do was sit in an observation room and watch CCTV footage of three hallways. At one hour intervals, my only job was to log the hallway’s behaviour. And when I say behaviour, I mean lights, temperature, intruders (if any). All that only took five minutes and left me with plenty of time for my novel. It was the perfect job.

Except, of course, it wasn’t. I wouldn’t be here if it was.

The job was from 12 a.m. to 7 a.m., which are pretty reasonable hours, if you ask me. The contract was only six pages; five were standard employment stuff. The sixth had a list of eight rules I had to follow:

>**Rule 1:** Do not fall asleep.

>**Rule 2:** Do not, under any circumstance, leave the observation room between 2 a.m. and 4:00 a.m. There is a bathroom in the room if you need it.

>**Rule 3:** Ignore any announcements over the intercom.

>**Rule 4:** If you hear knocking from inside the facility, continue writing your report.

>**Rule 5:** The experiment officially ended on 14 July 2016.

>**Rule 6:** If anyone tells you otherwise, they are part of the experiment.

>**Rule 7:** If the loudspeaker announces *"Trial 482 beginning,"* close the blackout shutters immediately.

>**Rule 8:** If someone asks to be let out, then they already were.

This was where I started to get finicky about the whole thing. I’d thought that the experiment was over, but here these people were, indirectly informing me that it wasn’t. I didn’t want to be caught up in some unethical human rights violation situation, but I needed the time and the money desperately. So my conscience eventually lost, and my job began.

I stayed in that observation room against my better judgement.

At exactly 2:17 a.m., screams would start. Then, an announcement: “*Trial 482. Beginning.*” Then I would close the shutters and be impervious to what went on outside. Every morning, my supervisor, Jeff Parsons (he told me his name was Jeff Parsons, but I have no way of knowing for sure) would enter at 7 a.m. sharp, and ask, “Did it know your name?”

I’d shake my head no, and he’d nod and say goodbye.

It went like this for the better part of 13 days. Day fourteen was when everything went wrong. Last night.

At exactly 2:17 a.m., the screams began. But this time, I could hear my name. It was garbled and distorted, but undoubtedly my name. I was already terrified at this point, but exactly a minute later, at 2:18, the screams stopped.

The screams had never stopped before.

At 2:19, someone knocked. Actually fucking knocked. On the observation room door. I waited for the PA announcement so I could close the shutters and hide, but, at 2:20, the only thing said over the PA was: “*Observer failure detected.*”

At this point, I couldn’t give a damn about the rules. I tried to close the shutters anyway, but they were jammed. I was left exposed to whatever happened out there when I wasn’t looking.

There was a fire, outside, but it seemed ephemeral. My intuition told me it was. Scientists were running around, test subjects were running around, all of them screaming their heads off. At 2:21, a woman ran up to the window and began pounding on it. I screamed. It was as if she was trying to reach me. Not escape. *Reach me.*

*YOU’RE EARLY,* she mouthed.

Then, I noticed something. The scientists weren’t looking at the fire. They were looking past me.

Behind me.

I looked back so fast I got whiplash, but there was nothing there. I turned back to the horrific scene outside, but they were all gone. No one was there. The fire had vanished. So had the people. I was alone, once more.

From 2:24 to 7:00 a.m., I began looking up the research facility obsessively. After stumbling upon some obscure 4Chan thread from 2020, I found that the experiment really *had* ended on the 14 of July, 2016. Everyone had perished in a massive fire.

It was then that my supervisor came to get me. 7:00 a.m. sharp.

“Did it know your name?” He asked.

And finally, I understood why he would ask such a question. “Yes.”

His face tightened. “I see. Then I’m terribly sorry to be the bearer of bad news, but—”

“I quit.” I told him. I knew what the bad news was, and it wasn't very bad at all: I didn't have to work here any longer.

I’ve moved back in with my ex. He’s understanding, only because I seem so fucked up. I still don’t know what I saw that night. My best bet is that it’s an event that won’t stop happening. An event that refuses to be history, at least from 2:17 to 4:00 a.m. every single day.

My best bet is that I wasn’t there to observe hallways at all.
