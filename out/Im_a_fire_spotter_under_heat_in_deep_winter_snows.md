Next year's retirement, and god help me I'm waiting for it. Thirty-three years. Thirty-three years with no one to talk to as cell service doesn't reach this far. Only voice in the room is an out-of-date television that can barely receive any channels and we won't get into static and picture quality. I came excited actually, to these surroundings that were like the superhero operations bases in my comics as a kid.

You get away from it all to leave it behind, right? To put something new on the brain, new purpose, new challenges. Jacqueline always told me that. We were imagining the honeymoon vacation that never worked out because of my job as a cop. One day, Rennie would grow up, go off to college, and we could afford to go anywhere. I thought by plane, then you could have more time once you got there. Jacqueline thought about taking a cruise, savor all of the coming trip before you reach it. Like some people enjoy the anticipation of Christmas more than actually having the tree and the presents and the dinner and stuff.

Christmas. Got all the trees I goddamned need, but who's gonna sit under it with me? Who am I gonna give a present to? Thirty-three hellish years. On and off I've been scared, terrified that I wouldn't recognize a human face when I saw one. Wouldn't know how to answer when I was spoken to, answers are cancelled outta the equation when you're talking to yourself. Could I love again? Grizzled and aged Rip Van Winkle who fell off the edge of humanity after my kid went to a correctional facility and my wife left me?

Seems I can't interpret nature anymore than my loved ones.

Year thirty-four, my new rebirth. Closet just behind the land surveillance room. All those calendars with the crosses on them? Testimony if you have to prove withstanding of sanity. It's about the dullest stretch of land for wildfires you could imagine. A rainforest. Everything here is wet and why did I not see that when I applied for the job? What do you do, climbing down the same flight of stairs to the base of the tower and up again when you've seen enough green forest to make you wanna scream? You live, like the feral, running in the wild.

Somehow my only brush over these past three decades has been of annoyance. The trees disturbed and rustling, the birds fleeing, the drone of the chopper as it lowers what you need down...for the next three-quarters of a year. Some scumbag flying gives you a personless wave and is gone...gone until two seasons have passed you by, if you'd call that ample.

I finally found it in myself ten years previous to spill blood in these mountains. It was bear meat, accessed with a TX Ultra 26.5 crossbow. My food supply was laid off in a forest, so I went into primal mode and started supplying myself. Hunger can do amazing things, especially when you get a kill on the first dart. 

We don't get fire in these forests, even though there's about triple the amount of water it would take to put a substantial one out. One night when I was following a wounded buck that didn't let its shortcomings have an effect on its speed, something exciting finally happened. I got lost. Couldn't see the frickin' markers I'd put on the trees as darkness fell and I hiked into higher altitude, so standing place was sleeping place. The ground was so damp I couldn't light a fire, and my eyes stayed open until dawn. Slept for the rest of the day when I finally got back.

Screw the buck.

Few days ago we got the first big winter blizzard. Seeing the world beyond my upper deck was out of the question. So I made a fire in the fireplace.

And I shouldn't have made a fire in the fireplace.

With wood stocked for two years from now, there's no need to worry about keeping it set. I select four solid logs to start with, from the older ones for optimum dryness. Set up the tipi shape like they teach you in scouts since day one with wood, dryer lint, and whatever declassified files in the library's worth of previous spotters' diaries. Once I see the first spark I nod and leave to make myself a drink while the fire gets going.

Opening the liquor cabinet, I take out a half-finished bottle of gin and top up a few tablespoons with tonic. Can't drink anything stiff here. If they do an inspection and they find the empty bottles, the chopping block follows. Jesus the fire smells...until I see the flame rapidly spreading along the wall. 

I drop my drink across the open stove burner in shock and it lights the hell up, a pillar of glaring orangey-red shooting up higher than I am tall. As my lungs fill with carcinogens and the stove detonates as the gasoline is exposed, I go for the extinguisher on the wall. But the fire has claimed that too. Time to leave my home of thirty-three years while I've still got my life.

No retirement party, no cake or presents. No well-wishing friends.

As I watch the remnants of the tower buried in smoke, I see the trees catch, the snow and ice melting under the intensity. Finally got a fire in the damned forest.

When does fire melt snow? And how can the two cross, snow blowing with the wind, fire against it? Like Sammy, the last spotter, put in his diary the year before I arrived: 

"I can feel the tongues as they stretch out from the fireplace. Somehow on the windiest night, they're thriving better than any fire I've built during my time here".

No phone, no hotline.

Gotta hope coast control sees the smoke as I stand here with heavy wet flakes falling on me.

And the flame tracing the path I have taken, headed right for where I'm standing.
