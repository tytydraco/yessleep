Three and a half days was all it took for me to realize that something was wrong in the house.

At first I wanted to attribute it to the lack of rest; after almost two months on the mainland with a shiny new job, going back to the routine had to hit me at least a little, right?

However, this time it felt… different.

I'll try to be brief because I haven't gotten to the bottom of this yet, and I might update as things happen. I want to document this, but I'd also like to ask for help. So, I'm going to start by listing at least three of the things that stood out to me the most.

* **First:** the moment I stepped inside, I realized we were remodeling.

On its own, it might seem like a silly detail. The house was old. Sure it needed a few touch-ups! A fresh coat of paint here, some plastering of the cracks there… However, that's not the kind of change I'm talking about. I would have accepted it without question if that were the case. But these changes were different. The couch out of place, chairs pointing towards corners, small tables tucked into awkward spots where there hadn't been anything before, shelves practically blocking doorways…

“Are we making a maze for Halloween?” I asked mom when I caught her moving one of my little brother's plastic chairs out of the way.

“Not at all,” she replied. “You know we don’t celebrate that here.”

“So?”

“So what?” She looked at me with a strange expression. I wouldn’t notice until later, under the dim light of the dining room, but the dark circles under her eyes were more pronounced than usual.

“Um, no. Nothing,” I shook my head, putting my hands in my pockets. My mom took another of the plastic chairs and placed it on top of the other, facing them against the wall. “Do you need help with that? I saw you moved the couch, too.”

“No, don't worry,” she said with a smile. “Why don't you go and check on your brother? He's doing his math homework. Do you think you could help him, honey?”

I bit my cheek and nodded. Honey wasn't unusual for her. Actually, that's what she called my dad, though. Not me. Still… for the time being, I let it go. I knew she got strangely nostalgic when I was gone for more than two weeks with my aunt and uncle, so that must be it. It had to be, because, just like with the furniture, I still don't know what other reason there could be.

“Okay, heh. You trust me a lot if you think I remember first-grade math, huh?” I said as I walked toward the hallway leading to the bedrooms. She shook her head and sat down on the couch. The television was off. I had the urge to go over and turn it on, like I always did, but before I could decide, she gave me a calm, almost vacant look.

“Would you mind closing the door when you're there?” she asked. I tilted my head, puzzled. “The teacher told me Rafaelito is having trouble concentrating. The last thing I need is to make it worse with all the noise.”

“Yes?” I said. “Are you going to move anything else? I can help you...”

“No, no. Don't worry.” She took a cushion and placed it on her lap, carefully tracing the intricate embroidered pattern. “Go on, help him. That way you'll be helping me more.”

* **Second:** while I was with my little brother, I noticed that one of the rooms we used for storage was open.

Again, not so unusual in itself, but what I glimpsed when I went to close it caught my attention.

All the curtain rolls, dusty boxes, and black bags of old clothes had disappeared. Or, if they hadn't disappeared, then they were piled up in very odd ways in the corners, then, as if to free up as much space as possible for something. But… For what, exactly? There was nothing in the way that warranted it. Or… Well, at least that's what I thought until I opened it up a little more.

“An old television?” I whispered. It was one of those old, bulky models. One of those my dad had probably taken from work a while back to fix up and put here. He used to do that—he liked giving things a second life. However, instead of leaving them on the floor gathering dust, he always took them to the garage with as much meticulousness and care as mom organized her sewing projects.

I ran my hand over its black casing, brushing away a timid layer of dust that, with the afternoon ray that came through the window, acted as glitter.

“Are they going to put it in Rafael's room?” I asked, noticing that it was unplugged. The cable was frayed, hastily patched with black tape… and, upon closer inspection, I saw that it looked a little burnt, despite the sticky moisture seeping beneath the tape.

“We're out of room to fit it, I fear,” said a male voice behind me. I jumped and straightened up as quickly as a child caught doing something mischievous.

“Dad?” I laughed nervously. My father, with his ever-present frown, raised an eyebrow. “Damn, haha. You scared me. Since when are you so quiet?”

“Your mom really likes the silence lately,” he explained, standing to one side of the door. His posture told me he didn't want me there. Was he going to start tidying that room too?

“Really?” I put my hands in my pockets, obeying his silent request. “When she called me, it didn't sound like it. Her voice rose above everything. Even above Blanquita, in the market.”

“She's been having migraines lately,” he said, and before I had a chance to ask, he closed the door and looked at me seriously. “Nothing serious, don't worry. We already went to the doctor. They said it was something to do with the light. That's why it's there,” he claimed, referring to the TV. I looked at the floor, lost in thought.

“So, that's why you two have been moving things around the house?” I asked. “And what's that about the…”

“Yes. More space seems to do her good,” he agreed, cutting off my question. I mentally backtracked.

“Okay, then. Are you sure you don’t need…?”

And just like that, my dad went to his room, leaving me speechless.

* **Third:** After dinner, I started feeling sick.

I had a stomach ache that wouldn't go away. I told my mom, and she said it must have been something in the salad that upset my stomach. Some seasoning or something. To be honest, I didn't usually eat salads with anything other than chicken. But that night's salad looked so appetizing that I couldn't say no, even though it had tomato in it. I hated tomatoes. Maybe that's what made me feel unwell?

“You don't have a fever,” my mom said by the bedside, taking the thermometer away. I felt like a little kid with his first cold, not a twenty-one-year-old. Although I suppose, when it comes down to it, you never really stop being that in your parents' eyes. “That's good. Maybe it won't hit you as hard.”

“Can I have the bucket?” I asked, nodding toward it. I didn't want to move. The warmth of the sheets was pleasant. “I don't feel like it, but... just in case.”

“Of course, honey,” she said. I closed my eyes as she ran her hand through my hair. “Call me if you need anything else, okay?”

“Okay. Uhm…” I said, biting my lower lip. “Should I text you or…?”

“With your voice, darling,” she said, stepping back. I opened my eyes slightly to see her tender smile. “My migraines don't let me look at my phone. If you call me, I'll listen.”

“Um, are you sure? Isn't yelling worse for you?”

“I promise I won’t,” she said, kissing my forehead. “Come on, get some rest. I’ll make you some soup tomorrow to help you feel better.”

“Thanks, Mom,” I whispered, burying my face in the pillow. The door clicked louder than usual, but I ignored it. I was tired and wanted to sleep. So I focused on the darkness and let the flashes of color transform into something more pleasant, like silly dreams. Or memories.

Everything but the fleshy, sinewy sensation of something stretching out from under the bed, making its way to nestle between my ribs.

I flinched, indifferent to the protests of my restless stomach. And when I bent down to spit out the bile that had been accumulating in my throat, I was hardly surprised to see the black substance slithering between a sickly yellow, the red of tomato, and the green of mucus.

I felt like an eternity passed between the second I spat out the last oily lump and when my eyes, lucid through the darkness of the room, saw it disappear.

Against my better judgment, I didn't want to call my parents. First, because I was a 21 year old man. Second, because... what would I tell them? That I vomited up burnt oil?

My stomach rumbled, threatening to repeat the experience. I lay down, a little shaky, and pulled the covers all over me, as if that could solve anything.

I know Mom would never put something in my food to harm me, so it had to be something else. However, with all this weird stuff going on, I'm starting to worry that she might do something else I haven't considered. I know my family. I know their personalities, and I know it's not in our nature to keep secrets from each other, just as it's not in our nature to hurt each other. So, I'm wondering…

Am I missing something?
