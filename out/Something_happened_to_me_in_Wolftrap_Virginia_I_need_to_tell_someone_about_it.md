I moved away from my family and friends in Colorado last week. I graduated with a masters in Mental Health Psychology and almost immediately after posting my resume I got a job offer from an institute in Virginia. The pay was too good to give up, and it fit with my goal to help the people that have been forgotten by society, those who were deemed too far gone. So I accepted it, I’d be stupid not to. 

After having a big going away party my parents insisted on having, I left. I packed up as many clothes and necessity’s as I could. With my dog Pepper riding shotgun, she’s always been my own little sidekick. We drove through town one last time, I wanted to remember the sights, sounds, and smells the best I could. I’ve driven out of town so many times, never having imagined the last time I would. I cried for a bit, I’ve never been too emotional, but this got me.

Once I got on the highway it all blurred together. I stopped periodically, but I made pretty good time. I drove all the way there in one go. 

My GPS said I had arrived but I didn’t see any houses until about 5 minutes after. I rolled into the center of town.

There were small stores lining the side of the street with dark woods standing behind them.

There was a general store with a broken window covered with plywood, and an open sign illuminating the front door. 

After that there was a pharmacy with a large Red Cross above the door.

Across the street there was a restaurant, the sign read “Rosie’s Diner”.

I turned onto a brick road. My new home was sat right at the end of a dead end road. Pepper had started whining not long after we drove into town. Must be she had to pee again, “This dog and her small bladder.” I muttered to myself. 

I parked. The engine clicked as it cooled. I opened the door and Pepper whined again, ears pinned back and her tail tucked. She’s never acted like this before, I mean this is the dog that I’ve had to stop from fighting a coyote twice her size.   
The landlord. Mato came out and waved at me, 

“You must be Ruth”. 

I replied, “Yep that’s me.. Mayto, right?” 

He sighed slightly, “It’s Mato, no big deal, it happens a lot. Long drive?” 

“Oh yes,” I said with a yawn

I continued “Quiet town huh?”

He forced a smile, “Very, I like to think it’s part of the charm.”

“Ready for the grand tour?”

I nodded and walked through the door. The air was thick and smelled like dust it felt like it was clinging to my throat with every breath. It was as if it had sat vacant for a long time. The floor creaked under foot, accompanied by a slight give in the floorboard. 

“Here’s your living room, kitchen, that doors the bedroom, there’s the bathroom. Over here is the basement, and this door leads upstairs but it’ll be locked. The other tenant, John lives up there,” he paused “he’s quiet and usually keeps to himself.”

“Well it all seems great Mato thank you.” I replied

“No problem at all, I’ll get out if your hair, rents due on the first, cash or check is good.” 

He started walking out the door, “Uh hey Mato, the keys?

“Oh I’m sorry, here you go” he said.

And then he left. The house went quiet just seconds after he crossed the threshold of the old wooden door.   
I heard a creak from the bedroom then I heard another, slower, heavier.   
I opened the door and yelled for Pepper to no avail. I looked out and saw her laying under my car, her head snapping from side to side.

I hesitated at the doorway, glancing back at the bedroom. I went out to get Pepper, looking for what she was seeing before I got too close. I didn’t see anything notable apart from the old weeping willow tree that leaned out from the woods. The branches hung low, reaching for the ground. I pulled Pepper out from under the car, she growled at me. She has never done that before. 

“I’m sorry honey, I know. But we’ll get used to it.”

I carried her to the house but stopped at the door. The bedroom looming at the end of the hallway. I stepped inside, feeling uncertain.  
I put Pepper down in the living room.   
She circled the plastic draped couch and laid down next to it with a low whine, ears still pinned back. She was staring at the bedroom door. 

I crept towards the bedroom door, cautiously opening it. As the door creaked open I thought I saw nothing. Until I noticed that in the middle of the bed was an indent, large and misshapen. Like someone, *something* had gotten out of bed mere seconds before I opened the door.   
I hesitated before entering. The air was too still. It felt wrong. 

I walked around the room, inspecting everything. It was extremely dusty, especially compared to the rest of the house. 

I reached out to touch the bed. My hand hovered over the sheet for a moment before I pressed down. 

It was warm. 

I jerked back. 

“Oh hell no,” I whispered. 

I quickly backed out of the room. Leaving the door open. Pepper barked and whined, still beside the couch.

“It’s fine” I said, trying to convince myself. 

“There’s no way I’m sleeping in there tonight.” I thought.

I grabbed a blanket from my bag, tossing it onto the couch. I laid down, staring down the hall at the open bedroom door. At some point I must have fallen asleep. 

I woke up, I checked my phone, 7:39. 

Remembering the events of last night I turned my head to the door, it was closed. 

Had I gotten up and closed it at some point during the night? I’d been so tired it’d be impossible to know. 

I got off the couch to look for Pepper. She had miraculously left the side of the couch. 

I found her staring out the window of the back door. I looked out and saw nothing, but I felt something. Uneasiness washed over me as I gazed upon the willow tree, I pulled down the blinds to calm Pepper, and to be honest, myself. 

I went out to my car and dug in some boxes until I found my work clothes. Jeans and a cardigan sweater. 

I went inside to feed Pepper and get dressed. I could hear John walking upstairs. His heavy footfalls caused dust from the ceiling to fall on my head. I brushed it off, patted Peppers back, and went out to my car. 

As the car started my phone rang. It took me a second to remember who the name belonged to. Alex, my older brother was calling. 

“Hey, how’s the new place?” He asked.

“Oh it’s.. different,” I replied.

“A little creepy, but at least the landlords nice, unlike your first.”

Alex laughed, “Yeah she was a trip, remember when she tried to charge me for when she broke the lock trying to get in?”

I laughed and agreed, but didn’t remember that, not at all.

There was a long pause. 

He turned more serious, “you didn’t sleep in there did you?” 

“What?” I replied, my grip tightening on the steering wheel. 

The call hung up. The radio was static. 

I navigated to my recent calls to call him back. There was nothing there. The last call I had in there was from yesterday, when I called my mom during the drive to my new home. 

I called Alex from my contacts.

He picked up after what felt like forever.

He answered with “Hey I’m at work, what’s up?”

“What do you mean what’s up? We were just on the phone and you were being weird as fuck!” I shouted.

“Ruth, I haven’t talked to you since you left. My boss is coming I gotta go. I’ll talk to you later okay?” And he hung up.

I sat in my driveway for too long, the clock showed that I would be late if I stayed much longer. 

I swallowed the fear I had for everything that  was going on, and pulled out to go to work.

My boss, Eleanor, met me at reception. 

“Ruth! Welcome, how are you settling in? 

“Oh it’s going great, it’s such a beautiful town.”

“Why thank you, I don’t personally live in town, but it seems to have a way of.. pulling you in.”

Eleanor invited me to walk with her and brought me to my office. The office was cold and clean. A light colored wooded desk sat in the middle of the room, with barren shelves that matched the desk. 

“I’ll give you a few to settle in, then I’ll take you to meet the patients.”

She left, I set down my laptop and a notebook. 

The chair creaked as I sat in it, it was clear it was pretty old. The leather was cracked and worn on the seat and armrests.

I got my computer logged into the internet and wrote down some notes. 

Eleanor came back, “All ready?”

I nodded and followed her out of the door. 

“Over here we have the main quarters. Where we have the level 4 patients, they’re who you’ll be working with.” 

She finished the tour of everywhere that matters for my job. And walked me into a room where there was a patient waiting for me.

“Ruth this is Danny, he’s been here for a while. I’ll be right outside the door if you need anything, and I’ll come check in after 15 minutes,” 

She whispered to me as she walked past, “He isn’t dangerous, not anymore. Don’t worry.”

I sat down across from Danny. 

“Hey Danny, how are you doing today?”

He stared at me blankly. 

I opened the file they had for him

It read as follows 

*Name:  Daniel \*\*\*\*\** 

*Date of Admission: 12/13/2012*

*Reason for Admission: Discovered in the forest with a decapitated corpse, death was under unclear circumstances.* 

*Patient Statement: “I swear I didn’t do anything to her. I love.. loved her with all my heart. Whatever that thing was it wasn’t human. It was in our house, it followed us in the woods. Christ. It killed her.”*

*Diagnosis: Severe PTSD*   
*Severe Paranoia*  
*Bipolar Disorder*  
*Schizophrenia* 

*Notes: Cannot rule out psychopathy.* 

I looked back to Danny, with a newfound curiosity for his situation. He was staring out of the small window to his side, facing the wood line. 

“So Danny,” He looked at me. 

“How are you feeling today, anything new you want to share?

“No.” Plain and simple.

“That’s alright Danny, you don’t *have* to talk to me. We can always just hang out until you’re more comfortable.

“You think I’m crazy, like everyone else does. You just want me to talk about how my wife died and why I did what they say I did” Danny accused. 

I stammered, “Of course not Danny,” 

He interrupted. “Please just call me Dan, I hate being called Danny.”

“Okay Dan. I don’t care about what they say. I just want to talk with you and try to help you feel better. 

“Sure you do, they all say the same thing. But you’re different, I can tell. You know what I saw, what happened to me. I see it in your eyes.” 

Not a moment too soon Eleanor opened the door. 

“Hey guys how’s it going in here?” 

Dan went back to blankly staring out the window. 

“Oh it’s good,” I replied “just had a chat with Dan here”

Eleanor beckoned me back to the hallway.

“Go back to your office and document everything he said, the police are strict about this one. They still haven’t found her head you know.”

I went to my office, with the contents of Dans file stuck in my head. Repeating over and over. 

“It was in our house.” 

“It was in our house.”

The rest of the day was mostly getting myself comfortable, meeting coworkers, and reading various files.

Strangely enough, several mentioned the same details as Dans, the “thing” in the woods. A death under odd circumstances. And worst of all, a missing head. 

The clock hit 5 and I went home. 

Pepper greeted me at the door, in a way that seemed like she was waiting for my protection, not my companionship. 

I know now that it was a stupid idea. But I put Pepper on her leash and went out to the willow tree with her. 

I mean, wouldn’t I be the crazy one if I let a bunch of psychiatric patients scare me? 

Everything I’d been experiencing can be explained away by any sane person. Old house settling, dog adjusting to a new environment, or just me scaring myself. 

So I decided I’ll prove it all wrong. I’ll go check out the damn tree. 

I basically dragged Pepper to the tree. Once I arrived at it I noticed a winding trail behind it, mostly covered by thorn bushes.

I pressed on. Squeezing past the bushes and dragging Pepper in tow. That is, until she slipped her collar and darted into the trees. 

I yelled after her to no avail. I could only hope I could find her further down the trail. I still had three hours of daylight left. 

I walked for 10 minutes, yelling her name periodically. 

I thought I heard her walking in the woods to my side. I heard a twig snap and turned, but I saw nothing, and heard nothing after. 

I kept walking, growing increasingly uneasy.  I could feel my legs trembling even worse with every step. 

I started to cry. 

God I hope Peppers okay. I don’t know what I would do without her. I know there’s bears in these woods. I really should’ve brought some bear spray. 

Then the sun set like a lightbulb blowing out. I looked at my phone to check the time, 1:47

I couldn’t have already been out there for that long, it was an hour at most. 

The trees felt closer in a way. Just minutes ago they were light up by the sun, then they were just silhouettes. 

“Maybe she found her way home and is waiting for me” I said to myself. 

I turned back but the trail didn’t look familiar. 

I kept walking until I heard a crunch behind me. Louder than the first time.

Then another.

And then another.

It wasn’t getting louder, no, it was getting closer. I spun around trying to find the source of the sound. 

All I achieved was losing track of which way led out. 

“Oh shit.” I cried out.

I picked the direction that felt the most right and ran. I ran for what felt like days but still I didn’t see the tree looming over the entrance.

At this point the plan shifted. 

If I turn back now I’ll just waste even more time. This trail has to end somewhere.

I ran again, hearing the crunching behind me still, growing louder and louder with each passing second. 

I thought I could hear Pepper barking up ahead, it was hard to tell with the wind whipping past my ears. 

And then I saw ahead of me, the end of the trail.

But there was no road. No field. No salvation. Only trees. And a rock in the middle of the trail.

No, not a rock. 

I got closer. 

It was Pepper.

Sliced cleanly down the middle. From tail to neck. 

Her head was gone. 

I broke, I fell to the ground in the fetal position screaming and crying. 

I couldn’t believe that my Pepper was gone. 

I could hear that the steps were getting close, within mere feet maybe. 

And then they stopped. 

I worked up the courage to turn and face the monster that had done this. 

Yet again there was nothing there. 

I heard a crunch and a rapid dragging sound behind me. 

Pepper was gone, a trail of blood leading strait into the woods. 

I ran like my life depended on it. I could hear the crunching of her bones grow distant as I fled. 

There were no footsteps behind me for the way out. 

Only the beat of my heart like a drum in a grand symphony. 

I was hit square in the face. 

It knocked me off my feet, almost knocking me out cold. 

I was made alert once more by the hot, wet liquid dripping onto my face.

I steadied myself on my feet and looked behind me. 

Hanging from a low tree branch was Peppers severed head, dripping blood from where her body should have been. 

I thought it had to be a nightmare, brought on by the ravings of a lunatic. 

But no, it was too real. 

Peppers eyes were gouged out, her tongue ripped like paper out of her mouth.

That’s when I noticed the best thing that could have happened in this situation. The branch that Peppers head was hanging from was one of the willow trees.

I could see the dimly lit kitchen of my house. Again I ran, my lungs burning like hellfire. I got to my car and left.

I left everything in that house. I called Mato screaming but all I heard was, “This number has been deactivated.” And a support number to call instead.

As I sped off I caught a glimpse of a sign in the yard through my mirror. 

“For sale”

The house that was dreary but still livable was now a run down, abandoned wreck that would only be worth the copper wiring in the walls.

I drove through town, noticing more of the same through my tear soaked vision. Every building was trashed, not a livable residence in sight, not a single operational store. 

It’s now been a week since everything happened. I’m in a motel I found somewhere along the road, I couldn’t tell you where I am. I can’t go home, not like this. Not without some sort of answers as to what monster plagues these lonesome hills. 

But what I can tell you is this. I just heard a creak come from the bed behind me, and then another.
