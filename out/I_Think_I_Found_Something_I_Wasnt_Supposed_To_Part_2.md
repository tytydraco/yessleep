I spent the morning with the map flattened out on the kitchen table next to a printout of the property lines I pulled off the county site, trying to get some sense of scale before I actually went walking around out there like an idiot with no plan. My grandfather's map isn't drawn to any real proportion, some distances are squished together and some are way stretched out, as if drawn from memory. I am also not even sure when this map was drawn. Either way it wasn’t exactly lining up to the counties.

Once I had something workable I grabbed Otis and headed out, figuring he might as well come along. Except he did his usual thing, planted his feet right at the tree line and would not budge no matter how much I coaxed him. So I ended up walking him back inside and heading out on my own.

Not exactly sure what I expected to find when I went out there, but what I found, when I actually found it was almost a relief at how normal it seemed.

Each spot that I came to was just a stack of stones, and not the well balanced kind. They were clearly hand stacked almost a pile with a wide base that got more narrow towards its peak. None of it screamed anything at me. My first thought was some sort of hunting marker, the property was big and I remember my grandfather loved hunting. I myself have never been a big hunter but I assumed having a natural marker to remember a spot would be better than some artificial one, but I could be completely off base with that. I actually started to relax a little out there.

The last marker on the map was further out than the rest, right up near what I think is the edge of the actual property line in the centerline of all the other markers, in a section of woods thick enough that the light was already going gray back there even though it wasn't anywhere close to sunset yet.

At first I didn't think there was anything at this spot at all, no stones or anything obviously placed like the others. I almost just turned around. But there was a small symbol drawn next to this point on the map, different from the plain circles marking everywhere else, kind of an angular shape, like a stretched out triangle with two shorter lines crossing near the top of it. Almost like horns. It's not a symbol I recognize from anywhere, not religious stuff I know of, not any surveying or hunting mark I've seen. I crouched down comparing it to the map for a good minute before I noticed the same symbol carved low into the base of a nearby tree, right down near the roots, not at eye level where you'd actually notice it walking by.

That's when I saw the other thing, a few feet off, half buried in the dirt and leaf litter at the base of that same tree.

It's a wallet. Or what's left of one. The leather's mostly gone soft and rotted. The damn actually came apart a little just from me picking it up. Inside, stuck together, was what I'm pretty sure used to be an ID card. Couldn't get a name off it at all, but could see the remnants of a letter or two. The lamination's cracked and clouded over, whatever ink was on it has bled and faded to near nothing, I could just barely make out where a photo used to be, no actual face left in it anymore. No way for me to know how old it is. Could be ten years

I didn't touch anything else out there. Bagged the wallet, took some pictures of the tree, the symbol, the spot on the map, and walked back a lot faster than I walked out.

Part of me wants to just bring this into the sheriff's office in town, except all I've got is a rotted wallet and a symbol I've never seen before, on my own property, that my dead grandfather apparently marked on a hand drawn map however long ago. Doesn’t really scream sufficient evidence of anything really.

I'm going to actually sit down and read through the journal properly tonight, front to back this time instead of skimming like I did before. I know I've been putting that off a little. I really wanted to explore the map first.
