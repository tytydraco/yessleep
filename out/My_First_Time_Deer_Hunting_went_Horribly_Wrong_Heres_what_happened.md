I was told the best thing I could do for my anxiety around forests was to journal about it. I think it started with my first time deer hunting all those years ago. I’m posting it here to see what you all think that *thing* might have been. 

A tiny, styrofoam cup warmed my fingers. My dad was outside filling the car with gas. He told me to get whatever I wanted coffee-wise, not that I had much of a preference since I was a fourth grader at the time. A bit had spilled over when I dozed off. I was doing my best to mop it with cheap brown paper napkins before he came inside. 

“God damn it, child” said my dad with a laugh once he saw my small disaster.  He helped me clean it up and filled his own cup.

The cashier was the sort of woman who looked well beyond her years from a daily habit of meth and tobacco. She gave us a pointed look when we came up to the counter, drinks in hand. 

“Ain’t she a little young for coffee?” she croaked at us with a voice raspy from smoking. 

“That’s up to me ma’am, but we are going  hunting if that makes any difference” my father replied as he got a few bills out of his wallet. 

“Ah” she started, clicking away on the cash register “Best of luck with that. I've heard the hunters have all been getting skunked. Some are blaming the cougars, others the bluetongue.”

“I'm sure we will be alright, it's her first season so I'm hoping we get something” 

“Well have fun out there, your total is on the screen” she said with a grin that had far too few teeth.

We paid, left Casey's and walked to the old beat up 4runner in the brisk November air. The faint green glow of the dashboard clock read 4:16 am. In the back seat lay my father’s rucksack from when he was in the Army. It was stuffed with a homemade ghillie blanket, a tarp, and some orange beanies. There were also our two guns. The first was the family shotgun with a scene of a forest carved into the gunstock. Every member of my family had gotten their first deer with that rifle, and this year, it would be my turn. My father was carrying a rifle with gold hardware called Henry, which, at the time, I thought was a funny name to give a gun. It shot the biggest bullets I had ever seen. 

“Don't say anything about the coffee, I don't want to hear your mom bitch about it," said my father as he lit up a Lucky Stripe and took a drag “Same goes for the cigs” 

“What coffee?” I smiled back, reveling in the small, shared rebellion. 

Between missing school for a day and getting to have a cup of coffee, I was the sort of high that only children could achieve when they do something that feels grown up. 

Rolling through the pitch black early morning,  we made our way away from the Kansas-Missouri border. The hazy cornfields gave way to soy fields which slowly became broken up with larger and larger chunks of the Ozarks. A bobcat darted in front of the car, a quick blip before ducking back into the brush. As we got further away from town, the remaining stars became more visible. I did my best to look up at them, pressing my face to the car window. 

The car was parked on the side of a soybean field bordered by the woods. The deer loved to eat from the crops and the landowner had told my father of a large herd of doe that did just that.  The cold morning air blasted me a little more awake when the car door opened. I pulled my father’s old camo jacket a little tighter. I could see my breath as we grabbed our supplies from the car. The shoulder strap of the family shotgun slipped over my head. 

“Remember, keep it upright when over your shoulder, and point towards the ground when you are carrying it. Don’t point it towards anything you don’t wish to destroy. Especially me." My father instructed

He gave me a proud little smile before tossing me a neon orange beanie and putting on the matching one.  
   
“Also, we have to wear these fuck ass hats. That way, if there are any idiots trespassing, they don’t shoot us… Probably” 

As we walked along the fence that separated the field from the forest, a section of the barbed-wire had come down. One of the rusted metal stakes that held up the barbed wire was rusted and encrusted in a thick, dark fluid. Similar patches of the substance traced back into the forest. My father also took note of it. 

“Looks like a deer had a nasty fall when it tried to jump over. Come on Kinsey.”

I stared for a second more before catching up with my father. We had to be in position half an hour before the sun rose. This way, we could take a shot the second it was legal. But despite this rule, just as we entered the woods, a few shots rang out in the distance. 

“God damn amateur hour,” my father scoffed. “Make sure you have your beanie nice and visible. There are jackasses afoot” 

I gave a little nod before tugging my hat down further on my head. 

I followed behind my father, trying to match each place he stepped. He always knew where to place each footfall so it avoided crunching down on the leaves. It was so dark, but my eyes soon adjusted. As we made our way through the woods, looking for the spot that my father had scouted out beforehand, we came across our first deer. Or rather, half a deer. 

It was a large doe, now swollen from decay. The upper half of her was in a tree, the missing half of her nowhere to be seen. She looked down on us, like a woodland guardian angel, with no judgment for us hunters in her glassy black eyes. She would have looked so soft and sweet, but the lower half of her face shortened any sentiment. 

Her mandible had been ripped from its joints and was now missing. It looked like when her jaw had been taken, the skin of her neck had been peeled along with it. Down to her chest, raw sinew had been exposed, still glistenly wet. At the base of the tree, was a pile of corn covered in more of that black tar. 

“Mountain Lion, I think,” my father whispered to me. “Don't worry, it's why I've got the 45-70. If we see something odd, get behind me. He paused to look over to me. I’m sure I wasn’t hiding my worry well, but I was doing my best to put on a brave face. 

“Try not to think about it much. Nature can be fucked.”   

We moved on. 

After walking further into the forest, we got to the area my father had chosen for us. Red oaks framed out a small clearing. Just beyond it, there was a patch of thick brush that looked like it had been crunched down.

“See that?” My father whispered, “That's where the deer bed down. Hopefully, we will see a few doe pass through the clearing to sleep.” 

We started to set up on a small slope  downwind from the clearing. My father took a drab tarp and laid it down. We laid in prone, the riffles able to just peak over the slope to aim towards the clearing. The ghillie blanket was thrown over the two of us, blending us into the fallen foliage of the forest floor. 

Even though I held the rifle as tight as I could to my shoulder, I felt myself starting to drift off as I started to warm up under the cover. The sky was just starting to turn a deep navy. A few birds had woken and gave little chirps. Next to my father, I knew I was safe. The novelty of the day and the cheap coffee lost its hold on me. The peacefulness of the woods lulled me to sleep as my eyelids grew heavy. 

I startled awake to the sound of something running through the woods. 

“A deer?” I asked excitedly as my heart jumped

I snuggled the gun tighter into the crook of my arm, careful to keep my finger off the trigger until I had a shot. 

“Fuck, I don’t think so. Hold fire Kinsey,” my father whispered before looking over to me and asking “Where are you asleep?” 

“Maybe” I peeped back as I blinked my bleary eyes. 

Before my father could further press, our attention snapped back to the approaching rustling. The sun wasn’t over the horizon yet, but it was lighter still which made it easier to see through the woods. It wasn't a deer, but a man. 

His face was turned away from us when he entered  into the clearing. Huffing and puffing, he hutched over like he had been sprinting a marathon. Long scraggly grey hair was tucked under a trucker hat. His pair of olive overalls looked wet. My father poked his head up slightly to get a better view of the man, but didn’t lower his gun. 

He hollered out to the man “Hey Bubba! What are you doing here? This is private property and there ain’t supposed to be anybody else out here.”

“Oh thank God, man” He panicked. “I’m so sorry we were baiting on your land, but you gotta help me. I don’t know what the fuck just got my brother. I think it might have been a cougar or something. I dropped my gun when I was high tailing it out of there. I’m not armed. Just, you gotta help me, man” 

When he turned in our direction, I realized the wetness was from blood. The color was more apparent on his front, crimson all over his face and in his aging beard. 

In the distance, something gave a gurgly whistle pitch that pierced through the trees. The old man was startled by it and started to back away from where the sound had come from. 

“Stay quiet ”, My father whispered to me.   
“Be still. If you see a deer, shoot it here.” He poked me right where my arm met my back.  
“If you see anything else, shoot it in the face.“

I nodded and did my best to sink into the forest floor, trying to be invisible to both the man and whatever else was out there. 

“Did you get a shot on it?” My father asked. The man in the clearing whirled around, not exactly catching where we were. His eyes looked around, wide and wild. 

“My brother hit it with some buckshot, but the thing didn't even stumble. It turned around and charged him. I fired a few times, but it just kept ripping him to shreds. I ran off…” explained the stranger, still looking around, trying to spot us with no luck. 

“...To get help,” he added, as if he needed to justify his action. There was a tinge of regret in his voice. 

The same strained groan echoed through the forest, starting out as a loud grumble before raising in pitch.  This time was much closer. 

“Alright, Bubba. I can help you. But not a word of this to the Department of Wildlife. I don't need them on my ass for this.” My father offered. 

The expression that crossed over the man’s face should have been one of relief. Instead, it contorted in fear. A heavy *thunk* sounded as something landed in a tree right to our left and that strained high pitch moan rang, ear splittingly loud this time. I stiffened in my hiding spot.   
The man tried to run off. He didn’t get very far before the thing from the tree launched into the clearing. 

This time, it wasn't a deer.  
Nor a cougar,    
Nor a man,   
But something in between. 

That black tar, no, its sickly ebony blood, matted its fur. Its teeth, human like, were clenched into a painful grimace. Its arms rippled with muscles as it bounded towards the stranger. It thrusted him to the ground. The gurgly yowl let out again through clenched teeth. It stuck its clawed fingernails into the man’s mouth and started to pull with inhuman strength. The man tried to scream as loud as he could afford with the weight of the monster pressing down on him, but not much more escaped him than a pained exclamation. 

My father was up on his feet, cocking the lever action with a satisfying click.   
“Get up, get up, leave the shit.” He shouted “We have to get back to the car” 

A sickening, wet *pop pop* came from the old man’s jaw as it was ripped off one side at a time and thrown to the side. The old man gave a final, rattly exhale and stopped his struggle. 

My father aimed his rifle and  shot as I scurried up from the ground. It hit the creature right in the side of its sneer. One side of its face was blasted open. In a mist of black blood and teeth shards, a gaping hole was left in its place. It stumbled and collapsed to the ground. 

We tore through the now quiet forest. I could hear my father reload and cock his gun behind me as we sprinted. When we got to the beat up car, he practically threw me into the passenger seat along with his rifle and a box of bullets. As the car doors slammed, in the not too far off distance, I could hear the howl echo through the trees. No longer restrained to whistling through a lockjaw smile, it was now louder and more desperate. 

“Shit” my father said under his breath as he pulled away from the field and drove onto the dirt road. He rolled down his window once we got a bit away. I looked at him horrified. 

“Shhhh…. listen.” he said quietly. 

You could hear the sound of something bounding through the forest, keeping pace with the car. He ragged out the 4runner.  I watched the speedometer rocket past 100 mph. 

“Watch for deer”, he told me, “the last thing we need is to hit one”.  

Suddenly, the noise of the pursuit stopped, and I saw a brief moment of relief pass over my father’s face. Yet, it disappeared as quickly as the darkness was getting replaced by the sunrise. 

Something crashed down with a metallic *crunch* on the roof. The car swerved but just barely remained on the road. That same, now freshly bloody, half-man half-cougar arm came through my father’s open window and grabbed his throat and patted around. 

Looking for something it could get its fingers into.   
Looking for a jaw.

I screamed loud enough that I could still hear myself over the creature's wet howl from the roof. I looked around, in desperation for anything that might help my dad. He struggled with the animalistic arm that clawed at his face. My eyes landed on Henry, stilled cocked and loaded. 

I grabbed my father’s rifle and I rested the butt of the gun on the floor board.    
I pointed it straight up at the beast.   
Straight at what I intended to destroy.   
I pulled the trigger. 

The barrel flashed and my hearing all but left me except for a loud ringing. My father slammed on the brakes and the creature was thrust forward, rolling over and cracking the windshield. The beast landed in front of the car, awash in the dusty LED glow of the headlights. 

The creature looked like a deer if it were made from all the parts that a taxidermist had left over. The four legs all looked like a humans’ arms covered with a deer pelt. The torso looked like it could have been a bear, dark brown and bulky. The head still had antlers, but it looked like the face of a mountain lion had been stretched too tight and stapled over the skull of a buck. Its chest now had a gaping bloody cavity where I assume my shot rang true.

“FUCK YOU” My father screamed, the fury of it dull in my ears which where still rung. He pressed down hard on the gas, ramming the car up and over the creature. 

I jostled around like we were going over a speed bump. Once we were over it, my father slammed the gear into reverse and floored it. Again, we thudded over the creature. Still not content, my father sent the car forward a final time, the ride less rocky than before since the creature was now slightly flattened .   
  
After this third hit. My dad stopped the car and we both looked at each other, bewildered. In the rear view mirror, the lump of the animal was still visible. 

“Hand me my gun, Kinsey”, my father ordered. 

I handed him Henry and the box of bullets. My dad got out of the car.  I saw him walk around to the body. He pointed down at where I think the head might have been and fired. He reloaded and shot again. 

At this, I turned to face the front, and hugged on to my legs, exhausted from the violence of the day. The sun was just now brushing its golden rays on the forest. The world was awash in fiery tones of copper and rust as the fall leaves lit up. 

Nature might have been pretty, but it was truly, deeply fucked. 

The trunk of the car was opened. A body was thrust into the back, alongside a head, now severed   
from its body.

“Dad,” I asked in fear,  “What are you doing?”

“Darling”, started my father as he got settled in the front seat. He was now covered in the black blood of the creature, “I haven’t a clue what this thing is. But, if you discover a new species, you get naming rights. So, tomorrow morning, I'm taking this down to the local university and I'm naming it. I’m still trying to come up with a good name, maybe something like an Ozark Bitchface.”

He clicked his seatbelt and lit another cigarette. 

“Not a fucking word to your mother. I don’t want to hear about it until we make the paper.” 

And with that, my first deer season came to an end
