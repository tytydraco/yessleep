I first noticed the man outside my house on a Thursday.

He was standing across the street, underneath a streetlight.

Nothing unusual about that, except he was staring directly at my bedroom window.

I watched him for maybe thirty seconds before closing the curtains.

When I looked again, he was gone.

I didn't think much of it until the following night.

Same streetlight. Same man.

Same position.

This time, I took a picture.

It came out blurry, but I could clearly make out someone standing there.

I showed my neighbor the next morning.

He barely glanced at it.

"Probably someone waiting for a ride."

I agreed, mostly because I didn't want to sound paranoid.

Then things started happening inside my house.

My kitchen cabinet would be open when I returned home. My television would be switched to a different channel. Once, I found a cup of coffee on my desk.

I don't drink coffee.

I live alone.

I started checking the locks before leaving and taking pictures of every room.

For a while, that helped.

Until I started noticing things that weren't there when I left.

One afternoon, I came home and found my bedroom window slightly open.

I always lock it.

I checked the entire house. Nobody was there.

That night, I barely slept.

At around 2:14 AM, I heard footsteps in the hallway.

Slow, deliberate footsteps.

They stopped outside my bedroom.

Then I heard three quiet knocks.

I didn't answer.

After a few seconds, the footsteps continued toward the kitchen.

I called the police.

They searched the house and found nothing.

One officer suggested I might have heard something outside.

I wanted to believe him.

The next day, I installed a small camera in my living room.

I didn't tell anyone.

For three nights, nothing happened.

On the fourth night, I checked the footage before going to bed.

At 2:14 AM, the footage showed me walking from the living room into my bedroom.

Eleven minutes later, my bedroom door opened.

A man stepped out.

He stood in the hallway for a moment, looking toward the living room.

Then he walked past the camera.

I paused the footage.

I couldn't recognize his face.

But I could recognize his clothes.

He was wearing the same shirt I had worn that day.

I looked down at myself.

I was wearing it again.

I immediately called my neighbor and asked him to come over.

He watched the footage.

Then he looked at me.

"That's you."

I told him it wasn't.

He pointed at the screen.

"Look at the shirt."

I told him to watch the entire recording.

We watched it again.

I entered the bedroom.

Eleven minutes later, someone wearing my clothes walked out.

My neighbor stopped talking.

The following morning, I sent the footage to my mother.

She called me almost immediately.

"Why are you doing this again?"

I asked what she meant.

She went quiet.

Then she said, "You know there's nobody else in that house."

I told her I had never said there was.

She replied:

"Then why do you keep asking us to check?"

I didn't understand.

I asked her when I had ever asked her to check.

She said she had received a phone call from me the previous night.

At 2:14 AM.

I checked my call history.

There was no outgoing call.

That afternoon, I asked my neighbor to come over again.

I wanted someone else in the house. I didn't care if he thought I was losing my mind.

We checked every room.

When we reached my bedroom, he stopped.

There was a wet footprint on the floor beside my bed.

Just one.

It was clearly visible against the dusty floorboards.

I looked at my shoes.

Then his.

Neither of them matched.

My neighbor crouched down to examine it.

He didn't say anything for a while.

Then he stood up and asked me something.

"Didn't you say you locked the bedroom window?"

I nodded.

He looked toward it.

The window was still locked.

We checked the living room camera again.

At 2:14 AM, I entered my bedroom.

Eleven minutes later, the man came out.

My neighbor leaned closer to the screen.

Then he pointed at the man's hand.

"Pause it."

I did.

The man was wearing a silver ring.

My neighbor stared at it for a long time.

Then he quietly said, "That's strange."

I asked him what was strange.

He said nothing.

My neighbor left shortly afterward.

He hasn't answered my messages since.

I haven't slept properly since.

Last night, I heard the footsteps again.

This time, I didn't stay in bed.

I opened my bedroom door and looked into the hallway.

Nobody was there.

But the front door was unlocked.

I checked the living room camera.

The footage showed me walking out of my bedroom at 2:14 AM.

I stood in the hallway.

Looked directly at the camera.

And smiled.

I don't remember doing that.

I haven't shown anyone the footage.

I'm afraid they'll say it's me again.

But there's something I haven't told anyone.

The man outside my window is still there.

Every night.

And last night, for the first time, 

**He was standing on the other side of the glass**.

I didn't move.

I didn't even turn on the lights.

I just watched him through the curtains.

Then my phone vibrated.

It was a message from my neighbor.

The first one he'd sent me since seeing the footprint.

**"I checked the footage again. You need to leave that house."**

A second message arrived.

"I didn't tell you this earlier because I thought you'd get upset."

Then a photograph.

It was a picture of my bedroom, taken from inside the house.

The timestamp was from three days ago.

I was asleep in bed.

**And beside me, standing perfectly still, was a man.**

I zoomed in on his hand.

A silver ring.

The same one from the camera footage.

I looked at the message again.

Then I looked through my bedroom window.

The man outside was gone.

I called my neighbor.

No answer.

I tried again.

Still nothing.

Then I received one final message.

**"Please don't tell him I showed you."**

I stared at the screen.

I hadn't told my neighbor about the man outside my window.

I hadn't told him I was watching him.

I hadn't even told him I was awake.

And then I noticed something in the photograph I'd missed.

The bedroom door was visible in the background.

It was slightly open.

Someone was standing in the gap.

I zoomed in.

It was my neighbor.

He was looking directly at the camera.

And he was wearing the same silver ring.
