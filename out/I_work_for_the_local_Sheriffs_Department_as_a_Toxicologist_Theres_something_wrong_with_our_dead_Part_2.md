\--------

I drove back to the lab with the blood and urine samples in my passenger seat. I pulled in to the drop-off parking spot directly in front of the building. Typically, that spot was reserved for temporary parking, but it was a Friday afternoon and most other people in the building had left for the weekend.

I briefly surveyed the parking lot before opening my car door. I grabbed the bag of urine samples and balanced the rack blood vials on my wrist as I walked to the front lab door, fishing my keychain out of my scrub pants pocket. I quickly unlocked the door's electric lock with my fob. I slipped inside the lab's front office area before securely closing the door behind me. While the electronic lock was solid, the front door was a bit flimsy and with the jail nearby and the building almost empty, I did what I could to avoid drawing the attention of any potentially violent recent-releases who may be wandering the area. There had been incidents in the past, and I just don't trust that front door to be my sole layer of protection from someone's misdirected, drug-fueled rage.

After setting down the samples near my desk, I walked a lap around the lab to see if any of my coworkers were still there. The lab was empty, so I took a seat in my desk chair with a sigh and started logging in the samples I'd just picked up.

I'd logged all of about five samples when I heard a loud bang outside. I flinched at the sudden noise interrupting the quiet lab. There was a second bang, followed by the sound of someone shouting nearby. Acting reflexively, I popped up out of my chair, walked up to the front door, and started slowly closing the blinds across the small window in the door. I looked around for the source of the commotion, and with the blinds only halfway closed, I froze.

Just outside the door was what looked like a man. He was on his knees, rifling through the large metal trash bin that'd been tipped over. That explained the bangs. That trash bin was decently heavy and loud when it got bumped into or moved around. I watched the man through the half-open blinds, unsure what he was rummaging through the garbage for.

The man shot out of the bin and stood up so suddenly that I flinched again and my heart rate picked up. He tilted his head up and appeared to be sniffing the air. Continuing to take in deep breaths through his nose, he slowly walked up to the lab door. When he was close enough to the small window, I noticed his eyes seemed to be covered in a milky white film, like severe cataracts. 

"Weird," I breathed, barely audible.

My comment to myself was apparently loud enough for the man to hear. He started screaming and throwing himself at the door. I caught a glimpse of spit being flung on to the small window as I quickly finished closing the blinds.

The banging on the door continued, and I rushed into the back room of the lab, closing a second electronically locked door behind me. I reached into my pocket and pulled out my cell phone to call watch command to come deal with this guy.

Just as I was about to hit the call button, however, the banging on the door stopped. The lab became quiet again, and I continued to listen for several more minutes before I opened the door to the back room and stepped into the front office area. When I slowly made it back to the front door and peeked through the blinds, the man was gone. All that remained were some spots of blood and saliva on the small window.

Shaken up, I texted my boss about what'd happened and reported that I no longer saw the man. He texted back after a few minutes to let me know he'd asked a patrol car to swing by and check the area. I breathed a sigh of relief and tried to get my hands to stop shaking long enough for me to finish typing in the information for the rest of the samples on my desk.

When I grabbed the final blood vial in the rack to log its information, I paused. This was the sample from the body Sandra had called Mr. Twitchy. I gently inverted the tube while holding it up to the light of my computer screen. It was almost completely opaque, and so dark of a red that it appeared black. There were a few chunks floating around, and I groaned. This sample was going to coagulate quickly and be a bitch to work with on Monday.

I finished logging the samples, went back to the back room to the lab refrigerator, and stored the samples in the coroner's section I kept clear for Friday pick-ups. I closed up, ensuring that everything that should be locked was, in fact, locked. I set the alarm for the back room, and gathered my purse, keychain, and water bottle from my desk in the front. I took another peek through the blinds at the front door to scan the parking lot. The patrol car must've come by at some point, as the trash bin had been returned to its usual spot by the door. They never did stop and check on me in the lab. Typical. The lab is never really important until it is.

Seeing nothing out of the ordinary, I set the alarm for the front room and walked outside to my car. I reached for the handle to my passenger side door to unlock it. Swinging open the door, I haphazardly tossed my things on my passenger seat while I pondered what to pick up for dinner on my way home. Just as I was about to step back and shut the car door, I heard running footsteps behind me. I had only enough time to turn around before being slammed into by that guy from earlier.

The force of his tackle had sent me tumbling backwards into my passenger seat. He started screaming again, and his bloodied hands grabbed blindly, looking for a hold. I placed a solid kick directly into the center of his chest. He clutched at my shoe as he started to fall back, but the blood on his hands caused his grip to slip. As soon as he hit the ground, I pulled my legs in, grabbed the car door, slammed it shut and hit the button to lock all four doors. I located my phone while climbing unceremoniously into the driver's seat. I immediately started my car and called watch command.

The man started to get up and ran at the passenger side of my car. He got a few punches in as I pulled out of the parking lot. The last I saw of him was in my rear view mirror. He just stood there, head tilted towards the sky, taking in deep breaths through his nose.

It felt like my phone rang forever, but finally someone answered.

"Watch command," a gruff male voice said.

"Hey. Hi. Um, sorry.. this is Mandy. From the crime lab. There's uh.. there's this guy outside, he attacked? Me. In the parking lot. Outside. I'm in my car and driving away now, but he's still there," I said, trying to keep my voice from shaking too much.

"Oh, yeah, patrol said they drove by and didn't see anything earlier. I'll call someone back out. Are you injured?"

I took a quick inventory of my body. "Uh.. no. Nope, I'm ok."

"Ok then, well sorry about that, Mandy. We'll get the situation taken care of. Try to have a good weekend, and we'll have Sergeant keep you lab peeps updated."

"Ok, thank you. Have a good one," I said as I clicked the end call button on my phone.

I drove home with my hands wrapped around my steering wheel so tightly that my knuckles turned white and my fingers ached. As I pulled up to my house, I took a deep breath and tried to stop my shaking. When I opened my car door and stepped out, I noticed blood smeared on the shoe that the guy had tried to get a hold of. Most of the blood was red, but there were some clots in the smear that looked nearly black.
