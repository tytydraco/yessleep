I know it sounds insane, but there is a man-baby in my apartment that thinks I am its mother.

I am not talking about an infant or a grown man. This is something else entirely. Something in-between.

Picture a six-foot-tall baby with facial hair and a large bulbous head. It moves like a child but bears the weight and strength of a Swedish bodybuilder. It also has a fixation with swearing if you can believe it...I’m not sure why.

It’s terrifying. I’m terrified.

Sound strange to you? I sure hope so. Believe it or not, everyone around me thinks I am the insane one for suggesting that this is anything but normal. And that isn’t even the half of it. I find myself in a surreal reality that is trying to coerce me into going along with this sick charade.

My mind tells me I am in danger. Reality whispers that I am safe.

*Reality*.

Such a weird concept.

The very fabric of my existence is not as concrete as I once thought. The laws of physics are no longer a comfortable boundary I sit within, but an ocean where I am eternally scrambling for the shore.

I pray my story is being written in its original state, and you are reading it with my words still intact. Unfortunately, it is more likely that the words are shifting beneath me like sand. The end result will probably end up being an elaborate lie that reality has breathed into existence to cover its tracks.

Unless I win. I have to win.

Before we discuss that journey, let me introduce myself. Recapping things I know to be true helps me catch the lies before they take hold. That’s what Dr. Han told me at least. But we’ll get to that shortly.

My name is Kara. I am 33, single, and my life has been relatively uneventful up to this point. I have a boring job, and I am a proud mother to a—

Dammit.

There it goes again.

I am not a parent. Never have been. That’s the lie that set this whole nightmare into motion. 

I was returning home from work on a quiet Monday afternoon when this all started. The start of the day was largely uneventful. After a handful of meetings, a dozen emails, and a few bouts of small talk with coworkers, it was time to head home.

I was looking forward to settling in for the night and enjoying some take out while my favorite garbage reality show plays in the background. Sounds like a perfect night compared to the nightmare I am dealing with now.

I entered my home and tossed my work accessories aside. Nothing was out of place, as expected. I flipped the lights and started unboxing my meal when an acrid smell hit me. I pressed the box up to my nose and realized it was coming from somewhere else—somewhere deeper in my apartment.

I opened the trash can. Fresh plastic and no sign of spoiled food was all I found. I followed the smell through the living room to my closed bedroom door, a door that was open when I left for work this morning.

I opened it slowly. It creaked open with a loud groan that made me wince. I peered in. I held my hand to my mouth to keep myself from screaming.

A large crib the size of my bed towered in the corner of the room. Large adult diapers littered the floor and left crusty stains all over the surrounding carpet. At the opposite end of the room sat the monster.

The man-baby.

A large hairy man with baby-like skin and a large, bulbous head coo’d and tossed toys at the wall, leaving deep dents. His eyes were asymmetrical and his teeth were nonexistent. A frizzy beard hung from his smooth red face.

He turned to me with an expression of familiarity, excitement, and rage. 

As he stood, he babbled. A mess of garbled syllables escaped his large lips. It was baby talk, but his deep cracking voice made it sound inhuman.

He stood clumsily on his large chubby feet and his face curled into a snarl. 

“B…bu…bi-“ bubbled out of his lips. Then he screamed a single word.

“BITCH!”

I watched in horror as he lumbered over to me with surprising speed. I turned to run but it was too late, he had me by the hair and dragged me backwards into the room. 

I screamed and punched at his balloon fingers but they didn’t let up. My right hand managed to snag a toy off the soiled carpet and I jammed it into his side, sending him into hysterics.

Before he could finish his tantrum I leapt out the room and slammed the door behind me. I held the handle while frantically scanning the area for some way to lock him in from the outside. 

After a few minutes of him yelling obscenities and pounding against the door, I realized he wasn’t even trying to turn the handle.

I don’t think he knew how.

The cops arrived a few minutes later, but the wait felt like an eternity. I told them about everything—the crib, the diapers, the man-baby. They asked how he managed to sneak in but I had no explanation for them. The guy didn’t even know how to open a door much less break into a house. 

I turned away when the officer opened the door to my room. He seemed to enter with ease and closed the door behind him. The silence made me uneasy. After a few minutes, he returned empty handed. No man-baby in handcuffs. 

“Wait, what are you doing? Where is he?” I started to panic. Did he manage to get out? Was he hiding somewhere else?

The cop gave an odd signal to the other officers and they began shuffling out. I could tell he was mulling something in his head before addressing me. He couldn’t hide the annoyance on his face but I could tell he was trying his best.

“Excuse me? Why is everyone leaving? Where is that—that thing?” My face was hot. I could feel my blood start to boil.

“Ma’am, I just need to ask you a few questions.” He dropped low and met my gaze before continuing in a very careful tone.

“You came home and was surprised to find that fella in the other room, correct?” 

I took a deep breath, trying to remain composed despite this unnecessary line of questioning. “Correct.”

He gave a subtle nod. “And you told officer Sulley that this man-baby doesn’t belong to you, correct?”

A laugh escaped me. His face remained stoic, awaiting my answer with impatience.

“You serious? You’re asking if I know this man?”

The officer stood up slowly and rubbed his temple. “Ma’am, this child is registered under your care. But I think you already know that.”

I felt a shift in the room. This couldn’t really be happening. Was everyone going insane?

“I don’t understand.” I stood up, backing away from the officer. I felt the room closing in on me. The creature in the other room started pounding on the door again, sending waves of nausea over me.

“Are you okay ma’am?” The officer started towards me with open arms as if to catch me but I recoiled. I was stumbling now, every second spent enduring this was sending me off-balance.

I could smell it again. The fumes from the bodily waste in the other room started to choke me. The officer was moving in slow motion before my eyes, his voice reduced to a faint noise I couldn’t decipher. 

I was on the ground now. The ceiling swam in my vision until the world faded to black and my mind embraced unconsciousness.

I dreamt the man-baby escaped. It tore down the door and descended on my unconscious body. It’s feral now. The bout of tantrums and my escape sent it into a boiling rage. 

It was hitting me. Pounding its sausage-like limbs into me like an ape.

My form turned to red mist against the carpet as it unleashed a barrage upon me. It hurt at first, then it didn’t.

Then I woke up.

I was in a hospital bed. At first, it felt like my prayers were answered. All I had to do was wait for a doctor to come in and tell me everything was okay. Maybe I got into a car accident and hit my head. Maybe I caught a terrible illness and this was all just a hallucination. I remember seeing a story on the news like that once.

Of course, none of these fantasies came to be.

All of it was real.

I was met with an array of doctors and government officials that took my account with indifferent expressions and impatience. 

They all said the same thing. The creature in my room was my child. 

“You must be experiencing some sort of mental dissociation” one psychologist told me.

“Don’t worry. We’ll get you straightened out. You’ll be back to caring for your baby in no time. He needs his mom after all.”

The words sent bile up my throat. I vomited all over her, sending her into a screaming fit that she carried down the hall outside my room. I would be lying if I said it didn’t give me some satisfaction. I’ve been here dealing with these people a long time now.

Too long.

After a few weeks of seeing the same familiar faces every day, I met someone new. A blind government official named Suzie. 

Suzie wasn’t like the other people I met with. She asked her questions with empathy. I could see it in her face that she heard me. Even if she didn’t believe me, she knew I wasn’t lying. That’s when she told me about the organization she worked for.

“I’m sure at this point you realized I’m not with the folks that have been pestering you these past few weeks.” Suzie pulled a pamphlet out of her bag and handed it to me.

“Well you’re the first person here that isn’t treating me like a monster for not wanting to raise a man in a diaper” I muttered. The nurse taking notes at the foot of my bed was staring daggers at me. Her lips pursed thinner than the pencil in her hand.

“Honey I may be blind but I see better than most” Suzie chuckled. She turned her head to the glaring nurse and prodded her with the butt of her cane. “I’m sorry I didn’t realize it took ten minutes to take a damn blood pressure reading.” The nurse dropped her face and scurried out of the room.

I giggled. Then Suzie tapped the pamphlet in my hands and leaned in close. “That’s better. We need to be alone for this next bit.” 

She explained that she’s apart of some “anomalous detection unit” within the government. Sits somewhere between intelligence and military in terms of jurisdiction. They scan cases like mine for evidence that could indicate reality is changed in some fundamental way. By the end of this conversation I felt like my head was going to explode.

“I don’t underhand” I said dropping the pamphlet in my lap. “This all sounds so…”

“Fake? Terrifying?” Suzie sighed. “Sorry, our resident physicist, Dr. Han, is much better at explaining these things.”

She patted my knee and continued. “If you choose to review your case with us, you will partake in daily sessions with me and Dr. Han. We’ll understand where the divide is between what you know and what the truth is and do our best to bridge that gap.”

I sat silent for a minute. “And if I say no?”

Suzie stood and put her hand on my shoulder one last time. “Unfortunately you’ll be left with these pricks. I do hope you consider our offer.” She expanded her mobility cane and started for the door. 

“Read through the pamphlet carefully and sign the release if you wish to partake. Someone will be around end of day to pick it up.”

I didn’t want her to leave. Her presence brought me peace I hadn’t felt since before all this. 

Before she left the room, she addressed me one more time. “I know this is terrifying, I’ve been where you’re sitting. I do hope you let us help you before it’s too late.” 

Then she was gone.
