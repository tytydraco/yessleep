This is a writing exercise I did whilst I was in juvie, the psychiatrists there called it “narrative therapy”: basically, I would write my past in the form of a story, in hopes of recontextualizing it. I left juvie years ago after I was rehabilitated, and never showed this story to anyone, as I thought they would believe me to be crazy, but I think it is fair to assume my experience will be taken more seriously in this place.

This is all, I hope my tale gives you some enjoyment, in spite of everything in it.

“My two parents are not currently present in my life, and I am still wondering if it was my fault, and if I hallucinated everything that happened just like my doctors suggest. I come close to believing their words when we consult during the day, but every night, when I am alone with my thoughts, I can remember how things actually happened, and I still feel like I can summon them with a single plea to the wind.

When I was born, my family's golden days were already behind me: I was told that we once had plenty of money, but my father fed all of our wealth to the casino's machinery, gambling addict that he was.

I lived surrounded by angels and monsters: my father was a very violent man, but also very cowardly, which made him smart enough to only take his anger out on those who were weaker than him, like me and my mother. My mother, bless her soul, always did her best to take care of me: even though life has always been unfair to her, she did what she could to make my father focus his frustration onto her, and not me.

As time went by, the beatings got worse and worse, but that did not worry me in the slightest: I had grown numb to the pain, and my father’s anger did not disturbed me anymore. What really made me suffer, though, was realizing that my mother was starting to grow resentful not only of my father, but of me, for all that she had to suffer in order to give the resemblance of a normal life. 

One day, finally, my mother said enough, and she decided to divorce my father. Ironically, it was during the divorce that I learned my father, in his own twisted way, did in fact love me, as he begged for my mother to not take full custody of me. She refused, and even though I felt bad for letting my father alone in the pitiful life he had built for himself, I was thankful for finally leaving that miserable purgatory I was born in.

I believed wholeheartedly that the divorce would make me and my mother grow closer, as she did not need to sacrifice as much in order to make me happy, but life has a way to sour even the best surprises: she met man, a middle class called Mark, and she started giving me less and less attention as we moved to his house and they started planning their future family.

Mark was a nice person, and today I have nothing against him, but at the time, I saw him as a competitor for my mother’s attention, and I believe he understood that dynamic deep down. The two worked for most of the day, and would go out constantly at night and weekends, leaving me alone at the house most of the time. I felt as if my mom and Mark wanted a fresh new start, and I was not a person, but a reminder that fresh new starts were only fairytales. It was a deeply lonely time in my life, and in retrospect, I miss it, I miss the feeling of being alone.

One day, my mother forgot to leave something prepared for me to eat. I was barely ten years old, and though I felt bad that my mother had started neglecting me to that extent, children have a way to never stay for long ruminating in the same thought, and I quickly became enamored with the idea of preparing something to myself and surprising my mom and Mark once they got back home.

I climbed the kitchen’s cabinets, and found myself some spaghetti and lots of tomato sauce. I had seen my mother cook before, so confident that I could replicate it, I cleaned the tears from my face, and started a child’s first attempt at cooking.

The result was less than ideal, and not only I burned the food, but also myself, gaining some pretty bad burning scars that still lie in my left arm to this day. 

The pain was worse than any of my father’s beatings, and even though I attempted to maintain a stoic and adultlike manner, I failed, and broke down in tears, as if the burning was the final crack in a dam that was condemned to fall months ago.

As I cried alone on the kitchen floor, holding my left hand against a towel like a newborn, I pleaded to anyone, someone, to help me, to make the pain go away. 

Something came to my rescue that day.

I felt a cold breeze run through me, and gasped once I realized I was floating in the air. I felt something carrying me, but right as I was going to panic, I heard it’s genderless, sweet and caring voice whispering to me a song. In juvie, years later, they let me pick up an activity in order to help me readjust, and I chose flute in an attempt to replicate the sounds it sang to me. Even though I became a very talented flute player (in my caretaker’s opinion, not mine), I was never able to replicate the beauty of those sounds. It was inhuman, abovehuman, like a siren’s song or the sound of a distant planet echoing through the neverending cosmos, to be heard by no living creature.

The presence, as I called it, took me to our house’s med kit, and gently guided my hands through the objects, helping me treat the wound as it calmed me down. The presence, as I could feel it, was of infinite size, covering me all around. I felt like an infant again, and wished this feeling would stay forever, but as I said before, no feeling stays for long in a child’s mind, and I asked, now believing in new fresh starts, if the presence could help me cook something this time.

I felt as if the presence was proud of me, and it guided me to the kitchen, gently holding my hand. It took control of my meek right hand, and started puppeteering me as I cooked its creative vision into existence. By the time Mark and mom arrived, I had a complete dinner ready.

My mother was surprised, but also proud of me for being able to do it alone. She noticed the burned left arm, and quickly became apologetic for leaving me with nothing to eat, and for days after, she gave me more attention than she had given for months. That was short lived, though, as Mark grew jealous and impatient, and she started neglecting me once more.

For the months that followed, the presence became my main caretaker: it would help me dress to school, wake and sleep at the right time, prepare me food when mom and Mark were not around, and even help me with homework. My mother was surprised with how well I was handling myself, and used it as more excuse to neglect me, as I clearly “very mature for my age”, and could take care of the house alone. I felt terrible as me and my mother grew apart, but the presence served as a welcoming antidote to my melancholy, filling the place in my heart that my mom once occupied.

One day, as I was alone at home once again, I heard someone knocking on the door. I looked into the magic eye, and I saw my father standing there. He called my name, and asked if we could talk.

I felt insecure for a moment, but emboldened by everyone around me praising my “maturity”, I believed I could handle my father, and opened the door to him.

He was crying, and I could smell alcohol in his breath as he talked. He hugged me, and I stood there, unsure of how to proceed.

He asked if he could come in, and I relented as the feeling of pity returned to me after so many months ago.

We talked for a very long time, and though my father did not ask forgiveness for the beatings, he said he regretted not treating me and my mother right, and that he wished he could make up for us.

I suddenly felt angry, as I remembered how much pain and suffering my father caused my mother, and how he made her hate me by proxy. Before I was able to notice it, I was shouting to my father, and he was answering accordingly, suddenly remembering the most effective insults to get into my head. 

He said enough, and that if I did not want to fix our family by love, it would have to be by pain, and he started dragging me outside, to his pick up truck sloppily parked into Mark’s front yard.

I felt afraid, thinking that my father would disappear with me forever, and I started desperately screaming for help. My father put his hand against my mouth in order to shut me up, but it was too late, the presence had already heard me.

When my mom and Mark came back, they found me sitting in the porch steps covered in a blanket as cops analysed a pick up truck parked over the yard, now completely splattered with blood and pieces of human bones, skin and hair.

The police interrogated me about what happened, and after it was clear I could not give a satisfactory answer (the cops refused to believe me), they gave me descriptions of a serial killer that was roaming around the state at the time, and it became clear they wanted to clear that case out by putting the blame of my father’s death onto him. I obliged, realizing there was nothing they could do for me.

Following the days after that, my mother resigned from work, and refused to leave me alone. I wish I could say I felt good again, but it was not the same type of care she gave me before: it was care born out of fear, not love, and her paranoia that something terrible could happen to me only made me feel more suffocated than before. It was not helped by the fact that I started having terrible nightmares about what the presence did to my father, and would wake mom and Mark up at night. More powerful than her fear and paranoia, though, was her guilt, and I hated myself for causing that, for reducing her to a parody of what she once was.

Mark started feeling jealousy, and this served as fuel to plenty of new fights between the two. That filled me with angst, as I was able to predict how the stress they were under would one day cause the houselife to become just as terrible as my parent’s first marriage was, and this time, it would be all my fault.

I begged the presence to fix that, to help me make the screaming, and shouting, and the anger, and everything else go away. The presence, caretaker that it was obliged, just not in the way that I wanted.

One night, my mother woke up in the shared bedroom she had with Mark, feeling the weight of something else in the bedroom with them, making the mattress shake.

As her sight adjusted, her ears also followed accordingly, and she lost her breath once she noticed a small figure leaning over Mark, and the sound of her partner choking on his own blood.

She screams as she falls from the bed, and retreats to the corner of the room as the tiny figure reveals itself against the streetlights coming from the gaps between the curtains: she saw me, sleepwalking and holding a kitchen knife, the same I once used to prepare food for my secluded dinners.

She calls my name, and I suddenly wake up, realizing that the presence is surrounding me, and making me walk towards my mother with a blooded knife. I plead for it to stop, and it refuses to hear me, arguing that everything it is doing is for the best, and in desperation, I scream at it, using the worst words and offenses I learned from my father and my mother in order to make it go away. The presence remains silent for a moment, and I feel the weight of its wrath tainting the air just as much as the smell of blood coming from the bedroom, but instead of attacking me, its anger falls over my mother, as it always does.

My mother starts floating onto the air, and the presence starts tearing her like a pack of wild animals, ripping skin, clothing, bone, meat and bones in a mad induced freezing. I scream in terror, ask for forgiveness, pray for it to stop, but it is useless. It quickly reduces my mother to a pile of blood and remains splattered all over the room. 

I do not know for how long I just stood there, just looking at the carnage around me, but it felt like an eternity. The police came, and this time, I was blamed for the whole affair, a juvenile serial killer.

I was taken to juvie, and here now I reside, waiting for what the state will do to me.”
