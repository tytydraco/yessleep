I was twenty-five at the time, newly divorced, and had brought my four-year-old daughter, Sarah, with me from a small town to the big city. We rented the top floor of an apartment building, hoping I could find a new job before the money we had ran out.

The rent was half of what the other floors cost because nearly a third of the apartment had been sectioned off as a storage room, where the landlord kept all sorts of old belongings and miscellaneous junk. In exchange for the cheaper rent, I had to go in and clean it three times a month. I thought it was a pretty good deal. The landlord told me that, aside from a few pieces of furniture that couldn’t be moved, I was free to take anything I found in the storage room.

Once I had finished settling into the apartment, I decided to start cleaning the storage room. I unlocked the door with the key the landlord had given me, only to be greeted by a cloud of dust that made me sneeze. Sarah followed close behind, eager to help Mommy. She had always been a good girl.

The job itself was simple enough. I just had to go around with the vacuum cleaner and, while I was at it, see if there was anything useful among the clutter. Sarah clearly hadn’t expected the mysterious room to be quite so filthy. She followed me everywhere, carefully stepping only on the spots I had already cleaned.

I soon found something worth keeping. Tucked inside a gift basket was a brown teddy bear wearing a top hat and a bow tie. It looked almost brand new and would probably make a nice playmate for Sarah once it had been washed. But Sarah didn’t seem particularly interested. She was much too busy playing with a bundle of colored yarn.

Still, I brought Mr. Bear out of the storage room with me. After I washed and dried him, he joined the army of stuffed animals that lived beside Sarah’s pillow. Sarah still didn’t like him. Every night, when I went in to tuck her back under the covers after she had kicked them off, I would find Mr. Bear lying on the floor. Sarah also complained that Mr. Bear pulled at her blankets during the night and pressed his furry face against her, which made her uncomfortable. Since she clearly wanted nothing to do with him, I reluctantly put Mr. Bear back on the bench and tossed him into the gift basket.

I soon forgot all about the little incident because I found a new job. I was busier than I’d ever been, but the pay was good enough for Sarah and me to move out of that cramped apartment.

Little by little, everything in our lives began to get better. Sarah settled into her new school and was praised by her teachers time and time again. My career was going well, too. My female supervisor was very kind to me, and she promoted me three times, in my third, fifth, and tenth years on the job. I stayed there until I retired.

When I was forty-five, I met the person who would become the true partner of my life. I went from being Clara Brown to Clara Evans.

Miller was eight years older than me, but he was a gentle, kind man. As it turned out, he was the grandson of the landlord of the very first apartment I’d ever rented. He confessed that he’d actually fallen in love with me a long time ago, but his family hadn’t thought much of a young single mother, and he hadn’t had the courage to approach me. Fortunately, God brought us together again twenty years later.

By then, Sarah had found a job and moved out. She no longer lived with me. For some reason, our relationship had grown somewhat distant. It wasn’t that Sarah had become rebellious. If anything, she was almost too polite with me, as though I were a stranger. She still called me “Mom,” but she continued to address Miller as “Mr. Evans.” My therapist told me it might have been because Sarah felt abandoned by me after we stopped sharing the same surname.

At first, I was deeply saddened by it, but I couldn't choose between my daughter and my husband. All I could do was tell myself that this was simply part of growing up, and that I had to learn to let go. Miller understood, too. He never seemed to mind when Sarah gave him the cold shoulder, whether deliberately or otherwise, whenever she came home. Every holiday, he would even send gifts to Sarah and her family.

I'm sixty-two this year, and I don't think there's much about my life that I regret. After Miller passed away this year, he left me the apartment building I had once rented. I decided to move back into the top-floor apartment where I'd originally lived, while continuing to rent out the other floors so I could put some money aside for Sarah. Her two children are about to start college.

To be honest, I was surprised to find that I still had the key to the storage room on my keyring. After all these years, I'd assumed I'd lost it somewhere along the way. So I decided to clear out everything inside and sell whatever I could, then renovate the room so Sarah would have somewhere to stay whenever she came to visit.

After putting on a mask, I began carrying things out of the dusty room one by one. Eventually, I found a gift basket packed to the brim with old belongings. The moment I caught sight of a piece of brown fabric sticking out from beneath the pile, memories from long ago came rushing back—Sarah had been four years old then, always following close behind me, kicking off her blankets every night. Her bed had been crowded with stuffed animals, but she had never liked Mr. Bear.

Ah, Mr. Bear. A strange warmth rose inside my chest, so intense that it nearly brought tears to my eyes. I decided to keep the toy as a little reminder of the past. Bracing my tired body against the wall, I reached as far as I could and tried to grab the piece of fabric, pulling at it in an attempt to free it from beneath the pile of junk. It was exhausting work for an old woman like me. It took me a long time to finally get hold of it, and I nearly lost my balance in the process.

But I managed to steady myself against the wall. When I finally had a chance to take a proper look at what I was holding, the warmth in my chest suddenly froze. A sharp, prickling sensation spread across my skin.

It wasn't the stuffed animal I had thought it was.It was an entire teddy bear costume.

The dry-cleaning tag read: “1989.04.12 Mr. Evans.”

That was the first year I moved into the apartment.

I think I'd better forget about this.
