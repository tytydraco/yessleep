**I work the night shift at an apartment building. Last night, I received a noise complaint from an apartment that has been empty for 11 years.** I've been working as a security guard for three years. Night shifts are usually boring. Most of the tenants are asleep by midnight, the elevators stop moving, and the only sounds you hear are the occasional footsteps from someone returning home late.

But last Thursday was different. At exactly 2:17 AM, the front desk phone rang. "Security office. How may I help you?" A woman whispered something.

"There's someone walking around in apartment 307." I checked the building directory. Apartment 307 had been vacant for eleven years. "Ma'am, are you sure about the apartment number?" "Yes," she whispered. "I can hear him dragging something across the floor."

Then the line went dead.

I grabbed my flashlight and headed upstairs. The third floor was unusually cold. As I approached apartment 307, I noticed something strange. The door was slightly open. I was certain it had been locked when I checked the building earlier. I pushed it gently. "Security. Is anyone inside?" Nothing.

The apartment was completely dark. Then I smelled something. Something rotten. I covered my nose and stepped inside. The living room was empty except for an old wooden chair positioned directly in front of the television. The television was turned on. Static filled the screen.

I took out my phone and called the building manager. Before he could answer, something knocked three times behind me. I turned around. The bedroom door was closed.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

"Hello?" A voice answered. "You're late." I froze. The voice sounded like an old man. I slowly approached the bedroom door and pushed it open. Nothing. Just an empty room. But there was something written on the wall in what appeared to be dried blood.

**YOU WERE SUPPOSED TO STAY OUTSIDE.**

I immediately backed away. Then my flashlight flickered. For one brief second, I saw someone standing in the corner. A tall, extremely thin man. His head was tilted sideways, and his mouth was stretched into an unnatural smile.

I screamed and stumbled backward. When my flashlight came back on, he was gone. I ran toward the entrance. But the front door wouldn't open. I pulled harder.

Nothing.

Then I heard footsteps behind me.

Slow.

Heavy.

Dragging.

I turned around. The man was standing at the end of the hallway. His clothes were soaked in something dark. He raised one finger and placed it against his lips."Shhhhh."

Then he started running.

I threw myself against the door, screaming and kicking until it finally opened. I fell into the hallway and scrambled toward the stairs. Behind me, I heard a loud crash.

Then silence.

I didn't return to the security office until nearly 4 AM. The building manager was waiting for me. He looked furious.

"Where the hell have you been?"

I told him everything.

The empty apartment.

The blood.

The man.

He didn't say anything for several seconds. Then he asked me something that made my stomach drop. "What man?"

I explained what I saw. He slowly shook his head.

"Listen carefully. Apartment 307 belonged to a security guard named Daniel. Eleven years ago, he murdered three tenants before taking his own life."

I couldn't speak.

The manager continued.

"His body was discovered inside that apartment. The police found him sitting on a wooden chair in front of the television." I felt sick. "Why didn't anyone tell me?" The manager stared at me. "Because I thought you already knew." "What?" He pointed toward the security monitor. "Look at the footage."

I watched the recording from the third-floor hallway. At 2:17 AM, the elevator doors opened. I stepped out. I walked toward apartment 307. I entered. Then, twenty minutes later, I came running out, screaming. But there was something else. Something standing behind me. A tall man. Following me. The manager paused the footage.

"That's impossible," he whispered. I stared at the screen. The man was looking directly into the camera. Then the manager zoomed in. My blood ran cold. The man's face was mine.

Same eyes.

Same nose.

Same uniform.

Same smile.

The manager slowly turned toward me. "Daniel?" I couldn't move. Then the office phone rang. 2:17 AM. The manager answered. His expression changed. He slowly placed the receiver down. "There's a noise complaint," he whispered. I looked at him. "From where?"

He swallowed. "Apartment 307." Then, from somewhere behind us, a familiar voice whispered: **"You're late."**
