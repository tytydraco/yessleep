I was 10 when this incident happened.

I lived in the Nillam Chawl. It was a simple, flat chawl situated in a hilly area of Mumbai. Since it was at the top and there was only one way in and out, it was a safe place compared to the rest of the area. And yes, not to forget the 13 dogs that were always roaming around the chawl. It was impossible for any stranger to walk in. The dogs would always howl from a distance the moment they sensed an unknown scent.

I would play all alone, unguarded, in the chawl. My mother would just put the latch and leave for the market, unbothered about putting a lock.

At night, though, it was really scary. My mother said there was nothing to be scared of, but how could I put my hand out of the window when it was pitch dark outside? It was always my duty to shut the windows, so I usually used a stick to hook the latch and pull it forward. The stick would go out into the unknown dark and come back in. I always felt a weight on the end of the stick, as if someone was trying to pull it.

Once, while trying to pull the stick back again, it slipped from my hand and fell out into the dark.

I leaned back immediately and thought about how I could go out and bring the stick back. But then my mother bravely opened the door, also trying to tell me, "There is nothing to fear." She got the stick and also pushed her hand out to grab the window latch. As she approached the latch, she shrieked and pulled her hand back. She rubbed her skin and frowned.

My heart was beating fast. "What happened, Mother?" She shook her head and again tried to pull the window, but this time warily. Her face was contracted, as if she was trying to hold something back.

Once she touched the latch, she pulled the window in no time. The window shut with a slam. "Thap!"

If you ever get a chance to visit my chawl now, mind you, it is all in ruins.

It was burnt ablaze in an accidental fire at midnight. No one knew how it happened. Some speculated a short circuit, some a cylinder blast. This was mostly conjectured by a similar cylinder blast in a nearby area.

But only if you do not sleep and keep your eyes open, you will see a pallid hand opening the window again and waving to us.

I heavily clutched my duvet with both my feet and hands, trying to cover most of my body. I would also cover my head and tuck the duvet under the weight of my body. But due to the heat, it was impossible. The pallid hand would just keep waving at you, and then, if you didn't respond, it would just retract out and close the window back.

Each day, it asked me by beckoning with a pull-back finger. Out of spite and just to make it go, I threw a pen at the hand. Funny, it hit it, and the hand just froze for a second and then shook it as if it was hurt and retracted.

Each day, I learned to shoo it away. Day by day, tiny things went missing from home: more pens, rubber, my toys. Mother and father wondered what was happening, but I never said anything. I was now protecting my family.

One night, the door opened again, and the pallid hand entered, but this time its fingers were all touching each other. Squinting my eyes, I realized it was holding something. It then went down, disappeared, only to rise up again with a lit stick in its fingers. Probably it was a matchstick.

The fire was amber in the dark. This is when it revealed its patchy skin. It was not all pale as I used to think all these days. The patchiness was uneven marks, darker and blistering.

I gulped and pulled my covers over my head. What to do, I don't know. It was like the hand was trying to see me using the light form the fire. I kept the cover on, though it was surely hot.

It was now hot, more and more hot, burning hot. Sometimes there was noise and screaming. I kept holding my covers tight. My feet were tingling with a stinging heat, until it was up to my lower body. I held my covers. I felt tugging and shaking, until I was feeling my hands go loose, as they were weak by now. And slowly, all the screaming and shaking stopped, except the heat. My head was still under the covers.

If you ever want to visit this chawl now, please never leave your stuff here. I am now used to living in this rubble. The pale, burnt hand still lurks here among the other burnt bodies.
