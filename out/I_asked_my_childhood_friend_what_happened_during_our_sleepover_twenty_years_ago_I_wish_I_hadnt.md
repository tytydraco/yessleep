I don’t like sleeping with my back to open spaces anymore. To be honest, I don’t know if I ever truly loved it, but I especially can’t stand it now. Have you ever turned around because you swore someone was behind you? That dreadful, heavy feeling fills your stomach while the hairs on the back of your neck stand up. The tingling in your spine as you can practically feel fingers reaching out to caress the nape of your neck. It’s unsettling, and even as I sit outside of this busy café, I still feel that chill deep in my bones. 

I’ve been tossing and turning at night trying to work through what I found out about that sleepover all those years ago, and I honestly can’t come to terms with it. My therapist, god bless her, recommended I write it out so my brain can work through what I’m feeling. She’s seen me at the lowest points in my life, and she’s always done her best to let me process through my emotions without guiding me. She said I’m the type of person that needs to “see from all angles” before I can truly accept something, so…this is my attempt to do just that. Maybe as you’re reading through what I’m writing, you’ll recognize these feelings and maybe I won’t feel so…alone. 

I guess let’s start from the beginning. I met Joanna when I was ten years old, and now, twenty-four years later, she’s still one of my best friends. I still remember every detail of the day she came crashing into my life. 

She is vibrant, a technicolor soul who lives her life cranked up to eleven at all times. I was a shy, gawky kid, the only brown-skinned child in a town full of families so old the roads and cemeteries were named for them. To put it bluntly, I was an outsider, and most people never let me forget that. What’s that old saying, there’s nothing as cruel as school children? During one particularly harrowing day, I remember walking through the halls with my head down and colliding right into Joanna. I stumbled backwards, shocked out of my reverie and embarrassed I hadn’t seen her in time, and she stood there looking at me with her mouth agape, her face twisted in a mix between disgust and confusion. Her eyes scanned mine, darting back and forth and then down to the bundle of books in my hands. I began chewing my lip, trying to will my tongue to form an apology, but being stared at so fiercely had me rooted to my spot. She raised her little hand up high. I was sure she was going to strike me, and I braced for the inevitable impact. THWAP! She brought her hand down fast and quick, knocking the books out of my hands, my papers and textbooks clattering loudly to the linoleum floor. 

She stared at me again, and I was trying my hardest not to make direct eye contact, my gaze bouncing between the puke green lockers and the glittery posters that peppered the hallway marketing the annual yearbook sale. I could feel tears welling up in my eyes, hot and threatening to pour over my lids when suddenly… 

She started laughing, doubling over as a heavy, deep laugh took over her entire body. She rocked back and forth as it consumed her like a wave until nothing but tiny squeaks came out of her mouth. She wiped her eyes and took deep gasps of breath, straightening up and making eye contact with me again. 

“I’m-“ she hiccupped between her final chuckles, “I’m so sorry. Your face, that was too funny. Here, let me help you.” 

I stood there, rooted with a mixture of fear, anger, and confusion filling my body. What…the hell…was happening? I stared at this small girl, her long brown hair braided into two twin pigtails that swayed as she laughed. Even though I had always been tall for my age, she was noticeably shorter than I, with her little, rotund face peppered with a sea of freckles. Her mouth took up most of her face, her big lips splayed in a giant grin as she kept muttering to herself, “ahhh, that’s so stupid,” as she bent over to pick up my scattered school supplies. She glanced up at me, her small brown eyes crinkled with the remnants of her previous laughter. “Aren’t you gonna pick your stuff up? Don’t be a litter bug.” 

“Litter bug?” I snorted, the anger outweighing the embarrassment I had previously felt. “I-I’m not a litter bug! You slapped me!” 

“Actually, I slapped your books. It was to wake you up! You clearly were sleep driving, I mean you crashed right into me.” She chuckled, shrugging her shoulders as if to highlight the absurdity of the entire situation. 

“Well…erm…” I struggled to find the right words, “I guess I was…driving under the influence of my thoughts”. I smiled down at her slightly.

Her eyes sparkled like her soul was recognizing mine, and that big grin swallowed her face whole again. “Ah! That’s funny! You’re funny!” She scooped all my belongings back into her arms and stood up, plopping them into mine. “I’m Joanna! What’s your name?” 

I stood, half stunned, half amused, a small smirk creeping across my thin lips. “I’m Arcadia. But you can call me Cadi.” 

Joanna stood and observed me, it felt like being put under a microscope and as she scanned me, I made small observations of her as well. As she perceived my mess of dark, unruly hair, I noticed that she had bows tied into her pigtails. As she looked at my much too large crewneck sweater and unfashionable baggy jeans, I noticed how cool her mauve pinafore dress looked. She had pins and buttons attached to the front of it with sayings like “gag me with a spoon” and “did I do that?” with Urkel glasses underneath. I crossed my red and white sneakers in a pigeon step, embarrassed at how dorky I must have looked to the coolest girl I had ever met. She stood straight, her untied boots adding at least two inches to her short stature. 

“That is…” she started, drumming her multicolored fingernails against her chin, “yeah, no, that’s definitely the coolest name I’ve ever heard. You’re cool. And you’re funny. I like you, let’s be friends!” Suddenly, she thrust her arm in between mine, snaking it around and holding on to me like we had been friends forever. 

That was the day Joanna Collins adopted me into her life. I wasn’t just an awkward kid anymore; I was her new best friend. We were inseparable, two peas in a radical little pod. Joanna shared all her favorite songs with me and she sung with every ounce of gusto in her heart. I felt like knowing her meant truly knowing myself, and all those weird little quirks about myself I felt like I had to hide felt like something to celebrate for the first time. 

The days fell away in a flurry of laughing fits, eating entirely too much sour candy, and of course, picking up my books any time she slapped them out of my hands. We spent every holiday, every weekend, every summer together. I took trips with her family to their lake house, where we would ride inflatable tubes pulled behind much too fast boats. We would scream and laugh as we launched high into the air, eventually crashing hard at night with the scent of sunscreen and BBQ lingering on our skin. She would snicker and make faces at me through the curtain of her hair as we bent over in prayer during church service with my family. That’s what I miss most about those days, how easily we laughed. 

The summer we turned twelve, after celebrating our birthdays, we were hanging out in her upstairs foyer when things started to change. 

“Did I ever tell you…” Joanna started fidgeting with the beads of the friendship bracelet that she was making. 

“That you’re a giant idiot? I mean I knew that from the first day I met you. It’s basically a terminal condition, but I love you all the same,” I responded back, not even looking up from my own tangle of purple and green threads. 

“No, ass.” She chuckled, “Did I ever tell you I can sense things?” 

“Like what?” I questioned, finally looking up and noticing she was looking off into the distance. 

“It’s hard to describe,” she said quietly, a serious look furrowed across her brows, “It’s like, I can sense things. Like I can feel what other people are feeling even if I’m not with them. It’s like I can sense things before they happen.” 

I stared at her blankly. She was chewing the bottom corner of her lip and still staring off. She turned to look at me, her eyes searching mine for understanding. I remember that when I look back, like she was pleading for me to understand something that was much bigger than I could comprehend as a preteen. 

“You can…you can sense things?” I asked, pausing, my face twisting up. 

“Yeah, I can sense things.” She said matter-of-factly. 

I squinted my eyes, and my face turned red as I squeezed to let out the most pathetic little fart. “Could you sense that?” I asked, before rolling over and laughing, my arms twitching above me like a dead bug. 

I remember she didn’t laugh, and that caught me off guard. I remember actually feeling ashamed, like I could feel the temperature of the room change. Like her disappointment dropped the air by two degrees. I quickly stopped laughing and rolled back over onto my stomach. I looked up at her sheepishly.  “Oh…um…hey, I’m sorry.” 

“Not everything is a joke, you know,” Joanna said quietly. 

“Oh, Jo jo…” I started, getting up to my knees “I just…this is something serious. Nobody ever takes me seriously. My own mother thinks I’m crazy. But like…it scares me sometimes.” 

In the years I had known Joanna, I had seen almost every side of her. The clown, the studious one, the lover girl, the soldier, but I don’t think I had ever seen her afraid like this. She was fearless, and until that point in our lives, I had only ever known that version of her. I remember the breath catching in my chest, like I was watching the mask of her façade crack slightly. Was I a terrible friend? How could I not have seen the delicate web of splinters before? 

“Is it happening again?” I asked, taking her hand into mine gently. I wanted her to know I was here with her. 

“It’s crazy. I mean…I’m not crazy, but…it’s spooky. Like,” she pulled her hand from mine and began fidgeting with her long brown hair. “Do you remember last year when my nana died?” 

I thought back, recalling that rainy weekend in November of last year when we were lying around in Joanna’s room listening to music. We were singing along to *Dashboard Confessional* when suddenly Joanna stopped singing abruptly. The hairbrush microphone she had been holding in her hands mere moments ago clattered to the floor, and she stood rigidly still. All the color drained from her face, and she crumpled to her knees on her bed. I noticed her out of the corner of my eyes while I was hitting a high note and turned to look at her. “Dude, what’s wrong?” I said, concerned. She stared straight ahead, not even seeing me. Her lip began quivering, and she said, “Please don’t get in the car.” Almost like a whisper. 

“Uh…those aren’t the lyrics. Are you okay, dude?” I waved my hand in front of her eyes, but she still looked straight ahead. Like she was looking through the walls and beyond to something greater. Her eyes began to well up with tears, and her breathing became shallow and shuddering. I looked down at her hands; her nails were digging into the soft meat of her thighs.

 “Stop, go back, please.” Her face twisted in pain, her eyes squinting as if pleading with some unseen entity. 

“What the fu-” I whispered, standing in front of Joanna now with my face inches from hers, searching her blank expression for any answer. “Jo jo…Joanna..JO!” I screamed in her face, shaking her slightly. 

She snapped to, the pained look replaced with confusion and annoyance. 

“Ow bitch, talk much? Why are you yelling in my face?” She looked at me quizzically, sneering slightly. Her face softened when she saw how spooked I was. “What…is there something on my face?” she joked 

“What the hell was that?!” I exasperatedly yelped at her, “You just scared the shit out of me, dude!” 

“What happened?” she asked, oblivious to the psychotic show she had just put on.

“Well, freakazoid, you stopped singing, and you just dropped. You were looking out at god knows what in the distance and muttering about not getting into the car. None of this is ringing any bells?” I asked, my hands flailing wildly to showcase my exasperation. 

“Hmmm….no. I think you’re making stuff up, dude. Maybe you’ve had too many sour gummy worms.” 

Joanna stood back up and brushed some blanket fuzz off her knees, noticing the red crescent shapes in her thigh where her fingernails had pierced the skin. “Damnit,” she muttered, “How did I scratch myself? Ugh.” She shrugged and tossed her hair back over her shoulders, picking up her hairbrush and getting ready to belt out the next song on the CD. 

I stood in the middle of the room, staring in utter confusion – what the fuck had I just seen? What on earth was that? Soon, the incident was a distant thought as the rest of the night continued on in a flurry of normal sleepover shenanigans before it was time for bed. 

I remember curling up in bed across from her that night, my feet at her head and hers at mine. As the hours ticked on, Joanna snored softly at my feet, but sleep eluded me. I remember staring at the bright neon of Joanna’s alarm clock. The time read 2:14 AM, and I watched as it switched to 2:15 before I sighed and sat up in bed. I looked down and practically pissed myself when I saw Joanna had quietly sat up in bed as well and was staring straight at me. 

“Jesus, fuck, dude. Don’t do that to me. I thought you were asleep!” I hissed in the quiet, putting my hand against the heart pounding in my chest. It was dark, but the green halo from her clock illuminated her features slightly. She looked…half asleep, honestly. Maybe I had woken her with my tossing and turning. 

Joanna stared straight ahead, as if looking past me. I turned cautiously to peer behind me, my eyes peering into the darkness, and I squinted, trying to make out what she was seeing. What the hell was she looking at? 

“I told her not to do it,” Joanna whispered softly 

I whipped my head back and looked at her, “What?” I said, my voice croaking out of my throat. 

“I told her not to. And she did anyway.” Joanna sighed, as if defeated. She pulled the cover up over her head and turned her back to me, nestling back into her sleep.

I was frozen, staring at her in utter disbelief. After a few minutes, which dripped by like molasses, I heard her softly snoring again and I knew she had fallen back asleep. I pulled my blanket up to my chin and curled up, turning my back to the darkness and forcing my eyes closed. The next thing I remember was opening my eyes again and seeing the sun cascading through her window. I could smell Belinda cooking breakfast downstairs and I could hear her humming along to some pop song from the 80s. My eyes hurt when it blinked, but I remember feeling wide awake. Joanna was still snoring softly, her hair splayed around like a tumbleweed, her arms outstretched above her head, and her leg crooked over and under mine in an uncomfortable pretzel. Somewhere deep in the house, a phone rang, and the low hum of the music paused. A few minutes later, I heard a mug shatter and a slight scream come from downstairs. Joanna and I shot up out of bed and raced downstairs, our hearts racing. 

“What’s wrong?” I asked as we came into the kitchen and saw Belinda bent over on the floor. 

“Mom?” Joanna asked softly, terrified of stepping into the minefield of broken ceramic in the kitchen.

Belinda looked up, tears leaving black streaks of mascara down her face, and stared at us. She whimpered softly and bit her lip, her arms encircled around her torso as if hugging herself. 

“Oh jo jo…it’s…” she paused, catching her breath, “It’s your nana…there’s been an accident.” 

Joanna stood rigid at the base of the steps, her breath catching in her throat. 

“Well…do you remember?” Joanna asked again, snapping me out of my memory. 

“Um, yeah…all too fucking well I remember that. Nana Stephanie was always so kind, that was a…” I trailed off, trying to find the right words. I didn’t think saying “that was an incredibly creepy time in our lives” was the right thing to say, so I opted for “that was a hard month for your family. What about it?” 

“Remember when I went all spooky and shit on you? You were so freaked out, and I tried to play it off like I didn’t remember…” she trailed off, putting a piece of her hair into her mouth and sucking slightly. She looked so small in that moment, I wanted to hug her and ask if we could change the subject. 

“Yeah, you tried to tell me I was crazy, but it was…well, it was something.” I didn’t want to shame her and tell her it was beyond terrifying. How can you be terrified of your best friend? Plus, I wanted her to feel comfortable enough to tell me what she was feeling now. 

“Yeah, well…” She started, standing up and walking over to the railing of the foyer that looked down into her entryway. “That day when we were singing, I felt something. It was like I was seeing something that couldn’t have been there.” 

I stood up and walked over next to her, putting my elbows on the railing and bending over it slightly to look up into her face. “I saw Nana Stephanie, only it wasn’t her as she was. She was all cut up and covered in glass.” 

I staggered backwards off the rail, my breath catching. “Jo, that’s not fucking funny,” I said. I remembered the funeral for Nana Stephanie, for some reason, she had requested an open casket in her will. I don’t think Nana Stephanie ever anticipated her face getting practically split in half due to her steering wheel, but the funeral home did their best effort to fix it. I remember her porcelain skin marred by an unnervingly white sliver from her temple to her chin, jagged like a lightning bolt. Reconstructed with putty as best as they could, but still incredibly noticeable. 

“Cadi, I saw her. As clear as I’m seeing you now. I begged her not to get into the car, I begged her to stay home. I saw her at 9 PM that night in my bedroom, mangled. She didn’t wreck her car until 10:45. How did I see her…” she trailed off. 

I felt my blood turn cold, and for the first time in my life, I had no words. I sank to the floor near my abandoned friendship bracelet project. I couldn’t process what she was telling me. 

“Jo jo, that’s…” words genuinely escaped me. 

“I know, it’s crazy. But it’s true. And that’s not the first time it’s happened, and I’m afraid it won’t be the last. I just…I have nobody to talk to about it, and I’ve wanted to tell you for so long, but I just…” 

My mind was racing a mile a minute. I finally found the question I had been meaning to ask but was too afraid to before, “so…why are you telling me now?” 

Joanna stopped pacing, her back turned to me. I could see her quiver slightly, like a chill had travelled down her spine. She turned slowly to me, her words coming slowly and measured. “I just…” 

She stared off behind me, and I felt the hairs on the back of my neck stand up. My mind was flooded with visions of that inky darkness in the corner of her room that night. I began to slowly turn my head, my eyes reaching the corner of my periphery when… 

“Girls!” Belinda’s voice rang out from the first floor, “It’s time for bed!” 

I slouched against a bean bag on the floor, feeling just as deflated as the chair I was propped against. Joanna smiled at me softly, bending over and offering her hand. 

“It’s nothing, Cadi, I didn’t mean to scare you. I was just thinking of Nana.” Her eyes darted between the space behind me and her friendship bracelet on the floor. “Red was one of her favorite colors. Remember that pretty ruby bracelet she had? That’s it…I was just…thinking of her and remembered something that spooked me.”

I stared at her with a look of pure bewilderment. She was scaring the hell out of me because of a memory? I took her hand and stood up slowly, the fear draining from my chest to the bottom of my feet and dissipating with each step towards the bathroom. Joanna laughed and shoved me playfully as we collided through the door frame into the bathroom to get ready for bed. I went to exit the bathroom, heading for the bedroom I knew practically as well as my own, when I stopped. Joanna had paused and was standing still in the bathroom, staring at her reflection intently. 

“You good, Jo?” I called back, pausing mid-step. 

“Oh,” Joanna snapped to attention, “Yeah, sorry.” 

We crawled into bed and turned off the lights. Joanna fished the remote out from under the covers and turned on the tiny TV in the corner of her room. We flipped through channels before landing on reruns of some cartoon we were definitely too old to be respectably watching, stifling our laughter into our pillows so Belinda wouldn’t chastise us. 

I don’t know how much time had passed, but my eyes snapped open suddenly, and the muffled static of the TV was droning in the background. The blazing sea of white illuminated everything and hurt to look at. I poked at Joanna, mumbling, “Jo, turn off the TV, it’s too bright,” and received a loud, deep snore in response. Without trying to open my eyes again, my hands searched for the remote that had been lost between our covers so I could get respite from the blazing light. Finally, I found it, and the tv clicked off with a satisfying hiss, allowing the world to once again be enveloped in darkness. I started to settle back down, turning my back to where the TV had been and ready to fall asleep again when… 

My eyes shot open, and I flipped around to look at the clock on the dresser next to the TV. The faded neon green numbers read 2:14 AM, and a cold pinprick feeling filled my chest. That was just a coincidence. A very unfortunate, very creepy coincidence. I turned my back to the clock and pulled the covers up to my chin, forcing my eyes closed. I was counting sheep in my head, willing my body to go back to bed, when I felt Joanna stir next to me. 

“Ah, Jo, I was…” I peeked my eyes open and looked to where she was lying 

I clasped my hand around my mouth quickly so as not to scream. Joanna was sitting straight up, rigid as a board, and staring into the darkness behind me. My heart was pounding so loudly in my ears that I swore that it would wake the whole house.

I couldn’t move, I couldn’t breathe, I stared in horror at Joanna sitting across from me, her eyes half-opened as if she was still sleeping.  I opened my mouth to speak when I heard her raspy voice whisper, “Don’t touch her.” 

Fear flooded my system like ice water, and it felt like I was drowning in it. My head was pounding and it felt like an eternity was passing and I was going insane with fear. 

“Joanna…” I managed to rasp out, my breath hitching in my chest 

“Please...” Joanna whimpered, never taking her eyes off the darkness behind me.
