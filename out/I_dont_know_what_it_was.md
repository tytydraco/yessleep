Hey it's Jax again.
I’ve worked as a fire lookout for many years and I used to think there wasn’t much left in the forest that could surprise me. You learn pretty quickly that being alone changes the way you hear things. A branch snapping can sound like a gunshot, and a squirrel running across the roof can make you think somebody is climbing the tower. I’ve seen bears, mountain lions, injured hikers, and once even a lost hunter who had somehow wandered nearly ten miles from where he parked. None of that bothered me. Three years ago, I was assigned to an old lookout tower that sat on a ridge about twelve miles from the nearest paved road. The tower was small, maybe fifteen feet above the ground, with windows on every side and an old metal staircase leading up to it. My first few days there were completely normal. The nights were quiet, the radio worked, and I spent most evenings reading or watching the sunset. Then, on my sixth night, I heard footsteps underneath the tower at around 1:30 in the morning. I looked out the window and saw nothing. The footsteps stopped. A minute later, they started again, slowly circling the tower. I grabbed my flashlight and shined it through the window, but all I saw were trees and rocks. Then someone quietly knocked three times on one of the metal support beams. I froze. There was no reason for anyone to be out there that late, especially because the trail leading to the tower was closed after dark. I called the ranger station, but nobody answered. I figured whoever was outside had eventually left. I was wrong.


The following night, I heard the footsteps again. This time they started much earlier, around midnight. I checked the windows and saw nothing, so I sat back down and tried to ignore them. After about ten minutes, the footsteps stopped directly beneath the tower. Then I heard a voice. “You awake?” It was a man's voice, calm and quiet. I immediately reached for the radio and called the ranger station. Still nothing. I told the person that the area was closed and that I had contacted the authorities. There was no response. A few seconds later, the voice said, “I know.” That was when I got nervous. I locked the door and pulled the blinds over the windows. For the next hour, I heard occasional footsteps moving around the tower. Sometimes they would stop for several minutes before starting again. Then something strange happened. My radio suddenly turned on by itself. Static filled the room, followed by a faint voice saying my first name. I stared at the radio for several seconds before shutting it off. I didn't sleep that night. At sunrise, I climbed down and inspected the area around the tower. I found footprints in the dirt. They were definitely human, but something about them looked wrong. They were bare footprints, and whoever made them had walked around the tower several times. I followed them as far as I could. They stopped about 50 yards away, right in the middle of the trees. There were no footprints leading toward that spot and none continuing away from it. I reported everything to my supervisor. He told me it was probably somebody messing around, and he arranged for another lookout to check the tower with me the next day.


My supervisor arrived around noon with another ranger, and we searched the entire ridge. We found nothing. No campsite, no clothing, no food, no vehicle tracks, and no signs that anyone had been living nearby. The other ranger joked that I probably had a mountain lion walking around underneath the tower, but I knew what I'd heard. I decided to stay anyway. I'd worked in remote towers before, and I didn't want one strange experience to scare me away from a job I'd spent half my life doing. For the next two nights, nothing happened. I started feeling embarrassed about the whole thing. Then, on the third night, at exactly 2:17 in the morning, I heard the footsteps again. I checked the clock because I wanted to know whether I was imagining a pattern. The footsteps came up the stairs. One step at a time. The metal stairs creaked under each step, getting closer and closer. I grabbed my flashlight and stood beside the door. When the footsteps reached the top, they stopped. Someone knocked once. I didn't answer. Another knock came. Then the voice from the previous night whispered, “I know you're in there.” I could hear breathing on the other side of the door. I stood completely still. After maybe thirty seconds, the breathing stopped. I waited another ten minutes before looking through the window. There was nobody on the stairs. I slowly opened the door and looked down. The stairs were empty. I locked the door again and stayed awake until morning. When daylight finally came, I found something that still bothers me more than anything else.


There was a single set of bare footprints leading up the stairs. They started at the bottom and continued all the way to the door. There were no footprints going back down. I photographed them and sent the pictures to my supervisor. He came up that afternoon and looked at them himself. He didn't joke this time. We searched around the tower again, but the prints simply ended at the door. That night, I decided I was done. I packed my equipment and planned to leave at first light. Around 3:00 in the morning, I woke up to a sound outside the window. Someone was quietly humming. I recognized the tune immediately. It was an old song my father used to sing when I was a kid. He had been dead for almost twenty years. I didn't move. The humming continued from one side of the tower to the other, as if someone was walking around the building. Then it stopped directly outside my bedroom window. A hand slowly appeared against the glass. It was pale, dirty, and completely still. I watched it for several seconds before it disappeared. I never heard the footsteps leave. I stayed awake until sunrise and drove away as soon as I could. The tower was closed for the rest of that season, officially because of maintenance problems. Nobody ever found out who had been there. What scares me most is that I've worked in forests long enough to recognize almost every normal sound they make. Whatever was outside that tower wasn't something I recognized.
I still work as a fire lookout. I considered quitting after that incident, but after many years, the job is part of my life. 

I've worked several towers since then, and I've never experienced anything like it again. Still, there are a few things I do differently now. I keep the doors locked at night, I don't investigate unexplained sounds alone, and I never answer voices coming from the forest after midnight. A few months ago, I mentioned the old tower to another lookout who had been working in the area for years. I asked him if he'd ever heard anything strange there. He got quiet and asked me why. I told him about the footprints and the voice. He stared at me for a few seconds before saying something I hadn't expected. He said the tower had a history. Years before I worked there, a previous lookout disappeared during the summer. They found his truck at the bottom of the mountain, but they never found him. According to the ranger records, his last radio transmission came at 2:17 in the morning. The transmission contained only static and three knocks. I checked the records myself afterward. He was right. I don't know what happened to that man, and I don't know what was outside my tower. Maybe there was a perfectly reasonable explanation. Maybe someone was playing a cruel prank. But after years in the forest has taught me not to dismiss things simply because I can't explain them. And whenever I'm sitting alone in a tower at night and hear three knocks from somewhere below, I don't look down anymore.
