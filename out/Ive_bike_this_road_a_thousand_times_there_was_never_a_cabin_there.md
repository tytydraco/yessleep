Back when I was around 17 years old, I went everywhere with my bike.  
I didn’t live in a small town, but it wasn’t huge either.  
Around 80,000 people lived there.  
It was actually really perfect.  
Not too busy, and still everything you could ask for in a town.  
A city centre, nice events, and enough people to meet and make friends.  
Around our town, there was mostly forest.  
And a little further away, something like a 15-minute bike ride, was an even smaller “town.”  
I say town, but it was more like a few houses stuffed together along one street.  
My name is Joel, and right now I’m 26 years old.  
My parents were divorced when I was very young, and I was very used to living in two places, having two birthdays, and going on two holidays.  
While I mostly lived with my mom, I still went to my father’s house now and then.  
I want to tell you something about that time when I was around 17 and decided, late at night, that I was going to spend the night at my dad’s house.  
I called him to ask if that was possible, and he said, “Yes, of course!”  
He even offered to pick me up with the car so I wouldn’t have to bike there.  
“I don’t mind picking you up and getting some food too,” he said.  
I refused because I knew it was just 15 minutes away, and I didn’t want to bother him with any of that.  
At around 11 PM, I messaged him that I was on my way.  
I checked the lights on my bike because I knew how dark the way there could get.  
My dad didn’t live in the same town as my mother. He lived a bit further away, in the town with the few houses stuffed together along one street.  
The way there was simply a long road going through dense forest and open fields.  
There weren’t many streetlights there either, so in the dense forest parts, it could get really dark.  
Around five minutes in, I got to the dark road, and without hesitation, I just kept going.  
I had biked it a lot of times already and knew my way around.  
Well, with knowing my way around, I actually mean that I knew how dark it got.  
But if you just kept looking forward, nothing would happen.  
At least, that’s what I thought…  
When I was halfway through the long, dark road, I arrived at the first open fields.  
On one side, it was full of corn, and the other side was just plain grass.  
It was still dark in the area, but because there were way fewer trees, you could see some things getting lit up by the moon.  
There was a small cabin at the back of the open field.  
Now, that’s not weird in any way.  
Except…  
There was never a cabin there.  
I had driven this way more than a thousand times, and never had I seen it.  
Not even in broad daylight.  
It had those cute wooden windows with a plus in them, if you know what I mean.  
Red curtains fell down to the side of the windows, and a dim yellow light came through them.  
Without hesitation, I stopped and looked at it for a while.  
So there I was, standing in the middle of the road, darkness around me, completely hyper-focused on this cabin in the back of the field that I had never seen before.  
At that moment, I felt something.  
Not a physical touch or a breeze.  
It was a deep feeling of fear, like it came from my toes, went all the way up, and shook me awake in an instant.  
You know that feeling when you almost fall from a height you know you won’t survive and save yourself at the last moment?  
It was exactly like that.  
When I looked to my right, I saw two headlights coming straight toward me at speed.  
I dashed back to dodge the car while the driver tried swerving to the side of the road, using his car horn multiple times.  
I fell backwards into the grass that was in front of the cornfield.  
Honestly, I was relieved I didn’t get hit and that the driver didn’t crash the car.  
I looked up when I heard the car door closing with a bang.  
There stood a figure next to the car.  
I still had no idea how the figure looked because it was so dark.  
It started running towards me with its arms reaching out.  
At first, I was scared out of my mind because I thought the driver must be mad.  
But when the figure came closer, I figured out who it was.  
Still running towards me, I heard a voice say,  
“Are you okay? Did I hit you?”  
I recognized her voice instantly.  
“Molly!?”  
“What are you doing here?”  
I have known Molly for almost my entire life.  
She lived next door in the little town my dad lived in.  
We used to go into the fields sometimes and hang out at the little group of trees that were behind our gardens.  
I was always into her back then, but I was too afraid to even say anything about it.  
She was a small woman with black hair and beautiful green eyes.  
When she laughed, she had those cute dimples on both sides of her smile, and she had the best personality.  
Very sweet and thoughtful, but also very forward in the way she said things.  
Just a genuine good person.  
Anyway…  
Molly stood in front of me, still panicked and reeling.  
“Don’t worry, I’m fine! You didn’t hit me with your car, and I’m not hurt.”  
When she finally caught her breath, she spoke again.  
“I thought I hit you, man!”  
“You scared me to death!”  
She said it while letting out a nervous laugh.  
We both laughed at the same time, and then she sat next to me in the grass.  
She asked me,  
“So… what are you doing here this late anyway?”  
I answered,  
“Well, I decided I wanted to stay at my dad’s tonight, and I didn’t want to bother him with picking me up.”  
Looking up to the field in front of us, she asked,  
“Then why are you standing here, in the middle of…”  
She stopped talking abruptly while looking at the cabin at the back of the field.  
“There is your answer, I guess. You know as good as I do that this cabin was never there.”  
“Yeah… that wasn’t there yesterday. Weird,” she said.  
It looked like she didn’t even hesitate before quickly saying,  
“That’s new, yes! You wanna go check it out?”  
I thought about it for a few seconds and saw this as a good opportunity to reconnect a little bit.  
Since we both had other things going on in life, we didn’t talk much anymore.  
I missed that.  
“Yes, let’s do it!”  
She parked her car a little bit further into the grass so it wouldn’t get hit by anything.  
Slowly, we walked over the dark open field together, on our way to the cabin.  
While she walked on my left side, I looked at her for a while.  
You could see her eyes shining in the moonlight, and her long black hair danced in the breeze.  
Even though she looked beautiful with the little dimples and freckles on her face, I did get distracted by what happened in the background.  
It was a fast movement of a figure.  
At least, that’s what I think it was.  
The figure moved into the dense trees, and I could hear some sticks break.  
I stopped walking for a moment but didn’t react with fear.  
I couldn’t.  
What if I scared Molly?  
I kept my eyes on the tree line but couldn’t find any movement.  
“What are you waiting for, slowpoke? Are you scared?”  
Molly said with a grin on her face.  
“Me? Scared? I’m the most mellow dude you know!”  
“True! Haha. Maybe I’m just a little bit scared myself…”  
As I started walking next to Molly again, she grabbed me by the arm and pulled me closer to her.  
I blushed because I wasn’t used to any girl touching me anywhere.  
“Why did you stop? Did you hear something?”  
I said,  
“No! I was just staring.”  
I started asking questions like,  
“What do you do lately?”  
And,  
“How are your parents doing?”  
While we were approaching the now nearby cabin, the sounds around us started fading a little bit.  
It wasn’t slowly.  
It was abrupt, like the noise-cancelling setting on your AirPods had suddenly been turned on.  
We both noticed it, but didn’t think too much of it.  
“Must be the trees,” I said.  
When we arrived at the cabin in the back of the field, I noticed something I couldn’t see from further away.  
Next to the small cabin was a path that ran into the woods.  
There were fences around those woods, and a gate that was locked with a lot of locks.  
Molly said,  
“Why would there be fences here? Are they trying to keep something in there?”  
“I don’t know, and I think I don’t even want to know.”  
She laughed nervously, and there was a small minute of silence.  
“Sooo… are we going in yet, neighbour boy?”  
I laughed at her and said,  
“You were never good at nicknames, were you?”  
She hit my shoulder gently, and—  
A hard, loud crack came out of the woods behind the fences.  
It didn’t sound like a stick that was stepped on.  
It was heavier.  
More loud.  
And you could almost feel it.  
A sound a big wooden tree would make if it was stood on by a giant.  
We both stopped moving and looked at the dark, dense forest.  
“That scared the shit out of me. Can we go?”  
Molly said while moving backwards slowly.  
“Let’s just get in the cabin. I think that’s our best option here.”  
I said it with a heroic voice, not knowing that this wasn’t the best option.  
I’m still not sure, but what I do know is that I still see the images and memories flashing in my mind of what happened when we opened the cabin door.  
Besides the incredibly hard sound we had just heard coming from the woods, it was still really silent around us.  
The only thing you could hear was the distant sound of a car passing sometimes.  
While standing in front of the brown wooden cabin door, Molly said,  
“What if someone’s in there?”  
“I’ve got a solution for that.”  
I knocked on the door three times.  
We waited for a little while but didn’t really hear anything or anyone.  
I grabbed the handle and slowly pushed it down.  
When I opened the door, I saw myself and Molly standing in front of us.  
At the same doorstep.  
The same plain grass field.  
And the road and cornfield behind that.  
We both stood there in complete silence.  
“What are we looking at?”  
I whispered to Molly.  
I could see the mouth of the Joel in front of me moving.  
That meant it was me.  
Or was it?  
How do I explain this?  
It wasn’t a mirror.  
The cabin was still there, but there was depth to it.  
I could reach in, like it was just a wall between two dimensions.  
In the background, I heard a dimmed sound of a car passing on the road.  
I clearly saw it passing on our side of the door, but on the other side, there was no car to be seen.  
Molly started pulling my arm to walk back from the door.  
With panic in her voice, she said,  
“Let’s just get out of here and watch a movie or something. I don’t like the way this is going.”  
I still couldn’t believe what I was seeing, so what Molly said didn’t reach me at all.  
“Joel!… Joel!!”  
When I came back to my senses, I saw Molly standing behind me, looking at the forest surrounded by the fences.  
“I saw something.”  
“Get me out of here, please!”  
I tried grabbing her hand, but something stopped me.  
From the door, I heard my own voice saying,  
“No! You don’t want to do this, Joel.”  
The Molly on the other side wasn’t walking backwards or screaming at all.  
She was just standing on the porch, laughing with her cute dimples and green, shining eyes.  
Molly screamed at me from halfway across the open field to come back and forget the whole cabin.  
The Joel on the other side of the door told me,  
“I can fix this fast and easy. Just let me do my thing, and we can both go our own ways.”  
He reached out with both hands and said,  
“Hold me with both hands, close your eyes, and clear your mind.”  
We stood there for maybe 20 seconds.  
The only thing I could hear were light footsteps going off the doorsteps we were standing on.  
After the 20 seconds, the Joel from the other side abruptly pulled down my arms and told me to sit for five more seconds before opening my eyes.  
When I opened them, we were both sitting there, arms still together, and I was completely lost in many ways.  
“I fixed it. I know you have questions, but this is just the way we live.”  
He stood up and walked back to Molly, who was now waiting at the side of the road, already opening her car door.  
While I was staring, I heard Molly from my side screaming,  
“Well?! You gonna come back anytime soon?”  
My Molly was still halfway across the grass field.  
“Coming!” I yelled back.  
I stepped away from the doorstep of the cabin and noticed that the sounds around me were loud again.  
But it wasn’t right.  
Maybe too loud.  
I looked back to see if the cabin door still showed the same thing.  
But the cabin was gone.
