My friends and I went on a camping trip for five people. *Five.* I have the booking on my phone: *\[Redacted\] National Park - Campsite Limit: 5 People*

I know for a fact that we showed up with that many. Ally’s car doesn’t seat more than that.

So how did we end up with six? 

The park ranger was the first one to notice. “You kids make sure to clean up after yourselves.” He lowered his shades and looked Jason in the eyes. “And next time, obey the rules will ya? Max of five people per site.” 

The ranger rode down the trail on his four wheeler before any of us could respond. 

I looked around at the confused faces of my friends. They were thinking the same thing I was. 

Jason was the one to put it into words. “What the hell was that about? We only have five people. And why was he giving *me* a hard time?”

My eyes connected with Sarah’s. Her face had gone pale. “Count again,” she said. “The ranger is right.” 

Each of us took a mental headcount. Just as Sarah had said, there were six people gathered around the smoldering fire. 

“This will be an easy one to solve. Who isn’t supposed to be here?” Gavin said. 

I glanced at my friends. We were a tight knit group. Gavin, Sarah, Jason, Ally, Steven, and me. We’d been close since college. Almost inseparable. According to logic, we all belonged there. 

A tense silence permeated the group. Ally broke it. “This is crazy. It’s not like one of us is an imposter or anything. We must have had six people the whole time. We just got too fucked up last night to remember.” 

“No, *you* got too fucked up to remember,” Steven chimed in. “I remember everything. We had six people the whole time.” 

I crossed my arms. “That’s impossible. Ally’s the one who drove us and her car only seats five with all the gear. Plus, that’s the camp limit. I wouldn’t have booked the site knowing we had six.” 

Sarah clung to Gavin’s arm. “I don’t like this...” 

Gavin rested a hand on the small of her back. “It’ll be okay. There’s a pretty easy way to determine who’s not supposed to be here. Whoever isn’t in the group chat is the odd man out.”

We all pulled out our phones. “That’s impossible,” Jason said. “Everyone’s in the group. We all sent confirmation texts that we were coming.”

I studied the faces of everyone around me. I remembered each of them accepting the invite. Still, a lingering sense of unease told me that something was off. 

“Let’s just go back to the car,” Steven said. “There’s not much else we can do about it right now. Hopefully we’ll get the driving situation figured out by the time we get there.” 

Jason pursed his lips. “He’s right. No point in worrying about it.” 

No one spoke as we packed up our things. We could all sense the tension in the air. It was palpable. Clearly, something wasn’t adding up. And soon, we would all find out what it was. 

“Everyone ready?” Gavin asked. 

“Looks like it. Let’s get out of here. I don’t like the vibes around this place,” Ally said as she started down the trail. 

I looked over to Jason. He shrugged and followed behind her. 

The walk back to the car was tense. Despite knowing that it had to be some sort of mix-up, there was an omnipresent feeling of unease clinging to the air. It felt as if each of us was trying to sniff out who wasn’t supposed to be there without tipping off the others.

After about twenty minutes of walking, Gavin stopped. “Uh, guys? I think we might have to go back…” 

“Why?” Sarah looked up at him, her brows knitted together. 

“I think I forgot my housekeys. I can’t find them anywhere.” 

Steven sighed. “Really, man? I’m not walking back with you.” 

Ally crossed her arms. “I’m not either. We can wait.” 

I didn’t like where this was going, but I didn’t want to intervene.

“You guys are a bunch of jerks, you know that? I can’t believe you.” Gavin shot us a death glare. Sarah tried to reassure him as the pair disappeared from view. 

Silence fell over us once again. 

“Maybe we were a little hard on him,” Steven said. 

Ally shook her head. “No. He does this every trip. He has to learn eventually.” 

Steven rubbed the back of his neck. “I mean, yeah, but maybe there was a better way to go about it.” 

I turned to Jason to get his take on things. 

Jason was gone.

“Uh, guys? Where’d Jason go?”

Ally raised an eyebrow. “I thought he was here the whole time. What happened to him?”

Steven’s face paled. “Maybe this is it. Whoever’s the sixth person, I mean. They’re trying to split us up and pick us off one by one.” 

“Stop,” I said. “We can’t buy into delusional thoughts like that. Let’s find Jason and get back to the car.”

Steven nodded. “You’re right. Sorry. Let’s go look for him.” 

“Jason! Jay-*SOOON!”* Steven shouted over and over, his hands cupped to his lips. Ally and I yelled too, but not as loud as him. I could tell that Ally had been rattled by Steven’s words. She wasn’t acting like her normal self.

Steven suddenly stopped shouting. “Wait. Is that… no. It’s a piece of Jason’s flannel.” He turned to us. “Come on. We have to check it out.” 

Ally and I shared a glance. Steven bolted off before either of us could catch up to him. I took a step forward. A knot of dread swelled in my gut. 

And that’s when it happened. 

All sound just… stopped. Like someone had clicked the mute button on a TV remote. There were no more birds singing. No more insects buzzing or rustling branches or flowing creeks. It was all encompassing. Total, deafening silence. 

I turned to Ally. She stood stock-still. Tears welled in her eyes as she met my gaze. I couldn’t hear the next words she spoke, but I could read her lips well enough. 

“Are we going to die?”

Our senses returned as suddenly as they had left. All the noises of the forest hit me at once. I could have cried, but I held it together. I never thought I’d be grateful for the sound of a gnat buzzing in my ear, but I was. 

I looked at Ally. She was shaking her head, her eyes clenched shut. “I don’t like this. Cassie, I don’t like this at all. I feel like something bad is going to happen.” 

I clasped her shoulders. She locked eyes with me, tears flowing down her cheeks. Her next words nearly broke me. 

“I don’t want to die.” 

“We won’t. But we have to keep it together, okay? I need you to trust me. We *will* make it out of here.” 

Ally wiped the tears away. “Okay. I trust you.” 

“Good. Now let’s go find Steven and Jason. If we don’t run into them after a few minutes, we can go back to the car and call for help, alright?” 

Ally nodded. “Alright.” 

I led the way in the direction that Steven had been running. I put on a tough face, but internally, I was sweating bullets. Something was wrong. *Very* wrong. And I’d be stupid to pretend that it wasn’t. 

We had only gone a few steps when we heard it. 

“Hey! Get off me. What the hell are you doing?” 

Ally and I sprinted toward the yelling. We rounded a tree, and there they were. 

Steven and Jason were on the forest floor, wrestling for position. Steven had blood on his arm. Jason’s face had been scratched. They both stopped when they noticed us standing there. 

“Ally, Cassie, we have to get out of here! Jason just attacked me,” Steven said, rising to his feet. 

Jason jumped up. “What are you talking about? *You* attacked *me*!” 

“I did not. Guys, Jason’s the imposter. He’s trying to kill us.”

“What the fuck are you talking about, man?” 

Steven crossed his arms. “If you’re innocent, then where did you run off to? Why did you disappear a few minutes ago?”

“I didn’t,” Jason said. “I went to find a spot to piss.” 

“Oh yeah? Well, why didn’t you tell anyone? And why didn’t you respond when we called for you?”

Jason’s face grew red. His fists were clenched. “I told *you* where I was going! And I didn’t hear any yelling, either. Why are you trying to frame me as the bad guy?”

I shook my head. “Look, I don’t care who attacked who. Let’s just get back to the car. We can sort this out later.” I turned around and began to walk away, expecting my friends to follow. 

I froze. I barely made it two steps.

The sound dropped again. It was heavier this time. I tried to move, but after just seconds, a new sound cut in. 

A sharp, high pitched ringing assaulted my ears. It was as if someone had cranked my tinnitus up to eleven. The sound was debilitating. So loud that it hurt to move. 

That’s why I didn’t have time to react before he crashed into me full force. 

My whole body hurt. The ringing in my ears kept me almost immobile. Even so, I doubt I could have moved anyway, even if it wasn’t there. I was in too much shock. 

I could feel nails digging into my flesh, holding me in place. Razor sharp, more like talons than anything human. But that wasn’t what had me paralyzed with fear. 

Steven was sitting on top of me. His grin was wider than it should have been. He had too many teeth. How did I not notice that? 

He held a stick above his head, ready to plunge it down at a moment’s notice. I could see it then. 

Steven’s flesh rippled. There was something beneath it. Something inhuman. 

Suddenly, over the ringing, Steven said something to me. It came through loud and clear. 

“Goodbye, Cassandra.” 

I shut my eyes. I didn’t want to die looking at that face. 

I expected to feel the stick driving through my neck any moment. Unimaginable pain before the release of death. 

But to my shock, a second later, the ringing stopped and my hearing returned. 

When I opened my eyes, Jason was standing over me. He held a rock splattered with crimson. Blood trickled from his ears. Steven lay crumpled on the ground. 

“What the…” 

Jason helped me to my feet. “I tried to warn you. Come on. We have to get out of here.” 

I nodded. He didn’t have to tell me twice. 

Ally, Jason, and I sprinted all the way back to the car. When we finally made it, we were sweaty  and out of breath. We threw what little stuff we had inside the car and got in. 

“Shit. What about Gavin and Sarah? I forgot all about them,” Ally said from the back seat. Despite the fact that it was her vehicle, Jason would be the one driving. He was the most unshaken. 

I pulled out my phone. “We have to call someone. What if Stev- that *thing* got to them?”

I didn’t even have a chance to dial before someone emerged from the path. 

It was Gavin. His clothes were splattered in blood. He trotted up to the car and pulled on the handle. He slammed a hand onto the glass when the door didn’t open. 

“Guys, come on. Let me in. We have to go. *Now*.” 

Jason stared at him. “What happened to Sarah?” 

Gavin’s hand fell from the door handle. “There was no Sarah. She was some… *creature.* Cassie was right all along. There were only five of us.” 

Jason didn’t waver. “How do we know you’re telling the truth?” 

“Try to picture Sarah’s face. Do you have any memories of her?” 

I tried to remember her. 

Not a single memory came to mind. I couldn’t even remember what color her hair was.

The car door swung open. Ally scooted over. “Get in.” 

After filling Gavin in on what happened with Steven, the four of us rode in silence away from the park. The further we got, the more my brain fog began to clear. Steven didn’t exist. Neither did Sarah. 

Then… What were they? 

I wasn’t able to think about it much. I watched as Ally’s eyes grew wide. “Jason, turn around. You have to. Please.” 

Gavin looked at her like she’d grown a second head. “Are you crazy? No way in hell we’re doing that!” 

“You have to,” Ally said. Tears formed in her eyes. “I forgot about him. God, how could I forget about him? We’ve been together for four years… We *have* to go back.”

Jason looked at her in the rearview mirror. “Why, Ally? Why do we have to go back?” 

Ally’s bottom lip quivered. I’d never seen her so worked up. 

“Because we forgot about Ben.” 

My heart dropped. I don’t know why I didn’t think to check before. I pulled out my phone and opened the group chat. 

I had been right all along. We had shown up with a party [of five.](https://www.reddit.com/r/HorrorJunkie123/)
