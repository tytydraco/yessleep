I was abrupt with that last ending. Sorry about that. I was a little overwhelmed.

I don’t remember much about our departure from the hospital. Maia led us back through the dark area, and she was really tired after that. It took a lot out of her, I guess. When we got back outside, I grabbed the keys from Shugart, and drove us back to the HunkaBurger Hideout—sorry, couldn’t help myself. I think I just needed something to do, a distraction from everything that was going on.

Maia and I ended up spending the night alone there. Shugart got a call from his captain, who said more of the hellspots were popping up throughout the city. He left to look into it, but promised to be back in a few hours. Hugh asked if he could ride with him. He had some contacts he wanted to hit up to see what he could find out about the Ring’s plan. He handed me the keys to his car and a two-way, just in case.

She had gone silent, so I left her alone in the office while I blew up the air mattress inside the old walk-in cooler. After that, I made us some soup, and we just sat there together and ate our dinner without saying anything. I asked her if she wanted to call her mom or Mina. They still didn’t know she was back. She had been afraid to speak to them when she was turning to stone, and I thought it might be a better time, since she was cured. Maia said no, though, that she’d talk to them later.

It really made me wonder about her and the weird way she had been acting. I asked her about it, and she told me that she was still trying to wrap her head around everything. She said needed some time and space to adjust to everything. I said okay, maybe a little too easily—I know that now. I was really worried about Sarah and wracking my brain to think of anything I could do to find her. I wonder if Maia knew that, and if her clichéd answer was actually an attempt to make me try harder. Maybe I should have pushed the issue more.

I sent Maia to get some rest while I kept watch at the back door. I thought I’d wait for Shugart or Hugh to get back, but my body had other plans. I was sitting there, trying to pay attention, when I nodded off and found myself somewhere else.

The smoke in the tunnel made it hard to breathe, and I woke up coughing as a result. I was lying on a smooth stone floor that was warm to the touch. Because of my past experiences, I already suspected that I wasn’t in a dream. That didn’t stop me from pinching myself, of course. But there’s no fool like a damned fool, am I right?

My eyes started watering almost immediately, aggravated by the dozen or so torches that were mounted into the rough-hewn walls of the tiny space. I could see that I was in a circular room with three paths leading out of it. I heard voices coming from the one to the left, and I didn’t know if I should go that way or try another. I was on my feet in a startled heartbeat when someone spoke from behind me.

“Hello, Will,” the voice said, and I turned around to see Phil leaning against the stone. He still looked very pale, and his eyes were more sunken than I remembered. He had been skinny then, but had lost enough weight since to look nearly emaciated. How, though? It had only been a couple of days.

His hair was thinner too, and when he opened his mouth to speak again, I saw empty sockets where several of his teeth had been. “Yes, I know, time is catching up to me. It does that, you know. There’s always a difference in its flow, depending on the realm you are in. When you come back home, your body wants to rush to catch up with it. So if I was in that Marble Hell for nine years, how many do you think that equals in our world? Twenty-five or so? That would put me in my late eighties.”

“Is this you?” I asked him. “Did you somehow bring me here, to wherever we are?”

“This place? It’s nowhere; something the scholars might call a liminal space. I’ve always preferred something simpler. I call it the pith. You are familiar with the little theory DL *borrowed* from me?”

“Yeah, the pomegranate. You still didn’t answer me, though. Did you bring me here?”

“I did! I figured since DL was cribbing off of my notes, I could be justified in picking his pocket.” Phil slowly pulled a short, black rod with a forked tip out from behind his back. He twirled it once clumsily before slipping it inside the robe he wore. “You don’t think you reached the Forest of Chains by chance, do you? DL wanted you scared. I want something else.”

“What’s that, my firstborn? I know you tricked me with Maia. Why would I trust you?”

“I didn’t trick you, Will; it was the only cure I knew. As for trusting me, I don’t need you to. No, trust human nature. Trust that I want to live, like everything that ever has. I need the Marble for that, and I know you have it. DL still thinks Sarah does, but I can smell it on you.”

There was no sense in denying it, and there was no way I was giving it to him. I chose to say nothing, just crossed my arms and shook my head. He sneered, but finally nodded.

“Yeah, bad sales pitch. Just think about it, though. It would be a lot easier to get your friend back if I was out of the equation.”

“You’d turn on DL?”

“Not really. I’d just stay out of it. I believe you, your cop, and my turncoat son might have a chance then. A slim one, but that’s not nothing.”

“The answer’s still no. I’m leaving now. If you try to stop me—”

“Yes, yes, off with you. But you will give it to me eventually. If you won’t do it willingly, then perhaps I’ll have to broaden your education on the definition of hell.”

I started to respond, to say something crippling, but he flicked the rod out and I was back in the safehouse. What a fucking last word freak.

I couldn’t sleep after that, so I spent the next few hours putting together the update I posted yesterday. Shugart came in right after I finished. He looked like he had gotten some sleep. God knows he needed it.

I asked him what he found out, and it turned out not to be very much. With the rest of the department overwhelmed by all the hellspots, he had pretty much had the run of the place. He had gotten the files on Phil and DL, but nothing in them amounted to anything of substance. The one they had for the Knotted Ring wasn’t even there. The log sheet had Melody’s name written on it, and even Shugart couldn’t brush that one off.

That left Hugh as our one big hope, and that was if one of his contacts hadn’t turned on him. He could already be dead and buried for all we knew. It left a knot in my stomach. When he asked Shugart for a ride and told us his plan, nobody tried to talk him out of it. I sure as hell hadn’t. I was so desperate to find Sarah that I probably would have let him try anything.

I considered calling him on the two-way he’d left for me, but I worried that the wrong person might overhear me. So, I couldn’t call him, and I didn’t know where to begin looking for him. All I could do was wait, and I hated how that felt. It’s the most useless feeling in the world.

I could tell that Shugart wasn’t as bothered by the waiting as I was. He told me he’d been on a lot of stakeouts. While we didn’t have anything else to do, he found us some seats and brought out some cards. He taught us a game called *Skat*, something his German grandfather had shown him. It played a little like spades, except the deck was smaller and the teams and bidding were different. A good enough way to pass the time, though I can’t say I ever got the hang of it like Maia did. That girl is just full of surprises lately.

It was already getting dark when Hugh came in, and I was so happy to see that he wasn’t hurt. He brought us some chicken for dinner, and he told us to eat fast while he told us what he had learned.

“I know where they took her,” he said. “It might not be where she is, but it’s a start.”

“A damn good one,” said Shugart, clearly impressed. “What are we looking at?”

“Well, that’s where it gets tricky.” Hugh took a sip of his drink and pulled out his phone. He opened it up and brought up a picture of a megachurch I recognized.

“*Fruit of the Tree*?” I asked him.

“Yeah, they aren’t subtle at all.”

“I thought it was Baptists there,” Shugart said. “Are you saying the Ring controls it or owns the place?”

“They own the building for sure. Not everyone in the congregation is a part of the Knotted Ring, but all of the leadership are.”

“And you’re saying we have to go in there tonight, Wednesday night, to get her back?” Shugart asked him. “What kind of attendance are we talking about?”

“Probably a few hundred people, and most of them will be DL’s people. When I was going they always had their deacon meetings on Wednesdays, and they all know the secret handshake.”

“Does it have to be tonight?” I asked.

“From what I was told, there’s something big going on tonight. They’ve called in higher-ups from all over the region. My guy thinks it’s a ritual, and probably a big one. I have a feeling—and I hope I’m wrong—but if they took Sarah, she’s likely to be the star of the show.”

“You mean like a sacrifice?” Shugart looked at me when I said it and then back over to Hugh.

“Wait a minute,” he said. “I ain’t trying to sully the good name of the cultist who told you this shit, but how can we be sure he’s even on your side? This could be a great trap. They could get us where they want us at a time of their choosing. We need to be sure before we go tromping into the lion’s den.”

  
“Cultists,” Hugh sighed. “Look, I respect you, man, but you don’t understand. It’s not as black and white as all that. A lot of them, especially the younger ones, aren’t really committed to the dark shit. They stay because they were raised up in it, like I was. The Cluster means different things to different people. One of my mentors believes it isn’t all hells, that there are heavens there too. I hope she’s right. So try not to reduce them all to Phil or DL’s level. I trust her with my life.”

“Yeah,” Shugart grunted. “And with ours too.”

“Hugh,” I said, interrupting to break the tension between them. “Is there anything else we need to know?”

He smiled. “That’s where it gets tricky. From what she said, it’s happening down in the sublevel, in a place they call the pit. It’s where they control all of the mechanical stuff they use in their shows. The problem with it is that the best access to it is from backstage, in the main auditorium. That means we have to go through the crowd.”

“Is that even possible?” I asked.

“I’ve got the start of a plan, but I’m not going to lie to you; it’s risky.”

He went over the basics of it with us while we got our things ready. He and Shugart had both brought more ammo, so we took the time to reload all the mags they had. Shug even had a couple of speed loaders for the little revolver he had given me. Hugh offered Maia his backup, but she declined. She’d never been the biggest fan of guns, even before everything that happened.

When he had finished telling us, and everything was ready, we all loaded up in Hugh’s car. He let Shugart drive—it seemed like a peace offering to me—and we set off on our ill-advised rescue mission.

After nearly an hour in gridlocked traffic, we finally made it across town. That meant it was well past eight before we even arrived at the church. The *Fruit of the Tree* campus took up six city blocks. There were several buildings on the grounds surrounding the gigantic sanctuary. The directory listed an auditorium, commons, a children’s ministry, a youth center, and many more. I was glad I wasn’t driving us, because I was already lost just looking at all of the nearly identical tan-bricked buildings. Luckily, Hugh knew the place well, and he easily guided Shugart through a handful of turns that brought us to a small parking lot behind the main chapel.

“That building behind it is mostly used for admin,” he said. “If everything goes to plan, we’ll be coming out from there. Does anybody have any questions about what we’re doing?”

“Why don’t we just go in that way then?” Shugart asked him.

“It wouldn’t give us access. The elevator there is exit only.”

The detective nodded, and when no one else said anything, Hugh continued.

“Alright, let’s do it then. If we get lucky, we might have a shot at this.”

Shugart chuckled as he checked his loads. “Maybe. Either that or we’re just gonna get shot. You know how these churchy types love their guns.”

We came up around the side of the huge building, creeping past all of the dark entrances as we made our way to the front. Shugart had taken the lead, and when we reached the corner, he whispered for us to wait there. I heard Hugh grumbling behind me while Shug kept going. I peeked around to get a better look at what he was doing

Shugart straightened his jacket as he walked down the sidewalk to the front door. There was a man standing there. He was a little younger than Shug, and despite his expensive tailored suit, the way he had posted himself suggested security. Shugart raised his hand in greeting, and I heard the two of them say something to each other. When he got close to the man, the guard barked something angrily before reaching inside his jacket. The older detective was too fast for him, though. He drove a quick punch right into the underside of the guy’s jaw, knocking him to the ground.

He gestured for us to come on, and we hurried across to join him. When we got to him, he was dragging the unconscious man into the bushes. Hugh gave him a questioning look.

“It’s just Melody,” Shugart snorted. He pocketed the gun the man had been reaching for. “He sold out the department for a place at the table, and they made him eat in the yard. I can’t blame ‘em, the guy’s a joke.”

When the crooked cop was safely stowed, the four of us walked into the lobby. It was a really impressive room for it to only be an antechamber. The thickly carpeted floor and domed roof gave it an air of majesty. The walls were decorated with a series of panels depicting all of the more popular scenes from the bible. The ceiling was a depiction of heaven with all the usual classic details, like clouds and golden gates. What was unusual about it was that inside each scene of paradise, there was something troubling hidden within. A grand mirror held a warped reflection, a shadow loomed threateningly, and a lake had rippling patches of water that formed sinister shapes. It was an odd choice for a church, and I’m sure I would have noticed more if we hadn’t been so hurried.

There was a set of double doors ahead of us and a service hall that circled the central auditorium. Hugh said that there would be guards patrolling the perimeter, so that wouldn’t work. I looked over at him. “You sure about this?”

He shrugged. “They won’t expect it. That’s gotta be some advantage.”

I could hear the faint sound of something akin to music coming from the other room, and when Hugh cracked open the door, it hit me like he’d turned it up to eleven. I thought I could feel it pressing against my eyes. It made the air seem heavier somehow. As the torrent bounced through my ears, my jaw felt stiff, and it occasionally clicked like it was dislocated or something.

It got worse when we walked inside, and I could tell that I wasn’t the only one affected by it. Shugart’s jaw was clenched so tight it was a wonder his teeth didn’t shatter. Hugh’s reaction was also worrying. He wore a haunted look on his face, and his left eye started twitching and his hands began to shake. Maia appeared to be unfazed, except for her obvious concern for the three of us. Before we even got to the stage, she was physically supporting Hugh so he didn’t fall down.

We skirted the back of the room, behind the congregants, as we walked. It wasn’t until we reached the side that I got a good look at the source of the strange music that was affecting us like that. A woman stood on the central stage, and in front of her lay a large slab of stone. It was run through with veins of varicolored gems and shimmering ore. She held her hands above it and moved them as if she were conducting a symphony. Each gesture evoked a different tone, and by weaving her fingers in intricate patterns, she blended them into a discordant wail that was captivating the swaying crowd.

They were all lost in her song. As we moved down the aisle right in front of them, no one noticed us, and we quickly reached the stage where the woman was working her magic. She glanced up as we approached her, but she never stopped playing.

“You took your time, Hugh,” she said, and I looked at him. He was deathly pale and shaking all over. When he responded to her, his voice quivered as if his teeth were bouncing in his skull.

“Hu-how long, Martha?” he asked her. “How long can you give us?”

“Not too long,” she said. “If I keep this up, half of them will never awaken.”

He nodded at her and then at me. “You heard her. We need to move.”

“Yeah, thanks, Martha.”

“Just stop them,” she said. “They tamper with things they can’t understand.”

I didn’t know how to answer that, so we just continued on. Hugh stopped us when we got to the back of the stage and showed Shugart and me the trapdoor. We opened it up and climbed down into the pit.

Shugart led the way, and we entered an area that was much smaller than I expected. Luckily, it was nearly soundproof, so we could barely hear Martha’s music anymore. There were a lot of mechanical contraptions with levers and pulleys scattered around. I could only guess at what they did. Along the wall facing the front of the stage, I saw a row of servers. A man sat on a stool at the end of them staring at a monitor. He had headphones in and was swaying in place like the people up above.

Hugh seemed to have regained some of his composure by then. He led us over to a steel plate that was set into the floor in the center of the room and took hold of the control box that was hanging on a cable from the ceiling. He pressed the button, and the plate split apart, opening upwards as a large metal booth slid out of the hole. It was the elevator he had talked about earlier. We all walked inside, and he tapped the down arrow on the wall.

We descended into the sublevel.

After nearly a full minute, we finally reached it, and the car settled gently on the floor. The door opened on the opposite side of the one we entered, revealing a long, concrete hallway with ceiling-mounted light bars running down the length of it. Everyone who had a gun armed themselves, and we walked down the hall towards the containment door at the end.

The door spun into the wall to reveal a small chamber within. When we went inside, it turned again to reveal a doorway on the opposite side. I moved to go in first, and I’m only alive because Shugart stopped me short of the opening.

A bullet whizzed by right in front of me right before Shugart shoved me behind him. He crouched and crept up to the doorway and fired twice blindly in the direction the shot had come from. The room in front of us was full of large plastic-wrapped machines and several piles of crates. The nearest ones were fifteen feet away from us. Shugart turned to us and whispered his plan.

“When I start shooting, y’all run for those crates. When you get there, you can lay down cover for me. On three.”

Three fingers went up, and he unloaded on them. The rest of us sprinted for the crates, and I took the rear to make sure the others made it. I had the little revolver out, and I intended to shoot at them on the run like I’d seen people do in the movies. I’m pretty sure all three of my shots went wild. As soon as I was close enough to the crates, I dived behind them to avoid return fire. I nearly knocked Maia over when I landed on top of her, and I chipped one of my front teeth on the side of the crate.

Since then, I’ve come to accept the fact that I’ll never be an action hero.

Maia helped me up, and I peeked around to see what we were up against. There were maybe a dozen men, all dressed in long, silver robes. Most of them stood far across the room from us in a circle around a pit in the floor. The two closest ones were facing us with guns in their hands. I watched as they ducked down when Hugh fired at them. Shugart ran past behind me a few seconds later.

“That’s where we have to go,” said Hugh. “The pit’s a portal to where the real ritual is taking place. That’s where Sarah will be.”

“How do we get past them?” I asked. “There’s so many of them.”

“I’ll divert them,” Shugart said as he fired a shot at the two shooters. “If I can get a little closer, I can shoot at the circle and make ‘em scatter. The rest of you get in there—quick as you can.”

“And leave you here by yourself?” Hugh said. “Fuck that. We’ll figure something out.”

“Only way it’ll work.”

“That’s suicide,” I said. “What if I go alone? That’s at least three to twelve.”

“Eleven now,” Shugart quipped. “I clipped that fucker on the right.”

“You can’t anyway,” said Hugh. “You’ve gotta be Ring to activate it. I’ll have to take you.”

“And I’ll stay and help,” Maia said. “Shug needs backup.”

I looked at her. She had been so different since we brought her back. With Sarah taken and everything surrounding that, I hadn’t let myself think about it much. I kept deciding that it was something for later, but I was just avoiding it. Whatever was going on with her scared me, because I had no idea who she was becoming after all she’d been through.

They were all waiting on me to say something, to make a choice, and I didn’t want to. I knew that if I did it and something went wrong, it would be on me. How could I live with that?

“I don’t know. Maybe—” I began.

“No,” she said. “You don’t get to do that, Will. This is our choice. You already blame yourself for too much that’s happened. Let us get you there so you can save her.”

“Yeah, kid,” Shug said. “It ain’t all up to you. We’re staying, but you still have to deal with whatever’s on the other side. You may be worrying about the wrong battle.”

“Check your loads,” said Hugh. “We’ll have to move fast, and you may need every shot.”

While I was replacing the bullets I’d wasted before, Shugart started firing again. I heard a man scream, and he and Maia began moving. I watched them advance to the machine the two men had been hiding behind.

I hadn’t even said goodbye to them.

“Come on,” said Hugh. “We’re going left.”

I followed him, and we ran as fast as we could. As we came around the last crate and out into the open, I saw the group of robed men scatter. Shugart had shot one of them, and the rest were running for cover. When we reached the pit, Hugh grabbed my hand and uttered a strange series of harsh sounds. It flared to life immediately, and he leapt in, dragging me with him. The last thing I saw before we were carried away was one of the cultists lifting a gun and firing at my friends.

There was no in-between with the portal. Leaping into it was like dropping down from a ledge. We landed feetfirst on a polished stone floor inside an immaculate temple chamber. The walls were carved with bas-relief murals depicting a number of different hells, and I recognized all of them. There were also large sculptures at each of the room’s four corners, portraying monolithic winged things that could only be demons. I didn’t have time to study any of it because we weren’t alone.

In the center of the space was a wide altar, and on it sat another one of the demon statues. It was much larger than the others, and it was sculpted as a woman with her lower half replaced by the body of mantis. She had three pairs of feathered wings on her back and clusters of writhing tentacles where each of her arms should have been. A group of five silver-robed figures stood around the sculpture. They were chanting the name, *Mezakere*.

Sarah was lying there on the stone in front of the half-mantis idol.

I glanced at Hugh, and we started walking towards them with our guns drawn. When we got a little closer, Sarah turned her head and spotted me. Her eyes widened, and I heard her say, “No, Will. Get out of here. You can’t—”

The cultists heard it too, and the two between us and Sarah turned around. One of them was DL. He said something quietly to the man beside him—probably telling him to continue with the ritual—and took a step toward Hugh and me. DL brought his hands up and laced his fingers together before bowing slightly.

“It’s good that you’ve come, Will. I think Sarah was getting worried.”

“Let her go, DL,” Hugh said. “It’s not too late to end this madness.”

DL turned to him, and his politician grin only widened. “Hugh! I’m so glad to see you here. I know you’re really mixed up about things right now, but I haven’t lost hope in you yet. As for her? Sure, go ahead and take her, with my blessing. We didn’t need her help after all.”

“So you’d let us, just like that?” Hugh kept his pistol trained on him.

“Not us, just you, Hugh.” DL freed a finger and pointed it at me. “I’m going to need Will’s company for just a little longer. But that girl needs medical attention. It would be irresponsible for any of us to delay that.”

I saw that Hugh was about to say something, and I cut him off. “So they go and I stay? Is that the deal? What about our other friends?”

“A misunderstanding.” DL pulled his hands apart and gestured at the room. “They can all leave. This was always going to happen, and it was always going to be you, Will. It’s destiny! Think on it. Why were you given this gift in the first place? Do you realize that you have seen more of the cluster than any living soul? We are meant to work together, Will.”

“Go get her, Hugh,” I told him before turning back to DL. “When I know that they are all safe, we can talk. If you’re fucking with me, I’ll shoot myself in the head and you’ll never get it. Can you handle *that* deal?”

Hugh was glaring at me, but he started walking toward her. DL raised a hand and told his men to let him pass. Sarah could barely stand, much less walk, but Hugh was able to pull her against him and brace her against him with his injured arm so they could limp their way to the portal. It obviously hurt him, but it allowed him to keep his gun ready.

When they were even with us, Sarah stopped him and spoke to me. “Don’t do this, Will. You understand what they’re trying to do. You can’t let them bri—”

That was when Phil appeared. It was as if the air in front of Sarah unfolded itself, and he was already standing there. “You will not speak of her, you bitch,” he hissed, and raised his hand to strike her. Hugh reacted instantly, pushing Sarah to the ground and firing three times at his father. Phil was too quick for him, though. He shifted into his phantom form and let the shots pass harmlessly through him. Then he turned on his son with his razor talons.

Hugh went down in a flurry of quick slashes, emptying his gun into Phil as he fell. DL took one of those shots. I watched him go from rigid to limp when a bullet struck him right above the left eye, and the back of his head filled the air behind him with a cloud of blood and gray matter. I dove toward Sarah, but Phil caught me in mid-leap and slammed me down hard onto the stone floor.

He was turning back into the old man he really was, panting as he stared down at his dying son. “Goddamn waste. You always had too much of your mother in you.”

I looked over at Hugh, trying to catch my own breath, but he didn’t see me. His eyes were locked in hatred for the man standing above him.

“Rather… that than be… like you,” he said. “I hope the devil’s…”

He couldn’t finish his last words. Instead, he lifted his backup gun and pressed it weakly against his killer’s stomach and pulled the trigger. Phil doubled over as the point-blank shot tore through his midsection. As Hugh’s hand fell bonelessly to the floor, his father lurched and spewed a gout of blood and bile on his son’s corpse.

I went for my own gun then. It had landed beside me, and I quickly snatched it up and raised it. Phil was staring at me with pure malice. He was about to speak when the men behind him started firing their weapons.

Two of them had turned away from their ritual and grabbed their guns. When they fired, I thought they were shooting at me, but they were aiming too high. When I heard gunshots behind me, I looked and saw Shugart approaching us with his pistol raised.

“Get up, boy,” he said as he fired a shot through Phil, who had already changed back into his phantom form. I half-crawled over to him and stood up to face our attackers just as he took down the second shooter.

“Maia?”

“Don’t worry about her,” Shugart said. He was staring down the sights at Phil, his body weaving with his shifting aim. “She’s fine. It was the damnedest thing. She said something and they just started shooting at each other. Told me to come to you.”

We both fired as Phil lunged for Sarah, but we had to stop before we hit her. He held her up by one hand and gestured toward me. I could see that his smoky body was darker in the middle, around the area where he had been shot.

“Those fools behind me are going to keep up their chant even though they’re praying to a god that can’t hear them,” he said. “You’ve gotta love the fanatics. So what’s it going to be, Will? I’m pretty much dead without it, but I promise you that if I go, Sarah’s going with me.”

“Don’t, Will,” she said.

“Let her go, Phil,” I said. “Haven’t you done enough?”

He laughed at that. “Enough? There’s no enough. Everybody does what they *can* do. Enough doesn’t enter into it. You gonna make me count, Will? Is that what you need? Hugh always did that. Brat never could take my word for it.”

“And now you’ve killed him.”

“I didn’t want to,” he said. “I don’t want to kill her, either, but I will if you don’t give it to me.”

“That dark spot’s getting darker,” Shugart said to him, but Phil ignored him. He lifted the hand that wasn’t holding Sarah and flicked his claws. He raised the longest one and pressed it into her cheek. A thin ribbon of blood began to run down, dripping onto the hospital gown she was still wearing.

“How much of her pretty face do I need to remove before you finally give in?”

I looked over at Shugart, but the look he returned didn’t give me any answers. Sarah was just shaking her head as much as she could with the claws so close. She was crying and softly saying, “don’t.” I wanted to listen to her, but I knew I couldn’t live with it if I did.

“Okay,” I said. “Set her down and I’ll take you. That’s the deal.”

“I said give it.”

“I won’t do it while we are over here. I need to know that she’s safe.”

“And why should I trust you, Will?”

I actually laughed at that. “Because I’m a terrible liar. Are we going to do this? You’re running out of time.”

“Tell him to back away and drop the weapon.”

I looked at Shugart, and he nodded. He ejected the magazine, pocketed it, and tossed the pistol aside as he backed away. Phil set Sarah down—he was actually gentle about it—and he walked over to me. When he was close enough, I held out my hand.

“Ready?” I turned to Sarah and then to Shugart. “Thanks for having my back.”

“Enough of that sentimental crap,” Phil said, and his vaporous hand closed over mine. “Time to keep your word.”

I smiled and nodded at him as I gripped his hand. “I plan to.”

I turned to flip us over there and saw Shugart just as his hand grabbed my shoulder. When the three of us appeared among the marble sculptures, Phil’s reaction was immediate. He snatched me up and swung me into the detective, knocking us both to the ground. He leapt at me a moment later, and I barely rolled away in time to avoid his vicious claws.

I heard a gunshot and looked over at Shugart. I had forgotten all about Melody’s gun. He fired twice into Phil, but neither shot seemed to hurt him. I grabbed at him, but he stomped my injured arm, and I felt the bone snap. He turned to advance on Shugart.

“How many of your friends must I kill, Will?”

I shot him in the back, and he turned to face me. “What was your plan, liar? Did you bring me here to attempt to kill me? You do not know the power of this place. While I am here I am above death.”

I saw Shugart coming around him, and I stalled to give him time. “I never lied, Phil. I didn’t say I’d give it to you. I just said I’d bring you here, and now you’re going to stay.”

Phil laughed at me as he drew out the rod he stole from DL. As he lifted it to point it at me, Shugart tackled him, knocking him down with the rod in between them. The detective staggered as he tried to stand. His eyes were wide, and he was clutching a piece of the rod that was protruding from his chest. I watched in horror as his entire form seemed to expand and withdraw, and he fell to his knees just a few feet from me.

“Kid…” he began, and then his body folded into itself, and he was gone. Phil stared at me from across the empty space where Shugart had just been. He still had the handle of the rod clutched in his hand. I screamed and charged at him.

When I got on top of him, I started punching him over and over in the face with my good fist. I hooked my broken forearm under his chin to keep him still. I felt only rage in that moment. The thing below me was a demon in my eyes, or the devil himself. It was he who had taken Maia, it was he who had hurt Sarah, and it was he who had done that to Shugart. He was never going to get to hurt another one of my friends again.

The phantom form quivered in my grasp. I could feel it in him, a power he had found and used as both a sword and shield. No more. I tore it away from him. I didn’t know I could do it, but I did. It tried to bond with me, but I turned it away and cast it back into the dark sea where it belonged.

I could feel the island responding to my wrath. Tiny stones shook and then slid before they launched themselves toward us. There were thousands of them, and they all awaited my command. I knew that they were all once a part of a single, original stone. I felt it taking shape as they found each other like pieces of an ancient puzzle finally made whole.

It was into that creation that I shoved him, ignoring his screams and pleas for mercy. I was past that. As the stone closed around him, I admired what I had made for one long moment before casting it into the dark void sea. After that, I picked up the broken handle and left the Marble Realm.

I vowed never to return.

The cultists were gone when I got back, so I grabbed Sarah and got us back through the portal. It was still open, and I found out why when we came through. Martha had taken charge of the Ring after DL’s death, and made them hold it for us. Maia was with her, and when I saw her, I told her what happened to Hugh and Shugart.

She was sad about Hugh, but said we’d find a way to get Shug back.

Martha showed us the way out, and we took Sarah back to the hospital. After that, things have been pretty quiet since Thursday morning. I expected the police to come back after me, but I was contacted by an attorney I didn’t know I had. She said that all the charges had been dropped.

The official story for what happened at the church is that nothing happened. Hugh didn’t get an obituary and Shugart is officially on leave. I guess everything goes back to normal now? Whatever the hell that is.

Maia came by yesterday and told me she can’t see me anymore. I already expected it, but it didn’t make it hurt any less. She said she needs time to figure out who she is now. I told her when she does to let me know. I didn’t get an answer to that, or even expect one.

I went to the hospital last night and gave Sarah back the Marble. It wasn’t mine to keep, and I don’t need it. I’m going to find Shugart, and I’m not done looking into the Knotted Ring. They can play nice all they want, but I don’t trust them. I know too much now.

Oh, and I finally went back to Hell last night at 1:18.

Maybe that’s my normal.
