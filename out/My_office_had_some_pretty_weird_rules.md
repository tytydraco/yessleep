Any of you ever worked somewhere with strange rules?

Like, really strange. Not things like *Don't decorate your desk* or *No talking for two minutes after a meeting*. I'm talking about genuinely absurd rules. Rules that make absolutely no sense given where you're working.

Well, that's what happened to me.

It all started four months ago. To put it simply, I was down on my luck. My rent had practically doubled thanks to the cost of living, and my parents had moved to the opposite side of the country. I was pretty much on my own.

Just in case it wasn't clear, I'm not going to specify any details about where this happened. Not to protect myself, but to protect you.

As I was saying, I was down on my luck. That is, until I saw a poster advertising a job at your average corporate office. It read:

>**Position: Accountant**  
**Working Hours: 9 a.m. – 5 p.m.**

If any of you have ever applied for a job like this, you can imagine how the rest goes. I wasn't exactly over the moon when I saw it, but I was pretty excited. I gave the number a call, went through a relatively short interview, and somehow got the job.

I started the following Monday, and immediately went out to buy some shirts, ties, and your standard office wear.

When I arrived, I was pretty nervous. This was my last straw, after all. If I fucked this up, I genuinely didn't know what I'd do.

My nerves started to disappear as soon as I met the receptionist. She was in her sixties and seemed nice enough. She greeted me with a smile, checked my name off a list, and directed me towards my desk.

The office was surprisingly large. Rows of desks, glass meeting rooms, a kitchen, and a few corridors leading to departments I hadn't been introduced to yet.

"If you need anything, go straight to HR. And make sure to read the rules."

That was what she told me before returning to her computer.

Every workplace has rules. So I wasn't particularly surprised when I found a printed sheet sitting on my desk. I figured it was just the usual employee handbook stuff, so I started reading.

Most of them were pretty basic. Then came the last eight.

>**RULES:**

>**1.) If you arrive at work and your desk has been moved, inform HR immediately and relocate to the meeting room. Do not attempt to move your desk back.**  
**2.) If a meeting appears on your calendar that you have not been informed of, do not attend it. Do not attempt to decline the invitation.**  
**3.) Your office PC only has one default wallpaper. If it changes, restart it immediately. This should remove the wallpaper and anything that came with it.**  
**4.) The photocopier on Floor 2 occasionally prints an extra page. If this page contains your name, shred it immediately. Do not read anything else on the page.**  
**5.) Emails originating from the management department should be deleted unread. Do not reply, even if the sender is someone you recognize.**  
**6.) If you hear somebody sobbing in an empty room, do not investigate. Report the incident to HR instead.**  
**7.) Do not open the black envelope in the second drawer of your desk until Rule 8 becomes relevant.**  
**8.) If a man wearing a janitor's uniform approaches you asking for his payment, give him the black envelope and do not communicate with him further.**

I've seen videos of office pranks like these, so I disregarded most of it and got on with my work.

The job itself was pretty boring. Mostly moving accounts, processing transactions, and handling finances. Nothing particularly interesting, but it paid well. I wasn't complaining.

For the first two weeks, nothing happened. My life was finally getting back on track. I'd made a couple of friends at work, and I was pretty sure I could finally move out of my shitty apartment.

There was one coworker in particular who I'd gotten along with. His name was Daniel, and he'd apparently been working there for about fifteen years. He was a pretty quiet guy, but he was nice enough.

One afternoon, I asked him about the rules.

He laughed.

"First week?"

"Yeah. Are they some kind of joke?"

He stopped laughing.

"Just follow them. You'll get used to it."

I asked him what he meant, but he simply returned to his computer.

I didn't think much of it.

That was until my desk moved.

It was a bleak Tuesday morning. I'd gotten so used to the office layout that I immediately noticed something was wrong.

My desk was supposed to be in the center of the room. Today, it was pushed flat against the wall.

I stood there for a good minute, trying to work out if I'd somehow remembered the layout incorrectly. I even asked Daniel if someone had rearranged the office.

He glanced at my desk, then back at me.

"Don't touch it."

I laughed, assuming he was messing with me.

"Seriously, what's going on?"

He didn't answer.

I remembered the rules and decided to go straight to HR.

The man sitting opposite me was in his late forties, maybe early fifties. He was clean-shaven, wore a grey suit, and had a nameplate that simply read HUMAN RESOURCES.

"You have read the rules, correct?"

That was the first thing he asked after I explained what had happened.

I told him I had.

"Then you understand what you need to do."

He told me to relocate to the meeting room.

I did.

Nothing particularly strange happened that day, but something started to feel off. I couldn't quite put my finger on it.

Over the following weeks, things got stranger.

My desk moved more frequently. Sometimes it was against the wall, sometimes facing the window, and once I found it sitting directly outside the meeting room.

I also started receiving calendar invitations for meetings I hadn't been informed of.

The strange thing was that they always appeared at exactly 3:17 p.m.

I never attended any of them, obviously.

One day, I made the mistake of asking Daniel whether he'd received the same invitations.

He looked at my computer screen, went completely pale, and told me to delete them.

I asked him why.

He simply said, "You don't want them knowing you've seen it."

I didn't ask again.

Then there was the photocopier.

I'd been printing some invoices on Floor 2 when I heard it start up behind me. I hadn't sent anything to print, so I turned around.

A single sheet of paper slid out.

I almost ignored it, until I noticed my name printed across the top.

I remembered Rule 4 and immediately grabbed the paper.

I didn't read it. I didn't even look at the rest of the page. I shoved it into the shredder and watched it disappear.

But just before the machine finished, I noticed something.

There was a second name underneath mine.

Daniel's.

I turned around, expecting to see someone standing behind me.

Nobody was there.

I went back to my desk and didn't mention it to anyone.

Things got even worse when I started hearing the sobbing.

It was faint at first, almost like an old speaker struggling to produce sound. I heard it coming from one of the empty meeting rooms.

I stopped outside the door.

It sounded like someone crying. Not quietly, either. It was the kind of crying you'd hear from someone who had completely lost control of themselves.

I was about to open the door when I remembered Rule 6.

I went straight to HR instead.

By this point, I'd had enough. I walked into his office and practically shouted.

"WHAT THE FUCK IS WRONG WITH THIS PLACE?"

He didn't even look surprised.

"I assume your issue concerns incidents relating to the workplace regulations."

How was he so calm?

I started explaining everything. The moving desks, the meetings, the photocopier, the crying. I told him that I was beginning to think someone was deliberately messing with me.

He listened without interrupting.

When I finished, he simply folded his hands on the desk.

"Please continue following the rules. Employees generally become accustomed to these occurrences."

That was it.

No explanation. No reassurance. Nothing.

I asked him why the rules existed in the first place.

For the first time since I'd met him, he hesitated.

"Because they are necessary."

I left.

I started noticing other things after that.

The receptionist never seemed to age. I know that sounds ridiculous, but I swear she looked exactly the same every morning. Same clothes, same hairstyle, same smile.

Sometimes I'd see a janitor's cart parked outside the HR office. It was old-fashioned, with a metal bucket and a mop that looked like it hadn't been replaced in years.

I never saw anyone using it.

Once, while heading towards the kitchen, I noticed a man in a janitor's uniform standing at the far end of the corridor.

He was facing the wall.

I assumed he was cleaning something, so I walked past without paying much attention.

But as I passed him, I heard him whisper something.

"Still waiting."

I didn't stop.

I didn't look back.

By this point, I was genuinely starting to lose it. I couldn't sleep properly, and I was constantly checking my calendar and desk whenever I arrived at work.

I even started opening the second drawer of my desk just to make sure the black envelope was still there.

It always was.

I never opened it.

A few more weeks went by, and I decided enough was enough.

I couldn't keep working somewhere that made me feel like I was going insane. I went to HR, handed in my resignation, and told him I wouldn't be returning.

He accepted it without much fuss.

"Your departure has been noted."

I asked whether I needed to do anything else.

He shook his head.

"That will be all."

I was practically out of the building before I started feeling relieved.

There were other jobs. I could find somewhere else to work. Somewhere normal.

But this whole thing isn't what made me want to post here.

No.

It was the janitor.

I saw him as I was entering the elevator.

He was standing near the reception area, wearing the same dark green uniform I'd seen before. He looked around for a moment before walking towards the HR office.

I don't know why, but something about him made me stop.

Maybe it was the way he walked. His arms hung unusually low, and his footsteps made almost no sound against the tiled floor.

He entered HR without knocking.

Through the glass walls, I could see the man sitting behind his desk.

The janitor stood in front of him.

I couldn't hear what they were saying, but I could see the HR representative shaking his head.

Then the janitor pointed towards him.

I remembered Rule 8.

For a moment, I actually considered going back to my desk and retrieving the envelope.

But I had already resigned. It wasn't my problem anymore.

Then the janitor opened his mouth.

And I don't mean that he simply opened it to speak.

His jaw dropped.

Further than it should have been physically possible. His head tilted backwards, almost touching his shoulder blades, and his mouth stretched open until it looked like his entire face was splitting apart.

His teeth.

Jesus Christ.

They were jagged and uneven, sharp like knives and needles. There were far too many of them.

The HR representative barely had time to stand up.

The janitor lunged forward.

I watched him devour the man.

I couldn't move. I couldn't even scream. I just stood there, staring through the glass as the HR representative disappeared into that thing's mouth.

Then, for just a second, the janitor stopped.

He turned his head.

And looked directly at me.

I don't know how he knew I was there. I was halfway inside the elevator, and the doors were already closing.

But I swear he smiled.

The doors shut.

I haven't gone back to collect my final paycheck. I don't want to look into those black pools of eyes again.

But things have started to become clearer ever since. The office as never dirty. Not one incident between employees was ever reported.

I think I get it now.

I know what the janitor was doing all along.
