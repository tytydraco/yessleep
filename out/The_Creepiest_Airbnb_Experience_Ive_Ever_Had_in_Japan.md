I’ve been to Japan quite a few times, and my love for the country is beyond compare. From anime and ramen to the culture, the history, and even the little things you notice while walking through its streets—there’s just something about Japan that keeps pulling me back.

But as someone who loves Japan, I’m also quite familiar with its darker side.

Japan has some of the scariest urban legends I’ve ever heard of. From *The Ring* and *The Grudge* to stories like Hachishakusama, these are stories I was already familiar with long before this experience.

And I have a few stories of my own.

This is one of them.

It was my fourth time visiting Japan. I was traveling with my boyfriend—who is now my husband—and my daughter. There were three of us, and we were staying around Shinjuku, more in the neighboring area, for seven days.

When I booked our Airbnb, I remember seeing the balcony in the photos.

It had this beautiful wooden backdrop behind it, almost like a little forest. There was an outdoor table and chairs, and the balcony looked huge. The listing said it was on the second floor, so it wasn’t particularly high.

I remember thinking, *This would be such a nice place to take photos.*

When we finally arrived, the apartment was actually quite nice. It was small, but perfect for the three of us.

As soon as you walked in, there were two double beds. Directly in front was the bathroom, and from the beds you could see the kitchen and the sliding glass door leading to the balcony.

It was simple, but comfortable.

We arrived in the afternoon, so we did a quick inspection of the place. And the moment I saw the sliding door, I got excited.

I opened it.

I took a few quick steps outside and looked at the wooden backdrop.

It was exactly like the pictures.

Beautiful.

Maybe I was too focused on the backdrop. Maybe I should have looked around more.

But I didn’t.

I went back inside, and we decided to head out for some shopping and sightseeing. We had just arrived in Japan, after all. We were excited.

The Airbnb was basically just a place to drop our bags and sleep.

We came back late that night, exhausted. We showered, changed, and went straight to bed.

The next day, I was meeting a friend who lived in Japan.

She was actually my classmate from high school. Every time I visited Japan, we made sure to see each other. She would usually spend one night with me, and we'd catch up like we hadn't been apart for years.

That day, we spent time together with my husband and daughter before eventually heading back to the Airbnb.

It was around 8 p.m. when we arrived.

As soon as we got inside, I started giving my friend a little tour of the place.

And, of course, I wanted to show her the balcony.

I slid the door open.

I stepped outside—maybe two feet or so—and excitedly pointed toward the wooden backdrop.

“Look at this!”

But my friend didn't move.

She stayed right at the sliding door.

“I’m not going out.”

I laughed a little because I didn’t understand.

“Why not?”

She looked around.

“Are you sure about this?”

“What?”

“Aren’t you scared?”

My friend isn’t a psychic or anything. But she has had a few strange experiences with ghosts in the past. Even when we were in high school, she was always cautious about things like that.

So, honestly, her reaction didn't completely surprise me.

Still, I was a little irritated.

I thought she was just being overly cautious.

I kept insisting that she come outside.

She refused.

And that was when I finally stopped and actually looked around.

The balcony was at the back of the building.

And directly in front of it was a massive tree.

I mean massive.

The branches were so close that they seemed to almost reach the balcony. The tree was practically at the same level as us, considering we were only on the second floor.

Some of its branches appeared to touch the wooden backdrop I had admired so much when we arrived.

Then I looked farther.

Across from us was what appeared to be an abandoned building.

I don't know for certain whether it was abandoned, but it certainly looked that way.

There were no lights.

Not one.

The building looked old and completely dark.

And then I looked down.

Directly below us was a playground.

A children's playground.

It was completely dark.

No children.

No people walking around.

Nothing.

Just an empty playground sitting there in the darkness.

I turned around and practically ran back inside.

My friend didn't say anything.

Neither did I.

We just closed the sliding door.

I remember thinking:

*We're staying here for seven days.* Don't scare yourself.

We eventually showered and got ready for bed.

My daughter and my husband were sleeping on one of the double beds. My friend and I took the other one, since we still had plenty to catch up on.

It was already around midnight.

We were lying there in our pajamas, talking about everything and nothing.

Eventually, our conversation slowed.

Then stopped.

The room became completely quiet.

And that's when we heard it.

Water.

Not dripping.

Not a faucet.

It sounded like a waterfall.

A loud, rushing sound of water.

My friend and I immediately looked at each other.

We were both lying completely still.

The sound continued.

Like a large amount of water was suddenly running somewhere inside the apartment.

Then we heard a flush.

I said,

"What was that?"

My friend looked at me, obviously scared.

"Was that the toilet?"

We both just listened.

And that's when it really hit us.

Neither of us had gone to the bathroom.

My partner and daughter were asleep.

And we were both still in bed.

Just to clarify, this was a studio unit. It wasn't some huge building where there were rooms all over the place. From the elevator, there was basically one unit per floor, and as far as we knew, we were the only ones staying on our floor.

The bathroom was also directly across from our bed. We weren't far away from it at all.

And the toilet was a Toto toilet, but it didn't have an automatic flush.

So who flushed it?

It only happened once during the entire trip.

We were both scared, but neither of us wanted to make a big deal about it. We just went to sleep.

I didn't want to talk about it because I kept telling myself, *I don't want this to ruin my trip.*

My friend and I would always tell this story to each other in the following years whenever we'd see each other, especially when I'd visit Japan again.

But that night, I didn't want to talk about it.

The next day, my friend had to leave because she could only stay for one night.

So it was back to the three of us.

This was around our third day in Japan.

I eventually told my partner what happened the previous night, but I didn't tell my daughter. My partner didn't really react much.

We're pretty similar in that way.

We both basically thought, *Let's not make this into something and ruin the trip.*

Then came our third night.

I work online, so even when I'm traveling, I sometimes still have to work. I had some projects that I needed to finish before going to bed.

My partner and daughter were already on the beds and were about to fall asleep.

I was overwhelmed with work, so honestly, I wasn't thinking much about what happened the night before.

I was sitting at the table, which was basically in the center of the studio. The sliding door to the balcony was only a few feet away from me.

Again, it was a studio, so there wasn't much space between anything.

All the lights were on.

I was quietly working when I heard a knock.

I stopped.

I looked at my partner and daughter.

They were both fast asleep.

I ignored it and went back to work.

Then I heard the sliding door.

It sounded like someone was trying to open it.

And it was LOUD.

It didn't sound like the door simply moving a little. It sounded like someone was actually trying to get in and the sound is like the sliding door was shaking.

I didn't even think about it.

I immediately went to my partner and told him it sounded like someone was banging on the sliding door trying to get inside.

He was scared too, but he got up and took a peek behind the curtain.

I forgot to mention that the sliding door had huge blackout curtains. We kept them completely closed, especially after what happened the previous night.

He looked behind the curtain toward the balcony.

There was nobody there.

Nothing.

Could it have been the wind?

Maybe.

But honestly, it didn't feel like the wind. The sound was loud enough that it genuinely sounded like someone was trying to open the door.

I didn't continue working that night.

I just went to bed.

The next morning, I decided to finally go back out onto the balcony.

It was daytime, so I wasn't as scared.

And there it was.

The huge tree.

The building that looked abandoned.

And the playground below.

Seeing everything during the day made me realize how creepy the whole setup actually was.

I stood there for a while just absorbing everything and even took a video of the area.

I still have that video to this day.

And I swear, when I watch it, I can see something in the building in the background.

I can't really explain what it is, and I'm not going to say it's definitely a ghost because I honestly don't know.

It could have been something completely normal.

But when I looked at the video afterward, I noticed something in that building that made me go, *What is that?*

By this point, I had basically accepted that the place was creepy.

And if you've ever been to Japan, you probably know what I mean when people talk about how quiet some areas can get at night.

It's true.

This particular area around Shinjuku wasn't nearly as busy as the places tourists usually see. At night, there were moments when you could look outside and barely see anyone.

And honestly, that silence made everything feel even creepier.

After that, whenever I had to work at night, I asked my partner to stay with me.

He would watch videos while I worked, and I would play music while working because I didn't want to hear every little sound around me.

When we went to sleep, I even started playing nature sounds because I didn't want to lie there in complete silence listening for something unusual.

Nothing else happened after that.

We stayed until the seventh day and eventually left.

It was on the last night when my daughter told me she heard the sliding door shaking following a knock. She was creeped out too, especially by the balcony. I told her about what we had experienced after the trip, and that’s when we realized we had both felt something was off about the place.

And that was it.

I still don't know what happened.

The toilet could have had some explanation that we didn't understand.

The noise from the sliding door could have been the wind, even though it really didn't sound like it.

And whatever I think I saw in the building could have been something completely normal.

My friend and I still tell this story whenever we see each other.

**It was probably one of the scariest experiences I’ve ever had in Japan. And I’ve had a few them. I might write about the other one next time.**
