When my editor, Elena, assigned me the story two months ago, I’d be lying if I wasn’t excited. This was going to be my first big story since joining the team at Pulse, one of New York City's largest newspapers.

The first victim, Patrick Drummell, 25, was a contractor for McKinsey who resided in SoHo. According to his colleagues, he was a stand-up guy who was well-liked by everyone in the office.

When I spoke to his colleagues on the phone, they informed me that Patrick often organized monthly happy hours, volunteered at his local soup kitchen during his free time, and donated to several charities whenever he could. Supposedly, he was a walking, neon green flag.

But no one is that perfect, and I later learned Patrick was far from it.

You see, Patrick had a nasty little habit of spying on women. He loved lingering a little too long in front of his window while his female neighbors casually undressed or had sex. And while that was extremely problematic, Patrick’s favorite vice was recording women as they used the bathroom in public places.

On several occasions, he would sneak into the bathroom at his local Starbucks and place a tiny camera slightly above the toilet, where he could get the perfect shot of women coming and going. On his lunch break, he'd return to Starbucks, order a cup of coffee (because Patrick was nothing if not committed to the bit) then retreat to the bathroom to retrieve the camera.

This carried on for three whole years without him ever getting caught. That is, until he wound up dead in his apartment two months ago. You're probably asking how I even know any of this. In good time, reader. In good time :)

On April 2, at 9:15 p.m., police said they received an anonymous phone call informing them of a "putrid" smell coming from Patrick's apartment on Greene Street. When police arrived at the scene, they discovered Patrick's decomposing body tied to a chair, with his eyes gouged out and neatly placed on a plate beside his rotting body. 

Next to the plate was a USB drive, and written on a small notecard beside it, read ***For Ember Brooks***. Sergeant Brooks had been a police officer for over twenty years, solving some of the city's most infamous cases. When she became Sergeant four years ago, it didn’t surprise anyone in her precinct, except for the numerous men who were vying for the same position.

After testing the package for prints and any harmful material, Sergeant Brooks and her team analyzed the USB’s contents and discovered a recorded confession from Patrick outlining all of his extracurricular activities. He also told police where they could find all his recorded footage before the video cut out.

Surely enough, in a safe hidden in the back of his closet, police uncovered hundreds of files containing footage of women being filmed without their knowledge. Turns out, Patrick wasn't such a good guy after all.

After days of collecting evidence, the NYPD was at a standstill. They were baffled by the lack of DNA found at the scene which I found slightly amusing. Honestly, don't they know how easy it is to keep a crime scene clean if you just plan ahead?

I guess not.

Police also questioned all of Patrick's close friends and family, but none of them were able to provide anything helpful. After two weeks of investigating his murder, another man was found dead in his apartment. Well, I guess I'd better get back to work :)
