Lex had always wanted to go to Paris. 

She had had a layover there on a family vacation as a kid, and that was the closest she’d gotten.

I myself wasn’t much for traveling. When it came to honeymoon planning, I would have been more than fine flying to some Caribbean island and roasting on the beach for a week with a drink in hand. 

But she was light years out of my league, and being married and all I figured we had a lifetime to do that, and so it was decided we would be spending a week touring Paris. Bistros, the Louvre, the Eiffel Tower. The works (or la totale, you might say).

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

We had a week of wedded bliss together. It was a dream. 

Every piece of art in the Louvre paled compared to her. 

Her smile at night was brighter than the lights on the Eiffel Tower. 

You can groan at the puns all you want. I just don’t think anything will do justice for how beautiful she looked that week.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

We had no plans at all entering our last day, and looking back I think that’s why it happened. 

We were just going to wander and check out whatever caught our eye, whether it be a used bookstore, an antiques shop, or an off-the-beaten path museum. 

A brief downpour forced us to take shelter in Notre Dame, where we had gone our first day. 

That was when we met Pierre.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

We had been standing there, more just killing time than looking at anything. The subject of possibly booking a catacombs tour had come up, and we were looking into tickets when a slim, impeccably dressed man approached us.

“Hi… I couldn’t help but overhear… do you want to visit the catacombs?”

Lex and I exchanged a glance. He had clearly been eavesdropping. But he seemed polite enough. 

To be honest, there was a weird allure about him that made us feel as though we had approached him instead. 

I can’t explain it, but that’s how it felt.

“….Yes? We were just discussing possibly touring the catacombs shortly here? Once the rain stops.”

He chuckled. 

Clearly a “silly Americans” kind of chuckle, but not really mocking in any way. Like the way you might laugh at a kid when they say something dumb.

“Oh no… you have to book WEEKS in advance.”  
he said, holding his arms out as though to imply that the catacombs were some sort of hot-ticket, must-see attraction. 

“But-“ he said, holding up a finger. “Do you like to dance?”

We again looked at each other. 

Shrugged, nodded. “We wouldn’t be opposed.”

“Perfect. I manage an underground nightclub in the catacombs. Amazing drinks. Better music. Meet me here at 9pm. I promise you won’t regret it.”

Our flight the next day wasn’t until the evening. We might risk cutting things close, but it wasn’t like we planned to get blackout drunk, you know?

“We’re in.” said Lex. 

“Excellent.” said the man. “I am Pierre.” 

He didn’t give a last name.

We shook hands. “Alexis.” “Brooks.” 

It was settled. We would meet Pierre around 9pm that evening, back at the cathedral, and he would show us to this underground catacombs nightclub of his. It was a chance to go off the beaten path and have an experience we would talk about for years to come.  
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I hope to never forget the way she looked that night - the way that black dress hugged her body in all the right places, the way her hair fell down her back, the luscious red of her lips.

I hope to never forget the way she looked.

Because that was the last night I had with her.  
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

We went to a bistro that night for dinner. She had tartare, I had frites. 

We fed each other little forkfuls of our dinner and shared a bottle of wine. 

I remember wondering how things could get any better than that.

It had gotten chilly while we were inside. We held hands as we walked back. Her dress was a thin cotton slip and her leather jacket was more for appearance than function, so I gave her my jacket. Her hand in mine, and the warmth radiating off of her gave me all the warmth I needed.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

We got to the cathedral five minutes before 9 to find Pierre already there, leaning against a pillar with a cigarette. 

He strolled over to us, his eyes gleaming as he looked us over - Lex more so than me. 

Dare I say, I didn’t blame him.

“Madam…you look absolutely splendid!” he said, taking his hand in hers and kissing it as she blushed. “And you, sir! Very sharp!” as he shook my hand.

He escorted us to a nearby SUV - a black Mercedes - opening the doors and gesturing us to get in. 

I noticed the rear windows were blacked out, and a sort of wall separated the front and back of the car, which struck me as odd. Pierre was quick to address when he saw me looking.

“Do not worry sir - you are completely safe. The club is extremely exclusive, and we wish to keep the location as secretive as possible.”

Lex put her hand on my knee, as if to tell me to relax. She tucked a curled lock of hair behind her ear and gave me a soft smile, enough to melt me instantly. 

She was right. We hadn’t had any reason to be suspicious of Pierre or this supposed nightclub as of yet. There was no reason to start now.

Pierre, now in the driver’s seat, opened the partition long enough to hand us 2 shot glasses of what looked like cognac. 

“Louis XIII to start your night the right way. On the house. Please, take these.”

We looked at each other. 

Toasted to our final night in the City of Lights, and drank.

Maybe those shots played a role in what followed. 

They probably did. 

Who knows?

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

The car twisted and weaved through streets we couldn’t see as the liquid warmed our bodies. 

Sitting there in the dark backseat with my brain slowly descending into a fog should have been enough to set off every alarm bell in my body. 

And yet, it wasn’t.  
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

After what I judged to be about 10 minutes of driving, the car stopped and turned off. Pierre came around and opened our doors and let us out. We seemed to be in some sort of garage. 

Not a parking garage - it looked to us like some sort of mechanic shop, but without a car to be seen. The garage door was closed behind us. 

Pierre led us to some steps at the other end of the room, up to what looked like a metal door like you’d find in almost any commercial building.

He opened the door to a staircase that felt as though we were stepping several centuries into the past. 

The smell of dust and wood and a tinge of rot floated up. Everything seemed to be cut into the rock. 

And yet, I could hear no music. 

Lex clearly could not either, and I could tell we had an inkling of the same feeling: had we been set up? 

We apparently didn’t hide our suspicion very well, because Pierre was quick to address it, as he had everything else.

“Down those stairs and around a few turns - not quite there yet!”

He gestured for us to step back, then stepped in front of us, going down a few steps before turning back at us and gesturing behind him, an inviting smile on his face.

“Please, follow me.”

And follow him, we did. Down the steps, and down a tunnel, making a right turn here, a left turn here. 

Soon, we began hearing it. A thumping bass that ran along the walls, the beats getting faster as we got closer. 

Then, spots on the walls intermittently, from what I assume were strobe lights.

Finally, we emerged into a large room. 

A man stood at the front behind a turntable, shrouded in smoke as a song I didn’t recognize rumbled from his speakers. An enormous bar stretched from one side of the back, where we were, to the other. I could hear music and dancing, and it seemed like the room was packed wall-to-wall.

A victorious smile etched across his face, Pierre turned to us and spread his arms, inviting us to take the room in.

“Welcome,” he exclaimed, “TO DEMOR!” 

And, with that, he disappeared immediately into the crowd.

Lex looked at me, smiling in a way that I hadn’t seen since her walk down the aisle just a week prior. 

I think she could see I was still a little hesitant, because she grabbed my arm and gave me a soft kiss.

“C’mon, babe. It’ll be fun!”

Funny how seeing your wife in a sexy black dress is all that it takes to get you in the mood. I let her lead me to the bar.

Aside from us, it was absolutely empty. Just us, and the one bartender, who lumbered toward us as soon as we sat down. His movements were stiff and awkward, like he was strung on a marionette. 

Closer inspection revealed him to be almost unnaturally pale and dressed like some sort of 1700s French nobleman, as though he had come directly from a historical reenactment. 

It almost felt as though we were being served by a walking corpse.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

After getting drinks, we set our stuff down and made our way to the dance floor, where the night became a blur. 

One drink became another, and soon it felt like we were back in my fraternity house on a Saturday night, the rest of the world fallen away.

At some point, the music stopped, and we peeled ourselves off of each other. 

Even without our outer layers, we were both soaked in sweat, and I, having succumbed to the white collar office lifestyle, was very much in need of a breather. 

As if he had been hovering on the edges of the crowd the entire time and could sense that I was taking a break, Pierre came over to us, extending his hand to Lex. 

“May I have this dance, Madam?” he asked. 

She looked at me. I shrugged, as if to tell her to go for it. She blew me a kiss as he led her into the crowd.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I’ve been wondering about some things from last night. 

The drinks. That car ride. The way he showed up at the exact moment. 

It makes me wonder if we ever had a choice.   
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I went to the bar and, soda water in hand, made my way back onto the dance floor right as a new song was beginning. 

There was a lot of fog and strobe lights, which made things incredibly disorienting and hard to see, but I was able to eventually make my way to the center and find them dancing.

I watched him spin her, and it brought me back to our first dance at our wedding. I felt a tinge of jealousy, but did nothing, not wanting to ruin the moment. 

Then he kept spinning her. 

I started to get a little concerned they were going too fast, but from what I could see of her face they seemed to be having fun, so I didn’t do anything.

Then things went off the rails.

He began to spin her faster and faster. 

The strobe lights picked up to the point where I felt like I couldn’t see anything at all. It was as though the room itself was spinning, when I was completely still, and I found myself having to fight back a wave of nausea.

At this point I could see them only intermittently. 

When I did, I began to panic. 

Lex’s hair was now spinning above her head, sweat flying off her face and spinning into the crowd… and her face. 

My god, her face. 

It was blank. 

Completely stone-faced. 

There were times where she flew by so fast her face almost looked smooth, featureless, like a blank slate. And I was too paralyzed to do anything.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I still question the last thing I saw before a thick blanket of fog filled the room, cutting my vision off completely. 

I don’t know if it was the fog, the strobe lights, the alcohol, a combination of the three, or something else entirely.

But I found myself looking down at Pierre’s shoes. And where there should have been those impeccably shined black loafers, I saw goat hooves. 

I saw them for no more than a second or two before the room filled with thick grey smoke, and then I couldn’t see anything at all.

I stood there, wrapped in what felt like a thick grey blanket for what felt like an eternity as the music pulsed around me. 

I could hear music and dancing, and the presence and the mass of people around me, and yet it felt like I was all alone in some sort of purgatory, with no one around.

Then, as though a switch had been flipped, the fog cleared and the music was… gone. 

It was silent enough to hear a pin drop. 

And in the middle of this room, everyone stood completely still. 

And in front of me, where Lex and Pierre should have been, there was nothing but a strong scent of sulfur. 

It was as though they had disappeared.

Then the people in the room began to slowly turn towards me. 

A thousand gazes drilling into my soul. 

And, I realized as ice began to fill every inch of my body, every single person in there was dead. 

They were in different stages of decomposition. Some looked like they had just taken their last breaths. Others were no more than skeletons in Victorian-era clothing. 

There were a thousand dead people in that room. 

And they were all staring at me.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

You might read this and wonder, why didn’t you try and call for Lex? What if she went to the bathroom or something? 

And to that, I say this:

I felt something from each and every one of those corpses in that room.

Hate. Nothing but pure hate. 

It wasn’t a question of what they would do to me if I dared to plunge into the mass of them to look for her. 

They were almost inviting me to do so.

So, you know what I did in that moment? 

I turned around, and ran.   
\_\_\_\_\_\_

I left my coat there, Lex’s purse and coat, and everything else we had brought. 

I heard footsteps echoing behind me, and I didn’t turn around to see what was following me. 

There were enough of them for me to know it wasn’t her. 

I somehow managed to find my way back to the stairs, then back out into the cold Parisian night. 

Leaned over and vomited, then managed to snag a cab. I’m sure I looked a little rough, but I’m sure the driver had seen worse.  
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

Only when I got back to our hotel, threw open the door, and saw her stuff - suitcase half filled, clothes on the bed - did I finally fall to my knees in the center of the room and break down. 

I cried for Lex, wherever she might have been. 

I cried for our marriage, for our hopes and dreams. 

And I cried for myself, because I sure as hell don’t know how I’m going to move along from this.  
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I went to the police the next morning and filed a report. They said they would see what they could do. 

I hailed a cab. Wanted to make sure I hadn’t gone completely insane.   
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

Getting there was a chore in itself, because I had no idea where to go or how to get there. 

Just a vague idea of how long it should take and when and where to turn. 

When we arrived to a crumbling, burnt out ruin of a house that I knew, just knew deep down was the place, with no sign at all of a stairwell down to the underworld, I simply got back in the car and asked to be taken back to the hotel.  
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

That was 4 hours ago. My flight should be boarding now. 

I’m lying in bed, hugging a pillow that smells like her.

Should you find yourself in Paris and discussing a trip to the catacombs, a man named Pierre may approach you and invite you to visit his underground nightclub that night.

Whatever you do, do not go.
