**Paranormal**

**Chapter One: Good Morning Wakefield County**

The day began just like any normal Monday morning in the small town of Wakefield County, Maine, for my family.

My mother, Lois, must've spent most of the morning preparing to go to work. I say this because she kept talking about some huge catering event yesterday while I was still sleeping.

I had to have been fifteen years old then if I'm not mistaken, but I was being woken up by my father, who also rode the train every day to spread the word of Christ as a preacher. His name was Jon Bobby Gale.

"Pfft... uh, Houston, we have a sleeping alien here on the moon. Closing in for a closer inspection."

My father said as he gently pulled my pillow out from underneath me while I was cuddling with it.

I went to react to what just happened.

But as I slowly turned to look around, I noticed my pillow being lunged at my face.

"What the hell... why!?" I groaned before frustratingly grabbing my pillow to swing it at my father.

"Dear Father, my heavenly Savior, I have once again awoken the Vas Alienum, please forgive me my Father. Hahaha!"

He just started calling me that.

But my mother always called me that...

Anyways, she wanted him to begin pastoral services, so he was becoming very fluent in Latin because she finally convinced him to practice parish preaching.

He's such a sod when it comes to her.

It's criminally cringey.

Even when he pushes back, she eventually gets her way.

I'm getting lost in thought, my apologies.

After I swung my pillow at my father's arm a few times, I stormed out of bed as he theatrically reacted to me getting out of bed and fell to the ground as I stood over him.

"What's going on here?"

My father and I both turned and saw my mother standing in the doorway with a half-smile etched on her face.

"Oh nothing, Moth-er. I was just bothering, or trolling, the Vas Alienum again."

He immediately stopped laughing and stood up to explain why he was on the floor.

"Oh gosh, you're such a sod," I stuttered to say because I was overstimulated by my father's cringiness.

Though I sorta liked it when he called my mom what I would call her every time she calls me Vas Alienum.

The "Moth-er."

It just sounds like such a sci-fi name, but it really ticks her off when I say that in rebuttal to her calling me Vas Alienum.

She looked at him with a disdained facial expression before completely ignoring what he just called her.

"Well, sorry to cut you two's role-playing short, but Sam, I made breakfast, and Jon, you're beginning to run late to catch the train."

My father looked at his watch, and before he could even say anything else, the three of us heard a loud knock at the front door.

The fist hitting our front door didn't sound like a very pleasant, friendly, nor neighborly knock.

Instead, it was a frantic, hollow thud.

"Who could possibly be knocking on the door?" my Moth-er asked.

Before my father went downstairs to go check, we heard our neighbor Sharlene's voice through the hard, heavy wood.

Sharlene's voice sounded reedy, stripped of her usual warmth.

"Mr. and Mrs. Gale? Please open up. I'm sorry. I know it's barely six, but..."

A sharp, ragged breath slipped through the crack of the door.

"Uhm... my four-year-old, Demetrius. He woke me up crying around three in the morning. He said he saw you, Mrs. Gale. Standing in our backyard. Just... staring at his bedroom window."

My parents immediately approached the door, with my mother slowly following behind my father.

I stood frozen at the top of the stairs, looking at the backs of my parents.

They stood completely rigid before opening the door.

"Hello, Mr. and Mrs. Gale. I'm sorry for knocking on your door so early. I don't know if you had already heard what I said through the door, but my four-year-old son, Demetrius..."

Before Sharlene could continue, my mom rudely interrupted her and said that they had heard her.

Which made Sharlene's voice tremble, dropping to a breathless whisper.

"...I checked all of my Ring cameras, and lo and behold, there you are. Only I see you were running past the Ring camera... backwards."

A suffocating silence dropped over the foyer.

I couldn't tell how my parents were looking.

I couldn't see their faces.

But a cold prickle spiked down my spine as I watched their posture.

When I saw the demeanor of my parents, I knew something was off and wrong.

They didn't move.

They didn't look at each other.

My mother's shoulders were pinned at an unnatural, stiff angle, her head tilting just a fraction of an inch too far to the left.

My father was clueless to her uncanny posture, but when my mother spoke, her voice wasn't defensive.

It was hollow, flat, and entirely devoid of human cadence.

Which shook both me and my father.

All the while, my mother had angrily asked to see the footage because she couldn't believe what she was hearing.

"You can't see clearly because you were moving past the lens too fast, but when I slowed the footage down, I was able to make out your physical description."

My father gently took Sharlene's phone to look at the footage.

"O-okay, this has to be some sort of joke or something because there's no way that's Lois... but it's you, sweetheart."

My father slowly raised the phone to my mother's face, and once she finally saw the video for herself, she was completely silent but shocked.

I could almost feel it as much as I could see it resonating from her body language.

Everything after that happened so fast.

My mother was constantly complaining to my father about why there was a woman who looked just like her in their neighbor's backyard as I was getting ready for school.

My father thought that it could possibly be a "doppelgänger," he believed.

Well...

He blamed it on the fact that she visited a fake fortune teller at the local carnival last week, and now her "bad fortune" that the fortune teller foresaw may be coming true.

This incited an argument between the both of them, but I just tried to ignore it by plugging my headphones into my ears and listening to Set Me Free by Jay Diamond.

I quickly walked out of the house.

I didn't want to get caught up in my parents' argument, but I couldn't shake the feeling as I was walking out of the house that my mother was staring at me the whole time.

By the time I got to school, everything felt normal again.

Until I was reminded of my strange morning by my principal, Mr. Huang.

"Hey, good morning, Ms. Gale. How are you and your parents? Is your father okay? I didn't see him this morning doing his usual preaching on the train. Hearing him speak those scriptures has become part of my everyday morning routine."

Is every man these days a sod? I thought to myself before I lied and told Mr. Huang that my father had a pastoral meeting this morning.

"Oh really?! That's great to hear! Well, I hope everything goes well for him then."

I smiled and went to walk away, but then Mr. Huang asked me to tell my father:

"Hobo Ken actually came on the train this morning and tried to fill in for your father today. Oh gosh, was it terrible. Ha! Ha!"

I couldn't help but chuckle at the fact that the local celebrity homeless man tried to preach one of my father's scriptures on the train this morning before walking away to catch the remainder of my first-period class.

"You're late, Ms. Gale."

Ms. Wilson gave me a cold, stern look as she extended her hand and waited for me to give her my tardy pass.

"I apologize. I've been having one chaotic morning!"

Ms. Wilson gave me a pass since I'm rarely late to class.

She was my English teacher, and I loved everything she taught us, unlike most of her students.

Once I got to my seat and sat down, my friend Colton immediately turned to bother me.

He threw a piece of his eraser at the side of my head to get me to turn around.

Once I did, all I saw was him comically holding up a ripped piece of paper that said:

**DUDE WHAT HAPPENED TO YOU THIS MORNING¿¡**

I couldn't help but laugh at him and his shenanigans.

I snuck my phone out of my pocket and gestured at it for him to check his phone.

I then texted him:

bruh why my neighbor, Sharlene, knocked on my parent's door this morning talking bout she has a video of my mom in her backyard late at night

Colton was shocked to see my text message and shouted:

"What?!"

Out loud.

Catching the attention of everyone in the classroom, including Ms. Wilson, who told him to calm his spontaneous reaction and put his phone away.

He said he was sorry before texting me:

well was it true? Was your mom in your neighbor's backyard? Late at night?

I didn't know how to respond.

Supposedly, there's a video of my mom in Sharlene's backyard, but my mother claims that it wasn't her.

So I just told him:

no

I didn't know what to say.

I was scared.

I also felt bad for lying to my friend Colton.

So I put my phone away before my eyes started to wander around the classroom while Ms. Wilson talked about some old folktales.

I didn't pay attention to the whole discussion, but what I did pay attention to was that some people believe that "Ole Nick" is a historical, deceptive nickname for the Devil.

But Ms. Wilson was discussing how ancient writers used this specific name to describe an entity entirely different from the Devil, one who never showed his true face, preferring to wear the bodies of others like secondhand clothing.

Hearing Ms. Wilson give a lecture on Ole Nick Hairy made me feel a chilling sensation crawling up my body.

Almost like ants were running across my skin.

Yet I was somehow intrigued by what she was saying at the same time.

"...while many grimoires warn of known entities like the Archfiend Abalam or the Devil, sixteenth-century poetry focused on a far more volatile threat. An entity known in the old country as Ole Nick. Or rather, Ole Nick Hairy.

"A transient, shapeshifting parasite.

"Writers also called him the Father of Lies because he never sought to fight the holy. He only mimicked them.

"He would steal the robes of a priest, or the skin of a respected reverend, hiding behind a wall of deception."

After hearing about Ole Nick, and having the kind of morning that I had, I felt like the embodiment of fear was looming over me.

It just so happened that I turned to look out the window.

It was more of a glance, rather.

But I thought I saw my mother outside of my classroom window.

When I looked again, it was just a woman who looked very eerily similar to my mother walking down the street.

Still, I was stuck in a trance, looking at the woman walk away.

Until I was jolted back to reality by the notification vibration coming from my phone.

It was my actual mother who had texted me.

She was checking in on me and seeing if I was okay.

I felt relieved to see her text.

But by the time I responded to her and looked back out the window, the woman was already fading into the distance.

“Mr. Bigsby! Return to your designated seat and cease your attempts to distract Ms. Gale from her class work!”

Ms. Wilson's stern words to Colton made the entire classroom quiet momentarily.

Before Colton erupted into a glorious, honking laugh that sounded extremely similar to a startled cartoon goose.

He had just been thoroughly busted by Ms. Wilson, caught red-handed switching seats with another student named William Williams.

Y'know... honestly, I have always found it a cosmic joke that the guy's parents looked at the last name Williams and decided, Yes, more of that, please.

I suppose that structural redundancy is exactly why the universe branded him "Peanut."

But before I get lost in thought again, I had turned around in my chair to see Colton brazenly occupying the seat next to me.

"Oh, see, what happened was, Peanut Butter Cookie here informed me that his eyeballs were failing him at this distance," Colton explained, waving his hands around while he spoke.

"He begged to copy my supremely well-written notes. I merely thought a temporary geopolitical seat-swap would benefit the greater educational good, Ms. W! I would never dream of disrupting the honorable Samantha."

Colton spun his web of absolute nonsense as he tried to comically explain to a deeply unamused Ms. Wilson that William couldn't see the lesson properly from his seat.

Meanwhile, I quietly sat on the opposite end of Peanut during Colton's "explanation" and then told Ms. Wilson that everything should be fine now that I was sitting in Colton's original seat and, once again, as far away from him as possible.

"Back me up here, Pistachio!" Colton squawked, betrayed.

William and the others just quietly laughed at Colton and his shenanigans.

He was the class clown, but he was actually way smarter than he probably seemed.

I liked Colton.

He's like this little brother I never had, seeing as I was an only child.

Anyways, I was overcome with guilt for lying to Colton when I told him that my mom wasn't in my neighbor's backyard.

So I decided I'd tell him everything I knew during lunch after I figured out how to explain it.

The bell rang.

**Second period.**

**Geometry.**

Great.

I gathered my notebook and stood up alongside everyone else.

The classroom immediately dissolved into the usual chaos of shuffling feet, scraping chairs, and half-finished conversations.

Colton was still unsuccessfully trying to convince Peanut that he was owed compensation for "educational consulting services."

As I walked toward the door, Ms. Wilson called out to me.

"Ms. Gale."

I stopped.

"Yes?"

She hesitated.

Just for a second.

Then she smiled.

A normal smile.

Or at least I thought it was.

"Tell your mother I said hello."

I blinked.

"Uh... sure."

"Good."

I didn't know why, but something about the way she said it made me uncomfortable.

Maybe it was because of everything that had happened that morning.

Maybe I was just overthinking things.

That certainly wouldn't have been a first.

I stepped into the hallway and immediately got swept into the current of students moving between classes.

For a while everything felt normal again.

Normal noise.

Normal people.

Normal school.

Then my phone vibrated in my pocket.

I pulled it out.

A new text message.

My mother texted me again.

**How's school sweetheart?**

I smiled.

For the first time all day, the knot in my stomach loosened.

I typed back:

\*Better now. Geometry tho :( \*

Almost immediately, the little indicator showed she was typing.

Then the message appeared.

**Don't worry. I'll see you soon.**

That was strange.

My mother was supposed to be catering an event all day.

I started typing a response.

I thought you were at work?

The typing indicator appeared again.

Vanished.

Appeared.

Vanished.

Then finally:

**“I am, but I was able to sneak away for a second to check on you. I picked up dad. He’s coming with me.”**

She always works hard but manages to make time for us.

But I don’t think that I ever got to see them, until later that day, of course.

Does that answer your question about why I claim to have had a strange and terrible morning?

Samantha sat back in her chair and looked at the camera that was recording her.

The room fell silent.

A voice from somewhere beyond the glare of the overhead lights finally spoke.

“Let’s go back to when you were in school. Did you ever see the woman again at school, and did you ever get to speak with Colton again?”

Samantha breathed heavily and sat uncomfortably before speaking again.

**End of Chapter One.**
