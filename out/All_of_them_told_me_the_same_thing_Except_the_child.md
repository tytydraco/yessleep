I found myself at the mall, devoid of all that is human. We strolled over broken glass and scattered pamphlets. The violet twilight that seeped through the shattered rooftop above was far from enough to unveil the looming shadows. The skeleton of the building was silent, and nothing could be heard. Not even our footsteps. Just the low-pitched buzzing that assaulted the back of our minds. It felt vague and familiar, yet wrong. He stopped in his tracks and turned his head, an unfamiliar expression from a familiar face. He had no eyes. He whispered : 





*he’s coming*





I found myself driving through the bare streets. The motor was not working. Nor did my hands. I was a mere spectator of my own paralysis. The wheels rolled cautiously, scraping the gravel beneath, no headlights, no streetlights, as the dusk settled in the city, turning the violet hue that was the sky into deep shades of indigo. The faint gloom painted black the shape of the buildings against the oppressive horizon, sparing just enough light for me to catch a glimpse of her in the passenger seat. She stared straight ahead, unmoving. At least I had thought so. Her eyes were hollow pits. Unhurriedly, she rotated her head, and so did I and the buzzing had returned, an agitating hum that felt more than heard. Though her face did not have eyes, they stared straight into me as her unmoving lips whispered:





*he’s coming*	





I found myself in my living room, dwelling in the blackness, the only source of light a cigar which I had never seen her smoke before. She shook her leg frantically as we sat in an abiding silence. I really, really wished I could turn the lights on. At this point, the buzzing never stopped. It invaded corners of my mind that I myself did not dare touch. Two pale beams tore through the darkness – headlights of the car that pierced inside, even though I did not have a driveway and my living room was on the second floor. I could see her eyes as she placed her hand on mine. I wish I hadn’t. They were grey. Frigid and distant. Hopeless. She whispered : 





*he’s coming*





I found myself on the stairs of his childhood home, the one I hadn’t visited since her father’s funeral. All that I had to do was open the door. As long as the doors were open, I would be fine. Everything would be fine. Everything would be fine, god damn it. A shape waited for me at the bottom of the stairs. A silhouette. A child I could not recognize, whispering at me. The buzzing grew louder. I could not hear what the child was saying and I asked it to speak louder. Whispering became louder, but so was buzzing. I could hear all and nothing. Please, I begged. Please tell me. The child gestured for me to come closer. Involuntarily, I leaned down and the child cupped its palm at my side. The buzzing was no longer in my ear - it was in my head, as if some wraith had put its spectral hand through my skull and was trying to rip the brains out. The child growled : 





***HE'S HERE***
