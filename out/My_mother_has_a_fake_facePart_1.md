I don't even know where to begin so I guess I'll start with my father's death.My mother didn't handle it well and to be honest, neither did I.She cut contact with all of the family, and I tried to call her, but I could tell she wasn't doing well mentally.I didn't know what to do, so I just let it go.I wondered if I could call anyone to stay with her.The first person I thought of was my aunt.She wasn't much of a help, though; my mother told me she was a druggie. She doesn’t have a phone, so I just gave up. I understand. Well, now I do realize that as a son, I was supposed to care for her.I realize that now, but not then.God, I wish someone had told me then.Because the situation devolved into something so terrible.I wish I could turn back time.Following our interaction, my mother never went outside for days; to be honest, neither did I. 

I spent months crying into my girlfriend's arms, venting to her; I regret that now. It took a mental toll on her as well, since she was also dealing with the death of her father. It was all too much, so she said she wanted a break.But in my heart I already decided she wasn't coming back. I don't blame her for leaving; I blame myself. I should have just kept it to my chest.

When I woke up on the morning of the funeral, I expected to get a call from him; usually on Sundays he'd call me, wake me up and tell me a pun.At times it was annoying; other times it was just what I needed to start my day. When he didn't do it today, I almost broke down crying. I imagined him mulling it over the entire week, thinking about the perfect joke for his son. I realized that the calendar said that today was the funeral; I cried into my pillow upon the realization.

I drove to the funeral, and it didn't feel real. My heart thumped out of my chest. If I walked into that building, that would be admitting he was dead. At this time, I was also dealing with the breakup, so I didn't know if I could walk in, be a man and not cry. But it wasnt like men were expected not to cry at funerals; I just wanted to be strong for my little brother. When I entered, I walked past my family. My uncle, grandparents, and close relatives. When I got to my brother, he hugged me tightly and cried into my arms. I cried too; I just couldn't handle it.But noticeably, my mother and aunt were absent from the funeral.My uncle pulled me aside,noticed it as well, and asked if I could go and check on her.I didn't argue about it.I knew the job was going to fall on me either way.I drove up to her house.

As I was driving up, I reminisced on old memories I had with my mom. We had so many jokes that were just for the two of us. I mean, if you weren't in our relationship you wouldn't see them as jokes. She used to joke she'd put me back in her belly when I did something bad, and as a kid I believed it.You know, I just didn't know how she would do it

As I got older, I grew so much resentment for my brother; in my mind, he took that love away from us that I cherished so much. My silent hatred for him grew so much that our relationship just slowly dissolved over the years. I wish I was more appreciative in the time that we had.

When I arrived at the house, I had this weird feeling that I couldn't place. It looked old and decrepit; the paint was drying, peeling off the walls, turning into an ugly colour, and there was trash all over the front lawn. I think I can place that feeling now. It was guilt; I felt bad that I couldn't come sooner. My mother was always strict about keeping the house clean. Seeing that Dad's death had affected her so much that she no longer took care of the house, it broke me. I had to go see her.

As I was walking in the yard, I realized the garbage bin was tossed; the contents spilled on the grass. It smelled so rancid I  almost fainted from the scent. It was like a combination of spoiled meat and rotten eggs. I put my shirt over my nose to cover the scent. I used my other hand to knock on the door.No one answered. I called out my mother's name 

“Ma?”

Mom!" I  called out once again. Are you home?”I noticed a car in the driveway, so someone was home. I heard faint footsteps in the dark. Someone came out. My mother didn't look much better; she was in a dirty nightgown, her hair was messy, overgrown and covering parts of her face, but from what I could see, her face was weird.
