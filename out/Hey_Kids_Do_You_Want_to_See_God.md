I was twelve years old the first time someone asked me if I wanted to see God. I haven't told anyone this story in over twenty-five years. Mostly because I spent those years trying to convince myself it never happened. 

I grew up in a small town in Kansas where nothing ever happened. The biggest excitement most years was the county fair or a thunderstorm knocking out the power. Everybody knew everybody. Doors stayed unlocked. Parents called you home by shouting from the porch.

Except for the house next door. Everyone called it the Y2K house. It had been built by a strange religious group a few years before I was born. They kept mostly to themselves. They filled the town with handwritten flyers warning that the year 2000 would bring the end of everything. Not fire. Not judgment. Revelation. They told anyone who would listen that the new millennium would reveal God. 

Then, sometime around New Year's Day...

They were simply gone. 

No moving trucks.

No farewell.

No bodies.

The house just… stayed. 

It was condemned after that. Too many structural problems. Even as a kid I could tell something was wrong about it. The windows never seemed to line up. The roof looked too large for the rooms underneath it. Every angle felt just slightly off, as if someone had learned how to build a house from memory instead of experience.

Nobody went inside.

At least...

Nobody was supposed to.

One Saturday afternoon I was just fooling around on the family PC while my parents were out shopping. There wasn't much to do back then. The internet barely worked in our town. Most of the time I just clicked through folders, played with Paint or stared at the desktop pretending I was hacking into something important.

I don't remember exactly what I was doing. I remember what interrupted me. Words appeared in the middle of the screen. Not inside a program or in a message window. They simply appeared.

**Hey there!**

I stared at them.

A few seconds later another line appeared beneath it.

**Wanna come over and play? :)**

I looked behind the monitor. Then under the desk. Like maybe somebody had somehow connected another computer. I shouted for my mom before remembering nobody was home.

When I looked back...

The words had changed.

**I am right next door!**

The next day at school I told Jimmy and Tom.

Jimmy laughed so hard milk came out his nose.

"You're such a liar."

"I'm serious."

"No you're not."

"I swear."

He folded his arms.

"Prove it."

"How?"

"We go over there."

Tom immediately shook his head.

"No."

Jimmy grinned.

"See? He's scared."

"I'm not scared."

"Then let's go."

Kids are stupid. Especially twelve-year-old boys. By the time school ended the next afternoon we'd already convinced ourselves it would be funny. The fence around the property had mostly fallen over years earlier. The front door wasn't locked. It barely closed anymore. The air inside smelled old. Not rotten. More like the smell in an old person's house.

Dust covered everything. Dinner plates still sat on the kitchen table. Children's shoes waited neatly beside the stairs. It looked less like people had moved out and more like they had simply stood up in the middle of dinner and never come back.

Tom whispered,

"I don't like this."

Jimmy rolled his eyes.

"Nobody asked."

We searched downstairs first. Nothing but dust and old furniture. Stacks of religious books everywhere. A room filled with outdated computers.

Then we heard something thump upstairs.

Jimmy smiled.

"Told you somebody lives here."

He started climbing. Neither of us wanted to follow. But we didn't want to be left alone downstairs either. The hallway upstairs was even stranger. The ceiling sloped in directions that made no sense. One bedroom door looked far too small. Another almost brushed the ceiling. At the end of the hallway, an open doorway led into a nearly empty room.

Inside sat a teddy bear about the size of a toddler. Cross-legged in the middle of the room. Its head was an old CRT monitor. The screen was black.

Jimmy laughed.

"That's so weird!"

The screen flickered with white static. A face appeared. Simple and pixelated, with a friendly smile. The bear looked directly at us. A bright squeaky voice filled the room.  
As it spoke the words appeared on the screen.

**HEY THERE KIDS!**

It waved.

My brain couldn't decide whether to laugh or run.

Then the next line appeared.

**DO YOU WANT TO SEE GOD?**

Tom answered first.

"No."

The smile didn't change.

The screen typed.

**WHY?**

The creature asked it exactly the way a disappointed child might.

Tom backed away.

Jimmy snorted.

"Because you're creepy."

**WHY?**

Another line appeared beneath the first.

Then another.

**WHY?**

**WHY?**

**WHY?**

The smile never changed.

The voice stayed cheerful.

Jimmy sighed dramatically.

"I'm not a wuss."

He stepped forward.

"I'll do it."

The screen froze. For one long second nothing moved. Then the smile widened. The teddy bear's chest split slowly down the middle with a wet, squelching sound. The fur peeled apart like damp fabric. Something black glistened underneath. The bear's little arms went limp. Its body sagged inward like an emptying shell. Then the thing inside began to slide free. A long black limb unfolded onto the floor. Then another. They were soft, glossy and boneless. Still, the creature kept coming. More of its body squeezed through the opening. Far more than could ever have fitted inside the bear. Six long limbs unfolded one after another. When the last of it slid free, the empty bear skin collapsed beneath its body like a discarded cocoon.  

The CRT never changed. It remained exactly where a head should be. Still smiling and watching us. None of us moved. The creature reached Jimmy in two silent steps. One arm wrapped gently around his waist. It lifted him as easily as picking up a child. Jimmy finally screamed. The creature tilted its head. Static rippled across the screen.

**IF YOU WANT TO SEE...**

Its stomach bulged. The swelling travelled upward through its neck. Its mouth opened. Black liquid spilled onto the floor. Dozens of human eyes spilled out with it. Blue and brown and green.  

They landed in the black ooze with soft, wet smacks. 

Jimmy screamed harder.

The pixelated smile widened.

**YOU NEED MORE EYES.**

It carefully picked through the pile. Like someone choosing beads for a necklace. Finally it selected one. Jimmy tried to pull away. One of the creature's hands rested gently against his face. Another reached forward. There was a wet tearing sound as it dug a hole into Jimmy’s cheek. His scream became something I still hear in my sleep. The creature placed the eye into the opening. Black slime spread around it, holding it in place. It leaned back to inspect its work. Then nodded.

**THIS IS NECESSARY.**

It reached for another eye.

Suddenly somewhere downstairs a door slammed open.

"Police!"

A man shouted. 

"If anyone's in here, come out now!"

The creature didn't react. It continued searching through the pile. The footsteps came closer. Fast and heavy. The officer appeared in the doorway. He took one look inside. Everything about him changed.

His gun came up.

"What the hell–"

He fired.

The bullet struck the creature's chest. It disappeared into the black flesh without leaving a mark. The creature giggled. The officer fired again. And again. Each shot vanished into it. The creature laughed louder. Not mocking him but delighted. Like someone had accidentally tickled it.

Then...

The laughter stopped.

It looked toward the officer.

The smile remained.

**PLEASE DON'T INTERRUPT.**

The officer fired one last time. The creature took one silent step.

Then it moved.

One moment it stood beside Jimmy. The next, the officer vanished backward through the doorway.

He screamed.

Something heavy struck the wall. 

Bone snapped.

Then came a wet, crunching sound. 

I finally remembered how to move. Tom was already running. I followed him. Neither of us looked back. We climbed through the nearest window and ran until our lungs burned. I don't remember reaching home. I remember hammering on the front door so hard my fists bled.

We moved away three weeks later.

My parents never explained why.

Nobody in town ever mentioned Jimmy again.

Or the police officer.

The old house was demolished the following year.

Or so I was told.

For years I convinced myself it had all been trauma.

Childhood memories are strange things.

They change and blur.

And sometimes they even lie.

I almost believed mine had.

Until yesterday.

I was just checking my email when words appeared in the middle of my monitor.

Not inside a browser.

Not inside a program.

Just...

There.

**Hey there!**

A second line appeared underneath.

**Wanna come over and play? :)**

Then, a moment later...

**DO YOU WANT TO SEE GOD?**

It has been waiting for my answer since yesterday.
