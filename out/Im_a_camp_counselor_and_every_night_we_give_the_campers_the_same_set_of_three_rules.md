1) Always go places in pairs.    
2)Never be alone for more than one minute.  
3)Knock before entering any room. 

These are the rules I was given when I first got the job two summers ago, and the rules I observed as a camper myself when I would come here almost every summer as a kid. 

 They are also the same rules I give my campers every night at our end of day bonfire meeting. 

The turnover at this job is high, it takes a “special kind of person,” my boss would say. Which is code for being able to keep your mouth shut, have a strong stomach, and keep campers and their parents happy. 

“As long as you and your campers follow the rules, it’ll be the easiest summer you’ve ever had.”

And in my two years working here I never had an issue. My group the first year has been mostly returnees, kids who had been coming to this camp since they were little. Same with the following year. They already knew about the rules and only needed gentle reminders. 

I had almost begun to forget how serious the rules were until I met my group for this summer. All new kids. 6 girls, 4 boys. Many of them told me this was there first time at any sort of camp.   At our morning introduction I could tell they were going to be a handful, they were a rowdy bunch who needed one to many reminders. In any other summer camp that wouldn’t matter. 

But here, it does. 

During one of the craft sessions that day I could hear my group talking amongst themselves. This summer camp does have a reputation, more so infamous than anything, so I wasn’t surprised to hear them telling the “urban legends” surrounding this camp. 

One of the boys in the group, Max; who I could already tell was going to be a troublemaker, started off. “Haven’t you guys ever wondered why they have such weird rules here? It’s because campers kept going missing, it took them 7 summers to figure out all three rules before campers stopped disappearing.”

Everyone at our table had put down their crafts and had their rapt attention on Max. “And it’s not just like they got lost in the woods or something or drowned. They would go off to use the restroom, walk into their cabin, and poof they’d be gone. The only thing they would find when they would go looking for the kids, was their initials carved into the closest thing to where they disappeared. A door, the floorboards, a stone-“

“Alright alright, that’s enough Max. I’ll go over the rules tonight at the bonfire, let’s not fill people’s head with nonsense.” 

I had to say something. Not that anything he said wasn’t true, but I could see the fear written on some of the kids faces. 

Night had fallen, and as a group we made our way down to the bonfire pits, as did the other camp counselors with their groups. The bonfire pits were set just far enough away from each other where voices didn’t overlap, but close enough to where anyone moving in between groups wouldn’t be considered “alone.”

My group had done a good job throughout the day even before I explained the rules. That’s usually how it goes, the first few days everyone sticks together like sardines. And then the group gradually loosens up throughout the summer. 

“Alright campers, you all know me by now. And you’re about to be introduced to our nightly tradition. Some of you may have heard of our camp, and the rules that come along with it. But that’s not going to stop me from going over them again, and every other night for that matter.” 

“Rule one, which many of you have already been practicing. You MUST go places in pairs, never go anywhere by yourself. Even to the bathroom, bring a buddy with you. Rule 2, if you do find yourself alone for some reason you have exactly one minute to find a buddy. 

Thankfully this should rarely ever happen as we have strategically setup our cabins and communal areas within a safe distance from each other, so it should never take you more than a minute to find somebody.” 

“And the most important rule of the night, which many of you may struggle with at first. Always knock before entering a room. This includes your cabin, the bathroom, the mess hall, any communal space that has a door you MUST knock on before entering.

I will be a heavy presence with you guys for your first official two days of camp specifically to enforce this rule as it’s the one most easily forgotten. But don’t worry, it becomes habit after the first week.” 

As I spoke the last rule I walked around the bonfire and gave everyone a lanyard with a small laminated piece of paper clipped to it with the rules listed on it. Not mandatory or regulated by the camp, but every summer I spent my own money making these to give to my campers. Extra Insurance. 

“Please wear these at all times for the first few days you are here, and feel free to wear it as long as you need after that to help you remember.” 

With that I doused the fire and gathered our group and we started the walk back to our cabin. I heard the hushed whispers behind me, some of the kids were pairing up, deciding who their buddies would be. I found pairs would change multiple times over summer. They would figure out who they got along with and who was most reliable. 

I oversaw the kids unpack and get settled, and then my longest duty of the night, stood by the bathrooms making sure everyone came and went safely. Thankfully as the rules become muscle memory I wouldn’t have to do this as often. 

With the endless stream of kids my kids began to wonder and I thought back to my previous summers here both as a counselor and a camper. My track record was impeccable, no incidents and no lost kids. A lot of parents specifically requested their kids to be in my group. 

I prided myself in keeping my campers safe. I never want what happened to my brother to happen anyone else-

My train of thought was interrupted by the last set of campers exiting the bathrooms. We walked back to our cabin together and I climbed the ladder to the loft area which was my “bedroom.” It has just begun to quiet when I heard a tiny wavering voice break the silence. 

“Are the stories true? Will something actually happen to us if we break the rules?” It was Emily, one of our youngest and smallest campers. It was also her first time away from her parents.

I wish I could’ve reassured her or lied to her, but I had to tell her the truth. 

“It’s true that it did in fact take seven summers for the camp administrators to figure out the rules. And it was seven summers too long. Multiple kids went missing during that time. At first it was chalked up to accidents, getting lost in the woods, drowning. Unfortunate but not unexpected when you’re at a camp this far out in the woods. But then the initials started to appear, and we couldn’t write it off any more.

My brother and I were campers during that time. It was our second summer at camp. And we had gotten pretty cocky. We didn’t really believe any of the rules and disappearances. We thought it was just ghost stories used to scare us.

One night, a group of us snuck out after the counselors had all gone to bed. This was before they stayed in the cabins with you and before we had nightly bonfires. We thought it would be fun to test the one minute rule. 

There were 5 or 6 of us, we would put someone in the middle and all stand around them in a circle. They would close their eyes and we could all scatter in pairs. If they stood there for one minute by themselves they won. Most of the time the person in the middle would give up about 10 secs in run like mad to find one of us. 

It was great fun, and we had played multiple rounds by this point, when my brother volunteered to be the one in the middle. I immediately was uneasy and tried to talk him out of it. 

He brushed me off and boldly stood in the middle. I was so uneasy because I knew he would be the one to stand there for the full minute. And the thought of that made me wonder if something really would happen to him. 

He started counting and everyone else fled, I pretended to flee but stood what I thought was close enough in case he needed to run to me. This time as he was counting something changed. The woods fell quiet. No wind, no birds, silence. 

The moon and stars that once illuminated our game had dimmed, and I had to really squint to see my brother. The closer he got to one minute the heavier the air began to feel, but he showed no sign of wavering. 

31,31,33,34

I tried to shout but no sound came out, or maybe it did, but he couldn’t hear me, but I could still hear him counting. 

35,36,37,38

I heard the slow footsteps of something approaching my brother from behind him, none of us has gone in that direction. I could see him laughing, thinking it was one of us trying to prank him. “Nice try but I’m not moving.” I started to run towards him. 

39,40,41,42,43,44

A shadowy figure slowly started approaching my brother, I started to run even harder my footsteps pounding against the gravel. I looked around and could see some of the other kids were frozen in place, only able to watch what was about to happen. 

I had just gotten with a hairs breath of my brother when I smacked into some invisible force and fell down, the back of my skull cracking against the ground. 

As my brother got to 60, the footsteps stopped, the figure was right behind him. He had a smug smile on his face his eyes still closed and was just about to call some taunting remark when the figure put its hand on my brother’s shoulder gave a slow wave, and they both vanished. And then it felt like something popped and it went dark. 

I woke with all the other kids the next day in our cabin. We all were terrified and when our counselor took roll call and saw he was missing, she simply shook her head and sighed. We all knew there was nothing that could be done.

The other campers never came back another summer. But I did.”

The silence in the cabin was immense and heavy. I could tell I had rattled them. 

“Why did you keep coming back?” Emily said.

“I didn’t want what happened to my brother to happen to anyone else, and I guess I hoped and am still hoping that maybe I’ll find my brother one day.”

“Whateverrrrrr, don’t you guys get it? This is just another lame story they are using to try and care us into following their dumb rules. No kid has vanished from this camp for yeeaaars.” Max’s voice rang throughout the hall cabin, dripping with confidence. 

I didn’t bother correcting him. But looking back, I wish I had. 

The next day came and went uneventfully. Mess hall in the morning, crafts and swimming in the afternoon, lunch, and then a hike before the bonfire meeting. Didn’t need to give many reminders about the rules, the previous night had done the trick. 

Finishing on the bonfire we started heading back to our cabin. I figured it would be a pretty quiet night. The hike seemed to have wiped a lot of them out. 

We were all settled into bed, I turned off the lights and climbed the ladder to the loft. I grabbed my headphones and slipped them on, my body aching as I climbed into bed. I turned up the volume, slowly drowning out the snoring and tossing and turning of my campers below. And before long, I was out. 

I don’t know how long it had been but the next thing I knew I was being shaken away by someone pulling at my arm. 

I pulled my headphones off and jumped out of bed to be met by Emily. Her eyes as wide as saucers, she was visibly shaking. “Everyone’s gone, they went to play that stupid game.”

Still half asleep I asked her what game. 

“The one minute game.”

That woke me up like a bucket of cold ice water. I scrambled down the ladder, and grabbed a headlamp as I pulled Emily along with me. 

“I don’t want to go,” she yelled pulling away from me. 

“You have to, I can’t leave you alone.”

That silenced her and she stopped pulling. We started running and calling the kids names but we were met by silence. The same silence I was encountered on the night my brother went missing.

I bit my lip with worry and started running faster, Emily struggling to keep up beside me. Finally we heard giggling and the shuffling of feet and I saw them. Kids hidden in pairs pointing and laughing, and Max in the middle. Confidently counting away. 

1,2,3,4

I left Emily with the other kids and started running towards Max, shouting his name. I knew I had a chance this time, he just started counting. But I was further away than I had been with my brother.

I started waving my arms as I was running trying to get Max’s attention just in case he couldn’t hear me. 

He opens his eyes and sees me running toward him, he smirks and sticks out his tongue, closing his eyes again and resumes counting. 

32, 33, 34, 35

My eyes scan the area behind him and I haven’t seen the figure yet, but I start hearing the footsteps.

My lungs and legs are burning, but I’m almost there. And then I see it, slowly approaching Max from behind. Almost translucent, its body covered in shadows, face hidden. The only flesh in sight is a pale hand resting by its side. It’s in no hurry. 

I reach Max just as he hits 40 and tackle him to the ground, we skid a few feet in the dirt and I taste blood. 

It takes me a few seconds to get my bearings, and Max it’s actively pushing me off him. 

“What the heck is wrong with you? I was about to beat the record?”

“Max stop come here! You need to stay with me NOW. It’s behind you.”

“Oh please, stop trying to scare me, I’m not a baby like Emily.” He keeps backing away from me, the figure is still there, watching and waiting. 

And as Max continues to back up, the figure begins to move closer to him. I jump up again and start running towards him. 

Max smirked and starts running away from me, making it a game. I reach him at the same time as the figure does, our hands on opposite shoulders. 

He finally sees the figure and his face drops. I’m unable to move, my hand frozen on his shoulder. The world is still for a moment. And then the figure reaches its other hand towards me and touches the center of my forehead. 

I collapse. 

I wake up the next morning, the sun beaming in through the shutters. I’m in one of the campers beds. I sit up slowly with a pounding headache. The kids gathered around me. 

I report what happened to my boss. He’s not surprised. He asks me if I’ll stay on again, and I’m surprised to hear myself say no. 

I pack my bags slowly, I can feel the eyes of the kids on me. Just before I step out the door a small hand slips into mine. It’s Emily’s. 

“I’m going to stay, I’m gonna wait for max.”

I turn to her, my smile barely reaching my eyes, “good luck.”

I walk out, and never look back. Please for your sake and your children’s, don’t try and find this camp. It’s not worth it.
