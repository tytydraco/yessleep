My grandmother worked as a maid for over thirty years back in the eighties at a huge two story Victorian house on Blackwood Lane. Growing up, she always spoke about it with a quiet, polite respect. She would talk about the grand chandelier, the hardwood floors, and the weird cold drafts that never went away no matter how high the heat was turned up.

A few weeks ago, my grandma decided to take me with her to visit the house. She wanted to catch up with the current owner, an elderly woman named Clara who had lived there for decades. I was happy to tag along and maybe grab a few old photos for a family album.

Clara invited us into her dusty parlor and poured tea into faded porcelain cups. The whole room felt suffocating, like the heavy air right before a bad thunderstorm.

Clara set her cup down and said out of nowhere that a woman who owned the place before my grandmother died upstairs in 1942. She told us the woman never really left, and that she comes back every single day at three in the afternoon.

I gave a short, awkward laugh because I assumed she was just telling local ghost stories. I asked if three o'clock was a fixed thing.

Clara did not laugh at all. Neither did my grandma. Clara turned her head toward the grandfather clock ticking in the corner and said it was 2:59.

The temperature in the room dropped instantly. My breath caught in my throat as a sudden weight pressed onto my chest.

Clara panicked and whispered that when she comes, we cannot look at her. She pointed out spots for us to hide and told us to get out of sight immediately.

Before we could even react, a loud bang echoed from upstairs. It was followed by the sound of something heavy dragging across the floorboards right above us.

We scrambled. Clara shoved me into a tiny utility closet near the front door, pulling it almost shut so there was only a small crack to look through. She and my grandma hid behind the heavy curtains in the study across the hall.

Through the crack in the door, I watched the staircase.

At 3:00 on the dot, she came down.

She did not walk normally. She glided with a jerky movement, like a video dropping frames. She wore a stained old dress, her matted hair hanging around a face that was totally wrong. Her jaw was unhinged into a wide open shriek, and her eyes were just black empty holes.

I held my breath and pressed my back against the wall, hoping she would not hear my heart pounding.

She reached the bottom step and stopped dead. Then her head snapped ninety degrees right toward my closet door.

Those empty sockets locked onto me through the tiny gap. I think she saw me. She stood there for ten horrifying seconds. A wet, raspy gasp bubbled out of her throat before she turned and disappeared into the shadows of the dining room.

We did not wait around. I burst out of the closet, grabbed my terrified grandma, and we ran out the front door with Clara right behind us.

I thought leaving that house was the end of it, but I was wrong.

Over the next few days, weird things started happening at my place. My car keys turned up inside the fridge. My wallet ended up on the hood of my car in the middle of the night. Then came the constant feeling of being watched.

By day four, I started seeing them.

It was not just the woman from the house. There were other things. Figures that looked almost human, but stood way too tall, or moved with joints bending backward. They followed me everywhere. I would spot them standing behind light poles in parking lots or hiding behind parked cars, staring at me with wide eyes.

I packed a bag, threw it in my trunk, and drove four hours to a new city. I rented a second floor apartment, thinking the distance and the city noise would keep me safe.

It did not work.

Last night, I woke up to a faint tapping on my bedroom window. Remember, I was on the second floor with no balcony.

I lay in bed completely frozen, staring at the drawn curtains. Behind the glass, I could see the outline of a head pressed flat against the pane. Then a bunch of dry, raspy voices started chanting in unison through the glass.

They kept repeating "we see you" over and over in a weird rhythmic whisper until the sun came up.

I packed up and left the apartment this morning without taking anything else. I just ran.

Now I am sitting on the floor of a cheap motel off the highway. My hands are shaking so bad I can barely type this out on my phone.

They found me again.

I can hear them right outside my room. The door handle is slowly turning back and forth. Heavy, wet thuds are hitting the wood as they push against it, whispering that same phrase through the crack at the bottom of the door.

The lock is about to give out. What do I do?
