I bought Sarah the watch for her birthday. One of those expensive ones with all kinds of sensors. She laughed and said she'd finally find out how loud I snore. I don't snore, but I didn't argue.

That night, I woke up to some sound. At first I thought I imagined it. But then I heard it again, through the wall, through the crack in the window. Someone was calling my name.

I reached for my phone on the nightstand. Sarah's watch was still transmitting data. 62 BPM. Deep sleep. I turned my head: she was right there, next to me. In the dark I could see her shoulder, the edge of the blanket. Steady breathing.

From the garden, another voice came through:

"Mark, please... I'm scared..."

I froze. It was her voice. Not just similar, exactly hers. The same tone she uses when she asks me to close the window in winter. That playful whine. But now, underneath it, there was something else. Real fear.

I looked at the phone again. 61 BPM.

If that was her out there, terrified, her heart should have been racing. I know her pulse. When she's scared, I can feel her heartbeat against my ribs when I hold her. But the line on the screen was steady. Calm. Like a sleeping person.

I got up. The floor was cold. I didn't turn on the lights. Why would I? I walked to the window, pulled the blinds aside.

The moon was bright. Frost lay on the grass. At the edge of the yard, near the treeline, I saw a shape. Sarah. She was wearing my old jacket, the one she always steals for gardening. She was clutching her throat.

"Mark, it's in the house... don't look at it... run!"

That was enough. I stopped thinking. I ran downstairs, grabbed the flashlight from the kitchen counter, and yanked the back door open.

"Sarah?!"

Silence. Not even wind.

I stood there for a second. Then I fumbled for my phone. A new notification: Device disconnected.

And right after, I saw her new message. I read it three times while my hands shook: "Hey honey! Woke up at 5, couldn't sleep. Took Jessie to the lake. Left my watch charging on your nightstand, it was beeping. Be back in half an hour. Love you! ❤️"

I slowly looked up at our bedroom window on the second floor.

The watch was on the nightstand. It couldn't be tracking her pulse. She wasn't there. It had been reading my breathing while I lay next to whatever was under the blanket.

And there was something under the blanket.

Only now I remembered. The outline of a rising and falling chest. Rhythmic. Like someone pretending to sleep.

The back door clicked shut behind me.

I spun around. Through the glass, I saw Sarah. Her jacket. Her hair. Her familiar shape. But she was smiling, and it wasn't her smile. Too wide. Too slow.

She tapped her knuckles against the glass.

"Mark... please... I'm scared..."

But it wasn't her voice anymore. The sound was hollow, unnatural, like something learning to speak.

I stepped back.

She pressed her palm to the glass. Her fingers stretched, too long, bending at the wrong angle, and left white streaks across the window.

"Mark..." now the thing behind the glass spoke in my own voice. "Turn around. It's right behind you."

And in that same second, the living room window behind me shattered.

I didn't hear glass. I heard it stop being a window. The sound was wrong, not like glass at all. Like tearing skin, with a crack and a gasp at the same time. She didn't want to get out. She wanted me to see that she could. And I saw. My legs stopped working. Not from fear, from my brain refusing to process what was crawling out through the frame. It was wrong. Not a monster. Not a person. Something that knew how to look like a person, but forgot how to be one.

I bolted.

Not toward the house, away. Barefoot across the wet grass, over stones, not feeling the cuts. Behind me, I heard scraping. Fast. Like a large dog, but heavier.

I made it to the garage wall. My hand hit the workbench, grabbed the camping axe we used for firewood last fall. I turned and swung blindly into the dark.

Crunch.

A soft impact. Bone inside. The thing made a sound that rang in my ears, high, sharp, wrong. It staggered, and I had maybe two seconds before it recovered.

I looked around. Too far to the house. Open yard was certain death. But the garage had a small window, high up, near the roof. Big enough to squeeze through, if I could reach it.

I grabbed the drainpipe and pulled myself up. The old brackets groaned but held. I climbed to the narrow ledge under the window, kicked the glass out, and tumbled inside onto the dark loft above the garage.

Below, a heavy thud hit the wall. I heard it try to climb, scrabbling, slipping. The pipe was too smooth to hold.

I sat in the dark, choking on dust, gripping the axe. My feet were bloody. My hands were shaking.

Through a gap in the wall, I could see the yard. The creature was circling the garage slowly, head tilted, listening.

Then from the front of the house came a whistle.

Short. Two fingers in the mouth, the way Sarah always calls Jessie. I knew that whistle.

The thing froze. Turned toward the fence. And moved, not toward me, but toward the gate.

I realised what was happening. The real Sarah was coming back. She had no idea. She was walking up the path.

I had to get down. Reach her first.

I kicked out the remaining glass and dropped, hard landing, rolled, got up. Ran along the fence, barefoot, not caring anymore.

Through the wooden slats, I saw a figure, blue jacket. It was Sarah. But she was alone.

As she stepped under the streetlight, I noticed her hand. She was holding Jessie's leash. But the clip at the end of it was empty, dragging heavily along the wet pavement, scraping against the stones. There was no dog. Just the red nylon leash, completely unclipped, swaying in her hand.

Fake Sarah stopped under the streetlight. Turned. Her head tilted impossibly far to the side. And from that dark silhouette, no sound came.

From the porch, from the shadows, came an identical whistle. A perfect copy.

The one on the porch curled up, hugging her knees, rocking slightly, the way Sarah does when she watches TV. She smiled at me, her face was exactly Sarah's, flawless and alive in the dim light. She tilted her head and spoke in Sarah's voice:

"We've been waiting for you."

I stood in the middle of the yard, axe in hand. There were two of them. One on the porch. One at the gate.

I looked at the one at the gate holding the empty leash. Then at the one on the porch, swaying gently, watching me.

I gripped the axe tighter.

Because I finally understood what I hadn't realised before.

They never attacked me. Never grabbed me. Never bit me. I ran from them, but they only scared me, lured me, herded me like cattle.

They needed me whole.

And now they were both waiting.

Waiting to see which way I'd move.

I fumbled for my phone and tapped the screen, triggering the "Find My Device" alarm on Sarah's watch, upstairs.

A piercing, digital siren exploded from our bedroom window.

It worked. The thing at the gate jerked its head up with a sickening bone crack. The one on the porch froze, her wide smile dropping into blank confusion. They turned their backs to me, staring up at the window.

Do I make a blind run for the open gate, or do I use this axe and fight?

I have to choose. Now. Before they turn back.
