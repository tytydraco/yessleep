I'm a 28-year-old woman and I travel alone pretty often. Hiking is one of my favorite things to do, and I've spent plenty of nights sleeping in a tent. I'm used to being alone in the mountains, although I wouldn't say I've ever completely gotten over the fear of it. This happened during a hiking trip in the Austrian Alps last summer, and it's probably the scariest thing I've ever experienced while traveling.

I'd been walking most of the day and was exhausted by the time I found a quiet spot to set up my tent. I hadn't seen another hiker for at least a couple of hours. I checked the weather forecast, put up my tent, changed into dry clothes and made myself dinner on my little camping stove. Nothing special, just instant noodles and some tea. After eating, I packed away my food, checked my phone one last time and made sure everything was ready for the morning. I took a few pictures of the mountains before it got completely dark. It was actually a really beautiful evening. The sky was clear, the air was cold, and I could hear the wind moving through the grass.

But I remember feeling uncomfortable for some reason. It wasn't anything specific. I just had this weird feeling that someone was watching me. I kept looking over my shoulder while I was getting ready for bed. To be fair, I get that feeling quite often when I'm alone. I have some background anxiety that never really goes away, and being in the middle of nowhere doesn't exactly help. So I tried not to think too much about it. I had pepper spray in my tent, which I always carry just in case. I put it next to my sleeping bag, zipped everything up and went to sleep.

I have no idea what time it was when I woke up. At first, I thought I'd heard something in my dream. Then I heard it again. Someone was calling my name. Not shouting. Not screaming. Just saying my name in a normal voice, like someone standing a few feet away was trying to get my attention. I was completely awake by that point. I need to make something very clear. I was hiking ALONE. I hadn't told anyone exactly where I was planning to camp that night. There was absolutely no reason for anyone to be standing outside my tent, and there was definitely no reason for them to know my name.

I can't even describe how terrified I was. I didn't move. I didn't reach for my phone or my pepper spray. I just lay there staring at the tent fabric, trying to understand what I'd heard. Then I heard footsteps. They sounded close. I could hear the grass moving under someone's feet, followed by a few seconds of silence, then more footsteps. I held my breath. All I could think about was the zipper. I kept imagining a hand reaching down, grabbing it and slowly opening my tent. I was waiting for it to happen any second.

But it never did. The footsteps continued for a little while. Sometimes they sounded like they were moving away, then I'd hear something close to the tent again. I never heard my name a third time. Eventually everything went quiet. I stayed in exactly the same position for what felt like hours. Every little noise made my heart race. Even when the wind picked up, I was convinced it was someone moving outside. I didn't sleep again that night.

As soon as it was light enough to see, I carefully unzipped the tent and looked outside. Nobody was there. I walked around the area, looking for footprints or anything that might explain what happened, but the ground was mostly grass and rocks. I couldn't find anything unusual. I packed up as quickly as I could and left.

A few days later, I mentioned the experience while talking to someone familiar with the local hiking routes. I didn't tell the whole story at first. I just said I'd camped in that area and had a strange experience during the night. He asked me exactly where I'd stayed. When I described the location, he told me that some local hikers deliberately avoid spending the night around there. Apparently, other people had reported strange experiences in that area, mostly hearing voices or footsteps when nobody was supposed to be nearby. I asked if anyone had ever reported hearing their own name. He didn't give me a clear answer. He just said he wouldn't recommend camping there alone.

I know there could be a perfectly normal explanation. Maybe another hiker was nearby. Maybe I was half asleep and imagined the voice. Maybe the footsteps were an animal. But I know what I heard. Someone said my name, clearly, twice. And the thing that still bothers me the most isn't even the voice itself. It's how close it sounded. Whoever or whatever it was, I genuinely believe it was standing right outside my tent.

I still go hiking alone, but I don't spend the night in isolated spots anymore. If I'm camping, I make sure there are other hikers nearby. I don't think I'll ever feel comfortable sleeping completely alone in the mountains again.
