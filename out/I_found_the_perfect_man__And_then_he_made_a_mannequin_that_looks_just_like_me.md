I met the man I’m seeing when he hit me with his car.

To be fair, it was my fault. It was late, already dark, and I’d just shot across the street without looking. This week marked my transfer to a new college, one that’s supposed to have the best bio-engineering program in the country. As soon as they accepted me, I moved to a city I’d never seen before. A city I wasn’t sure I liked.

The strap on my backpack had just broken earlier that day, and I couldn’t tell you where the nearest Walmart was. So I went to a corner store. The red fluorescent sign flickered over the shade-drawn doors, and headless mannequins in T-shirts and leggings posed outside. Inside, a single lightbulb hummed, and it was so dark that the person behind the counter was just a pair of watery eyes. I found a nylon backpack at a ridiculous markup and paid for it while the figure behind the counter just breathed. It was a plasticky rattling sound, like dice being shaken together in a cup.

And then his hand caught mine, heavy and fleshy, but it was also strangely segmented. Deep lines at the knuckles and base of the fingers, like where plastic sheets connected. His voice, hardly a whisper. “Welcome home. You’ll like it here.”

I grabbed my new backpack and tore across the street. Lights blinded me. A horn blared. And then I was on the ground, a tire track on my new backpack. I heaved, my eyes shining with little white stars. The facts trickled in slowly: I’d been hit by a car. A slow-moving one, probably crawling to a stop at a red light. And a man was hauling me to my feet. His strong arms pulled me to the sidewalk, and I blinked dumbly at him.

*He’s not real.* Something, some ancient, primal piece of me, recoiled from him. Something about his face. His brown hair, perfectly tussled. Blue eyes, peering deep into mine. Smooth, poreless skin. He should’ve been a male model. He *shouldn’t* have been holding me.

“I’m so sorry.” He had a perfect voice. Rumbly and deep. I shook my aching head. I had to be in some kind of shock. “Are you alright?”

My eyes trailed to his suit. Tailored. His sleeve cuffs were blackened with dust. “Yeah, I’m…I’m fine.”

“Were you running away from something? You looked scared.”

I lifted my head, and I saw the headless mannequins facing me from across the street. My fingers sank into his wrists. I was slurring. “Please…get me out of here.”

He quirked an eyebrow. “Are you asking me out?”

That’s not what I meant, and I could tell he knew that. But I couldn’t push the thought of that hand out of my mind. I didn’t want to be alone. “Yeah?”

“That can be arranged.”

If he hadn’t hit me with his car, I’d have never met him. Broke college students and government officials don’t usually run in the same circles. He introduced himself as Lucas. He took me out for oysters on the half-shell and white wine at some rooftop hotel. He chatted about the news, asked me about myself, and cracked jokes I found myself laughing easily along to. But every time I closed my eyes, I saw the hand. Every muscle in my body ached, and even though it was forward of him to invite me to his penthouse apartment “for coffee,” I agreed. Far better than being alone in my own apartment, which I was pretty sure had a roach infestation from the previous tenant. Far better than thinking about those headless mannequins and that segmented hand.

When we got to his apartment, a woman was there, perched at his bar with a laptop open. He introduced her as his PA, Penny. I stepped back, taking in the open windows and the tasteful furnishings. I didn’t want to look at Penny too long. Something about her was *wrong*.

She was too perfect. She had shiny blonde hair put up in a tasteful French knot and poreless skin that shone like glass. Her clothes looked like they came from a catalog, her cream shirt peeking out from an open pinstripe jacket. Little gold rings with green stones glittered on each knuckle. I glanced down at my watch. It was past 1 am.

Her eyes flickered from Lucas to me, and then back to him. They widened for a second, and then she cleared her throat. “Sorry, just burning the midnight oil. I’m…” She stopped. Frowned. “I’m…”

“Penny,” he said.

“Right. Penny. Well, I should be going.” But she was staring straight at me, as if she were trying to tell me something, but her eyes were too flat to read.

After she left, me and Lucas ended up watching a movie, and I fell asleep on his couch. Something Hitchcock. When I woke up, he was already gone, but he left me with his phone number written on the back of his business card. It was clutched tightly in my fist.

I went to the bathroom to fix my hair and figure out my next moves. Last night was stupid, and my body ached, but overall I was pleased with myself. I’d never asked a man out before, and Lucas, by all means, was a catch. But when I closed my eyes, I saw the headless mannequins. I couldn’t shake the image.

I ran his comb through my hair and stopped. A chunk was missing. Almost a perfect square, right above my ear. And my sweater. I’d been wearing a white sweater over my T-shirt. I scrambled around his penthouse, lifting the giant cushions from his oversized couch. No sweater.

I decided it didn’t matter. I had a class at twelve that I had to get to. It was a cool, fall day. I took an Uber from his apartment to campus, and I saw Penny sitting on the stairs outside the library. Her laptop was balanced on her knees.

She waved at me and I sat beside her. For a moment, she didn’t say anything. Her breath was soft and strange, rattling like the man’s in the store. “So…bio-engineering, huh?”

I didn’t remember telling her that. “Yeah.”

She looked over my head. “I have something to tell you. Something important. But I don’t remember.”

A wave of cold washed over me. I wanted to get as far away from her as I could.

“I, uh, I should get to class. But thanks for visiting.”

She drew in a long breath. That plasticky, rattling sound. “I should be here,” she whispered. “This was supposed to be my life. I think I…I think I *was* going here before I met him. But it’s all cloudy now.”

Her eyes snapped back to mine. Her shoulders jolted with a pop. She reached over and grabbed my hands. They were freezing cold. “Don’t come back to the apartment, okay? Don’t. Don’t…I’m sorry, what was I saying?” She laughed nervously.

I tried to pull my hands away, but her grip tightened. “I really got to get to class.”

“I miss my old body,” she whispered. And then I finally wrenched my hands away as hard as I could. A sickening ‘crunch’ sounded, and something cool and cylindrical landed in my palm. Penny startled. She lurched to her feet and ran, causing the other students milling about to turn and stare at me.

I opened my hand.

I was holding a smooth, rounded, crayon-sized stick of plastic with a ball joint sticking out at the end. There was a gold ring with a green gem around it. I turned the stick over and saw a fingernail etched on the tip. I dropped it and watched it roll down the stairs.

There had to be a normal explanation as to why this strange woman had given me a plastic finger with one of her rings around it. But if there was one, I couldn’t think of it. I shot to my feet and raced after her. She wasn’t very fast; she was wearing pumps that clacked loudly on the sidewalk. She hunched forward, cradling her hand against her chest.

“Penny?”

She wheeled around, shaking. She threw up both hands, as if to shield her face. “Leave me alone!”

Her left ring finger was missing. I’d seen her typing last night; I remembered the rings. The sky spun toward me, and I thought I’d be flattened against the sidewalk. So much pressure on my chest.

I had to go back to Lucas’s apartment. I had to understand what was happening there. I should’ve tried to break in, but he had a *penthouse* and a *doorman.* So I texted him. Stupid, I know.

*I think I left something in your apartment. Can I come back?*

He texted back almost instantly.

*Sure! I’ll have Penny pick you up :)*

My heart dropped into my feet. I was about to text him that that was a terrible idea. Penny was already across the street, shuffling along with the others on the sidewalk. And then she stopped, stiff. Her head jerked up. She waved her four-fingered hand at me and shot me a smile, all teeth.

I froze, my heart beating faster and faster, the image of those headless mannequins so clear even with my eyes open. Penny shot across the street, so fast that she had to be running, but she made short, little strides.

“You’re Nina, right?”

I took a step back. “We were just talking.”

She tilted her head. “Oh, I guess we were. Well. Lucas asked me to pick you up.” Her brows furrowed deeply. “I have a silly, silly bad feeling. Do you ever get that? A…premonition?”

I swallowed. “Are you…okay to drive?”

“Oh, sure! Come with me!”

And I did. My joints felt stiff. They creaked and clicked as I followed her to a parking garage. Once I got my sweater back, I’d never interact with Lucas or Penny again.

In the car, Penny squeezed the steering wheel so tightly I thought I saw the white bone of her knuckles poke through her skin.

When we got to his penthouse, Lucas was sitting at the bar. He looked even handsomer now. His eyes were brighter, his muscles straining against an open-collar shirt. He grinned.

“Are you looking for your sweater? It’s in the master bedroom.”

It shouldn’t have been. I’d spent the night on the couch. Penny sat on a barstool, opened her laptop, and started tapping furiously. Every hair on my body stood on end. I looked up at Lucas and felt that panic ratcheting through me as I searched his face uselessly for a flaw. Nothing.

I looked over at Penny, but she didn’t look up. Instead, Lucas pointed toward his closed bedroom door. “Right in there.”

“Is…everything okay?”

“Of course, Nina.”

I crossed the soft carpet and cracked the door open. I heard a slow, plasticky rattle.

“You’re so silly, Nina,” Lucas called from behind me. “You dropped your sweater, so I left it on the bed.”

I closed my eyes, saw the headless mannequins flickering behind my eyelids, and then forced the door open. The air in his bedroom smelled of ‘ocean-blue’ candles, and he had all black lacquer furniture and a low, king-sized bed.

A woman lay in his bed.

Half-tucked under his sheets, unmoving. I stepped toward her, knowing I should run but unable to. When I got close enough, I saw that her eyes were painted shut, two half-moons, and her skin was plastic. She was wearing my sweater, the chunk of my stolen hair wrapped around her mitten-like fingers.

There were footsteps behind me. “Welcome home, Nina,” Lucas said, his voice warm. “It’s not a bad life. You’ll like it.”

Penny poked her head around the door. Her nose was sliding off her face, a triangle of smooth plastic that landed with a thud on the floor. “I tried to warn you.” She frowned as I stumbled backward. I was sobbing, maybe screaming, but my ears felt like they were stuffed with cotton. “Or at least, I think I did.”

The sheets shifted. The rattling sound came louder, and I shrank back into the corner.

I watched as the mannequin in my sweater opened its eyes.
