First, a little background: my name is Endy, and I’m 14. I was a premature baby, born to my parents when they were just 16—they’re only 30 now.

​Lately, they’ve started acting strange, wild, and completely abnormal. For starters, they’ve stopped using any appliances. To give you the full picture: we live in a two-story house deep in the middle of a forest, and our only water source is a well about 40 meters away.

​My parents have started obsessively staring at that well every single night. On top of that, their voices have become gravelly and rough. Every night, without fail, between 1 a.m. and 4 a.m., they head out to the well. They don't come back until dawn, and when they do, they are unnervingly cheerful and full of energy—EVEN THOUGH THEY HAVEN’T SLEPT A WINK.

It’s hard to even type this out. My hands are shaking, and my nerves are completely fried—everything feels like it’s about to snap. I’ll try to describe exactly what happened last night, even though just thinking about it makes me want to scream.

​My parents… they went to the farm nearby. They dragged back one of the sheep—a smaller one. They didn't even try to hide it; they just dragged it straight to the well. And then, they just… they killed it. Right there. I was watching through my old toy binoculars, and I saw everything. The worst part wasn't just the killing—it was what came after. They stood over the edge and just threw the carcass down into the well. The dull thud as it hit the water down there… it’s a sound I’ll never forget.

​I dropped the binoculars the second it happened. I couldn't hold on anymore.

​If I hadn't seen that—if I hadn't witnessed what they were doing at that well—I might have still been able to drink the water. But now? Just the thought of it makes my skin crawl. It’s like I’ve developed this intense, visceral phobia of water. I feel sick even looking at it. But I’m parched, and I’m desperate. I think I’m going to have to force myself to push past the disgust and try to find some water from the creek deeper in the woods instead. I can't touch what’s in that well.

UPDATE (4 DAYS LATER):

Sorry if this sounds unhinged—my hormones are all over the place, and honestly, this WHOLE FUCKING SITUATION is driving me to the brink of a breakdown. My nerves are completely shot.

UPDATE (2 AM):

​I had to know. I just had to. I waited until they were busy near the shed, pacing back and forth like they always do at night. I grabbed a rock and hurled it as hard as I could into the woods on the opposite side of the yard. It made a loud crash—loud enough to draw them away.

​I ran. I didn’t even breathe, just sprinted to the well. I clicked on my flashlight and leaned over, my heart hammering against my ribs like a trapped bird. I aimed the beam straight down, expecting to see something, anything.

​Nothing. It was completely empty. A black, hollow void. It felt like a fucking glitch in reality—just dry, cold stone. It made zero sense, and I could feel my mind snapping right there. I didn’t have time to process it, so I grabbed the bucket, lowered it down, and hauled it back up, sobbing under my breath.

​When the bucket cleared the edge, I almost threw up. There was water in it—dark, murky water—and floating right on top, staring back at me, was the eye. The sheep’s eye. It was fresh. It was so fucking fresh I could still see the wetness on it.

​I panicked. I slammed the bucket back down into the well, and as it hit the bottom, I peered over the edge again. Everything was gone. The water, the eye, the evidence—it just vanished. It was empty again. Just… gone.

​I’m back in the forest now, shaking so hard I can barely type this. My hands won’t stop, and I feel like I’m going to lose my shit any second now. That place isn’t a well. It’s not even a place. I don’t know what I’m looking at anymore. I just want to wake up.

UPDATE: I’m losing my mind. For real.

​I went back to the well. I don’t know why—maybe I’m already infected, maybe I’m just out of my mind. I had to see it again.

​This time, it wasn't just a sheep eye. When I lowered the bucket, it came up full of dead chickens and roosters. Piled on top of each other, twisted, broken, smelling like absolute death. For a split second—just a millisecond—the veil slipped. I didn’t see stone. I saw a mass of writhing, rotting meat. Then, like a glitch in the damn matrix, it vanished. Back to empty, hollow stone. I nearly puked my guts out.

I spent the day trying to force myself to sleep—my heart was pounding so hard I thought I’d have a stroke—but I needed to save my strength. I waited until nightfall to head out. Walking through the woods at night is a nightmare, but I figured if I hiked the whole night, I’d reach his place by sunrise.

​It was hell. The shadows kept moving, and the silence… god, it was heavy. But I made it.

He looked into the camera, his face pale as hell, and he whispered, "You're next." Then his face… it shifted. He started sobbing, talking about how he couldn't stop going to the well, how something in the dark kept calling his name, pulling him closer every single night until he finally just jumped. Huh. That’s what he meant by "next."

​I’m back in the woods now, shaking so hard I can barely type. My hands won’t stop. I can’t stop crying, I can’t stop screaming inside. It’s calling me. The well. It’s not just a pit; it’s a parasite. And I think it’s finally hungry for me.

I just grabbed the kitchen knife and walked right up to him. I didn't hesitate. I drove it into him right in front of my mom. The blood… it felt like heat. Like life.
