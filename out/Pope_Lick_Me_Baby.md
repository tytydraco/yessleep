Everybody knows the legends about what lives out at Pope Lick Trestle, even if they've never heard the name. A goatman-troll-thing living on/around/under a bridge. Gotta be one of the oldest stories ever told.

Peter had dragged me along on plenty of investigations, but most of them hadn't felt this… silly. A few weeks ago we were trying to record EVPs in an abandoned convalescent home. Driving around looking for... Mr. Tumnus was far less exciting by comparison. 

“How much farther?” Sarah asked. 

She wasn't any more enthusiastic than I was. We had been in the car for nine hours, and were hoping to make it back late that night. 

“Just five more minutes and we'll be there, guys. I'm sorry it's taken so long.” 

“Whatever man,” I said, “you're driving on the way back so I can sleep.” 

Peter looked at me and smiled. 

“Turn left here.” He said. 

I did as he said and steered the car down a long, gravel road. 

“Why do you believe in this stupid goat guy anyway?” Sarah asked, staring hard at the back of Peter's head. 

She’d liked him since the eighth grade. It was obvious to everybody, except Peter. I brought it up to him once and he said he thinks she's just ‘chill like that.’ 

“Because of all the injuries and deaths associated with this place! There are like two dozen or something.” 

“Oh great, so we'll be tired and disemboweled.” 

Peter laughed. 

“Nobody's been disemboweled here, silly. A lot of people have been struck by trains, and a few have ended up jumping off the side. Don't worry though, we're not even going up on the tracks. All we have to do is drive the car and idle underneath the bridge.” 

“Wait,” I chimed in, “you're telling me we're not even getting out of the car?” 

“That's right, cupcake. You won't have to tire out those big muscles of yours.” 

He reached over and poked my bicep, pretending to fawn over my arms. He was chuckling to himself and making stupid little moaning sounds. 

“Bro,” I said, “I swear to God there's something wrong with y– oh fuck!” 

I slammed on the brakes. The car skidded, fishtailing slightly as the wheels struggled to hold traction. There was an animal ahead in the road, small and white. It bounced off the bumper and went rolling toward the treeline. We came to a stop with the car sitting perpendicular to the road. 

“Fucking hell. Is everybody okay?” I asked. 

“Fine,” said Peter. 

“Yeah, I'm okay,” said Sarah, “what did we hit?” 

“I'm pretty sure it was a goat,” I said.

“Oh fuck you, don't say that,” said Sarah. 

“What? It was just a go–” it dawned on me why that would freak her out, “Oh c'mon. It was a goat. Not a goatman.” 

“Whatever, let's just go.” 

I silently agreed, putting the car in reverse to turn back. It lurched a couple inches, then suddenly stopped. I pressed the pedal harder. Then the engine, the lights, all of it just cut off. 

We were sitting in the dark of an Appalachian forest. I tried to restart the car, but it wouldn't budge. It wouldn't even try to turn over. Nobody liked what I said next. 

“Fuck. Guys, we might have to walk back.” 

“Uhm, no. I'm not doing that shit. Call a tow truck or something.” 

Sarah sounded pissed off, but I could tell she was actually just scared. I looked at my phone, no service. 

“Shit,” Peter had been looking at his own phone, “I'll walk back and call for help. You guys just wait he–” 

There was a massive thump on the hood of the car, rocking the whole thing on its suspension. Peter went silent. 

“*whatwasthat*” Sarah whispered. 

“*imnotsure*” Peter replied. 

He turned on his phone's flashlight and shined it through the windshield. I did the same. The light barely breached the glass, and it took a minute for our eyes to adjust. Peter gasped before I fully saw it. 

“What? It's just a goa-” what I'd mistaken for four legs came into focus. Elbows on the fronts, and knees at the back. Its limbs were folded in half, standing on the joints. 

I knew the car wouldn't start, but I was twisting the key in the ignition anyway. It brought its pale face closer to the windshield, lighting up more of its features in the glow of our flashlights.

Its eyes looked delirious, like a person exhausted beyond exhaustion, and it had the dark circles around them to match. Its mouth hung open in a grin that was cartoonish, far too oval-shaped to be natural. It stared at me for a moment, then snapped its gaze to Peter. It moved even closer to the windshield, pressing its snout and obscenely long tongue against the glass. 

Suddenly, Peter spoke up. 

“Oh, shit, hey man. It's been a while.”

“What?” Said Sarah. 

“Yeah, I haven't seen you since high school.” 

“I…” I was hesitant to say it, “I think he's talking to the goat.” 

“On the train tracks you say? Yeah, I'd love to see it. Let's go!” 

Peter quickly unlocked his door and slid out of the car. The goatman unfolded itself and stood up. It had to be eight feet tall. They started to run away together, and Sarah took off after them, screaming for Peter to come back. 

She made it about a dozen paces before the goatman turned and charged her. His curved horns slammed into her midsection, then he lifted her up and tossed her aside with a twitch of his neck. She soared through the air. The center of her spine hit a pine tree's trunk, and I heard the sound of her back breaking as her body tried to bend around it. 

He grabbed her up, limp now but screaming, and continued running off with Peter. I thought about chasing after them, trying to save them. 

But then the car started.
