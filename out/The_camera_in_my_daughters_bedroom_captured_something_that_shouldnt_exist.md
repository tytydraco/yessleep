Hello. My name is Damien Edwards. I live in a particularly woody area of suburban Oregon. A few years ago, my wife Margaret and I had our beloved daughter Amy together, and she's been the focus of my life since the day we first brought her home from the nursery.

For the past 11 years, I've tried my damn hardest to keep her safe, to varying degrees of success. I might not be the best father, but I do try. However, nothing could have prepared me for what I saw a few nights back.

Recently, there has been a substantial increase in break-ins in the neighborhood my wife and I live in. The homeowners' association has tried to report this to the local police department, but they've been "coming up empty-handed" with the few investigations they've done. So basically, we're just left to handle it ourselves.

There have been a few suggestions thrown around on how to keep the neighborhood safe, mostly by my neighbor across the street, Kelsie, but none of them have worked all that well. For now, the best advice we've been given is just install cameras in potential entry points, and keep something in the house to defend yourself and your family. Of course, wanting to make sure Amy wasn't hurt in a break-in, my wife and I did just that.

It sort of became a nightly tradition for me to stay up way too late checking the cameras around the house, particularly the one in Amy's bedroom. There's a window on the far wall next to her bed which keeps her room cool at night, but also serves as a potential entry for whoever might intrude on our property. Besides a racoon scratching at her window, I hadn't seen anything of note on her camera... at least, not until I saw that thing last Tuesday.

There wasn't a crashing of glass, there wasn't a smashing of wood, there was no way for me to know something had entered her room. Despite that, the nagging feeling in my gut told me something was wrong, and that I needed to check up on her camera. What I saw when I changed the signal to watch her made my blood run cold. There, standing right outside Amy's window and slowly lurching in, was something I couldn't explain. It wasn't human, it wasn't even natural, but it was living nonetheless.

I almost thought I had fallen asleep at my desk and was just having a nightmare. I tried waking up, but I didn't. This was real. I shook my wife awake as hard as I could, told her to call the police, grabbed one of my hunting rifles, and ran into Amy's room, breaking down the door and staring down the thing leering over her bed.

It looked like something out of a 70s horror novel. It seemed about 8 feet tall, it's body was entirely composed of a pillar of flesh and protruding bone matter, and it had various pieces of scrap metal jammed into its body to seemingly replicate limbs. Its head was a cracked computer monitor, with tubes on its back pumping a viscous blood into the head. On its shoulder was a blinking camera, most likely what it used to see. Despite all that, I was most drawn to its arm, a fleshy appendage with a large, jagged claw of bone matter at its end, hovering right over my precious baby's body. She was almost too deep asleep, so she didn't even know it was there.

I didn't think, I just fired away at the thing. I blew a hole right in its torso, and it made a screech like a wolverine before crawling out on all fours and scurrying into the woods behind our house. I considered chasing it out there, but decided against it, as I didn't wanna die out there and leave my wife and daughter unprotected.

The police arrived the following morning, reviewed the footage I showed them, and told me to never mention this again, or they'd have me taken to a mental institution in a nearby city. I was infuriated, but I didn't wanna push anything, so I hesitantly agreed. My cameras were wiped, and the officers left without another word.

My daughter, as it turned out, was close to being killed and presumably eaten by the thing. She didn't wake up the next morning after the incident, so we brought her to a local hospital, and she was treated for small doses of a chemically altered form of strychnine. She, thankfully, wasn't killed, only rendered unconscious. While there, the man treating her said this wasn't even the first time this week he had dealt with this, so I imagine we weren't the first house this thing has tried to enter.

I don't know what to do from here, as I'm worried I'm the only person who has documented this creature. I feel I need to say this, though. If anyone in the Pacific Northwest is reading this, keep your home secure and keep yourself armed. My family might have been lucky, but I can't guarantee the same luck will come to you if you don't prepare. I don't care what happens to me if I share this, I just want to keep Amy and Margaret safe. I have to let this information be known. If I don't now, it might kill me before I get another chance.
