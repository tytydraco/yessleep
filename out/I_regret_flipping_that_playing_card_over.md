Most nights after work, I walk home. It’s a peaceful time to decompress. But, a walk home a couple of weeks ago wasn’t at all peaceful. 

It began when I came across a face-down playing card in the street. I playfully guessed aloud that the card would be a Two of Spades and flipped the card over. To my surprise, the card was, in fact, a Two of Spades. 

Thinking I heard laughter coming from the shadows next to me, I paused my music. 

“That was a good guess!” I heard someone in the dark remark. 

To say I felt unsettled is the understatement of the century. 

I walked slowly backwards toward the streetlight across the way and stood there for a couple of minutes with my phone ready to call my roommate. After not hearing anything or seeing anyone, I decided to keep walking home. I figured I was just tired and hearing things. 

As I continued on my way, I saw yet another face-down playing card in the street. I’ve seen enough horror movies to know you shouldn’t pick up something like that twice. 

“You don’t want to go two for two?” A voice said from the bushes to my left. 

Panic started to ensue. 

I began to run but was stopped in my tracks by someone blocking the road ahead of me. He was so close I could smell him. The smell of a candle that was recently blown-out overcame me. 

“Why don’t you want to play with us? C’mon, it’ll be fun!” He shouted in a whiney, childish tone. 

“Us?” I thought. 

Now two “people” stood in front of me. 

They were dressed like clowns on acid. Bright colors. Smeared makeup. Neon hair. Sagging balloons hanging from belt loops. 

I started running toward the closest house to me. Before I could get there, the clown-like menaces blew glitter in my face that burned and cut my left arm with a playing card as sharp as a knife. I fell to the ground. 

A white purse appeared, and one of the clowns magically pulled a baseball bat wrapped in barbed wire out of it. 

“This is for your sweet little ol’ neighbor!” 

Both clowns started hysterically laughing. 

“Justttttt kidding,” they said in unison. 

I watched them join hands and dance around a streetlight while giggling. 

“We just wanted you to play with us. We’re sooo bored.” 

I sat silent and stunned for a moment. I watched them mock me as they pantomimed a re-enactment of me falling to the ground. 

“I’ll play with you if you let me go home and change. I’m cold.” I said. 

The clowns looked at each other and shrugged. “Okie! But we know where you live. Don’t try to trick us”. 

I nodded and got up from the ground. My eyes were still burning, and my arm was still bleeding. I looked back at them to see if they were really letting me go. Eyes blurry and now swollen, I could only make out the clowns as a mosaic of bright colors and odd shapes. I, with growing speed, ran home. 

Once inside, I called 911. While the operator seemed skeptical of my story, he said they’d send someone out. I hid in my bedroom. 

But my new playmates weren’t done with me. 

I heard a tap at my bedroom window. 

“Hey, Ms. Two of Spades, we are waiting for you. Come back out. We have another game to play. You’ll LOVE this one.” 

My hands were sweating so badly I could barely hold the phone to call 911 back. 

“Hi, I just called. Please send someone out right now. These people are at my house. I’m worried they are going to break in. I’m already bleeding. Please!”

That’s when I heard my roommate’s car pull in. The tapping at my window stopped. I looked outside,  and no one was there but my roommate and her boyfriend. They came inside. I called to them from the bedroom. 

They waited with me until the police arrived. They photographed my glittery, swollen eyes and the cut to my arm. We collected video footage from the security cameras on the street, but no one could be seen in the videos except for me. 

Police accused me of abusing substances and lectured me on the dangers of hallucinogenics. My roommate believed me, but neither of us could prove what had happened. 

I still don’t have an answer for who or WHAT those clowns were, but I know they are still around. Most nights, I can hear a tapping at my bedroom window and voices trying to lure me outside. 

“Why don’t you want to play with us? We are sooo much fun. Come outside and see. We are just looking for someone to playyyy with!” 

I regularly think back to flipping over that playing card on my walk home and wonder what kind of nightmarish game I got myself into and when, if ever, it will end.
