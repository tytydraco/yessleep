I’ve been wearing mouth tape to bed for the past year or so. “What is mouth tape?” Sounds like I’m into … stuff, right? Regardless, no, that is not why I slap a piece of hypoallergenic fabric, or whatever it is, across my lips before slipping beneath the sheets at night.

It started with snoring. Well, not all snoring, but at least the kind that sounds like a death rattle. I don’t have sleep apnea, but I do tend to wake the neighborhood when the windows are open. At least that’s what my wife says.

And, hell, it worked. In fact, instead of sounding like I’m cutting logs, I was sleeping like one. Not only that, but I no longer woke up feeling absolutely famished, like I hadn’t eaten in days.

My wife was still plagued with aggressive morning hungries. I tried to convince her that the tape was the way to go, but she gave me the usual side-eye. Why would she care – she’s still losing weight somehow.

What was strange to me at the time was how tricky it was to buy this stuff online. I’d find it on Amazon one day, then it’d be gone the next. Not out-of-stock – gone. When I was finally able to place an order, I’d get weeks' worth, because I never knew when it would be back again.

There’s nothing fancy about it. It’s just a black piece of sticky fabric. What’s so onerous about that?

Anyway, I wasn’t exactly interested in looking like a censored poster for the rest of my life. So after a few months of wearing the tape, I decided to try a night without it.

I tend to get to bed a bit later than my wife, so she was already asleep when I got there – it was a weeknight, don’t judge. If I was back to snoring again, I was sure she’d let me know in the morning or, hell, elbow me in the middle of the night.

I was asleep nearly the second my head hit the pillow. What felt like a second later, snoring woke me up. My mouth had been trained shut from the months of wearing the tape – it wasn’t me. It wasn’t my wife.

I slowly rolled onto my back to face the ceiling. It seemed unusually pitch dark, as though the ambient charging lights throughout the room and starlight from the windows were extinguished. But I could make out something there in the intersection of wall and ceiling above my wife.

It was more “nothing” than something. The best way I can describe it is a living void. Whether it was self-induced or not, I was paralyzed. Whatever it was slithered – for lack of a better word – down the ceiling toward my sleeping wife. Just above the headboard, it stopped. Like it was waiting.

My wife lay on her back, sleeping soundlessly. I opened my mouth to say something to wake her, but before any words could come out, the void stirred and made a god-awful snoring sound. Somehow, I felt like it was looking at me.

I slapped my mouth shut. It went back to waiting.

My wife, who up to that point hadn’t moved a muscle, opened her mouth and mumbled, “You’re snoring again.”

The void jerked awake. This time its snore became more like a gurgle. It spilled onto the bed and quickly made its way to my wife’s face and open mouth. The gurgle became a sucking sound quicker than I could finally let out a scream. All sound stopped as I ripped off the covers and hit the bedside lamp’s switch.

My wife was screaming, but only because I was. The room was empty – or, I suppose you could say, less empty. She was pissed. I was relieved. In the morning, we were both exhausted, but we were not hungry. Never again.

Why do I wear mouth tape? It started with snoring.
