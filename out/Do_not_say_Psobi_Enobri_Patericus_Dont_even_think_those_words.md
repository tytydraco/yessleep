Psobi. Enobri. Patericus.  
  
Those words changed my life.  
  
I thought they would change it for the better, but I should have read my grandfather’s diary more carefully.  
  
It’s so unfair. All I wanted was to talk to my daughter. Just once.  
  
One quick conversation.  
  
That’s all.  
  
Now I wish I had never stolen that damn diary from my brother. But there’s no sense in feeling sorry for myself now.  
  
My hands won’t stop shaking.  
  
It’s like something is strangling my throat, making it difficult to breathe.  
  
I’m so afraid. Not of death, but of what will take my place.  
  
I’m not ready to let it consume what’s left of my soul, if such things even exist.  
  
A gateway between our mortal world and whatever lies beyond it. That’s what my grandfather described. It sounded ridiculous.  
  
Like a desperate, lonely man who had spent too much time alone with a bottle of whisky.  
  
I should have known better.  
  
There’s a reason the stories about the supernatural exist.  
  
Gods. Demons. Ghosts.  
  
That odd feeling of being watched from the dark. As though someone, or something, waits for you to turn your back on it. To turn off the lights.  
  
I hate myself.  
  
How could I have believed I would succeed without paying the price my grandfather described?  
The instructions were simple.  
  
A faint reflection of the eyes was all that was needed.  
  
No candles. No bones. No pentagram.  
  
Of course, the words needed to be pronounced correctly. The instructions made that very clear.  
  
Psobi. Enobri. Patericus.  
  
Draw out the vowels.  
  
Roll the r’s.  
  
Don’t rush the last word.  
  
I still remember how stupid I felt saying them the first time.  
  
Even if the things my grandfather described existed, the ritual was too simple.  
  
Too elementary.  
  
Too uncomplicated.  
  
How wrong I was.  
  
The change came after a few hours. Or maybe the next day. I’m not certain anymore.  
  
I remember now how I felt its presence the moment I spoke the chant correctly. How the darkness in my room acted strangely, as though a shape lingered just at the corner of my eye.  
  
The eyes are the gateway between the realms. I know that now.  
  
I looked deep into mine.  
  
Something was there.  
  
I asked for my daughter. Then I asked again.  
  
The laughter I heard wasn’t from my daughter.  
  
It was from my own mouth, even though my lips didn’t move.  
  
That was a week ago. Or maybe it was yesterday? I’m not certain anymore.  
  
It took away my memories first.  
  
Then my sense of time.  
  
The reflection in my eyes is demanding.  
  
My hands don’t feel like mine anymore.  
  
I begged it to release me.  
  
It didn’t refuse.  
  
It made me an offer instead.  
  
If I could open another gateway, it would leave me alone.  
  
If I could pave the way for its thousands of brothers and sisters, it would leave me alone.  
  
I understand now why my grandfather, no, I think it was my brother, who put that strip of cloth over the words. I can’t remember.  
  
It’s the perfect trap. A real work of evil.  
  
The words don’t even need to be spoken aloud. A mere whisper in the mind is enough.  
  
Psobi. Enobri. Patericus.  
  
A cold feeling running down your spine is the first sign.  
  
Then comes the horrible feeling of something standing right behind you.  
  
Don’t look. It’ll only upset it.  
  
Look in a mirror. Then you’ll see.  
  
Maybe not at first.  
  
Look deep into your own eyes.  
  
It’ll hide there.  
  
Ask a question if you dare.

You’ll see.
