*“Light as a feather, stiff as a board.”*

The chant that, to this day, I still couldn’t get out of my mind; the phrase that led to years of chasing things I never should’ve crossed paths with.

I grew up in a group home, and throughout my time there, I was able to make friends with the other girls; being an older kid meant you were least likely to be picked off the shelves like a bestseller. Unlike the newborns and toddlers, we stayed longer in St. Margot’s, waiting for the perfect family to come in and sweep us away to live happily ever after—like in the family channels on television.

That dream was dead the moment we decided to play with something we never should’ve—everything in my life did change forever in the fall evening of ‘94, in the small town of Massachusetts.

I tucked the Tiger Beat magazine I'd snuck in from my school bag under my bed as Sister Madelene began calling out from the halls. “Lights out, children. I don’t want to see groggy faces tomorrow before school.” Turning to my left to check on the other girls, I saw Aileen pressing the side of her head against the pillow, still wearing her glasses. 

Meanwhile, I could hear snickering to my right, from under the covers on Paula’s bed, prompting me to tell her. “Be quiet, sister is coming.”

Tiffany hurried from the vanity near the door to get to her bed; she’d been brushing her long blonde hair in front of the mirror, a routine she did every night, before slipping under the covers in a fluid motion. Marissa, who was shining her leather boots with the polish she stole from Mr. Pim’s tool shed, sauntered to her own bed without a single nervous bone in her body.

By the time Sister Madelene came into the room, the five of us were already in bed, pretending to sleep; after closing the door quietly, it only took a few seconds for her voice to start booming from the end of the hall, scolding the boys in the other room. 

It practically lasted for a minute until we heard her footsteps right outside our door again. Sister Madelene came back to double-check. The door stayed open for a moment, as we heard her speak to the baby in her arms.

“Now… it’s your turn for bed. No more crying this time, okay, Aubin?” The baby cooed in response.

 Aubin was a new addition to the home; the boy practically appeared out of nowhere one morning three months ago. Sister Madelene said he’s her sister’s baby, and that he’ll be staying with us for a while. We didn’t mind at all; since he was adorable, with hands so small and soft, and a shiny, bald head so big, he was endearing to look at, and knowing he wouldn’t be gone within just a week or two like the other babies, we felt comfortable getting to know him better.

The silence lasted for five minutes, until Paula asked, “She still ain’t returned the ‘weejee’ board?”

“No… all thanks to you,” Marissa whispered back, her scowl practically bled into the words she spoke.

“It ain’t my fault? I swear that glass-thingy showed an eye—”

“Shhh!” Tiffany’s spit traveled across two beds. “Sister could still very well be out in the hall, so I suggest keeping it down.”

We let a moment pass, listening to every quiet creak in the building, until we were sure that Sister Madelene had gone into her room. After listening to the wooden creaks above the ceiling follow the sound of her bedroom door closing shut, all our covers were tossed aside. 

The five of us huddled in the middle of the room; Paula sat with her legs crossed, turning to Aileen to ask. “Are you sure about this game? Seems like bogus to me.”

“I heard the girls at school play it at sleepovers; they said it works—”

“Let’s just do this, already, before the nun gets back.”

As Marissa pushed to start the game, Aileen told us all to sit in a circle, then asked for a volunteer to lie in the middle. “And then the rest of us will put our fingers under the body, and we call for help from the spirits to help us lift her, chanting, ‘Light as a feather, stiff as a board’ until she floats mid-air.”

“That sounds morbid,” Tiffany said, twiddling at her curls.

“Morbidly cool.” Paula was grinning ear to ear. “Quick, someone lie down. I want to help lift to see it myself.”

Choosing was a bit complicated; none of them wanted to lie down. They all had their own reasons; Aileen didn’t want to be the subject of being ‘open to the spirits’, Tiffany didn’t want to get her hair messed up, and Marissa just didn’t want to. I was getting impatient; the game wasn’t going to start anytime soon with these girls refusing, so I volunteered to get the game off the ground. 

The first mistake was playing the game in the first place; the second one was volunteering to be the sabbath cow.

With my hair spread out on the hardwood floor, the four of them surrounded me, two on my left, two on my right, as they mimicked Aileen’s hands, stacking on top of my chest.

“Your hair is so shiny, Dahlia,” Tiffany said, running her fingers briefly through the strands. “And orange.”

 “Don’t take your hands off her back. Close your eyes, all of you.” Aileen interrupted to lead the game, for everyone to follow. “We are here to summon the spirits of this house. If you are listening, make this body weightless."

I lay awkwardly at first; I did have my eyes closed, which made the silence even more deafening. Nothing weird happened yet; I wasn’t sure what to expect, since clearly none of us had played the game prior, and when they began to lift me off the floor with the power of their fingers, I barely moved at all—it didn’t work.

“Too heavy, let’s try again,” Aileen said, and she called out once more, but louder. "We are here to summon the spirits of this house. If you are here, make this body weightless!"

*Silence*

A complete, utter silence blanketed the whole room, as though air itself ceased to circulate; even the grandfather clock from down the hall stopped ticking. The air felt thick, as though the girls were pressing harder against my chest.

“Is this even working… maybe we’re not doing it right.” Tiffany’s voice felt muffled despite her proximity.

“Aileen?” Marissa took a hand out from under me to shake her shoulder, but Aileen’s eyes were focused on mine; her brown eyes stared back at me with an expression I’ve never seen from her before—pure, unadulterated fear. 

I was about to ask what was wrong, but nothing came out; my mouth hadn’t even moved.

*I couldn’t move.*

Aileen collected herself, shaking her head before taking a deep breath. “Yeah, I’m… let’s continue.” 

”Light as a feather, stiff as a board.” The words spoke out faster than their mouths could; I tried to speak again, yet nothing would come out, leaving me frozen, staring at their faces. The back of my head began to feel light as my feet lifted off the wooden floor; they continued to chant. ”Light as a feather, stiff as a board.”

My entire backside was no longer on the ground—*I wanted to scream.*

”Light as a feather, stiff as a...” They stopped chanting; I felt my nightdress drifting behind me with my body completely off the floor. I could only glimpse their petrified expressions in my peripheral vision, unable to move my head. 

Tears trickled from my eyes down to the side of my face as I cried; the ceiling felt closer and closer, almost as though the tip of my nose could feel the cold, lightless bulb. All corners of my vision were about to go dark. Cold spots covered my skin, as though four pairs of hands held me up to the ceiling, gripping my limbs, squeezing my flesh with hands as cold as ice. 

I screamed in my head until it manifested into a throaty croak, turning into a muffled yell, reverberating deep within my chords.

“Stop!” Aileen called out from below.

*Thud*

I heard it before I actually felt it; I fell back onto the wooden floor, headfirst—the back of my skull collided with the wooden floor, slamming with a sickening thud. The pain didn’t register at all; I immediately jolted up, screaming and sobbing uncontrollably. The four of them wrapped their arms around me as we huddled by the wall. 

I didn’t even know what I was saying in that moment, but the commotion from us five was enough to catch Sister Madelene’s attention. Her hurried footsteps trailed from the ceiling, down the stairs, then to the front of our door—she barged in, wearing her long-sleeved nightgown. 

“What is going on here? Why are you girls screaming?” 

The boys from the other room stood by the doorway as they took the steps to walk into the middle of the room, unknowingly standing in the very spot I was lying on. She froze, with her bare feet on the creaking wood beneath her.

“Go back to your rooms, now.” She said calmly, turning to the boys. 

They retreated to their rooms, yet my stomach dropped as my eyes met hers, because in her eyes I saw the same look Aileen held—I saw fear in her eyes, but she took a step closer, one foot after the other, until she crouched in front of us; I could still see the uncertainty in her eyes, but despite that, she still managed to ask us, “What happened? Girls, talk to me.”

 Tiffany’s jaw almost unhinged as she recounted everything to Sister Madelene; her words had gone numb in my head as she spoke with the precision of a bumbling bee. 

“So when we had our hands from under her, Dahlia began floating!”

*Thud*

A hard thud resounded from the wooden ceiling; before Sister Madelene could even give a response to what she was told, she wouldn’t be given a chance to—as muffled sounds began to emit from the ceiling, right where the nursery room was. Sister’s widened eyes met mine, as a damning realization settled between us.

*Aubin*

She rushed out of the room to run upstairs; her footsteps stomped through the floorboards above, then she barged into the room. We waited for the sound of the door opening, and for Sister Madelene to calm the baby with her soft voice, but instead we heard her panicked calls from out in the hall upstairs.

“Aubin!” She called out, while the baby’s cries got louder.

When I realized Sister Madelene was trying to slam her body against the door, I got up immediately to run upstairs; the others trailed behind me, while we passed the boys’ rooms, where they were peeking from their doors, debating amongst themselves to leave their rooms or follow orders.

Getting up on my feet, Marissa held my wrist, stopping me from bolting out of the room. ”What are you doing?”

“To help Sister; she needs our help.”

“I-I think she’d want us to stay here; we’re not allowed to leave our rooms after bedtime—” Tiffany reasoned, but it was evident on her face that even she knew it didn’t matter whether they stayed or went, and it was that moment the latter meant more than the other.

Staring directly into Tiffany’s eyes, her unshed tears brimmed at the corners, I told her. “Aubin needs our help.”

The five of us rushed upstairs; Sister Madelene was still outside the nursery room, banging against the door, trying to use her strength to open it, while the poor baby was crying hysterically inside the room; hearing the guttural screams coming from a body barely the size of a bed pillow struck against my chest, and seeing the state of the woman of the house was no better—she was on the verge of tears, trying to get the door to slam open. 

“Open the fucking door!” She cursed out loud, taking us aback, hearing the former nun swear out loud for the first time; we didn’t even question who she was cursing to, as we immediately moved to try and open the door with her, although it wouldn’t budge with just two or three bodies.

It took all six of us to break the door open. 

Marissa, Paula, and Tiffany stumbled onto the nursery floor while I rushed with Sister Madelene to check on little Aubin, but she stopped midway. The sister’s bare feet halted on the creaking wood once more. In the middle of the room, I froze with her and Aileen; because in the darkest corner where the crib sat, the baby had gone silent, as he… 

*floated*.

I couldn’t get a clear look; the moon could only do so much to bring light into a darkened room, where the corners not only held secrets, but also the only baby in the home. Aubin floated in mid-air; he had gone completely silent, his blanket draped on his body like a tablecloth, leaving only his head uncovered, with an unmoving expression. His eyes widened, staring up at the ceiling, void of any motion or even consciousness.

Babies are barely cognitive; I knew that, but the sight of Aubin frozen and floating, while seemingly dead, was mortifying. I turned to Aileen, who held the same face as I did, then to the three who looked up from where they lay, with their quiet gasps and stifled breaths. 

We all stood in disbelief at the baby floating lightly and rigid, until Sister Madelene finally spoke.

*“Lassa lo bambino, no es tuyo pa guardar.”* She said under her breath; I couldn’t understand a word as she repeated it, only louder. 

After the third time, she practically yelled it out at the corner, and only then did Aubin drop back down to his crib with a light squeak and a breathless gasp. He began to cry again as she took him into her arms immediately, cradling his little body while whispering and rocking him. “It’s okay, little one, you’re safe… you’re safe.”

Aileen fiddled with her fingers before turning to me with glistening eyes. “This is all my fault. I’m so sorry.” She said; it seemed speaking it out loud was what made her cry. Her face had been riddled with guilt throughout the whole time. But I held her, reassuring her that it wasn’t her fault; although it was she who brought the game into the home, we were all equally responsible for participating, and for welcoming… something, or someone into the walls of the home.

It took minutes until the heavy atmosphere clogging the room finally dispersed, after Marissa opened the nearby window, letting the sheer curtains blow inwards from the October winds outside; the room was quiet except for Sister’s murmurs and Aubin’s dying cries.

Until Paula spoke up from near the door. “Sister…” She called out for the woman’s attention while Tiffany and Marissa were standing outside the bright hall. “Is this your *Jesus* candle? I found it on the floor rolling around.”

She held up the candle with the graphic image of Jesus Christ on the thick glass. “That’s probably what fell,” I said when I noticed the thick glass candle barely had a single crack or dent, despite falling from an elevated place; Sister Madelene instructed her to light it on again with a match from a nearby drawer. Paula did as she was told, finally bringing warm light back into the dark room. 

“You’re sleeping with me tonight.” Sister said to Aubin, while handing him his favorite dolphin stuffed toy. Although his eyelashes were damp and teary, with the plush toy in his mouth, everything seemed all right and just in his world again.

“Girls, go back to bed.” Sister Madelene turned to look at me, Aileen, and the three by the door. “And please, no more games. We’ll talk tomorrow.”

That was enough of a signal for us to rush back downstairs and hide ourselves under the covers of our beds. The girls were out like a light; their gentle snores were a clear indication of that; meanwhile, I could barely sleep, staring up at the ceiling once more, recalling the feeling of being as light as a feather and unable to move even a single inch. 

Was that how Aubin felt in that moment?

The thought of the harmless baby going through the overwhelming feeling I felt brought nothing but dread into my chest, but not as much as the thought of the growing darkness in the corner; what seemed like just a shadow shouldn’t have brought tears to my eyes or conjured thoughts, but I was afraid.

In fear of what I felt hid in the dark, scared of the fact that I knew, even at thirteen, in the fall evening of ‘94 in Massachusetts, that evening was only just the beginning, and that nothing was ever going to be the same again, in the quiet halls of St. Margot’s.
