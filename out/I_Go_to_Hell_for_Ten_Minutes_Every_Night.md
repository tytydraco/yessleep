It happens every night at 1:18 am. I go to hell and then I’m back in my bed ten minutes later.

My nightly jaunts were a source of daily horror when I was younger, but I’ve learned to live with it. I make allowances and I prepare. A bite-guard keeps my teeth from chattering or grinding depending on which flavor of underworld I encounter. Earplugs dull the screams of the damned and keep me sane. Other than that, a few towels to catch the sweat and gore are also handy. They’re far cheaper to buy than expensive linens.

There are other considerations I’ve been forced to make in light of my personal *inconvenience*. I never stay out late and I never stay over anywhere. I have my reasons. I tore a tendon back in high school and had to be hospitalized for it. I only stayed for one night, but it was enough. Four patients and a late shift nurse all vanished because I was there. I wonder sometimes if they had family.

I have another rule, an important one, but I broke it yesterday, and Maia paid the price. She stayed over and I still don’t know why I let her. I guess I was tired of living like a monk. 

A relationship had always seemed like forbidden fruit for someone like me. How could I have something like that with someone without putting them in danger? But then she came into my life with all of her dizzyingly perfect imperfection. That crooked smile owned me right down to my socks, and I knew I was in love with her before we finished our third date.

Last night was the night. I couldn’t put her off anymore, and I didn’t want to. There was something easy and natural about how it happened, and there was no awkwardness afterward. We were just happy. That little voice in my head tried to warn me. It told me that there was still time, but we were so warm, snuggled up together. I fell asleep and damned the woman I love.

She woke me up screaming and I pulled her against me instinctively. My eyes opened upon a dark void and I felt the chill in the air on my naked body. We floated there together in something that wasn’t air or water, but a little of both. She trembled in my arms and I told her it was going to be okay. We just had to stay together.

I had forgotten the wailing and the shrieks of torment. I had blocked them out for so long that they were only vague memories. Those cries chased us like banshees as we drifted through the soupy black, traveling toward their source.

“Did we die, Will?” she asked me. “Did we go to hell?”

I didn’t know how to answer her. Anything I said would just scare her more than she already was. I told her it was only for a little while, and that we were safe as long as we were together. We were getting closer to an island of marble in that inky sea. I knew from experience that there were spirits there, and chilly isolation was their torment.

We washed up there on the worn stone of the shore. It was like something from the Greeks or Romans, sculpted pillars and arches. Weathered statues stood here and there but I knew that they weren’t art. They were spirits that had finally succumbed to the isolation. They had become one with their prison. I had wandered that place before and heard them speak. I’d seen a single tear run down from an unblinking eye.

I felt it coming like I always did. We only had seconds left. I smiled at her in pure relief and told her she was safe. As our surroundings began to dim and we began to fade, a restless soul made a liar out of me when it tore her from my startled grasp.

Three days have passed now and she’s still gone. People have called me and asked questions I can’t answer. There was even a cop this morning, a detective. I lied to him and told him I hadn’t seen her in a week. I could tell he didn’t believe me.

My nightly visits are unpredictable—the latest of them were locusts, ice, and the endless war. I’ve seen them all before, but I can never be sure which one I’m going to get. *How do you find someone when there’s only a random chance?* I know where she is but I don’t know how to get there.

Lord help me, please tell me what to do.
