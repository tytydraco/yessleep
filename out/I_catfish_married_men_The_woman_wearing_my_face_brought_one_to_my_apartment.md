Graham sat on their sofa staring at his screen. Behind him stood the woman in red, though her body was only a pale distortion.

Beth zoomed in.

My face was not the only one inside it.

Dozens overlapped beneath the blur.

Women.

Men.

One child.

Daniel.

His eyes were swollen. His mouth hung open around something dark.

“When did you take this?”

“Yesterday morning.”

“Before Daniel separated her from me.”

“I don’t know who Daniel is.”

“You wouldn’t like him.”

“She was already in our house.”

That bothered me.

I had assumed the woman began using the profile after leaving my body.

But Beth had photographed her before Daniel poured the first bag.

Before the garage.

Before I knew she existed.

“How long had Graham been messaging the account?”

“Four days.”

The woman had been visiting Beth’s house while she was still inside me.

Maybe Daniel had been wrong about separation.

Maybe he had not pulled her out.

Maybe he had only cut whatever kept her returning.

Beth showed me another photograph.

She lay asleep on the sofa.

The picture had been taken from above.

From the ceiling.

The woman crouched on Beth’s chest, looking down into her face.

“She never touched me,” Beth said. “Not until tonight.”

“She knew you could see her.”

“I think that’s why she kept coming back.”

“Is she gone?”

“No.”

“How do you know?”

I looked at the wall where the red glow had disappeared.

I could feel the missing place inside me.

Not emptiness.

A pulled tooth leaves emptiness.

This felt like a door left open during winter.

Cold air moved through me in slow breaths.

“She’ll use the profile again,” I said.

My phone lay on the dining table.

The dating app was open.

The photographs remained the same.

Red dress. Black dress. Bikini. Golden retriever.

The bio now said:

I’m better than your wife.

She can see me prove it.

Beth took the phone.

“What are you doing?”

“Deleting it.”

“I’ve tried.”

“Try harder.”

She deleted the profile.

It returned.

She deleted the app.

It reinstalled itself.

She powered off the phone.

The screen went black, then lit again. The dating profile opened without asking for a password.

The distance between disbelief and acceptance is shorter than people pretend.

Beth handed the phone back.

“When it messages him again, call me.”

“Why?”

“Because I can see her.”

“You also hate me.”

“Yes.”

“That seems like an unstable working relationship.”

“I am not working with you.”

“Good.”

“I am making sure you do not bring that thing into anyone else’s house.”

“That sounds a little like working with me.”

Beth reached for the tire iron.

I stopped arguing.

Before leaving, she looked at me for a long time.

Not through me.

At me.

I felt the warmth of it.

Being seen by someone who despises you is still being seen.

I slept for three hours.

When I woke, dried blood had collected beneath my nails, and a bruise crossed my stomach in the shape of five fingers pressing outward from inside.

My phone lay beside me.

One conversation had appeared.

Daniel’s profile.

No photograph. No age. No location.

The typing bubble pulsed at the bottom of the screen.

Three dots.

Gone.

Three dots.

Gone.

Then a message appeared.

YOU LET HER INTO YOUR HOME.

I typed:

She came through Graham.

The reply arrived immediately.

NO.

Then:

HE CAME THROUGH HER.

Daniel sent a photograph.

It showed my apartment during the power outage. Beth and I stood near the mirror. Graham lay between us. The woman in red was pinned beneath Beth’s foot.

The photograph had been taken from inside the wall.

Behind the woman, almost hidden beneath the red dress, something smaller was crawling out of Graham.

Hairless.

Limbs bending in too many places.

No face, only a round opening surrounded by tiny square teeth.

It was not looking at Graham.

It was looking at Beth.

Daniel sent one final message.

SHE WASN’T THE ONLY THING HE BROUGHT YOU.

I called Beth.

She answered on the first ring.

For several seconds, neither of us spoke.

I heard road noise, her turn signal, her breathing.

Then a second breath came through the phone.

Slower than hers.

“Beth,” I said.

“I know.”

“Where are you?”

“Driving to the hospital.”

“Do you see it?”

“Yes.”

“Where?”

“In the back seat.”

A faint clicking filled the line.

Dozens of tiny teeth tapping together.

“Do not look away from it.”

“I’m driving.”

“Use the mirror.”

“I am.”

“What is it doing?”

“Opening its mouth.”

The clicking grew louder.

“Pull somewhere public,” I said. “Bright lights. People.”

“There’s a gas station ahead.”

“Go there.”

Her engine climbed. The second breathing continued behind hers.

Then it stopped.

“Beth?”

“I still see it.”

“What changed?”

“It’s coming over the seat.”

The clicking became frantic.

Beth shouted.

Her horn blasted through the phone and stayed down.

Someone outside cursed. A child began crying. Car doors opened.

Then I heard a little girl say, “Mommy, what’s that?”

The clicking stopped.

Beth inhaled sharply.

“What happened?”

“It looked at her.”

“Can she see it?”

“Yes.”

The child screamed.

Something struck the inside of Beth’s car. Metal boomed, followed by a scraping noise beneath the floor.

People outside were shouting now.

Someone opened Beth’s door.

“Is it gone?” I asked.

She checked the mirrors.

“Yes. It went through the floor.”

“Stay where people can see you.”

“I know.”

“And do not go home.”

“I know.”

She hung up.

The conversation with Daniel disappeared.

The app returned to my account.

A new message had been sent to another married man.

His profile picture showed him beside his wife and two young children. He had not cropped any of them out.

The message from my account said:

I know your wife can see them.

He had replied:

See what?

The typing bubble appeared.

I tried to delete the message.

The button moved away from my thumb.

I tried to close the app.

The screen stayed open.

A photograph appeared in the conversation.

It showed the man asleep beside his wife. Between them, beneath the blanket, something small was sitting upright.

Its limbs bent in too many places.

Its round mouth was open.

The wife was awake.

She was staring directly at it.

Then another message appeared.

Not from the man.

From her.
