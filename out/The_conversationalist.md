Growing up just south of Salem, Massachusetts, you can’t help but become infatuated with Halloween. It’s always a fun time of year. Everyone gets in the mood for the spooky season, even if they hate to admit it.

I was a year out of high school. Most of my friends had gone off to college and lived on campus. I stayed behind and lived at home with my parents to save money. This left me and a couple of friends to get creative with how we spent our free time. And trust me, we had a lot of it.

My friends and I were a mixture of outcasts from the nerdy and jock groups you’d find in your typical high school. Due to the circumstances of living in Massachusetts and our love for Ghost Adventures, we liked to do spooky things. We spent many nights running away from security guards at forgotten mental institutions and abandoned hospitals. We were shocked to discover how many were within a reasonable driving distance.

We were always trying to figure out whether ghosts were actually real.

One day, we all went to the mall to walk around. We entered Spencer’s Gifts to gawk at all the sex toys hanging from the walls. They also had some pretty sweet lava lamps that I always thought were underrated, especially if you were smoking weed.

That’s when we came across it. A Ouija board, manufactured by Milton Bradley. We bought it for about $20 and didn’t think twice about it. We knew how we were going to spend our day.

Dom, Calvin, and I debated which house to go to. Calvin’s house it was, because there’s no way in hell I was opening that can of worms in my own home. Honestly, my mom would probably see the demon possession as a blessing. Finally, a reason to get rid of me that she could blame on someone else.

We landed at Calvin’s house. The last homeowner had suffered a brain aneurysm and died in the living room. We figured a death in the living room had to count for something in the haunted-house department.

We waited for Calvin’s mom to leave for work. We didn’t want her catching us looking like a bunch of weirdos. Three jobless losers sitting in her living room, trying to conjure a séance.

We tore the plastic covering off the box and pulled the board out. We were all nervously giddy. We put the planchette on the board and started asking it questions.

Six eyes, all locked in, watching this thing to see if it would whiz across the board.

“Is anyone there?” Dom asked.

Nothing. No movement.

We asked more questions, but the planchette sat still.

“What a waste of 30 minutes,” I said.

“I’m hungry. Let’s get Taco Bell.”

We left the board and headed to the nearest Taco Bell.

On the car ride, I looked in my rearview mirror and noticed Calvin fidgeting around.

“What’s wrong?” I asked.

“This is going to sound crazy, but it felt like something just tapped my shoulder,” Calvin replied.

“It must be the board. It’s following you,” I joked.

“Nothing happened. It’s all in your head.”

We all shrugged it off.

We secured the bag at Taco Bell and then went back to Calvin’s to play Super Smash Bros. I looked back at the board. Still sitting open, just the way we had left it.

“Maybe we should try again.”

“Let’s do it. It’s darker out,” Dom agreed.

We all thought this was a fair assertion and took our seats at the table.

We started asking the board questions again. Nothing.

“I’m done with this,” Dom said as he walked back to the N64.

“Is Dom scared?” Calvin jokingly asked the board.

I felt the planchette move beneath mine and Calvin’s fingertips.

“Holy shit!” I yelled.

The planchette slid to the top of the board, landing on YES.

There was an eerie silence in the room as we all stared at each other.

“That had to have been you guys,” Dom said.

“I swear to God it wasn’t. Look at my fingers.”

We all glanced down. Our fingers were hovering over the planchette.

We all burst out laughing. I don’t know if it was nervous laughter or the fact that the ghost knew Dom was a big bitch.

We started asking more questions. Most of them were silly. For example:

“When will the Red Sox win the World Series again?”

“Is global warming real?”

“Did the government kill JFK?”

We got response after response. It was remarkable to watch. Sometimes the planchette would zip to the answer. Other times, it would move at a snail’s pace. It didn’t matter. It was all intriguing.

Eventually, the serious questions started coming out.

“What’s your name?”

PETER

“How did you die?”

NOT SURE

“Do you sleep?”

NO

“What do you do for fun?”

FOLLOW

“Are you going to follow me?” I asked.

YES

“Why would you follow me?”

NO REASON

Needless to say, we were all a little freaked out by this point. I don’t know about you, but I don’t like being stalked by phantoms of the night.

I was still a little skeptical. I thought perhaps Calvin was pushing the planchette toward the responses.

“Do you know what number is on the back of my shirt?” I asked Calvin.

“No clue.”

“Okay, Peter. Tell me what number is on my shirt.”

I stood up from the table and took a step back.

“Okay, this is getting weird now.”

“Come on, sit down. This is fun,” Calvin exclaimed.

I took my seat.

“Not a fucking chance,” Dom said. “I’m perfectly fine right here.”

“Is heaven real ? Are you even human?” Calvin asked the board.

I remember starting to zone out. I was staring at the board, and the voices around me started to sound like I was underwater.

That’s when I heard it, clear as day.

Right next to my right ear.

Breathing. It sounded like someone’s mouth was pressed against my ear, wheezing in and out.

The hairs on my arms stood up.

I looked at Calvin’s face. His mouth was barely off the table.

“D-do you hear that?”

He slowly nodded his head.

“Calvin… we have to say goodbye now,” I pleaded.

“Okay, Peter. Have a good night.”

We moved the planchette to the GOODBYE phrase on the board. We both got out of our seats.

The planchette started to spell something.

No one’s hand was touching it.

TOO LATE.

We immediately Googled how to dispose of the board. Apparently, the only way was to burn it. That’s exactly what we did.

“Welp. I’m definitely never doing that again,” I stated.

“Easy for you to say. I have to sleep here,” Calvin replied.

I hopped into my Toyota Camry. “Have fun with that!”

I drove away from Calvin’s house.

The drive home was uneventful. Although, I spent most of it checking my rearview mirror and convincing myself I was being paranoid.

I got home, took a shower, and went about my usual routine. I half expected something creepy to happen while I was alone in the bathroom, but nothing did.

Eventually, I crawled into bed.

I remember thinking how ridiculous the whole afternoon had been. Three grown dopes playing with an Ouija board and convincing themselves they were talking to a ghost.

I rolled over, pulled the blankets up, and closed my eyes.

I heard it again.  
Breathing.  
Loud wheezing breaths coming from directly  
behind me. I pulled the covers over my shoulder and tried convincing myself it was all in my head.

That's when I felt the mattress sink. I stopped breathing. Something was lying beside me.  
I could hear it breathing.

Then, without thinking, I whispered:  
"Peter?"  
The breathing stopped
