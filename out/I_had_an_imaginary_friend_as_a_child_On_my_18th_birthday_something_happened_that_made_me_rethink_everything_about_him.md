Childhood can be so beautiful and sickeningly sweet. I remember little about my childhood, because memories can get lost in the vastness of our minds, in the deep ocean we live in.

But even forgetting some memories, there are small things I will never forget. Even as the years pass, I will carry these memories to the cold grave where my body will rest.

I remember climbing the huge trees near my house, swimming in a muddy river and coming back covered in dirt, running from my mother to avoid taking a bath.

I remember my dog named Void.

Yes, it sounds like a strange name, but I liked it.

And I remember my best friend, Oliver.

Oliver was quite tall. His body was thin, so thin that his ribs showed through his skin whenever his lungs filled with air. His arms were long, as were his fingers.

I remember his old and torn clothes, but something I don’t remember is his face.

It was on my 7th birthday that we met.

I was sitting by the lake, watching the sun disappear between the trees, when I felt a cold hand touch my shoulder.

When I turned to see who it was, I saw my best friend for the first time. Only today do I realize how cold his hands were.

I didn’t spend much time talking to him, because night was coming and I remember I had an almost irrational fear of the dark, something from childhood I still carry to this day.

I went back home as soon as the sky started to darken.

I remember the smell of pumpkin soup, the damp wood, the smile on my mother’s face, and seeing, through the window, Oliver still by the river, his slender body turned toward my house.

My mother worked a lot to keep food on our table, so she would always leave in the early afternoon and come back at the beginning of the night.

At that time, we didn’t have the conditions to enroll me in school, so I stayed home alone. Well, not alone anymore. Now I had Oliver watching me.

I still remember a little of our first adventure, at the beginning of our bond.

My mother had left early for work that day, and I was tidying up the house when I heard a heavy knock on the door. I know my mother told me never to trust strangers, but something inside me told me to open the door, and I did.

Oliver was standing in front of the door, and I could notice his rapid breathing. We went near the trees, and I remember little of that moment. Whenever I try to recover those memories, something stops me.

The fragments I still have are of Oliver holding a bird in his hands and doing some kind of magic. The bird fell asleep with his touch, such a deep sleep that it dropped to the ground.

Oliver started walking toward my house, and I followed him.

I can clearly remember our way back home, the muddy path we followed, how Oliver, even being tall, always walked slowly, the way I wouldn’t stop talking and how Oliver never said anything. And I also remember the sounds of his bones cracking with every step he took; the sound was like a branch breaking.

That sound was loud, as loud as he was.

That night, my mother gave me a late birthday present. I still remember the smile I gave when she handed me a coloring book with a small box of colored pencils.

I didn’t know how to read. Well, I didn’t go to school and had no contact with other children. So I asked my mother to read the name of the book.

"Oliver and his friends."

That’s when I gave him that name: Oliver.

The days passed calmly. I could never remember all of them, but I always remembered how Oliver was present in all those moments.

When my mother appeared, he disappeared without making the usual sound of breaking branches.

I also left my bedroom window open, because Oliver liked to watch me sleep, to watch me calm and innocent.

One day that is still very present in my memory was when Oliver didn’t appear at all. I was waiting for him by the muddy lake, at our meeting spot. I cried a lot.

He was my only friend, and he had abandoned me.

Some time later, I went back home and saw him.

Oliver was at my bedroom window. Not outside, but inside the house.

I could see his nails tearing pieces of wood from the window, his ribs completely marked on his almost translucent skin.

I ran to my room.

I wanted to hug him, feel the cold touch of his skin against mine, talk to him about everything.

But as quickly as those thoughts came, he was already gone.

My room was empty, without a trace of presence, but something made me happy.

The gift Oliver left for me.

A small leech on the window.

That was not my first gift. He always gave me some things as a way of leaving reminders of himself.

A bird feather, shed snake skins, leeches, river spiders, and even scratches on my arms, bruises on my legs. My mother thought they were from my frequent falls from trees.

I remember that it was from that moment that my relationship with Oliver began to grow. Now he was always with me, even when my mother was around.

She seemed not to see him, but I remember seeing him touching her shoulders, her waist, and even entering her room.

Oliver and I used to play in the rain. I would always get dirty, but my smile was always there.

Oliver, on the other hand, never smiled, I think.

He never liked the games I liked. He preferred to stay at the lake, in the forest, or in my room, and I always followed him.

I vaguely remember the day he took me back into the forest.

This time, his steps were fast and the sound of breaking branches mixed with the taller and more distant trees.

His cold, fleshless hand grabbed my arm hard and... I can’t remember the rest.

The memories are gone.

After that, I got sick for a few days.

My body was aching, my bones felt like they were breaking with every breath, and my skin was hot, like the flames of the candles that lit my room. I remember seeing Oliver standing at the window, holding a dead leech.

My illness only went away when Oliver touched me. His nails scratched my skin, not very deeply, but enough to make the blood run between his fingers.

Tears ran down my cheeks and the illness, like a nightmare, quickly disappeared.

In less than three days, I was already playing with Oliver again.

My mother smiled when she saw me healthy, but she never knew that Oliver cured me. Thinking about it, maybe he cured me from a disease he gave me himself.

Sometimes I told my mother about Oliver, but I don’t think she ever believed anything I said about him.

Every night, when I closed my eyes to sleep, I felt the touch of Oliver’s cold fingers on my arms.

I never asked him to stop.

I think I was afraid of what he would do if I did.

Over time, my relationship with Oliver began to grow cold, just like his skin.

He didn’t appear as much in my house anymore. It took longer for us to meet at the river or in the forest.

He was disappearing, but I still remember how intensely he played with me when he appeared.

Sometimes he would wake me up during the night and squeeze my arms, my legs, and my stomach.

Those squeezes hurt a lot and I couldn’t scream.

He wouldn’t let me.

So the tears did the job for me. They flowed so much that even today my eyes are swollen.

I can’t remember what happened after that. I wanted to, but I couldn’t.

With time, Oliver never appeared again.

I remember crying every night missing him, remembering playing with him, even the painful games he used to do.

He was my only friend, and I was afraid of being alone again.

A few years later, we moved to a big city. My life changed completely.

It was strange not being able to climb trees, not to bathe in the river every day, or catch fireflies to light up my room.

But now I had friends. Real friends.

But every night, when I slept in my new house, I missed Oliver a lot. He was still my friend. Every night he was with me, even without saying a single word.

I spent my entire childhood and adolescence thinking that Oliver was just an imaginary friend, a result of the loneliness I lived in my childhood.

But something inside me wouldn’t let me believe he was just my imagination.

He felt so real.

But everything I thought about Oliver changed today.

It was today, on my 18th birthday. Many friends were celebrating with me.

And everything changed when my mother said she had brought a special guest: Matthew, her ex-partner.

And it was when my eyes met his that I noticed something strange.

He...

he was Oliver.
