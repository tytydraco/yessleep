*3.      Take in the mail*

*4.      Sing to the crib*

The list of chores was written on a sticky note on the fridge, stainless steel with a digital display, thing probably cost more than my car. I read the last chore over again. *Sing to the crib*, they had explained it to me when I arrived but still it gave me the creeps.

 

 

Pulling into the driveway that morning, I had to almost scrape my jaw off the floor. Such affluence I had never seen in all my life. Rounding the fountain in the front courtyard, the manor came into view, its enormous Victorian architecture blocking the rising sun behind it, creating an almost holy glow around its silhouette. The family were out the front when I arrived, loading their Luis Vuitton suitcases into their Range Rover. Standing on the steps leading into the house was Mrs Coldwell, a woman of fifty, though I would never have believed it if she hadn’t told me herself. She was the image of elegance and class, wrapped up in her fur coat and hat, her long thin legs adorned in black tights guiding the eye down to her black 5-inch heels. Noticing my arrival, she waved me over, a warm smile over her face. Mr Coldwell was standing by as his driver loaded the luggage into the car, he turned to face me, offering his hand for me to shake.

“Thank God, you’re here” I tried my best to not wilt before him as I clasped his hand. The mans booming voice and razor-sharp jaw made me feel small in his presence, which I suppose is exactly how he had managed to rise to CEO of Noxar Industries. The man was ten years older than his wife but still he also seemed cut from marble.

 

 

“In sake of full transparency, you really saved our arses when you replied to our ad. Usually of course, we would have had our butler take care of the house while we were away but just yesterday, he has seemingly taken ill.” He gave me a reassuring smile which told me his praise was genuine. The real truth of it was, they had saved my ass. I had been down to my last £50 when I had seen their ad for a week of housesitting paying £2000. I had initially assumed it was a scam but with so little to lose I had applied anyway. I suppose many others had thought the same as somehow; I got the job. I watched as his wife descended the steps to greet me, with each bounce her coat would rise slightly revealing a slither more of her upper thigh.

“This is my wife Margaret” Mr Coldwell announced proudly, gesturing to her now outplaced hand for me to also shake, I would’ve preferred to kiss her cheek.

“And this is my son Adam” he murmured gesturing more solemnly to the boy stood out to his side, a late teen on his phone. He didn’t raise his head to face me, his foppish black hair obscuring his eyes. In disapproval, Mr Coldwell theatrically cleared his throat, and the boy finally rose his gaze, shooting me a quick nod which seemed to double as “You alright?” and “Fuck off”.

 

 

Mr Coldwell led me into the house, the thick oak door giving way to the vast foyer. The space was so large and extravagant it bordered on parody; it reminded me more of a museum than anyone’s home. Mr Coldwell pompously guided me through the manor, stopping at every painting and ornament to explain its gilded history. In the living room, he introduced me to the dog, Harry, a sweet golden retriever which seemed to warm quickly to me, licking at my hands when I went to pet him.

“You can keep him outside if you like” Mr Coldwell seemed taken aback and somewhat offended at how Harry reacted. I suppose it’s no surprise for wealthy men to get possessive, even over their dogs. 

  


Moving back into the foyer and up the stairs, we passed the enormous portrait of Laurence Coldwell, the man who built this place. Dressed in a suitably flashy outfit for the era and holding himself up by his cane, his stern stare surveyed the foyer below with contempt. The upstairs split into two hallways each leading down their respective ends of the house. Mr Coldwell explained that to the left were the family rooms and his study and to the right was the guest rooms and the nursery, which is where I would be staying.

“Nursery?” I hadn’t seen a young child outside. He begun to explain.

“Yes, well… it was for our youngest, Harry... until we… lost him” His earlier layer of pride had shed like snakeskin. What was left was an old man, falling adrift into the worst parts of his mind.

“I was meaning to ask you… for a favour. After the death, Margaret… struggled to cope. In fact, this the first time we’ve taken a trip away from home since it happened, and well… She’s developed this habit… a coping mechanism of sorts. Where she… sings… sings to the crib, like she used to sing to Jacob” He turned to me with pleading eyes. Surely, he couldn’t expect-

“Well, I sort of told her, in order to get her out of the house, that I would have someone do it in her absence…” He couldn’t even ask outright, instead leading me to the question with this weird dance. It was because he knew it was lunacy, but he also knew I wasn’t in a position to refuse.

“Sure, of course. Anything you need” It was pathetic.

 



The house is empty now. It looks a lot different in the dark, something about the vast space makes me feel uneasy. More hiding spots, I suppose. I tried my best to shake off these morbid thoughts as I moved back into the foyer. Henry brushed up against my leg which embarrassingly made me jump, the dog has barely left my side since I got here. I decided to have an early night, I’ll feel a lot better in the daytime. Moving up the stairs, the floorboards creaked with every slight shift of weight, everything seems too loud in this broad silence. Henry skipped up the steps ahead of me, I imagine I’d feel a lot worse about this place if he wasn’t here. I took the right down the hallway to the guest rooms, before he left Mr Coldwell had not so subtly mentioned that all the family rooms on the left were locked. 

  


The guest room is a pretty simple affair for such a lavish place, I opened my bedside drawer and got out my clothes for the night. I had just begun to change when from the walls came this long winding groan, a stark reminder at the age of this place, probably the pipes. Despite this rationalisation, the sound had greatly unnerved me and I suddenly became aware the dog was no longer with me. It didn’t take me long to find him; he was stood just outside my door facing the room opposite mine – the nursery. *Sing to the* crib, I had no intention of doing it. After all, I don’t even think Mr Coldwell expected me to, he just needed something to tell his wife. But what if they found out I hadn’t done it, for a family of their station it wasn’t unlikely that this place was covered in secret cameras. This was a dream gig, a life raft in the sea of debt I had recently found myself in. Was I really going to risk screwing that up because of some crib? 





I shuffled around Henry and tentatively tried the door…

But it didn’t budge, I even forced my shoulder against it, but still it remained sealed. I said a silent prayer of thanks and turned back to my room. Now at least if they were watching, I would have an excuse for why I didn’t do it. I tried to call Henry over from the door, but he remained still, staring straight ahead. I tried again, patting my knees but still he didn’t waver. All of a sudden, he leant back onto his hind legs and reached out with his front paws, placing his weight on the door and with a subtle click, the door came ajar. That wasn’t possible, I had almost ripped the handle off forcing my way in there just a second before. I took a moment to consider; in truth I had been looking for a way to get out of it before I had even tried to open the door. Perhaps, I had imagined I had tried a lot harder than I really had, merely loosening it slightly, but there was no getting out of it now. Henry pushed his way into the room, leaving me alone with the cavernous black of the doorway.

 

 

I sat before the threshold giving my eyes a moment to adjust. The room was barren and could hardly be described any longer as a nursery. There were no toys of any kind, the wallpaper had been stripped away, and all furniture had been removed. The only remnant of what this space use to be was the crib in the centre, empty. I reached in for the light switch on the wall to my right. Lighting the place did little to bring it any comfort, the bulb was starting to go, much dimmer than the lights in the rest of the house, leaving shadows in the corners of the room. Laid next to the crib was Henry, a tragic scene to behold, I wondered what he understood of what had happened. I hurried to get it over with, I didn’t wish to be in this room any longer than I had to. I pulled the lyrics of the song up on my phone and began.

 

  


“Rock a bye baby, on the tree top,

When the wind blows the cradle will rock.

When the bough breaks the cradle will fall,

And down will come baby, cradle and-“

 

Suddenly, Henry rose and skittered from the room so fast he hit into to door on the way out, closing it behind him. I continued.

 

“Rock a bye baby, gently you swing,

Over the cradle, Mother will sing,

Sweet is the lullaby over your nest

That tenderly sings my baby to rest.”

 

A long winding groan rose from the pipes again shaking the room. Hesitantly, I continued.

 

“From the high rooftops, down to the sea

No one's as dear as baby to me

Wee little hands, eyes shiny and bright

Now sound asleep until morning light”

 

The light above began to flicker, about to die. Time to finish this up.



“Rock a bye baby, on the tree top,

When the wind blows the cradle will rock.

When the bough breaks the cradle will fall,

And down will come baby, cradle and all”

 

With a pop the light goes out casting the room in black. I fumble behind me for the door and grasping the handle I pull…

It doesn’t budge; I pull harder now. Nothing. With both hands, I clasp down the handle and yank my whole body back. Finally, the door flys open but sends me tumbling back towards the crib. I rise to my feet and scramble out of the room slamming the door behind. I don’t care anymore; I’m never doing that again. I step into my room and take a moment to compose myself.

 

 

After that scare, I had trouble getting to sleep that night. Just when I would be about to drift off, I’d hear some creak or groan from the house and shoot up. Looking at the door expectantly waiting for something to burst in. Eventually though my exhaustion took me.

 

 

When I awoke this morning, the terror of the night before was gone, seemingly washed away by the morning sun. I could now look back at the night objectively and conclude everything that had happened could be explained as symptoms of an old house. Moaning pipes, faulty lighting and a fidgety door were hardly out of the norm and were certainly not cause for alarm. I was in fact beginning to feel rather embarrassed by how I had reacted when I came down to the kitchen this morning. I had just made a bowl for Henry, when I decided to reconsult the chore list they had left me on the fridge to ensure I hadn’t missed anything.

 



*1.      Feed the dog*

Check.

*2.      Water the plants*

Check.

*3.      Take in the mail*

Check.

*4.      Sing to the crib*

Unfortunately, Check.

*5.      Lock the nursery door*

All at once, the terror of last night was fresh and sharp. Had it really said that? Surely not, after all I didn’t even have a key for the room. Cautiously, I crept up the stairs. This time Henry didn’t follow, electing instead to remain at the bottom of the steps, watching me ascend. With every step, the stairs would let out a monstrous creak, the rhythm did nothing but increase my anticipation. But when I finally came to the upstairs landing, breath trapped in my lungs, I found nothing. Staring down the right hallway I found that the nursery door was in fact still closed. Again, my fear gave way to embarrassment, this house was too big, an unruly beast playing tricks on my mind. I went to my room to get my phone, maybe hearing a familiar voice would help put me at ease. I took it from the bedside table and had begun to dial my mother when-

Sitting on the bedside table, next to where my phone had been, was a key. Attached to it a label said “**NURSERY**”. With it was another sticky note like the one on the fridge.

*Thank you for singing to me!*
