I am scared of circles.

I don’t know if there’s a phobia specifically related to the fear of circles, but I am scared of them. Mainly 3D-shaped spheres.

The first time I encountered that entity was three months ago.

An accident put me in a coma for two days.

When I woke up, I looked out through the hospital window. The morning sun was rising over the horizon, and the streets were empty, as they usually were on weekends.

Despite the sun being partially hidden beneath the horizon that day, I felt my eyes hurt much more than usual. I thought it was some medical equipment that had been left on in the dark, or perhaps a lamp had been turned on.

There was nothing but me and my IV machine in the room. No patient, no nurse, nobody. The flash was coming from outside.

I covered my eyes and tried to see through my limited vision to determine what the light was. I expected it to be nothing more than reflected sunlight from one of the glass windows of the skyscrapers.

At first, that’s what I thought when I saw the source of the light.

Except there were no orange-colored rays that usually appear when morning sunlight reflects through glass.

All I saw was pure white.

It was not a reflection of light.

It was light.

Its exact shape was uncertain, but it looked like an orb.

I thought I was dreaming at first. I was still comatose.

*What a strange dream,* I thought.

But the light ascended upward. Moreover, I could feel the pain from the accident that had gotten me hospitalized.

That was the final confirmation.

I wasn’t sleeping. This wasn’t a dream.

This was reality.

The light disappeared from my view as it ascended, but I could see it floating above the approximate location of the main road, higher than any of the skyscrapers. It looked like a sun shining down directly beneath the building.

But the actual sun?

That was literally on the horizon.

I had watched the sun rise from the horizon, and yet there was another ray of light coming from God knows where.

I opened the window and peeked outside, but the thing was no longer there.

When the doctors found out that I was awake, they told me about the accident and said it might have caused me to hallucinate. They said I needed to be monitored for at least another week.

So that was what I thought about the light: just a hallucination.

The rest of the day went smoothly. There were some check-ups, followed by visits from friends and family.

I remember that night.

A week later, it was foggy again.

I woke up to the sound of something, and the clock said twelve.

The fog consumed the street like some *Silent Hill* shit. Even though it was midnight, the road was not empty.

And within the fog was an illumination that made me think that whatever it was, it was too out of place and far too bright to be considered a streetlamp. There was no pole or structure attached to it.

It moved.

It floated slowly.

And when it reached my eye level, I remembered the light from a week ago: the strange orb of light, like a firefly.

But fireflies aren’t as big as a human face.

And they don’t -  they don’t make you feel as though they possess sapience.

The reason I knew that thing was not an ordinary sphere, or something ordinary in general, was because it was diffusing smoke toward the earth.

A bizarre imagination began to form. It turned into a hypothesis, and then into certainty:

This circle was the root of the fog in the city.

The same fog that had literally consumed nine blocks from my point of view was being diffused from this mysterious will-o’-the-wisp.

The sphere’s magic continued.

And this was definitely black magic.

Black magic indeed.

Something ascended toward the sphere.

A human figure.

No.

It was a human being.

The person floated upward, looking dazed - almost hypnotized. There was literally no mechanism beneath him. The man was wearing business attire: a black suit and black pants. He was levitating through the sky, toward me.

Toward the light.

When his face entered the bottom of the sphere, it did not come out from the other side.

Then his upper body.

Then his legs and feet.

Not a single part of the man came out.

What the sphere did next was flash a bright red light for half a second before entering my hospital ward.

There were no windows open or anything.

It passed through the wall and floated right in front of me.

I could barely keep my eyes open because of the bright light that consumed the room. I felt like screaming at the abnormality in front of me, but the light repeatedly flashed red in a slow beat.

In the beat of a heart.

Something told me that whatever the orb was communicating, it wanted - or at least expected - me to touch it.

I didn’t want to touch it.

But then again, I had this feeling that if I didn’t touch it, this thing would never leave me alone.

I didn’t know why I thought that way.

I just did.

As I slowly reached for the orb, I didn’t know what I expected.

What I felt was something - 

Otherworldly.

It was painful - very painful. I could feel my body on fire, but my hand, the part touching the orb, felt no pain. It was cold as stone.

What I also felt was something being jammed into my eyes. I was no longer in the dark hospital room. I was looking at what appeared to be a giant theater. Right in front of me was a movie screen.

That "giant screen" floated in the air, and it wasn't showing any movie. I was looking through the first-person view of a man who was vomiting near a car. The first-person view suddenly turned toward someone approaching, and the man ran. Perhaps it was the owner of the car.

The video then reflected an image of the orb in the sky - the same orb that had floated over the city. The person began to float and was pulled into the orb.

As soon as the video showed the man enter, I suddenly felt something hit the palm of my hand. It felt like some kind of object had been bashed against it. I subconsciously rested my arms on the chairs, and there was something on the chairs that had spawned there.

The cinema screen then showed me a fancy handwritten script.

Overseer chose: PASS JUDGEMENT

The screen shut off, leaving me in complete darkness. An instant later, I was back at the hospital, lying on the bed as if I had been there the entire time.

It was morning.

When I started to come to my senses, I thought again that I had been dreaming about something like that. Dreaming about the orb.

There was no sign that anything had entered the room. There was no sign of a break-in, and when I looked outside, the city was clear. There wasn't even any smoke coming from the exhaust pipes of the cars. The morning commute was clean.

The rest of the day, I was visited by some of my friends, including some shady friends - those who were bullies, but not really bullies.

After spending too much time in bed, I decided to go for a walk. The nurse gave me permission and also told me to return by one for lunch.

For the first time in a few days, I walked.

It was refreshing to begin walking instead of being confined to a bed. Now, I felt like a human being again. My destination was the indoor garden, where I was going to sit and look at pieces of Mother Nature that I wouldn't be able to see for another week.

I didn't like the experience. Instead of peace and quiet, a blabbering brat had made the place loud and noisy. I cut twenty minutes from my break and started heading back to the ward.

As I passed the reception, my vision caught glimpses of the television, and I had to stop again.

Not because I was tired or because my legs hurt.

Because on the television was the headline:

VANDAL MISSING

The footage showed the mugshot of the same man I had seen on the screen, the man who had been referred to as Lenny.

And this "Lenny" had disappeared.

It was the same man I had seen in my dreams.

But I had never seen him before.

How could I have dreamed about that man?

Or was the entire ordeal inside the orb real?

And did I do something?

I sat down to think, and then I remembered that in that memory fragment of what happened inside the orb, I had pressed "Pass Judgement."

Whatever the case was, suddenly, it was freaking me out.

This "Lenny" who had disappeared and the "Lenny" who had appeared in my dreams... Something told me that, for some reason, it wasn't a coincidence.

Deep down, I felt an unnatural sense of fear.

And then I remembered the button.

The screen.

As I walked tiredly back to the ward, I remembered what had been giving me that sense of unnatural fear.

It was the sphere.

The sphere that had visited me in my dreams.

The thing that had sucked me in.

When I returned to the ward, I couldn't find any traces of the thing actually being there and visiting me. Not to mention that, in my dream, the thing had been floating.

But the circle - the orb that I had believed was part of a dream?

I didn't think it was.

It was not a dream.

I had actually been transported somewhere between night and morning that day.

In reality...

These questions began to fill me with an unnatural fear.

Vandal Missing.

The screen showing the vandal's crimes.

The button labeled "Pass Judgement."

And if that wasn't a dream - and if I had pressed the button - what did I do?

I spent most of my time after that day, besides eating, recovering, and getting tested, heading to the computer lab that the hospital provided for patients and visitors. I tried to find information about the eldritch being I had seen.

I searched various possible combinations of words to describe what I had seen. I'm pretty sure the next user was probably confused when they saw a search history containing words like "black hole," "orb that sucks people," "bright black hole," "judgement orb," and basically anything else related to the word "orb."

Nothing came up.

Not even a single thing.

The next thing I searched for on the internet was the name of the guy who had disappeared.

This time, I got a lot of reports saying that he was still on the run, that the police were conducting a manhunt, and, of course, considering what had happened to him...

I felt like the cops would never capture or find this Lenny.

Eventually, I gave up and decided that this wasn't a big deal.

But it was a big deal.

I may have done something and broken the law, if that's even how you could describe my actions.

Was it even breaking the law?

The question I had been asking ever since I gave up wasn't "What was the orb?"

It was:

"What did I do?"

Because the next week, I saw that thing again.

Two days before I saw the orb, a new patient entered the ward.

A guy named James.

The ward was later accompanied by a policeman and a doctor outside, watching guard.

It turned out that this man I was hospitalized with was a thief who had been shot in the leg during a robbery. He would be transferred once he was fully healed.

That was what James told me.

What the cop added was that James had also been caught stealing a lot of wallets, making him a repeat offender.

Knowing that he was an unlikable figure, we both began to talk less frequently.

That night, I heard a thud that woke me from an uneasy sleep.

While my eyes were closed, I was conscious. The door outside didn't open at the sound of the thud.

I guessed the cop was either asleep or didn't think there was any need to guard the place since James was injured and wouldn't be able to escape for another few days.

I looked to see what the sound was and saw the orb approaching me again.

James was gone.

This time, I gave myself a reality check.

That was when I lost any remaining belief that this was all a dream.

The fear that this thing was in front of me continued to grow, but the orb approached me anyway.

I got up, walked toward the door, and thought about opening it.

Before I reached the handle, something made me think:

What would happen if I kept running away from it?

Would this thing chase me for the rest of the week?

The mysterious nature of the orb made me have weird and bizarre imaginations despite knowing nothing about it.

The most terrifying conclusion was the fear that the orb would suck me in and put me on trial for refusing to do whatever this "judge others and choose their fate" thing was telling me to do.

I let the orb suck me in.

I prepared for anything.

Whatever happened in there was beyond anything someone could imagine.

I mean, who would have honestly imagined that there was some dimension where I got to choose who gets judged?

The screen was the same.

The chairs were the same.

And this time, I kept my hands together before I pressed the buttons that had spawned for some reason.

They appeared.

And this was the first time I saw the full view of what the buttons looked like.

There was a blue button on the left that said:

SHOW MERCY

The red button on the right said:

PASS JUDGEMENT

This time, I took the chance to explore the theatre, wondering just what the true nature of this place was. This was definitely not a dream; that was one thing I was certain of now. But I kept wondering: just what was this place, how did it even work, and why did it exist?

I turned around. There were no chairs, just my chair. There was no projector, nothing that could be used to bring movies to the screen.

There was a door leading out, but it was locked. I expected that.

Just me, a single chair with buttons, and the video, which began to play when I turned back.

Immediately, I guessed this was the POV of James. He had beaten up a man in the street and grabbed his wallet. The shouts of "Get back!" and "Stop him!" grew fainter as they got further away, accompanied by the grunts of passersby who had witnessed James mug a man.

I approached the chair and looked at it. Mercy and Judgement. And then I looked at the wrongdoings of James.

I started to think: show judgement for his crimes...

Then that's when I remembered the Missing Vandal, whom I had pressed Judgement for, and who was said to have disappeared. It was possible that he had gone into hiding, but something told me that wasn't the case.

By pressing Judgement, I may have actually made him disappear from - probably - the face of the world.

If that's the case, thinking about what happened with the vandal, if I chose Judgement, it felt like I might be saying "yes" to the question, "Should every single robber in the world be sentenced to disappear from the world?"

If - if that really is what Judgement does.

Minutes passed, maybe - but considering the last time I entered the orb was at night, and that I spent probably ten minutes at most before returning to the real world, where it was already morning, I guess the right term would be "hours passed."

If an hour was a minute, then I had already spent seven hours here.

The thought disturbed me.

Seven hours of absence from the world. The hospital would be in a huge panic.

I sat down and pressed the "Mercy" button, and when I popped out of the orb, it was morning.

My curtains were closed, and outside, I could hear a commotion - a scream.

It was James, and a man who I guessed was the security guard. While I didn't know the whole context, the words James said were, "Something is with me," and "I've been tortured." The security guard was cursing as he tried to calm the living daylights out of James.

Through the curtain, I saw James. He did look normal, with no physical scars or anything that proved he may have been hurt physically. But he looked very frightened of something. He was looking around the room like crazy, as if he were surrounded by something we couldn't see.

His eyes, his body - every part of him was shaking with fear.

It was enough to make me think that this guy was going to have a heart attack soon and likely die.

He was dragged out. The nurse asked if I was okay. I replied yes.

I wasn't.

Honestly, I didn't know what to feel. On one hand, I still had doubts about whether what I did in the orb was what caused James to go crazy. On the other hand, I still didn't believe that the orb was capable of doing such things.

My seven hours of disappearance were not questioned, as nobody was allowed to enter the ward during the night, but I think that also had something to do with the influence of the orb.

The nurses who had monitored me for a week let me go that evening.

As a celebration of my discharge, my family and I had dinner at a pub, where we sat down near a TV showing the news.

The very first news report discussed the incident at the hospital. It all led to the sudden insanity and paranoia of James, and all of this was blamed on the police for "neglecting a mentally ill person's condition, which led to the disturbance of another patient's stay."

That other patient was me, who was just as confused as the rest of the police.

My family seemed not to know that it was me they were talking about.

It may not have ruined my stay, but it did ruin my appetite.

The orb.

Yes - the orb was what was making me think strangely right now.

The main thing that creeped me out was how the orb made me play God.

I ain't one.

I am just a regular student with regular human physics...

I am not a god.

And I don't want to be such a god who is forced to do whatever this orb says.

People sometimes say that when strange things happen to someone that are beyond human understanding, it might be a divine sign - that the person was gifted something by a god to do something that others cannot do and would envy.

I don't think that's the case.

The next few days were a mix of my daily routine and me trying to find whatever information I could about this "orb." Not just online, either. I practically travelled to every single bookstore, library, and even second-hand store, hoping to find anything that could answer my questions:

What is the orb?

Why was I chosen?

How do I even escape this?

And if I do, what are the consequences?

The obvious outcome of my search was that I hadn't gotten a single answer.

Well, I tried one day.

Escape from the orb.

It was around eleven on the weekend. I was coming back from the nightclub after a campus club activity when the orb appeared on the street leading towards my house.

I tried this time to walk away from the orb and take an alternate route. When I returned home, I figured the orb was no longer any of my problems.

That was when I entered my room and saw a circle of light floating outside my window.

You know what I mean.

It was outside my window.

It was persistent. It angered me and scared me at the same time, mainly because I never wanted this, and I still don't want to play God.

My neighbour came out for some reason, and I expected that he wouldn't see the orb.

But he also stopped where the orb was.

I realised that he was seeing what I was seeing.

We both looked at each other before I heard a muffled scream coming from my neighbour.

He was being dragged inside the orb.

That was what I saw.

For the first time, I encountered this - the orb dragging someone inside.

It looked to me as if my neighbour was being melted. At the sight of someone I was familiar with getting sucked into the orb, I ran away from home.

I could hear my parents wondering if the running was coming from me, but I guess they didn't see me.

Thanks a lot for the reminder, mate.

I turned around to see if the orb was chasing me.

There was pitch blackness, with only the light of the streetlamps, which was small, and I knew it was definitely not the orb.

Sighing in relief, I was about to call my family and tell them that I was staying in a hotel for the day.

A thought about the orb - and the concepts and behaviour surrounding it.

The all-consuming orb.

That this shit would never - and I mean never - stop.
