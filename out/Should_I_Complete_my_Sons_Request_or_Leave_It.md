I am a simple man. I stay safe in my Arkansas neighborhood (if you aren't living in this Arkansas neighborhood, I don't think you're living a safe life). I work a 9-5 job. I eat dinner every day with my wife and children, and I use Colgate for toothpaste. Get the idea?

I'm fairly happy with my life, okay? Sure, there are a few imperfections here and there. I've been challenged at work lately, and my father is currently in hospice care. Yeah, I'm not a big fan of my father. I wouldn't care much if he died. He wouldn't care for mine, after all.

But do you know the secret to our happiness?

My wife and I generally avoid the media. As a matter of fact, I haven't read anything on r/nosleep, but I know it's the right sub for me to post. So, sorry if things aren't 'typical' with this post as they should be, but I've read all the guidelines. Line-by-line. Word-by-word. Letter-by-letter.

I also don't allow my children to watch TV. It may expose our children to the 'horrid lifestyle' and will infect them with the irreversible despair of the non-Arkansas lifestyle. I've never watched TV myself, and I'd rather not.

Our children are homeschooled as well.

But today, my son did it. He watched TV. He defied my expectations today.

He went to a friend's house, even though we'd explicitly told him not to go anywhere outside the lawn, or to talk or listen to strangers, but he did anyway.

I didn't know why he would do such a thing, but I read about it in our home library. Ancestral instincts, they call it. Ancestral instincts to escape your home during puberty.

Because it only happened a few hours ago, I remember it all clearly. It was noon, and my wife was making crème brûlée. I was in my study. No one noticed our son was gone until he came home, dirt covering his cheeks and hands. Yet, I said nothing about his hygiene. Do you have any idea of where he caught that dirt from?

My son came into my study, his hands leaning against the edge of my desk.

According to him, he didn't do much: "Just went to the neighbor's house. We made some popcorn and watched some TV."

"TV, son? TV? I thought you knew better than that," I replied."Grounded."

"Wait! Not just yet!" he exclaimed. "I have something to tell you. I saw something while we watched, and I got an idea! Please? Can I say it first? Ground me later, but I must tell you something-"

"What?"

"I learned how to predict the future. It's easy. Real easy. Anyone can do it."

I shrugged. "All this mumbo jumbo is for the people not in Arkansas. I told you not to watch TV."

"The cat will die tomorrow," he said. "I can predict unfortunate events. I can predict death, injury, and natural disasters, but I can't guess anything fortunate. So don't go on asking me to bet on horses, but you can ask me when grandpa's going to take his final breath."

"That's enough TV for you!" I said. "Look, the side effects are already showing!"

"I know how to save your dad, though," he said.

My son. My twelve-year-old son. What did he learn from TV? Now I'm never going to even drag my face towards a big black screen.

Even though Reddit is media too, I'll still post here, but I won't read anything except for the replies underneath, because I'm desperate to get the answer to the next sentences he spoke. But promise. I won't consume any strange media after that. Promise. If you're kind, you'll help me keep it.

"Don't you want to save your dad?" he said, and I remained motionless. Oh, it'll pass, I thought. The effects of TV will wear off hopefully, right?

"Don't you want to save Grandpa?"

"Fine," I said. "Tell me. What're your TV plans?"

His hands started to shake.

"Tie my hands," he said. "Tie my hands behind me. Tie my hands behind me for five years, and Grandpa's life will be prolonged another twenty. Tie them tightly. Tie them as tightly as you can."

"No," I said. "Enough of this TV. You're never watching it again in your life. Not again, no. It will not happen again. Sure. Let my grandpa die. God has plans, and he will die when he needs to."

"If God has plans," I said. "Then God also planned for me to have my hands tied behind me and to save Grandpa."

"That's enough," I said as my son walked outside my room. "You know what? You're grounded for a month. No more going outside."

I slammed the door upon him, the dusty crust of it probably falling upon my son. But it's okay. He had enough mud on his face already.

"I want to save grandpa!" he exclaimed. "Get me a rope, but you'll have to do the tying."

Yesterday, my son and I were playing in the yard. I tossed the ball, and he tossed the ball. And we talked about how long we could play catch without one of us dropping the ball. How wholesome, you might think. And today, ever since he watched just thirty minutes of TV, his perspective of life has already become so macabre.

I know the average non-Arkansas resident will think I'm overreacting, but you guys don't react enough. You guys witness horrors every day. Trash on the streets. Gossip ruining people's entire lives. And yet, you act as if it's alright. You guys keep going on with your day.

Maybe I shouldn't post here at all. It'll be of no use. You're going to think I'm complaining about nothing compared to what you guys witness daily. But, if I do get a reply, thank you in advance.

Should I complete my son's request?
