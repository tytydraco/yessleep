I remember it vividly; it was a Tuesday. My parents had gone on one of their date nights, effectively leaving the house all to me. My name is Tyler. I'm nineteen, and I'm still home alone even now. I don't really know what happened to my parents after I got that alert a few days ago; maybe they got somewhere safe.

I know what I told you just now was a lot. I should probably explain what the hell I'm talking about. After my parents left for the date night I told you about, I immediately booted up my computer. I was home from college on winter break, so I didn't mind staying up late to play with friends since I really had nothing else going on. My phone sat face up on my nightstand, meaning I had my back to it while sitting at my desk. I was playing a zombie game called Left 4 Dead, I think, with a couple of buddies from school when I heard something just beyond my headphones. It sounded like an alarm, and for the first thirty seconds, I ignored it, thinking it was just something from the game. The shrill pitch continued, and finally, I pulled my headset off, quickly realizing that it was, in fact, my phone going off with some kind of EAS alert from its perch on the nightstand.

I grabbed it, but reading the text, I quickly realized the system must have been hijacked or had a malfunction or something. It said: "The moon opened its eyes. Avoid eye contact. Avoid any individuals who have made contact or have recently been outside for an extended period. Ignore all voices, even those of loved ones. Close and lock all entrances that lead to the outside." No way, surely it was a prank. It had to be. That was my first thought reading the bold white text currently taking over the entirety of my phone screen. I didn't understand, even if they had been taken over by some asshole trying to be funny. How did they come up with something so specific? I'm no genius, but if I had been in their position, I wouldn't have ever come up with something so elaborate. However, even with the thoughts going around my head, I decided I should go along with it, even if I didn't know how to feel about its authenticity.

I informed my friends of what had happened and turned off my computer before I could hear their opinions and swiftly went around the house. I pulled curtains back and made sure all the windows were locked, upstairs and downstairs, before I pulled the cloth back into place, putting the two sides of the curtain together as tight as possible without ripping the rod off the wall. I panicked for some reason. I mean, here I was saying that I didn’t even believe this stupid alert but was running around my house like some kind of doomsday lunatic. I clicked the locks on all the doors, back door and front, before sliding the deadbolts on both into place.

I pulled my phone off the charger once I was done checking the entrances. I remembered my parents. Damn it, my parents, my parents were still out doing whatever and I had no clue where they were. I pressed my mother’s contact hard enough I thought I might crack my screen even through my screen protector. It rang once, twice, three times before I quickly hung up. I tried not to panic, I did, but now it seemed real to me. Like maybe somehow this alert was real and I was not only home alone, but I was alone with whatever was going on outside and I had no clue where my Mom and Dad were.

I was on the verge of an anxiety attack at my current circumstances, but before I could get any further, I heard it: a knock on my front door. Not like someone who was in danger or caught outside, no, it was light, intentional, like whatever or whoever was currently standing outside was just a neighbor or something trying to introduce themselves after moving in. I stopped. I don’t know what in my body caused it, but I got a feeling, and I dropped to the floor and scurried behind my couch. A voice quickly came from the thing standing outside the door: “Hello, are you in there?”

I stilled. I didn’t know what to do. I was tempted to respond, I can't explain why, but by some miracle I held my tongue, letting the words from outside settle like heavy fog. I replayed them in my head and couldn’t wrap my mind around something. Why had the voice sounded so wrong? Like if someone recorded themselves and distorted it ever so slightly, in a way you can tell it wasn’t truly human but it was close enough to not understand why it wasn’t. The voice returned, seeming to catch on that I wasn’t falling for whatever game it was trying to play: “Open the door, the moon is beautiful tonight, you need to look.”

That was two days ago. Now I’m sitting in my room. I used nails from the garage to hang a thick blanket over my window. The voice gave up a couple of hours after it spoke to me the first time, seeming to realize either someone wasn’t home or they weren’t interested in what it had to say. It hasn’t been back since. Not that I’m complaining, but that also means that I haven’t had human interaction in almost three days. I always preferred silence but now, I think that was because I had never experienced true, deep silence until right now, and I'll be honest, I hate it. I wouldn’t go outside; I had decided that yesterday. I’ll last if I can in here. I’ll let myself starve before I open that damn door. I had no idea what was going on. Is it global, or just my town? I didn’t know. The same alert had been playing on repeat the last two days with no new updates—that was almost as bad as the silence.

If anyone else is going through this or got the alert, please let me know. Let me know how you're doing. I just want someone else to tell me I’m not crazy. I’ll take any advice you can give me. I'll update you all soon if I’m still around.
