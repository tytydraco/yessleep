It was 2005.

I was still a kid back then, and my family and I had decided to take a short vacation to a small town not too far from our city.

It was just going to be three days.

My mom, my dad, my sister, and I packed our things and got everything ready. Before leaving, we made sure the house was secure. The lights were turned off, the doors and windows were locked, and the alarm system was set.

We also had to make sure our pets would be okay while we were gone.

We had three little fish that lived in a tank in my mom's room.

We had a mixed-breed poodle that stayed in the backyard.

And then there was our rabbit.

We had found him near a large open field outside the city. I don't remember exactly how he ended up there, but we decided to take him home.

He had become part of the family.

We kept him in a large box outside, on the second floor of our house. It was big enough for him to move around comfortably, and we made sure he had plenty of food and water before leaving.

I remember looking at him one last time before we left.

He was just sitting there quietly.

I never imagined that would be the last time I'd see him alive.

We checked everything one more time.

The doors were locked.

The alarm was on.

The pets had food.

Our truck was in perfect condition.

Everything was fine.

So we left.

The trip itself was wonderful.

We stayed with family and I spent most of the time playing with my cousins. We ate together, talked, laughed, and did all the things you do during a family vacation.

For three days, nothing happened.

Eventually, it was time to go home.

We said goodbye to our relatives and got into the truck.

I remember staring through the window as we drove along the highway.

Trees passed by one after another.

I was tired, but I kept thinking about everything we'd done during the trip.

I remember feeling happy.

Then I fell asleep.

When I opened my eyes again, we were already home.

The first thing I heard was our dog barking.

He recognized the sound of our truck and the gate opening. He was jumping around excitedly, making it obvious that he knew we were back.

We got out of the truck.

My mom.

My dad.

My sister.

And me.

Everything looked normal.

The house looked exactly the way we had left it.

Nothing appeared to be out of place.

The alarm had worked.

The doors were still locked.

There were no broken windows.

Nothing seemed wrong.

Our dog was perfectly fine.

We went upstairs to check on the fish.

They were alive and swimming around normally.

My sister and I were happy to see them.

For a moment, everything felt completely normal.

Then my mom said we should check on the rabbit.

We walked toward the door leading outside.

I remember my mom reaching for the handle.

Then she stopped.

I don't know why.

She just stood there for a few seconds.

Then she opened the door.

And everything changed.

I don't remember exactly what I said.

I don't remember what my sister said either.

I just remember screaming.

My mom immediately tried to block our view.

But it was too late.

We had already seen him.

Our rabbit was dead.

Not just dead.

He had been completely torn apart.

There was blood all over the floor.

His body had been ripped open.

His intestines and other organs were scattered around him.

His heart was lying nearby.

The smell hit us almost immediately.

It was horrible.

I remember covering my mouth because I thought I was going to throw up.

My sister started crying.

Her face had gone completely pale.

My mom looked like she couldn't understand what she was seeing.

None of us could.

We had left him there three days earlier.

He had been alive.

He had food.

He had water.

The box was outside, on the second floor.

And the house had been locked the entire time.

So what happened?

The first thing we thought of was a cat.

Maybe one had somehow gotten inside.

But that didn't make sense.

The box was high enough that it would have been difficult for a cat to get into.

And even if a cat had somehow reached him, why was the rabbit's body torn apart so violently?

My dad inspected the area.

There were no obvious signs of forced entry.

No broken doors.

No broken windows.

Nothing had been disturbed.

There wasn't even anything obvious that could explain how another animal had gotten upstairs.

Then another possibility crossed our minds.

Maybe someone had done it.

Maybe a person had somehow gotten into the house.

That thought was even worse.

Because if a person had entered our house while we were away, they could have done anything.

But again, there were no signs of a break-in.

Nothing had been stolen.

Nothing else had been damaged.

Only the rabbit.

My sister asked the question none of us wanted to answer.

“What did this?”

Nobody said anything.

For a long time, I tried to convince myself that there had to be a normal explanation.

Maybe it was an animal.

Maybe something had gotten into the house through a place we hadn't noticed.

Maybe we simply hadn't seen the evidence.

I was a kid.

I wanted to believe there was an explanation.

Something that made sense.

Something that could be forgotten.

But there was one detail that always bothered me.

The rabbit wasn't simply dead.

Something had done this to him.

It had torn him open.

And whatever had done it had left almost everything else untouched.

The fish were fine.

The dog was fine.

The house was fine.

Nothing was missing.

Nothing had been broken.

Only the rabbit was destroyed.

To this day, I don't know what killed him.

Maybe it really was an animal.

Maybe someone got into our house.

Maybe there was another explanation that we never found.

But there is one thing I remember that still bothers me.

After everything was cleaned up, my dad went outside to check the area around the house.

He came back a few minutes later.

He didn't say anything at first.

Then he asked us a strange question.

“Did anyone leave the back door open?”

We told him no.

It had been locked.

He nodded.

Then he said something that I didn't understand at the time.

“There are scratches on the other side of the door.”

I asked him what he meant.

He wouldn't explain.

Years later, I finally asked him about it.

He told me there had been several deep scratches on the inside of the door.

Not the outside.

The inside.

As if something had been trying to get out.

I still don't know what killed our rabbit.

But sometimes, when I remember that vacation, I think about those scratches.

And I wonder if we were really alone in that house while we were gone.

Because if something had gotten inside...

I don't think it came in through the door.
