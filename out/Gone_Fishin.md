Hello. I’m leaving this post here as a warning to anyone who may read it. Whether you believe the words in this letter is entirely up to you of course, but you should know that I will and have staked everything on these words to prevent anyone else from losing someone they love. I lost my brother. That was 15 years ago now, to the day. If you ask anyone else in my family, no one remembers him. I must’ve been singled out, because I was the only one there that day. The day I watched my brother touch the sky.

Fifteen years ago I was 13, my brother (Carter) was 16, and we lived on your standard country property out in Kansas. A large metal green gate blocked off road access to a long gravel drive way. A few sheds were planted on the property, their contents unimportant. The electric fence ran through the interior of the property, sectioning off where the animals were allowed to roam. The house sat right in the middle of the property, painted the ugliest green I’ve ever seen. Out back was the pond. It sat lower on the property to collect any rain water we might get. Finally was the tree.   


Now the pond might've only been 30 or so yards across, but it was still surrounded by messy stacks of reeds that grew off the bank like grandad’s nose hairs. On top was a bunch of lily pads, soaking in the sun like my sister does on her days off. The real center piece though was the tree. This towering stack of willow wood hung over the top of the water, its long drooping branches reaching down to caress the stillness away. The little oblong leaves would spin down and smack the water, and every time you could see a little fish pop up to nibble, hoping for a bug.   


Carter and I used to help pop out around the farm, carrying the feed bags, letting the animals out, collecting the eggs, milking the goat, usual stuff. No matter what, Carter never failed to give me a tough time, calling it, “Brotherly love.” Picking smashed eggs out of my hair, catching the metal end of a cattle prong, or finding myself being tossed into the pond became a daily nuisance. I tried to tell mama about what he was doing to me, but she said that we needed to get along better, cause we were brothers after all. I was sick of it, the idea had crossed my head once or twice, that I’d probably be better off without a brother.   


The pond that afternoon was rippling like the lord was smacking the waters edge with a drum stick. Nature's snare drum playing a lovely tempo, as the whistling through the trees joined in. The breeze brought the long lay lines of the willow down to earth, its strips of branches cascading across the water's edge creating a curtain of greenery. I liked to imagine that that is what a window into the sky would look like. I could imagine stepping through and flying amongst the birds, away from here. Anywhere else as long as there was supple ripened fruit to pluck from the branches and crystal clear water to wash it down. I could climb with the squirrels and run with the foxes, anything would’ve been better than being a farm hand.  


After a day of chores, my favorite thing to do was sit along the edge of the pond. I would squish my toes into the mud below the surface and catch little frogs that’d jump along the bank. Carter also enjoyed the pond, but his favorite thing to do was fish. He’d sit out there and cast the line for hours after the sun went down, reeling them in and letting them go. I never had the patience, I could've sworn he had caught every fish in that pond at least twice by now, probably named them all too. I know I would’ve.  


One day we went out to spend our time at the pond, the wind was blowing something fierce that day. Fallen leaves were being kicked up and dragged across the water, lawn chairs were toppling over and racing each other across the yard. The branches of that willow tree were streaking across the open summer air. I was with Carter, we had just spent the last several hours guiding a bunch of cattle to another lot, so we were pretty exhausted. That’s when we saw it.  


As we walked up to that sturdy aging willow tree, the wind suddenly died down. The long lace tendrils gradually slowed until they became completely still. So still, that I could’ve sworn it was a picture, the way nature played along with this little joke. This picture would’ve been different though, because connected to the tree was something that wasn’t there the day before. An old weathered hempen rope was wrapped around the thickest branch of that tree, it dangled taught, carrying the weight of a large rubber tire. We hadn’t seen a swing out here on the farm before. In fact, if you would've asked us if we wanted one, I’d probably have told you that swings were for babies. Maybe that’s why pop installed it without telling us first, because if he did we might’ve complained? Oh well, it’s here now, and it ain’t gonna swing itself.  


Carter and I spent hours on that old tire swing. We would launch ourselves off of it and into the water over and over again. The sun had started wrapping its warm tender fingers around the horizon, when we heard mama call from the window. “Ya’ll boys get back on inside now, time for supper!” I looked over to Carter who desperately wanted to do one more swing into the cool summer water before we called it a night. “I got to get even higher, gimme a push would you?” He asked as he climbed back onto the rustic round of rubber. I obliged, and grabbed a hold of the back with both hands. Carter was also taking it seriously, because he wrapped both feet around the top and tucked them into the inner groove, then gripped the rope with both hands. This was gonna be the best one yet.  


I started by leaning backwards, and then forwards, grabbing some easy momentum. Once the swing really got going, Carter and I synchronized our movements together to get every inch of go-go we could muster. Then I started counting,  
“One…”  
I stood on my tippy toes now, bringing the swing as far forwards as I could reach. Carter was too excited to see the result, his knuckles around the rope reminded me of the day paw caught that snake in the chicken coop.  
‘Two…”  
The air still laid completely still, not even a breeze had interrupted us while we played. That should’ve been the warning sign, but we were kids. Carter had the better chance of understanding, he was the one who would fished. How were we supposed to know that when you tugged on a bobber, the surface would ripple above.  
“Three…”  
As the tire reached its backwards apex, the combined strength of the two of us were going to send my brother across the pond. That was the plan at least, until I looked down and realized my feet were no longer touching the ground.  


Carter was just as confused as I was, we had been expecting to move forward, but instead were being reeled directly into the sky. The tire swing we had been swinging on had never been connected to the tree, it had been suspended next to it by some unseen force in the clouds above. By the time I understood what was going on, we were already 70 feet off the ground and there was only one thing I could do. Jump. I looked up to Carter and in the briefest of understanding nods, we knew the plan.  


My knuckles shot from white to red as all the blood flew back into my finger tips as I let go of the tire, then came crashing down into the lily pads below. Water shot up my nostrils and my eyes began to burn after the pond treated my sinuses like a speed bag. The sudden shock forced me to sharply inhale, pulling in a bit of muddy water forcefully down into my lungs. My toes hit the bottom of the pond, which I’m glad they did, because without that sense of direction, I might’ve swam down instead. When my head finally broke the surface, I began coughing up a storm. Bits of mud splashed back into the water as I took in my surroundings. The shimmering moon of twilight was now bouncing off the rippling surface of the pond when I came to the realization that I didn’t see Carter.  
Was he still under? I started feeling around with my hands and feet, seeing if I could touch his body anywhere in the water. My foolish task quickly came to an end when I realized the reason I couldn’t feel Carter was because I could still hear him. His scream got more distant as he continued to be pulled into the clouds, hanging upside down, one leg still stuck in the tire. I desperately scrambled to think of anything I could do to solve the problem, but it was too late. The brother I had wished away, had been taken away.  


I climbed out of the water and ran into the house, mud soaking into the floor with every step. I tried to cry out, “Mama, mama, Carter got pulled into the sky,” and was greeted with a wooden spoon to the back of the hand. “No son of mine is going to track mud into this house. I don’t know who this Carter is, but you’re going to clean up this mess then wash up for supper.” I begged and I pleaded, but the more I spoke, the more crazy looks I got from everyone around the farm. No one knew who my brother was, but me.

I don’t know what that tire truly was, but after doing some more research over the years, I have discovered that Carter wasn’t the only case. Multiple disappearances witnessed by individuals for people that I couldn’t find any records of ever existing. Claims that these people were dragged into the sky while hanging onto swings, ropes, chains, and other sorts of things that weren't there before. This guy in Montana says he watched his cousin float off into the clouds after flopping himself onto an innertube. It’s like their brothers and sisters were plucked from reality and never tossed back. I just pray that this story will give someone the insight to protect someone they care about, and not make a dumb mistake like me. Cause here I am, 15 years to the day, and I can see a tire swing in my backyard that I don’t remember hangin’.
