As a veterinarian, I've seen all sorts of things.

Countless dogs that ate their favourite chew toys, parrots that plucked out their feathers and injured cats that had fought with strays.

Hell, my first day on the job had me shoving my hand down some poor python's throat because it had eaten a TV remote.

Pets are different from farm animals, at least in my experience. Cats and dogs can get horrible illnesses, but livestock diseases are a whole different beast. Mad Cow Disease, Footrot and Whipworm are some just to name a few, but most illnesses these animals get are far more violent than what the average tabby gets.

That's why, when I heard what a local farm found one morning, I shrugged it off.

When I saw it, however, it *confused* me.

I set off early that morning with my best friend, 'Robyn'. We had been friends for years, and only recently we had decided to start a veterinary practice after the one we previously worked at went defunct. It took a while for us to get the funds, but we got there.

The desperation in the man's voice persuaded Robyn to come with me. It was his wife's favourite cow, and if anything were to happen to it, the consequences would be devastating. You get the picture. He needed an expert, and one wasn't enough.

When we reached the farm, we followed the farmer out to a southern field away from the house. I drove past the farmer's house often, as it was on route to the town's nearby woodland. It was my thinking place, so I'd give the guy a wave every now and then when I saw him.

"So," I rubbed my forehead, bewildered, "you found it like this?"

"*Her*." The farmer mumbled under his breath.

"What?"

"She's a girl," He took a drag of the cigarette and sighed, "Rosie."

I licked my lips and pulled my gloves on. "Any previous illnesses?"

He shook his head.

The cow was nearly dead. Flies buzzed and wobbled around her head, suckling at her taut white fur. She was laid on her side, legs slowly pedalling air like she was trying to walk. It looked like a parasite had infected her.

"Alright, Rosie," I cooed, stroking her head, "let's have a look, eh?"

I felt her torso and checked for bloating. I barely pushed down on the tight flesh, expecting a bulge from one of her stomachs. Instead, my hand fell though Rosie's side, a rancid squelch erupted from the carcass. Her muscles felt like wet paper, and I felt the cold mush cover my gloved hand, making me shiver with disgust.

"The hell?" The farmer stumbled backwards.

"Christ!" Robyn exclaimed, shock evident in her voice.

I yanked my hand out of Rosie and stared at the animal. She let out a weak moan before she slowed her legs to a stop.

"Flashlight." I waved my hand at Robyn.

She quickly gave it to me.

With my dirty gloved hand, I stretched the hole I made as wide as I could, then lit the opening up with the flashlight.

My stomach dropped in an instant.

Deep down in the hole, the organs of the animal were gone. No stomachs, no intestines, nothing. I searched for her ribs and spine, only to find nothing but rosy muscle. A grimy black slime coated the inside of her back.

The cow was completely empty.

"*What?*" I fell backwards, baffled by the sight.

"How is this-" The farmer began. His eyes were bulging, flicking from the hole to Rosie's lifeless face.

Robyn squatted next to me. "What the hell is this?"

"I haven't got a clue," I peeled off the gloves and looked up at the farmer, "I'm sorry."

He was silent, ogling at his animal.

"Sir?" I asked.

He stuttered, whispering under his breath.

"Sir?" Robyn rocked his shoulder gently, "this could be a case of scavengers," she tried to reassure him, "it can happen-"

"Where is it?" he swallowed thickly.

"Where's what?" Robyn spoke softly, like soothing a wounded animal.

What he told us next still makes me sick.

"Her calf," the farmer mumbled, "she was about to give birth."

We told the farmer it seemed like a parasite had rendered her immobile and scavengers had eaten the insides along with the calf. It was such a shitty excuse, but what else could we say? We didn't even know why she was like that ourselves.

He didn't even react. He just stood there, speechless.

Only when we finished for the day, we talked about it.

"That was impossible." Robyn huffed, her eyes bouncing from one corner of the car to the other.

"No shit," I sipped on my flaky paper straw. A dribble of coke came through.

I watched her think. She was drumming her fingers on the dashboard.

"What could it be though?" I shrugged.

Robyn was quiet for a moment.

"Aliens."

I paused and gave her a look. "Aliens?"

She nodded slowly.

Robyn, like most academic individuals, was a massive geek. She was one of those people that had a random hyper-fixation on a topic or event, like Titanic nerds. Her special fascination was aliens. Ever since I met her, she was the type of girl to fantasise about worlds beyond our own and to search the stars for other life. On the first day of college, she had already plastered a decades-old poster of *Aliens* on her wall, and was attempting to stick up another poster of *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* on mine. I still remember her freezing when she saw me, lips drawn into a thin line and her arms quivering as she held her pose. Such a dweeb.

Whenever she saw something slightly out of the ordinary, it was always aliens.

So, I responded how I usually did - flippantly.

"*Right*, and that horse we saw was a unicorn too-"

"I mean think about it," she waved a fry around, "a cow, which was due to give birth any day, has nothing inside of it, no bones, no organs, no calf," she popped the fry into her mouth with a soggy squish, "*it's gotta be*."

"There weren't any entry points either." I mused.

"Exactly!" she shot up in her seat. "E.T gone rogue!"

"Shut up," I pushed her shoulder, "don't be stupid."

"I'm not," Robyn pushed me back, "it's first contact."

"And first contact is us?"

She gave a nonchalant shrug before digging into her food bag for the escaped fries. *What specimens we were*.

When I got home, I sat in the shower for ages. *Paper-thin skin and no innards? She was pregnant*? It simply wasn't possible.

That night I didn't sleep too well. Rosie's empty corpse just played over and over in my head, how easily her skin gave way, her glassy eyes finally glazing over after my hand fell through her torso.

I headed to work the next morning, tired.

I went straight to Robyn's 'office', gently rapping on her door with a hint of anxiety. It was more of a broom cupboard, stuffed away in the back corner of the dingy building. We had 5 rooms in the place, and they weren't much bigger, with the main examination room taking up most of the space. Robyn had the larger office.

"Come in!" Her voice was loud.

With a yawn, I entered, accidently slamming the door into the filing cabinet by her door. The place reeked of incense and sage.

Robyn looked up at me, eyes wild and red.

I yelped and glared at her.

"Dude," she shook her head, "this is crazy."

"What?"

She beckoned me over, fingers trembling. I wandered around the desk to meet her.

"Look," she pointed at the screen, "it's exactly what we saw."

On the screen, an article read: '*Bizarre Bovine Autopsy*'

Apparently, a couple of states over, a healthy bull was found in a field, hollow as a drum.

"Bullshit." I murmured.

"Seriously?" Robyn cocked a brow.

I rolled my eyes and leaned back. "Come on, coincidence much?"

Robyn grabbed my arm and pulled me back. "There's more."

Skipping through tabs, Robyn showed me an abundance of stories, all about animals being scooped clean and left clinging onto some form of disturbing sentience.

These occurrences went as far back as the late 40's, animals all over being found in the same way. The earlier ones were certainly more grotesque than recent findings, mainly because of how the animals were sewn back together. Some were put together with a type of glue, others weren't even put together at all. The closer we got to the present day, the more hidden the entry points were, until eventually, over the past ten years, the animals had no entry points at all.

When missing people started to come into the articles, I had to stop.

"This can't be-" I tripped over my words. I sat on her desk and shook my head. "No."

"Yeah," her chair creaked when she sat back, "this ain't a coincidence."

"Animal harvesters?" I spluttered.

"Maybe in the past, but nowadays?" she shook her head. "No points of entry, unless they sucked it all through the mouth or the ass," she gestured crudely, "and you and I know that would leave *something*."

I nibbled my lip and thought. I was still sceptical. I sucked in a breath and choked on the incense. "Why have you got that stuff at work?"

"Keeps away the bad shit," she waggled her fingers mystically, "also you know how I feel about air fresheners."

"Air fresheners don't make people stupider!" I called to her, already out the door. "I've told you to stop reading that shit!"

She yelled something back, but I didn't hear. I was too busy stewing over the articles Robyn found.  That, and I had a dachshund to de-worm.

After work, I headed over to the local creek to clear my head. There was a secluded area downstream which I always went to whenever I needed time to think or have some time to myself. It was hidden by heavy canopies and thick foliage, and most people didn't know it even existed. I myself found it by accident after stumbling in there with an ex-boyfriend, half-drunk. Funnily enough, I went there after our break up.

On my way, I had to drive past the farmer's place, so I looked over to see if his truck was there.

It was there. So he was home, and presumably the wife.

There was also a sleek black sedan parked next to the rusty vehicle.

I slowed the car down to a stop, parking off the road behind a hedge.

Slowly, I rolled down my window and leaned out slightly, careful not to make too much of a scene.

It was hard to see, so I tried looking through my phone camera, zooming in on the kitchen window. I could see a middle-aged woman standing by the sink, face blurry. Her husband, the farmer, was sitting on a chair, head in his hands.

Two pairs of thin, snowy hands gestured at something in the room before their owners slipped into view. When I saw them, I dropped my phone in shock.

There were a pair of men dressed in black suits and shades. Their skin had to be as pale as their shirts, and they were clean-shaven with colourless buzz-cuts. I snapped a few pictures for evidence later. Also to confirm that I wasn't going crazy.

Randomly, my phone began to flicker and my camera focus went hazy when the suited men touched the farmer. The next few seconds came in flashes on my phone as it continued to bug out. One of the men touched the farmer's forehead, causing him to drop his head down onto the table. The woman presumably screamed, her gaping jaw letting out a muffled wail and she fumbled for the knife draw. My phone switched off at that moment, and I fumbled for the on switch. As soon as I focussed on the house again, I was met with the gaunt face of one of the men in the kitchen window, looking dreadfully neutral about being spied upon. The woman wasn't in view.

Never in my life have I ever driven so fast.

When I arrived at the creek, I jumped out the car and dashed into the treeline, head spinning like a lunatic.

It was a ten minute walk from the entrance, so not far. It was a five minute run if you kept the speed constant.

With the sound of soft crunching under my feet, I clambered my way through the brush and to my secret spot. Birds sang and whistled in the trees, and small animals scattered on the forest floor, preparing for winter. I kept my hands in my pockets and my scarf perched on my nose. The cold fall air was crisp and the sun was still high in the sky, slowly sinking behind the clouds.

Eventually, I slipped through the bushes to the hidden trail, where the rushing water lapped at my feet. Wilting flowers decorated the rocky ground and the spitting water sprayed the petals.

I sat on my usual rock and exhaled.

Here, I could see the running water gushing past, bubbling and frothing. The trees on my side had orange and red leaves, with the other side having wiry pines, both sides covered in dew.

It didn't click at first, but I hadn't seen any animals around me.

Squirrels would often scuttle across the ground, hopping over pinecones and dashing past my shoes. I would bring nuts and nibbles of cake for them to feast on. Sometimes they'd sit by me, whilst I contemplated life. Other times they'd skitter off with a cashew shoved in their mouth. But, they always came. Never in winter, but always other times of the year.

I scattered some cake crumbs on the rocks and behind me on the loose ground, in case the more skittish ones were scared of me.

I watched the stream.

The possibility of aliens wasn't on the table. *No.* I *refused* to put them on the table. Those men were just people. Journalists probably, I thought, stupidly in denial. But I knew. I knew they weren't journalists. I kept thinking about what Robyn would think about this, alien-crazed as she was. *Surely she would find this to be somewhat disturbing*?

Aliens weren't real.

*Besides, what did they want*? *What were they getting out of this*? *Anything*? *Why*?

A sound disrupted my thoughts.

It was the gentle scrunching of leaves.

It came from behind me and I prepared myself to get more cake from my pocket. Rushing, I opened the Ziploc bag with my bundt cake inside.

The sound was heavy, like there were an abundance of squirrels running across the floor.

"Hang on will you?" I broke the pieces up.

Then, the wind died and the sound finally became clear. I immediately stopped.

That wasn't the sound of dozens of squirrels running.

It was something heavy dragging itself across the floor, stopping and starting like a slug trying to escape the rain.

I squished the sticky sponge in my fist, heart racing.

I didn't move. My body froze and my stomach flipped with each excruciating heave of that heavy mass.

A loud whoosh came from behind; the sound of bushes being pushed away.

I held my breath when raspy gasping joined that awful dragging sound. It reminded me of a baby wheezing.

I focussed ahead, staring deep into the pines, hoping that wretched thing would ignore me and slither off into the undergrowth.

When I looked at a certain tree across the water, I swear I saw something dribbling down its bark. I squinted to get a better look.

A cold, icy finger poked my lower back.

I stifled a scream and spun around, wobbling back from whatever had touched me.

I nearly fainted.

There, about two feet away from me, was a man.

He looked as thin as a rake, skin pallid and leathery. He was on his hands and knees, scuttling forward like a new-born seeking sustenance. There were two deep black holes in his face; rotted eye sockets. He opened his mouth with a woeful groan, his gums toothless and turned crimson with coagulated blood. I couldn't see his tongue.

This wasn't a man.

This was a *corpse*.

My hands splashed in the water, soaking my sweater.

He crept closer, spindly arm still outstretched and desperate. Tiny gaps in his fingers showed that his fingernails were gone.

I yelped when he placed a frail hand on my leg, attempting to grab at my jeans.

Out of instinct, I kicked the man's face so hard he flew backwards.

His face gave a sickening crunch as my foot went straight through his nose and mouth. It broke instantly, his pasty skin shattering like a dinner plate.

I choked. The man's head hung on my foot from his mouth, no longer squirming or reaching out for help. With a grunt, I tore my foot out of his face and watched him crumple to the floor like a ragdoll.

He wasn't moving anymore.

Softly, I pushed his head back and gagged. His jaw was gone, and I could see down his throat into the rest of his body.

I whipped my phone out of my pocket and switched on the flashlight.

Foolishly, I prayed he wasn't like those animals in the articles.

Unfortunately, my prayers weren't answered. Rather, I saw the hollow remains of a man. No organs, no bones, nothing. All he had inside was his inner layer of skin, which was a deep ruddy red. A grimy, ebony ooze dribbled out of his jaw-hole.

I dialled Robyn. She was the only person I could think of ringing. She'd understand, she'd get it.

She told me she would be on her way. I told her to hurry.

I waited outside the brush, bouncing on my toes. My heart hammered in my chest, and bile churned in my stomach.

When Robyn arrived, she dashed over to me, concern painted all over her face.

"You okay?" She held my shoulders.

I was lost for words.

"Allie?" she shook me with force.

"Um," I snapped back to reality, "yeah, just, you have to see-"

"Where?"

I turned around and dragged her through the bushes to the man's resting place.

To my relief and horror, he was still lying there, jawless and dead.

"Shit." Robyn staggered forward.

She lurched behind a tree and I heard her hurl. I followed her and massaged her back as she threw up. When she finished, Robyn stood up and hugged me. I hugged her back, tears running down my cheeks and soaking her hair.

She trembled in my arms, and when I held her tighter, I realised I was shaking with her.

"You scared too?" I asked.

"A bit, yeah," her voice wavered. She pulled away, eyes watery, "is this real?"

I shrugged pathetically. "I saw some guys round the farmer's place."

She glared at me, eyes wide as saucers. "You're kidding."

"Done up in suits and shades," I pulled my phone out of my pocket, "here, have a look."

She swiped, her face paling with each picture.

"Please tell me you weren't seen." The wobble in her voice made my heart break.

All I could say was, "I'm sorry."

Robyn put her hands on her head and sniffled. "We're so fucked."

I cursed my stupidity. "We need to tell someone," I suggested, "police maybe."

"No," Robyn stood in front of me, guarding the body, "they won't believe us, or they'll throw us to those guys."

I paced back and forth. We couldn't carry him out of here, no way. We also couldn't leave him out in the open. That's when I remembered the cave behind a collection of boulders further downstream. Like I previously mentioned, when I broke up with my boyfriend I had a strop down to the creek, depressed. On that day, I walked the furthest I had ever walked down that trail, not caring where I was going, just following the path until it stopped. Halfway down my trip, I stumbled across a cave. It was small, I had to crouch to get in myself, and cosy. You couldn't hear anything in there, apart from the occasional dripping of water from above. I sat there and cried for a while, before simply enjoying the peace. No one ever came by there.

I pointed. "Follow me. There's a cave downstream."

Robyn looked at me, aghast. "We're going to stuff him in a cave?"

"Do you have any better ideas?"

She kept her lips tight before sighing. "No."

I signalled for her to grab the legs; two thin sticks wrapped in flimsy, translucent skin. I carefully lifted the man's shoulders, feeling weightless.

We trudged along the water with rushed care. I almost tripped over a crack in the rocks below, making the man's face hole to splutter out remnants of his skin. I heard Robyn heave and nearly drop the legs.

It was still light, so we were wary of any walkers or rangers passing by. Thank God we never saw anyone. I wouldn't know how to explain myself if we were caught.

We reached the opening, the pair of us skulked in, searching for a place to drop him. In the end, we found a small hole in the rocky floor, barely big enough for a man to lay in. Both of us eyed the split.

It was going to have to be a squeeze.

Robyn carefully tip-toed her way in, face scrunched up in concentration. The floor was damp, easy to slip backwards and smash your head in.

After forcing his body into the tight space, Robyn had no choice but to walk over the corpse. I winced with each crunch and crack as she tried her best to slink her way in between the rocks and over the man's brittle body. She tumbled into me when her foot punctured his shin.

We turned and looked at him. He was all crushed and full of holes, barely looked human.

"Now what?" Robyn asked.

I swallowed. "No clue."

The two of us looked at the man. *I couldn't leave him there, but did I kill him*? *Was he already dead*? It sounded selfish, but I didn't want to be arrested for manslaughter, so leaving him there was a nasty option. *Would the family even want to see him like this*? On the other hand, the family needed closure. So, I had an idea.

"Anonymous report." Robyn pondered on my suggestion.

"The family knows about him, and we stay out of it."

She hummed, thoughtfully. Her top lip twitched, almost forming a scowl. "Fine, but we'll have to come back and clean our fingerprints."

I exhaled through my nose. It was a plan. A shameful one, but a plan nonetheless.

"I'll bring a phone, you bring some bleach, gloves, stuff like that. Got it?" her voice had a harshness to it.

My throat twisted so hard it hurt. "Okay."

The ride home was quiet.

I kept my hands firm on the wheel, radio droning on in the background.

This was too big for us. Way too big.

I ate dinner with a funny feeling in my stomach.

My curtains were closed and every door in the house was shut. If Agent J wanted to stalk around my house, I'd hear him. I also made a note to check all areas of the house when I came back. I wasn't going to live in a bugged house.

Sitting silently, I waited for Robyn's text.

It was one of those times I wished I had a pet. A cat or a dog. Something so I didn't feel so alone. So *vulnerable*.

Not even something big. Probably a Jack Russell Terrier. I had one of those as a kid, a little white and tan one called Monty. He was as hard as nails and so clever. He would go after anything small like mice and squirrels, teeth gnashing, but he also liked to snuggle up with you when lightning struck. Sweet dog all in all. Poor boy went missing when I was 12. My parents and I took him on a walk off-leash. Something caught his eye, and before we could do anything, he had already ran off straight into the trees, hopped over some branches and disappeared from view. My dad sprinted after him, yelling his name at the top of his lungs. His barking went on until, eventually, it stopped, just like that. I cried for two weeks. Don't think I ever got over that. He was my best friend, my first and only dog.

They told me they never found him, but they did. Heard my dad crying to my mom about it. With the state Monty was found in, dad thought a bear got him. Mom told me it was worse than that. In college, we got drunk together after I came home for Christmas. Out of curiosity, I asked her about Monty after watching The Wizard of Oz. I can remember her soft face going all dark and stony.

According to her, they found him by a tree. Blood streaked up the bark, leading up to the leaves above. He had been dropped, that was sure.

To be concise, he was split in two. Straight down the middle along his spine.

"*Fucking creeps in the woods*," Mom spat, "*they treated him like a medical student's test subject*."

Mom never mentioned it again.

My phone buzzed in my palm.

*'Let's go' - Robbie.*

I grabbed my flashlight and cleaning supplies, then set off.

There weren't many cars on the road.

The streetlights fluttered past and gradually removed themselves from my view, the car's headlights being the only thing illuminating the road.

The night was calm and the stars glowed against the dark navy sky. I had no idea what was going to happen. I really didn't.

When I arrived at the woods, I was met with a pair of truck headlights and a sullen Robyn leaning on her hood.

I parked up next to her. "You got the phone?

Her left eyelid flickered. "Let's get this over with," she murmured, "we've got work tomorrow."

I bristled at that.

The air was crisp and cool, winter soon on its way. I could barely see the sky under all the leaves, with the odd star peeking through like a searchlight.

I walked ahead, knowing the trek like the back of my hand.

If you've ever gone camping in the woods, you know how quiet it gets when the sun goes down. The birds stop singing, animals are sleeping and the wind is non-existent.

Pair that with total darkness, and you're practically in a sensory deprivation room with exception of the odd owl screech and glimpses of the moon through the trees.

I could see my breath in front of me and my nose became cold. I bundled my scarf further up my face, leaving just my eyes uncovered.

Robyn stayed glued to my back, flashlight waving slightly with each step. She nearly fell over a root, cursing.

"You okay?" I turned my head to Robyn, who had done the same as me. Her wide brown eyes were the only things visible.

She gave a muffled, "uh huh." She also stook a gloved thumb up.

We went on, frost covered leaves cracking underfoot.

When we slipped through the brush, the sound of rushing water filled our ears. I lowered my scarf to take a deep breath in. The chilly air flowed into my lungs and made my chest frigid.

It was already this cold in early fall. Not too out of the ordinary, but still not common in that area.

We trudged on, my heart slowly beginning to beat just that bit faster.

The walk to the cave felt longer than before. Whether it was because it was dark or simply anticipation, it felt like years before we made it there.

Robyn flashed her light down at the ground to make sure we didn't slip whilst I kept mine firmly ahead to watch out for trees.

Finally, we arrived at the mouth of the cave.

With a full body shudder, I signalled for Robyn to get her burner phone out and call the police. We crept forward, using the inside of the cave as a guide. I reached the little hole the man laid in. My throat tightened as I imagined his wife's face when the police would relay the news.

However, when I shone the light into the man's makeshift tomb, all sympathy for his widow turned into pure fear.

Where the desecrated body should've been, an empty gap remained.

The corpse was gone.

"We need to go." I hissed to Robyn, trying to keep my voice steady.

"What?" she asked, voice a smidge too loud, "I've not even rang the-"

Her voice caught in her throat as I felt her presence next to me. "Where is he?"

"Never mind," I grabbed her arm and tugged her along, "we need to go."

She stayed still, mouth agape.

I took one glance outside before pulling her out of the cave. We speed-walked to the exit, my eyes fluttering from one tree to the next. My eyes had somewhat adjusted to the darkness and I could see where the shadows of trees were, albeit a little hazy. The moon helped a little when it peeked through the leaves.

Robyn was stumbling behind me, her heavy breathing being the only sound we heard apart from the dead leaves crunching.

We had almost reached the treeline when something caught the corner of my eye. I stopped in my tracks and Robyn fell into me with an, '*oof*'.

"*What the fuck are we stopping for*?" she snapped under her breath.

I stared into the trees. At first I thought it was my mind playing tricks on me. The dark does that people, y'know? But this? It looked too clear to be a mental trick.

What I saw was a figure shambling aimlessly about 20 meters away from us.

Its arms were all curled up like a dead insect. Its skinny legs were bowed inwards and the neck was crooked to the side like it had been snapped. I would've thought it was an addict, but its torso was full of holes.

Robyn dug her nails deep into my arm. "*Is that*\-"

A foul, guttural growl came from the figure, body clicking harshly. Suddenly, a louder sound, a deep thrumming bass echoed in the air, making my ears ring. I slapped my hands over my ears and squinted at the figure. The sound ceased after a few seconds, and in an instant, the person shuffled quickly further into the forest, swaying side to side.

We watched in horror as the figure almost ran towards something deep in the woods.

The man was alive. Or at least his body was.

I turned to Robyn, my blood colder than ice.

She turned to me, those eyes filled with some kind of shock-filled excitement.

Before I could react, Robyn had already taken off after him, flashlight shining around wildly.

The air left my lungs. I barrelled forward, chasing after the faint white light ahead. I nearly smashed my face on about a dozen trees and my legs were whipped and scratched by bushes and nettles, each step burning my thighs. I was lucky I wore jeans that day. I tried to stay focussed on Robyn, who had zipped off into the dark.

Soon, the dull glow of Robyn's flashlight came closer and closer as I approached her. She had stopped just before the gap in the forest. It was a circular opening in the woods, which the local kids called it, 'The Devil's Ring'. Original I know. Teenagers liked to mess around there on weekends and they had parties there on Halloween. People say they've seen unexplained things there like demons (hence the name), strange lights and unusual sounds that, apparently, rattle windows. I always put it down to drugs.

Now, I get what they saw and heard.

Robyn stood, fixated on the living corpse which wobbled back and forth in the middle of the opening.

I watched his body with her, heart in my throat.

The deafening bass erupted from the sky, as I dropped my flashlight and looked up at what was causing that piercing sound. The man did the same, bent neck facing upwards.

It went quiet for a moment.

Then, a bright flash blinded my vision, making me crouch, disorientated. My eyes strained to adjust to the light. I rubbed them, stray tears escaping.

The pale white light showed the man’s broken body as it gazed up in steely obedience.

Appearing out of thin air, a thick black ooze floated down from the light source, weaving and growing as it descended. The thick oily mass expanded to the size of the corpse, before stopping directly in front of his face.

It floated and swirled around his head, almost thinking. It had no shine to its surface, all black and viscous like tar.

Once its inspection was done, it returned to his face. It hovered, still and waiting.

Faster than lightning, the mass shot into his broken jaw, filling his body with a vile slurp.

My heart raced violently in my chest, bile gurgled in my gut and throat. I couldn't look away.

His body lurched forward with an aggressive stumble, rickety legs popping outwards and straightening. His back snapped backwards, grotesquely cracking as his chest was pushed upwards, black tendrils seeping through the holes.
