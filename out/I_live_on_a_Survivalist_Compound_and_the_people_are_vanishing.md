The people are vanishing and they’re not being remembered. I’m the only person that seems to remember them in the first place. It starting to get weird and I just don’t know where else to seek help than here.

I’ll give some context to where I live and maybe that will shed some light onto the situation. Or at least all the light that I’ve gathered thus far. Ever since I can remember, my parents had been worried about the end of the world and the break down of society as we know it. To the point on my eighth birthday they had sprung the news that we were moving to a survivalist compound. My stable life that was filled with friends, school and the typical childhood of any regular family were uprooted over night.

My parents had sold all our belongings, and had made the trip to the compound. I can’t give you an exact location as I don’t even completely know the exact location either. Heck, I’m writing this on an old laptop that I found in one of the missing people house. I didn’t steal it, because as far as Mrs. Gwaltney was concerned her adult son had never existed. She didn’t even blink when I had commandeered the old bag and a few other things from the room.

My life inside the compound had been a staggering twelve years. Unlike a lot of the kids that were around my age, they had never left the compounds or the large mountainside that we resided in. To them this living out here as recluses’ completely detached from the outside world was perfectly normal. I had always considered leaving and venturing out into the world, but what skills did I have to offer?

Fire arm proficiency, skilled at archery, skinning animals and other survival skills! The only option I could see forward was a wildlife survival guide, but I don’t want to be out in the woods anymore.

I’m sorry I’m rambling, but I had grown close to the people inside of the community. It was my hobby and pastimes to get to know new members and old members alike. They all were different and unique and it was an easy way to get out of ‘*intensive training*.’ Either way, I can’t remember the first time someone had vanished, but I do remember the day that I noticed that people were gone. Poofed out of peoples memories as if they were nothing but a figment of my imagination.

I’d learned the hard way when I had raised a fuss when I noticed my best friends was missing. Tabitha, or Tab for short, she was one of the kids that I had grown up with. Even though Tab had been born on the compound, she was a curious soul and someone that I could confide in. Just like the three other people now that had gone missing, no one blinks an eye! It’s as if they’re nothing more than a figment of my own imagination.

The looks I’m getting are enough to make me feel like I’m losing my mind. But I’m going to get to the bottom of it, I owe it to Tab to find out the truth. For now I’ve been collecting things from those that have vanished.

Let me start from the day that Tab vanished. If I retrace the day, maybe, we’ll be able to figure something out!

It was a humid day, early July, the sun was just peaking over the far mountain top. I had climbed up to the roof of my house and was watching the sunrise. The community hadn’t completely woken up, but from my vantage point, I could see a few people moving about.

They were the community typically early risers, Mr.Jones, Margret, the twins, and Mother Isabella. Each of them heading off to do their jobs: Mr.Jones managed the electricity for the community, the twins were his apprentices. Meanwhile, Margret was off to get breakfast prepared, today was communion day. Meaning that the whole community would gather for breakfast, and then morning mass as held by Mother Isabella. She and her husband the late Father Mark had established the community. Building it from four families into a massive town with probably close to two hundred people residing on the mountain side. Bitting on the jerky that I had taken from the pantry, I happened to catch the eyes of Mother Isabella.

Her golden hazel eyes swung upwards to meet mine, a shudder ran down my back, it was uncanny how she always seemed to notice me. Mother Isabella’s mouth stretched into a warm smile, it even reached all the way to her eyes. Her full mouth was spread in a dazzling smile, I felt my heart pound in my chest a little faster.

She had been young when I had arrived twelve years ago, probably no older than nineteen and she had been married to the late Father, who had to be well into his late fifties. It had always seemed strange that they were a married couple, especially given the fact that Mother Isabella didn’t seem to care for her husband.

To outside eyes she was as pious a wife as one could get. But I remember catching her laughing after his funeral last summer. He’d pass away due to some disease, my parents had whispered about cancer in the thyroid. About it being too far gone to do anything about.

Mother Isabella had thought she was alone, but I’d seen her behind the recreation shed, her beautiful face wasn’t a mask of sadness as it had been earlier during the ceremony. No, she’d been laughing and smiling as she cursed his name! I had been so startled, I knocked over a bucket as her piercing eyes had found me. Relief flooded them the minute she saw it was me. Even now her words echoed in my head.

“Winnie,” my name sounded like a prayer on her lips. “You wouldn’t tell anyone about this would you?” She tilted her head, letting the caramel brown locks of hair slide over her shoulder. It whispered across her face almost like a lover caress.

My mouth felt like lead, I didn’t know how to respond. Once I understood the massive age difference between the two of them I’d been grossed out. But I couldn’t speak about it, I’d asked my parents about it and my Father had flown into a massive speech. While my Mother just sat there and nodded her head like a wooden doll. That had been the last time I voiced my thoughts to them. I gave Mother Isabella a curt nod and she kept beaming. Moving towards me she had gently cup my cheek and given me a light kiss on the forehead.

“It’ll be our little secret,” her breath was sweet like flowers, the scent seemed to wrap itself around me. It had made my stomach swim. Ever since then my eyes had followed her, she had become the defecto leader of the community. Father Mark had been its leader, but now that I watched everyone. She had always been the leader, her *husband* had just been the figure head.

Bitting my finger, I couldn’t help but break eye contact with her first. Standing I stretched my back as I gathered my notebook into my backpack. Double checking that I had my trusty knife in my boot, I patted my hip and found the other one as well. Opening the smaller zipper on my backpack, I saw it there sitting like a rock inside the bag. A gun, it’s not something that you suppose to carry inside the compound, unless you were part of the peacekeepers.

I could get into some serious trouble for keeping it on my person. But I was a rule breaker, always had been, I think it disappointed my parents, but there wasn’t much they could do. Everyone in the community viewed me as a helpful daughter, sister or friend. One had to keep up illusions if you wanted to subvert a close knit community. Plus my own parents even if they were mildly disappointed in me, weren’t the type to invite criticism onto themselves.

Clambering down the safety latter that went down from the three story home that I lived in. I hit the ground with a soft thump, the ground here on the side of the house wasn’t paved. The main streets and those that lead to the front entrance and back entrance of the compound were all paved. The pathways that lead to the houses were outlined with stone.

Glancing at the front of the driveway, I noticed that the jeep that normally resided there was nowhere to be seen. It looks like Dad had taken it out, maybe, on an assignment from Mother Isabella and the council. Rolling my eyes, I just moved onwards looking left and right down the small alleyway that separated the houses. I quickly moved between the houses, slowly but surely the lights from the other houses began to click on. People were waking, and soon we’d all be expected at mass.

Making a sharp left I arrived at the back of Tab’s houses. My eyes roved the backyard looking for her, but she wasn’t outside. Normally, I’d arrive to find her in her hammock, her trusty brown beaten notebook open and her scrawling her thoughts inside of it. Reaching up, I undid the latch to the fence that was around her backyard. Moving swiftly forward, I didn’t move to the back door that was washed in light, the sound of humming floating in the air.

It had to be Tab’s Mother, Monica, she was always humming something. Not wasting anymore time, I ran to the ladder that was also attached to the side of Tab’s house. It wasn’t as well kept as my family’s ladder, that was in part because Tab’s family’s house was older. The house had sprawling  ivy that climbed upwards and moss that had started to grow in other places. The shudders on the windows were a faded red that was closer to a dirt auburn from years of the elements abusing it.

I easily scaled the ladder reaching the secondary floor without issue, I reached up and rapped my knuckles across of Tab’s window. Sometimes she stayed up too late and didn’t get to sleep till early, I figured this much be the case.

*Knock Knock Knock*.

The rasp of my knuckles was quick and concise. I didn’t want to alert Monica that I was here. Even if most people liked me in the community, Monica always thought that I might be a bad influence on her daughter. Given the fact that I was born on the outside.

“Tab! Wake up,” I half whispered half yelled as I knocked. Her curtains were drawn and I tried rattling the window. Trying to draw it upwards, but it was locked. Tab rarely locked her windows, heck, no one really locked the windows in the community. Most people didn’t even bother with lockings the doors, everyone here was in some weird way family.

There was no crime, well crime that was punishable with jail time. Given anything that involved a close knit community that was secluded there were lose with *problems*. I’m sure you can understand the meaning of that!

“Tabitha, I know you don’t sleep that deeply. Wake up and let me in! Your Mom is going to see me and throw a fit!” I pressed, leaning in close to the glass, straining my ears to see if I could hear her laughing.

But there was nothing, leaning back, I reach for the knife that was on my hip. It was the first knife that my Father had given to me. It was also the first gift that I had received here in the community, he insisted that it was important for me to become accustomed to using a blade.

A KA-BAR not any old one either, but one that my Grand Father had owned, and then passed down to my Father and then to me. It was metliciously cared for and a good companion for many situations. Slipping the blade underneath the worn wood of the window frame, I began to wiggle it forward until I felt the latch. Smiling I pushed it forward and heard the click as the knife flipped the lock.

Not wasting anytime, I palmed the window frame and dragged it upwards. It went without fuss and the room of my best friend came into sight. Her messy room full of paper and pencils, along with other arts and crafts that were clumsily placed throughout the room. My eyes quickly jumped to her bed, but when they landed, I found that the bed was empty. The covers weren’t even ruffled, it didn’t appear as if the bed had even been slept in.

“Tab,” I called in a hushed whisper. Trying to not be to loud, but loud enough that if she was in the room she would be able to hear me. I waited for a few beats, but no response came, not even a maker or pen falling from her trying to keep up the game.

I frowned, quickly I pulled the window down and scampered back down the ladder. Not wasting anytime I marched to the back door, I could still hear Monica humming away. As I climbed the stairs up the back porch. Monica came into focus, she was drinking a cup of tea while flipping through a book. Her long salt and pepper colored hair was held loosely together in a braids. Sucking in a deep breath I rasped my knuckles across the glass. Turning she tilted her head to look at me for a moment before she stood up. Pulling the sliding door back she regarded me.

“Hi, I’m sorry it’s so early but is Tabitha here?”

“Tabitha?” Monica questions.

“She was suppose to meet me. We had some things that we needed to take care of before morning mass,” I quickly explained. All the while lying through my teeth. Yes, we had plans but it wasn’t something that I would ever tell her Mother. Normally, it was apologizing for being out late and not explaining why that had happened.

“Winnie, honey are you feeling alright?” Monica asked me. Her voice was strange almost kind as if she was worried about me. I blinked before nodding my head.

"Yeah, I feel great. So is Tabitha in the bathroom or did she leave with Lake?” I asked, again, trying to stay on topic.

Monica blinked slowly at me, as if I was asking her a hard question. Her whole body language read as if she was debating on whether she should call someone for help. I smiled trying to be as polite as possible in case Tab was in some real hot water with her Mother.

“Who are you talking about?”

I blinked looking at Monica who wore an expression that was dead serious. In the first place she wasn’t the type to jokes like this, and secondly she was giving me that smile. You know the one that you give to someone who’s a little slow on the uptake and that you think is crazy!

Maybe, she had gotten a little stronger supplement from the Doc? I tried again this time explaining who Tabitha was to her.

“Tabitha your daughter. She looks more like her Father than you. You both have a bit of a tough love Mother Daughter relationship.”

“Winnie do you want me to have Lake take you to see Doc? You haven’t been climbing again have you? Remember he said that you should be careful concussion can be really nasty,” she said softly. Talking to me as if I’m a wounded animal.

“Are you sure you don’t need me to take you? How could you not know who I’m talking about?!” I said, finding that I was starting to get annoyed.

“Lake?” Monica called, turning her head to me as she called for Tabitha’s older brother.

“Are we seriously doing this right now?” I asked. Venom leaking into my words. This was ridiculous, I had half the mind to tell her off and leave. I’d just go and check our normal hang out spots to see if Tabitha was there!

She ignored my question. Her face was a mask of fake kindness. Uncomfortable was the word that I would use as we stood there the question lingering in the air like a bad scent. The sound of pounding footfalls could be heard as he came down the stairs. Then appearing not even a minute after his mom had yelled for him, came the golden child. The pride and joy of Tab’s family, Lake her older brother.

“Winnie?” He asked as he looked over his Mother’s shoulders. Lake was tall and handsome if you were into that sort of thing. He had chestnut colored hair that had natural highlights and he was the best at nearly everything. We’d also briefly dated, and it had ended less than cordial.

I shook my head and took a step back from the two of them. This was crazy and frankly not something that I had expected from Monica. I knew that she didn’t like me, but this was odd acting as if she didn’t know who I was talking about. Did she think Lake would go along with her little joke?

“Lake. Where is Tabitha?”

He blinked slowly at me, his eyes glassing over, before he seemed to come back to reality. A look of pure confusion on his face.

“Who’s Tabitha?” He asked. My stomach dropped through the floor, he had the same confused and concerned expression as Monica. It was as if they didn’t know who I was talking about.

“Stop joking around! Tabitha as in your sister!” I said. Completely exasperated as I took another step back. He stepped in front of his Mom, concern for me now coloring his face.

“Winnie. There no one here named Tabitha, and a sister you know that I don’t have any siblings.” He reached a hand out and tried to touch my shoulder. Another shudder ran up my spine as I dodged his hands.

“Only child?” I nearly stuttered.

“Of course, it’s why we got along so well,” he said rather sheepishly. My mouth went dry as the gears in my head turned sharply. Lake truly believed that he was an only child. I turned to look at Monica. My stomach swam as I tired not to be sick. The same look was on her face.

“This isn’t funny! If you’re playing a joke on me, I really need you to stop now. What about her room up stairs? Can you explain why you have a room full of girls clothes up stairs?” I snapped. Feeling the thin strand of sanity that I had.

He held my stare, his eyes searching mine. After not finding what he was apparently looking for he released a breath. Retracting his hand he rubbed his neck as if he was trying to think through this as well.

“Mom, why don’t you call Winnie’s parents.” He was still looking at me. Looking at me as if I was the crazy one talking about his sister, my best friend, as if she didn’t exist in the first place.

“Okay,” Monica said quietly. Almost a whisper as she turned away from us and headed into the house.

“I’m not crazy! You have a sister named Tabitha. Who is two year younger than you, and loves to read and write. You’d always make fun of her and she absolutely hated it when we started dating a year ago. She baked me a cake for breaking up with you!” My mouth moved before my brain, dumping out information anything that I could think of would jog his memory.

He stared at me blankly almost pitifully.

“Don’t look at me like that!” I screamed. It was loud to my own ears. He winced at my tone, but held up his hands in almost defeat.

“Fine, let’s go look at the room then,” he acquiesced.

We ascended the stairs. I noticed that the family pictures in there frames only held three people in the picture. My mouth went dry at that. Tabitha wasn’t there, it was as if the pictures had been taken without her.

The walk upstairs was quiet and the rising dread bubbled up in my stomach. It felt like I was walking towards a grave rather than a room. Lake’s form was tall and blocked most of my vision as we made our way. But we arrived at the room, and he pushed the door open.

A breath left my mouth before I could stifle it. The room was devoid of Tab’s things. There were a few boxes and a bed that was stripped of all personality. How was this even possible?! I was just knocking at her window with everything inside. Reality slapped me in the face as I took it in, it was as if she had never been here! Lake stepped aside giving me a better view, but I thought I might be sick.

“It’s an empty storage room. Be honest with me did you take something?” He asked, voice low as he turned to look down at me.

My body shook as I tried to wrap my head around this reality. But I couldn’t accept it, I wouldn’t accept it. My friend was real, but her own family didn’t remember her.

“Win,” he begged his tone cajoling like one would coax a child with. There would be none of this! I had to go, no, I needed to get out. Turning I moved towards the stairs, picking up speed as I went. He was behind me his voice ringing out.

Words were just sounds as the roar of my own thoughts came crashing over me. I thought about leaving through the back door but then I’d come across Monica. Reaching the end of the stairs I threw open the door and quickly raced out of the house. Lake followed me for a bit but then gave up, I was fast and when I didn’t want to be caught I was even faster. I skipped morning mass, and hid out in our secret tree house.

It was something that we had put together over the years. Climbing the ladder, I threw open the make shift door and there was no one in there. My heart sank, but I noticed the Polaroid’s that we’d taken. Tab had begged her Father to get the simple camera and he’d finally did. Reaching out I yanked one of the pictures from the wall. She was still in the Polaroids!

My heart hammered in my chest as the face of my smiling best friend came into focus. Tears ran down my cheeks, I sat there for a while holding the photo’s collecting them all from the walls. Whatever had gotten to her house, and their pictures hadn’t managed to get here. It was only me and Tab who knew about our secret spot.

I’d collected all the pictures and scribbling and hid them in my backpack. The sun was starting to set, when I finally decided to make my way back to the compound. I sunk in through a hole in the wall by the western entrance. That night my Father had scolded me something fierce about making a scene. While my Mother just sat on and watched the whole thing.

When I’d asked them if they remembered Tabitha, their expressions had gone slack, almost like a chicken that had stunned itself. But their response had been nearly identical to Tab’s family.

“Winnie are you sick? You don’t have a friend named Tabitha,” Dad said matter of factly. He’d shaken his head, glancing at my Mother who also looked confused.

“I’m not sick, none of you seem to remember her!” I snapped.

“Wilhelmina,” he snapped. “Don’t take that tone with me.”

I snapped my mouth shut. He rubbed the spot between his eyebrows, his salt and pepper hair looked unruly. He exhaled, and fixed me with his eyes, they were hard as he spoke. Although, I can’t remember a time in recent history where he hasn’t looked at me with the disapproving stare.

“Go upstairs, I have enough to deal with already. The last thing I need is you causing made up drama.”

I opened my mouth to speak, but he shot me a stare that made me close my mouth. I’d almost been tempted to show him the pictures, to prove a point that I wasn’t sick or crazy or anything else he was thinking. But I’d decided against that, the reality of my situation dawning on me. If her own family can’t remember her, then mine surely wouldn’t be any better.

I’d gone upstairs and that night I hadn’t slept. Honestly most night since that night I hadn’t really slept. Fear was my fuel as I thought about being snatched up in the night. In those half sleep deprived and half manic nights I had come up with a plan. I’d pretended that what happened that day was from a concussion, and that I hadn’t been in my right mind.

The lie was bitter on my tongue, but it had done its job. Everyone had accepted it. But others started to vanish, I’d begun keeping a registry of everyone that was in the compound. Keeping a date by everyone that had vanished and the last time I had seen them. It was hard because when I’d snuck into the office, to get the official town registry it was altered. Not like someone had taken a pen and scratched through peoples names. They were completely erased as if they hadn’t been there to start with. Plus, everyone been acting strange, there more morning masses and people are moving around more in the evenings.

I plan to investigate more around the compound and try to figure out what happening to everyone. This is a documentation that I exist, in case, I get removed from everyone’s memories! But for now I plan to investigate some tunnels, I found the other day outside the compound. I’d found some tracks that lead into them. I’ll let you know what I find when I get back.
