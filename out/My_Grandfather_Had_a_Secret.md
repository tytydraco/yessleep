When I got the call telling me my grandfather had died, I didn't feel sad. I didn't feel anything at all. We used to be close. He'd even given me a key to his house back when we still had a good relationship. But over the last few months, he'd become a horrible person. He wouldn't let me visit him, and whenever I called, all I got was a barrage of insults.

My lease was ending in a few days, and I figured I could move into my grandfather's house. I wasn't exactly thrilled about the idea, but I didn't have a particularly well-paying job, and saving money on rent would help.

My buddy Rory helped me move. I opened the gate and headed toward the front door. I unlocked it, and we stepped inside. When I turned on the lights, I was stunned.

There were strange symbols scrawled in chalk all over the floor. I couldn't make sense of any of them. The walls were covered in painted eyes. Terrifying eyes.

I heard Rory take a deep breath.

"Holy shit. Owen, are you sure you want to live here?"

I looked at him and told him I didn't have much of a choice. He simply nodded.

We slowly made our way through the house, checking one room after another. It was the same everywhere. Walls covered in eyes and floors covered in symbols.

Eventually, we came across a locked room. The door was also wrapped in chains.

"Do you know what's in there?" Rory asked.

"No. I have absolutely no idea. And I don't even know how I'm supposed to get inside."

Considering everything else I'd seen in that house, though, it didn't seem like the biggest problem.

I could tell Rory was eager to get out of there.

"Hey, man, I gotta go. I promised Amy I'd spend more time with her."

I gave him a suspicious look.

"Sure. I get it."

I thanked him for helping me move and watched him leave. When the door clicked shut behind him, an uneasy feeling settled over me.

I ate dinner, watched some TV, and went to bed in the room my grandfather had decorated just the way I'd wanted when I was younger.

Around midnight, a series of loud bangs jolted me awake.

BANG. BANG. BANG.

I turned on the light, grabbed my phone with the flashlight on, and slowly followed the sound. I switched on the lights as I made my way through the house.

The noise was coming from the locked room.

I'm not a coward, but I packed my things and drove straight to Rory's place.

He offered me a spare room, and the next day, he came back to my grandfather's house with me so we could figure out what had caused the banging.

When we arrived, Rory looked up.

"Hey... See that open window up there? Could it be in that room?"

I thought about it.

"Yeah, the location would make sense."

The corners of Rory's mouth twitched.

"And what if the ghost is just a window banging against its frame in the wind? Like it might be doing right now?"

I was embarrassed. Very embarrassed.

I thanked him for his help, said goodbye, and headed inside. I found my grandfather's tools and decided to open the room.

It must have taken hours, but when I finally managed to remove the last chain, I gathered all my courage and opened the door.

The room was empty except for a life-sized painting on one wall, its canvas covered in a mixture of different colors.

"Great," I said to myself. "So this is the secret of the mysterious room."

When I told Rory about it over the phone, he burst out laughing.

"And you were scared to open it! Honestly, though, after everything we saw, I wouldn't have been surprised if you'd found a corpse in there."

We said goodbye a few minutes later. I closed the window and left the room.

I decided to clean up the house. First, I'd scrub all the chalk off the floors. The next day, I'd buy some paint so I could start covering up the eyes on the walls.

I was just about to start cleaning when my phone rang. An unknown number. I hesitated for a moment before answering.

"Hello, am I speaking with Owen?" a man's voice asked.

"Yes, speaking."

"My name is Johnathan Harris. I was your grandfather's attorney. I'm calling about the house you're currently living in."

I frowned.

"Has something happened?"

"I'm afraid so. Shortly before his death, your grandfather asked me to revoke the deed transferring the house to you."

"Wait," I said, confused. "So he wanted to take back the house he'd signed over to me?"

"Yes," Johnathan replied.

I fell silent for a moment, trying to process what he'd just told me.

"Hold on. Are you telling me the house isn't mine?"

"Legally, the situation is more complicated than that. We'll need to examine the reasons your grandfather had for taking this step. Since he's passed away, the process will be more complicated than it would be under normal circumstances."

"What am I supposed to do in the meantime?"

"That will depend on the outcome of our review. For now, I'd advise you not to make any structural changes to the property."

We said our goodbyes, and I stood in the middle of the living room with the phone still pressed to my ear. I couldn't believe what I'd just heard.

I knew my grandfather had been unbearable lately, but I never expected him to do something like that.

Angrily, I grabbed the mop and started washing the floor.

Suddenly, I heard a whisper right beside my ear.

"Oweeeen..."

I spun around, but there was nobody behind me. However, I noticed black stains on the freshly mopped floor. I touched one of them. It was wet and slimy. The stains led toward that room.

I wanted to run, but I couldn't. Something was forcing me to walk toward it. I had no control over my own body. My legs were trembling, yet I kept moving forward. It felt as though a cold draft was blowing against my back.

I reached the doorway. The room looked exactly as it had before, except for the painting.

The colorful blotches were beginning to form a figure.

I blinked several times to make sure I wasn't imagining things.

I wasn't.

I heard the voice beside my ear again.

"Oweeeen..."

This time, it was clearer. I recognized it. It was my grandfather's voice.

"Destroy the painting."

I froze. I couldn't move.

The voice spoke again.

"You have to stop it."

I bolted out of the room and ran toward the front door. I grabbed the handle and pulled desperately. Nothing happened. I tried again, this time using both hands, but the door wouldn't budge. It was as though some heavy object had been shoved against it from the other side.

A slow creaking sound came from behind me.

I turned my head, but I couldn't see anything.

I ran into the kitchen. My eyes landed on a lighter. I grabbed it, took a bottle of vodka out of the fridge, and hurried back to the room.

I burst inside and looked at the painting. The figure was now clearly visible. It was an old man with a terrifying grin. I watched as his mouth began to stretch wider.

I wasn't going to wait any longer. I poured the vodka over the canvas.

I flicked the lighter and watched as the flames slowly consumed the painting.

Then something came from inside the canvas that made my heart pound even harder.

Someone took a deep breath and let out a hoarse cough.

Burning hands burst out of the painting and grabbed me by the shoulders. I could feel my skin sizzling. I tore myself free and ran out of the room toward the front door.

I tried the front door again.

This time, it opened.

I ran outside and called the fire department and the police. My shoulders were red, and blisters were beginning to form on my skin in several places. The firefighters found nothing inside the house, and the police looked at me as though I'd lost my mind.

I couldn't blame them. If I hadn't experienced what happened myself, I probably wouldn't have believed it either.

I'm staying with Rory now.

Today, I met with the lawyer to discuss my grandfather's estate. As I was leaving, he handed me an envelope that my grandfather had supposedly wanted delivered to me personally. Apparently, he'd insisted that I read the letter myself.

I stared at the envelope for a long time before finally opening it.

Inside was a single sheet of paper. Written in my grandfather's shaky handwriting were these words:

*Owen,*

*Under no circumstances should you move into that house.*

*And NEVER open that door.*

*You must not let it out, and you must not do anything to that painting.*

*And most importantly, Owen, if you start hearing my voice, don't listen to it.*

*It's not me.*
