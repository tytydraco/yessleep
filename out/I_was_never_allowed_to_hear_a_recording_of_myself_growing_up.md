Three weeks ago, my roommate handed me his phone and asked, "Is this you?"

It was some kind of prank video from one of those YouTube channels that film people without their permission and publish it to get views. I barely got past the first five seconds of the video, but I didn't care about the prank anymore since this was the first time in my life that I've heard myself speak, from what I remember anyway.

And I didn't expect this. Not even a little bit.

When I was growing up, In speech therapy, I'd seen videos of other kids with cleft palate, how it has this slight nasality and how some consonants sound softer. I'd had this picture of my voice somewhere in my head.

What emerged from the speakerphone was a voice that sounded like it belonged to a sixty-year-old man who had smoked one pack of cigarettes per day since the age of fifteen. Low, harsh, worn out by something that had absolutely nothing to do with any cleft palate. It was not mine in the least. It was not my age either. It chilled my heart even before I was aware of why.

I'd never actually heard my own voice before that. No voicemail, no home videos with the sound on, nothing like that. If anyone asked, my father just said I didn't like the way I sounded, and I never questioned it. I had a cleft palate as a baby that left me with a mild speech impediment, speech therapy through most of elementary school and into middle school before it finally evened out. A rule like no recordings doesn't get questioned by a kid. You just grow up with it. It becomes part of the furniture.

I made him play it back to me. And then again. And each time, I watched his face carefully for any sign of what I felt. None. Only boredom in wait of the punchline.

"Does my voice sound like that?" I asked. "Right now. Talking to you."

He looked at me as if I had asked him if there was a moon in the sky.

"What kind of a question is that? Yeah, obviously."

"No, really listen carefully, does it sound like that?" and I indicated the phone, that low gravel voice of it, the one that surely should not come out of a twenty-four-year-old, the one of an old man who smoked since he was sixteen.

He humored me, actually thought about it this time. "Well, sort of nasal, maybe. As if you are congested. Other than that, yes. This is just you."

“Nasally.”

I repeated it, as if it were a word from a totally different language.

“Yeah, that’s just how you’ve always been a little bit, ever since we’ve known each other. No biggie.”

“Not raspy. Not like some guy who’s been smoking his whole life, since he was a kid, fifteen years old.”

He chuckled, as if it was what he’d been waiting to hear all along. “What? No! You just sound like yourself. You’ve never sounded like an old smoker in your life. You good?”

I wasn't. Because I was hearing a voice that had nothing to do with mine, worn down at the edges in a way no cleft palate ever caused, and he was hearing the same five seconds of audio and describing something else entirely. A little nasal. That was the whole complaint. I made him play it twice more before he handed the phone back and told me to relax, it was just a prank video.

After he went to bed I couldn't sit still in the apartment. I took my phone out to the parking lot, mostly just to be away from the walls, away from him hearing me talk to myself and thinking I'd lost it.

I opened the voice memo app. My hand was shaking a little, which felt stupid, it's just recording your own voice. I hit record.

"Hello."

I played it back. Same voice. Not close to the one from the video. The same voice. Low, rough, worn down, coming out of my phone speaker in an empty parking lot at eleven at night.

I hit record again.

"Hi."

Same thing.

My actual voice, the one I'd apparently been hearing my whole life filtered through my own skull, was not the voice that existed outside of it. It never had been. I just never had a way to check before three weeks ago.

My heart was going fast, faster than it should have for standing still saying two words into a phone. Not scared exactly. Something colder, a hum under my skin I didn't have a name for.

I went back inside. My roommate's door was shut, his light off. I sat at the kitchen table with my laptop instead of going to bed, because there was no chance I was sleeping, and pulled the video back up.

This time I watched the whole thing, not just the five seconds I'd stopped at before. About ninety seconds, shaky phone footage from across the street. The same coffee shop. The same sidewalk.

But I wasn't there.

I frowned and dragged the progress bar back to the beginning.

The guy filming walked past the coffee shop, yelled something at someone off camera, laughed to himself, then kept walking.

That was it.

I scrubbed back to the part where I'd been standing.

Nothing.

No one came out of the coffee shop. No one answered him. There wasn't even another person on that side of the street.

I refreshed the page.

Same video.

I checked the upload date. Same channel. Same thumbnail. Same title.

I watched it again from the beginning.

Nothing.

Five minutes ago I'd watched myself turn toward the camera and heard a voice that couldn't possibly belong to me.

Now it was just... gone.

Like I'd imagined the whole thing.

Except my roommate had handed me the phone. He'd watched it too. He'd heard me speak.

I hadn't made that part up.

I scrolled down to the comments hoping someone had mentioned the guy from the coffee shop.

Nobody had.

Not one person asked where I had gone. Not one comment mentioned someone walking out of the shop or answering the guy behind the camera.

I just sat there and stared at the screen until the page timed out.

Even while falling asleep, I couldn't get the idea of my voice off my mind.

The next day morning I figured that there was one way to find the answer and I made a call to my dad.

"Hi there Dad, odd question but do we have any videos of me when I was little?" I tried to make it sound light.

"Nothing like that. You know that."

"You always said that I did not like hearing my own voice."

"I don't remember saying that."

That came out of nowhere. The answer was always that you don't like hearing your own voice when I was growing up. I let that sink in for a moment, then moved on to the next question.

"Is there anything Mom recorded before she left?"

It was quiet for the longest time after I asked that question. It was such a long silence that I actually checked my phone to see if the line was still connected.

"Don't," he eventually said. "Don't bring Her into this."

He hung up before I could ask why. I sat there in the kitchen a long time after, listening to an apartment that was completely quiet. I don't know what's going on. Am I crazy? Is my voice actually like that. I don't know anymore.
