I should have started at the beginning. I started in the middle because that’s where the handwriting changes.

I need to say this first, because a lot of you asked, and because I did the stupid thing anyway: I did not stay in the bathroom. I turned every light in the apartment on. I checked the yard. I checked the parking lot. I checked the back seat of my car like a person in a movie. Nobody. The motion light still works. I tested it. I also called the non-emergency line and felt like an idiot explaining that someone had photographed my window, so I hung up before it rang.

Maya never showed up last night. At 11:40 she texted: Flight delayed. Tomorrow. Do NOT talk to anyone else about Mom. I’m serious.

I asked her what that meant.

She left me on read.

The notebook is 80 pages. Mom filled 61 of them. The last 19 are blank except for that date on the final page. She started it in June 2019, five months before she died. Or before someone died. I don’t know how to say that yet.

I’m going to type out the parts that matter. I’m not changing her words. If this is a joke she planned six years ago, it’s a very long joke.

June 3, 2019

They called again. Same man. He said the window is closing and I already used my extra year. I told him Nathan is 26 and Maya is 29 and they are not part of this. He said that was never the deal. He said the deal was the name.

June 11, 2019

Dr. Okonkwo wants another scan. I already know. I knew in April. I am not telling the kids until I have to. If I tell them now Maya will take over and Maya cannot know the rest.

June 18, 2019

I went to the house on Binder Street. The curtains were the same. I sat in the car for an hour. A woman came out with a little boy. He looked like Nathan at 8. Same ears. I followed them to a grocery store and I hated myself for it. The boy’s name is Cole. I heard her say it when he ran ahead of the cart.

July 2, 2019

I called S. She cried. She said Cole still asks about the man in the photograph. I told her there is no photograph. She said there is. She said it was in the box I told her to burn. I am so tired of being the one who remembers the rules.

July 19, 2019

The man came to the hospice consult. He was not a doctor. He sat in the waiting room and read a magazine and when I came out he said, “Helen. You look like her today.” I said I have always looked like me. He smiled like I’d told a joke. He said they can still do the quiet version if I sign before August. I asked what happens to Nathan. He said Nathan keeps his life. He said that is the generous offer. I asked what the other offer was. He put the magazine down and left.

August 4, 2019

I signed. God forgive me I signed. Not for me. For the name. If I don’t give them a body they will take a living one. They were very clear about that. They have done it before. They showed me a file with a different family in Ohio. The son was 24. There was a funeral. The mother is still paying a phone bill.

August 9, 2019

Maya keeps asking why I’m suddenly organizing the storage unit. I told her I’m nesting. She doesn’t believe me. She is too much like me. That is the problem.

September 1, 2019

The other boy is gone. S called at 2 in the morning. She said they came to Binder Street and they were polite and they had papers and Cole went with them because they used my voice on the phone first. I don’t know how they did that. I have not spoken to Cole. I have never been allowed to.

September 2, 2019

They sent a photograph to the Nokia. It was a hospital bracelet. COLE VOSS. DOB 03/14/2011. That is Nathan’s birthday, eighteen years later. I threw up in the sink.

October 11, 2019

The diagnosis is real. That part they didn’t have to fake. I don’t know if that makes this better. Dr. Okonkwo said eleven weeks if I’m lucky. I asked him if he’d ever had a patient wake up as somebody else. He thought I was joking.

November 3, 2019

Funeral is set. Maya picked the hymns. Nathan will do the reading because he thinks he should. I wrote this in the parking lot of the funeral home. If you are reading this, Nathan, I need you to understand I was already dead in the way that mattered. What they bury will look like me. It will have my hands. It will have the scar on my left knee from the bicycle. It will not have the memory of Binder Street. That stays with me. That is the only thing I get to keep.

November 8, 2019

Last page before I give the box to the unit. Rules for after:

1. Do not tell Maya until you have talked to S. Maya made her own arrangement. I don’t know when. I don’t know what she traded.

2. The Sunday calls keep the girl off the list. If the calls stop, they notice the girl. Then they notice you.

3. You will turn 33. That is the age they used for your father. That is the age they will come to see if the name is still sitting right on you.

4. If a photograph arrives, they are already close. Do not look for the person holding the camera. Look for the person who is missing from the room.

5. The other boy was already gone. I need you to believe that or you will try to find him and you will give them a second name.

Then the entry from two days after the funeral, which I already told you about.

And then nothing until the last page. The August 28 line. That one is in a shakier hand. It looks like it was added later. Years later. The pencil is darker. The paper around it is cleaner, like the notebook was closed for a long time and then opened once.

I don’t know who wrote that last line.

It looks like her. It looks exactly like her.

I called Dr. Okonkwo’s office this morning. I used the old insurance ID still in a photo on my phone. The receptionist was kind. She said Dr. Okonkwo retired in 2021. She said she could request records if I was next of kin. I said I was the son. She put me on hold.

When she came back her voice was different.

“Mr. Voss, the file we have for Helen Voss shows a discharge. Not a death. November 6, 2019. Against medical advice. There’s a note that says the family requested no further contact. There’s also a second file under the same social. That one is sealed. I can’t open it from here. I probably shouldn’t have said that.”

I asked her what the second file was named.

She lowered her voice.

“It’s just a first name. Cole.”

I sat in my car outside a grocery store and tried to breathe like a normal person.

Then I did the other thing I wasn’t supposed to do. I looked up Binder Street.

There is a Binder Street. It’s 40 minutes from here, in a neighborhood I have no memory of ever visiting. I drove there. I told myself I wouldn’t get out of the car.

The house is a blue Cape with a chain-link fence and a plastic toddler slide on its side in the yard. Nobody came out. I sat there for twenty minutes like my mother did.

In the front window, taped to the glass from the inside, there was a child’s drawing.

A woman. A little girl. And a third person with no face, just a gray circle, standing a little apart from them.

Under it, in crayon, someone had written: FRIDAY GRANDMA COMES.

I took a photo. I am not posting the photo.

When I got home the Nokia was on the counter where I left it. New text. Not a picture this time.

You went to Binder. That was dumb. Maya lands at 6:40. Do not let her in if she isn’t wearing the ring. The real one has a scratch on the inside of the band. If she isn’t wearing it, it isn’t her.

I don’t know who S is. I don’t know how she knows I went to Binder Street. I don’t know how she knows Maya’s flight.

I called Maya from a different number, a Google Voice thing, because I am losing my mind in increments.

She picked up on the first ring.

“Nate. What number is this.”

“Are you wearing Mom’s ring?”

Silence. Long enough that I thought the call dropped.

Then, very carefully, the way you talk to a dog you don’t trust: “Who told you to ask me that?”

I hung up.

It is 4:51 p.m. Her flight lands in less than two hours if the text is right.

I went to look at Mom’s ring, the one Maya was supposed to have inherited. The small sapphire. I have a photo from the funeral. Maya’s hand on the casket. The ring is on her finger.

I zoomed in.

There is no scratch visible, obviously. It’s a photo.

But Maya’s hand in that photo has a thin white scar across the knuckle of her index finger. She got it opening a can of pumpkin the Thanksgiving before Mom died. I remember because I teased her and she told me to cook my own dinner.

I have another photo of Maya. From Christmas two years ago. Her hand around a wine glass.

No scar.

I have been looking at my sister’s hands for two years and I never noticed.

August 28 is the day after tomorrow.

Someone is going to knock on my door in about two hours, and she is going to look like Maya, and I do not know which version of my sister I have been talking to since 2019.

If I let her in, I’ll post what happens.

If I don’t, I’ll post that too.

I put the sapphire ring photo next to the Christmas photo on my phone and I cannot stop looking at the knuckle.
