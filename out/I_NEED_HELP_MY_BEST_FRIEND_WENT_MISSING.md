The title says it all. My best friend went missing. It’s been 2 years exactly since my buddy never came home. 

I’m going to give y’all some context before I get into the case. I’ve known Anthony Woodland since I was 14 years old. We met in gym class in 8th grade during the end of 2020. We’ve been best friends ever since. 

Anthony had a bad habit of dating crazy girls. Like, not “I’m gonna murder you crazy” but the stage 5 clinger crazies. The ones you have sex with twice and they think they’re in love with you. He dated a few of those in high school. All of which have strong solid alibi’s and did not commit the crime. 

Anyhow, into the actual story. 2 years ago it was a Thursday. The next morning, Anthony’s parents called me frantic asking if he was at my house. I told them no, he wasn’t. I hadn’t heard from him since 2 pm the previous day. 

I raced over to Anthony’s parent’s house. We were only seniors in high school. It’s not like he had a job he traveled for or anything. He had absolutely 0 reason to just take off. His parents weren’t abusive in any way, so he didn’t run away for a new life. This all stuff that we knew 100%. 

His mom showed me that his last known location was at his ex’s house. Maxine’s house. I took a deep breath. I had no idea they were even seeing each other again. 

Maxine was probably one of the weirdest girls that Anthony ever “dated.” I use air quotes because they were officially a couple for like two weeks, then broke up, but later went back to hooking up. They were extremely on and off. 

We decided to call the police, but since Tony had really only been missing for a tiny amount of time, they said there wasn’t anything they could do, but they’d keep a look out for his truck and last known worn clothes. 

Mrs. Woodland was rightfully pissed that the police refused to do anything, but unfortunately they couldn’t. Tony had just turned 18 two weeks ago, we could technically legally leave and not be stopped. But I knew he wouldn’t do that, and so did his parents. 

I asked Mrs. Woodland a question, “How did you not notice Anthony not come home last night?” 

She sighed, “I don’t know. I fell asleep super early after day drinking with Dennis yesterday. I woke up to pee at like 2 AM, but didn’t think to check Anthony’s room. He’s an adult I can’t really make him come home anyway.” 

It was a fair point. We went through all the details we all knew. Mr. Woodland, or Dennis, as Mrs. Woodland refers to him, said that Tony seemed off yesterday, almost upset. I agreed. When we’d played Minecraft that morning he just wasn’t in his usual chipper mood. 

Mrs. Woodland pointed out that usually in November that Anthony is down during the colder months. He’s always been that way. Dennis and I did agree, but it just seemed worse. 

“I mean it makes sense. He’s got seasonal depression and then he gets back or is hooking up with a crazy girl again, of course his mental health was in the toilet,” I responded. “You guys don’t think he…?” 

They both answered with seconds, “No. There’d a be note if he did right?”

“There’s not always one, but I know Anthony would write one. Hell, he would’ve written one for every person he knew. There would’ve been a binder of notes,” I said. This was definitely not a suicide. I hoped. 

Dennis explained that he said goodbye to Tony around 4:30 PM yesterday. He asked where Anthony was going but he either didn’t hear him or just ignored him. Dennis said he didn’t push because Tony was already in a shitty mood that morning. 

After that no one heard from him. So I went home, and now we just have to wait. Mrs. Woodland, or I should really be calling her Hannah, as that’s her actual name, posted a missing poster and details about Anthony on every Facebook page. Obviously I was assuming this had something to do with Max and her family. I mean, they were the last to see him alive, right? 

For the next day everything sucked. We didn’t receive any communication from Anthony, and by this point his phone was completely offline. The texts didn’t even send, calls went straight to voicemail, and his location was shut off completely. His Life360 account was deleted as a whole. His AirTag on his keys were deleted off Hannah’s find my phone along with his phone. His other devices never went with him. 

When it was finally long enough, the cops began building a case. The first thing I did was go down to the station to share details along with Dennis, Hannah, and Anthony’s little brother Wade. All of our alibis checked out, and we were all let go and our statements taken. 

They then went and got Max’s family and took statements from them. They had strong alibi’s, and even showed a ring doorbell video of Anthony leaving their residence at approximately 8 PM Thursday night. So they didn’t really do anything, according to the cops. I didn’t believe it though. This was just the beginning. 

Since there was no reason to believe that Max’s family, the Felmings did anything, they didn’t ask to search their home or property. But Max’s dad, Arden, offered to let them search the house, and the police did. They didn’t find much of anything, and what they did find all led to nothing. So the Flemings were off scott free. 

There was still no body turning up. That was the weird part. So it wasn’t exactly a homicide yet. From there, the case went cold. They took statements and everyone had alibis that proved they didn’t do it. They wondered why Tony’s location never changed once he left, but the cops just chalked it up that he paused his location or he turned it off. He likely didn’t want his parents knowing he was with his crazy ex. 

For the next 4 months, we heard nothing, and we all just assumed that Anthony was a weird case of disappearance. I watched his folks work tirelessly to dig for more information, but there was just absolutely nothing. Whoever did this, if anyone did this, cleaned it up extremely well. 

It was the day of graduation, I was sitting in the middle as my last name is Johnson. I was staring at Anthony’s seat in the W section. He was supposed to be here. I wiped a tear away, and focused on the principal speaking. 

After graduation, I found my family and took some photos. They hugged me tight and said they were sorry that Anthony wasn’t here. We went home, I partied with some friends in memory of Anthony. It’s what he would’ve wanted. 

The following morning I woke up with a splitting headache to my phone ringing. I answered it. It was Mrs. Woodland. Apparently they just found Tony’s truck. I flew over to their house to get more details. 

I sat down at the counter with the Woodlands as Hannah spoke, “This is from the lead detective. This happened last week but they weren’t able to update us until now. Last week on Wednesday we received a call from a local mechanic who claimed someone dumped their truck on his property. When we went out there, the truck description matched Tony’s truck, but the stickers, decals, and license plate had been removed. After running the VIN number, we confirmed it was his.” 

Dennis, Wade, and I looked at each other. No one knew what to think. Why would Tony dump his truck at a mechanics shop? Especially one here in our hometown? 

“We brought the mechanic down for a statement as the truck had clearly been sitting there for a while. It was covered in dust, the battery was dead, and there was a nice family of rats living in the engine bay. The guy claims that it showed up a few months ago but he never said anything thinking the owner may come back looking for it,” Hannah continued. 

Dennis sighed, “That’s so dumb. If he’d reported it months ago, when it showed up, we may have had a fucking lead on where Anthony is!” 

“Yeah this fucking idiot. Hold on, let me finish. The detective says they asked him about the decals and license plate missing, and he claimed that those were gone when the truck arrived. When asked for an alibi, he said he was at home with his wife the night Tony disappeared. His ring doorbell proved it. He arrived him at his residence at 6:03 PM. Anthony hadn’t even left the Fleming’s residence by then.” 

“Well we knew the mechanic didn’t do it,” Wade said trying to lighten the mood. 

No one laughed. We just sat in silence. Another roadblock. The police looked that truck up and down for any sort of evidence. They even took finger prints from the steering wheel, but there was so many partial ones and they overlapped, that not the smartest person in the world could decipher it. They tried to check the dashcam, but the SD card had been removed and all data was deleted. 

The case went dead. Sometimes a random tip would come through that someone spotted Anthony in a town over, but it never led to anything. 

But that was all forever ago. He’s still missing. Please, please, please, help us. If you meet anyone with the name Anthony or anyone with short brown hair, deep brown eyes, a light mustache, 5’8, and wears derpy clothes, just ask if they’re okay. I miss my best friend. Please come home if you’re reading this Anthony, please. (I will update if anything happens)
