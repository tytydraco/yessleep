I shouldn’t have been out there that day. It was my weekend to go home, to bask in the air conditioning, sit on my couch, sip a beer, and watch baseball. Instead of this well deserved break, I was out in the oppressive heat guiding about two dozen middle schoolers up a three mile trail to a campsite, like a shepherd except the sheep ask stupid questions and make bad jokes.

There are worse jobs then a camp councilor I suppose, I could’ve been doing inventory in a warehouse again. My group had been angels that week, fourth graders who liked nothing more then to catch fish and frogs, eat s’mores, and build forts. This morning they packed up their bags, said goodbye to their new friends, and went to their respective buses. As I was beginning to gather my own things to head home for the first time in a month, my boss strolled up to me and pulled me aside.

“Hey Skip, hows it going? I have a proposition for you”

“Yeah?”

“We had a little incident last night with the teen campers, fight in the boys cabin, but that’s not the main issue”

“Yeah? Then what is?”

“Livy and Ethan where nowhere to be found and it took Zeke coming over from his cabin to break up the fight”

“No shit”

“They showed up an hour later smelling like skunk though, meaning I can drug test and finally fire those dumb asses, but that leaves the rest of their campers who weren’t involved with no councilors to take them on their tent campout up on the point.”

“You want me to do it”

“Time and a half skip, you’re a trooper and I know you’ll do it, John and Noel will meet you up there”

Mr. Kurtz had me there, and while I’ll complain about it, I’ll gladly take overtime pay to take some kids on their “rough wilderness campout” for 2 nights up on the point.

The day was hot, the July sun beat down on the wide brim of my hat, making it feel crisp to the touch. The air was oppressively still, not even giving the hint of a breeze for any relief. Taking a dink from my water bottle felt like taking a sip of soup, yet I still greedily drank, knowing soon we would arrive at the pump, and then soon our campsite. The pines towered above our heads, making the winding path seem as though a giant, winding maze. Between the trunks of the trees grew thick brambles and ferns, with the occasional new tree poking its head above the green tangled blanket.

Soon enough we arrived at the point. The campsite was a clearing on top of a ridge overlooking the lake far down below, the cloudless day made the water shimmer like a gemstone. All around the trees where like hairs on a head, blending together to form a unified, thick coating to nearly everything you could see. At the very edge of your vision you could see the silos of a few farms, standing like medieval watchtowers standing above all around them. The fire pit sat right on the edge of the drop, with a semicircle of benches like a classical theater sitting all around. The campers began to fan out in search of good spots to pitch tents and sling hammocks, breaking out into their respective cliques.

I sat in the shade on a rock, fanning my slick face with my hat, and waited for the inevitable calls for help to rise above the drone of the young teenagers talking and joking with their friends. Sure enough, I was only sitting for three minutes before I was going between every tent group helping them assemble their well worn tents. Improvisations had to be made between parts breaking and campers forgetting to grab what they where told too, but that might be my fault for only reminding them seven times. When the tents where all up, I sent the campers to collect wood for our fire. John and Noel would be here soon with the food for the weekend, and the sooner my belly was full of off brand chips and hot dogs, the better.

My coworkers rounded the corner soon enough, and the kids where tasked with setting up the fire before we lit it. Their arguments and discussions provided good chatter as a backdrop to our own gossip.

John fanned his face, taking off the hat emblazoned with the obscure collage mascot where he played football, his dark face speckled with sweat

“Why does management keep hiring such dumb people, Ethan and Liv where on thin ice as it is, and they thought they could get away for a smoke session with *this* group all alone?” He gestured his arm to the bickering kids.

Noel brushed her dark hair off from her face, pink from the heat and the sun.

“Kurtz is on the warpath now, he wants this place run like the navy but upper management keeps holding him back”

“I think I would welcome that at this point” I said “I have been here 3 summers in a row now and they always have me running around like a headless chicken fixing whatever for them, yeah it would suck not to goof off but some of these new hires just can’t get with the program”

The campers came over then to inform us that they had gotten the fire all ready to be lit, it did not catch the first time, and John and I had to struggle for another fifteen minutes to finally light the cooking fire. The kids soon crowded around the fire jockeying for position to cook their dinner. After they had some food and a desert of s’mores in them, They began settling down around the fire, with their side conversations slowly dyeing down as the sun sank lower into the July sky. Eventually one of them piped up.

“Can we here a story?”

A chorus followed in agreement, and we exchanged looks.

“I got one, its a scary one if that’s ok?” I said

Nods of agreement came from the kids

“What do you think we are, babies?”

“Ok ok, this is one I heard from my father and his father before him, its a real story of this area. You guys might not know this, but these woods are ancient, they have been here for thousands of years, but there was a time when they where young. The glaciers had just retreated at the end of the ice age, and people began living here in large numbers. While it was warmer then it was before, the winters where still bitter. One such band of people had had a lean year, and with the unforgiving cold approaching, they had to do something or they would surely die.

The chief gathered his two sons, and sent them out to try to find something, anything that would save them. They went out deep into the young woods, carrying the fate of their people on their backs. The elder brother was strong, yet this strength had made him arrogant, he would not listen to the thoughts and ideas of his younger brother. The younger brother was small, but his eyes where keen and his mind sharp, it was he who found the tracks of a heard of deer. They followed the tracks till they found the heard, thick as fleas on a dog, grazing in a clearing on the slope of a hill. The elder brother wanted to shoot as many deer as they could with their bows, but the younger brother had an idea, to get the deer to run themselves off the side of the steep hill into the rocks below.

The elder brother would not listen to his brother, and began to ready his bow, when the younger decided to act, knowing the elder would waste their tribes only chance at life. He ran like a man possessed, driving the deer off the side of the hill, just like he had said. The meat would be enough for the winter, they went back to the tribe and they rejoiced, for they had been delivered.

The chief was proud of his cleaver young son, and also distraught with the elder son’s bullheadedness. ‘A man who can not hear the ideas of others is no leader,’ and he gave the birthright to his younger son.

Rage and jealousy began curling around the young man’s heart like a snake, worming and weaving itself into every facet of his thoughts, until every waking thought was rage.

Eventually, years passed, and the chief died. As was the customs of their people, he would need to be wrapped in a new buckskin for his burial. This responsibility fell on the sons, and following the burial, the younger man would be chief. Jealousy had been building, like water behind a dam, and as the years had worn on the dam had grown frail, and as the brothers trekked into the woods, the leaks began to show.

It did not take the brothers long to track a deer, a large buck, with antlers like outstretched arms. They shot, and they shot true, and the deer fell. The young brother knelled over the deer, taking his knife out to give their pray a quick and dignified death. The back of his brothers head, the rock on the ground, the black heart, they all gave way. The stone smashed into the skull, and the kinslaying had been done.

Animals have spirits, and while not particularly good or bad, they wish to respect the order of things, honor and the natural law. Only a coward strikes a man in the back, and only a fiend spills the blood of his own family. The spirit of the deer, while fading, was still aware of what was happening around it, and the rage that filled the spirit was enough to bring it back to the mortal coil.

The blood of the deer began to flow back into its wounds, the arrows where pushed out and clattered to the ground. The beast stood on its legs and towered above the kinslayer, who stood in shock.

The beast came down on top of the brother, and they rolled, bit, scrambled, the man in a desperate attempt to escape the debt he now owed creation for his crimes. When it seemed like he was about to break free from the animal on top of him, the body of the beast became like thick mud, flowing and forming around the kinslayer until he was engulfed, and his screams muted.

The man did not die, for the beast believed there where worse things then death. If you go out into these woods at night, and listen closely, you can still here the muffled screams of the man and the wet, guttural cries of the deer. Over the centuries they have became almost one, and they wonder the earth, the man looking for someone, anyone, who he can inflict a fraction of his own pain on, so keep your tents closed, and if you hear bumps in the woods at night, don’t go investigate, or the monster will…

GET YOU!”

I got some of the kids to jump, mostly the ones who weren’t paying attention. A satisfying amount of them had wide eyes, yet none of them were crying, the hallmark of a successful camp ghost story.

“Alright, with that,” Noel announced, “everyone brush your teeth and go to the bathroom, we’ll be waking up early to make breakfast and to get ready for our hike, so make sure to get a good night sleep!”

“I kinda liked that story” John leaned in and said, “Is that actually a family legend?

“No, this is one I’ve been work shopping a while, I’ll be thinking about tweaks to it on the hike tomorrow”

“Last time we where up here John told the kids the whole plot to *Rudy* as their bedtime story” Noel chimed in

John stood up and stretched, “That one is a *classic,* and they loved it”

Noel got up and began pouring water on the fire “Zeke will give history lectures to put his campers to bed, these kids will listen to anything”

“Well, they seemed to like my story, hopefully it’ll keep them in their tents”

The night was oppressively hot, a night where no matter how much you drank you still feel thirsty and the humidity is like the breath from a gaping maw. You toss and turn yet sleep seems to never come. I tried to think of pleasant things, but my mind went back to the cool house, the soft sofa, the cold beer, and the soft hum of the television. To try to make myself feel better I thought of the overtime pay, of all the beer and pretzels it would buy, but a future pretzel brings little comfort to the man in the present trying to sleep on the hard ground.

I reached for my water bottle, but quickly found I hadn’t secured the lid right and it had leaked all over the floor of my tent. I groaned, not only was I hot and thirsty, I also would have to air out my tent in the morning. Herding moody kids is already rough, but doing it while your head swims and thoughts refuse to form is torture. Reluctantly I put my shoes on, the worn fabric rubbing on my bare heel, my socks had gotten the brunt of my leaky water bottle, and opened my tent to the inky black of the night.

The fear of the dark is one of mans oldest fears, from when we hunched over watch fires, looking into the pitch darkness, hoping nothing would stare back. At this time, I was not terribly scared of the dark, but it gave me a sense of unease. The sliver of moon provided an echo of light, yet the shadows between trees made lakes of shadow, black as tar. I shoved down the unease, I had been in the dark camping a hundred times, this time was no different. I trudged to the spigot, outside of camp a little ways, down the trail past a bend. As I leaned over and began to fill my bottle, the sound of a faint groaning became audible, slowly, at first barley above the rush of water, then slowly growing louder. The blanket of sleep was now completely thrown off me as my eyes began scanning the black.

The sound was like a pair of crushed lungs trying to draw their last breath, of weak desperation. It was getting louder, and closer. My mind began racing. I came to the conclusion it must be a sick animal. A hurt animal is a desperate, and thus a dangerous animal. I began plotting my course back to the safety of my tent when there it stood before me.

Ridged knobby legs sat atop two hooves, graying on the edges and cracked, like the great ruins of some monument from a long dead civilization. The torso was covered in mangy matted fur, and the hips twisted and bent in angles never meant to be. Lifelessly the two front legs hung behind the barrel of the torso, now heaving with desperate breaths. The origin of the wheezing towered above me, its lifeless inky eyes staring no way in particular, its mouth agape with teeth rotted to a dark green. The antlers where as wide as I was tall, bleached to a spectacular white that cast a ghastly glow around it. Jetting out of the torso was a human hand, grasping as if drowning, the skin like that of leather, dried and tanned as if it had been in the sun for a century. Barely visible beneath the sea of tangled fur was the face of a man, only one eye was visible. The deep brown iris and furrowed brow conveyed only one thing, rage.

A void filled my gut as adrenaline flowed to every corner of my body, run, Run, RUN!

It only took a few seconds for the thing to close the distance, I felt the sharp alters in my lower back and then my feet leaving the ground as I was thrown towards the dark sky. I landed on my back, hard, with my head bouncing off the packed dirt below. The air was knocked out of my lungs, and before I had the time to inhale, the thing was on top of me. The white antlers where pressed against my face, each bump and spike catching the skin, leaving my face shredded and bleeding. Instinct drove me now, I grabbed the alters and pushed them away from me as hard as I could, but it was like fighting against a bolder about to crush you. As they inched closer and closer to my face the hand began clawing at my chest with its long, gnarled nails. Pain shot through me like lighting, I could feel its hand trying to reach inside me, I could feel it poking through my ribs, grasping at my organs. Panic was taking hold, and I began desperately kicking my legs up at its hips. One blow landed true, and I was able to shift out from under it.

Blood stung my eyes from the thousand cuts on my face, what was left of my shirt clung to my body, soaked in my own warm blood. The thing righted itself, now on all fours, and studied me, plotting its next move. The gnashing of the rotten teeth filled my ears, rising above my own desperate panting. The lone hand began clawing at its host, still frenzied from our exchange.

I began pelting the thing with stones in an effort to keep it at bay, hoping, praying that it wouldn’t strike again. I was panting, gasping, fighting the burning pain in my chest to get more air into my body. If I went down to the ground, I would not have the energy to get back up. I yelled at it as loud as I could, a mix of spit and blood spraying out of my mouth as I did. It faltered, but only slightly. As it readied itself for another charge, however, beams of light began emitting from the campsite behind me, I had woken up the campers.

It galloped off back into the inky black, and as it did I felt static in my mind. I tried to turn around and walk to my saviors, but soon found myself first on my knees, then on my front, the dirt mingling with my blood. I heard Noel’s panicked voice, but it felt like I was sinking below the water. Soon the world turned black as I sank out of consciousness.

I awoke in a hospital bed some time later. An endless stream of camp employees, lawyers, and conservation officers picked my brain for every detail of that night. After the first few hours, psychologists became the primary visitors.

They said it was a number of things, at first it was a rabid bobcat, but why alters then? Then it was a deer with chronic wasting disease, but that doesn’t make them attack people. Then it was a rabid deer, but then again it was way to big to be a normal deer. They chalked up all the fantastical elements of the story to my dehydration, and told me it was just a rabid deer.

I know what I saw, the creature from my own story, came to life. Maybe eventually I’ll lie to myself enough to believe it really was just a rabid animal, but I haven’t yet. When I try to sleep I still hear the wheezing, still see the hand and the accusing, angry eye. I don’t think ill ever go into the woods again, or sleep with my lights off for that matter.

But hey, I still got my time and a half.
