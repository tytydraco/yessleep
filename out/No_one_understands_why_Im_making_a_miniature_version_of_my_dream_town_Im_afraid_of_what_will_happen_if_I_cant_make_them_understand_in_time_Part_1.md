Have you ever had that dream where you suddenly realize you own another house, bigger and cooler than your real house, and you wonder why you don’t live there?

Well, eight months ago I started having a dream like that but instead of a house, it was an entire town.

I hadn’t been sleeping well and a series of events in my life, including a job loss, a messy divorce, and a resulting cross-country move for a new job opportunity were contributing factors.

But if I am to be completely honest, I’ve never been one to fall asleep and stay asleep easily.

That fact, combined with a brief obsession with the work of Carl Jung in college, got me into the habit of keeping a dream journal. It’s one of the more consistent habits I’ve managed to keep over the ensuing 20 years. If I dedicated myself to the gym the way I have to my dream journal, I’d probably be competing in bodybuilding competitions.

It’s an easy habit to keep when you wake up at random times all night. Far easier than maintaining a gym routine, at least for me.

Waking up frequently throughout the night means I tend to remember my dreams and I have developed skill in recognizing when I’m dreaming. Lucid dreams come naturally to me and I’ve trained myself on techniques to stay asleep as long as possible while lucid. Those techniques may only tack on 2-5 minutes or so, but dream time doesn’t work like time in the waking world.

An extra 5 minutes could be tantamount to an extra few hours or even weeks, depending on what my brain decides to do.

The one that works best for me is intensely interacting with my environment: picking up objects and turning them over in my hands so I can feel and see every detail, asking questions of people who are clearly meant to be background characters, eating food (although I’ve never managed to get flavors to work), and when all else fails, lying down to “go to sleep” in my dream.

That last technique usually results in being transported somewhere else, so I only “sleep in my dream” when other ways to stay in it are failing.

This is all to preface that finding myself in my own town wasn’t all that strange.

My first visit landed me in a small downtown area, if you could call it that, which consisted of an actual Main Street. I remember laughing the first time I saw it and chiding myself for my lack of originality.

Main Street. How cliché!

Then I stopped and examined my thought: chided *myself* for *my* lack of originality.

That’s when I realized the town belonged to me.

Do you ever just “know” something?

In that moment I *knew* the town belonged to me and understood this was probably a lucid dream.

So I did what I usually do when I try to keep my lucid dreams going. I started exploring and tried to examine every detail.

Main Street held a few residential homes and a number of small businesses.

For the most part these seemed quite ordinary.

I remember a cafe with a picture of an anthropomorphic bagel holding a coffee mug and   grinning widely to reveal a schmear of cream cheese teeth, the spaces between them defined by small rows of poppy seeds.

There was a convenience store advertising Pep Squad  
Sodas, each label featuring a tiny blond cheerleader doing a split, arms raised, hands obscured by fluffy red and white poms poms.

Two busy gas stations seemed far too close together but the closeness didn’t appear to affect business for either. Long lines of cars sat along every row of pumps.

I didn’t question why so many people were waiting to fuel up in this tiny little town.

Other than that, I saw a law office,  a dentist, a darkened bakery that appeared closed, various  restaurants, a brick pub with walls that felt rough against my hand and left a residue of red powder behind, and an appliance store that mainly seemed to sell vacuums.

Then there was the bookstore.

The bookstore was easily the largest business in town, stretching across what could easily be four or five other storefronts in length and extending deeply towards the other side of the street. Other buildings stopped about halfway across and transitioned into small parking lots or courtyards.

The first night I had the dream, I stopped into the bookstore.

There I noticed two things right away.

First, the books were real, in that I could pick them up and read them. Some were classics I recognized, Melville’s *Moby-Dick* and Kafka’s *The Metamorphosis* notably standing out on that first visit. Others were ones I’d never heard of by unknown authors, but when I picked them up and opened them, they were filled with legible words that seemed to form coherent sentences.

Don’t ask for book reports. I don’t remember all the details, but I do remember how the books were tangible, complete, and free from the illogical tropes usually characterizing dreams books.

The books did not grow or shrink or turn into bowls of soup in my hands. They were written in clear English and the words did not leap and jump across the page.

The bookstore was extraordinary in its ordinariness, if that makes sense.

Mundane in a way dreams typically aren’t.

The other thing I noticed was that the proprietor of the bookstore was my recently-ex wife.

Except she wasn’t my ex-wife, even if she was a dead ringer for her.

My ex-wife is a middle school music teacher, not a bookstore owner.

Her name is Jess, not Daphne, which was the name in block letters on the silver name tag she wore.

And most of all “Daphne”, unlike her real world doppelgänger, was happy to see me when I walked  
through the door.

She greeted me by my name, smiled, and said: “Everyone at The Blue Cat’s been waiting for you!”

“What’s The Blue Cat?” sprang from my lips even as I simultaneously noticed the sign over Daphne’s head.

The Blue Cat Readery.

Is “readery” a word?

Daphne raised her eyebrows and pointed one finger up towards the sign.

“You already know, don’t you?”

And it seemed like she meant more than the name of the store.

I was about to ask who was waiting when I was rudely thrust back into my own bed in my sad, middle-aged do-over apartment.

My disappointment was brief. I quickly turned on my reading lamp and grabbed my journal and pen from the nightstand. I know all too well that not furiously scribbling down everything I remember right away usually ends in only remembering I had a dream I wish I could remember later on.

I didn’t realize this first entry about my dream town would be the opening act of a saga that would soon dominate my life.

That part began around two weeks later when I found a miniature 1:12 scale version of The Blue Cat Readery on eBay, a perfect replica of the bookstore in my dreams complete with a tiny figure bearing an uncanny resemblance to my ex-wife behind the front counter.

A jolt of electricity shot up my spine and my scalp began to tingle.

Even as my brain tried to make sense of the impossible, my fingers seemed to move of their own volition and the next thing I knew, the item had been purchased, promising delivery within 5-7 days.

It was like I was physically compelled to buy it.

It was the first of 109 pieces I would buy in the following months, the first step in a domino-string of events that led me to my current life and realization of the path I have no choice but to walk.

Sooner or later, everyone will understand one way or another.

I just hope they understand in time.

I hope you understand in time.

Maybe no one will read this or care, but I have to try.

For everyone’s sake.

While we can still change the course of events.

It’s too much to address all at once.

The clock is in motion but if I unload it all *too* quickly, people will dismiss anything I have to say, chalk it up to a midlife crisis or mental breakdown.

So I’m finding balance.

I just hope it’s the right balance because I’m not getting any second chances.

And neither are you.
