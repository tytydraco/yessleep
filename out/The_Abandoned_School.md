This happened to me in June 1999, when I was 16 years old. At the time, I lived in Volgograd, Russia. Summer vacation had just started, so there wasn't much to do. My friend Mitya and I spent most of our days hanging around the neighborhood, riding our skateboards and occasionally exploring abandoned buildings.

There was an abandoned school on the outskirts of our neighborhood. It had been closed for several years, and nobody had ever bothered to renovate it. The building was slowly falling apart, most of the windows were broken, and bushes had grown all around it.

There were all kinds of stories about the place. Some people said there had been a crime there years earlier. Others claimed that a few teenagers had gone inside at night and disappeared afterward. Adults always said it was bullshit and just another local rumor.

That only made Mitya and me more curious about the place.

We liked exploring abandoned buildings. Back then, there wasn't much else to do. Neither of us had the internet at home, and neither of us had a cell phone, so during the summer we were always looking for something to get into.

That day, we rode our skateboards until almost evening. We'd bought them just a few days earlier at a local market and had spent almost all the money we'd saved up on them. Mine was black, with some cheap graphic on the bottom. It already had a few scratches, but I didn't care. I loved that thing.

Later that evening, we stopped at a small kiosk. They sold chips, gum, chocolate and imported soda. Back then, getting an actual Pepsi or Coca-Cola still felt like a little luxury.

I bought a Pepsi. Mitya got a Coke.

We sat on the curb drinking them.

Then Mitya suddenly said,

"Hey, let's go to the school tonight."

I knew exactly which school he meant.

"That one?"

"Yeah."

"Why the hell would we do that?"

"You've been saying you wanted to go inside."

"I said during the day."

He laughed.

I don't know why, but that evening I really didn't want to go there. Normally I would've been the one suggesting something like that.

Eventually, Mitya talked me into it. We agreed to meet at the same kiosk at ten that night.

I got there around ten to ten. It was still fairly light outside, although it was starting to get dark. Summer evenings in Volgograd stay light for quite a while.

Mitya showed up about fifteen minutes later.

He had a small flashlight he'd taken from his dad. He also brought a folding knife that he carried around all the time. At the time, we thought carrying stuff like that was pretty cool.

We headed toward the school.

Once we got past the houses, there were fewer and fewer people around. Dogs were barking somewhere in the distance. A few guys were sitting near a garage, talking. Music was playing from an open window on the first floor of one of the houses.

Then even that disappeared.

There was just the road and the occasional car.

We saw the school about ten minutes later.

It looked much bigger in the dark than it did during the day.

The fence around it had been broken in several places. We found a gap and climbed through.

At first, nothing seemed particularly scary.

We were even joking around.

Mitya shined his flashlight at one of the walls.

"Imagine going to school here."

I laughed.

We got inside through a first-floor window. Most of the glass was already gone, so it wasn't difficult.

It smelled like dampness, dust and mold. Pieces of linoleum, paper and old notebooks were scattered across the floor.

We walked down the hallway.

Some of the classrooms still had desks in them. Someone had carved names into one of them with a knife.

Mitya found an old notebook and started flipping through it.

"Look. The grades are still here."

We laughed about it for a while and kept going.

The second floor was darker. Mitya's flashlight started flickering, so he smacked it against his palm a few times.

That's when we heard the first sound.

Something fell downstairs.

We both stopped.

"What was that?"

"I don't know."

"Should we check?"

I said we should just leave.

Mitya looked at me and grinned.

"What, you scared now?"

I didn't answer.

We headed back.

Near the stairs, I noticed footprints in the dust.

They looked fresh.

I remember that was the first moment when I genuinely felt uncomfortable.

Mitya noticed them too.

"Someone's been here."

"Let's go."

"Wait."

He shined the flashlight down the stairs.

There was a staircase leading into the basement.

We hadn't noticed it before.

We went down.

The farther we went, the colder it became.

At the bottom was a long hallway with several doors. Most of them were closed.

There was light coming from one of the rooms.

At first, we thought it was a flashlight.

Then we realized it was candlelight.

Mitya switched off his flashlight.

We moved closer and looked through the doorway.

There were people inside.

Five of them.

They were all young. One had long hair, another was wearing a leather jacket, and one had a T-shirt with some rock band's logo on it. They looked like the kind of alternative kids you could see around the city back then.

But what was around them was definitely not normal.

There was a pentagram drawn on the floor.

Candles were arranged around it.

There were photographs hanging on the walls.

Several animal skulls were lying in one corner. One looked like a dog's, but I couldn't tell what the others were.

And in the middle of the room, there was a person lying on the floor.

At first, I thought they were unconscious.

Then I saw the blood.

There wasn't as much as you'd see in a horror movie. Just a dark stain on their clothes and on the floor.

A knife was lying nearby.

There was blood on the blade too.

I felt like I was going to throw up.

Mitya grabbed my sleeve.

I wanted to tell him we needed to leave, but at that moment one of the people in the room looked up.

He was staring directly at the doorway.

We froze.

For a few seconds, he just stared.

Then he said,

"Who's there?"

Mitya shoved me.

We ran.

I don't even remember how we got back to the stairs.

Someone shouted behind us.

Then we heard footsteps.

We reached the first floor. Mitya slipped on the stairs and almost fell, but caught himself.

I kicked the remaining pieces of glass out of the window.

We climbed out.

I caught my foot on something and almost fell, but I didn't stop.

We left our skateboards inside.

We just ran.

It took us about ten minutes to reach the houses.

The whole time, all I could hear was my own breathing.

When we finally stopped, Mitya said,

"The skateboards."

I looked at him.

"Forget them."

"But they're still in there."

"Let them stay."

We went home.

I didn't tell anyone.

The next day, I tried not to think about what we'd seen.

But a few days later, Mitya came to my house.

He was holding my skateboard.

I recognized it immediately.

"Where did you get that?"

"It was outside my apartment building."

I took it from him.

It was dirty, and there was dried mud on the wheels.

Someone had written something on the bottom in red:

WEDNESDAY 10:00 PM

At first, I thought it was paint.

Then I realized it was blood.

I asked,

"You didn't tell anyone, right?"

"No."

"Neither did I."

We stood there for a while, just looking at the skateboard.

Then Mitya said,

"We're never going back there."

I agreed.

That should've been the end of it.

The school was eventually demolished. Mitya and I never went back, although we talked about that night for years afterward. Sometimes he'd joke that we should've gone back for the skateboards, but I always told him it was better to just buy new ones.

We stayed friends after school too, so every once in a while the subject would come up.

One day, a few years later, we were sitting at his place and somehow started talking about it again.

I asked him,

"Do you remember the skateboard they found outside your apartment?"

Mitya frowned.

"What skateboard?"

"Mine. The one we left at the school."

He was quiet for a few seconds.

Then he said,

"There was something I never told you."

"What?"

"I took my skateboard that night."

At first, I didn't understand.

"What do you mean?"

"When we ran out, I went back for it."

I looked at him.

"What the hell? You told me we left both of them there."

"I know."

"Where was it?"

He shrugged.

"On the stairs. I just grabbed it and ran."

I didn't say anything for a while.

Then I asked,

"Then whose skateboard did they find outside your apartment?"

Mitya went quiet too.

"You said it was mine."

"Yeah."

He looked at me.

"So your skateboard was with you the whole time?"

I nodded.

We sat there in silence for a few seconds.

Then I asked,

"Are you sure you actually left yours at the school?"

I didn't answer.

Because I was sure.

We left both skateboards inside that school.

But if Mitya really took his, and mine somehow ended up outside his apartment a few days later, I have no idea who brought it there.

And I still don't know why someone wrote the exact date and time we'd arrived at the school on it.
