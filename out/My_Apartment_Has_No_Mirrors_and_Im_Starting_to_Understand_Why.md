I moved into unit 4B because the rent was stupidly low for being so close to downtown. The listing said “recently renovated, utilities included, no pets.” What it didn’t say was that every mirror had been removed. Not a single reflective surface anywhere. No bathroom mirror, no medicine cabinet, no full-length on the back of the closet door. Just blank drywall patches and screw holes where they used to be.  
I asked the landlord about it when I signed the lease. He was an older guy, Mr. Halvorsen, with the kind of tired eyes that have seen too many tenants come and go.  
“Previous occupant had some… issues,” he muttered. “We took them out to help her. You want mirrors, you can put your own up. Just don’t come crying to me if you don’t like what you see.”  
I laughed it off. People are weird. I bought a cheap full-length mirror from IKEA that same afternoon and leaned it against the bedroom wall. Easy.  
The first night I woke up at 3:12 a.m. with the distinct feeling that someone was standing behind me. You know that prickle on the back of your neck? I sat up, heart hammering, and looked at the mirror.  
It was empty. Just my rumpled bed and the dark room behind me. No silhouette. No one there.  
I chalked it up to new-place nerves and went back to sleep.  
By the end of the first week, I noticed the mirror was always slightly off-angle when I came home from work. I’d leave it straight, facing the bed. I’d come back and it would be turned five or ten degrees toward the doorway. I started taking photos with my phone every morning before leaving. The mirror was always in a new position by evening.  
I confronted the building manager. She just shrugged and said, “Old building. Settles funny. You want maintenance to bolt it down?”  
I said yes.  
They bolted it. Heavy L-brackets into the studs. Solid.  
Two nights later I woke up again, this time at 3:33 a.m. The room felt colder. I looked toward the mirror and froze.  
My reflection was already sitting up, staring back at me. I was still lying down.  
It didn’t move when I moved. It just watched me with this patient, almost pitying expression. Then it slowly raised one hand and pressed its palm against the inside of the glass, like it was trying to reach through.  
I screamed. Scrambled out of bed so fast I hit the floor. When I looked again, it was just me—disheveled, terrified, breathing hard. The reflection matched perfectly now.  
I slept on the couch for the rest of the week with the mirror turned face-down.  
Things got worse.  
I started finding long black hairs in the shower drain that weren’t mine. I’m blond. These were thick, coarse, and at least eighteen inches long. I don’t know any women in the building who visit me.  
My phone started taking photos by itself. I’d find new pictures in my camera roll taken at odd hours—always from the corner of my bedroom, angled toward the bed. Sometimes I was sleeping. Sometimes I was sitting up, staring directly at the camera with an expression I don’t remember making.  
The worst one was timestamped 3:33 a.m. last Thursday. I was lying in bed, eyes open, but my face was wrong. The smile was too wide. My eyes were completely black, like someone had colored them in with marker.  
I deleted them all. Then I put the phone in the freezer for a night like an idiot, because that’s what people do in horror movies and I was desperate.  
It didn’t help.  
Last night I came home from a double shift and the mirror was gone.  
Not turned down. Not broken. Gone. The brackets were still screwed into the wall, empty. There were scratches on the drywall like something had clawed its way out.  
I called the landlord. No answer. I called the building manager. Straight to voicemail.  
I searched the entire apartment. Nothing. Then, because I’m a moron who apparently wants to die, I checked the bedroom closet.  
There was a new mirror inside.  
It was standing upright at the very back, behind my hanging clothes. Tall, cheap, with a plastic frame. The exact same one I’d bought from IKEA. But the glass looked… thicker. Greasier. Like it had a thin layer of water on it.  
My reflection was already standing there waiting for me, fully dressed in the clothes I had on right now, even though I was still in the doorway.  
It smiled first.  
Then it stepped forward—out of the mirror—and walked straight through the hanging shirts like they weren’t there. It moved exactly like me, same slight limp from an old knee injury, same nervous way I rub my thumb against my fingers when I’m scared.  
I ran.  
I’m typing this from my car in the parking lot of a 24-hour Walmart two towns over. I have my go-bag, my laptop, and $680 in cash. I’m not going back to that apartment.  
But here’s the part that made me type all this out instead of just driving until the gas runs out.  
I just opened the camera app to check my face in the front-facing lens, because I needed to see something normal.  
My reflection isn’t moving right.  
It’s blinking a half-second too late. And when I smile, it doesn’t.  
I’m posting this from my phone now because the laptop screen just went black and I can see something moving behind me in the reflection of the dark windshield.  
If you live in the Oakwood complex off Highway 14, do not take unit 4B.  
And whatever you do, do not put up your own mirrors.
