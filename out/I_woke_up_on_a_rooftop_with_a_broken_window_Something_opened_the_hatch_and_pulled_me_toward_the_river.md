I don't know how I got there.

I woke up in total darkness. I don't remember falling asleep. I don't remember where I was. The air was cold and damp, and I could smell dust and rotting wood. My head was pounding.

I turned on the flashlight on my phone.

There was a broken window next to me. And there was nothing outside the window — just the open sky. I was on a roof. A private house — I could tell by the shape of the yard below. The moon was half full, casting pale light on the trees.

And then I heard it. Footsteps. Coming from inside the house. Slow. Heavy.

I froze.

And then — the hatch. Someone was opening it from below.

I backed away, my heart hammering. Then I heard shouting from the street. Someone was yelling outside — probably drunk people, or neighbors. I didn't care who they were — I just wanted them to see me.

But before I could scream, something moved near the hatch.

A long pole. No — it wasn't a pole. There was something round in the middle of it, and around it were smaller sticks that looked like fingers. It was reaching for me.

I ran to the other side of the roof. And then I saw it.

A face.

It was looking at me from the edge of the roof. Pale. Eyes completely black — no light, no reflection. Just two black holes staring at me in the moonlight. I couldn't move.

The floor beneath me began to give way. The roof started to collapse. I could feel it buckling. Then it grabbed me. I don't remember if it pulled me down or if I fell myself. But the last thing I felt was cold air rushing past me — and the sound of water below.

A river.

I woke up on the riverbank. It was still night — maybe an hour later. My clothes were wet, and my legs were covered in scratches that looked like finger marks. A strong, bad wind was blowing — I could hear trees creaking and branches snapping. I had no idea where I was, but I recognized the shape of the house in the distance. I ran back toward it.

The broken window was still there. I climbed inside. My room door was open. I went in and closed it behind me. I hid under the bed.

Then I heard it — something crawling along the wall outside, right toward my window. I held my breath. The window shattered. Something slowly entered the room. I saw long, thin fingers — like the pole from the roof, but longer, about a meter long. They started touching my things — my desk, my chair. I was terrified.

I bolted from under the bed and ran. And I saw it.

The creature was outside, but its body was round and spider-like — its neck was impossibly long, stretching through the window, with that pale face and black eyes. Its arm was reaching for me. I ran to the kitchen and locked myself under the table. I dialed 911.

The operator answered. I whispered: *"Someone broke into my house — there's something here, it's not human — please send help."* They said they'd send a car and told me to stay on the line. But the line went dead.

Then I heard the kitchen wall crack. That same long arm broke through the plaster and started grabbing everything — plates, pans — throwing them around. It was feeling for me. It would find me any second.

I ran again — this time to the basement. I hid in a corner, behind some old furniture. About two minutes passed. I saw a shadow — a human silhouette, about two meters tall. But it wasn't a normal shadow — it seemed solid. I blinked. It was gone. I thought I was hallucinating.

Then I noticed things were missing from the basement — boxes, tools — they had vanished. Something was very wrong. I was so exhausted and scared that I passed out inside a pile of old bags.

I woke up this morning. Everything was gone — no broken window, no scratches, no creature. The house was normal. I called the police station to ask why they never showed up. The officer said: *"Sir, we never received any call from you last night. Are you feeling all right?"*

I don't know what happened. But I keep thinking about those eyes — and why it let me go.
