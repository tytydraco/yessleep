*Have you lived a life of sin?*

 

Maisy Philips had butchered her husband and two children. We found the husband first, he was sprawled out at the bottom of the stairs, neck broken.  The first of the two children, the son aged five, we had found in the bath; floating face down, blue all over. The daughter, aged sixteen, was in the kitchen. We counted twelve stab wounds over her chest, though it was the slit to her throat which had finally killed her. Maisy, we had found last. A trail of blood from her daughter’s body had led us to her hiding place in the pantry. She was cowering in the corner; her eyes were red with tears, and the sleeves of her white wool sweater were caked in dried blood. She put up no fight as we dragged her to the car outside – she had called us after all. Though, all the way, she pleaded in a weak voice that she hadn’t done it. I lost my cool and was too rough forcing her into the back of the car; my partner Henry was quick to reprimand me. A man of forty-two, twenty years my senior, he had been assigned to me as some form of guidance figure. But what did I care about this women’s comfort - after seeing what she had done.

 

 

Henry and I didn’t talk for the whole thirty minute or so drive from the suburbs back to our station in the city. Maisy picked up the slack for us, continuing her petition from earlier through her sobbing. She claimed that she didn’t remember anything from after picking up Michelle from her track practice at five. When she came to again, she was already standing over her dead body, knife in hand. This only moved to further piss me off, not only was she still denying it but what a poor story she had come up with. Henry remained unphased however. I think I could even see pity in his eyes.

 

 

We pulled into the station at just gone ten. Upon arriving, Henry told me to go around and get Maisy out while he made a phone call (stressing to do so carefully this time). I obliged and stepped out; the October chill was out in full force tonight. While gently guiding her out of the car I noticed she had stopped crying. However, she had now begun violently shaking. Pausing his phone call, Michael called out from his window that I was to take her inside straight away and sit her in the furthest interview room. He also told me not to bother checking her in as he would do it when he came in, I moved to protest, but he had already returned to his call. This was not up for discussion.

 

 

I moved through the sea of people in the lobby of the station. In any other place Maisy’s current state would have caused quite a scene, pale and shaking as she was, still covered in blood. But through this madness no one seemed to notice us as we parsed through the crowd to the interview room. I had just gotten Maisy settled and cuffed to the table when Henry finally arrived. He shot a quick look outside and seeing no one was coming locked the door behind him. He looked panicked.

 

“You alright?”

 

He didn’t seem to register my question, electing instead to pull a chair out and place it across from Maisy. However, he didn’t sit in it but simply returned to the door and begun peering out of the closed blinds facing the rest of the office.

 

“Are we expecting someone?”

 

Again, he didn’t answer me. We stayed like that for what must have been ten minutes. Both standing, him in one corner me the over; all the while Maisy just stared at her reflection in the metal table. In my boredom I began to consider the rooms camera, it was just above Henry’s head. Though, it had been out of operation since Tuesday. Was that why Henry had asked for this room specifically? It was then that his guest arrived.

 

 

A man clad in a black hooded raincoat entered; it must have been raining outside now. The man was older than Henry, maybe by about another ten years. Henry took his coat revealing the man was wearing a clerical collar. He sat down in the chair that had been left for him across Maisy, but she had not raised her head to regard him.

 

“Hello Maisy, I’m Father Jacobs but you can call me Matthew if you like?”

 

He had brought with him a cup of tea, probably gathered from the machine in the office, and he placed it in front of her. But still, she didn’t raise her gaze from her reflection.

 

“My friend Detective Henry has asked me to ask you a few questions if that’s alright?”

 

I turned to Henry in protest, but he merely shot me another hostile glance. He was really starting to annoy me with this obscurity.

 

“Now I’ve been told you don’t remember anything from what happened tonight, is that true?”

 

For the first time she rose to regard him, lightly nodding her head.

 

“That must’ve been scary.”

 

I was biting my tongue so hard I thought it might bleed.

 

“Have you had any blackouts like that before?”

 

She took a moment.

 

“Yes, once. This morning when I was taking the children to school. If the other driver hadn’t swerved, I would’ve surely hit him.”

 

“Maisy, have you ever cheated on your husband?”

 

With no more grace or ceremony had he delivered this question than any prior. In turn Maisy answered with same mundanity. I mean in her current position of what importance was it.

 

“Yes. I met a man named Christopher about a month ago”

 

“Is he rich?”

 

“No, but he is rather handsome and very sweet. Much sweeter than my husband ever was.”

 

The woman was pouring her soul out and couldn’t be less bothered. Even her earlier faintness and shaking was gone. Did priests just have this effect on people?

 

“Were you planning to go away with Christopher?”

 

“Yes.”

 

“Is that why you let him into your home?”

 

“How did you- Yes… Yes, I did.”

 

“You lied earlier, didn’t you? This morning wasn’t your first blackout. You blacked out with Christopher.”

 

“How- Yes but- How did you… my head hurts”

 

She was now holding her head in her hands; her shaking had returned and her nose was now bleeding. Henry asked me to leave and get her some ibuprofen and tissues from the staff room. I came back about five minutes later (I had taken some pleasure in making her wait) but when I returned the interview room was now empty.

 

 

That was a week ago now, Henry still hasn’t been back into work. According to the records clerk no Maisy Philips had ever been checked in, Henry had lied. I had held off on reporting anything, despite my issues with Henry, I had respected him, I thought I trusted him. That was until today. This morning we got a call from the owner of a local scrapyard. Maisy Philips had been found dead.
