To whom it may concern, 

There is an old village north of here. 

It called out to me once. It spoke to me in my dreams numerous times. I can’t explain to you how I know it did. I just know it. I dreamt I had fallen from space and through the core of the earth. Down there are the dead, and down there I saw my own remains. I know I was buried there, in that unknown place. 

I’ve been preoccupied with the subject of death ever since my father died. We weren’t close, but that was the first time I really thought about it. The idea that all you are and all that you’ve seen can mean nothing because you’ve sunken into oblivion and are no more. 

At first I thought that recurring dream was just my mind working through my own confusion over the subject of death, and my own mortality. The first time I truly felt it meant something was when I found out my father was born there, in that village, and that, as far as anyone knew, his entire lineage could be traced back to it. My forefathers made a life for themselves there once and I’d never known. I told you, we weren’t close. I barely knew the man, or saw him. So I thought to myself, how could it be that I was dreaming of a place I never knew I was from? Well, “from” in some sense at least. I’ve never been. 

My wife Astrid keeps telling me I’m overthinking it,  and that I’m spiraling over the idea. She says I always spiral over a certain issue when life is too quiet, and she’s not wrong, but this is different. There’s no way I could’ve known any of the latter information I just relayed to you. I don’t think she believes me though, and I can’t blame her. It’s hard to believe such a thing. Especially when her focus is on more important matters such as the upcoming birth of our first child. I’m worried too, excited, but worried. It's our first time being parents you know? Everything will change for good, so I do understand why Astrid isn’t as supportive as she usually is, and honestly, this is something I need to figure out by myself. 

I’ve decided I need to clear this out of my head. I’m going to meet some family up there. I know I have a grandmother. 

**\*\*\***

I was on the road for about a day. I left Astrid a note telling her where I’ll be, and when I’ll be back. I didn’t want her to worry. Besides, she’s got her brother. He lives nearby with his wife. If she needs anything, he or his wife will take care of it till I get back.

The village was unlike anything I’d seen in our country before, but not necessarily in a way that would please me. The main thing that struck me was how empty and frankly boring it seemed. I drove through the streets as I navigated my way to my family home. The home my father grew up in. There was nothing distinguishable about this place except perhaps potholes in unpaved roads, a notable lack of street lights, and sparse homes in between. You could see some deadwood in the distance amongst the sandy landscape, but that was about it. I would never think to visit here if not for all the questions on my mind.

In any case, I met her. My paternal grandmother. Eloise is blind and slightly hard of hearing. You’d think this would hinder my communication with her, but on the contrary, I found her very pleasant. She’s a quiet woman, but she’s very hospitable. She was glad to meet me. At least I think so. She lives alone in a somewhat dilapidated yet large house, and of course she has a carer hired for her by some relatives. Her name is blanking on me, but she’s a young lady with a foreign accent I couldn’t place. She was the one who led me up to the guest room. “I’ve been having dreams. Strange ones. It’s very difficult to articulate, but essentially I was here and I saw the dead below the ground. I saw myself too. I was buried here. What does it mean? Elouise tilted her head towards me. As though she felt a shift in the air, as though she could somehow see where I was seated. I watched her expression change into something I did not recognize. “That’s not you.”

“The body you saw is not yours. Certain things are generational. They run through a bloodline. Perhaps that’s why you’re having dreams, but this has got nothing to do with you. You weren’t born here. You weren’t here as a child. You’re safe.”

“What do you mean by that? I’m finding it difficult to piece together these riddles.” 

“You don’t need to know. I told you, I’ve said enough. I’m sorry dear, but it’s best if you go now. Your wife needs you, does she not?” 

I understood it was time for me to stop pushing the subject. That was the end of my conversation with Eloise.  I left shortly after that. She was right; Astrid needed me. Questions were still floating through my mind; I can’t lie, but whatever it is I couldn’t continue to abandon my marriage just because I was having an existential crisis.

Astrid found it difficult to forgive me for leaving without a word. She’s refusing to speak to me, and I understand but I was only gone for a week. I told her so.

“No! I never said that.” I’ll admit I raised my voice a little, but it’s only because I was frustrated with her. She was choosing to misunderstand me. She walked away. Ever since then we haven’t spoken.

“Astrid? Are you okay?”

“But if we go, when?” 

“We’ll talk about that later.” 

I looked at her for a moment. I was genuinely sorry I had upset her this much. I didn’t know what to say except that I loved her. 

“I love you too.” 

**\*\*\***

I contacted Elouise’s carer. I asked if Astrid and I were welcome, and I told her I wanted to introduce my grandmother to my family. Of course, she welcomed us. I half expected that, as I mentioned, she was a pleasant woman. 

Weeks had passed, me and Astrid didn’t spend much time packing before we were on the road. I asked her if she was sure about this and she reluctantly reassured me every single time. I glanced at her several times during the trip. I tried to read her face. Astrid was always reserved. She was an outspoken woman, but paradoxically she was always reserved when it came to how she really felt deep down. Once she decided something she committed to it and never let her feelings get in the way. It was one of the things I didn’t like about her. It was hard for me to read her emotions. Like now, all the way to our destination she didn’t say a word about regretting it, but I can tell you I felt the tension.

This time I saw the town in a different light. A certain fear gripped my chest as I drove through those familiar streets again. I couldn’t understand why. I just chalked it up to my fixation going too far, and now I had dragged Astrid into this. This is the last thing she needed. I was insane. I had actually lost it. What was I doing? 

“Astrid, let’s go back,” I told her as she gripped my hand. “Are you kidding? We drove all the way. We have to see this through now.” 

“Since when are you this invested? I thought you hated that I cared so much.” 

“I did, but it matters to you so it should matter to me.” 

“No I’m serious. I’m ready to put this behind me. I’ll go see a shrink, anything. None of this means anything; I was clearly going through an episode. Maybe from stress or the funeral, I don’t know. This just doesn’t feel right anymore.” 

Astrid’s eyes widened. “What’s gotten into you? You keep changing your mind. My legs didn’t cramp for hours on end for nothing. Let’s at least meet Elouise, okay?” 

That was the end of it. I didn’t know the source of Astrid’s sudden desire to stay, but I knew I couldn’t argue once she’d made up her mind. Truly though, deep down, I knew this had gone too far. I don’t fully know why, but I was truly afraid of this place now. I was afraid of how it consumed my thinking. Astrid was right from the start. Regret filled my chest and I wanted nothing more than to turn back. 

**\*\*\***

We’ve been here for two weeks now. My wife Astrid had taken a liking to the place. I had no idea why; it was truly off-putting. Every night she sits below the window, and when I question her she tells me she’s “basking in the moonlight.”

“Astrid, baby, what do you mean? As if there’s no moonlight back home?” 

“Don’t be a smartass. There’s light pollution in the city. This is nice! Give it a chance.” 

“Whatever. I can never win an argument with you.”

Day after day I figured she wants to stay a while longer. Whenever I ask her about returning to our home she says her brother is looking after the house, and that she needed a break more than she knew. She tells me I should go back if I want. As if I’d do that.

I’d never leave her here alone, so I started working on my soon-to-be-due work projects from here, Elouise’s home. I stay up late working. I don’t know; I guess I thought I needed to outdo myself. The fear of my boss getting fed up with me once and for all, as nice as she is, is always in the back of my mind. 

Most nights I hear nothing, but other times I hear Elouise and Astrid staying up all night laughing. It gets annoying. I love Astrid, but I can’t understand her anymore. This is ridiculous.

**\*\*\***

Astrid’s laughter got louder each time I heard it. Though Elouise’s home is large I could still hear her from downstairs. 

I followed her voice, as she wasn’t in the bedroom. I’ve checked. 

At the end of the hallway I saw my wife’s silhouette, but her laughter had ceased. “Astrid?” 

I couldn’t believe it. First it was me, and now Astrid was acting strange. I had to put a stop to this, and quickly, I thought. 

I approached her darkened silhouette but each time I stepped closer she faded further like a mirage. Now I knew something wasn’t right with me, or her. I didn’t know anymore. I shouted her name. Hoping she’d hear me and come shake me out of my trance or wake me up if this was a nightmare. I yelled her name until I heard her footsteps approach me; soon her shadow was looming over me. But there was nothing. The noise disappeared. I could’ve sworn she was right behind me, and that her shadow was physically pulled from under me. 

Perhaps it was some kind of animal or this was it. My suspicions were true, and I was losing my mind. 

I’m slowly accepting it, especially since she woke up in our bed as though nothing had happened the next morning. “Astrid, I’m losing my mind,” I told her as she slowly rubbed her eyes. 

**\*\*\***

I don’t know how long it’s been anymore. I don’t bother with my work projects. I figure at this point I’ve lost my job anyway. 

My wife insists we’ve only been here a week. It doesn’t make any sense. Elouise goes about her day as normal. The carer comes and goes. The house taunts me more and more every day. No, this damn town does. There are laughing noises in the distance every night. I don’t know where they come from, and now there are smiles twisting into shape on these filthy walls. They’re mocking me.

Astrid says I’m crazy, but she won’t leave. She refuses to. This isn’t about me anymore, and she doesn’t bother pretending.

She talks about how beautiful it is. Like she’s been here a thousand years. She goes on and on about it. “This place sees me. It’s the most beautiful thing we’ve ever seen. I feel like we were meant to come here you know? Like God gave you that dream so we could visit.“

Bullshit. She doesn’t even believe in God.

**\*\*\***

I’m not proud of what I did, but I had enough. I left Astrid behind. I had to get out of here. Astrid can stay with my grandmother until she comes to her senses, but I was losing mine. I wasn’t going to let that happen. I drove for what felt like hours, but truthfully I don’t know how long I drove. 

Every single time, I ended up at the same fucked, cursed point. On a rocky road amongst dispersed homes overlooking deadwood in the distance. What in heaven's name is going on? 

I’ve broken down in tears several times over however long it’s been now, but now my tears have run out. There was no way I was going back to that damn house now. I had to figure out how to leave, and quickly. 

The last thing I remember is parking our car in the middle of a dirt road. I left it there and I walked out. There was no light besides the dome lights in the car’s interior. I turned my head to the night sky and chased the moonlight that Astrid apparently loves so much. I ran, and I ran and I ran. 

That’s all I can remember. 

When I opened my eyes again I was lying on the ground, covered in sand. The only thing in my field of vision was a strange little house unlike any others here. It seemed like a mud house, with light escaping from underneath its front door. 

Of course I had no other choice but to knock, ask for help, as futile as that was. 

To my surprise, the door creaked open.

There was a woman with beady green eyes behind it. I couldn’t tell her age; it didn’t matter. “What do you want?” 

“I need help. I don’t know where I’m going. I’m just walking; I’m not from here. I know you have no reason to help me but”

“Come in. Hurry.” 

**\*\*\***

The woman… she didn’t give me a name. I wouldn’t care to remember it anyway. I asked her what was going on, why I couldn’t leave, was I going insane? 

“Why are you here?” She asked me. 

“Do you know Elouise? She’s my grandmother.” 

“Elouise knows me. Her mother was an old friend.” She smiled. 

“Why can’t I leave?” 

“It’s not my place to speak on such matters. This is a dangerous town.” 

I know I shouldn’t have, but I grabbed her shoulders and shook her. I demanded she give me answers. I was sick to death of this vague nonsense. From Elouise, from her. I had to leave. I had to figure out what was up with my wife, what was up with the strange dreams. 

She grinned at me again. “Sit down then, I’ll tell you, but you can never tell Elouise.” 

“Tell her what?” 

“You need to get your wife out there. You need to leave. Elouise isn’t who you think she is. She’s keeping you here against your will.” 

“Why?” 

“Because she’s a sick person? Is it not clear?” 

“You just say things. That’s not an answer.” 

“I don’t think you’re in any position to ask questions.” 

“Never mind that. What do I do? How do we leave? I couldn’t even leave on my own for the life of me.” 

“You don’t. Well not yet. You need to bring your wife to me. She’ll be safe. Her and your baby.” 

I was taken aback. I hadn’t told her my wife was pregnant. “How did you know?” 

“What?” 

“I never told you we were having a baby.” 

“Everyone knows everything here. This town speaks to us.” She said. Her narrow green eyes glimmered with conviction. 

After everything I’d seen, I had no reason to doubt her.

“How do I get my wife out? She won’t leave with me.” 

She dug her hands into a leather bag lying on the ground behind her chair, as though she was waiting to. Then pulled out a stone; at least it seemed like a stone to me. 

She handed it to me, and I examined it. It was a rock, with what looked like a strange rune carved into it. “Take this with you. It breaks black magic momentarily. Bring her here.” 

“Magic? Is Elouise a witch?” 

“You could say that. You could also say she’s an evil woman. I’ll explain it to you soon enough.”

**\*\*\***

I didn’t know how. It seems this place truly is as insane as I knew in my gut. When I walked out my car was waiting for me, seemingly untouched. 

I drove past the mud house until it was out of sight, until I was back at Elouise’s house. It wasn’t hard to find Astrid. I saw the worry in her face drained of color, drained of patience, and I knew she’d been waiting for me. 

“Where were you?” 

I didn’t have the heart to tell her I was a coward. That I was so terrified I tried to leave her. 

“Astrid, you have to come with me.” 

“Why?” 

“We need to leave. Your brother was in an accident. He’s hurt, bad.” I felt guilty lying to Astrid, but I knew I was justified in my doing so. 

“Charles is hurt?” 

“Yes. We’ll be back honey; don’t you worry, but we”

“We need to check on Charles.” She finished. 

I was surprised that what that woman said was true. Astrid was finally ready to leave with me. I held her hand in mine and led her out. I didn’t know if she could tell it was trembling. 

We drove for so long under the cover of the night, which proved as hard as I expected; I didn’t even know if we would actually be safe soon. 

**\*\*\***

As she promised me, I found her house again. Astrid didn’t question why we stopped here. She seemed to be under a trance. To be frank, this seemed to be a while in the making. 

As soon as we were through the door she grabbed Astrid’s shoulders and sat her down. 

She looked up at me questioningly, and in return I glanced at her in confusion. “Where is the stone?”

“With me.” 

“You need it to leave. Give it to me.” 

I fumbled through my pockets. Those seconds felt like hours dragging through. I felt my body freeze over, then overheat again as though she’d set a fire under me. “It’s not with me,” I said with a lump slowly forming in my throat. “Can’t you make another one?” 

“No, I can’t. That was the only one and now you’ve lost it. If you can’t find it you’ll never leave this town. You’ll die here.” With urgency rising in her voice she continued. “If you left it at Elouise’s house, you need to go back there and get it.”

“You don’t have a choice. Just go.”

“You can’t take her. Not if you care about her. Not if you care about your child.”

“You know I will. I’ll treat her as though she was my own child.” 

“…. Thanks. We’ll never forget your kindness.” 

I was out the door, heading back to my grandmother’s house. I began to think if maybe I was hallucinating again. Or if it was actually possible for us to leave. In any case, I would do anything to get us out of here. 

Elouise’s house wasn’t far from here, but every dreadful second I was driving back there felt like eternity.

“Yes, it is. The town knew your wife was carrying life.”

“It’s not your fault Elouise. This decrepit place takes what it wants. What’s in the soil?! Why does it want us?” 

“I don’t know.” 

“What can I do?” 

“I told you.”

I left my grandmother’s home. I drove for hours. Between the deadwood. Across from it. Across the sand. Between abandoned homes. Through the streets, and off the streets. There was no sign of the strange mud house. No sign of Astrid. 

I roamed the town like a madman for days, dizzy with hunger and thirst. I screamed Astrid’s name, over and over, but she never heard me. 

Well, I don’t know. 

Believe me. God knows I tried. 

I drove until I saw nothing, and soon I realized, this was a familiar path. I was leaving the town. Anxiety gripped my chest. Instinctively I stared at my rearview mirror. There she was. The green eyed stranger, but no Astrid. She was holding an infant in her arms. She was smiling at me, mocking me. 

The tires screeched as I turned the car back towards her. I didn’t know what my aim was. Scream for my wife to be returned to me? Run her over? I didn’t know. 

But she was no longer there, and I was back on my path away from this strange Northern Town. Now it seems, it was refusing to let me back in. At least today. 

**\*\*\***

It’s been a decade since I last saw Astrid. For years she was legally declared a missing person, then eventually she was declared deceased. 

It seemed everyone thought that, whatever happened to my wife, she was never coming back. My neighbors turn their heads away when I pass them by. They think I hurt her. I think that hurts the most out of anything. 

I have dreams about her; they don’t stop. When I’m awake, I can’t remember what that town looked like, but in my dreams I always, always remember. It’s always the same dream. 

I see piles of bodies. I see my wife’s rotting corpse a far cry from the woman she was. My chest almost bursts with grief and joy as she stretches her arms towards me. To me she is still beautiful; she is still my world, my wife. I walk up to her and she cradles my face as I look deep into the pitch-dark void inside her eye sockets with nothing but a white, putrid, thick liquid seeping out of them. And then she collapses into sand, becoming one with the ground. Becoming one with the soil of that cursed town. I watch the ground swallow the sum of her parts, and I watch more mud homes springing out of the ground in the distance as it continues to swallow her whole. In those dreams I always dig through the sand until I am buried too. 

I find myself face to face with what I thought years prior to be my own remains, but it’s Astrid. It was always her. Only my Astrid and an unborn child in her womb rotting alone in the ground, without me. The only part of me that is dead is whatever died with her. Whenever I try to hold her, die with her, the ground wraps around me and I slowly rise until it spits me out.
