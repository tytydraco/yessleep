The golden rays of sunlight shine across the branches of the trees onto the thick forest floor. The clouds in the sky are few and far between. A narrow stream of water flows through the forest to the lake, going past the life on land to the life in water. As I bow down for a refreshment of water, I hear the sound of something carelessly breaking a twig beneath its feet. It makes me a little more cautious of my surroundings, even though it is most likely a squirrel. As I lean down to get another drink of water from the creek, the sound of artificial thunder rains across the sky, drowning out the silence of the land, filling the void with the sound of everything running in fear from the hunt. The sound of thunder rained once more but landed beside me. The sound of the artificial thunder is not new to me.

 While I was young and still under my mother's care, it was during the night when I could hear the sound of the artificial thunder rain across the sky. Nothing broke the silence that night except the thunder that filled it. I stayed snuggled up to my mother; her sense of safety and warmth brought me comfort throughout the night. I still remember how she would pull me close until the rumble of thunder would pass. I also remember the sound that rained upon us that night, the artificial thunder brought lightning with it to strike my mother where she once lay. It took her not only her life, but her body too. Even though I ran as fast as my feet could go, the pain got worse step by step, but I still ran. They were faster than me, even at my fastest; they still were faster. The only remnants of my mother were the blood trail left behind from them dragging her limp body throughout the forest. I followed that blood trail in hopes I could see my mother one last time. I ran more and more, even though the pain in my legs got unbearable, I still ran for her. The trail began to get thinner and thinner until it ran dry. I lost my mother that night; however, I never lost that fear that they might come for me next.



The sound of artificial thunder rained across the land once more; however, this time striking me down. Striking punishment on the innocent. Pain from my wound surged up my leg, firing signals to run, to run as I did for my mother.  Another rained throughout the forest. My leg was barely working. With each step, I felt my severed tendons twitch, straining towards a purpose they could no longer serve.  I powered through the pain, trying to overcome the fact that now my leg is useless to my escape, tripping myself into a thorned bush. As I fell, the barbed vines severed their own piece of me. The fall felt never-ending, lost in time, not knowing how long it had been. Retaining me there, I saw the beauty of life in the roses nearby, glowing a dark, deep red, the same color the ground beneath me was becoming. Seeing the beauty in the pain that life may bring, being happy that thorn bushes exist, knowing beauty can hurt.  As I take in the last bit of life, I bask in the fact that I no longer have to live in the fear of being the hunted.
