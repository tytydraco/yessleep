My best friend John and I grew up together. We were seniors at the local high school in a quiet, suffocating little town up in the northern U.S.—the kind of place tucked deep inside endless miles of towering pine trees. You know the type: where the air always smells like pine needles and damp soil, and the woods feel just a little too quiet when dusk falls.

 

I remember Halloween evening like it was burned into my retinas. The sun was bleeding out into a bruised purple, and the bite of the late October chill was settling in. John and I were heading out to the town’s annual street party. I was wearing a cheap, tacky Dracula costume—plastic fangs and a nylon cape that flapped in the wind. John, on the other hand, was built like a brick house. He was the varsity basketball star who spent half his teenage life sweating in the weight room, so naturally, he was wearing a tight black compression shirt and jeans, showing off every inch of his muscle to impress the girls. The only actual "costume" he bothered bringing was a battered Jason Voorhees hockey mask.

 

"Seriously?" I asked, eyeing him up and down as we walked down the asphalt. "Just a hockey mask and your gym shirt? What are you supposed to be, a slasher on a budget?"

 

John flashed that cocky, million-dollar grin of his. "Damn right. Trust me, man—a hockey mask and big arms are all it takes to get the girls talking."

 

It was around 5:00 PM when we reached the end of the road, right where the town began to fray into the deep woods. Standing all by itself on a lonely, isolated lot was the old Victorian house. It was a terrifyingly beautiful ruin—gables like jagged teeth, rotting wooden wrap-around porch, and glass windows so dark they looked hollow. Local urban legend said a witch used to live there back in the day. As a kid, I always thought that was a joke. *A witch in a Victorian house? What is she, a Gothic schoolteacher?* I’d walked past that place hundreds of times and never saw a single soul. The shutters were perpetually sealed.

Yet, there was one thing that never made sense: every single Halloween, without fail, the front lawn of that dead house was decorated with a witch mannequin and carved jack-o'-lanterns.

 

And this year was no different. Right in the middle of the overgrown yard stood a hideous, crooked witch mannequin astride a broomstick. A ring of thick wax candles flickered violently in the twilight breeze around its base, and right at its feet sat a freshly carved pumpkin, its hollow eyes glowing.

 

I pulled up short, tugging John’s sleeve. "Doesn't this weird you out?" I pointed toward the Victorian house. "I've lived here my whole life and never seen a single shadow move behind those windows."

 

John shrugged, his shoulders flexing under his shirt. "Town gossip. Just some stupid story our moms pulled out of their asses to keep us from breaking in and getting tetanus."

 

"Come on, look at it!" I pressed, pointing at the witch mannequin. Its painted face was contorted into a vile, stretched-out grin, like it was frozen mid-cackle, ready to plunge that broomstick right through our throats. "Every year, someone sets this shit up. Who?"

 

"Who cares?" John nodded, but then a slow, mischievous smile spread across his face—the kind of smile that always meant we were about to do something profoundly stupid. "Actually... you know what? Follow me. Let's make this night interesting."

Black, dead glass. Nothing there. But my skin prickled all the same.

 

"Watch this, Dave. It's Halloween, let's go wild," John chuckled.

"Hey, grab your phone! Take a picture for my Insta! The girls are gonna lose their minds!"

My jaw hit the pavement. I stood there, utterly speechless, staring at him.

 

John stopped right in front of me, adjusting the heavy, hollowed-out gourd over his shoulders. "What do you think? Way better than a generic hockey mask, right?"
