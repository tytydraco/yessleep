Now, I don't mean intellectually. He's actually quite smart. Like, 4.0 GPA in college smart. 

It's just that whenever someone asks him a question, he takes an unusually long time to respond. Not because he doesn't know the answer. He does. In fact, I'm convinced his brain has already processed the question, formulated a response, and moved on to something else entirely. It's his body that seems to be struggling to catch up.

Almost as if there's a delay between the moment he thinks something and the moment he's physically capable of expressing it.

I've asked him about it before. He says he doesn't know when it started because he doesn't even notice he's doing it. As far as he's concerned, everything is perfectly normal.

It's something I've always found endearing, actually.

I even joke that he lives in a different time zone.

But lately, the delays have been getting longer.

Much longer.

"Hey, Daniel, did your mom tell you what we need to bring today?"

He didn’t respond.

"Did you ask—"

"Yeah, just dessert."

"Just dessert? Well, I think we have some unopened ice cream in the freezer."

I opened the freezer and pulled out a carton of chocolate ice cream.

"We'll bring this—"

"I bought some ice cream last week," he said, pointing toward the freezer.

He stood up from the table and walked over. He stared at the empty shelf for a few seconds.

"Where'd it go?" he said.

"Daniel, I already got it. Are you okay?"

Silence.

His eyes were fixed on the empty shelf. There was something deeply unsettling about the way he stood there. His expression wasn't confused or concerned. It was completely vacant, like someone had pressed pause on a person.

I waved my hand in front of his face.

"Daniel? Here, sit down."

I took him by the arms and carefully guided him back to the couch.

"Just sit here. I'm going to finish getting ready. Give me two minutes, and then we can leave, okay?"

He didn't answer, so I just went into the bedroom to change.

When I returned, Daniel was still sitting in the exact same position.

Same posture. Same expression. Same fixed gaze.

"Are you ready?"

"Yes, I'm okay."

"Okay... well, let's go, then. Are you okay to drive?"

"Okay."

Daniel normally drives us everywhere. I have an intense fear of driving, for whatever reason. The second my hands touch the steering wheel, a wave of anxiety washes over me. It's like being trapped inside a metal box of death, surrounded by other people who are just as capable of making a mistake as I am.

But I figured he was fine.

He said he was fine.

The drive to my in-laws' house started off uneventful. Daniel sat upright, both hands on the steering wheel, staring straight ahead. He looked perfectly normal.

Until we approached a red light.

Daniel wasn't slowing down.

"Daniel, it's red."

The car continued forward.

"Daniel!"

I slammed my foot against the floor, searching for an imaginary brake.

Daniel slammed on the brakes at the last possible second. The car screeched to a halt.

"Daniel! What the hell is wrong with you? It's RED! There's something seriously wrong with you. Screw your mom's birthday. We're going to the hospital."

I unbuckled my seat belt and practically threw myself out of the car. I hurried around to the driver's side, opened the door, and helped Daniel out.

He didn't resist. He didn't ask what was happening. He simply let me lift him out of the driver's seat and guide him around to the passenger side.

I pushed him gently into the seat, fastened his seat belt, and ignored the curious pedestrians watching us.

My anxiety spiked when I got behind the wheel.

"Daniel, can you give me directions? I don't know where I'm going. There are cars everywhere, and stoplights everywhere. I hate stoplights. Do I go? Do I stop? What do I do if it's yellow? I don't know!"

Daniel didn’t respond. He didn't move at all.

“Daniel?!”

I took a deep breath and just drove straight.

Yes, straight.

I didn't know where I was going, but eventually, I spotted a red cross in the distance.

Thank God.

“Nothing's wrong with me,” Daniel said as we walked into the emergency room.

“Well the doctor will be the judge of that,” I responded.

I gently kissed him on the cheek before the nurse took him to the exam rooms. 

“I love you,” I whispered.

I watched him disappear through the double doors before sinking into one of the waiting room chairs.

I must have dozed off because, before I knew it, someone was standing in front of me.

"Samantha?"

I opened my eyes, trying to adjust to the fluorescent lights.

"Yes, that's me."

"I'm one of the attending physicians. We've completed a physical examination and a PET scan. Everything appears normal."

"Normal?"

"Yes. No obvious abnormalities. No signs of neurological damage or anything that would immediately explain what you've described."

"But he can barely respond to anything. Sometimes he just sits there for minutes at a time. Today, he almost drove us through a red light."

The doctor nodded.

"Is Daniel getting enough sleep lately? Has he been under any unusual stress?"

"I don't think so."

"Well, prolonged stress and sleep deprivation can affect cognitive function. Anxiety can also contribute to some of the symptoms you're describing. I'd recommend monitoring him, making sure he's getting adequate rest, and following up if things worsen."

"That's it?"

The doctor paused.

"Excuse me?"

"That's all you can tell me? Everything is normal?"

"I understand your frustration, but based on the results, there's nothing immediately concerning that we can identify."

Then Daniel peeked around the corner.

"Hey," I said, standing up. "Are you okay?"

"You drive," he said.

When we got home, we ate dinner.

Or, rather, I ate dinner.

Daniel sat across from me, occasionally lifting his fork. He barely touched his food.

I couldn't stop thinking about what the doctor had said.

There was no way everything was normal.

"Listen, Daniel. I think this condition is more serious than the doctors are making it out to be. I'm okay with giving it a night, but tomorrow I think we should look into some specialized doctors. Maybe a neurologist. Someone who can run more tests."

I waited.

"Are you okay with that?"

Nothing.

I sighed and put my fork down.

"Daniel, I'm serious. I'm not sure we can function like this forever. It's hard to maintain a relationship when I can't even have a conversation with you. I don't know what's going on, and honestly, it's starting to scare me. But I'm willing to put in the work. I love you. I just need to know that you're still in there somewhere."

I smiled.

"Do you love me?"

Silence.

"Do you want to be with me forever?"

Nothing.

I looked down at my plate. My appetite had disappeared.

"Okay. Fine. We can talk about this tomorrow."

I finished eating and carried my plate to the kitchen before settling onto the couch.

Daniel was still sitting at the table.

Then I heard something.

Click.

Before I could get up, the front door swung open.

Two men came rushing inside. Both wore black masks and dark clothing. Both carried guns.

"HANDS UP! NOW!"

I screamed and threw my arms into the air, completely paralyzed with fear.

One of the men rushed toward me, grabbed my wrists, and violently twisted my arms behind my back. I felt the cold metal of handcuffs snap around them before he shoved me face-first onto the floor.

"Don't move!"

My heart was beating so fast I thought I was going to pass out.

"What is this? What do you want? Take whatever you want!"

The man ignored me.

Then I heard the other one speak.

"Hold up. What's this one doing?" he said.

Daniel was still sitting at the kitchen table.

Same position.

Same expression.

He hadn't even turned around to look at the men who had just broken into our home.

One of them approached him cautiously.

"Hey."

The man waved his hand in front of Daniel's face.

His eyes didn't move.

"Hello?"

He snapped his fingers.

Still nothing.

The second man walked over, keeping his gun pointed toward Daniel.

"What's wrong with him?"

"Must be drugs."

"Hmph."

The first man leaned closer.

"Hey, buddy. Can you hear me?"

Daniel stared straight ahead.

The man looked back at his partner and shrugged.

"Listen, dude. If you don't react, we're just going to take her. Is that okay?"

"Yeah, that's fine," Daniel responded.

“What, Daniel? DANIEL!” I screamed.

“Oh, there he is!” the man continued. “Listen, she’s a beautiful girl, the boss is going to have so much fun with her. It’s okay if we *touch* her right?

“Yes, of course,” Daniel responded.

“Daniel! HELP ME!” I yelled.

The men were laughing now. They stood me upright.

“Look, we’re going to have to break her leg, for safety measures, of course. Do you want us to do that?”

“Yes, I do,” Daniel responded.

One of the men jammed his shoe into the back of my leg. I screamed in agony.

“Daniel?” I could barely muster.

I lifted my head up to see Daniel still frozen, a small tear slowly falling down his face.

Then the men pushed me out the door.
