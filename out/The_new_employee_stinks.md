This is something I've had on my chest for a long time now. I don't really have anyone to tell, so I thought I'd share it here to get some peace of mind.

This happened around two years ago, before I went to university, during my gap year. I used to work as a waiter in a restaurant in Oxford, where I live in the UK.

Most of it was just the usual stuff you'd expect. I was a young adult working to save up for university. The job wasn't amazing, but the pay was good since it was in the city centre. There were occasional incidents here and there, but nothing major.

Things really started getting weird around November of my first year working there.

We had a new employee, and despite not seeing her for the first two weeks, I'd already formed a mental image of her from what my coworkers had told me.

She was old, apparently. Grey hair, short, and scrawny. But what everyone seemed to mention most was her smell.

They described it as a stench unlike anything they'd ever smelled before.

I remember thinking they were probably exaggerating. My coworkers weren't exactly known for being subtle, and they loved having something new to gossip about. Still, the way they described the smell stuck with me.

The third week was when I was finally due to cross paths with her. It was my first morning shift in a while.

I clocked in that morning and went onto the floor. Everything was set up perfectly for breakfast, which was rare. Most mornings, things were only just about ready by the time we opened. My coworkers were usually pretty miserable and, if they had a spare second, would be outside smoking rather than doing anything else.

That morning, though, everything was in place.

I was about to walk into the kitchen when I noticed a strange smell.

It was sour, almost like something had gone bad.

I couldn't really put my finger on what it was or where it was coming from. I just remember thinking that something smelled putrid.

I walked closer to the kitchen.

The smell became stronger.

Suddenly, I was hit with this immense odour of rot and something acidic. I remember my eyes watering almost immediately, and my nose started to sting.

What worried me more was that the chefs weren't in the kitchen.

I looked around.

Nothing seemed out of the ordinary. If anything, the kitchen was neater than usual. There was no obvious source for the smell. No overflowing bins, no food left out, nothing that seemed capable of producing something like that.

I assumed the chefs had gone into the walk-in fridge to find whatever had gone bad, so I stepped further into the kitchen.

I took another two steps.

That's when I noticed the figure standing there.

At first, I couldn't really make sense of what I was looking at.

It almost looked mummified beneath her clothing. It was small and extremely thin, with clothes hanging loosely from it's body.

I could tell it was the old woman I'd heard about from the clothing, but there was very little else about it that seemed immediately human.

The face seemed hollow.

The eyes looked loose in their sockets. The nose was long and sharp, seemingly drooping from the bridge. The mouth was dry and withered, and it's skin was pale, dotted with occasional freckles.

The hair was grey, but it didn't really look like hair.

It looked almost like plastic.

I couldn't understand the expression on it's face.

It wasn't anger, or fear, or anything I could immediately recognise. There was something about it that seemed almost contradictory. She looked incredibly weak, but there was also a kind of sorrow in her expression.

I couldn't explain why.

By this point, the smell was overwhelming.

There was no doubt anymore that it was coming from her.

I felt sick.

I didn't know how to react. My body seemed to freeze as I stood there, staring at the figure in front of me. I felt my eyes beginning to tear up.

It was like a flood of unwanted information had suddenly hit me. Like I'd realised something I wasn't supposed to.

The woman looked at me.

She turned her head towards me painfully slowly. There was something about the movement that made me question whether she was even human. I vaguely remember hearing her neck creak as she turned, although I can't be sure. My memory of those few seconds isn't exactly clear.

Then she spoke.

“Everything's finished. You can go relax.”

The voice made me shudder.

It sounded like nails being dragged across a blackboard, somewhere between a whisper and a groan. Somehow, though, it seemed horribly fitting for whatever was producing it.

I tried to say something. Anything.

Nothing came out.

I could only stare at her.

The only reason I eventually managed to take my eyes away was because one of the chefs, Jude, suddenly rushed into the kitchen from upstairs, through a door that led outside.

He walked downstairs completely calmly.

He came over to me, walked straight past the woman without even acknowledging her, and told me to go wait outside with the other chefs. His tone was more annoyed than anything else, like I'd done something wrong by standing there.

I didn't question him.

I wasted no time getting out.

Outside, all of the chefs were waiting. None of them seemed particularly bothered, apart from one of the apprentices, who looked like he'd seen a ghost.

I stood there trying to process what had just happened when Jude walked over.

“That woman hasn't showered in days, mate. I bet on it.”

The other chefs nodded in agreement, their faces showing a mixture of disgust and irritation.

“She smells so bad,” one of them said. “We can't even stand in there.”

I just nodded and went to stand next to the apprentice chef, as he seemed to be having a similar reaction to mine.

“You good, bro?”

That was all I could bring myself to say.

“No, y-yeah, I'm alright... Just... tired.”

The lie was as clear as day. I could see the pupils in his eyes shrink as he spoke, and he wouldn't look in the direction of the kitchen.

I had a gut-wrenching urge to call it a day and leave.

But I couldn't.

I knew I was probably going to get fired if I walked out over a smell, especially after some previous incidents between me and my manager that I won't get into right now.

So, for the time being, I had to see the day out.

Working with that thing.

After a couple of minutes, Jude told everyone to get back to what they were doing. Apparently, the woman had gone to do the kitchen laundry.

I didn't know why, but hearing that made me feel even more uncomfortable.

When I eventually walked back down to the kitchen, nothing seemed out of the ordinary.

The restaurant was now filled with the usual noise. Customers talking, cutlery clattering against plates, orders being shouted from the kitchen. Everything had gone back to normal.

The woman was nowhere to be seen.

I definitely wasn't going looking for her.

So I just carried on.

I collected plates, tidied tables, handed out water and did all the usual things I'd been doing for months.

But I couldn't get the thought out of my head.

Why was it only me and the apprentice?

Why was it that only the younger workers had reacted to her?

I tried to convince myself there was a simple explanation.

Maybe the older chefs were just used to her.

Maybe they'd already smelled it so many times that they'd stopped reacting.

That was what I kept telling myself, anyway.

It didn't really explain the apprentice.

Every time someone opened the kitchen door, I found myself looking over without meaning to.

The woman still wasn't there.

At least, I couldn't see her.

The morning went on normally. Orders came in, people complained about waiting too long, glasses needed filling, tables needed clearing. The sort of completely mundane stuff that usually makes a shift feel like it's never going to end.

But every so often, I'd catch that smell.

Only for a second.

I'd be carrying a tray across the restaurant and suddenly I'd get this faint sourness in the back of my throat.

Then it would be gone.

The first few times, I thought I was imagining it.

Then I noticed the apprentice doing the same thing.

We'd both pause whenever it happened.

Neither of us said anything.

Around an hour later, I was taking a stack of plates back towards the kitchen when I saw him standing by the doorway.

He wasn't doing anything.

Just staring inside.

I walked past him and glanced in.

The kitchen looked completely normal.

The woman wasn't there.

“What's up?”

He didn't answer.

I looked at him.

He was still staring into the kitchen.

“Mate?”

He finally looked at me.

“Did you see her?”

I knew exactly who he meant.

“Yeah.”

He swallowed.

“No. I mean... did you actually see her?”

I didn't know what to say to that.

“I think so.”

He looked away.

For a few seconds, neither of us spoke.

Then he quietly said, “I don't think we're supposed to.”

I remember laughing.

Not because it was funny.

It was just the kind of nervous laugh you make when someone says something that sounds completely ridiculous, but you don't know how else to respond.

“What do you mean?”

He shook his head.

“Nothing.”

Then he walked off.

I stood there for a moment before carrying on with my shift.

I didn't bring it up again.

At least, not then.

A little while later, one of the older chefs shouted for me to bring something through from the storage room.

I went downstairs.

The storage room was underneath the kitchen, and there wasn't much down there besides boxes, cleaning supplies and old equipment we never seemed to throw away.

I found what I needed and started heading back upstairs.

Halfway up, I stopped.

I could smell it again.

That same sour, rotten smell.

Only this time, it was much stronger.

I stood on the stairs, listening.

Nothing.

No footsteps.

No voices.

Just the muffled noise of the restaurant above me.

Then I heard something from the kitchen.

A quiet sound.

Almost like someone humming.

I don't know why, but I didn't go up.

I just stood there listening.

The humming stopped.

A few seconds passed.

Then I heard a voice.

“Everything's finished.”

I froze.

It was the same voice from that morning.

Only this time, it sounded like it was coming from directly above me.

I don't remember deciding to move.

I just turned around and went back downstairs.

I stayed in the storage room for probably five minutes, pretending to look for something I'd already found.

Eventually, one of the chefs shouted down asking where I was.

I went back upstairs.

Nobody seemed to have noticed anything.

The kitchen was busy again.

The apprentice was working.

Jude was complaining about something.

And the woman was nowhere to be seen.

I started to wonder if I'd imagined the whole thing.

Then I noticed the apprentice looking at me from across the kitchen.

He'd heard it too.

I knew he had.

Neither of us said a word.

The rest of the shift passed without anything else happening.

When I finally clocked out that evening, I was exhausted.

Not physically.

I just wanted to get away from the place.

As I was leaving, I saw the apprentice standing outside by himself.

I nearly walked past him.

Then he spoke.

“You're going to see her again.”

I stopped.

“What?”

He looked at me.

For the first time that day, he didn't look frightened.

He just looked tired.

“You'll see her again.”

“How do you know?”

He stared at me for a few seconds.

Then he said something that I still think about.

“Because she knows you can see her.”

He walked away before I could respond.

I stood there outside the restaurant, watching him disappear down the street.

I remember thinking that I'd never go back.

I did, obviously.

I needed the money.

And that was probably the first mistake I made.

The bus on the way back should have been completely unassuming.

Usually, I'd just sit there, put my headphones in and stare out of the window until I got home.

That evening, though, I kept catching glimpses of her.

Not really her.

My imagination.

I'd glance out of the window and, for a split second, think I could see her standing somewhere along the pavement. Sometimes she'd be at a bus stop. Sometimes further back, almost hidden between people.

Every time, I'd look again and there would be nothing there.

Eventually, I stopped looking out altogether.

There was nothing outright frightening about her.

If anything, she looked weak. Frail, even. I could probably have knocked her over without much effort if I'd wanted to.

But that wasn't what bothered me.

There was something more to her.

She wasn't frightening in the way you'd expect someone frightening to be. There was no obvious threat. No aggression. Nothing that made me think she was going to attack me.

She was just unsettling.

The only way I can really describe it is like staring into a mirror for so long that your reflection starts to distort.

You know you're looking at yourself.

You know there's nothing wrong with the mirror.

But the longer you look, the less familiar the person staring back becomes.

That's how she felt.

Like there was something completely ordinary underneath all of it, but the longer I thought about her, the less I could convince myself that I'd actually seen a normal person.

I spent most of the bus journey trying not to think about her.

It didn't work.

By the time I got home, I'd convinced myself that I'd probably overreacted.

I'd had a weird morning.

I'd been tired.

The smell had probably gotten to me.

The apprentice had clearly been shaken too, and that probably made everything feel worse than it actually was.

I remember telling myself that I'd wake up the next morning and laugh about it.

I didn't.

Because when I got into bed that night, I could still smell her.

Not strongly.

Just faintly.

Like something sour lingering at the back of my throat.

I checked my clothes.

Nothing.

I showered.

Still there.

Eventually, I fell asleep.

The following morning, I was considering calling in sick, but I knew it was going to land me in even more trouble with my manager. At the same time, though, a part of me wanted to know more. I wanted to understand what the apprentice chef had been talking about and why he seemed so certain that I was going to see her again.

When I checked my rota for the day, I was actually relieved to see that Harvey was the supervisor working that morning. Harvey had always stuck up for me, whether it was in front of customers or in front of my manager. He was also just a sound guy. Very outgoing, not overly polite like some of the older staff, and he acted like he was around my age despite being about ten years older than me. Knowing he was going to be there brought some relief back into me.

The bus was thankfully just the usual. No smell, no strange sights, nothing that made me think of her. I got off and started walking towards the back entrance, since I was starting a little earlier and reception would still be closed.

When I turned the corner, though, all the staff were outside again.

The apprentice wasn't there.

I checked the rota and saw that he'd called in sick.

That immediately put a weight in my stomach. I don't know why, but seeing that he wasn't there felt much worse than it probably should have. It made everything he'd said the day before feel a little more real.

What gave me a momentary distraction from that was Harvey.

He was smiling, but not in his usual way. It was the kind of smile someone gives when they're trying to stop you from worrying. I knew straight away that something was wrong.

I walked over to him.

“Morning,” I said.

“Morning, mate.”

He was still smiling, but his eyes kept moving towards the kitchen.

I looked over as well.

“What happened?”

Harvey didn't answer immediately. He looked at me for a few seconds, then glanced back towards the kitchen.

“Have you smelled it yet?”

“Yeah, everyone has.” I knew that I hadn't answered his question properly. Everyone could smell it, but to me that wasn't the problem. Harvey's expression changed almost immediately. The smile he'd been forcing disappeared, and his gaze became noticeably more weary as he looked around at the other staff before taking me aside.

“Bro, you gotta tell me if you can see that woman like I do. I don't know if I'm going mad or...” He trailed off, looking at me as if he was waiting for me to confirm something.

“Yeah... I-I do.”

I didn't let him finish. His confession was all I needed to hear. Harvey sighed and rubbed the back of his neck, looking towards the kitchen again.

“I don't know, mate. She just seems... off. Ben said he's too scared to come in today. You know Ben, the apprentice? He didn't want to tell the manager, but apparently he was asking if he could take the day off. He said he didn't feel right coming back in.”

He paused for a moment before continuing.

“Apparently you had the same reaction as him?”

I nodded. I didn't know what else to add. My mind was already fogging up with all the information I was getting, and, more importantly, all the information I didn't want to get. It was strange hearing another person describe exactly what I'd experienced, especially someone like Harvey, who was normally one of the more confident people there.

For a moment, I genuinely wished I saw her like everyone else. I wished I could walk into that kitchen, notice nothing more than the smell, complain about it with everyone else and carry on with my shift. At least then I'd have something ordinary to blame it on.

Instead, I knew there was something wrong with her.

And now I knew I wasn't the only one who could see it.

Harvey looked like he didn't know what else to say. For the second time, it occurred to me that maybe we were the only people who could actually see her. I was about to ask Harvey more about what he'd seen when that familiar acidic tingle caught in my throat. I didn't see her, but I knew she was at the bottom of the stairs leading down to the kitchen.

“It's all finished. You can go relax. I'll start with the laundry.”

It was that same breathy, groaning voice. The hair on my arms prickled instantly, and I felt beads of sweat form on my forehead as my blood seemed to run cold. Harvey just shot me a look before following the others downstairs, which left me standing outside by myself.

I stayed there for a while, seriously considering leaving and running straight back home. Then I started thinking about what the apprentice had said the day before. What if she knew? What if she knew that I could see her? What if she knew me?

It felt stupid to even think about at the time. How could she possibly know anything about me? We'd spent hardly a minute together, and she'd barely said anything to me. There was no reason for her to know who I was.

Eventually, I realised I was just standing there avoiding going to work. So, somewhat sheepishly, I went downstairs to clock in.

Breakfast went smoothly. There were no issues, no complaints, but I couldn't shake the feeling that the room was too ordinary. Usually, the toaster would've been left on the wrong setting, the lights would still be dimmed from the previous night's dinner, or some other tiny thing would have been overlooked. Nothing major, just the sort of mistakes people make because we're all human.

This time, everything was in place.

It bothered me enough that I started deliberately looking for something wrong. I checked the tables for a slightly uneven setting, looked around for a patch of floor that hadn't been mopped properly, and even checked things I normally wouldn't have noticed. I couldn't find anything.

The stupidest part was that I knew how ridiculous I was being. There was no reason for me to be worried about the restaurant floor being tidy. Maybe she was just a good worker. Maybe she'd spent the morning making sure everything was ready before anyone else arrived.

Except I hadn't seen her do any of it.

I hadn't seen her cleaning, setting tables, checking the kitchen or doing anything else. As far as I knew, she'd barely been around at all.

And yet everything was perfect.

I could only act on my curiosity once breakfast had finished. I checked the rota for her name, hoping there'd be some sort of clue as to who she was, or even where she'd come from. I felt the colour drain from my face when I saw it.

There was no name.

It just said new employee.

She'd been there for three weeks by that point. We should've had her name. There should've been some record of who she was, even if it was only something basic in the staff system.

I went into the kitchen and asked Jude.

“Who? That hag? Haven't got a clue, mate. Why?”

Of course he didn't.

I found Harvey in his office, just staring into what looked like complete oblivion. I stood in the doorway for a moment before speaking.

“Harvey, do you know the name of the woman? The new one?”

I was careful not to say anything else. I didn't know why, but I suddenly had this horrible feeling that I shouldn't talk about her too openly. Not while she might be somewhere nearby.

Harvey looked at me, then quietly shut the door.

“I don't... and neither does Paul (my manager).”

He seemed strangely accepting of that fact, almost like he'd already spent some time trying to make sense of it himself. Then he looked at me.

“What the fuck is she, man?”

I couldn't give him an answer.

It was the same question I'd been asking myself since I'd first seen her.

Harvey started flicking through documents around the office, checking through folders and searching through things on the computer. I stood beside him, watching as he went through everything he could find.

There was nothing.

No application. No personal details. No employment record. Nothing that explained where she'd come from or who had hired her.

The only thing we could find was the same thing written on the rota.

New employee.

Harvey just stared at the rota. His eyes seemed almost plastered to the spreadsheet, like he was trying to find something that wasn't there.

“Harvey... you good?”

He didn't respond.

In a way, that answered my question well enough. He eventually put his head in his hands, looking completely bewildered, and I slowly backed away from him.

Lunch tables needed to be set up by twelve, and I'd done absolutely nothing so far. I left the office and headed back towards the restaurant floor, trying to put everything I'd just seen out of my mind. I turned the corner and was immediately hit with that same odour I'd been dreading.

I looked ahead and could feel my eyes watering almost instantly.

She was there.

Just standing there, at the end of the narrow hallway between the restaurant and the office. She wasn't doing anything. She was just staring directly at me.

My voice seemed to disappear. My mouth was so dry that I couldn't even swallow, and for a few seconds I couldn't bring myself to blink. I just stood there, looking at her while she stood completely still.

“Nearly finished. Go relax.”

Again, that same emotionless, hollow groan of a voice.

I waited.

She still didn't move.

My mind started flooding with questions. What on earth was she talking about? The tables looked exactly as they had twenty minutes earlier. If she'd been doing anything, I hadn't seen it.

She continued staring at me.

I could feel the hairs on my arms standing up.

“H-Harvey...”

That was all I could manage.

I found myself crying. The tears were rolling down my cheeks, but there was nothing to cry about. I was scared, yes, but it felt as if she was making me feel it. The fear didn't feel entirely like my own.

Harvey gently walked out of the office and looked into the hallway.

“WHAT THE FUCK?!”

He immediately started scuttling backwards, knocking hard against the wall behind him. His arms were flailing as he pointed wildly in her direction. This was nothing like Harvey. He kept his cool all the time, even with the most annoying customers. Harvey never, ever screamed at anyone.

Only then did I look back at her.

I swear on everything, I could feel my heartbeat in my throat. I tried to scream, but my voice died in my chest. My eyes felt dry and plastered open, and I couldn't seem to close them no matter how hard I tried.

The room was completely set up. Every table was perfectly arranged and nothing was missing. Everything looked exactly as it should have, and yet she still hadn't moved.

Then I noticed her jaw.

It was on the floor.

Not like her mouth was wide open. Her jaw had somehow fallen completely out from its place, laying separately beneath her face. There was no blood. The skin around her face had become dark and withered, and what little colour she'd had before was rapidly draining away, leaving her looking almost completely grey.

She still didn't move.

Then her fingernails began falling away one by one.

I watched them hit the floor.

Her teeth followed.

One after another, they simply came loose and dropped, yet she remained standing there as if absolutely nothing was happening.

I couldn't move.

At that point, I didn't even turn back to look at Harvey.

She muttered something.

I couldn't understand it. Whatever she was saying was distorted by her limp tongue, which seemed barely capable of forming the words. Then even that disappeared from her mouth and fell to the floor. I saw her eyes start to slowly move down, exposing the muscle and nerves holding them in place. It didn't look like flesh. It didn't look organic at all.

I was crying harder now. My eyes were stinging from being so dry despite the salty tears, and I could barely breathe.

Then I felt a strong arm pull me out of the trance.

Harvey had gotten over his own fear. He grabbed me and dragged me backwards into the office, slamming the door behind us.

My nose felt strange. My mouth was completely dry. My eyes felt glassed over, and everything around me seemed distant.

Before I knew it, I saw nothing but darkness.

Then a voice repeated itself inside my head.

“I know.”

I couldn't escape it.

“I know you.”

I felt my body convulse.

“I know you see.”

I tried to move, but I couldn't. Suddenly, I felt hands grabbing and restraining me from all directions.

When I woke up, I'd been admitted to the Accident and Emergency unit. Apparently, I'd suffered a severe panic attack and had stopped breathing properly. That's what Harvey told them had happened at least.

I was discharged after being told that I was lucky not to have developed exposure keratopathy in my eyes. They gave me some eye drops and told me to keep using them for a while.

A day later, I quit my job. I was never going back again.

Some time later, Harvey and I talked about what had happened over the phone. He told me that my mouth and eyes had been completely open, refusing to shut despite the noises I was making. I'd just been staring at the thing standing in front of me.

He said he saw me start moving towards her and immediately dragged me away.

I don't remember doing that. If I really did start walking towards her, I don't want to imagine what would've happened. I don't think I can imagine what would've happened.

Now, two years and many jobs later, I still find myself thinking about what happened back then. What it was. What it did. What happened to the apprentice. What happened to Harvey. Why was everything always clean. There are so many questions that I never got answers to.

My sense of smell has long since disappeared, and with it, most of my sense of taste. Other than that, everything else about my life is great. University is amazing, I'm on top of my studies unlike I was back then, and I got a new job a couple of months ago.

Things have been normal.

Until recently.

My job hired a new employee.

And lately, everyone at work has been saying the same thing.

That she stinks.
