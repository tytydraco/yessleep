Hey, so it’s been a while since the last update. I know you’re probably wondering what happened, and to be honest, so am I. It’s been kind of a whirlwind the last few weeks, and my memory of things is hazy and maybe a little bit out of order, but I’ll try to tell it to the best of my abilities.

So first of all, I’ll be honest, I didn’t do anything about my laptop being locked that night. I just finished my work quietly and then we got ready for bed and fell asleep together, and then the next morning I left his place and went to work like normal. But I couldn’t stop thinking about it. It was on my mind all day, and during my lunch break I finally texted Clark something like *Hey, do you have some time tonight? I want to talk about something that’s been weighing on me, even though I feel a little crazy for bringing it up*.

He read it, and I even thought I saw the typing dots come up, but he didn’t respond all afternoon. By the end of the day though, I was pretty frazzled, so although I was still wondering what was going on with him, I didn’t mind too much that I couldn’t have that hard talk immediately. Even aside from being left on read, it’d been a shitty day. I’d spent most of it getting chewed out by one of our clients, and then by my boss on behalf of the client, and so I was pretty frazzled by the time I finally got home to my own place. I flopped down on the couch with my phone already out and saw Clark had reposted something random on social media, which indicated he was at least alive and looking at his phone.

*Hey*, I texted him again. *Did you get my last text?*

Maybe it was all real, I thought, as I put my phone onto my stomach and lay back on the couch to stare at the ceiling. There was a plausible explanation for everything. Maybe his phone really was dropping texts sometimes. Maybe I did happen to bring some vague illness home from the bar and it hit him harder than it hit me, right on the day I changed my phone passcode, and I was being paranoid because I’d happened to overhear my friend trying to sound tough when she was drunk and hitting on a guy.

Maybe, maybe, maybe.

When you looked at the material facts of it, Clark had done nothing but treat me right so far. Discounting how surreal this whole past-life situation is, of course, because what mental framework does one have for that until it actually happens, and we’re forced to navigate it? But he’d acted as reasonably normal and human as I’d expected him to act.

My phone buzzed, startling me out of my philosophical digressions, and kept on buzzing. Clark was calling me.

“Hello?” I said, putting it to my ear as I sat up.

“Hey,” Clark said. His voice somehow managed to crack in that single syllable.

“Is everything okay?” I asked, sitting up further.

“Yeah,” came the crackly voice. “Well, no. Not really. Um.”

I waited.

“Tammy died today,” Clark said after what felt like forever. “I had to put her down.”

“What? Holy shit,” I said, grasping for words. “She-she was young, right? Middle aged? She seemed so healthy.”

“Yeah,” he mumbled, “yeah. It was like, sudden-onset liver failure or something. The vet wasn’t really sure what happened either. It just happened really fast.”

“Oh my god! Are you okay?”

“I’m not doing too well,” he said. “Maybe you could come over tonight?”

I scrunched up my face and sighed, silently and away from the phone, and immediately felt bad about it. His cat had just died and he’d been suffering and I was just thinking about the long day I’d had at work.

“Yeah, I’ll be there in a bit,” I said. “I’ll get some takeout for us.”

I fed my cat Mila her dinner and took a quick shower before getting back in my old car to drive across town to him. By the time I arrived with the takeout at his place, the sun had been down for a while and it was as if I’d been driving around past midnight. The day felt longer than it had any right to be.

I gave Clark a hug as soon as he opened the door. The bag of takeout dangling from my hand awkwardly thumped into his back. Maybe I don’t feel sorry enough, I thought, if I’m still self conscious about things like that. 

We sat at his kitchen table. The takeout bag was placed on the chessboard, which was still sitting untouched in the corner since I’d beaten him with that randomly chosen move. Neither of us looked at it. I took Clark’s hands in mine across the top of the table.

“How are you holding up?” I asked quietly, not wanting to disturb the newborn sad silence that permeated his apartment. “I’m so sorry.”

“She was a good cat,” Clark murmured, staring at the table.

“One of the best,” I added, unsure what else to say. It was so hard to tell how he was feeling sometimes.

Clark reached for the bag of takeout and started untying it.

“God, sorry,” he said. He opened his box and started eating. “Sorry. I just feel like I need to take my mind off things.”

“That’s okay,” I said, taking mine out of the bag. I barely had an appetite myself, after imagining how he must have felt during his cat’s last minutes, and how scared Tammy must have felt. Still, I hadn’t eaten dinner and it was late, and I halfheartedly poked at my food in a vain attempt to get something down.

“Maybe we could just watch a movie?” he asked, chewing. “Something simple.”

“Sure,” I said. My eye caught a jigsaw puzzle on his shelf. “Or maybe this?” I got up, an excuse to leave my food, and took a box off the shelf. On the cover, several hundred pieces came together to form an acrylic painting of a flower shop. “You never put this one together?”

“I think we did once,” Clark said. “In another lifetime.”

“Let’s do it in this one,” I said, feeling a pang of jealousy at the other me. Clark shrugged and pushed his empty takeout box away to make room for the puzzle. It felt surreal, just sitting there doing a puzzle after his cat had died, but what else were we supposed to do? At least it gave our hands something to keep busy with. It was mind-numbing, a little bit, but I was more okay with it because Clark just seemed so despondent. Maybe that wasn’t the right word. He just seemed kind of shocked into a state of zero emotional affect.

I remembered the thing I was going to ask him. We’d been at the puzzle for almost an hour at that point, and had filled in most of the edges. I had a piece in my hand and was turning it around trying to find a place for it, just as I was turning the question around in my head to find a good way to ask him.

“Hey,” I said as I slotted the piece into its correct place, “did you do anything with my laptop last night when I was over at your place?”

Clark’s hand, drifting over the sea of puzzle pieces, stopped.

“What do you mean?”

I knew I’d seen those words.

“When I got back from the bathroom and I opened it, I saw some text on the lock screen. But it disappeared after a second.”

“Text?” Clark said after a moment, his hand still hovering in the air.

I said nothing, trying to figure out where to take the conversation from here. Should I confront him about the laptop? Directly, or obliquely? Or just forget about it? I guess Clark had interpreted my silence as waiting for him to say more, as he did sometimes.

“Oh, yeah,” he said suddenly. “Yeah, Tammy was walking on the keyboard. I closed the lid so she wouldn’t lock you out of your laptop.”

I *knew* I’d closed my laptop when I had gotten up. Because I was paranoid. Or at least, I was pretty sure I had. But Clark seemed so casually, confidently certain that I hadn’t.

“Was she stepping on the enter key or-” I began, and then stopped once I noticed Clark was hunched over the table.

“Sorry,” he said. “It’s just a lot to think about her right now.” He sniffed, his head deep in his folded arms. “I don’t know if I can do this.”

“Okay,” I said, one hand gingerly on his forearm, like I was soothing something that was wounded and dangerous. “That’s okay.”

I sat there. So did he. Neither of us moved or spoke. The kitchen clock on the wall ticked quietly. I kept thinking they were the sound of small footsteps on the floor, that a cat’s tail would swish against my leg.

“Are you thinking about her too?” I asked.

Clark brought his face up from his arms and nodded, staring vacantly. His eyes were dry. “It’s always hard, losing her. Losing a pet, I mean.”

“Yeah.” Searching for words, I ended up thinking of my own family pets that I’d had to say goodbye to. Part of me, the childish part, wanted to stubbornly cling onto them as long as possible. But a more mature part knew I shouldn’t want to cling to them if they were suffering.

“Would you be okay with staying here tonight?” Clark asked, breaking the silence.

“Of course,” I said slowly, still thinking about everything that had happened tonight. There were too many moving pieces to arrange in my head, especially during a conversation like this.

“Sorry,” he added. “I think before all that, I interrupted you. You were going to say something, but I forgot what it was.”

“I forgot too,” I said, completely unsure how to bring up the laptop thing again.

I ended up staying the night with him. We just watched a movie (one of my favorites, as usual) and cuddled for a while. I felt too weird to have sex with him after that, so we just went to bed. I told myself, lying there in the dark, that I would find a way to bring it up tomorrow in a way that wouldn’t set him off.

But then when my alarm went off the next day, I realized it would take me longer to get to work from his place instead of mine, so I was in a hurry to get out the door. And then when I got back home to my own place after work, I was too tired, and I wasn’t at his place anyway, so I told myself I’d do it later. The urgency of it had already started to fade. What was one weird incident with a laptop, when he’d been so nice to me the entire rest of the time? He was still Clark. My boyfriend across timelines. He’s done so much for me.

We started spending more time together after that, either him coming to my place or, more often, me going over to his since his apartment was a little bigger. I brought more and more things over to his each time - some more clothes here, my favorite pillow there.



It felt natural. It felt like a while, and also no time at all, until I opened the cat carrier and Mila cautiously stepped out into Clark’s living room.



Sometimes, these days, I still think about that night with the laptop. Maybe I did just very stupidly forget to close my laptop when I got up to use the bathroom. Maybe Tammy, rest her soul, had her paw on the enter key and another key at the same time.

Maybe, maybe, maybe.

A cloud of uncertainty seems to follow me around these days. Clark is so certain about everything. I guess that’s one advantage of having done this relationship once before.
