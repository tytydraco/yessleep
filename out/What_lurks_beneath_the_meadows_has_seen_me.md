I'll try to be brief, because brief are my thoughts. The blank page stares back at me and I don't know what to do. Everything I've wanted to explain, to write, scratches the depths of my mind with the furor of a foreboding. I can think of the images, the smells, the sensations, but the moment I try to make them tangible, the idea hangs on the tip of my tongue —of my fingers— as if it doesn't belong there, but in the most horrid lack of purpose I've felt in my life.

It was just some dead sheep at the start of the week: miscarried lambs and deformed fetuses that surrendered to the night's serenity and the painful turmoil of the first-time mothers. The few I could see were torn to pieces. Most of them, stomped on by the adults of the flock. Others were dragged along the placenta through the barbed wires that circled the shed.

One morning, Old Irene made me leave my ranch in the hills to examine the disfigured remains of an animal. Under the candlelights, I could see it was another lamb. Its legs were twisted and its neck broken. I could tell it never got to walk due to its flaccid hooves. The mother was nowhere to be seen.

“She ditched it and fled,” I explained on the phone. My boss sighed. “They went up there. It was screaming. They don't scream at this hour.”

“Could it be that someone is giving them something?”

“So they dump them? Why?”

“Cause people are evil,” she said with certain apprehension. I kicked the lamb with the edge of my sandal. “And evil people are hungry.”

On Sunday night, eleven thirty or so, the dogs were added to the equation. At first they barked at the dark; raised hackles, upright ears and bare fangs. The few that had owners were forcefully retired with collars and sticks. The ones that didn't were found by sunrise, stabbed in their thighs and ribs. One of them ended up with a disfigured snout, and judging by what we saw in the riverbed, it was the work of a stone straight to its jaws.

“There aren't any stolen animals” I commented to one of my fellows the next morning. The sheep had come down to drink; they ran along the lagoon's reeds without a care in the world. “Do you think it's a boy? The one who's screwing with us?”

Fermín wiped the sweat from his forehead without taking off his hat.

“Do you think it's a kid?” I repeated.

The man shook his head. “He'd first need the balls to come here.”

I was getting ready for sleep when the screaming started at the foot of the hills. They carried on for two mornings, rested for one and returned for two all over again. Horrible screeches, witchlike. Since I kept a gun, I fired at the sky at least four times as a warning. More than once did I receive a call asking what was up. I explained and no one believed me. Could it be close to my farm? I asked myself with each negative reaction. The cries didn't stop and the sheep kept miscarrying. One lamb at a time, despite the numbers not matching the amount of females we had. Neither the females nor the people who saw what was happening. Because by the next week, I was the only one who noticed the smell. Sometimes sweet, yet burnt. Other times, it was strong; candles from saints which burned beyond their wax. Carbonized matches that corroded the bottoms of plastic bags. A sulphuric miasma had slipped by my doors, my walls. Putrid spectres hung from the ceiling, always heeding my every move.

Until Tuesday night.

Mondays were for sowing, the elders used to say. On Tuesdays, we foraged.

And just like a damned hound, parting through the night to ambush its prey, I found it there, down with its four legs by the dry trunk of a guayacán.

As if it was immersed in some sort of lagoon, the knots on its fur rose and fell in a hypnotic dance which defied all laws of physics. A spectacle of shadows and figures which turned the star's twinkles into constellations in its oily, matted coat.

Crack. *CRACK.*

The animal shook its head so suddenly, for a minute I thought it would dislocate.

“Er diablo!” I gasped, at the verge of tripping. The creature didn't notice me; it was too busy swinging around a piece of leather to do so. “W-What is…?”

A foamy whiff drippled off its mouth. Neither solid nor liquid. Ice tendrils that swirled around whatever was being dismembered. I stepped back once, then twice, before raising my gun and praying for the best. Risk lost meaning. Whether I died or lived, it didn't matter. It had the same value to me because I knew, despite not being able to prove it yet, that it was him. I knew that was the thing tormenting the sheep. I knew that every night that cursed animal sneaked through the hills to my ranch to scream its prayers and scratch the walls with its long, brawny steel claws. I knew it, and because I knew it too well, I dared to shoot it one, two, three times straight to its head. A clean, immediate way of finishing the job…

If only a single bullet had brushed it.

“W-What…?”

My eyes opened so fast and so wide, for a second I wasn't sure I actually did it.

The creature, despite not turning around immediately, locked its rusty glimpse into me. Red, like the blood hanging from its jaws. Maroon.

Thump.

Laughter erupted from the sky. It came from everywhere and nowhere.

The creature stood up in two long, shaky legs brimming with tar.

Thump, thump.

There you are, it growled under my skin. My bones buzzed and my head spun in a tornado of impossible images filled with colors, smells and sensations that made my body tremble with what I imagined was the might of a thousand lightning.

“Jesus…” I babbled, about to lose balance. The creature pulled me forwards, along its static energy. Every muscle in my body gave in; my fingers let go of the gun and my knees nailed me to the ground like goddamn stakes. Another bloody lump that would feed the hills. “My God, my…”

Wind howled over my head with the same treacherous grief that haunted so many of my dreams.

There, there…

The creature became one with the meadows, tangling up its porcelain skin with the visceral body it stole from the night. 

Forgive me, forgive me, was the only thing I could think as I watched it kneel in front of me.

It's inside of you, it whispered with my own vocal chords. I wanted to protest. To clench my jaws and rip its eyes out with my bare hands. However, its rusty beak pierced my palate, forcing me to skip one, two, thirteen heartbeats which my heart fought to drag out as much as humanly possible.

Let me go, I wanted to scream, but he had ripped my tongue out. Its claws stretched out my mouth until the cartilage splitted from the skin with a tsk from the blood and saliva. Leave me alone, gurgled from my trachea, warmly reverbing against its earthy nails. A sob; a beg; a curse. The back of my cranium hit my left shoulder blade with a thump, thump that suffocated the rest of the sounds.

I'd like to say that I spent the following hours fading in and out of consciousness. Unfortunately, I know that´s not how it went. I know everything I experienced, from the first pinch to the rise of dawn, had been intact in my memories for some time. The sensation of nebulous claws stretching my lungs, breaking my bones, ripping apart my veins and drowning my senses with its treacherous sulfuric whiff… All products of the same monster. Of a single nightmare that extended beyond necessary and rediscovered with my painful screams the principles that defined fear.

Now, one week after the encounter, my mind is insisting on playing games with me. It wants to make me forget that I lived despite the ghosts of its fangs still hanging in my throat. I can't shake off the feeling that I'm missing something. An emptiness that mocks my heartbeats with the sharp tik, tak of its feral pulse.

The people around me insist that everything was the result of a bad trip or a sudden drop in blood pressure; a play of lights and substances that left me sprawled in the meadows with my body intact and unexplainable symptoms of hypothermia. However, I know what I saw. I know what I felt, no matter how many times my body expels the idea.

With its steel beak it shattered unshaped craniums. With its claws, stripped away the pleads of something that once pretended to be alive. Pretended to be human. Furious moans, filled with loss and impotence which modelled its terror until it infected the weakest minds with its impossible abhorrences.

Had I been one of them?
