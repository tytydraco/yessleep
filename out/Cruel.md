And the answer came as easily and bitterly as anything.

“Because we’re friends.” There was no forgiveness in my tone, and there was no mocking triumph in his face. He just looked dumbstruck. With that, I pulled out my phone and called 911, continuing to pin him.

A lot of things happened after that. To sum it up, there was an extensive investigation. The Walkman helped prove that I acted in self-defense, at the very least. This was backed up by numerous interviews given to me, my classmates, and faculty including the counselor. For some reason, Tyler did not attempt to defend himself, going into detail about the sort of guy he had always been: a bona fide psychopath. It was incredible, seeing someone dismantle their own image as a model student in the way he did. I guess he figured the game was up. The coup de grâce was Tyler’s sick trophy—the broken bottle covered in blood and covered in Tyler’s fingerprints and matched the shards found at the scene of Bill’s murder. At the end of it, due to the seriousness of his crimes and using the evidence and testimony provided, the prosecution pursued the strongest possible sentence they could despite him being a teenager.

It wasn’t a triumph for me. A psychopath was now behind bars, but I had allowed him to get away with so much of his behavior. People like my parents, the counselor, and other classmates always said it wasn’t my fault, that Tyler did all of those horrible things. And maybe I wasn’t directly at fault. But it wouldn’t bring back Jack and Bill, erase Ben’s actions, or change the fact that I was friends with a monster, and I pretended he wasn't one until he decided to “try” murder.

I saw Tyler only once during his incarceration. We didn’t talk. I could barely look at him. At last, he cleared his throat and asked, “Hey, uh, Matt?”

“Yeah?” I muttered.

“Are…” he asked, sounding sheepish on the other side of the Plexiglass, “…are we still friends?”

I couldn’t answer. I just looked at him, dumbfounded.

“I-I know it’s different, and the whole fight thing made it…weird. But…well, if I get out, will we be friends?”

“…I don’t know,” I murmured, because I really had no idea what else to say.

“I’ll let you think about it. God knows I’ve got time.”

So casual and sincere, even after everything.

I didn't see him again, and I never found out if he was released or not.

The events of my junior year were a source of trauma for years, but trauma doesn’t stop time. And after the debacle involving Tyler, I moved on as best as I could with counseling and making better, healthier friendships. I graduated high school, went to college, got a job, got married, and I built a better life. My husband, Travis, knew about Tyler ever since we began dating.

Recently, he and I were just sitting on the sofa. He was talking about our plans for his mother’s birthday.

“So I’m thinking we could rent a boat, ride out on the lake,” he suggested. “How’s that? Do your mom and dad like boat rides?

I nodded. “Yeah, we always used to take rides out to watch 4th of July fireworks. I—”

I then trailed off as I noticed the date, and my face dropped.

His face, usually so chipper, turned somber.

“Right. It’s today, isn’t it?”

“18 years, Travis,” I murmured. “18 years and I still remember when I first met him.”

He nodded. “Come here, Matt,” he said softly, holding me for a while.

I didn't cry. I barely cried about Tyler. But by that point, Travis knew me well enough to know when Tyler was on my mind. He never told me to just not think about him, or to think positive; he usually didn't say anything. He just held me, just to let me know that he was there to listen, but that I never needed to explain myself to him.

Overall, life has been better since 2009.

But now and then, I look at the old K-Bar I’ve kept after all these years. I never touch it, placing it at the top of my closet. My nostrils fill with cigarette smoke. My ears fill with the drawl of Tyler’s casual cruelty. I see an empty baseball field and a bloody bottle. I think about why I have to live with this, knowing what I do about Tyler, and wondering if there was still more that I didn't know. *Why, Tyler?* I ask in my mind. *Why did you leave me with all of this?* But I know the answer. Kids are cruel, and teens are monsters, but Tyler was always special. He was one of the worst. And yet he left me with terrible memories and happy ones alike, as well as many unanswered questions because of the same reason he never tried to hurt me before that night.

*Because we’re friends.*
