It started out as a joke.

We went to our first open house, and when we got back, the light in the kitchen wouldn’t turn on. It turned out to be a blown fuse, but my husband started joking—“Maybe the house is jealous.”

I rolled my eyes. He looked up at the ceiling. “Don’t worry. She had nothing on you, babe. No rain shower, no granite countertops…”

I chuckled under my breath.

It was less funny when we got home from touring a new construction townhome to find the bathroom flooded. Water overflowing the bathtub, skating along the tile floor, dripping down the stairs. Thankfully the contractor said it hadn’t been overflowing long, and he thought there hadn’t been any damage to the subfloor. But it was still hours of cleanup, and that night we had an argument—

“You must’ve left the faucet on.”

“You were the last one in the bathroom!”

“I don’t even take baths, Carlie!”

We found a sweet little colonial on the edge of town with a lot of property. Perfect for starting a family. The night we sent in an offer, we opened champagne and decided to light a fire in the fireplace. Something we hadn’t done in ages.

Evan checked the flue.

I know he did. I saw him do it. I *saw* him crouch on his hands and knees and peer up the chimney to be extra sure.

But as the fire grew, thick black smoke wafted into the room. The flames curled outwards. In seconds the room was pooled with smoke and the flames were shooting out. I screamed and Evan ran to get the fire extinguisher.

When the firefighters came, they said the flue had been closed. And that we should spend the night elsewhere because of the possible carbon monoxide and other toxic fumes.

We came back yesterday.

We came back because of course I assumed they were all coincidences. Not that the house was plotting against us. Mad at us for moving out. That would be crazy.

Today I got the call from the realtor that our offer had been accepted. We were going to meet her at 2 o’clock and sign the papers. Evan and I went into the garage, into our little Civic, and started the car.

A metallic *creak* sounded behind us.

I turned around—to see the garage door rolling down.

I reached up and pressed the open button. It wouldn’t budge. “Stop the car!” The garage darkened as the door blocked the sunlight. “Evan!”

He finally cut the engine. “That’s weird,” he said, mashing the button. “Why won’t it…?”

I got out and walked over to the button on the wall. Pressed it. A blinding strip of sunlight appeared, the metal squeaked as the door began rolling open. “Must be something wrong with the receiver or whatever,” I said as I got back in the car.

He clicked into reverse and we began backing up, again. I leaned forward, pulling down the mirror to put on some makeup.

*Snap.*

*What the hell was—*

I screamed as the car shook. I lurched forward, my head slamming the dashboard. *Oh no, what the hell, what—*

*Evan.*

He was still sitting in the driver’s seat.

Blood everywhere.

The metal garage door between the two halves of his body.

*Oh God, no, no—*

I opened the door and stumbled into the garage. A slice of sunlight poured in underneath the car. Blood dripped into it.

*No, no, no.*

*“Help!”* I screamed. *“Someone! HELP!”*

I screamed it even though I knew there was no way Evan was alive.

I ran to the door. Smashed the garage door button. It wouldn’t open. One of the springs had snapped, still wobbling near the ceiling. I ran into the house, stumbling over my own feet, screaming.

I ran to the front door.

It was locked.

I turned the deadbolt. Tried to. It was stuck fast.

Same with the sliding glass door.

*How?!*

I finally remembered my phone in my pocket. I dialed 911. I gave them my address. “Please, please come soon,” I sobbed, gripping the counter.

There was a pause.

“You said 214?” the woman on the other end asked.

“Yeah.”

“That address isn’t coming up, ma’am,” she said. “Are you sure—”

“I’m sure!”

“… Ma’am, there’s no 214 on the map.”

“Yes there is! I’ve lived here for eight years!” I screamed. *You know an ambulance wouldn’t help anyway.*

“Ma’am?” the tinny voice from the speaker asked.

“Stop!” I screamed. *“Stop!”*

A snapping sound cracked through the air. I looked up. There was a line on the kitchen wall, blurry through my tears. A seam, a crack, buckling open.

I stumbled up and backed away.

The crack widened. No beams or insulation—just darkness. Pitch black like the forest on a moonless night.

*What the…*

Hissing whispers filled the air. My body lurched forward, and then I was sliding towards the void, as if some magnetic force were pulling me. I screamed, but even my own voice seemed muted, muffled compared to the growing silence of the void.

My foot slipped into the gap. I pivoted and grabbed the side of the wall, holding on for dear life. *“Stop!”* I screamed. *“Stop!”*

With a sudden *pop* of air pressure, the force stopped. A beat of silence. And then—sound. Clinking of pots and pans. Whispers. The faucet running. And then, drifting out of the wall, my own voice.

*“I remember that! That was so crazy.”*

Evan’s voice.

*“I know. And then that guy stopped in front of us…”*

My heart twisted as I heard our laughter coming from the void behind me. It was replaying a conversation we’d had a few nights ago. Right here in the kitchen. But the sounds were out of order, as I heard myself complaining about the flood, the water running down the stairs. Clinking of dishes and thumps of shoes on tile.

And then—

A voice I didn’t recognize.

A woman’s voice.

Garbled through the staticky void. The click of high heels on tile. A murmur. *Oh, no, no.*

I yanked my foot out of the wall and walked to the table, shaking. I didn’t even notice the blood until I sat down. There was no longer a foot, really. Only blood and mangled flesh.

I turned around to see a hand reaching out of the slit in the wall. A hand that looked like my own, down to the green fingernail polish. It grabbed the corner of the wallpaper and pulled it over the crack, as the void resealed itself.

The tile floor felt warm under my feet.

*Why is it warm?*

The walls seemed to pulse. Maybe they always had. Maybe I’d never noticed. Just as I stared at the cabinets now, and noticed the wood-grain pattern repeated itself, looking more like eyes than anything else. Like the fake eyes on a moth’s wing.

*Mimicry.*

My heart dropped as I stared at the house, as what was left of my foot throbbed in pain. Then I hobbled up and stumbled towards the door. It unlocked for me this time, letting in the sunlight, pooling around my legs, feeling as warm as the house itself.
