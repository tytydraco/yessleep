I sat angrily at the little plastic patio table with its red tablecloth, munching on some kind of chocolate cookie and drinking fucking orange juice. I really wasn't in the mood for fruit juice, especially given the circumstances, but at the end of the day, this was still supposed to be a kid's birthday party... would've been, anyway, if there were any kids.

My son, Liam, had just turned nine, and not a single kid we'd invited from his class showed up. I thought we were past this kind of shit these days, kids singling someone out and bullying them like that.

Ann and I had gotten into it, too. I lost my shit when nobody showed up for the party, and I was planning to march into the school on Monday and demand some answers. But Ann said that would only add fuel to the fire. The other kids would just shut Liam out even more.

So I sat there, quietly stewing, watching Liam on the backyard swing with his head hanging low, crying to himself while Ann sat beside him trying to comfort him.

I never thought that when we brought Liam home, we'd ever have to deal with something like this. Yeah, Liam isn't my biological son. Ann and I adopted him when he was only a year and a half old... Why we chose to go that route doesn't matter. Not many people even know we're not his biological parents. We'll tell him when he's old enough. But right then... right then, I had no idea what I was supposed to say to him.

  


"How is he?" I asked Ann as she walked toward me from the far end of the yard.

  


"How do you think?" Ann said sadly.

  


"Fuck..." I muttered. "Little assholes..."

  


"Enough... Julian, please." Ann closed her eyes wearily. "We should've known this might happen. Liam keeps to himself. You know that. Maybe we shouldn't have pushed this whole thing in the first place. I'm a terrible mother, Julian..."

  


Ann couldn't finish. She broke down quietly, covering her face with her hands.

  


"Hey. Hey, Ann, don't do that." I immediately jumped up from the table. "Shh. You're not a bad mother. Don't talk like that."

  


"But..." Ann sniffled.

  


"No buts," I said firmly, pulling her into a hug. "You know what... screw all this shit. I'll order pizza. Everybody can get whatever they want, and the three of us can just..."

  


Ding-dong.



The loud doorbell cut me off. I was so caught off guard that I completely forgot what I'd been planning to do with the pizza and the rest of the afternoon.

Ann looked at me with tear-filled eyes, confused. I just shrugged.

The doorbell rang again, buzzing loudly.

  


"I'm coming!" I shouted from the backyard.

  


I looked at Ann for a moment and wiped the tears from beneath her brown, reddened eyes.

  


"If it's a guest, don't let them see you've been crying," I said jokingly. "They'll think the clown's that bad."

  


"What clown?" Ann stared at me, confused.

  


"The one I hired for the party," I said, dead serious.

  


"You hired a clown for the party?" Ann stared at me in disbelief.

  


"Of course not." I smiled. "I fucking hate clowns.”

  


I didn't even bother answering the third, even longer ring. I just stomped toward the front door, already pissed off. Whoever it was, they were seriously impatient, and if they turned out to be an asshole too, I was probably going to punch them in the nose.

I walked up to the door and yanked it open.

I was expecting just about anything: the mailman, some deranged parent who had actually decided to show up for the party, maybe one of the neighbors. Hell, even the clown crossed my mind.

But there was just one little blond boy standing alone on my doorstep.

The second I saw his nervous little face, I calmed down. If anything, I actually started to feel hopeful.

  


"Hey there, little man," I said. "Who are you?"

  


The boy didn't answer. He just stared quietly at me with his big, dark eyes. Then he raised one hand and pointed at the open front door. More specifically, at the little party invitation sign Ann and Liam had made together.

  


"You here for the party?" I asked cheerfully.

  


The blond kid gave me a slow, cautious nod at first, then followed it with a much more definite one.

  


"Well, come on in!" I said, gesturing for him to enter.

  


The kid broke into a happy smile, like that was what he'd been waiting to hear all day.

I led him through the entryway and into the kitchen.

  


"You in Liam's class?" I asked him.

  


He shook his head. I stopped for a second and turned toward him.

  


"Then how do you know him?" I asked, frowning.

  


The boy seemed to be searching for the right words. He was probably nervous, scared shitless even. But who wouldn't be at nine years old, standing in a stranger's house surrounded by people you didn't know? His parents were pretty damn irresponsible too, if they'd really just...

  


The boy just pointed to the schoolbag lying in the hallway.

  


"From school?"  I said with a smile.

  


The little guy gave an uncertain nod.

  


"Who was it, Julian?" Ann asked as she came in from the backyard. "Oh..." She stopped in surprise. "And who are you?"

  


"One of Liam's schoolmates," I answered for the boy, who was obviously pretty nervous.

  


"Um..." Ann pursed her lips. "Where are your parents?"

  


"Oh, come on, Ann," I said dismissively. "Give the kid a break. Let him go play."

  


I gently pressed a hand against the boy's back to get him moving toward the backyard.

But he didn't budge.

It was like his feet were rooted to the floor.

  


"Go ahead. You can go play," I said, gently nudging him again.

  


Without saying a word, he suddenly took off and ran into the backyard.

  


"Julian?!" Ann snapped at me as soon as the boy was outside. "Who is this kid?"

  


"I told you," I whispered. "He goes to Liam's school. His parents dropped him off, and he came for the party. That's it."

  


"But where are his parents?" Ann pressed.

  


"I don't know. They were probably in a hurry and just dropped him off."

  


"Then how do you know he really goes to school with Liam?" Ann continued.

  


"I don't know!" I raised my voice. "He told me, okay? Is that better? Ann, come on, for fuck's sake... I let a sixty-pound kid into the house, not some axe murderer. Worst-case scenario, he spends the night."



Ann didn't say anything, but I could tell from her face that she was pissed at me for snapping at her.

  


"Sorry," I said once I'd calmed down. "But look at them. They're sitting out there talking."

  


Ann slowly turned around and looked at the boys.

Liam and the strange little boy were sitting together on the swings, talking.

  


Ann and I just stood there quietly, watching the kids play outside. Liam was showing the new kid his remote-control car, steering the little thing around like a real race car driver. Liam spent the whole time lying on his stomach on the patio, working the controls, while the other boy sat cross-legged beside him and watched. He paid such close attention to everything Liam was doing, you'd think he was watching some kind of miracle.



Liam only played with it for a few minutes. Meanwhile, Ann started getting some plates ready, and I went to grab the pizzas we'd ordered. By the time I got back to the kitchen, the boys had switched places. The strange little boy was sitting there with the remote in his hands, just waiting. He stared down at the controller he was supposed to use to drive the car while Liam explained something to him, pointing at the different controls. It looked like Liam was showing him what each button did. Kind of weird for a nine-year-old these days not to know how to use something like that.

Eventually, the kid awkwardly started driving the little off-road truck. I was still watching them from the kitchen. The truck hit a little bump, caught some air, and flipped over.



Then, to my surprise, Liam burst out laughing.



I watched happily as my son, who'd been sitting alone crying less than half an hour ago, was now laughing his ass off. The strange little boy just stared seriously at Liam.



Then, after a moment, he started laughing happily too.

  


"Ann," I called to my wife. "Come look at this."

  


"Yeah, I see it," Ann answered from behind me. "He's finally having a good time."

  


"Told you everything would be okay," I said, rubbing Ann's back.

  


"I know..." Ann trailed off. "But if his parents come to pick him up, I'd like to talk to them."

  


"Whatever you think is best." I shrugged. "I'm gonna go see if the boys want anything."

  


Ann gave me a sad little smile. I pursed my lips and headed out into the backyard. Ann always got a little more withdrawn, tense, and sensitive around this time of year. Every year, as Liam's birthday got closer, it became a lot harder for me to reach her.

  


"All right, guys, you want something to drink? Something to eat?" I asked as I walked over to them.

  


"Uhhh..." Liam thought about it. "What do we have?"

  


"What don't we have?" I joked. "Beer, wine, vodka, whiskey. What's it gonna be?"

  


"Dad!" Liam said with a grin.

  


"All right, all right." I waved him off. "We've got soda, juice. What do you want?"

  


"Well..." Liam thought for a second. "Can I have some Fanta?"

  


"Hmm." I put my hands on my hips. "For now, yeah. What about you, buddy?"

  


I turned toward the strange little boy.

He just stared blankly at me, like he had no idea what I was talking about. He kept looking at me with those big, thoughtful eyes.

  


"So what do you want?" I asked him.

  


The kid didn't say anything. He just pointed at Liam. It was like I was trying to have a conversation with someone who couldn't speak.

  


"You want the same thing?" I raised an eyebrow at him. "You can say it, you know. I'm not gonna eat you."

  


The boy awkwardly lowered his eyes, like I'd embarrassed him.

  


"Actually, buddy," I said, suddenly realizing something. "I don't even know your name."

  


The kid didn't move. He just stared at the ground and at his feet. Not a sound came out of him, even though I'd seen him laughing and talking with my son.

  


"Dad..." Liam finally spoke up. "He said his name's a secret. He only tells people who deserve to know."

  


"And you deserve to know?" I looked at my son questioningly. "You know his name?"

  


"Yeah," Liam said proudly. "But I'm not telling you! Then I wouldn't be a good friend."

  


I smiled warmly.

My son might finally have a real friend, even if the kid was a little weird. At least Liam wasn't sitting alone in the backyard feeling miserable anymore.

So, accepting their little kid rules for what they were, I headed back inside to Ann.



Ann was watching the two boys in the backyard with a gloomy look on her face when I came back into the kitchen.

  


"Did you talk to them?" Ann asked.

  


"Yep," I said cheerfully. "And that's two Fantas coming right up!"

  


"They both asked for Fanta?" Ann turned toward me.

  


"Yeah." I shrugged.

  


"Uhhh..." Ann thought for a second. "What's his name again? The other little boy asked for Fanta too?"

  


"Yeah..." I scratched my head. "Yeeep. He did."

  


"What's his name, Julian?" Ann asked, frowning at me.

  


"I don't know." I rolled my eyes. "He wouldn't tell me."

  


"What do you mean, he wouldn't tell you?" Ann pressed. "You don't even know his name?"

  


"No, believe it or not, I didn't interrogate the kid," I said, getting annoyed. "He's a little weird. Liam says his name's a secret. But why the hell should I care as long as my son's having a good time with him?"

  


"Julian!" Ann snapped. "We don't even know who he is. We haven't even seen his parents. He could be some kid who's in trouble, or God knows who he is. Doesn't any of this seem suspicious to you?"

  


"Please, Ann, give it a rest." I rubbed my forehead. "You're doing the same thing you did with that cat last year..."

  


"Oh, no!" Ann pointed at me irritably. "That was completely different. That was dangerous..."

  


"Mom? Dad?" Liam's quiet voice came from behind us.

  


Ann and I both spun around at the same time to see what Liam wanted.

Our son was standing alone in the kitchen doorway. I could tell from his little face that he'd heard us arguing, but he didn't want to say anything about it.

  


"What's up, little man? What do you need?" I asked after a few quiet seconds.

  


"When are we gonna eat?" Liam asked. "We're really hungry."

  


"Oh," Ann said kindly. "I'll bring the pizza out for you and your little friend right now. Did he say how many slices he wants?"

  


Liam just shook his head.

  


"Does he even know what we're having?" Ann continued.

  


Liam shook his head again.

Then he glanced at me like he was waiting for me to rescue him from all the questions.

  


"Go on back outside and play, Liam." I nodded toward the backyard. "I'll take care of the food for you guys."

  


Without another word, Liam ran back outside to the other boy, though I suddenly couldn't see where the kid had gone.

  


"Why did you send him away?" Ann asked, glaring at me.

  


"Because you were about to interrogate him," I said simply. "Give him a break for a while. It's his birthday... And the other kid's just weird. Ann, I used to collect boogers in a box when I was ten... Him not talking isn't that big of a deal."

  


Ann gave me a reluctant little smile.

  


"Now come on, give me the drinks and the pizzas," I said playfully. "We've got two hungry guests waiting."

  


Ann had just set out the plates when her phone started ringing on the counter.

  


"Oh, shit." Ann looked at the screen. "It's Mom. I'm gonna talk to her for a bit, okay?"

  


"Go ahead." I nodded. "I'll take care of things in the meantime.”

  


By the time I carried everything out to the little patio table, the two boys were already sitting there, waiting for the hot, steaming pizza. Liam was eating his usual cheese pizza. It was his favorite. The strange little boy just sat there watching Liam stuff slice after slice into his mouth. He stared at my son like he was some kind of museum exhibit.

  


"Go ahead, you can eat too," I joked with the boy. "It's not poisoned."

  


The kid just looked me up and down like he didn't even understand that I was joking. Then he looked back at Liam, and eventually, he slowly took a slice of cheese pizza too.

Kids. They're all the same, I immediately thought. We ate our pizza quietly in the warm summer air. The two boys nibbled away like a couple of little mice without saying a word, while I stared absentmindedly toward the kitchen, thinking about Ann.

  


"Good, guys?" I asked, snapping back to reality.

  


"Uh-huh," Liam answered immediately.

  


"Uh-huh," the little boy said almost right after him.

  


For a second, I was so surprised I didn't even know what to say.

  


"Well, look at that. You do have a voice," I finally said with a smile. "You gonna talk to me now too?"

  


The boy didn't answer right away. He just held his pizza gently and stared at me. Then he glanced at Liam, like he was waiting for something from my son.

  


"No," the strange little boy said. "Just now."

  


"Well, that's nice." I gave him a friendly grin. "How about you at least tell me your name, then? I'd kinda like to know who you are."

  


The boy went quiet again and just watched me. It was like he had to carefully think through every single answer as if he were taking some kind of test instead of just saying yes or no.

  


"No," the boy finally answered.

  


"Why not?" I asked with an awkward smile.

  


"Because we're not friends," the boy said simply.

  


"Oh." I pretended to be surprised. "And here I thought we were getting along, buddy. So how do I become your friend?"

  


The boy's expression changed.

His face turned completely serious, like I'd just said something horrible to him. He stared at me with those big eyes, like he was searching for something on my face. Like when somebody has something smeared on their cheek and you can't stop staring at it.

Feeling uncomfortable, I kept up the awkward smile. But eventually, I couldn't take the kid's piercing stare anymore.

  


"You done with the pizza?" I blurted out, just to break the tension.

  


"Uhhh..." Liam mumbled. "One more."

  


Liam quickly pulled another small slice onto his plate.

The strange boy just kept staring at me.

  


"You okay, buddy? Did I say something wrong?" I asked after a few seconds.

  


"No," the boy answered simply. "I was just looking at you."

  


"Uh-huh..." I pursed my lips. "Why?"

  


"JULIAN!" Ann shouted from inside the house. "COME HERE, PLEASE!"

  


I didn't say anything else to the boys. I just affectionately ruffled Liam's hair, glancing every now and then at the strange little kid, who was still watching me.

I shook my head in confusion, then headed back into the kitchen.

  


"Ann?" I asked quietly, leaning against the kitchen wall.

  


Ann just held up her index finger, telling me to wait. She was still on the phone. Ann was close with her parents, and she and her mom could spend hours talking about absolutely nothing. The two of them could also be unbelievably paranoid. Oh, don't let Liam go by himself. Oh, that shelf is going to fall down. Oh, have you checked the tires on the car lately...

  


"The cake." Ann pointed toward the fridge. "Take it out, please, and put the plates out too."

  


"That's it?" I asked, squinting at her.

  


"For now." Ann shrugged. "I'll be out there in a minute."

  


I rubbed my temples and got to work on the tasks I'd been assigned. Ann had specially ordered some kind of healthy fruit cake or whatever. Liam liked fruit, so at least we'd gotten lucky there, but did he really have to eat healthy even on his birthday...

As I reached the kitchen counter with the cake, I noticed that the two boys weren't sitting at the table where I'd left them. I looked around the backyard suspiciously, trying to figure out where they'd gone. But neither Liam nor the strange little boy was anywhere in sight.

I glanced back at Ann, who was still on the phone.

And I knew damn well that if something happened now, there'd be hell to pay. Better for me to go check on the boys myself.

I only made it as far as the kitchen door before I spotted the two little guys crouching beside a nearby flower bed. Liam was poking at something with the kind of fascination you'd expect if he'd just made some groundbreaking discovery.

I was about to call out to them, but I stopped myself. The two boys were talking quietly.

  


"Why are we the only two kids at the party?" the strange little boy asked.

  


Liam didn't answer.

I couldn't see his face, but I knew he was squinting. He always did that when he was scared, uncomfortable, or just didn't want to answer an awkward question.

  


"I don't really have many friends..." Liam finally said, to my surprise.

  


"I see," the boy said. "Did you have friends before?"

  


Liam just shook his head.

  


"Your mom. And your dad," the boy continued. "Are they your friends?"

  


"I think so," Liam answered uncertainly. "I love them, and they're my parents. I think they're my friends too."

  


"Do they love you too?" the boy pressed.

  


Liam didn't answer right away, but I wouldn't have known how to answer a question like that either. Not because I wasn't sure. Liam was my son. I loved him, and I always would. What kind of fucking question was that, anyway?

  


"Yes," Liam finally said.

  


"Okay," the boy said simply. "How long have you lived here?" he asked, suddenly changing the subject.

  


"Uhhh... I think Mom and Dad have lived here for twelve years," Liam said thoughtfully.

  


"And you?" the boy continued.

  


"Me?" Liam hesitated. "I was born here."

  


"You sure?" the strange boy asked bluntly.

  


"Ummm..." Liam didn't seem to know what to say. "I think so..."

  


That was when I finally realized what they were talking about.

It was like they were somehow working their way toward the fact that Liam wasn't our biological child, even if neither of them realized it. And I sure as fuck wasn't about to have a conversation on my son's ninth birthday about where he came from and the fact that he was adopted.

  


"Guys, there you are," I called loudly, stepping out of the kitchen doorway.

  


They both looked at me in surprise, like they'd been up to something they shouldn't have and I'd just caught them.

At least, that's what I thought...

I could see the surprise on Liam's face.

The strange little boy, on the other hand, just stared at me with a dark expression, his eyebrows drawn together. Like I'd just interrupted something very important.

  


"Cake's coming," I blurted out awkwardly. "Go... go sit back down at the table, please. Just give me a minute."

  


"Wooo-hooo!" Liam jumped to his feet and practically ran back to the little patio table.

  


The other boy stood up beside the little flower bed too. Then, with a faint smile on his face, he walked off after him.

I just swallowed nervously and returned the smile.



I walked back into the kitchen, lost in thought. This new kid was strange. Where the hell were all those questions coming from? And why had he seemed so angry at me at first?

But I didn't have much time to think about it. Ann walked into the kitchen from the living room. She wasn't on the phone anymore, and I could tell from her face that I'd forgotten something.

  


"Where's the candle, Julian?" Ann asked almost immediately.

  


"What?" I snapped out of my thoughts. "I don't know... where is it?"

  


"I told you, it's in the drawer." Ann rolled her eyes.

  


Then, in typical annoyed-mom fashion, she dug out the candle while I just stood there waiting with a sheepish look on my face.

  


"Your parents doing okay?" I asked Ann as she fiddled with the candle.

  


"Mom's about the same. Dad's back is really bothering him," Ann answered simply as she lit the number nine candle. "But we'll talk about it later. Let's just get through the birthday first."

  


Ann gave me a warm smile, and I rubbed her shoulder. Even if it didn't always look like we loved each other from the outside, Ann and I had been through a lot together. By now, we knew each other's personalities pretty damn well.

  


"Here, let me," I said when Ann started trying to figure out the best way to pick up the cake. "I'll carry it."

  


Like some kind of professional waiter, I slid my hands underneath it, gave my wife a little nod, and headed outside.

The two boys were sitting on the bench waiting. The strange little boy whispered something to Liam just as I stepped outside, and my son burst out laughing. I never would've thought I'd care what two nine-year-olds were whispering about, but right then, it really bothered me.

Still, this wasn't the time for doubts.

  


Ann started loudly singing "Happy Birthday." Being the good husband and father I was, I joined in with my less-than-beautiful singing voice.

Liam smiled happily.

The little boy beside him, though, sat there like a doll.

He just watched us, looking back and forth between us and my son. It was like he was confused and suddenly had no idea what he was supposed to do. Then, when we got to the second "Happy birthday to you," the boy seemed to work up the courage and started singing along with us.

As much as this was my son's birthday, and it was supposed to be his own special day, I couldn't stop focusing on that boy.

I was starting to think Ann had been right.

There was something more going on with this kid than him just being a little weird.

  


The little celebration went by quickly and simply, like it did every year. Ann cut the cake. She gave Liam a kiss and a big hug, and so did I. But the whole time, I kept watching the boy.

He didn't seem to know what to do with himself. He either sat there watching us or studied Liam, practically squinting at him. When we clapped, he'd wait a moment and then clap too. He asked for a piece of cake exactly the same size as Liam's. When Liam got the present from his grandparents and eagerly started unwrapping it, the little boy seemed almost just as eager to see what was inside.

It was like he was copying everything Liam and my family did.

But I could tell something was bothering Ann too. She kept shooting suspicious little glances at the boy, then whenever she looked at me, she'd pretend she hadn't noticed anything.

We all ate the cake and enjoyed it. Then Liam went through all his presents with the boy, explaining who each one was from and which one he liked best. My brother and his family and Ann's parents hadn't been able to make it, but at least they'd all sent Liam something.

I was watching the fading sunlight stretch across the green grass in the backyard when Ann grabbed my wrist.

  


"Honey, come inside for a second, please," she said quietly. "Help me with the dishes."

  


I could tell from Ann's face that the dishes had absolutely nothing to do with why she wanted me inside, so I didn't argue. I grabbed what was left of the cake and helped carry everything into the kitchen.

Liam was showing the little boy some kind of robot action figure, though the kid clearly couldn't have cared less about the toy. The strange boy was watching us as we went inside.

  


"Julian..." Ann immediately set the plates down. "The little boy... what are we gonna do with him?"

  


"What do you mean?" I asked.

  


"You know what I mean." Ann's eyes widened meaningfully. "He's weird... and are his parents even coming to get him?"

  


"If they show up, we'll talk to them," I said matter-of-factly. "I think there's something wrong with the kid."

  


"Julian, come on." Ann smiled and scolded me. "Don't say things like that."

  


"But it's true." I shrugged. "You saw what he's doing. It's like he's copying how everyone acts. What to do and how to do it. Autistic kids do that kind of thing, don't they?"

  


"Oh, Julian." Ann shook her head. "The kid gives me a weird feeling too, but... I don't know. I just wish someone would come pick him up already."

  


I nodded awkwardly.

You don't normally say something like that about a little kid, but I was already starting to wish he'd get the hell out of our house.

  


"They're on the trampoline," Ann said, looking past my shoulder.

  


I could already hear Liam laughing loudly. We'd bought the trampoline the year before and set it up at the very back of the yard. Ann didn't let Liam use it very often, but I guess today was one of the exceptions.

  


"So what do you think?" I asked Ann. "What do we do with the kid?"

  


"I don't know," Ann answered immediately. "I don't even know who I could call to come get him. But I don't want him spending the night here."

  


"Yeah, of course," I agreed. "I'll call the cops if nobody comes for him by tonight."

  


"Oh, no." Ann looked horrified. "What would the neighbors say? And I'd rather it didn't come to that."

  


I nodded in agreement. Then something suspicious caught my attention.

The silence. 

The laughter we'd been hearing was gone. So was the loud squeaking of the trampoline.

  


"DAAAAAAD!" Liam suddenly screamed in pain.

  


Every one of my senses kicked in immediately. Like some kind of protective instinct, I spun around and, without even waiting to hear what Ann had to say or how she'd react, charged into the backyard like a fucking lunatic.

  


"OWWWWW! DAAAAD!" Liam kept screaming.

  


The sound was coming from the trampoline.

It was hard to see through the safety net, but I could make out one of the boys sitting there trembling while the other stood over him, perfectly still.

I lunged toward them like a wild animal and ripped open the trampoline's safety net.

My anger only flared hotter.

Liam was clutching his right hand against himself, his face terrified, while blood slowly trickled from it in thin red lines.

The strange boy was still standing rigidly in front of him.

He wasn't moving.

  


"What the fuck...?" I muttered angrily, not giving a shit about swearing anymore.

  


Because that was when I saw the boy's face.

His eyes were locked onto Liam like the eyes of some wild animal. There was a thin red smear around his mouth.

Liam wasn't saying anything anymore. He wasn't screaming. He was just quietly crying.

But I could see his hand...

It looked like the boy had bitten into it and spent a while chewing on it.

  


"What the fuck did you do?!" I screamed at the kid, grabbing him by the arm.

  


I yanked him so hard that I thought I was going to fall backward myself...

But the boy didn't move. He didn't pull away. He didn't fight me or thrash around.

He simply didn't budge.

It was like I was trying to pull a tree out of the ground with my bare hands. I grabbed him and yanked again, but only his arm moved slightly.

Neither the trampoline beneath him nor the boy himself moved an inch.

  


"WHAT THE FUCK?!" I roared. "GET THE HELL OUT OF HERE!"

  


The boy suddenly started blinking, like he was coming out of some kind of trance.

And then, while I was still pulling on him, he suddenly moved with me. Weak and clumsy, exactly the way you'd expect a nine-year-old kid to stumble when a grown man was yanking him around.

By then, my anger had gotten to the point where I didn't care who heard me or what any of the neighbors thought.

I wanted this little fucking demon out of my house.

I kept a tight grip on the kid's arm and dragged him across the backyard, past the toys and everything else.

Ann didn't say a word, but I saw her rush toward the trampoline.

The little boy hurried along behind me without making a sound or putting up any kind of fight. His little legs stumbled beneath him, his thin, weak arm almost disappearing inside my hand.

I didn't care.

I dragged him all the way to the front door.

Then I yanked the door open and threw the kid out onto the porch so hard that he dropped to his knees outside.

  


"Now get the fuck out of here while I'm still asking nicely!" I shouted angrily.

  


The little boy stood up and brushed off his knees like he'd just fallen down on the playground.

Then he looked at me with a completely calm expression.

  


"Get the fuck out!" I shouted at him. "Don't let me ever fucking see you here again!"

  


Then I slammed the door so hard the whole house shook. I stood there in front of it, breathing heavily, too pissed off to move. Like I was waiting for the little bastard to come back.

  


"Julian?" Ann said behind me.

  


I blinked and turned around, trying to calm myself down.

Ann stood there trembling with fear, holding Liam tightly against her while he was still quietly crying.

  


"What's going to happen to that boy?" Ann asked.

  


"I don't give a shit," I answered coldly. "He found his way here somehow. He can find his way home.”

  


Turned out to be a fucking great ninth birthday.

We spent the rest of the afternoon sitting at urgent care until it got dark. The wound on Liam's hand wasn't deep, but it had bled pretty badly, so Ann insisted we get it checked out. And she was right. Hell, we had no idea who that kid was, where he'd come from, whether he had some kind of disease...



They didn't stitch Liam's hand up. They just cleaned and treated the wound and told us to keep an eye on it. But hey, at least that killed another three hours of the afternoon.



Ann and I agreed we wouldn't tell anyone about who the little boy was. It was better that way. There'd just be questions, the cops would get involved, and then I'd have to explain why I'd thrown a nine-year-old kid out onto the street by himself.



I still didn't regret it, though.

That fucking kid was really starting to get to me. By the time we got back from the hospital, it was already dark. Ann and Liam stayed in the car for a little while so I could check the house first.

Like the kid was somehow going to be in there. I'd thrown him out with my own two hands.

The house was empty. Everything was exactly where we'd left it.

  


Once they came inside, Ann and Liam slowly made their way upstairs. Ann wasn't angry with me, but somehow I still felt like I was the asshole in this whole story.

I quietly cleaned up everything that was left from the party downstairs. The paper, the plates, the leftover pizza, and the half-melted cake we'd forgotten outside. I cracked open a beer, which I desperately needed after a day like that, and dropped into one of the kitchen chairs.

I just sat there staring into space like an idiot.

  


"You okay?" Ann asked as she stepped into the doorway.

  


"I'm fine," I said, taking a long drink of my beer.

  


"I'm not mad at you..." Ann continued. "But... you scared me. Liam too. That boy was... I don't know how to put it... really strange. And then when you got so angry too..."

  


"You don't have to be scared, Ann," I said calmly. "You know... I only lost it because of that stupid kid. He ruined Liam's birthday. It's bad enough the kids at school already give him shit. Now he's gonna show up with a bite mark on his hand. What do you think that's gonna do?"

  


Ann didn't answer. She just stared guiltily at the floor like a little kid who'd just been scolded.

  


"I'm so fucking sick of all this," I said angrily, taking another drink of my beer.

  


"Julian, I know..." Ann tried to say something.

  


"It's not going to get better," I cut her off. "Liam's not going to fit in here. We've talked about this before. They're not going to accept him. That's just how it is. I've been saying for years that we should move somewhere else."

  


Ann shrank into herself again and stared at the floor.

Moving had been a topic between us for a long time, but Ann had always insisted on staying. Supposedly, she loved the neighborhood and the house.

Then again, she hadn't made a single friend in the fifteen years we'd lived here.

  


"Forget it," I finally said to Ann. "Is Liam okay?"

  


"Yeah," Ann answered softly. "He's taking a bath. This whole thing really shook him up. I can tell he's scared."

  


"Can't blame him," I said irritably. "If I ever find out whose screwed-up kid that was, I'm definitely gonna have a talk with them. Do you know what happened on the trampoline? What did Liam say?"

  


"Nothing," Ann answered immediately.

  


I could see on her face that this was what bothered her the most.

But we knew Liam. He was our kid, after all. Whenever something happened that embarrassed him, or whenever someone hurt him, he'd shut down. He'd suffer in silence and wouldn't say a word. Even when we asked him directly, he'd just squint and refuse to talk.

  


"He won't say a single word about what happened," Ann said, her eyes filling with tears.

  


"Hey. Hey, Ann, don't cry." I immediately jumped up from the table. "Everything's okay."

  


"But... but..." Ann sobbed as I wrapped my arms around her. "I failed as a mother again. I'm not even his real mother... I never will be..."

  


"Shhh," I said, holding her and gently rubbing her back. "Don't talk like that. You're a great mom. You couldn't be a better one."

  


Ann stood there silently, sniffling against me. Whoever that little boy was, he'd really fucked up our weekend.

  


"Liam really didn't say anything?" I asked again. "Like what the boy's name was, or who he even was?"

  


Ann just shook her head.

I didn't push it any further. If Ann said she didn't know anything, then she probably didn't.

  


"The only thing he said was that when he goes to bed, he wants you to come give him a goodnight kiss too," Ann said finally, wiping her eyes.

  


"I will," I said with a tired smile.

  


It had been a long day, and I was starting to feel like it was about time I got to bed too.

  


I let out a long sigh outside Liam's bedroom door.



I felt like shit about everything that had happened. Just like Ann felt like she wasn't a good mother, something was eating away at me too, even if I didn't show it. Ann had already gone to take a shower after putting Liam to bed. In the meantime, I'd gone around checking that everything was locked. Better safe than sorry.

But standing there outside Liam's room, I was having a hard time pretending to be strong.

I took a deep breath and opened the door.

Liam was lying in bed with the covers pulled all the way up to his neck. The glow from his little night-light cast soft, yellow patterns across the walls of his room. I could tell from his face that he wasn't asleep. His eyes were open, and he was just staring out the window.

  


"Liam, why aren't you asleep? What's wrong?" I asked quietly.

  


Liam didn't answer. He lay almost completely still in bed.

I stepped closer and gently stroked his head. Only then did I realize just how terrified he looked. I figured that with night finally settling in, all the weirdness from the day had started weighing on him.

  


"Liam, buddy, what's wrong?" I asked, sitting down on the edge of his bed. "You don't have to be scared anymore. Dad took care of everything. You're still scared of that weird kid, aren't you?"

  


"Uh-huh," Liam said quietly.

  


"You don't have to be scared," I said reassuringly. "You know your mom and I are here, and we'll protect you. I threw that little asshole out. He's not gonna hurt you."

  


"But..." Liam squinted.



Then he suddenly sat up and leaned close to me.

I thought he wanted a hug, but instead, his small, sweaty hand grabbed the sleeve of my T-shirt, and he whispered into my ear.

  


"He said you can't just make him leave."

  


"What?!" I pulled Liam back and grabbed him by the shoulders.

  


There was something horrible in the fear on Liam's little face. He was sweating and trembling. I'd never seen that kind of terror on another person's face in my life.

  


"When did he say that?" I asked Liam loudly. "What else did he say?"

  


"He said we invited him in... and now he doesn't want to leave..."

  


"What? What are you talking about?" I asked, completely confused.

  


Liam just started squinting again as tears filled his eyes. His mouth trembled, but then, with the tiniest movement, he peeked past my shoulder.

I immediately whipped my head around...

Something glinted in the darkness inside the little wardrobe, visible through the slats in its wooden doors.



My heart started pounding harder, and I swallowed nervously. Liam yanked the covers all the way up to his neck.

I slowly stood up from the bed without taking my eyes off the wardrobe.

I took a single step toward it. The glint flashed again from inside.

Like the eyes of a cat or a wolf shining in the darkness, watching us like prey.

  


"What the fuck..." I whispered.

  


Then a strange sound came from inside the wardrobe.

The kind of sound you hear in nature documentaries.



Or maybe not even in those.

It was an animalistic, threatening growl, mixed with something human. Something ancient.

The blood froze in my veins, and a kind of fear took hold of me that human beings probably hadn't felt in thousands of years.



Whatever I'd let into our house...



It was still here with us.
