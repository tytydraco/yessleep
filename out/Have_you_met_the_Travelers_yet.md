You must have noticed them in your city by now: men and women, of all shapes and sizes and races and styles, wearing backpacks. Not schoolyard bags; big camping backpacks, overstuffed and worn over both shoulders to support what must be monstrously heavy loads. They don’t seem to mind, now do they? Wandering forth, hiking along roadways, tramping up and down parking lots, crossing streets. They don’t even seem to notice. They hold no maps but are clearly visitors. They have zero interest in finding a particular place but are always on the move. Instead of stopping to ask for directions they’ll stand motionless on a street corner, staring at nothing in particular as people and cars pass them, until finally at last they’ll move off in some random direction with aplomb determination, confident that every stride takes them closer.

Closer to what? Beats me.

These are the Travelers — things sent from afar to observe us. As far as I’ve been able to discover there is no final destination, no place they came from nor place they end up in. Every day brings with it a new host of Travelers, a remix of the previous day’s, the clothing and physical features all combined together in a new way. You may never see the same one twice — I haven’t.

Try it yourself, pick out a random one: it might be an asian man in hiking boots, or a skinny black woman wearing oval sunglasses. It could be a white hermaphrodite or a Native-American elder. It doesn’t truly matter which one, I used to have theories about templates and defaults, like an algorithm operating from a set of preselected human features it absorbs from the world. That was before realizing none of it matters anymore.

I know they aren’t human because I have spoken to them, or tried to anyway. Their mouth moves up and down in a perverted imitation of the jaw movement of human speech, but they produce no audible sounds. Hit one and they won’t react. Drive a car into one and they may get tossed aside or end up under the wheels... then pull themselves back up, straighten out their clothes and carry on as though nothing has happened. These aren’t human reactions.

Observe how yours walks, where it waits, how it finds its way. Watch as it slips into a back alley or through an open side-door and vanish the moment you take your eyes off it. My friend and I discovered this completely by accident while we were watching a squirrel gather scattered corn from a tipped trash can, then run into an abandoned commercial courtyard for delivery trucks, a place where all the doors had long since been boarded up. A moment later, out popped a Traveler.

Improbable spaces, impossible thresholds.

So, we have determined they’re not human, but have we determined if they are dangerous?

Myself and my friend, a man I call ‘B’ because he has a great big tattoo of a B written in a flourishing script on his neck, spent a weekend tracking one of these things. We loaded a cooler up with enough water and food to last us a week, put it in the back seat of my car, and drove around town until we spotted a good one. This one had the appearance of a freakishly tall man that made it easy for us to keep him in our sight. That was the difficult part, keeping it in sight, because at any moment he could slip through a door and escape, so if we needed to move because traffic was piling up behind us, B would proceed on foot and I would circle the block. It was a good system, one we quickly perfected; easy to track the thing, easy to keep driving slowly, easy to keep account of each other. I made that single tank of gas stretch for almost three days, and we went two nights without any sleep.

One such trip around the block brought me back to where B and the Traveler had been, now an empty sidewalk. In the waning daylight I peered out at both sides of the street, looking for a glimpse of either of them. I circled the block. No one. I circled again. Still nothing. I parked the car up on the sidewalk, desperate to spot any sign of my friend. The only thing I could think of was that B had tailed the Traveler down the alley that sat squeezed between a frozen yogurt shop and a shop that sold formal attire, and he had backed it into a corner.

Panic tightened in my chest. What if he needed my help? I had been out here, circling the block like a dummy, while he had one of these *things* cornered. I hopped out and followed the likely path. The alley was featureless, not even wide enough to serve as parking or a place to store dumpsters. It terminated in a brick wall connecting the two stores, and an aged door with flecked paint and no handle.

Even then, I didn’t want to believe it. I scoured the rest of the block looking for them, then got back in my car and went up and down every street in the neighborhood. I went back to the spot I had last seen them and banged on the glass door of the clothing shop moments after they had locked up for the evening, told them my cat had gotten into their building and could they please unlock the door in the alley? Said they knew of no such door, and didn’t feel much like following a disheveled, stinking man down an alleyway at night.

I went home and just lied on my bed. I found sleep before morning and didn’t wake up until one in the afternoon. This time I tried the yogurt place and the shop minder there followed me around the corner to the very back of the alley, huge ring of keys dangling off her finger.

There was no door. There wasn’t a back wall. The alley continued on out to the street on the other side. These two buildings weren’t even connected. I mumbled an apology to the shop keeper and wandered out into the sunlight of the sidewalk of the other street. I have a careful eye and the patience of a lunatic obsessed with the impossibilities of the world, and have seen since how these thing slide into existence from behind objects. Where there wasn’t one before, they’ll pop into being, or make their own doorway. It happens out of sight, always, so I can’t say for sure.

Improbable spaces, impossible thresholds.

Have you seen them? Have you seen the Travelers? This feels like something significant is about to happen, though I don’t know what. It feels like something we need to solve immediately but no one will believe me. I’m little more than a gibbering conspiracist screaming about the deep state and lizard-human hybrids.

And if you don’t have any Travelers in your city yet — just wait. I've spotted them in three different cities so far.

Some of them might even have a tattoo on their necks. One of a big B in a flourished script, right there for you to see. Right there in the open.

Watch them.
