I remember the car ride vividly. My friend had picked me up from college, and we were finally taking a trip together. It’d been over a year since we’d been together, so everyone was excited to go. There were five of us. Todd, who I’d known since fifth grade, was the only one I knew well.

The car was packed. Everyone’s gear was piled on their laps, and trash littered the floor. I figured the summer weather would make the hiking trip even better. Summer was always my favorite time of the year.

“Well, how’s everyone’s semester been?” Todd asked, keeping his eyes fixed firmly on the road.

“Well, computer science has been kicking my ass recently,” I said, earning a small chuckle from everyone else.

“That’s good, Robert. I’ve been studying law, and it ain’t too bad,” Samuel said, resting his head on Olivia’s shoulder.

“I’m going into engineering. Not too bad. I’ll get paid a lot when I’m done,” Olivia added, leaning back against Samuel.

A subtle, comfortable silence settled over the car. All that could be heard was the familiar hum of the tires on the highway. I looked out the window for a moment, watching the vibrant, green trees.

“God, you guys are all studying such practical subjects. I’m not really going into a traditional career. I’m studying philosophy,” Valentina said. She had a slight Colombian accent.

“Philosophy? Are you gonna be the next Socrates?” I retorted. She laughed a little. The silence came back soon after, although I didn’t mind. I was just happy I was getting to know everyone better. I was glad I made her laugh. Most people never really found me funny.

“Well, have you guys been to the Olympic National Forest yet? I’ve been here about four times,” Todd remarked to everyone else.

“I know my way around. It’s pretty easy to get lost, though. Nearly happened to me once,” Todd added, chuckling a little.

“I’ve never been here before. But the pictures look beautiful,” Samuel said.

“Yeah, yeah, it is. We’ll be there in about two hours. So get comfortable,” Todd added as he checked his phone.

Unfortunately, this was one of those rare summer days when the rain refused to let up. A blanket of thick, black clouds overtook the sky, and small raindrops began to strike the dashboard and windows.

After a few minutes, the rain intensified significantly. It was so loud that I could barely hear Samuel sitting beside me. Valentina looked nervous. I wondered if she was even a hiker. Samuel and Olivia remained cuddled together, seemingly oblivious to everyone else in the car.

I’d loosely known Olivia since middle school. She was quite tall and very attractive. Samuel was a lucky man. The first time I met her, she was helping a kid back up after he tripped.

Before long, the rain was so heavy that it felt like we were driving through a river. Todd cursed and threw his hands up in frustration. For a moment, I thought we might have to pull over.

“Damn! If it rains anymore, we might need a boat!” Todd yelled. Nobody laughed.

“Did you hear me? If it rains anymore, we’ll need a boat!” Todd yelled again. I laughed, but nobody else seemed to notice. The rain gradually weakened, and eventually it was gone. Not long after, we saw the entrance to the forest.

The trees were tall and densely packed, their canopies blocking much of the sunlight. The forest floor was covered in ferns and damp earth. I could occasionally hear birds and other animals calling to one another.

After a few minutes of driving down a dark, winding road, we arrived at the visitor center.

“Alright, let’s just go in and get permission to hike. We’ll be out for about four days,” Todd said as I pushed open the car door. The warm, moist air washed over me as I got out.

“Wow, feels good out here!” Samuel yelled as he stretched. Olivia stood right next to him. We all walked to the door and entered the visitor center. Compared to the warmth outside, the visitor center was cold.

It seemed nearly empty except for one employee. Through the windows and fog, I could see the beautiful, distant snowcapped mountains. The sky still looked like stone.

“This place is nice. Better than I last saw it,” Todd said to the group as he walked up to the employee. Olivia and Samuel went their own way, while I wandered over to the souvenirs. Everything looked cheap and flimsy.

The rain made the place feel strangely isolated. I didn’t want to stay here that long. As Todd and the employee spoke, I heard footsteps approaching behind me, growing louder with each step.

“What are you looking at, Robert?” Valentina asked. The light from outside reflected off her dark hair.

“Oh, I’m just looking at all this junk,” I said, turning toward her. She was noticeably shorter than me.

“Junk? Nothing is junk, Robert. Everything has its   
own purpose and meaning, even if it’s as simple as a plastic toy,” she said as she grabbed a tree magnet.

“Oh, I never thought about it that way. Did Plato say that?” I replied. Todd’s conversation with the employee drowned out ours.

“Alright, thanks, man. I want a quiet trail. Nothing too crowded, you know?” Todd asked.

“Oh, probably that one,” the employee said as he pointed to an isolated section of the map.

“Ok,” Todd said. An awkward silence followed.

“Well, uh, what about the weather? Will it continue being rainy and ugly?” Todd asked.

“Nah, probably not. Not too long at least,” the employee answered. Todd nodded.

“Just be safe, you know. And don’t slip in the mud. A few people have gotten injured that way. The weather changes out there, man,” the employee told Todd. Todd nodded and went over to Olivia and Samuel.

“Alright, Robert and Valentina, come on!” Todd shouted, his voice echoing slightly across the center. Olivia held open the door for us.

“Thanks,” I said.

“Alright, the trailhead isn’t far from here. We can park and get all our things before we go,” Todd said as he got back into the car. We all followed. Todd cranked up the heat, and we began driving. Fog covered much of the forest, and the shadows stretched across the road.

“It’s getting a bit dark, isn’t it?” Samuel questioned, looking around.

“I don’t wanna sleep near the trailhead, Todd,” Samuel added.

“No, we probably won’t. We can stop walking after an hour or two,” Todd answered.

“Yeah, well, I’m not walking at night,” Samuel replied. Todd sighed and didn’t reply. I rolled down my window, letting the sounds of the forest fill the car. Birds chirped, insects buzzed, and the fresh smell of damp earth drifted inside.

“Sure is nice out there,” I said, looking at the scenery.

“Yeah, pretty nice,” Olivia replied as she looked out her window.

“How long will we be out here again?” Samuel asked, seemingly nervous.

“Again, only a few days, as I said,” Todd responded.

“We’re taking the Sentinel Peak Loop. Forty-five miles altogether. It’s not going to be easy, but we’ve got four or five days to finish it,” Todd said.

“Forty-five miles? God, I don’t think I can walk all that,” Olivia replied.

“Trust me, I’ve walked longer. It isn’t too bad,” Valentina said to Olivia, to which she groaned.

“From what I’ve seen, it’s beautiful this time of year, and it’s not like we’re beginners. We got this,” Todd said as he pulled into the parking lot. I was anxious, but I knew we could make it.

“Alright, everyone, grab your camping stuff and food. We won’t see anyone, so we’re fine,” Todd said as he got out of the car and opened his trunk, grabbing his things. Everyone else followed.

“I needed this break,” I said as I grabbed my things and walked close behind Todd. We started down the trail. Fog concealed most of our vision beyond about thirty feet.

Still, the summer heat hit us. After a few minutes, I could see Olivia and Samuel were sweating and tired. Even Todd was sweating.

“Yeah, you and I need to connect to nature more often, you know?” Todd replied. The trail didn’t remain beneath the canopy the entire time. Occasionally, we would walk along a mountainside.

“You’re damn right, Todd,” I said, looking into the dark green pine trees and stepping over any exposed roots. I could hear Valentina’s footsteps closely behind me. Samuel and Olivia walked a little behind us. They weren’t as experienced as the rest of us, and they seemed content to talk among themselves.

“When was the last time we hiked together?” I asked as Todd maintained a steady pace a few feet ahead of me.

“Oh hell, I don’t know. Probably last year, when you tripped and nearly broke an ankle, remember that?” Todd asked, laughing. I laughed and grabbed his shoulder.

“Yeah, yeah, I do,” I replied, still laughing.

“What about you, Valentina? Why are you studying philosophy and hiking? Where’s the correlation?” Todd asked, still slightly laughing.

“My mother liked hiking before she passed. She wanted me to enjoy it as well, so I’m carrying on her legacy,” Valentina said.

“Oh, ah,” Todd said, momentarily lost for words. His tone softened.

“But I don’t think much about it. I still enjoy hiking, and this trail presents a worthwhile challenge,” Valentina went on.

“Trust me, this trail is no challenge,” Todd joked. I laughed slightly, but Valentina didn’t seem entertained. I nearly tripped on a root, but Valentina thankfully caught my hand. She had a firm grip.  
The trees encroached upon the trail, making it hard to move forward at times. 

The wind had started to pick up again, sending the branches swaying and the bushes rustling. The smell of wet bark and earth filled the air around me. Occasionally, the sun would peek through the trees and blind me for an instant before hiding away again.

We walked for a little longer. The animals continued calling to one another. Valentina had grown quieter. I assumed the long hike was beginning to wear on her. Todd guided us forward. Soon, the shadows beneath the trees grew longer and darker.

“Hey guys! Maybe we should, like, take a break?” Olivia called out from behind us. Todd rolled his eyes and turned around.

“We can… go to sleep soon. It’s not too dark out.” Todd paused, looking deep into the sky. He considered for a moment.

“Let’s just hit the hay, Todd,” I said. He stared for a moment before looking to the ground.

“We can walk for another… thirty minutes,” he said, continuing to walk forward. No one objected. Crickets started to chirp, and before too long, stars filled the sky. We walked until we finally found a designated camping area.

“Here, let’s go,” Todd muttered as he headed down the small, overgrown path. The equipment on my back had grown heavy, so I was relieved to finally toss it on the ground. Everyone began setting up their own equipment. Todd finished before everyone else.

“Alright, I’ll start a fire,” Todd groaned as he went looking for wood. I wasn’t as skilled with tents as Todd, so it took me a few minutes to finish. I could see that Samuel and Olivia struggled, but Valentina was helping them. Todd emerged from the darkness.

“Alright, I have all the wood,” Todd said as he threw down all the wood and twine. It took a few minutes to get the fire going, but soon it illuminated the small clearing. Finally, Olivia and Samuel finished setting up their tent.

“That was fairly quick, Todd,” Olivia said. Todd looked up from the fire.

“Well, I’ve only been doing it for fifteen years, so it’s pretty easy for me,” Todd replied.

“How long did we walk?” Samuel asked.

“A little over an hour. That’s why I wanted to walk more,” Todd replied. He sounded annoyed.

“Well, I’m sure we can walk more tomorrow,” I said, sitting down on the log next to the fire with everyone else.

“Well, if we walk two miles an hour for… seven hours a day, we can finish in no time,” Todd said, throwing more sticks into the fire. Samuel sighed, resting his forehead on his hands.

“It’s not too bad, Sam. It’s just walking,” Valentina reassured Samuel. He sluggishly nodded.

“I think I’ll just go to sleep,” Samuel said, grabbing Olivia’s arm and pulling her into the tent with him. Todd looked at Valentina, and then me. He paused a second.

“I guess I’ll go to sleep as well,” Valentina said, heading toward her tent. Todd glanced in her direction before looking back at the fire. The gentle rustle of the leaves picked up all around us, causing shadows to creep and drift.

“Doesn’t really feel like summer…” Todd stated, looking around into the trees. The air felt thick and cold. My senses began to dull, and my eyelids grew heavy.

“Shit, man. I might just have to go to sleep now,” I yawned, getting up from the log.

“Just make sure you’re going to your tent and not Valentina’s,” Todd said as he laughed. I chuckled a little, fumbling around with the zipper.

“I’ll just stay out here for a few minutes. I need to be alone for a second or two.” I had already entered my tent before Todd finished speaking.

“Alright, goodnight,” I said, zipping my tent up. I laid out a sleeping bag and pillow, shutting off the lantern. Olivia and Samuel spoke in the tent next to me. The wind picked up, rustling the leaves and bushes. The insects buzzed. I yawned and began drifting off to sleep.

“Yeah, well we only have to walk another forty-two miles,” Samuel said, his voice muffled by the wind and tents. The fire began to dim, and everything became pitch-black. Just before I fell asleep, the wind came to a halt, and the bugs stopped calling for one another. It fell dead silent.

I could hear Todd pacing near the fire outside. Even Olivia and Samuel were silent. I got up, listening intently. Todd continued pacing until, suddenly, I heard him fall.

It stayed silent. The wind started to blow once more, and the insects chirped and buzzed. I opened my tent and rushed outside. Todd was passed out on the ground, his jacket sleeve pushed up.

“Todd! Todd!” I yelled, falling to his side. I grabbed his shoulders and shook him.

“Augh… what the hell?” Todd groaned as he slowly opened his eyes.

“Todd, what the fuck were you doing?” I asked, helping him back up.

“I-I don’t know… I must’ve passed out. I don’t remember anything…” Todd said, looking around dazed. Olivia poked her head out of the tent, and Valentina soon followed.

“What happened?” Olivia asked, getting out.

“I passed out or something… just let me lay down,” Todd groaned as he walked toward his tent. He held his right arm, and his posture was slouched.

“Are you alright, man?” I asked, confused.

“Yeah, yeah. I-I think so,” he replied, getting into his tent.

“What’s wrong with him?” Valentina asked in a confused tone.

“He must’ve… passed out,” I replied.

“Well, he looks fine enough. Goodnight, I guess,” I said, getting back into my tent.

“Yeah, goodnight,” Valentina said. Olivia went in not long after. I thought about Todd for a few minutes, but I rationalized it as exhaustion. I’d try to talk about it tomorrow. Eventually, I fell asleep. As my eyes slowly closed, the warm sun illuminated through my tent.

“No, asshole! I wasn’t doing anything!” I could hear Samuel yell from outside. I opened my tent and saw that Todd and Samuel were arguing.

“Asshole? I’m just telling you not to look through my backpack!” Todd yelled.

“Let’s calm down,” I said. I’m sure I was unintelligible, as I was half awake. Samuel paced around the clearing, visibly annoyed.

“Can you stay still, Samuel? Do you have fucking ADHD?” Todd screamed.

“Don’t say that!” Olivia yelled back, walking up to Todd. I ran between them, pushing Todd back. He was a pretty large guy, so he was easily intimidating.

“Fuck you! And I heard you two having sex in the tent! Couldn’t you have been quieter?” Todd yelled, pointing his index finger at Olivia’s face. Valentina just watched.

“Todd, calm down, man,” I whispered.

“Goddamnit!” Todd yelled back, walking away. The camp fell silent. After the tension seemed to die down, Olivia and Samuel went to one side. Todd just sat down and stared into the thick trees. I walked up to him, crouching down beside him.

“Todd, what the hell has gotten into you, man?” I asked. He didn’t answer, ignoring me.

“Man, come on. Don’t act like a child,” I said, putting my hand on his shoulder. Again, he didn’t respond.

“Todd, answer me. What’s with you, man?” I asked again, my tone growing impatient and annoyed.

“Piss off, Robert,” Todd calmly said. He had always been socially awkward, but never an asshole.

“Did you hear me, Robert? Piss off,” Todd spoke firmly. I needed to give him space, so I nodded and walked away. Everyone sat in silence for another ten minutes. Todd never moved. Valentina eventually spoke.

“It was very silent this morning,” she said. It took us all a second to respond.

“Yeah, weird,” Olivia replied. Samuel hugged her. A few minutes later, Todd interrupted the silence.

“Alright, let’s fucking go. I’m sick of this place,” Todd yelled, putting his things in his bag. We all silently agreed. We didn’t want to anger Todd anymore. After a few minutes, we left the clearing and started hiking again. Todd was mostly silent and walked about twenty feet ahead of us.

“What’s with him?” Valentina asked.

“Hell, I don’t know. Maybe he hit his head when he fell and changed his personality?” I replied.

“You’re probably correct. Let’s hope he doesn’t get any more violent,” she said.

We passed by many cascades and waterfalls, and Valentina had something to say about each. I needed to speak to Todd eventually, and that seemed like as good a time as any.

“Todd, do you want to talk?” I asked, quickening my pace to stay up with him. He walked very quickly.

“About?” he replied.

“Uh, I don’t know. I mean, you were being a bit of an… asshole back there,” I said. Todd’s expression changed, but his eyes stayed locked on the trail.

“Let’s go this way. It’s quicker,” Todd said, veering left. I didn’t question it. He was the one with the map.

“Well, Todd?” I asked.

“I wasn’t an… Samuel was looking into my things and being a moron,” he replied, turning his eyes over to me.

“Well, you don’t have to be that rude… I’m sure he didn’t mean any harm by it,” I added. Todd remained silent.

“Yes, he was,” Todd firmly said, his eyes locked onto mine. He kept walking down the trail.

“Well, cool it down, man,” I said, looking away at the ground.

“Yes… he was,” Todd said in an even more firm voice.

“Alright, man,” I replied. He was making me uncomfortable. I never knew him to be like this.

“Yes, he fucking was,” he said as he stopped   
walking, fixing his attention entirely on me. Everyone else was still behind, but they were watching. Everything else fell dead silent. The only thing audible was Todd’s rough breathing. I could feel his eyes becoming ingrained in mine, their stare almost impossible to escape. I looked around, too afraid to look back.

“Alright… Todd. I didn’t mean to insult you,” I told him. He stared a moment before walking ahead. Eventually, everyone else caught up.

“What was that?” Samuel asked.

“I-I don’t know. He’s just being a jackass…” I replied. I was still shaken up. Todd moved farther and farther ahead of the group, but we could always see him. He led us deeper into the forest. I would’ve objected, but I was too scared to say anything.

“Where the hell is he taking us?” Samuel asked, looking at Olivia and me.

“I-I don’t know. He’s been here before. We can trust him,” Olivia replied. Samuel looked visibly annoyed.  
“Well, I’m gonna tell him to take us back to the path. I’m sick of this shit!” Samuel yelled, pushing past me and Valentina. I ran up behind him.

“Listen, Todd’s been aggressive. I wouldn’t do that,” I pleaded, but it fell on deaf ears. Samuel grabbed Todd’s shoulder, stopping him.

“Hey, listen, man, we’re sick of your shit! Take us back to the path, now!” Samuel said. Todd promptly threw a right hook at him, sending him falling.  
“Agh…” Samuel groaned, blood pouring out of his mouth as he got up. Todd continued walking forward.

“Sam!” Olivia yelled, rushing to his side. I helped him up. He wasn’t badly injured. He’d be fine.

“God… he punched me,” Samuel said, looking around. There was a silent, mutual agreement that we wouldn’t mess with Todd. We followed him for another hour, passing by a variety of places. All the while, I could hear Samuel mouthing off to Olivia. We walked for seemingly hours, and the shadows grew long and eerie. It was dusk.

We walked for the better part of a day, and my legs ached. I could feel my ankles becoming weak and unresponsive. I’d never walked as much as this. I could hear Samuel groaning and Valentina taking a drink from her water bottle. This would be a very long walk.

The trails became increasingly strange. Some disappeared into dense patches of forest, while others seemed to end without warning. But Todd somehow knew exactly where to go, so we kept following him. After a few particularly uncomfortable minutes of walking through thick brush, we found a clearing.

“We’ll sleep soon, then we’ll set up camp,” Todd said quietly. I could hardly hear him. I don’t recall him saying another word after that. Not long after we finished, he went to his tent. Everyone sat by the small fire.

“If he ever did that to me, I would’ve knocked him on his ass,” I said to Samuel.

“If he gets any worse, we might just have to leave him,” Valentina said quietly, to which we all laughed. We tried to remain silent so as not to wake Todd. I ate some tuna, the least I could bring on this trip. Valentina silently got up and entered her tent. Olivia and Samuel followed. I didn’t want to be out here alone, so I went into my tent as well.

As I settled in, the silence and cold arrived again. First, the wind stopped blowing, and then the insects went silent. I turned off my lantern, listening intently. I couldn’t hear much of anything, but I kept waiting. Quietly, I could hear something moving around. First it moved against the leaves, then the brush, and then it stopped altogether.

I was terrified. I grabbed my flimsy knife and looked in the direction of the sound. Softly, I could hear something slithering across the ground. I let out a sigh of relief. It was only a snake. A moment of relief filled me, and I laid back down, but my moment of silence was interrupted by someone’s tent opening. I listened closely.

Judging by the proximity, it must’ve been Todd. I got up and opened my tent. I turned on my phone flashlight, looking around.

“Todd? What the hell are you doing?” I asked, looking around. As I exited my tent further, Todd came into view. He was still wearing his shoes, his jacket, his phone in hand. He stared into the dark forest.

“Todd!” I yelled as silently as I could, fully exiting the tent. Still, there was no sound. I walked behind Todd, placing my hand on his shoulder.

“I know, Todd. These last few days have been fucked, but we’ll get through this,” I said. Todd remained motionless, staring into the trees. I shook him, trying to get his attention. I shook harder. Still nothing.

“Todd, let’s just go back to slee—” Before I could finish, Todd broke out into a sprint. I flinched. I could see him escape into the pure darkness, concealed by its dark hands.

“Todd! Todd!” I yelled, pointing my flashlight into the forest. Slowly, I could hear the air blow and the leaves and grass being pushed by the wind.

“Todd! It’s not fucking funny anymore, man! Come on!” I yelled, running to the edge of the tree line. Olivia and Valentina emerged from their tents, confused by the noise. That was the last time I saw Todd.

“Where the hell did he go?” Olivia asked, walking behind me. Beyond the eerie stillness of the forest, there was nothing.

“I-I don’t know. He just ran off!” I replied.

“Shit, shit, shit, shit,” I repeated, pacing in a circle. Todd was the only one who had any real idea how to get back, so without him, we were lost.

“Well then, how will we get back?” Valentina asked. I could see Samuel emerge from his tent.

“I don’t know. We can try and go back the way we came,” I replied. The reality of being lost in the woods hadn’t yet dawned on me. An uncomfortable silence fell over the group. I spoke.

“Well, where will we go now? It’s still a three-day walk to the exit, and that’s without a map!” I yelled. Valentina seemed lost in thought, and Olivia went to Samuel for comfort. I knew I’d have to replace Todd’s role as the group leader.

“Well, let’s finish sleeping, and we can start hiking back tomorrow,” I said to the group. Valentina nodded.

“Well, you don’t have a map, do you?” Samuel said, pulling away from Olivia and walking over to me. The thought entered my head that Todd must’ve had one, so I rushed into his open tent. Inside, I could see his untouched sleeping bag and miscellaneous junk.

“God! We’ve walked for how long? Twenty miles? And now he decides to go, asshole!” Samuel yelled from outside.

“There has to be a map…” I muttered, looking around. I looked in a few bags but found nothing. Todd had barely touched his food or water. His tent seemed hardly lived in.

“Find anything?” I could hear Samuel impatiently ask.  
“Nothing,” I replied, defeated. The realization finally began to sink in. We were truly lost. I muttered incoherently as I tossed things around, desperately searching for a map. Eventually, I gave up. Olivia and Samuel stared at me.

“There’s nothing there,” I said, putting my hand on my sweaty forehead. Olivia’s eyes began to tear up, and Samuel quickly started to comfort her.  
“What about his phone?” Olivia pleaded.

“He… ran off with it,” I replied. Olivia reached for hers, pulling it out. She didn’t have any cell service or any downloaded maps. I checked my own phone, but I had no service, and I hadn’t downloaded any maps.

“We’re fucking lost,” Olivia cried, burying her head into Samuel’s shoulder as she hugged him. I couldn’t think of anything reassuring to say.

“It’s fine. We’ll be out soon,” Samuel said, leading her back to their tent. I stood in the billowing wind, looking at my feet. The grass subtly moved from left to right, my feet interrupting the process. I turned around and went back into my tent. The ground felt particularly cold and rough that night. I didn’t sleep well.
