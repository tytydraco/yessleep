Do you like movies? I do, and so does Jake. When we saw the opportunity to work at a movie theater, it seemed like a no-brainer. We thought nothing could ruin movies for us, but we were wrong. They say that really good movies and really bad ones can leave an impression on people. Though I never imagined that impression would be so direct and that the people who had seen it would be changed irrevocably.

For our part, we did it because we needed another job. Hoping for something simple. Even if it was low pay, low effort, at least it would help pay the bills since the last gig had not gone so well. Though I suppose the owner couldn't pay us because he was worried about the [bigger picture](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/s/qs7yaBrac0).

Anyway, the place we found this time was a bit of a change. It was an opportunity to work as ushers for a small theater that had opened up nearby. The pay was slightly above average, so that was nice at least. All things considered, we figured we would give ushering a try; after all, how bad could it be?

Jake surprised me and was already waiting in the van, ready to go. He seemed excited, and before I could even close my door and put on my seatbelt, he asked,

“Do you think we will get to see free movies when we want? I heard that's a benefit to working at a theater.”

I buckled up and sighed,

“I’m sure we can, though with what time I don’t know. We are going to need to find other jobs to cover the difference between the pay we were supposed to get for our last job, so I’m not sure how you can be happy at a time like this.”

Jake was already scoffing, and the annoying grin was spreading on his face,

“Come on, you know you are a little excited too. Plus maybe we will get lucky, and the place will be an ex-insane asylum with ghosts coming out of the screens.” He held up his hands in a mock scare and started to laugh.

I was annoyed but smiled grimly when I considered his idea might not be too far off. After all, with our luck in work, he might be right. Either way, we had to get going.

I nodded and gave him the thumbs up. Jake smiled again and hit the gas.

“Let's go!” He shouted, while cranking up “For Whom The Bell Tolls” by Metallica.

We made it there in less than fifteen minutes. Surprisingly, we were able to find parking nearby. The old theater downtown had been revived and had a new sign out front indicating it was now called “The Carcosian Theater”

“Weird name.” Jake mused.

“Maybe it’s Italian?” I speculated.

“Well, whatever, let's get inside and meet our new boss,” Jake said while sounding vaguely annoyed,

“Try and be nice; not every manager is a secret psycho or evil prick,” I told him, while doubting the validity of my own words based on our history.

“Yeah, I guess, though you never know; it is just a theater.” Jake trailed off. Before I could say anything, he rushed towards the building, but not before looking back and shouting,

“After all, what’s the worst that could happen?” I shook my head and raced after him.

We stepped inside the front door, and the place was surprisingly nice for a small movie theater. Fine art hung on the walls, and there was a fancy red carpet along the ground that would have made Hollywood proud.

“Snazzy,” Jake said while poking around. I pulled him along; we had to hurry; we didn’t want to be late for our orientation.

We stepped into an impressive lobby, which could have doubled for an opera hall with its size.

There was a large box office in the center and two branching halls which must have led to the theaters.

Jake and I moved up to the box office to see if anyone was there. No one was inside, and I called out a quick,

“Hello? Anyone here? We are the new hires here to see Mr….” I blanked for a moment and turned to Jake,

“What was his name again?”

Jake shrugged,

“Mr…. Boss… man, is he in?”

He gave me a lame thumbs up, and I sighed,

“Yeah, nice save.”

We were about to start looking around when we heard a chiming bell down the hall, and a man had appeared in the lobby, from somewhere behind us. I almost gasped since I hadn’t heard anyone come in. Jake had a similar reaction, and we saw a man in a bright yellow suit approach us.

He was incredibly tall and wore a bemused look on his sallow face. What struck me immediately when looking at him was that he had no hair on his head, not even eyebrows; his skin was also incredibly pale. I tried not to stare, and I looked to Jake to make sure he was not either.

The man extended a hand to us, and we each shook it in turn.

“Pleased to meet you both, my name is Castor Hasturing. This is my theater. I’m so glad you two could come. We do not employ many people here, but I assure you this is a worthwhile endeavor. To help show the world our wonderful picture and change hearts and minds in the most profound way imaginable.”

He held out his hands, gesturing the grandeur of his statement. Then he stopped looking at us, and carried on with some odd movements as if he was acting for some unseen audience.

He turned back, put a hand on his chest and spoke again,

“But you two must be ready to get to work. Come with me; I will explain my expectations.”

Hasturing started down one of the halls, and we followed wordlessly, or rather I did.

Jake tried to get my attention, and when he did, he mouthed a few statements about how Mr. Hasturing looked. I frowned and quickly shook my head, trying to get him to focus.

We caught up and reached a small office close to one of the theaters, which was closed off with velvet ropes.

We stepped inside and were bid to sit down in some rather comfortable armchairs.

“Nice digs, this is a step up from the folding chairs most places give us,” Jake said loudly while leaning back. I was afraid he would just kick his feet up on the small glass coffee table, but he at least refrained from doing that.

Mr. Hasturing clapped his hands together and did not seem bothered by Jake’s casual attitude.

"Alright gentlemen, this is a new business venture, not some generic theater that shows the trivial movies produced in Hollywood; this is an entire experience that will change the way our audience views the world entirely. Yet we are forced to still employ some menial laborers for certain tasks; that is where you two come in."

He continued,

“We show one picture here, a film so sublime that in order to properly experience it, the guests who come in will view the picture over the course of three days. Your job is simple. You are to clean up after each showing. And you are not to enter the auditoriums once the movie has started. You are also not to engage with customers directly after the show. Just clean the theaters and prepare them for the next showings. You are also needed to ensure no bystanders or other people who have not been selected are able to sneak in; absolutely no looky-loos. Do you think you can handle that?”

He asked us pointedly.

“Yeah, sure thing!” Jake said straight away,

“Yes, certainly.” I quickly added in.

Mr. Hasturing seemed satisfied and continued.

“We will, of course, need to cover a few other details as well. Some general ground rules, as it were. The first is absolutely no digital or potential recording devices allowed. You will be issued a radio, a flashlight, and cleaning supplies; that is all. Just clean the auditoriums once the movie is over and reset the film reels by the projector. Perhaps if you two do your job well, you might even be allowed to view this transcendent masterpiece yourselves.”

Mr. Hasturing grinned at us, and it was much worse than the neutral expressions he had carried thus far. The way his pale skin drew back in that curved grin looked almost painful, like his face was too tightly pulled back over his angular skull.

Jake nudged me and winked while giving a thumbs up, and I realized he was still excited about the potential of free movies. I was not sure exactly what sort of movie would be playing at this kind of theater, but the buildup about this supposedly great film made me doubtful, not excited.

Jake and I both put on our best good worker faces and just smiled and nodded at Hasturing's suggestion. Hasturing nodded and seemed satisfied.

“Very good, then come with me and I will introduce you to the other employees. We have a fairly small operation here. Our last usher, young Theo, had an accident and had to leave our employ. I may have run him a bit ragged, which is why I hired you two to take over all the duties as ushers.”

I was concerned by the implication of what happened to the last usher, but I brushed it off and followed along.

Mr. Hasturing led us to a small counter that featured various movie concessions and other food items.

Hasturing leaned onto the counter and called out,

“Cassilda! Come here, we have some new coworkers.”

A pretty young woman emerged from the back, long black hair flowing over her shoulders. She had pale white skin just like Hasturing, but instead of being off-putting and ghoulish, hers seemed pristine and beautiful. She had sharp eyes of a dazzling green color, and I had a hard time not staring as she walked up to the counter to greet us.

Jake apparently did too, and his jaw was hanging halfway down. I subtly kicked his leg, and he sorted himself out as we both walked up to meet her.

“A pleasure to meet you both. I don’t know how much I will be seeing of you, but it’s always nice to know who we are working with. I promise this place is the best; I mean, have you seen the signs?”

She stared at us, and we looked at each other, unsure of how to answer.

Mr. Hasturing stepped in,

“Alright, Cassilda, don't confuse them on their first day. They will follow the signs, very soon I’m sure…” He paused awkwardly, and I was wondering just why they were talking so much about signs, before he continued,

“The theater signs. We use the signs to navigate around this large theater. Each theater follows a numbered route that loops around the building. It will make more sense once you have had a chance to take a spin around the complex. But come on, let's go meet our ticket taker, Sam.”

We followed Hasturing back near the entrance, and we approached the box office. He knocked on the small door on the back three times in quick succession,

“Sam, are you there? We have new blood.” He waited for a moment, then knocked again,

“Sam!” No answer; he looked back to us apologetically,

“I'm sorry, it seems Sam is unavailable at the moment. I’m sure you will see him eventually. Come one lets get your gear. Showtimes begin in less than an hour, and we will hopefully have many people beginning their journey here tonight with the first part.”

“The first part of what?” Jake asked. I was wondering that as well.

“Why the film sequence, of course. This experience is not some threadbare ninety-minute film. The majesty of the picture is split into three parts. To truly process how profound each part is, audience members watch the film in three separate acts over three separate days.”

“Oh, okay…”Jake replied, sounding as confused as I was.

“Are all of your movies like that?” I asked.

Mr Hasturing frowned,

“No, you misunderstand; there are no other movies here. No other mundane films could share the screen with this profound work of art; we will only display the one picture; we only need the one picture!”

He shouted the last word, and the intensity he had about this movie was unsettling. Both Jake and I found it strange. It was a theater that had dozens of screens but only showed one movie? And that movie was split into three parts, and wouldn't be shown all at the same time? I had never heard of anything like that before.

I looked to Jake, and he shrugged. We watched Hasturing start to walk away, and before following, Jake whispered,

“Maybe it's porn…You know weird theater rules, odd schedule…lots of cleanup…” He started laughing, and I held my face in my palms. This one was going to suck.

We caught up with Mr. Hasturing, who seemed to move along at speed, but stopped at several of the paintings on the walls and regarded them with an odd rueful sigh.

Eventually we reached a small backroom, and he gave us each a yellow vest and a radio. He then produced two flashlights for us, and after handing us those, he pointed to a small lockbox and held out his hand,

“Phones, please.”

I was not a fan of giving up my phone for any job, but I was not willing to walk away from this gig just to hang onto it. We both handed him our phones, and he was satisfied.

“You can reclaim them when your shift is over. The last show is out at midnight. On Wednesday you will need to stay until 2:00 am, so I hope that is acceptable.”

I was nodding my head, but Jake spoke up,

“Can we have our phones back for breaks?” Hasturing frowned and repeated himself,

“You can reclaim them when your shift is over.” Jake frowned, and I shrugged.

Once we were all set, Hasturing told us to stand by since the first audience was about to be let in.

“Did you want us to help people get in the theater?” I asked.

“No, that will not be necessary. All customers' seats are assigned; please just wait until your services are needed.”

“So no cleaning other stuff or anything?” I asked,

“No, that will not be necessary. The rest of the grounds will be taken care of; just concern yourself with the theaters. Good luck, gentlemen, and remember…” He looked back to us with that same disturbing grin from before, “No peeking.”

He laughed and turned his back on us while waving a hand over his head like a pantomime farewell.

Then he departed and left us standing in the halls outside the theater, slightly confused, but otherwise ready for our first night's work.

“Man, how long do you think the first movie is? Because we are going to be waiting a long time without our phones.” For once I agreed with Jake’s complaint; it was going to be a long night.

Right on time, a rush of people filled the halls. It seemed like a pretty standard crowd for a big movie release. The people looked pretty normal. I was surprised, since I half expected a bunch of overdressed socalites with how Hasturing played up the artistic mastery of his great film.

Jake was waving at people and greeting them. I rolled my eyes and was concerned we were not supposed to engage the guests, but Jake quickly shut me down, saying,

“Hasturing said not to go in or engage the customers after they have watched the movie, nothing about before.”

I admitted he was right, and he continued his unofficial role as greeter. As I watched more of the crowd pile in, I noticed no one returned the greeting; everyone seemed intensely focused on getting in, as if finding their seats was the most important thing they had ever done. I heard Jake call out to Nick, an old classmate from high school.

He was moving with the group into a theater, but he either didn't hear us or pretended not to when we both tried to get his attention and see how he was doing. I felt a bit insulted, but figured maybe he had to sign an NDA about the movie, like he was an early reviewer and couldn't talk. I told Jake what I suspected, and he responded crassly,

“Or….he didn't want to see a familiar face because of what he is about to do in there.” He made a motion with his hands over his crotch, and despite my eyes rolling, I laughed a little bit at that one. But before Jake could continue with the bit, I quickly replied,

“Yeah, but if you are right, then I call not it on cleaning the seats.” I grinned triumphantly, and Jake looked irritated and beaten at his own game, before smiling and shrugging it off.

We watched the last of the stragglers enter the auditoriums. We nodded to each other and started our rounds to ensure the doors were closed to each theater.

We made it back to the main lobby after about ten minutes and started the long wait until the show was out. I had no idea how long that might be, but after nearly an hour of standing there talking to Jake about our top favorite movies in every genre we could think of, we started to get bored.

“I’ve got an idea,” Jake said, complete with eureka pose and all. “We go say hi to that hotty at the concession stand. Cassy or whatever it was.” He was grinning manically, and I was already shaking my head.

“No.” I quickly interjected. ”We don’t know how long the movie is; what if it finishes and we fall behind?”

He scratched his chin, considering what I said.

“Well, new plan. How about you stay here and watch out for people leaving, and I go talk to her. Come on, man, do me a solid.” I sighed and then nearly laughed out loud when I looked at him and saw he had taken off his little hat and was holding it in his hands with an expectant look. I still thought it was a bad idea, but I just waved him away.

Thirty minutes passed, and I was getting pretty bored just standing in the lobby by myself. I figured if Jake hadn't returned by now, he must have actually seen Casilda there and maybe, even more surprisingly, might have been able to talk to her this whole time without her filing a restraining order against him. I was considering checking on him when I heard a loud clicking sound. Suddenly the door to the nearest theater opened slightly. I heard something loud like music in the background din of the theatre. I almost went to check and see if the door had opened accidentally, until the first few people started filtering out.

I backed up and moved out of the way as they left. I wondered if that clicking was a lock of some kind. Had these people been locked in during the entire movie?

More people exited the theatre, and it seemed like a general air of excitement was present. People seemed happy, strangely happy. There was no discussion about the movie amongst the crowd, just joyful looks and the occasional clapping of hands on each other's backs. Like their team had just won a big game or they were all congratulating each other on the achievement of finishing the first part of the movie. The motions seemed a little odd, but I guessed they must have enjoyed the film.

When the last of the guests had left, I knew it was time to go in. I was annoyed that Jake was not there. But I had to get started, since we had a lot of theatres to clean. I pushed a small garbage bin inside and turned the overhead lights on. I was a little shocked and slightly appalled at the state of the place. Food garbage was on nearly every seat. Popcorn spills in the aisle and drinks turned over and trampled. I remembered the radio on my hip and quickly turned it on and called the other channel I knew Jake was on.

After a bit of static, I finally heard a response.

“New phone, who dis?” I heard an unfamiliar laugh in the background and had a good idea what Jake was still up to. “Very funny,” I responded. “You know if you don't feel like working I will gladly take your paycheck too, now come on! This place is a mess; get down here to theater 1 and get started.” There was a pause on the line before a sarcastic, “Yes sir.” Rang out, followed by static.

I had cleared the first couple rows by the time he came in. I glared at him, and he just shrugged half heartadly and began throwing garbage into the bin.

“Damn, those guys don’t mess around; this is worse than a crime scene,” Jake commented after looking around at the mess. “Yeah, that's why I was trying to get your attention. I don't know how we do all these theaters. But for now, let's get started, and while we are at it, I’m going to change the reel for tomorrow's show; you finish up this one and try not to get distracted again.” I glared at him knowingly, and he just grinned sheepishly while pointing to himself,

“Who me? Never.” I heard him chuckling even as I left him alone to clean the theatre while I ascended the steps to the projector room up top.

I saw a small booklet open on a table indicating how I was supposed to swap out the reel. I carefully disconnected the first day's film and stored it in a container nearby. I found the container with the day two reel nearby. It had a strange little symbol on it that felt vaguely familiar. I couldn't place where I had seen it before, but somehow I knew I had.

I realized it had been nearly three whole minutes of me just blankly staring at the strange symbol. I snapped out of it and opened the lid of the container. I pulled out the new reel and loaded it onto the projector. I nearly stopped to stare at the symbol again until a scream broke me out of the stupor; it was Jake.

I rushed down to meet him and saw him flailing his arms. He saw me coming down and ran to meet me,

“Holy shit man, holy shit. There's a guy here, I think he's dead.” I raced down to the fifth row of seats and sure enough a man lay on the ground, partly covered in debris. I looked down at him and was horrified to see he was staring up at the ceiling, unblinking. Small trails of blood leaked down his eyes and from his mouth. The worst part was that he had a wide smile on his face that was just as unmoving as his eyes or lungs.

“What happened to him? He looks….happy. What the hell?” I exclaimed in horror.

"I don't know, man, but we have to tell Hasturing." Jake said.

"Should one of us…stay with him, while the other gets help?” I asked. Jake shook his head quickly,

“Nah man, screw that, let's get the fuck out of here and tell Hasturing to call the cops.” I agreed with him, and we both bolted out to find our boss.

When we got to the lobby, we realized that we didn't know where Hasturing was. He was not in the small backroom where we had met with him earlier. Jake suggested we go ask Cassie, but she was not there either. The place was oddly empty. Instead, we saw an unfamiliar sight: it was a short, pale man in an ill-fitting yellow vest emerging from the box office. That must have been Sam. We quickly approached, and he gasped out loud as we startled him,

“Oh my! What's the problem?” He cried out.

“We need to find Mr. Hasturing now. There has been an… incident in a theater.” I said as calmly as I could. Jake quickly spoke up, a bit less delicately,

“Yeah, we got a body in there. Someone straight up died.” Sam seemed startled and quickly went into the box office and brought out a phone that seemed to be a landline. He called a number and, after waiting a bit, started speaking with Mr. Hasturing. After finishing, he hung up and walked back over to us,

“Mr. Hasturing will be here shortly. Please, show me the body to confirm. We quickly moved back to theater 1 and climbed up to the fifth row of seats to show Sam. But to our shock and confusion, the body was gone. All that remained were small outlines of the blood and congealed soda pop that had formed around the location where it had been.

“What the hell!?” Jake exclaimed, and I was equally confused.

“It was right there!”

“What was right there?” A calm voice inquired as Mr. Hasturing stepped into the theatre.

“A body, we found a corpse right there; you can still see the outline.” I pointed to the faint trace.

“Yeah, it was, and it looked fucked up; it was smiling, and it had blood coming out of the eyes. They had to be dead; they couldn't have just walked away.” Jake added in terrified exasperation.

“Now, now, gentleman. I must insist that we calm down. I know you have seen something frightening, but I will say, some viewers of our masterpiece can experience trance-like levels of euphoria that might have led to an incident where it seemed like they stopped cellular respiration, but I assure you, everyone is fine. They probably left after you went to come get me. We are the only ones here after all, like you said; a corpse couldn't just get up and walk out, could it?”

Hasturing looked at Jake like a father might regard a child who was lying to get out of going to school.

“But…” Jake started, but Hasturing held up a hand and cut him off.

“I assure you everything is fine; however, it will not be if we leave the other theaters a mess. Now, if you two want to keep your positions here, I would advise you to resume your responsibilities. That is, if you would like to be paid.” He left us in the theatre, and Sam followed behind the boss, giving one last glance at us before departing as well.

Jake and I both looked at each other and knew something here was wrong, but we didn't know just how wrong it was yet.

The rest of the night was a paranoid haze. We managed to finish the other theaters and wrap things up at around 2:00 am. We discussed the body a few more times, but eventually we had to come to terms with the fact that we would either have to leave and forfeit our pay, or keep going and try to ignore the disturbing encounter.

Since this was not our first time seeing strange things, we decided to give the second day a shot and resolved to return tomorrow. 

We were not prepared for how much worse it was about to get.
