When Professor \[REDACTED\] invited me back to his office after class, I was fairly certain it was about my performance. Latin wasn't one of my strong suits despite he and I getting along very well. We shared a love for ancient civilizations, and spent many days after class just chatting about lore and mythology. You would never have guessed that knowing I was failing Anthropology 101. 

It was a cold winter morning when he asked me to stick around after class. I could tell by the tone in his voice that it wasn't to just chat about ancient Mesopotamia. His office was messier than usual. Notebooks and loose pieces of paper littered his desk, not to mention the crumpled up pieces surrounding the trash can. I did notice that he seemed distracted as of late as if he was working on a side project. 

"Please excuse the mess," He said as he tried straightening up his desk. "Go ahead and have a seat."

As he cleared up a bunch of the loose paper, trying to create a neat pile by tapping it on the desk, I noticed an old book that was previously covered up. I leaned forward to get a closer look. There was writing on the cover, it was in Latin. I couldn’t make much out, seeing as how I'm doing poorly in Latin 101. When I looked up, it was obvious the Professor could tell my interest was piqued as he had a huge grin on his face. 

"I have it on loan from the museum, they want me to transcribe it for them. Go ahead, take a look."

I was concerned about its fragility due to its age, so rather than picking it up, I stood and stepped closer to the desk. I opened the cover to see the pages were brittle, but could be turned with a gentle touch. I couldn't believe he trusted me enough to let me anywhere near this, let alone touching it, it was an artifact. 

"The book itself may be extremely old," He continued,  "but the contents are ancient. They believe that it was transcribed from scrolls, that may have been transcribed from tablets that could go back thousands of years."

I flipped through a couple more pages. I only recognized a handful of words, and could translate even fewer than that. I then closed the book as gently as I could. 

"That's pretty cool." I say as I sit back into the chair. "So, am I here to talk about my performance?"

He sat down in his chair and just gazed upon my face. It was as if he was trying to study my expressions, attempting to figure out what thoughts were going through my head.

"Steve?" I asked, becoming agitated by his silence. 

"You're doing fine, I'm sure we'll get it all worked out. No, I'm quite excited about this project I'm working on, and while I did sign an NDA, it's just too much to not share with someone who would appreciate the findings. Besides, I've read enough contracts in my day, as long as none of this leaves my office, I haven't technically violated the contract."

He wasn't lying, he was visibly excited, almost manic. For some reason that made me nervous. However, that book did intrigue me. I felt drawn to it. I got the kind of butterflies in my stomach you feel while preparing to get on stage in front of hundreds of people. I looked back at him the way he gazed upon me. I squinted my eyes. 

"Once I leave this office, it will be as if I never entered at all." I said right before a grin spread across my face. 

He was infected by that grin, and mimicked my expression. "Fantastic! Here's some of my transcriptions, let me know what you make of it" 

He handed me one of the notebooks. I flipped to the first page and began reading. While it was written in English, it was still incomprehensible. There's an old phrase that comes to mind 'This reads like stereo instructions.' It comes off as a bunch of meaningless jargon. I'd even go so far as to calling it esoteric. 

There were bits of intelligible expressions that would come through every now and then, but every time they did, I felt an extreme anxiety wash over me. It's difficult to describe, but the closest thing I could compare it to is if you were being told that the sun was about to explode wiping out all life on earth. 

I began to feel queasy. The lines on the paper would blur. It was as if my body was having a negative reaction to the writing. It was like a virus that my immune system recognized immediately and outright rejected. I tossed the notebook onto the desk and laid my head back rubbing my eyes as a headache began to set in. I lifted my hand up over my forehead, my thumb and index finger resting on my temples. Palm and three remaining fingers shielding my eyes from the oppressive overhead light as I opened them to see the Professor staring at me. His eyes were begging for a reaction, as if to ask 'and?'

"It's gibberish" I say, shaking my head, truly believing my own words at that moment. 

He continued to stare at me for a long moment. 

"Huh." He lied back in his chair. A look of disappointment spread across his face. "So, nothing?"

A thought darted into my mind and vanished immediately. I felt an urge to chase that thought, but I felt a deep sorrow arise from the thought of just entertaining the idea. 

"Well, there was, um, well no, uh. Fuck!" I felt like I was trying to chase my own train of thought. For a moment I could swear I heard voices. I don't mean Steve's, but other voices, including my own, but not coming from me. 

Waves of anxiety washed over me as I felt my grip on reality weaken. It was almost as if my brain actively fought against recalling the bits of only cohesive information that I was able to gleam from that damn writing. My stomach began twisting, I felt I was going to be sick. He must have saw it on my face. 

"Hey! Snap out of it!" He yelled as he leaned forward in his chair. 

A battle was being waged in my head. I felt the strongest urge to recall those forgotten words while another part of me fought hard to keep those very words suppressed. Steve came over and knelt down in front of me. 

"Hey, let's say you and I go grab a beer? Or shots, you seem like the type of guy who likes tequila."

I turned my head towards his direction, but looked straight through him.   

"Why don't we call this an extra credit session? I think we can raise you a whole letter grade."

He snapped in my face. He then clapped as loud as he could. The world came rushing back. I felt immense dread, it hardly fell even after the offer of alcohol and grade change. My eyes focused. I was now staring at the wall to Steve's right, rather than through it.

"yeah, I think I'll take you up on that offer" my voice monotone.

"Great! Let's get you that beer!"

"Sounds good"

My voice was still monotone, but I could feel the color begin to fill up the world again. I could also feel a stinging sensation on my right cheek. 

I woke up the next morning in my dorm hung over as shit. I could barely remember anything from the previous day. Everything slowly came back to me over the next week. Everything except for how I got home from the bar, and whatever it was that I read in that notebook.  I felt a slight urge to try and remember, but every time I did, I felt panic and would immediately stop. 

I didn't even realize at the time how inappropriate it was for a college professor to be buying a student alcohol, but it was clear to me later how guilty he felt for putting me through, whatever that was. 

During the next class, Steve was more manic than I had ever seen. He kept mentioning this ancient civilization I had never heard of. He went on about their religion and the gods they worshipped. For a second I thought I was in my Anthropology class. These gods were not in fact gods, but beings that existed in the fourth dimension. How would such an ancient civilization know anything about the fourth dimension? And more importantly, What did any of this have to do with Latin?

During the next course he delves even deeper into the intricacies of their religion. This time I felt like I was in a philosophy class. It was so bizarre. Hell there was even math all over the white board. This was supposed to be a Latin class. I even heard murmurs from the math majors that what was written all over the white boards was the mathematical equivalent of gibberish. I was starting to worry that Steve was losing his mind. 

I was seriously considering dropping the class, and maybe even trying to look into getting Steve professional help. However he was back to teaching Latin the next day. There was something off though. It almost seemed as if he was frightened. Paranoid maybe, but almost just straight terrified of something. He acted almost as if he was trying to hide the fact that he was scared. Not just from us, but from himself, like he was trying to ignore it. He was sweating a lot for a winter morning, and he would visibly shake at times. I don't think it was a shiver caused by the cold though. I was glad he was back to teaching Latin, but my worry had not subsided at all. 

A few days later in the next class, his eyes were red and watery. He seemed lethargic, almost sorrowful. He reminded me of Ben Stein the way he would drone on about the subject he was supposed to be teaching. At times he would stop to stare out the windows, rest his head on the wall, or stare at his own hands. Something was very wrong, and I needed to talk to him. 

Once the class was dismissed I tried to make my way up to the front, but I had to push my way through the mass of students trying to leave through the back. Once I made it through, Steve was gone. I went over to his office and stood in front of the door for a moment trying to gather my nerves. I could swear I could hear a soft weeping on the other side of the door. I finally reached up and knocked. He didn't answer, so I knocked again, still with no answer. I could still hear the sound of very quiet sobbing. I tried the handle but it was locked. 

"Steve?" I said with a raised voice.

Another faculty member walked by. 

"Professor \[REDACTED\]?" I said even louder. 

I heard a sniffle, and a soft whimper. I sighed, and resigned to talking to him later. 

The next day he didn't talk at all. He just sat at the front of the class. Hundred yard stare out the Window. I decided I needed to get up to the front before the class was dismissed, but right when I stood up the rest of the class took that as their cue to leave. I pushed my way through and was surprised to see that he hadn't moved from that spot. I walked up to him and spoke. 

"Hey, you okay?"

He didn't respond. He didn't even acknowledge my existence. 

"Steve?"

Nothing, just a blank stare on his face. I walked up and snapped my fingers, clapped my hands right in his face. He didn't even flinch. I then put my index finger under his nose. I felt his breath, and let out a sigh of relief. 

"Bro, talk to me, what's going on."

Still absolutely no reaction. I didn't know what to do. I considered going to get help, so I turned around. I stood there for a moment trying to think of who to go to when he finally spoke. 

"It's all a sham"

"What?" I turned around to face him. He continued staring out the window. 

"An illusion"

"What, what's an illusion?"

"Existence"

The hairs stood up on the back of my neck. 

"What do you mean?"

"We don't exist, nothing does, it's just all an illusion"

I didn't know what to say. Part of me thought he finally lost it, he was gone, he needed real psychiatric care. However, another part of me knew he was right. Bits and pieces of the small amount I read from those pages seeped back into my brain. 

"E..ev..even if that were true, what does it matter? As long as I can perceive existence, then it doesn't matter. As long as I can perceive happiness, I don't care if it's an illusion, it's real to me."

"It's too late."

"What the fuck does that mean?"

"Once you've drunk from the forbidden fruit, they will find you, and when they do, you will only perceive infinite suffering. "

"Who?"

He started humming a tune that gave me the creeps. It was slightly off key, and had an ominous vibe. I'm not a musician, so I don't know the exact notes, but I'll try my best to transcribe what it sounds like. If it doesn't make sense, I'm sorry, maybe I can upload a sound clip of me humming it later or something.
