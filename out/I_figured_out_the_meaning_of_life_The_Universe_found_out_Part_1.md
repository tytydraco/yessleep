The Human–Universe Relationship:

1. The meaning of life is to, ‘move’. Expunge potential energy. Avoid heat-death.
2. If something could happen and proceeds to transpire, there is a timeline beneficial to the universe, available to you. 
3. Moments of faith or divinity, glimpses of looking behind the veil, and instances of synchronicity can be indications of both a good and bad timeline. 
4. The universe feeds off you in both good and bad timelines. Survive 

The sound of rolling thunder broke my attention. I hadn’t realized I was once again lost in thought. One hand on the cold wall, the other holding an index card of rules. My head slowly craned up, and I proceeded to pull my eyes away from the piece of laminated paper. 

Windows in the periphery, various lights and noises around me, and a budding orange cityscape blurred like fire in the window behind the index card in my hand.

The sun was going down and in these hands, again, were a set of guidelines. A list of rules that meant everything to everyone. Well. Well, they mean everything to me. 

Crude at first. Refined by trial and error. Shortened, simplified, and circular. Rational. Easy to understand. Reasonable instructions. With purpose. Rules, written with intent. For survival. For clarity against the dark corners of the mind. Clarity through life’s trials. A permanent reminder, inked into routine plain paper.

An index card.

I thrive in the dark, I swear I do. 

Like a lightbulb still on holding strong after hours in a cold dark department store. Tonight felt different though. 

But – why?

I read the card over again, carefully. As if it were the first time I wrote it in college almost a decade ago. 

Sliding the card into my back pocket where it lived, I looked outside the windows towards the night. Hazy and dark, the night embodied and stoned into a visualized patience. Waiting over the dimming glow of the evening sun. The growing dark in the distance felt like a presence looking back at me. Stars like eyes in the sky. 

Dark and almost malicious. Pitch black, Bible black. A tone unheard. Studied and written about. Feared and dreamt about for hundreds of years. Ocular poetry.

The window frame was metal and robust, built to last. The metal had a slight shade of blue that was easily visible against the contrast of the beige paint covering the dressing room walls. 

A flash of light seemed to overpower the dying presence of the sun. A storm was building in the distance off the horizon. 

I know. Having a list of rules like that must make me look insane. I’d agree. It is insane. But reality is insane. What isn’t insane?

Name something that isn’t. Society is completely bonkers. Dreams can feel real. Money can make a man murder. Perception is reality. Besides, if you ever see or experience what I have, this wouldn’t even be a question.

Everything in life is seasoned with hints of madness. 

What – did you think the shadows under the bed are immune to following the rules?

My perception of reality isn’t as insane as you might think. That’s actually why I’m writing this down. 

Trying to make sense of it all, in my own way. On paper, as a story. 

To keep track of the chaos by merely being aware of it and staying observant. Detailing it for others. A pathetic thought. But that's all I can do for now. 

Maybe one day, someone will understand what I’m trying to say. Until then, well.. 

Madison Square Garden was absolutely beautiful. New York City. I felt unreal. It’s my night, again. Can you believe I’m performing here? I am thankful. 

I am.  
   
Watching that lame Godlizza movie as a kid, I would be very surprised to learn one day my band would be performing where that abomination of a movie’s creature laid its sequel-bait egg army.

Lightning bounced between the clouds in the distance.

Flashes could be seen out of the windows from anybody inside the dressing room, paying attention or not. Occasional heads popped up from phones and vanity mirrors to check that they weren’t losing their minds.

Actually seeing this abnormal natural light show during a beautiful normal sunset.

“Am I really doing this again?” 

The words came out against my will, a whisper against the background noise. Like waves of a thought too loud to be satisfied by just remaining inside this pathetic meat sack for much longer.

As if holding it in would cause more damage to the body than the chance anyone happening to catch it would. Nobody heard. 

Nobody ever heard me when I needed them these days. It’s probably a product of fame and success.

I wonder if I’m on the right path, doing the right things at the right time. Maybe I don’t need anyone to hear me? 

Yeah. There have been times I’ve needed to be heard. But I’ve always figured it out. Even the weird shit.   

Another few branches of lightning bounced between the clouds off in the distance.

My hand was cold and almost numb against the wall from supporting my weight for too long. I adjusted my lean and positioned myself to something more comfortable, avoiding stretching or damaging my clothes.

I don’t wear anything fancy during my shows. A black band shirt. Black jeans. Hair in the same man-bun my Dad disapproves of. 

Turns out, you can buy your fathers approval though. He doesn’t bring it up anymore. 

More lightning. This time lighting up a kingdom in the sky. Clouds bubbled and rolled over each other, erupting far into the atmosphere.

The empire of clouds made the towering skyline feel much more like a human play-thing compared to a revolutionary architectural achievement. Shame, the skyline looked great on a good night

So much for the age-of-man, I thought. This storm was going to be rough.

The night was settling further into a silky purple colored blanket, seemingly comforting the skyline as it approached. A foggy mist blotched the dimming night sky, and as if on queue, the street lights came on block by block. Followed quickly by the inside lights of the late night scene.

Pushing my weight into the wall I leaned further into the windowsill, allowing myself to get a better look at the sky above the arena. A full moon. Bright stars, decorating its luminescent glow, shone in the clear sky above the arena yet to be downed out by the oncoming clouds. 

I sighed, remembering more than I should. You know how it goes, right? 

Time for the show. 

“The lord giveth, the lord taketh away.” I said whipping around, loud enough for anyone close to hear. 

“Mom used to say that.” A voice answered from my side. Alan, my brother and manager.

Some teacher as a kid told me to keep ‘it’ in the family. If you can. ‘It’ being, success. I thought it was a great idea. It has had its flaws and it has been a constant learning experience but it works for us. Well, it works for me. I swear I know what he does. 

I looked at him with a concerned face. He wore a black band shirt, too. Snug under a crisp navy blue denim jacket that he picked up sometime this morning. Black jeans. A pricey wrist watch strapped firmly in place, and phone in hand. A band lanyard draped around his neck, decorated with various pins.

“Used to? Did she pass on to the great beyond? Brother – ugh, don’t throw around the evil-eye like that.” Sharing the wisdom with an exaggerated roll in my chest. Catching my brother in the eyes, turning away from the oppressive night sky and the cold iron of the window frame.

He looked back with a warm smile. He seemed to be thinking about how to phrase something. I never was the best at reading my brother’s face. He slapped his knee as if to physically force his face away from the phone screen, glued to it again.  

“And since when are you a man of the Lord?” He said with a bit of a smirk, looking up then back down. A smirk that hinted at previous conversations, a life lived in tandem, a friendship irreplaceable. 

When did he get that new phone? And where’s mine? This asshole. 

“Since you found your wife? There is a God – clearly.” Slowly falling to my knees. Like a man stricken in Walmart, who just received terribly tragic news over the phone.

“You made me.. Believe.” I held the pose.

I always hold the pose.

Lightning struck the ground somewhere closer, and a rumble engulfed the arena. Enough natural grumbling to feel it over the reverberations already caused by the music playing on stage. Alan reached over to grab and pull me off the floor. 

“Shut your ass up and go back to your doom and gloom normal-self for me? Don’t be so deep. And don’t bring my wife into this either.” He said with a smile, trying to lift me up, struggling through laughter. 

My head hung low hiding a smile, while my body made itself heavier. I said, 

“I’m not bringing your wife anywhere. She works for us. There’s a good chance she’s about to walk through that door, right now.” I point behind me without looking back. Still on my knees, head down, face now covered with fake sorrow.  

A door flew open and a voice pierced through the white noise of diligent backstage technicians, and the costume and make-up staff. Veronica, our stage manager. 

“Ace – “ Her voice pierced the white noise of the room, just loud enough to be heard from where we were standing. 

The true test was reaching us over the musical stylings and jovial eruptions of our beloved Fred, though. 

About eight months back at some random festival we were doing, I couldn't keep up with which if asked, there was an older gentleman outside the venue. Strumming happily with an old rustic acoustic guitar. He couldn’t afford a ticket and, hell, I’m not even sure he wanted to go in – but he didn’t need to, ya know?. He shouldn’t have needed a ticket either. 

He was the show. 

Right outside of this venue was a mixture of blues and bluegrass, jazz, and rock and roll. I felt inspired. Someone grabbed a chair from the bus. No one joked about it. They all knew where my head was. My people are my people for a reason – good people.  

I was in awe. 

He needed a job and I needed to, deep down, give back to the community. Any community. To society. I was too busy those days. Success was coming in too fast, if you can believe it. Then we found Fred.

We snatched him up like children saving a rabbit. And, hired him after listening to his unique style and unparalleled stories for 40 minutes. Arriving late to our set. But with a cheerfully surprised new staff member. The crowd didn’t mind us being late at all, it was great.  

Alan likes to say we’re the biggest band since the Beatles. I don’t even like the Beatles. And ‘band’ is just the colloquially accepted term. I am a ‘creative ass’ singer and song writer, with a beatmaker. As online commenters like to mention. I’ll own that. 

We used to hire different musicians for almost every show. College students, mostly. Almost as good as the professionals. Of course, I vet them. Veronica finds them. We make it work. It’s getting harder and harder to make it work for cheap now we are so big. Alan has been floating the idea of a permanent band staff. 

I definitely like the idea. About time. Then we might be a real, actual band-band. 

Fred watches me perform from backstage. I’ve found he understands my lyrics more than most people. Catching me at a glance after verses and between songs. Rearranging his face accordingly whenever I sing in his direction. The way musicians communicate. 

He’s been there for us ever since. Arriving late to his own engagements more times than I can count. And all to make sure we were ready for our show and feeling good. Seeing if we were feeling the vibes. A good solid person, through and through. 

He refuses to tell us his age. Often saying, “no way do I want y'all to think of me as some old man.” Nobody has ever used his age against him. Well. Hell. We needed a band-dad. 

I’m quite fond of him, so I did a background check. He’s sixty-two. I haven’t told him yet. For tax purposes, my personal shaman and creative advisor. We’ve done shrooms a few times now so I think it’s official. 

Top three nicest people I’ve ever met. It makes me smile thinking about how important he is now. Not just some dude outside a venue strumming his faves. Not just to me, to the world. Even if it is through me. He keeps staff morale high. He keeps us all laughing.

That’s important on the road. Fred is proof I'm on a correct timeline. I just know it.

I looked over, around Alan, and found him in a corner with his guitar resting against him. His phone in his hand.

Odd.

He never uses his phone. He wore a black band hoodie with baggy white sweatpants. Headphones leading from one ear down to his phone. His mouth was moving and his head was angled down. He was talking to somebody. 

I turned around to face the door where the voice was resonating from. Standing up, I look over to Veronica. It’s always easy to track where her voice was coming from, even with everyone warming up and getting ready around us.

Dang. I really needed to stretch. I made a mental note to do that before going on stage.

I reach out and grab a nearby bottle of water, cracking it open and taking a quick couple sips, before setting it back down on the foldable table. 

Alan backed up, turned around, and threw on his real smile for the first time all evening. 

“Ace – On in twenty minutes.” A short woman in big round black glasses and a pink band shirt. Black jeans. Veronica. Our stage manager and Alan’s wife. Damn, she was loud. Surprises me every time. 

Fluorescent lights buzzed above. A few fans lazily spun above us, spreading the various smells of show business around the room. Beige walls. Ugly. And a white popcorn stucco acted as the skin covering the ceiling walls. Also ugly. 

I turned and gave a quick smile at my brother. He was a lucky guy. I don’t think he even saw me look at him. I spoke up,

“Twenty minutes? Veronica. Tell me at five, please.” I yelled over the noise. 

I’m not allowed to make the joke about being born ready anymore. Not after passing out from heat exhaustion during a festival in Orlando. I took a few quick steps towards her, Alan followed.

“Hey, grab me a quick slice of pizza from the other room? I just need a bite. And a coke? Please. How are you by the way? I haven’t seen you in almost – ” pantomiming an exaggerated look down to an imaginary watch. 

“Forty minutes. We almost called the police.” I rushed to her side. Going for her hand and flashing puppy eyes. 

She giggled, Alan laughed. They really are a great couple.

They knew each other and were dating for a few years before any of this even happened. Before my eyes, two people become one. And with some great tectonic shift these two people became the rocks I stood upon. I’m a very thankful brother-in-law.

She’s great at her job, too. 

Lightning rattled the building again. Fred stopped looking down and threw a glance to the windows before quickly returning to his conversation. Must be important, I thought. 

A handful of the dancers stopped chatting and getting ready to turn and look towards the windows. Alan broke the silence, face in his phone again.

“Hey look here. The world’s biggest Coke-a-Cola fan ended up 2026’s largest company sponsor. Great job Ace.” Patting me on the back, looking over with his usual grin that followed noteworthy yet funny milestones. He gestured towards his phone, urging me to look. 

I knew what it said without having to read it. Having a brother for an agent and manager has its benefits. I turned to look back out the windows.

“Hell yeah brother.” I said with my eyes on the now purple moonlit sky. Oppressive clouds now closer than before. 

I have a solid idea what he does, I promise.

He continued, seeming a bit confused at my reaction, “aren’t you happy?” Veronica stood by his side. Smiling ear to ear. 

A laugh rattled out of me in the form of a chuckle. Of course I was. But, what do I say?

There’s a lot of things I want to change about this life. About the whole world. That laugh came from a different place than they probably imagined. It was the laugh of a man who wanted to give back but kept receiving. With no clue what the hell to do about it. 

I used the laugh anyhow, pivoting to what they wanted to hear. I turned around with my eyes closed and a smile plastered all over my face.

“Of course, dude.” I laughed, “does the band know yet?” I say reflexively, my mind still elsewhere. Opening my eyes in time to catch his stare briefly. 

He didn’t look worried. Good. I’m used to fooling my brother. But, Veronica? She’s a little tougher. Keen as a fox. 

Alan looked back down at his phone, pressed a few buttons, and typed out a quick sentence. Then, pressed down on what I assumed to be the send button. Shoving the phone in my face.

“Lazer knows. Fred is about to know. The dancers and technicians will know.” He smiled, reaching for Veronica’s hand. Another win for the books. 

“Great job man.” He threw me a light punch to my chest and continued, “you earned this.” He looked at Veronica. She spoke up,

“Imagine the free soda, Ace.” She said with a wink. She’s got me there. Between my attention disorder, migraines, depression, and a secret list of super-important universal rules? Soda was an oasis. One of my last few secret weapons. I’m a thriving popstar, if asked.

Lightning struck a skyscraper not too far from us, rattling the stadium down to the trash left by workers in the depths of the foundation. The rattle seemed to grip the arena by the bones. A couple car alarms began screaming outside.

For a second, the flash lit up the room, and I saw something to my right. My attention shifted.   
   
Something began to manifest out of the corner of my eye that wasn’t there before, so I turned to the right fully. I saw something again, obscured by darkness. 

Another flash of lighting baked the sky with light, and I saw it. 

For a moment, I froze in terror. All the blood rushed to my head and legs, immediately in fight-or-fight. In the corner of the dressing room behind the table of stacked merch boxes. Partially covered by a rack of clothes, concealed behind various fabrics, she stood still as a stone. 

It was an ethereal older woman. A specter, drenched in hollow moonlight. An older white gown resting on her body. Without moving my head too quickly, I turn a little slightly more in her direction. 

Nothing? I kept staring at the corner of the room. 

Lightning struck again. Unnatural light filled the room once again. A persistent flash from subsequent busts of lightning illuminated the room for several moments. Others looked up and out the windows, distracted by the light show. 

This time I noticed she was looking right into my eyes, not just looking in my direction. She was looking at me. Directly into my soul. She remained stone still. Impossibly frozen in space. She looked like a painting constructed with the explicit intrusions of causing pain and despair on the viewer. 

She looked at me smiling. A malicious yet confident grin. She looked at me as if I were meant to know why in the world this was happening. Know why she was smiling at all. Like I was meant to have some grasp on why she was here. Or how I summoned her to this damn arena. 

My legs were beginning to shake. 

A flawed sense of stability attempted to ground me amongst the growing panic. I calmly resumed breathing again the best I could. I hadn’t even noticed I had stopped. 

Alan and Victoria were looking out the windows at the storm, ignorant to me for the moment. Good. This is my problem. This is my life. 

Her eyes were dark, void stricken and bottomless. She looked opposing and expectant. Hungry. She grinned something awful. Powder caked her face, covering her gray cracked skin like varnish over old chipped wood.

She’s NEVER looked this way. This was new. A twisted new experience. I went for my index card and stopped. Resting my hand against the seams of my back pocket.

Focus.

I have seen her grin like a normal person in broad daylight. I’ve seen her laugh, cry, smirk, and dance. Laugh giddy like a child. She has walked behind people, people who felt not a single thing. She has waved at me from behind crowds during signing events, wearing a band shirt. 

Sightings are few and far between throughout my life. Definitely ramping up with success, that’s for sure. Enough times to keep a mental note of her appearances. Twelve times. She has allowed herself to be seen twelve times. 

This would make it thirteen. 

Dammit!

No, not tonight. The number I consider unlucky? Come on, man. Why tonight? Is it because of the venue? I had no idea. There’s always a bigger show. More people to perform to. 

Like a truck driver making an important call at the last moment, a decision with thousands of pounds of weight behind it, I left the rules in my pocket. 

If I grabbed the card, if I went for the rules, then I’d be playing the game willingly. Right? I was fairly sure. I didn’t want to play any damn game tonight. 

The universe can starve. I’m not engaging with it. I’m not engaging with anything supernatural tonight. Please. Just one night, go hungry. Release your grips from the tender meat of man. Get your claws out of me. Just one night. Tonight means so much to us. 

It felt as if a blood stained mouth laughed at me from behind the veil. Though I couldn’t hear it, I could feel the warmth of its breath against my skin in the form of fear and anxiety. Crawling all over me. Fouling me, from my feet up to the nape of my neck. The woman in white adjusted her gaze and cocked her head, never releasing her grip on my eyes. 

The Old Hag was back. Using the moonlight as a guide, I stared straight into her being. Angry. I found her dark center by force of will. Her expression faltered. Mildly curious, but resistant, willing to stare me down. Like a certified gilded representative of darkness. 

I reached down deep into her center with the determination of my glare. You’d know it if you ever felt it. You don’t forget that feeling. I reached into her essence, and I squeezed. I squeezed hard. For the thirteenth time. Be gone. Weird bit–

She moved several rapid steps towards me. Navigating the rack of clothes and table of merch with inhuman speed. With ghastly ease, with soiled grace. Like she was puppeted from up above, each step a still frame of a movie. I closed my eyes out of shock. My heart beat racing out of control. Very slowly, I opened my eyes.

She was mere steps from me. Looking right at me. 

She just smiled. A strange smile. Unknown to me at the time. The rest of the world faded away from my attention. 

Just me and her. 

What did I do? What should I do?

More lightning, and with the faint amount of light awarded to me I could tell she was now gone. While trying to regain control of my now blinking and watering eyes, I tried to keep up with Alan and Veronica’s conversation. My thoughts racing. They were in their own little world looking at Alan’s phone. Good – 

She’s an omen. She’s always been an omen. For as long as I can remember. Childhood nightmares? She’s the one who chased me. She was the monster under the bed. Horrifying and awful, but the longest friend I’ve ever had.

Once I saw her in a supermarket as a little kid. Mom and I walked past her like another guest in the store. A few days later? Lilo, my pet hamster, was dead. Recent experiences with her haven’t meant – death. But, that association stuck with me. It helped understand her. Dang it. 

Gotta keep it together. 

Veronica and Alan were talking about something in a whisper, sitting side by side resting on a free table. A new expression on their faces.  Whatever the subject, it definitely had their attention. Good, I thought. They didn’t notice anything. Let’s keep it that way.  

Not this again. I can’t think about her now. We’re at fucking Madison Square Garden. 

The garden. The beginning to so many symbolically and a right of passage for any mega star artist. Just another fucking show for me, right? Just another show, I told myself. 

I don’t believe in gods or devils, nor burning bushes or blasphemy. I believe in the rules. 

The rules clearly outline that if it were absolutely necessary for life to continue? The bush COULD have burned. It probably did light on fire. But dammit if there is a god? They have no friend in me. I briefly believed in god once. It felt like drowning. 

The architecture of thousands of years of faith still boils in my DNA and I often am stricken with pain when trying to accept my own understanding of totality. That of a godless-uncaring universe. That there is just us, a hungry universe, and trillions of ways to feed it. Trillions of possibilities. Billions of different meal options. 

At least we have a relationship with the universe. It takes two to tango, after all. 

The ways IT benefits and WE don’t are endless. You can’t compare it to anything, besides atoms. And even then? What are we but children playing in nettled vines? Does it hope we find solace amongst the flowers and weeds? Pathetic. 

I decided a long time ago to follow the rules to protect myself. Nobody taught me shit. Not about this stuff. Nothing prepared me for what came after understanding. Nothing prepared me for what was to come with knowledge. Some days I’d take it all back. Forget the truth and live in blissful ignorance. Have restful sleep again. See the world differently. Like a horse wearing blinders. 

But, I can’t. I still have my hairline though, don’t worry.

Besides, the show must go on.  

But whatever is happening to me? It isn’t based on some established model of the ‘Old Hag’ archetype. Not anymore. This time she was different. Like she has a mind of her own. I had the faintest idea she wanted to tell me something. Impossible. I shrugged off the notion. 

They NEVER have a mind of their own, the things I see. They always act like the way you imagine them to. The worst possible way. The fear? It’s important to them.

Rule one. Rule three. 

At the dawn of time, when all that existed was the potential to become, things evolved to survive. They clawed their way through the evolutionary ladder. And now act just as their oppressor, feeding off others, tormenting the biologically living. 

Being used to something doesn’t mean you have to understand it completely. Kind of like how the world works. You don’t understand all religions, commerce systems, and governments. But, you are used to them. Used to them enough to live with them – but you don’t understand them. 

These experiences? Everything out there in the cosmos that interacts with us is merely a being too evolved for us to understand. Every myth, fairytale, ghoul, ghost, and god.

An angel in the outfield that moves you enough to make the right choice and call your old buddy when they need you? A demon in the darkness that scares you into submission, bullying you enough to want to move states for University? It doesn’t matter. It’s the universe feeding, extracting your potential.  

These things alter our timelines. They extract potential energy from the stream. But I’m no expert. Well, to you maybe. 

Everything I have seen has been over-analyzed. I have the rules. I understand enough for things to start showing themselves to me. 

What kind of statement is that?

You might think I’m dealing with all of this quite well. I’m not. Rest easy. I put on my mask of acceptance like a good dog. Gotta keep up appearances for the band. For my brother. For the people. 

Atoms Nettled forever – after all. 

Realizing deep thought had taken over, I finally shook it off. I threw on a smile to fool my brother and walked over to them. They were both standing there, looking at me, waiting for a response. Their attention now back from the storm and their previous conversation.   

“Bro, you know about my migraines. You know how I was as a kid. You know what meal I have specifically requested after waking up from a potentially coma. Of course I am happ – ” he cut me off. I almost forgot we just landed a killer sponsor. 

His eyes moved away from his phone as he looked at me and smiled, “cheese burger and a coke. I remember.” He laughed some more and turned to remind Veronica about my request for a snack. She smiled, congratulated me again, and she walked away for a slice of pizza. 

Sometimes I think about what would happen if I told him everything I’ve seen. Jeez. Maybe, one day.

“How the hell did we get here man?” Hands in my pockets, I say turning back around towards the windows. Noticing we were alone for the moment. 

It was almost fully dark outside now. Black clouds towered so high I almost couldn’t find the moon this time. It was a faint white glow behind a towering wall so opposing I almost took a photo to send to Mom. 

Alan responded,  

“You got all artsy and shit. Then you used your brain. I don’t know, a little luck? Maybe with my help?” He shrugged with a soft and professional smile, practiced to perfection.

“No, I know. I mean, how did we get – here? The potential we have. It’s just so much – ” I looked out the window and hesitantly trailed away. This conversation normally goes one of two ways. 

“Ace – “ He stopped. Looking up from his phone again. That damn phone.

I didn’t look over.  

“Daniel.”

Uh oh. My government name, I’m in trouble. Begrudgingly, I turned around to look at him. He was sitting back down now, eyes fixed on me.  

“You’re on in like, seventeen minutes.” he sighed. “You know this conversation is too much for me. I know a little bit about what’s on your mind. Your theories, your rules. The hallucinations from your condition. I know it’s a lot.” 

Being put in your place while comforted by your younger brother never feels good. 

My hallucinations, my theories. There it was, avoidance. Normally he just reminds me it’s too much for him. Or reminds me I could be hallucinating due to stress, or maybe my depression condition. He’s seen the rules and we talked about it once. One late night, visiting the folks for the holidays. Just us. We were up late still watching the television, everyone else was sleeping. Family Guy marathon. 

He told me he can see why I’m depressed with rules like these.

Yeah, I see his point there. He also told me that, if it’s all true, he doesn’t want to think about it. A valid response. 

He continued with his persistent smile and comforting tone. 

“I love you. But not now. I can’t keep up with it. We’re working, bro.” He squeezed my shoulder. 

“Do you have the card in your pocket?” He gestured down, relaxing his demeanor into something more recognizable. Having depression so bad, you hallucinate, really makes it hard to share any of this. Even with your own brother.

“You got us this far.” He finally conceded, sat down next to me, and put the phone in his pocket. He briefly looked around the room. Partly checking everything. Partly looking out for Veronica. 

Dancers in the corner, stretching together. Veronica was on her phone, probably talking to another curious third party. Can of soda under her arm and a slice of New York’s finest export on a white paper plate. Alan saw and turned towards Veronica, gesturing at her hands.

“We can talk later. Tonight is big. Huge even. Nothing this big has happened yet –” I cut him off. 

“Instances of happening – ” I stop and quickly recompose. “Ok, fine.” I also conceded, standing tall.

Rule two, I thought. It was my night after all. 

He heard me, closed his eyes for a moment, then looked at me like two bricks rubbing together. Concern began to roll across his face. I interjected quickly, 

“Look – Alan. Just let me vent. Okay?” I laugh, awkwardly but warm, throwing a hand on his shoulder back at him. “It is a big night.” I agreed. It was a big night.

He got up and walked towards his wife – for my snack. 

I looked away, back towards the windows and the storm rolling in. Raindrops were already fighting the glass. Gaining ground. 

Water, water. Everywhere. 

Thunder rolled over the sky, landing with a thud onto the ground. Lighting bounced again between the clouds. The reverberations hit the city streets and the ground seemed offended by the unrelenting attacks from mother nature. 

Slightly turning, making sure to be out of view, I swallow and sigh. Can’t be seen as nervous. Turning back around I felt like a captain of a ship in rough water. Strong and stern. Willing and honoring an unspoken pledge — to go down with the ship.
