My boyfriend and I have been dating for a month now. It’s been so good, but so surreal. So many of our encounters have felt so star-crossed, and I know so many of them are gonna stay with me for a while, in the way you keep a good memory close to your heart, I think. I’ve just been having a lot of thoughts about it lately and I’m trying to organize them here, so sorry for the word dump.

“Julie — you lost a tooth biting into a caramel apple in first grade. You were so ashamed of it that you swallowed it in the middle of class and didn’t tell anyone.”

That was the message Clark sent me on the dating app. I couldn’t tell if that was scarier than what he sent me next.

“We were together in a past timeline. I was given the choice to do it all over again, and of course I said yes. I know this sounds crazy, trust me, but this is what you told me to tell you.”

So of course when you see something like that, you dismiss it as insane. I ignored his message and let it pile up in the ever-filling inbox of messages from men.

It kept eating at me, though. The really insanely weird thing was that it was true and that nobody knew it. It was something I’d kept close to my chest and never told anyone, not even my parents. To them, my first lost tooth was something that had happened when I’d fallen on the playground, and the Tooth Fairy had taken pity on me and left me a quarter under the pillow for no tooth that evening.

I had a lazy Sunday afternoon that weekend where I lay on my bed with my cat and re-opened the dating app to clear out the messages. Sooner than I’d expected, Clark jumped out at me again, with his confident smile and message about the caramel apple.

I’d told myself nobody would hear that story except maybe the person I trusted most, if I ever met them. Now, all of a sudden, here he was, or here he was purporting to be.

I thumbed through this guy’s profile to figure out what his deal was. Some pictures with a few friends, a cat of his own, went to a good state school, had a nice face and a vague-sounding email job. Exactly my sense of humor, too. Hot for sure, but not comically so to the point where I’d be worried I was getting catfished. Not like anyone else would know that caramel apple story. I didn’t even have social media, outside of a Facebook account I hadn’t used since high school.

My finger hovered over the red button to decline his message with all the others, and hesitated.

I had been at the end of my rope with online dating for a while now and was about to uninstall the app. Maybe I’m a horrible romantic, or addicted to the possibility of what might be, but I matched with him. One last terrible date, I told myself, and then I’d quit my job and become a nun. 

I was looking for nearby nunneries (there weren’t any) when I checked the app again to see if he’d replied. Two hours of rapid back-and-forth talking later, we’d set up our first date.

He knew my exact coffee order and was already sitting at the table with it when I got there. He gave me a hug when I walked in and it already felt like everything I’d wanted. He smelled amazing, but I resisted commenting on it as we sat back down.

“I knew you’d walk, since you live two blocks from here.” Clark said. “Out the door one minute late. I know where you’re coming from.” He raised his hands. “But I’d never go there! Unless you invited me, of course. That’d be weird.”

“Yeah,” I said, glad I’d picked a place with people, “that’d be really crazy.”

“Yeah, this is really crazy,” he echoed, and laughed a little. The tension lessened. He seemed a lot more awkward in person. I wondered if I did too. The rapid-fire jokes we’d shared had been so instantaneously electric, but then again maybe he’d known exactly what to say. He definitely had the old-soul energy that he’d made a joke about in our initial text conversations.

There was a pause. I wasn’t sure whether to ask the question, but did anyway.

“So,” I asked. “How did all this...” I gestured vaguely. “How did all this happen, anyway? What’s going on here?”

“It's a long story,” he said, equally vaguely. He looked down at his order, which was a small black coffee. He hadn’t touched it. “I don’t want to get into it right now.”

“No?”

He smiled uncomfortably. “It’s kind of a heavy topic.”

“No shit,” I said, simultaneously intrigued and kind of pissed off. “Maybe a hint? What the hell are you talking about with the past life? I — another me — told you to tell me that about the tooth in the apple?”

My patience with him was rapidly evaporating, and I think he could sense it. The conversations we’d had with each other in the days leading up to our date had been instantly familiar, almost too much so, warm and easy like slipping into a warm bath or a limb going numb from anesthesia. I was a person of sharp edges, and I didn’t like that feeling.

“Well, you know if you’ve been in a relationship with someone for a long time,” Clark began haltingly. “Which we were. You end up talking about your pasts, and what you’ve both been through, and maybe the conversation turns towards a question like, if you could do it all over again so we could end up together, would you, and obviously the answer is yes.”

“Yes, but how did it actually happen?” I pressed. “Was there some kind of, like-”

“Later, later,” he said, smiling. “Let’s just try to get to know each other, organically. This is just as weird for me too. It’s my first time trying to get to know you again.”

“Yeah.” Something about the way he phrased that sounded weird but I had other things on my mind. I realized I barely knew anything about him. He’d been asking me how my weekend was, and normal things, like hobbies, but I realized if he was for real, there was probably no point to him asking any of that because he already knew. “What do *you* do for fun?” I asked. “And also, what do you even do for work? What’s an Applied Systems Generalist?”

I’d stalked him plenty before our date, obviously, but he seemed normal with a light social media presence. The most he’d posted in the last year was on LinkedIn, about a conference he’d attended.

“It’s really boring,” Clark said. “It’s basically just applying business processes to new domains to optimize them. You have to turn them around a lot to fit, like puzzle pieces.” He turned his hands into claws and pantomimed rotating them against each other. “And if they don’t fit after a while, then...*KRSH!*”

He made a crunching noise and mashed his palms together. “I make them fit. That’s kind of my thing.”

“Uh huh,” I said, already not paying attention. “And then what about outside of work?”

He shrugged. “This and that. I like making ceramics.”

“No way,” I said, sitting up. “I’ve been wanting to get back into it. But I guess you knew that already.”

“Yeah!” he said. “There’s a little studio by my place that I go to on the weekends.”

“You didn’t pick up ceramics just for me, did you?”

“No! Come on. I told you we were in a good relationship before. We just fit together really well, and our similar interests were a big part of that.”

“You have any pictures of your stuff? I wanna see.”

“It’s really work-in-progress,” Clark said.

“Come on,” I said. “We’re both amateurs here. I’m sure it’s better than you think.”

“Fine,” Clark said after some hesitation. He pulled out his phone and tapped in an elaborate passcode with both thumbs, like an actual one with letters, before showing me his creations.

Even despite my comment about both of us being amateurs, I still struggled not to laugh at the small, misshapen collection of cups and bowls. They exhibited the dexterity level of a particularly dim child’s creations at a summer camp. They were covered in hand indentations from where he’d gripped them too hard. One of them had exploded in the kiln and he’d taken a picture of the pieces anyway.

“The journey is more important than the destination. Carpe diem, you know,” he said defensively, seeing my face. “I like to document the journey.”

“Do you do anything other than pottery?” I asked. *The Killing Moon* had come on and I realized I was unconsciously tapping my foot. “Oh, I love this song.”

“I know,” Clark said, with a smile. He regarded me quietly from behind his glasses. “I like chess,” he said after a moment. “If you want to come over, we could play tonight.”

I almost said yes then and there. It felt like cheating. The ability to just instantly get into a relationship with someone who knows you wholly, to feel so understood without putting in the hard work, was so tempting. But my gut told me to take it slow.

“Let’s just stick with coffee for today,” I said, watching for his reaction. Clark nodded slightly, neither happy nor surprised, the same way I would when a mug I’d left a bit damp had exploded in the kiln. “Next time, though.”

On our second date we got dinner, and both the food and the conversation was so good that I ended up going back to his place. We played a bit of chess, but never finished the game because we ended up having sex for an hour straight. It was the best I’d ever had, although to be honest it if it weren’t I’d have left him then and there, because how could you actually know someone in a past life except what they like in bed. But he was really eager, and knew exactly what I liked.

At this point though, after a month, I do honestly believe him that we’d been in a relationship in a past life together. He knows everything about me, what I like, what pisses me off and what I laugh at. The sex is still amazing, and I feel more known by him than I have by anybody. It's so surreal, in a good way, knowing nobody else on the planet feels like this. 

I just wonder sometimes, is all. What if we *were* in a relationship, but it for some reason it wasn’t a good one? And something about our conversation that day on our first date, and little things he’s said and done still feel strange. I know it’s a weird start to a relationship and I also really want to get to the bottom of how exactly he ended up in this situation to begin with, which he’s still reluctant to tell me.

It was mostly just that bit about it feeling weird because it was his first time trying to get to know me again. I barely thought about it when he said it because I was so overwhelmed by everything, but it was odd enough for me to remember it all this time later. Like yeah, of course it would feel weird, but why say specifically that it was his first time trying to get to know me again?
