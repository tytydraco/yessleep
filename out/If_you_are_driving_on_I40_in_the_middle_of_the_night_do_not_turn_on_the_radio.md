If you have ever been listening to your car radio while driving down I-40 in Oklahoma during the middle of the night, you can thank me for picking whatever shitty pop song was playing at the time. I care little for music, but even less for people, which is how I found myself as the night-shift broadcast engineer in one of the few remaining radio stations that don’t just set and forget a playlist. I actually consider myself less as a DJ and more of a glorified security guard/janitor hybrid. Honestly, I think that the main reason that I haven’t been fired yet is because the morning shift always arrives to an organized workspace and clean bathrooms. My compulsive personality narrowly manages to trump my misanthropic nature and night after night I find myself cleaning up other people’s messes.

My actual workload is quite simple. Queue up enough songs, mix in the required local injury lawyer advertisements, and then make sure that the broadcast stays on air. I can manage the tedium. Between what I do and having a normal desk job, it is certainly the lesser of the two evils. I don’t mind the hours either, with enough coffee, I can usually power through an eight-hour shift.

The late-autumn sun had set hours ago, yet my shift was only beginning when I noticed the first sign that tonight was not going to be like all of the others. The glowing red light which indicated a successful transmission began to flicker. That alone wasn’t too irregular. It happened occasionally if there was some sort of storm or inclement weather. But the night was clear. I took my headphones off and tapped the bulb a few times.

Yes, this is the expertise that they pay me for.

When that failed to fix the blinking, I rose from my seat. I could safely reset the terminal while losing no air time if I fired up the backup system. The only issue was the secondary system was so old and dysfunctional that it wasn’t even kept in the station itself. Instead, it was isolated in an equipment shed a stone’s throw away from the main building.

I pulled my jacket over my shoulders and checked for my pack of smokes in my pocket. If I was going to make the expedition, I may as well make it worth my time. Stepping outside, the cold air hit my face like a thousand microscopic shards of glass. I was so taken aback by the freezing temperature that I almost didn’t notice a van parked in the corner of the lot. Besides my crap bucket of a sedan, the van was the only other car in the parking lot. The rest of the crew should have been long gone, and the station was in such a remote location there was no reason for any other vehicle to park here, even temporarily. I eyed the van warily as I quickly walked towards the equipment shed.

The frigid air caused my fingers to lose their dexterity and I fumbled with my keys for longer than I would have liked. I made my way inside and powered the system on. After several moments of mechanical whirring, I was satisfied enough to return to the station. As I was twisting the key back into the lock, I was suddenly blinded by the bright glow of the van’s headlights. I stared back at the van, trying to glimpse into it to see if I recognized whoever it was. I could see nothing past the sharp glare, but whoever it was could see me completely exposed. I decided that I had enough and I fled from the shed, walking quickly back to the safety of the station.

As I was walking, I heard the van door open and a man’s voice called out.

“Hey!”

I pretended not to hear as I pushed myself inside and locked the glass door. I retreated towards the safety of my booth, out of eyesight of anyone attempting to peer inside. I paused in the hallway, dreading what I hoped would not come next.

Several gentle knocks at the door.

“I’m sorry for scaring you! Please, let me in.” When he spoke there was something unnatural about his words. It was a man’s voice, but his pronunciation was almost child-like.

Alarm bells rang in my head. This was stranger-danger 101. It was one of the few things they taught us in school that actually managed to stick with me. Probably to an unhealthy degree.

So I listened to what I had been taught. I walked away. I entered the comfort of the studio room and was relieved to see the transmission light had stopped blinking. My heart rate returned to a normal cadence as I convinced myself that if the man was genuine, he would be alright. It was cold out, but it wasn’t freeze to death in your van cold.

Even through the closed booth doors, I could hear him knocking relentlessly against the glass. I put on my headphones, drowning out the sound with an overplayed Sabrina Carpenter song. I had almost been able to forget about the entire encounter before something unusual happened.

The phone in the studio rang. The sudden ear-piercing ring caused me to nearly jump out of my shoes. In all of my shifts, I had not once heard the phone ring before. The phone was used by the day-shift DJ to take calls from local listeners and engage in various games to win small prizes like concert tickets. There was no reason for anyone to be calling it so late at night.

After the brief startle, rationality returned as I deduced that the call must be from whoever was outside. It could be a worker who had left something in the building and needed to recover it. Without putting myself on air, I picked up the phone.

“Hello?”

The voice that answered was frantic, almost panicked and broken up with a significant amount of static.

“Whatever you do, do not let it inside.”

“What? What do you mean?”

The connection began to cut out and I could barely hear the caller’s next sentence.

“It-” Static. “already” More static. “too la-” The call dropped.

I smashed the redial button, but only got a dial tone.

Was that some sort of messed up prank call? It couldn’t be. It was too much of a coincidence to happen on the same night as the man appearing outside.

I was gathering my thoughts when something else caught my eye. The studio booth was… different. It was a detail so minor that if I wasn’t a neurotic mess, I may have never noticed. My coffee mug had been washed and hung up on its rack.

Cleaning my mug was always the last thing that I did on my shift. I would wash it, place it exactly how it was on the rack, and then get in my car and go home before any of the morning shift workers got in. When I got up to turn on the backup machine, I wasn’t even finished with half of my drink.

Hesitantly, I stood and poked my head outside of the studio door. The hair on my arms rose as I scanned the hallway. Keeping my steps quiet, I turned and walked into the men’s bathroom.

Pristine. The floors were freshly mopped and the trash had been taken out. I hadn’t even begun my cleaning on the bathrooms this shift, and I know damned well that the other workers didn’t manage to leave it in such untouched shape for an entire day.

“What the…”

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

The knocking at the glass door turned to banging.

Nothing made any sense. I ran back to the booth and I slammed the door, determined to hunker down for the night. Whoever the man was had no reason to be here in the middle of the night, and it was not my responsibility to help them if they got themselves stuck out here in the middle of a cold front. The banging didn’t cease and I had no choice but to put on my headphones, avoiding whatever the hell was outside the station. Several more hours of pop music would be my cross to bear if I wanted to escape the night unscathed.

But when the headphones covered my ears, a song was playing that I did not recognize. A song that I would never have queued. It was unlike anything I had ever heard, otherworldly and enchanting. I grew mesmerized by the foreign tune only for it to be broken up once again by the piercing sound of the phone ringing.

I removed my headphones and picked up the phone.

“Do not listen.” The voice was once again a garbled mess, but I was able to make out the words. “Turn off the backup system.”

“I can’t! The person is outside and I would need to go past him to get to the equipment shed.”

“Do not listen. Turn off the backup system.” The voice repeated.

“Who are you?”

“You need to turn it off. It is already inside.” The connection was weakening.

“Fuck this.” I slammed the phone down, ending the call.

None of this was worth my life. I would happily burn the station to the ground if it meant that I got to drive away from the forsaken place. The reaffirmation made my decision easy. I would grab my keys, I would get in my car, and I would floor it home. My only hurdle would be the stranger outside who I could easily blow past if I got the jump on them.

My mind made up, I reached for my car keys-

Gone. From the same place that I kept them every shift.

Helplessness overcame me. Prank call or not, I knew that there was something in the station. I was not safe in my little box and I never had been. With seemingly no other choice, I stepped outside of the booth and rushed to the front entrance. It was the first time that I got a good look at the man outside. He was short, wearing a green coat and beat up winter hat. My first thought was that he was likely homeless. My second was that I could probably beat him in an altercation if it came to it.

The man was staring down at a device in his hand that looked like an old phone. In his other hand he held a large pair of thick headphones.

“What do you want?” I called to him. He didn’t look up and he did not reply.

I stepped closer cautiously. “What do you-”

The man finally noticed me and his eyes went wide.

He gave me a thumbs up and then pointed to his ear. His pantomiming made everything clear. The man was deaf. His voice was not unnatural at all, it was the voice of someone who had not heard language in who knows how long. The man pointed to himself.

“Friend.” He said. “I can help. You need to turn off the backup system.”

I was beyond confused and more than a little unsettled. I did not trust the stranger, but I was also certain that there was someone or something else in the station.

“Why?” My stance was guarded.

He frowned, although it seemed as if he could read lips. “No time to explain. Please, trust.”

Trust.

The word felt like poison coming off of his lips. Trust was not something that should be granted for free. It was the world’s most valuable currency. It needed to be earned.

Noticing my refusal to budge, he continued to plead. “You are not safe.”

Before I was able to make up my mind, I heard a quiet tune begin to hum from the studio booth. I had not activated the loudspeaker, and it was not a song I recognized.

I instinctively turned around and stared down the hallway.

I wish I hadn’t.

The sound of off-rhythm wet slapping interrupted the alluring tune as a shape began to form. The mass occupied the entire width of the hallway as it stumbled towards me. It had a long neck and a defined face, but the rest of its body seemed to be an amorphous blob. Legs and arms extruded and retracted from its torso with squelchy-wet noises as it moved. As it waded towards me unevenly, I thought of all of the undiscovered species deep in the trenches of the ocean. Not a single one of those bottom feeding fish could hold a candle to the abyssal monstrosity that presented itself.

The bulk of its slick black body was covered in a sheen of slime, similar to that of a slug. It didn’t walk as much as it dragged itself towards me, using whatever appendage it could to carry itself forward. As it approached, its thin long neck bobbed up and down in a sinuous pattern, holding up its large head with fish-like eyes. It had no mouth, but it sang a gentle melody all the same.

“Do not listen!”

But it was too late. I was already entranced and walking towards my fate with open arms. The world around me faded, and became a blur of wondrous colors painted by the cosmic radiance of the creature’s music. I was entirely lost in another world. A place that I was content to live forever as eons passed by on Earth. I felt every emotion that I had ever experienced before cranked up to the limit. Time slowed as I bathed in the euphoria. I wanted more. I needed more. I had been fed a morsel, but had the appetite of a king.

Glass shattered.

The sound dulled. As the music stopped, my heart wrenched with impossible pain from everything that I had lost. I had only been graced by the creature’s song for a moment, but it felt like I had given up a lifetime of joy and wonder.

Reality came back in waves. First, I saw the abyssal creature lurching closer. Second, I realized that I was now wearing noise-cancelling headphones that had been pressed over my ears. Third, I felt the deaf stranger pulling me out into the bitter cold of night through a shattered glass door.

As the freezing air pierced my skin it was clear that I had fully returned to our world.

The man’s lips moved, but I couldn’t make out what he was saying.

He pointed towards the equipment shed, his eyes pleading.

The key to the shed was still in my pocket. For whatever reason, this stranger seemed to think that turning off the backup machine would in some way help us against whatever the hell had infiltrated the station.

The headlights to his van were still on. I only had a moment to make up my mind.

I threw the key at the man’s chest and I ran. I fled towards the van, without bothering to turn back until I was behind the wheel. I let out a sigh of relief seeing that the van’s key was in the ignition.

In the beam of the old van’s dusty headlights I saw the creature emerge from the radio station. It paid the van no heed as it stumbled slowly and unnaturally after the deaf man. It somehow appeared even larger now that it was out under the night sky.

The stranger did not flee. Instead, he stood at the door of the equipment shed and worked the key into the shitty lock. He was unable to hear the guttural squelching as the monster approached, and he would never hear its beautiful singing that had brought me to another world.

Or perhaps, he already had.

I quickly scanned the van, recognizing pieces of equipment in the back. Signal interferers. He was responsible for jamming the transmission. He knew this thing would be here and he had a plan to stop it.

My mistake hung over me like a dark cloud. If only I had listened earlier, he could have helped. I gripped both hands on the wheel. I should be furious at myself for being such a coward, but I was not. My heart was simply empty. After hearing the monster’s song, even for the briefest of moments, I wasn’t sure it could ever be full again.

I didn’t know, but I did need to try.

I screamed so loudly that I could hear myself through my headphones and I slammed the gas. The van accelerated and I refused to let up until I drove the vehicle directly into the creature’s slug-like form. The impact sent my forehead flying into the steering wheel.

A seatbelt would have done wonders for me in that moment.

I was dizzy, deaf, and aching, but I was also proud. I didn’t run away. Thanks to me, the stranger should have had enough time to slip inside the equipment shed. Using what little strength I still had, I fumbled out of the driver’s side door and fell to the blacktop, coughing uncontrollably for several moments. Fighting through my daze, I stood to take a better look at the monster that I had just rammed.

My heart dropped to my stomach as I realized that it was nowhere to be found. A wide streak of oily liquid traced a path to the shed. I followed it and pushed inside.

The sight was enough to drop me to my knees. The deaf stranger was already dead. The creature had ripped him apart so quickly and viciously that I couldn’t begin to process what was limb and what was torso.

I was too weak and too defeated to run. The abomination turned towards me, its amorphous body oozing and bubbling like its insides were a thousand little bugs trying to burst from their sacs. A three-fingered appendage clawed out of its stomach and it reached towards me. It knocked off my headphones and wrapped itself around my head. The texture was gelatinous and wet and I could smell a mixture of salt and death. The creature extended its long neck putting its face directly in front of mine.

Dead eyes stared at me as the head bobbed up and down.

Then it began to sing.

As I listened, I saw the world that I was going to, but I also saw the one that I was leaving.

In the morning, the crew will return. I am certain there will be no mess. There will be no van, and no body in the equipment shed. Every morning the crew will come in and find their coffee mugs clean and the bathrooms mopped. They will not fire the misanthropic night-shift broadcast engineer who they never even cross paths with because he keeps the station clean. In return, the broadcast engineer gets to queue the music.

If you are ever driving down I-40 in Oklahoma during the middle of the night, do not turn on the radio. If you do, you can thank me for whatever alluring, cosmic tune is playing.
