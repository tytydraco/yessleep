“There’s nothing there.” I said confused. I turned my head from my living room wall to where if there had been something I would’ve seen it. I waved my hand, my own shadow went over it as normal, and as I lowered my hand I called out to my wife, “Hey, Jess!” My voice was shaky, and I wasn’t taking my eyes off of the shadow that seemed to stain the wall. “Hey! Come look at this!” My hands started to get clammy, I thought either I’m gone crazy, or my eye strain has finally caught up to me. 

“What?” She walked in from the bathroom, then she saw it. “What is that?” She furled her eyebrows. 

“So you see it too, right?” I glanced over to her. 

She nodded, “yeah I see it. Did you do something to the wall?”

“No no, I just saw it. There’s nothing that would make this right?”

She looked over to where I had before, “I don’t think so. Do we have a carbon monoxide detector?” She looked at me, “Uh, no, no I don’t think we do. 

Why?” I stepped away from the shadow, “Do we need one?”   
“I think we’re hallucinating the same thing, we definitely need to get one.”

“Ok, let’s go get one, the store shouldn’t close for a-“ I looked at my phone, it was 10:32pm, “What the fuck?” I didn’t even notice the sun had set. “When did it become so late? It was just a little past lunch, how did so much time past.”

She grabbed my shoulder, “Let’s leave, this is bad.” I didn’t argue, she looked scared, and we were in our car heading off to a motel. Before we could get into our car, she tapped me, “That’s Robert, right?” I looked over to his house. Our neighbor, a skinny old man was standing outside his house, staring through his window. We stared at him for about a minute, he didn’t move once. She shoved me and we went into our car.

We were on the road for a little before I asked, “How long were we staring at that?” I kept my eyes on the road. 

“*We* weren’t.” she said, “You were standing there for hours, I didn’t know what to do.” 

My chest tensed, “W-what? Why didn’t you try to stop me?” “I did try, I tried everything, I even slapped you.” She took a breath, “You were in this weird trance, you looked so intensely, it was creepy.” I tapped the steering wheel with my fingers, gripping it tighter now. 

“Ok.” My voice came out monotoned, I nodded nervously, turned up the radio and we drove in silence. I saw the crappy sign, “One stop motel.” I read aloud as I pulled into a parking spot. We got a key, and we slept the night away, not saying a word to each other. I woke up to the sound of hacking and coughing so loud and clear it felt right in my ear. I shot up from stiff bed and rushed to the bathroom. 

“Jess, Jesus Christ what the-“ I nearly passed out, the smell was so bad. She was hurling out thick black mucus that came out in buckets. It was everywhere, covered almost the whole bathroom, slowly dripping and landing loudly on the floor. She closed to her and grabbed the one clean towel and wiped her mouth. I helped her stand up, she was paler than the moon, her eyes were sunken in. I slowly guided her to the bed. “Are you ok now?” I looked away, “Well, you know, sorry.” 

“No no, I’m ok, it’s all out I think.” She paused, her whole body slightly shook, her voice came out weak. “I hope.” She let out a quick breath from her nose. 

“Do you still want to go get that detector?” 

She looked up and nodded, “Yeah, I’ll be fine let’s just go.” We were off on the road, we didn’t say much. I stared at the road and glanced over at her from time to time, she never stopped shaking, just a constant little buzz. I parked, we walked in, and we were immediately lost. “Let me go ask someone.” I walked over to a worker who looked about 16, “Excuse me-“

“Aisle 12.” He cut me off.

“Huh?”

“The detectors, aisle 12.” He tapped his foot.

“Ok… thank you.” I turned around and Jess followed me, we turned into the aisle. “Oh this is absurd.” I said. 15 people seem to have our same problem. One through an elbow, hit a small women, knocking her down to the ground. 

“Oh god!” Jess yelled as they all began to fight each other, a pack of animals all because of the carbon monoxide detectors. “Let’s just go home, I want to sleep in our bed now.”

“Yeah me too.” We walked away from the increasingly violent mess, the same teenage worker stood still staring wide eyed. Not just wide, not just open, his eyelids looked pulled into his face. He didn’t blink, didn’t move an inch and we rushed out. I sped home, we needed rest, and we could deal with everything later. We reached our neighborhood, I slowed down, finally feeling a calm wave come back to me. Everything was beginning to be blocked out of my mind.

“We’re all outside.”

“What?” I looked over to Jessica.

“Look, they’re all outside.” She pointed out the window. That’s not what she said the first time right? I looked out the window, everyone was outside of their houses, staring our car down. Staring me down. I sped up, my hands tightened. I checked the rearview, everyone that we passed ran straight through their windows. I pulled in crooked, I stepped out and looked over to the neighbors house again. His front window was smashed in, smeared red on the glass spread down the wall. It was dark in his house, pitch black. Jess never followed me, she sat in the car and I didn’t mind. The couch was comfortable, the shadow seemed to have grown across the wall from the time we left, trailing to what I was sitting.  

My lungs burned, my mouth was dry, I felt conscious again. I stood up, looked around the sun was still out. I stumbled towards the kitchen, I needed water. The sink smelled foul, a mixture of grease exhaust and death. I turned the faucet on, black sludge slowly pooled out like honey. “Jess, where the hell is Jessica?” I turned around, she was there. She was barely there. A husk, skinny to the bone, her eyes dried out, hands grabbing for me. I approached, “J-Jess, god no.” I grabbed her hand, my own went through her skin. I gagged, I could smell it in my mouth, and I pat it on the floor. Black sludge. I ran out of the house, pushing through the door made me sick. I ran to Robert’s house, through the window I saw his family. All in a circle, dry, and chipped versions of their former selves. The walls, the chairs, up to their own legs were stained in shadow. They seemed to be going at each others throats, his wife had stabbed him in the stomach. I blinked, they looked at me. 

“Jack.” Rang through my ears, I turned around and saw her wide eyes staring. “Come back home, you look so bad, you need rest.” She tried to grab me, her muscles and skin cracked and peeled as she slowly reached for me. I smacked her hand in fear, then watched it fall of her arm and land in the grass. I looked back to her face, she had a deep frown, it made the skin around her lips and cheeks peel off and expose pale muscle. I felt a hand on my shoulder, I didn’t even turn around, I ran down the street, the moons glow burnt my eyes. 

I ran, and ran, for I think miles. I should have been out of the neighborhood, I shouldn’t see any houses. I shouldn’t be back at my house. We all stood in the front yard. They looked at me, all smiling with their arms out. The faint silhouette of Jessica walked away from the door, waving me in. I focused back, they all made a tunnel for me. “Will you help me?” We said. They nodded. The house was beautiful, painted with the shadow, warm and cold. Every step gave me five as I went to the shower and turned it on. Quick black rain poured on me, cooling my skin. I passed by us, being guided to the circle. Hundreds, from the whole neighborhood. They all stared at the center, black poured from our mouths and harden at the feet. I took my place, smiling wider than everyone else.
