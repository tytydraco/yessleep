When I heard that, I decided it had been enough time and eventually went back to the house, only to discover a happy family inside. I was shocked to see the bank had resold it, though I’m not too sure why. It was a house without an owner, reselling is normal.

When I asked about the closet in the room by the stairs, they seemed confused as to what I meant. They had moved in not too long ago, acknowledging that there was quite a long period between when they bought the house and when they were allowed to move in. I thanked them and went on my way.

I tried to see the house I lived in till I was nine, but it was locked behind a gated fence with barbed wire. I could make out a glimpse of an old house, rotting, caked in a cracked, dry, muddy yellow. I could see a branch of a tree trapped within and partially covered. The same dried up color traveled down the branch and mummified the tree. It only slowly faded out along the ground, ending a couple hundred feet from where the fence was.

“Peculiar, ain’t it?” I whipped my head around to see an older woman staring past me. “Woah,” she said, “I didn’t mean to scare you.”

“Um,” I tried to find my voice and turned back around to where she was looking. “Yeah. What even happened here?”

I heard her clothes rustle as I assumed she shrugged and took a sip of the mug she held in her hand. 

“Some things,” she said with a raspy voice. “They’re better unanswered. That’s what they told me when they blocked it off at least.”

We sat for a second in silence, only interrupted by an occasional slurp from the woman behind me. She chuckled a little under her breath, obviously hesitant to say what she wanted to say.

“But,” she finally continued. “When it gets real late, and no one’s around, sometimes I come out here and I can smell my own mother’s sweet caramel and brown sugar pie drifting through this here fence.” I shifted uncomfortably on my feet as I turned back around to her. All she did was point at the fence and let out a stifled laugh, not noticing the fear in my eyes. “First time I smelt it, I about nearly took a bite out the damn fence!”

“Do you hear anything?” I asked, a little too fast. She just looked at me a little puzzled and I quickly brushed it off. “Sorry,” I said, shaking my head. “Have a good day.”

I practically ran back to my beat down car. She just stood there and looked back at the fence, not reacting as I turned on the engine and sped away. 

It took me a while to really get back on my feet, not really having anything or anyone to help me out. Eventually, I was able to get a semi-stable blue collar job and even found a girl I fell in love with. We moved in together, and though I never forgot my brother or even that specific sound he would make, it slowly became overshadowed by new memories and people. 

The only reason I’m writing this at all is because my girlfriend recently went on a trip with some of her friends out of state. I was in favor of her going, I thought it would be fun for her and I could use the time alone for working on my architecture degree, but she was obviously upset about something.

She called me after the first night.

“I don’t know if I can do it,” she finally opened up to me. “I mean, I couldn’t even sleep without hearing you snoring.”

“What?” I laughed. “I snore?”

“Pretty loudly.” She joked back.

“Well, are you going to be alright without it?”

“I don’t know, it just makes me feel so… warm.”
