I live in the Appalachian Highlands of Pennsylvania. How I got here is a separate, but related story for those interested. I have lived and continue to live a life that makes me feel crazy to talk about, but I need you to understand, I've been through the therapies, gotten my gold stars, and the most I do is smoke medical marijuana for the parts that feel a lot more grounded but less easy to talk about. I am NOT deluded, but critical analysis of what you should and should not believe is something I feel REQUIRES getting a little schizoid these days. Either way, I like to see myself as a rational person, bewildered to find parts of nature that would inspire myth. I do not believe in any higher or lower power detached from the guts of the world, and I believe those are formed of mathematical frequencies of rest states. It's just historically bad form to regard yourself the scientific sort as you indulge in the validity of the occult and pseudoscientific.

With that out of the way, who can recall a legend of a great serpent and some god and guard of men putting it down to make room for us to gut the planet with equal parts condemnation and permission? Thor slaying Jörmungandr, Marduk slaying Tiamat, Yahweh condemning Lucifer to the body of a serpent and inadvertently leading to him tempting for the fruit, allowing us to see him as more than just a snake, something we can see in other things today. Many of these stories were born in oral traditions and refined by institutions for palpability in written word. Some stories still only exist in whispers and experiences, and I hope to share some of mine.

An oral tradition struggling in coverage and preservation in written form is that of the precolonial Lenape mask deity and protector of the land, Mesinq. I hope to be respectful here. When the "creator god" from the sky cast the horned serpent to the riverbeds, Mesinq questioned humanity's place in these ancient hills. It's said he was slapped across the cheek and plowed through the mountain face, carving the valleys of the highlands. He lost half his face, and was tied to the woods to protect them from hunters seeking to take too much. I think the parallels here speak for themselves. Humans centering themselves cuts out inconvenient info about our relationship with the earth and other life, and fills the blanks with divinity. 

As I said, how I got here is a different path than we are walking down today, but I'll write it if there's interest here. One thing of note is a distinct, magnetic affinity to rot. It was terrifying and I noticed it EVERYWHERE. Now its just the change people dont like, the scary ones. Nature isnt hostile, its just not built for us alone. Some traditions say burning sandalwood as offering can bring the mask deity's protection, so I started keeping the smell around, incense, champa and stuff.  After a while burning it on either side of our A/C unit all summer, I started noticing what I figured was a sort of rust streak down, figured these things happen, its functional and can be replaced at worst case. Maybe it was just incense-dyed condensate. One day, though, I was emptying our bucket of drippings, from the radiator on the thing, and I noticed a series of spore clusters floating around.

At first, I didn't think much of it, but when I put the bucket back, I noticed the rust wasnt streaked-it was mounding, dripping. A slimey, moldy, beautiful, bright-orange biofilm puddled on the moulding of the windowsill over the siding of the house. Or should I say molding? Jokes aside, I knew then what I was looking at was not some abstract artifacting of decay, but a rampant cacophany of mingling forms of life, exchanging traits in a violent primordial soup of assimilation and integration. 

And I had just sent its genetic best down to the riverbed.

Too many little streams to make much of this, so I feel safe saying our property has, on its edge, the stream that carved our holler. But since then, things have started to get better. I got a new job, I've had a creative renaissance in my life, Ive started branching out more socially. Everything is going fine, wonderfully even. But then the noises started.

First it was just knocks at the door, creaky steps that saw the floor as optional. Our two cats would notice something I couldn't, but I'd follow their eyes and get this chill, and remember those old appalachian granny witch rules tiktok basically memed. Even in calling whatever has shown up, limiting exposure feels important when its close. But after I poured those spores into the stream, it got louder. More frequent. My fiancee started seeing lights floating by the window at night, I started to see them in our room but know not to acknowledge anything until it is gone or the sun has returned. I started hearing a chittering noise by the stream one night and decided to call myself in, but in the morning I noticed the brush between our toolshed and the tree beside it pushed down like something low to the ground had crawled out of the stream.

These little things became more common, but the other night, I heard caterwaul. That awful noise pumas and other mountain lions make in heat? Like a woman screaming in agony, or something imitating one. The issue is, I heard the rumors that floated every couple years and how they amounted to nothing, I triple-checked land surveys and zoo rosters, and there are no cats of such a caste in any reasonable yowling distance. It reminded me of that old gemini home entertainment video, with the guy in the field? I went back inside for the night.

There's still little knocks and chitters, but nothing quite like that again yet. I just can't help but feel like the thing on the wind thats called me here for so long was given something it needed in my offering of life, chaotic and pure and resilient to the toxicity of our machines. Like some ancient unseen beast is shaking off its limbs, and like its gotten me to play some part in that.
