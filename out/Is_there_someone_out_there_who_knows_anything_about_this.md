I can’t seem to find anything about this on Google; like, none of the symptoms or circumstances seem to correlate with anything online. Taken individually, some symptoms have matches—for example, toxoplasmosis can cause a rodent to lose its fear of cats. This makes it so that once the infected rodent ultimately gets ingested by the cat, the parasite can enter the cat’s gut, the optimal place for survival. But this doesn’t necessarily manifest the same way in humans. Additionally, when you add in the symptom of irritability, things fall apart. I cannot find a singular diagnosis or explanation for this. I’m looking for any entomologists, psychiatrists, parasitologists, demonologists—you name it—to explain what the hell is going on.

For context, I’m about to go to college in September, meaning this year, my summer break is extra long. My family and I were able to fit in a much awaited trip to visit my sister in the bay area. She lives in a one-bed, one-bathroom apartment with her boyfriend and axolotl. My sister researches worm regeneration in a lab that sometimes deals with axolotls, so she has a permit to own one in California. Despite the lack of space, they were more than happy to let me sleep on the couch for a week. I have barely spent any actual time with her boyfriend. Like, he came to visit our family a couple years ago, but most of the time I just chat to him while FaceTiming my sister, so I have no idea if this is how he normally acts and if it has had any affect on my sister. Just thought that’d be important information.

Anyways, I was super excited to visit. My sister had assured me multiple times over call that she had managed her mosquito infestation, something that was important to me because mosquitos seem to be extra attracted to my blood–the only attraction nobody would ever want lol. But sometimes I’d see her swatting the air or clapping as if to squish something between her palms, but despite my questioning she would continue to reassure me no mosquitos were in the apartment—only fruit flies. Thankfully, the traps were arriving the same day I was.

I remember on the way up to the highest floor, I made jokes about the rules they wanted me to follow during my stay. My sister and I love horror stories (which opens the possibility that she has been pranking me my entire stay, but it would be too elaborate and too ongoing for my usually serious sister). We read a lot of r/shortscarystories and noticed a kind of horror that involves lists of rules.

“Don’t take a shit at 3:00 A.M., or Brian will eat you,” I remember joking in an ominous, yet silly voice.

“Don’t drink my Doctor Pepper, or my eyes will turn red and you will die, oooo” Brian teased, adding onto my jokes. This, along with him wiggling his fingers sinisterly with a creepy grin and crazed, icy blue eyes, made me and my sister laugh hard.

“Don’t leave the toilet seat up or I, Charlotte will drown you in it,” joked my sister, and we all lost it.

But they kinda just made it a little awkward for me because they kept dragging it the whole way to the top floor. Like, more than normal. It wasn’t even that they found the added jokes funnier and funnier—they just kept at it for an unnaturally long time. All of us are neurodivergent, so social cues kinda puzzle us all, but this was just weird. Nonetheless, I tried fake laughing each time, no matter how difficult it got, especially by the end of things.

“If you see both Brian and Charlotte staring at Melon for too long unblinkingly, kill her however you see fit as fast as possible and run,” Charlotte said, “Just stab her, or something. Just do it”.  
I looked at her shocked yet made sure to smile to lighten things up. My sister always got mad at me when I made any dark jokes about her axolotl, so this was weird-weird.

Anyways, thankfully the uncomfortable exchanges ended when we entered the apartment. I went to use the kitchen sink to quickly wash my hands and freshen up when like 6 fruit flies flew out from I don’t know where, disturbed by my presence. Thankfully, my sister had just picked up the package containing the traps from her locker, and was already setting them up.

Everything was, you know, normal after that. For the first day or two, at least. My sister’s boyfriend was great, we had a blast celebrating his birthday, my sister was lovely as always, constantly asking how I was enjoying my stay… all that fun stuff. 

But we had all noticed the number of flies increasing. I wasn’t necessarily scared of them, but it was annoying to have to hunch over my food to protect it from any greedy flies looking for a quick snack. Only one of the vinegar traps was working. The rest of the traps were void of flies, and the blue light ones in the outlets were practically barren save a small fly here and there.

We had soon discovered the reason why the first trap caught so many fruit flies. I was taking out the trash by their desk as my sister had instructed me, and like, 30 billion escaped once I opened the lid. My suitcase had been trapping the bugs inside by adding weight to the opening, and now they were all, well, *outside*. My sister wasn’t mad at me, she had found it understandable that I was frightened, but told me to “close the lid right away next time.” 

So as you can gather, the number of flies sky-rocketed. They swarmed every wall but they were just small enough to give me some space too. Nevertheless, I got impatient with the fly traps. My sister and her boyfriend gave me consent to make my own traps. I used balsamic vinegar and plastic wrap with little holes for the fruit flies, and even made a little gnat trap in case we had gotten the wrong species. 

No luck. No flies.

“I’m going to dump your traps out,” Brian said. This was fine by me because my sister ordered more UV traps.

But those traps weren’t cutting it quick enough. The flies were starting to fall and die in Melon’s tank, which made me worried for her health. But my otherwise paranoid sister told me it was no problem. I didn’t expect that.

To add to my frustration, while admittedly there were a couple of fruit flies stuck in the UV trap in the living room, there were none in the room my sister and her boyfriend shared. Knowing they liked sleeping in the dark, Brian confirmed, to my disappointment, that he unplugged the trap at night. He would not accept my solution to simply flip the trap over, as Zevo’s have rotating plugs. He walked away from me, mid suggestion, shaking his head. Damn. Okay. Fuck me then. 

That reminds me, one day while I was watching Charlotte cut up a worm for Melon, she told me she no longer felt “that way” about bugs, “that way” meaning my disgust and fear of the flies whenever they got too close. Of course she wasn’t scared, she worked with these guys before taking on worms for some time in the lab. It also probably helped that, when her schizophrenia was undiagnosed, she used to always cry about “ants on the walls and everywhere” that my mom and dad couldn’t see. She got used to bugs, real or hallucinated.

She started to take my fear almost personally, though, which is the weird part.

“God, they’re just flies dude,” she repeated often, losing patience.

One day, I asked her if she had any jars she didn’t want so that I could make another trap. I kind of felt like a scientist like her. Seeing what worked and what didn’t while learning more about the animal motivated me to make more traps. At first she consented to me making the trap, but after I told her I’d use a rotting cherry as bait, she snapped at me.

“No, actually. No more traps. You need to be patient. The traps we got are good enough,” it was the tone she used when she was pretending to be an adult, when in reality all I saw was my goofy big sister. But this time it kind of frightened me.

And to add insult to injury, her boyfriend laughed at me, tauntingly passive aggressive.

I let it go, but to see them so angry at seemingly nothing confused the shit out of me. Geeze, alright. I won’t mess with the flies again.

I could have used corn instead of a cherry, now that I think about it. My sister would constantly ask if anybody wanted corn multiple times a day. It was the leftover corn we received from Brian’s mom on his birthday. But it was like, 4 or 5 day old corn. The bag was just open in the fridge and flies would always get to it, then die from the cold. I was grossed out that suddenly she was obsessed with the fly-graveyard corn. And usually she will make elotes with corn, but she just kinda gnawed the cob raw, no seasoning, no mayo, no nothing. It would get stuck in her teeth too, making the whole thing a bit too much for me. And then she would use those kernel-ly teeth to scold me for my “uncontrollable and weird hyperfixation” on solving the fly problem.  
Oh, and they also refused to throw away this fucking left over pie also from his birthday even though the flies were trying with all their might to enter through the tight plastic pie keeper.  “I’ll eat it at some point,” Brian insisted. Usually, these two had the world’s worst contamination OCD. It's so weird, I’m telling you.

But this next thing was even weirder. I’m a light sleeper with a buttload of sleep disorders, so every night of my stay I’d wake at like 2:00-3:00 in the morning. On the 5th night, my brain didn’t just randomly decide to wake me up like it usually did. It was the sound of shuffling this time.

I knew after having it countless times before that this wasn’t just sleep paralysis messing with me—though, all the same, I couldn’t move, but I promise I wasn’t hallucinating. So, imagine this: I squint in the dark without my glasses, and I just see Brian, back facing me, drinking something. He turns slightly, and even with my squinting, the lack of light other than the blue glow from the UV trap, and his elbow almost obscuring my view, I saw that he had never actually thrown away my homemade trap. He was drinking the sudsy balsamic vinegar, desperately, as if chugging fresh water after spending days stranded on the salty ocean. 

I’ve always raged at characters in horror stories that keep important events/information to themselves, but admittedly, I didn’t dare bring this up again out of honest, genuine, true fucking fear and bafflement.

He started day 6 off with a “Hey Charlotte? Is there something wrong with the brita? This water tastes like liquorice.”  
I feel like that had something to do with it.

Later that day, Charlotte finally saw the problem with the fruit flies after having to wash like, thousands down the drain each time she did the dishes.

“You know what? I think your fruit trap would be smart,” she relented.

I stabbed open a rotting cherry, plopped it into the jar, and added a paper funnel to trap any stupid flies who knew how to get in but not how to get out. Hopefully, since there was no poisonous liquid, Brian wouldn’t try to ingest it. Weird thing to think about now, my just like casual concern of a grown man poisoning himself. God, I’ve petsit dogs who had less strange habits than this fucking couple.

I even set up a redbull trap after finding many had flocked to my open redbull after I left it on the counter for a little bit. I put a “DO NOT DRINK. FRUIT FLY TRAP” sign on it.

But absolutely no flies fell for it. What the fuck.

The seventh day, we had to clean up because my parents were finally coming up to see the apartment in person. I added wine to make the cherry trap more appealing to the fermentation-attracted flies. My sister insisted we leave the traps out until the very end of our cleaning, but once Brian came home, he demanded they had to go. Still, my sister and I kept them out.

Once done with all my other assigned chores, I asked what I should do, and was yelled at by the usually gentle and communicative Brian to “fucking get rid of the traps,”. When I asked where to dispose of them, he yelled at me again about how he had already taught me about drains and where to dispose of what, and which chute to use for trash and everything. I don’t know, I mean, doesn’t that kind of remind you of drain flies? And like, the insects did love them trash cans, idk. I hope you see what I see without calling me crazy.

Anyways, I went to dump out the red bull trap but discovered that it was just a hollow can when I went to tip it over. I want so badly to say I have no idea how the liquid vanished, but that would be a depressing lie. Also, Brian never liked alcohol. It made him nauseous and he wouldn’t even drink on my sister’s 21st. But when I came back into the living room after disposing of both traps I saw him holding the bottle of wine I used. But suddenly, he shook his head, and put it back. He seemed confused, like he didn’t know why he was standing there or holding it.

The two were just so aggressive. Is it just me? And sometimes, if I got too close to them, and the traffic and construction noises of the city settled down, I could hear this strange buzzing, just circling inside them in an infinity loop. And it was harder with my brown-eyed sister, but if I looked into Brian’s piercing blue eyes deeply enough without getting uncomfortable or blinking out of impulse, I could see these small swirling doodles, like tv static, behind his eyes.

Let me know if I’m tripping out but… do fruit flies have capabilities that we don’t yet know about? I hope you understand my question, I genuinely don't know how to word it, especially not directly, without sounding fucking stupid. I wish I could just put you into my brain so you can see my experience staying at the apartment with them and feel exactly how I felt being with them. Then you would understand.

Anyways, my cursed visit ended, I hugged Brian, and my sister and I left with my parents for a quick trip to Napa. I am writing this in the hotel room at 4:00 AM, my sister sleeping soundly in the other bed beside mine. Anyways, big day for her. She’s going on a wine train. She was always so excited to go to Napa because she just couldn’t wait to try the wine.
