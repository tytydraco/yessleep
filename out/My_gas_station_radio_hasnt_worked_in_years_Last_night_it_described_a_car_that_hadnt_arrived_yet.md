I work the night shift at a gas station in Uzbekistan. Last night at 3:17 AM a voice came over the radio under my counter, and it described a customer who hadn't arrived yet

I'll say this up front: I'm not making it up. I'm typing this from my phone in the back room and my hands are still shaking, so sorry for any typos.

The station sits on a highway in Navoi Region, about forty kilometers from the nearest village. From 10 PM to 6 AM it's just me: the register, the pumps, a kettle, and an old two-way radio that truckers used to use. Nobody has touched it in years, but the owner won't let me unplug it. He says reprogramming it costs too much. So it sits under the desk and hisses, and I've gotten used to it like you get used to a fridge.

At 3:17 the hissing stopped. I remember the time because I glanced at the clock to check whether the power had gone out. It hadn't. Then a calm, warm voice came out of the radio:

"This is Azgaron, Dead Air Station. Don't be scared. I'm not from the tax office."

I figured it was some trucker messing around. I said, "Who's on the channel? Give me your call sign." Silence, then the same voice, a little amused:

"My call sign is a good one, but you don't need it. Look at pump number two. A white Nexia is about to pull up. Headlights off. The driver won't get out."

I looked out the window. The lot was empty. No cars since evening. I actually smirked.

"Ninety-two, like always," the voice said. "He asks for ninety-two every single time and never remembers how he paid."

A minute later a white Daewoo Nexia rolled onto the lot without a sound, like someone was pushing it, and stopped at pump two. Lights off. The window was cracked open and I didn't hear an engine.

The radio whispered: "Don't go out there. He's looking at the camera, not at you."

There's a camera over that pump. I checked the monitor. The Nexia was on screen. The driver's seat was empty. The glass is tinted, but not so much that you couldn't see a silhouette. There was no silhouette, and the driver's seat was soaked in something dark.

Then the thing I still can't explain. The counter on pump two started running by itself. I stood there watching the numbers spin on the display while the nozzle sat in its holster and nobody was anywhere near the car. Forty liters, sixty, eighty. That car's tank holds fifty.

"He's not filling the tank anymore," Azgaron said, soft, like a bedtime story. "He's filling a debt. Thirty years ago he drove off without paying at this exact spot. It was a different station then, with a different operator. Want to know what that operator did?"

I didn't. The radio apparently disagreed.

"He walked out and handed him his change. And ever since, somebody has to be sitting here at night. Somebody who'll walk out. You understand? Somebody has to walk out, or the meter never stops."

The display hit 99.99 and froze. The Nexia's engine started, the first sound it had made all night, and the high beams came on. Straight into my window.

I didn't go out. I sat there counting to a hundred because I couldn't think of anything else to do. Around seventy, the high beams went off. Around ninety, the car was gone. No tire tracks, no sound.

The radio went back to its normal hiss. At 3:41 I checked the lot again. Empty, like nothing had happened.

In the morning the owner came in and I showed him the footage. On the recording you can see me behind the glass, an empty lot, and the counter on pump two cranking itself up to 99.99 liters. No car in the video. Just the numbers. He deleted the file without a word and told me not to tell anyone. Then, at the door, he added: "If the radio talks, don't answer it." I asked what happens if you do. He didn't reply.

My shift starts at ten tonight. I don't know if I'm going in.
