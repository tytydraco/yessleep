"Don't look up at the ceiling" she told me, her eyes brimming with tears of sheer panic.

Her hands gripped mine, forcing me to follow her even as she walked backward toward the basement stairs.

I was torn between following her or glancing up, until an icy chill washed over the back of my neck. That was when I realized the shiver running down my spine was just a reflection of the terror in my girlfriend's eyes.

How the hell had we ended up in this cramped room in the middle of the night? 

Everything had happened so fast. The shoplifting at the supermarket, followed by the police chase, had led us to this abandoned house right along the railroad tracks.

We had climbed up a side ladder and found the upstairs windows missing their glass and locks. Inside, everything was coated in a thick layer of dust that clung to our clothes like white ash.

In the dim light, it was hard to make out the objects around us, until she tripped over something on the floor that let out a loud metallic clatter. I caught her before she fell, though I ended up tangling my own feet in the thing.

Leaning down to check, I saw it was a heavy-link chain, like the ones I'd seen on ship anchors. For just a second, a glint shone among the metal, taking the shape of a gold coin.

By the time she begged me not to take it, it was already in my hand. I’ve always loved stealing, especially when it’s something valuable like gold.

Outside, the red and blue lights of the squad car flashed up as it pulled to a stop right in front of the house.

In a whisper, I told her it was best to head downstairs and hide on the ground floor. We crawled to keep the flashlights from catching our bodies.

However, the ground floor was completely empty, offering nowhere to hide. The basement door was wide open.

The rusty stairs gave off a strong smell of mildew, and feeling our way in the dark, we managed to slip down there just before the flashlight beams swept across the room.

Once in the basement, the pitch darkness gave way to a narrow crack that let in the glow from one of the railroad signal lights.

We waited anxiously to hear the patrol car's engine again, the one sign that we had escaped getting caught once more. Then came the sound of heavy links dragging.

When the car finally pulled away, we thought we were out of danger. That was when she looked right above my head, and her eyes went wide.

Now, she was taking slow, deliberate breaths, still pulling me toward the stairs. The moment I took my first step, whatever was above me made a slithering sound, followed again by the drag of heavy chain links.

I lost control, my legs shaking like a puppet's.

"Wait," she pleaded in a barely audible whisper, "leave the coin on the floor."

I didn't think twice. Despite my trembling hands, I crouched down and carefully set the shiny object on the ground.

The noise above me stopped dead.

Slowly, we made it to the stairs and back up to the ground floor. We climbed out through one of the broken windows, and then we ran like hell, crossing the tracks without looking back even once.
