Drug addiction is probably one of the most miserable vices a person can have. I know this from experience. Chasing a fleeting euphoria as your body falters under its influence. We all have our excuses, reasons we all let ourselves be destroyed by it. But sometimes, it really is just someone broken trying to fit the pieces with whatever they can use as glue. 

That was my problem. Unfortunately, like many others, I used substances to keep me together. Nothing pharmaceutical, my “glue” was a poison that wrecked my body the more I got used to it.  

Heroin, skag, brown, dope; there’s plenty of names for my poison of choice. 

A rush of dopamine so high, it sends you flying far above your Earthly hell, all until you’re sent crashing back down, tired and numb to the brimstone around you. 

I tried being smart about it. Not taking big doses. Spacing them out. But I always found myself taking just a little bit more, just a little more frequently, until I was slowly consumed by its vicious cycle.

Now quitting is an entirely different thing. While getting into it could’ve been a design of your traumas and/or environment, getting out is a choice entirely of your own. You can have a good support structure that helps direct you towards that choice but it’s always yours to make. We all have our reason for pushing through the fire to once again find peace in sobriety, and for most that’s seeing through its deception and finding you’ve put yourself in a hell of your own making.   
You just want out. Not because it just destroys you, but everything around you. I wish I could say that was my reason, that I scorched and froze myself, through aches and tremors; for a clean and noble purpose.

I quit because of *that place*. That terrible trap, through that crack in that damned wall. Fear and guilt are what made me quit, their cold talons on my heart stronger than any pain going cold turkey could bring. Anything to put me as far away from that place that I could possibly be.

Even now though, I feel it calling to me. Paradise keeps sending me visions of hell, clawing its way deep into my psyche.

My therapist suggests that I talk about it, but I can’t say anything about it to them. So I’m writing it down here, for strangers to see. I’m hoping, praying, that getting it off my chest helps bring me some closure and alleviates some of the stress I’m going through. This is my confession.

It all started on a Friday night in late October one year. I had just got my paycheck from my dead end job and I was itching for a fix. I was meeting my dealer, Randy at our usual meetup spot. Deep in the woods nearby town, at an old abandoned wood mill. I liked the spot because it was just on the outskirts of town and no law enforcement ever thought to go there. I sat at the stairs in a huge open area of the building. I held myself for warmth as the wind hit me. My jacket did only so much against the bitter chill of night. 

As the wind subsided I checked my phone. 9:12 p.m, no notifications. Footsteps approaching me quelled the impatience that was beginning to form.

“Hey sorry I’m late dude, had to make a stop for another client along the way.” Randy said as he walked over to the stairs. He was wearing a bright orange hoodie almost a size too big, worn out jeans, and a grey beanie over his bald head. He scratched at his ginger beard.

“It’s fine, but let’s be quick about this. It's cold, I’m tired, and right now I’m just ready to go home.” I responded, dragging myself to my feet. A subtle ache began to settle in my body while nausea slowly crept in.

“Alright fine, but first…tryna spark up with me?” Randy pulled a cigarillo packed with weed as he responded. I simply shrugged and nodded. “Sweet shit dude. Lets go upstairs and walk around, I’m feeling a bit restless.” he said, producing a sharp inhale through his nose. 

We trekked upstairs while he lit it and let out a few puffs. We walked aimlessly through the halls as we passed it to each other and talked about how our lives were going. Although I hate to admit it, Randy was one of my only friends at the time. We’ve known each other since highschool, and although we didn’t talk much then, we ended up being pretty close friends once he was my plug.

I never asked what got him into selling, and he never asked why I bought what I did. I think that’s what I liked most about him. I knew he grew up poor in an abusive household, and I’m sure he had a good idea of my grief. He had his demons, and I had mine. We respected that.

We started lapping the building in our conversation, tracing long trails of smoke behind us. The layout upstairs is essentially 4 hallways joined together along the perimeter of the building, essentially making a big rectangle if you were to look at it from a sky view. So we would just continue straight until we’d hit a corner and head right, down another hallway.

Moisture dripped down from what remained of the ceiling, the moon glared at me through the large holes that formed in it. As we walked, I admired the graffiti work on the walls amidst conversation. Whenever we passed a window, I’d look out and stare at the army of trees that surrounded the building. At some point along our third lap of the building, we were walking down one hall when something stood out as we were ready to round a corner. 

“What the fuck is that dude?” Randy asked as he squinted his eyes at it. “That wasn’t there before, right?”

“No..I don’t think it was.” I responded. At the end of the hall, where the corner was supposed to shepherd us to the right, a path to the left sat. It wasn’t a corner, it was an intersection now. “You better not have laced us, man.” I said. 

“No, dude. I wouldn’t do that.” Randy scoffed. I shot him a glance with a furrowed brow. “I swear dude, on my brother’s life.” He said as he walked ahead to lead the investigation.

“Don’t say shit like that.” I snapped at him. He ignored me and continued toward the intersection. He peeked around the left corner before shooting me a “check this out” head wave. I sighed and trudged to him. 

When I got to the intersection, I stared down the path on the left. It was a simple empty hallway, leading to a wall. On the wall, a large crack ran from the ceiling all the way down to the floor. It was about 2 inches thick and unfathomably deep. Deep pools of pitch black peered out from its depths. 

Moonlight seeped from the decomposing roof like a waterfall, illuminating the words: “Gate to Paradise” spray painted in bubble font over the fissure. They were colored in a purple-to-green gradient and accompanied by different crudely drawn arrows pointing towards the center of the large crack. 

“What the fuck dude?” Randy asked as he turned to look at me. 

“I don’t know.” I said, wincing at the aches that started to settle, becoming more apparent. Strangely though,  it felt like there was some magnetic pull attempting to chorale me towards the wall. “Listen, how bout I just give you the cash and I can just get my skag and go home.” 

Randy ignored me. As if in a trance, he simply walked towards the wall with a puzzled expression. He moved slowly and intentionally.

“Hey, did you hear me?”

He didn’t respond, instead he stopped dead in front of the stone fracture and lifted his hand,  stuck his arm towards the anomaly , jutted it forwards, and…

It disappeared. Not entirely, rather it looked like at some point his arm stopped being 3 dimensional and fractured into a 2 dimensional being to squeeze into the crack.

“What the fuck?” I asked. I was so shocked that I didn’t realize I had already journeyed deep enough into the hallway to see the whole thing. “Does… it hurt?”

“N-no. No, it just feels like open air. I…I can go deeper too. I…” he paused as his mind began to lag behind his words. “…I kinda want to.”

Without warning he charged into the crack, twisting and bending flat as he disappeared into its abyss. 



Without thinking, I put out my arm. I wasn’t scared or even curious. It was like I was spontaneously being spurred on by Randy’s march into unknown lands. It twisted and contorted between dimensions.

Like Randy said, no pain-just empty air. It was noticeably warmer though. It had that hospitable and inviting heat that comes from a fireplace during winter. I pushed forward as the heat enveloped me along with new sensations. I was rushed with an overwhelming peace of mind and every ounce of pain in my body was instantly soothed. When I was all the way through, my head was swelling with a sense of complete zen and quiet joy. 

Randy and I stood in a hallway, almost identical to the one we came from. Well, if you stretch the definition of “almost”.



The moon bled crimson light into the hall, dazzling against a slight fog that had settled in. Outside a nearby window the sky itself was enveloped with a black ink that cast dark shadows on the trees, stretching an abyss far beyond the horizon. A low, rhythmic hum filled the air with an unnaturally calm ambience. 

The dimensions of the building weren’t adding up either.

The paintings were the real stand out. Loads of character paintings scattered across floors, the walls, and what remained of the roof above. All different art styles and renditions; graffiti, old-timey cartoons, 8-bit, caricatures…the list was endless. Paintings of settings were littered amongst them and arrows and symbols scattered anywhere there was room. 

As a work of art alone, the collage was impressive. That’s not what stood out though. I only needed a glance to see they were moving.

Characters pranced and played together, speaking through speech bubbles that would form out of their mouths. A rendition of Starry Night blew harsh winds across the stone wall while stars twinkled in its sky. Arrows raced each other across the halls, symbols twitched and danced, all never leaving their concrete canvas. It was as if they were animated by some high definition projector that was just out of sight. 

Exchanging glances, we both knew we had no words. My thoughts could barely collect themselves through the rush of serenity from the air, the impossibility of the moving art simply scrambled them.

I took one step, Randy followed my lead, and we began to slowly pace the halls. We enjoyed the sensations of peace, we marveled at the living art, and we journeyed through familiar hallways with a newfound ignorance. We shared no words in Paradise on our initial taste of its fruit.

A cartoon fox chased a squirrel with a mallet in some slapstick shenanigans on the ceiling. Arrows played tag on the walls. A graffiti alien played catch with a hyper-realistic dog on the floor. 

“I feel like I could die happy right now dude. I don’t care how this is happening, but this feels great!” Randy finally said as we rounded a corner. 

“”…yeah”, I mustered in response. Two thoughts were coming to the forefront of my mind: 

1. I’ve known Randy for years now and somehow I’m only just now realizing how much he says dude  
2.I have never felt such profound bliss. 

“I’m certain we aren’t laced, but I couldn’t give a rational explanation for any of this.”

We were preparing to round another corner when, “Whoa, wait dude.” Randy threw his arm in front of me when we got to the end of the hall. “What’s this?” he asked, pointing dead ahead of us.

It was just like the entrance to paradise. Hidden in the shadows, a hallway that turned a corner into an intersection. It was incredibly dark though, the ceiling above it was completely intact so no moonlight cut through it. In the center of the intersection, another painting sat.

In the same bright letters that led us here: “Limits of Paradise”. Underneath them two more paintings. Green numbers that read 9:40, then 9:39, then 9:38, 9:37,… it was counting down. Next to it, a crudely made smiley face winking on a loop like a gif. 

“We’re being timed.” I said, staring at our time being shaved away. 

“I can’t see shit down this hallway, but I think it’s a dead end?” Randy said, peering down the new path. “Hold on, ye-yeah it’s a dead end. There’s something over there too…”

“Can you tell what it is?” I responded.

“Just some more art I think. But it’s huge, and dark..” 

I walked over to him as he examined it further, “The hell do you mean dark?” 

When I got to him, I stared down the hallway myself. “Why are you peeking over the corner like a peeping Tom, let’s just go over and look.” I said. 

“”I don’t know, just don’t feel like going in there. It’s like it’s the only place here where I don’t feel calm.” He said. “Hey wait, there’s something else there. Yeah it’s those bright ass letters.” 

Looking into the depths of the corridor, I sensed what he was talking about. I could feel my serenity get sapped away as I stood next to the new hallway. The low hum was fading and was being replaced by the echo of perspiration dripping onto the chamber floor. I looked around and saw that no other paintings sat in the area besides the timer and the smiley face. In fact, as I watched the paintings down the hall a behaviour they were exhibiting was becoming clear to me.

They were avoiding it.

I took another step over to its edge, the large shadow it cast caressed my feet. Deep into its depths a wall sat . On it something massive and dark, like it was etched into the wall. Above it that same gradient bubble font broke through the darkness to read: “The Gatekeeper”

I looked back at the timer, “8:15”. 

“I don’t wanna find out what happens when that timer counts down to zero.” I said, watching its silent countdown towards zero.

“Way ahead of you, dude.” Randy said. He pulled out his phone and with a few taps on the screen, a timer was set for 5 minutes.

“Come on dude, let’s enjoy it while we can.”

So, we did. The farther we walked away from the intersection, the farther back in our minds it sat. Serenity once again washed over us and we were helpless against its influence. We walked the halls and explored the upper level of the building. As we marched, I noted more styles of paintings. Massive castle settings, with streaks of lightning that lit up the wall when it struck. Art of different people interacting through speech bubbles as they talked about their day. A nun and a hooded figure throwing tomatoes at each other with little onomatopoeia scribblings of the word *splat!* Popping up every time they hit each other. Every now and then, I’d come across an abstract painting of a humanoid caught in a loop of crying big, bright blue tears that would form a puddle at their feet. 

We entertained ourselves with the skits of some of the characters and the Gatekeeper left our minds until his phone began to buzz. We found our way back to the crack, and slipped through just as we came in. Upon leaving, the cold night air immediately snapped me back into my senses. Familiar pains settled in as my body itched with subtle aches. I waved Randy goodbye and began my way towards the stairs.

“Whoa, hold up dude. We still have a transaction to uphold.” Randy said as he dangled a plastic bag above his head.

“Oh…yeah” I said, shamefully walking over. I completely forgot the reason we even met up here. I fumbled in my pocket and slipped him a wad of cash. 

After our exchange, I paused for a moment before asking, “Same time tomorrow?” 

There was no hesitation in his reply, “Yeah, for sure.”

Just like that, Paradise had made itself just a normal part of our lives. Even when I huffed vapors from a dirty spoon that night my thoughts were still stuck with that place. I don’t know why, but I ended up drawing a layout of the building.

The factory is essentially a big uniform rectangle. On the bottom side of the rectangle, the stairs sit in the middle pointing towards the rectangle’s interior. At the top left corner of the building’s layout, another path that juts to the left takes you to paradise. If you drew it right; the path to paradise is up the stairs, take a right, then another, and finally a left at the intersection 

In paradise, the basic layout is essentially the same, with just a few things out of place. This explained why the dimensions felt off. The only difference is that the stairs are on the top side of the rectangle. On the bottom right corner, another intersection forms to meet the Gatekeeper. I don’t know why I’m explaining this, I guess even now my thoughts are still there. 

I never really left, not entirely, not since *that* night. Talking about it dredges up those memories, reignites that magnetic field that calls to me. I’ll keep sharing my story though, it helps alleviate my stress. Things have been getting rough lately, especially with him talking to me.

He’s been whispering to me through the bathroom door.
