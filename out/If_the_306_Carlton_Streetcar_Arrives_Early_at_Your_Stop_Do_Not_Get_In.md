I want to give you a bit of context first.

Recently, I started working early morning shifts at my restaurant job, which meant taking the 306 night streetcar along Carlton. It’s officially scheduled to hit my stop around 3:15 AM, but the Carlton car has a bad habit of running a few minutes early. To make sure I never miss it and end up late for work, I usually head out to the stop by 3:00 AM.

Typically, the streetcar pulls up sometime between 3:12 and 3:15 AM. But today was different. I got to the stop at 3:00 AM, and by 3:03 AM, the streetcar was already screeching to a halt in front of me. I was surprised—it was almost twelve minutes ahead of schedule. Not wanting to risk missing it and standing out in the freeze, I just boarded.

Inside, the car was practically empty, save for a single passenger sitting all the way in the back. He was wearing an oversized black overcoat, his face completely swallowed by shadows.

I took my usual spot right at the very front. You can call it paranoia, but waiting for transit in Toronto at three in the morning makes you cautious. You don't want to get caught off guard by a random mugging or someone swinging a dirty needle. Sitting up front lets me inspect everyone who boards and exits at every stop. So, I settled into my seat just like any other day.

About fifteen minutes into the ride, we were approaching my stop. As usual, I stood up and hit the stop-request button on the window frame.

The streetcar didn't slow down.

I figured the driver just missed his mark, so I hit the button again for the next stop. Again, nothing. He blew right past that one, too.

That meant another seven or eight hundred meters added to my walk, and I was pissed. I slammed the button a third time, muttering under my breath, but the streetcar just kept rolling. He had missed three full stops by now, adding almost a kilometer and a half to my commute.

Exasperated, I walked straight up to the front to see what the hell the driver was doing. I leaned over to look inside the cabin, and my stomach completely dropped.

There was no driver.

The cabin was entirely empty, yet the streetcar was still gliding down the dark street on its own.

Panic hit me instantly. I couldn't comprehend what I was looking at or how the car was even running. I raced to the doors and tried to physically wedge them open, but they wouldn't budge. I started slamming every button I could find, but nothing responded. Desperate, I tried forcing my way into the driver’s cabin, but the door was locked tight. Nothing in the entire car was responding except for the stop-request chime.

For the next five minutes, I ran up and down the aisle in a full-blown frenzy, trying to make sense of what was happening. Despite how long and open the streetcar was, a suffocating wave of claustrophobia slammed into me.

Then, without warning, the streetcar ground to a sudden, heavy halt.

Every single light inside cut out at once, plunging the car into pitch darkness. It was pitch black outside, too. The engine noise vanished, leaving a heavy, terrifying silence—so absolute I could hear my own heart hammering against my ribs.

For thirty seconds, complete darkness and silence were all that existed.

Then, the overhead lights flickered back on.

Outside, the streetscape looked completely familiar—the exact same stop where I had boarded. I glanced down at my watch; it was only 3:04 AM.

*Fuck,* I thought.

I’ve been exhausted these past two weeks, barely getting any sleep between my high-protein routine and pulling brutal shifts at the kitchen. To drop off and dream all of that in less than a minute was fucking crazy. I figured the sheer lack of sleep was finally catching up to me, making me hallucinate.

I was still sitting right in my seat at the front. Although it felt insane in my head, everything in the real world seemed fine. *It was just a dream,* I told myself. *Just a quick sleep-deprivation hallucination. As long as that shit doesn't happen again, I'm good. Back to real life.*

Another fifteen minutes passed. My actual stop came into view again. I stood up and hit the stop-request button just like usual.

And again, the same fucking thing. The streetcar didn't slow down.

*Fucking shit. Déjà vu.*

I tried telling myself the driver just missed the mark, so I hit the button again. It skipped that stop, too. A wave of total dread washed over me—it felt like my entire body was shutting down instantly. My hands began to shake as I forced myself to push the button a third time. Same thing. It wouldn't stop.

I finally grasped the terrifying gravity of the situation I was in. Driven by pure compulsion, I walked up to the driver's cabin and looked inside. Nobody there.

I couldn't move. Complete shock locked my body in place. I just stood there, paralyzed, for the next three or four minutes.

The lights went out again.

Complete, pitch-black darkness—no sensory stimuli whatsoever. It lasted for another thirty seconds before the lights flickered back on.

3:04 AM.

Outside, it was the exact same scene—the very same stop where I had first boarded.

Amidst all the panic, I realized I had forgotten to check one crucial detail: the guy sitting in the back when I first got on. He hadn't requested a stop once. Nobody had entered or exited the car because the doors had never actually opened. There was no one else on board except him.

And then a sickening detail hit me. Wasn't he sitting all the way in the last row when I boarded?

How was he two rows closer now?

He hadn't made a single sound. He had stayed completely still through all of this crazy shit, not moving an inch. I was too terrified to walk back there, but I gathered every drop of courage I had left and slowly forced my feet down the aisle toward him.

He sat two rows behind my usual spot, slumped in the shadows. Where his face should have been, there was only pitch darkness—an absolute void tucked inside the collar of his coat. Under the dim yellow overhead lights, the rest of him was terrifyingly wrong. He wore thick black leather boots, and extending from his sleeves were disproportionately long, lean arms in black gloves. They hung almost to the floor, still as stone.

He couldn't be human. Whatever nightmare was happening to me was tied directly to whatever *he* was. But how do you interact with something that feels entirely outside of reality?

Have you ever been trapped in a room so black and small that the walls seem to press against your chest, leaving you so helpless you can feel your entire world crumbling around you? That was the exact sensation choking me.

My stomach churned into a tight, violent knot. Hot bile rose in the back of my throat, and a cold, clammy sweat broke out across my forehead and neck. Every breath I pulled in felt thin and useless, like trying to inhale underwater.

I ran for my life. I sprinted all the way to the front of the car faster than I’ve ever moved in my entire life, swearing to myself that I would never turn around to look back there again. It scared the absolute shit out of me. My body couldn't stop shaking, and out of nowhere, tears just started streaming down my face.

Like clockwork, the same cycle repeated. About fifteen minutes passed, blowing straight through my stop. I knew that around 3:23 or 3:24 AM, the exact same power-down was going to hit.

*There has to be something I’m missing,* I thought. *I know I’m trapped in a loop, but there has to be a way out.*

I pressed my face against the glass, frantically searching the streets outside for any detail, any clue. That’s when I noticed where the streetcar always came to a dead stop during those thirty seconds of darkness.

It was right beside Allan Gardens.

After all these years of hearing people talk crazy shit about that place—all the dark history and urban legends surrounding those shadowy glass greenhouses—it couldn't just be a coincidence.

And boom. The lights flickered off again. Thirty seconds of absolute, suffocating darkness.

*Boom.* The lights snapped back on. 3:04 AM.

Even though every instinct in my body screamed against it, even though I just wanted this nightmare to end, I looked back.

He was right behind my row now.

I collapsed into a seat, completely paralyzed. A crushing wave of despair hit me—I felt like giving up. Every attempt to force the doors, every button I slammed, every frantic escape route had gone nowhere. Nothing worked.

I don't know what happens next. I think this is where I die.

I tried everything. I had almost two hours inside this nightmare to figure something out, to find a way out, and I couldn't do anything. Every single thing I attempted failed. So if you're reading this, consider it my only warning:

If you're ever waiting on the curb in the dead of night and a 306 streetcar pulls up ahead of schedule—empty, quiet, and out of nowhere—do not get on. I don't care how cold it is, I don't care how tired you are, or how badly you just want to get home. Just step back and wait for the official, scheduled car.

And if you do somehow make the same mistake I did and end up on board, reading this from inside… don't waste your time doing what I did. Don't waste your energy slamming against locked doors or screaming into an empty driver's cabin. Look for patterns immediately. Try to find anything, *anything* to break the cycle before it locks you in. Because once it catches you in its loop, it slowly strips away every single option you have until there is nothing left to do except wait.

I've accepted my fate now. I can hear the brakes grinding outside, right beside the pitch-black trees of Allan Gardens. The lights are starting to flicker.

If you ever see an empty streetcar rolling down an empty street at 3:00 AM, just know it might not be a streetcar at all.

It's just waiting for someone like me to step inside.
