I live alone in a small house in the middle of the street in a happy little neighborhood. Nothing strange ever happens here. Most houses have families with at least two kids, a picket fence, and a husband that’s screwing the nanny. The usual suburban slice of paradise or whatever. 

Really, if anything is out of the ordinary in this neighborhood, it’s me. I'm a single woman, no pets, I drive for DoorDash by day and write horny “literature” for thirsty housewives at night. 

I’m usually driving for about six hours straight and then spending about another five hours writing each day. It may seem excessive, but if you consider the fact only about thirty minutes worth of my writing is NOT mindless coffee drinking and unreadable dogshit, I do what I must. Because of this I don’t have time to cook, even if I knew how. My father left when I was a child and my mother could burn box mac and cheese. I’m absolutely useless in a kitchen; however, there is one kitchen gadget I was always particularly good with. 

The microwave! 

The great and powerful microwave was my savior most days. If I was in too much of a hurry to wait in the drive-thru I could just pop a burrito or Hot Pocket in the microwave for a few minutes and I was good to go. 

Yep, cooking was actually pretty easy with the help of the microwave, until today. 

It was a fairly normal spring day. I had just enough orders to keep me going straight on through lunch, though, so I decided to stop home for a Hot Pocket. Ham and cheese, my favorite! 

I popped it in the microwave, started the timer, then leaned my back against the counter. I took out my phone and began checking Instagram when I heard it. 

It was quiet, so quiet I had to strain my ears to hear but there, just above the buzz of the microwave, I heard it. It sounded like moaning? No, singing! And I swear it was getting louder. I looked out my kitchen window to see if any kids driving by were playing their music too loud, but the street was dead. All the kids were in school; and, judging from the empty driveways, the parents were probably at work. 

When I turned back to the microwave I saw there was only ten seconds left on the timer. Just before the alarm went off I was almost certain the sounds were coming from inside the microwave. I shook away the thought and took out my Hot Pocket then got back to work. 

Later that night while working overtime on my "second job", the previously mentioned "literature," I decided to heat up some instant coffee. Unlike most other people in the twenty-first century, I didn't have an instant pod machine. I put plain ground coffee in my mug, added water, and threw it in the microwave. That's it, no sugar, no cream, just the dark and bitter truth. I did as I usually do, two spoonfuls of coffee grounds in one of my plain white mugs. I had a set of six coffee mugs. Mom only drank wine when she was over and my stepdad has a heart condition. I'm the only one who drinks coffee. Another reminder of how alone I am. 

After adding some warm water to the mug and stirring the grounds, turning it into a dark brown sludge, I popped it in the microwave and let it run. While scrolling through Instagram and admiring all the women that I'll never look like or the famous men I'd probably never be with, I began to hear it. The singing again. It was 1AM! There's no way someone in this neighborhood would risk the fury of the HOA. I leaned my ear towards the microwave, cupping my hand over my ear to hear better. 

I could hear it clearly now, not singing, but chanting, and it was definitely coming from inside my microwave. I peered inside to see if something was wrong with the plate but it was rotating just fine. That's when I noticed what looked like little, tiny people in brown cloaks circled around my coffee mug. I blinked in disbelief then rubbed my bloodshot eyes. 

When I opened them again, I realized what I saw was just coffee droplets on the plate and my coffee was bubbling over the edge, popping and spitting all over the microwave. I quickly swung the door open and grabbed the mug before realizing how hot it was. I yelped and dropped the mug. The cheap ceramic fell onto my stovetop and shattered to bits, splashing all over the stove, walls, and even me. 

I quickly rinsed my hands under cold water to relieve the pain before cleaning the mess and throwing my dirty clothes in the wash. I crashed on the couch about a half hour later. 

On Sunday I ate lunch at a local burger joint then brought home a pizza from Little Caesars so I didn’t use the microwave. I almost hit a black cat on the way home, but it turned out to be a crow and flew off. I decided to chalk it up to bad eyesight and made a mental note to get an appointment with an optometrist. 

Monday morning I decided to have some leftover pizza for breakfast. Unfortunately, I’m not a big “cold pizza” girl so I popped the last two pieces on a plate and threw them in the microwave on sensor reheat. Same as I do for everything. Sometimes I'll have to put something in twice, but it usually works the first time. As always, I took out my phone and began scrolling to squeeze every bit of dopamine I could out of the minute and fifteen seconds it would take to heat my meal. I decided to listen to some music and ignore whatever sounds came from the microwave. 

No sooner had I opened Instagram than the microwave beeped, indicating it was done. I thought it went a bit fast but maybe I had daydreamed away some of the time. I took out the plate and carried it to the couch when I noticed the plate was cold. I poked a slice of pizza with a fingertip and it was cold as well. Perhaps I’d hit the wrong button? I went back to the kitchen, popped the plate back in the microwave, and made sure I hit the right button this time. 

I watched it spin the whole time. This time I was certain the full minute and fifteen seconds had gone by. I removed the plate before it even finished beeping. Still cold! I cursed under my breath, threw the plate back in, and hit the button! One minute! Start! 

One minute…? Hadn’t it been a minute and fifteen before? I looked up at the ceiling thoughtfully as I tried to remember. 

Beep! Beep! Beep! 

It was done already? Again? I swung the door open and stared at the pizza slices sitting lifeless on the plate. I thrust a finger into the cheesy surface and caused a small crater as the sauce squelched under my finger. Freezing cold! I turned to glare at the bright green letters spelling out “HOT MEAL” scrolling along the small screen. Such a brazen lie. 

I decided the microwave must be dying or something, so I begrudgingly downed the frigid slices before heading to work. 

On Tuesday night my mom and stepdad would be over for dinner and board games. Putting the stress of the microwave behind me, I prepare myself for the stress of hosting my parents. As usual, I was sure they'd ask me if I was dating anyone yet. They always did and I always told them the same thing, “Noone serious.” I wasn’t lying. Everyone I had ever been with before had left soon after I’d invited them over. Some would even leave the same day. Maybe it was how my house looked or how I looked. Either way, it got tiring. 

Sure enough that night was no different. Mom was the one who asked this time. Seemed like they took turns. The week before, my stepdad asked. This time I was tired, too tired to hold back. This time I joked, perhaps a little too dryly, “If I want to die alone, I will.” My stepdad put his fork down as my mother pursed her lips, stopping just shy of taking a sip from her wine glass. The silence hung in the air like a fog, begging to be lifted by even a whisper. 

Instead, the silence was broken by a loud and cheerful beep, startling everyone and causing my mom to spill her wine. “Dammit. Good thing it’s white,” my mother exclaimed over a second beep. As my mother began dabbing at the spill I stood up and walked into the kitchen as the third and final beep rang out. 

When I looked at the microwave, I couldn’t believe my eyes. Instead of seeing “HOT MEAL” on the screen I saw “OPEN ME” scrolling across the screen in glowing, green lights. I stared at it, frozen in fear. I remembered the strange sounds I’d heard recently that seemed to come from the microwave. Before I realized it, my hand was on the handle of the microwave door. Just before opening it I caught movement out of the corner of my eye. 

Snapping my attention to the object moving towards me, I nearly leapt out of my skin as my stepfather walked up to me. 

“Heatin' up some joe?” He asked in his chipper, Midwestern accent. 

Catching my breath, I took my hand off the handle and told him that the microwave wasn’t working right. When I looked at the screen again it said 8:56. Just the time. He asked to take a look at it for me but I told him I’d just get a new one tomorrow. I shouldn’t have said that. 

On Wednesday morning I woke up with a pain in my neck and a burrito wrapper on my pillow. When I went to my kitchen to get some Tylenol I saw it. 

In the center of the floor sat a pile of burritos surrounded by a circle of Hot Pockets. If I had a history of sleepwalking I would have found this scene ridiculous and laughed as I cleaned it up. But I don’t sleepwalk. At least I never did before. Something really strange was happening. That’s when I noticed the microwave was running. The timer showed only 0's. I didn’t even look inside before pressing cancel and stopping it. Without thinking I whipped open the door and was greeted by a giant cloud of black smoke. As the room began to fill with smoke I wretched at the smell. Whatever was in the microwave was certainly cooked. 

I let my kitchen air out and cleaned up the Hot Pocket circle then managed to take the very well-done object out of the microwave without throwing up. I prayed it was just a burrito as I threw it in the trash outside. 

It took an entire can of air freshener and a candle to get rid of the smell. 

I don’t think it was a burrito. 

I called in sick and spent the entire day looking for a new microwave. I hoped whatever I experienced or did last night was just a sleepwalking moment. I chalked it up to the stress of having my parents over for dinner and tried to put it behind me. 

To my surprise, after hitting every hardware store in town and nearly running out of gas, I discovered there was a grand total of zero microwaves in stock. 

My mother, now drunk enough from day-drinking to call me, asked me to come over for lunch, but I declined. When I told her why, “I can’t today, Mom. I’m trying to find a new microwave," she seemed taken aback by this, as most sane people would. Her only daughter was turning down free food because of a microwave? 

She compounded this well know fact by saying, “I’ll pay!” 

She paused and I swear I could hear her lips curl into a sneer on the other side of the line as she sweetly slurred, “We can order DoorDash! Weren't you supposed to be working today, sweety?" 

I rolled my eyes and replied, “No thanks, I called in sick. My luck the boss would show up with our order.” 

Mom chuckled and after hearing my conundrum she suggested I check Craigslist or Marketplace. 

“A small, single woman meeting random internet strangers? I like my organs on the inside, thanks.” I told her. 

After a few more polite words, we said our goodbyes and hung up. My mother was probably very concerned about my mental health after that call and I couldn’t blame her. Was a microwave really causing all my troubles? 

I bit the bullet and ordered a new microwave from Home Depot on finance. I even paid extra for it to arrive at the earliest time, Saturday at 9AM. “Just two more days.” I reassured myself. 

I hit McDonald’s on the way home for a quarter pounder and fries. Then I hit the gas station for some wine. The apple didn’t fall far. I’m aware. 

That night I slept restlessly, maybe the wine, or the quarter pounder, or both. I got up to go the bathroom sometime around 3AM and violently emptied my stomach of its wretched contents. After flushing the putrid mess and washing my face I stepped out into the dimly lit hallway and stopped dead in my tracks. 

It was quiet in the house, save for one thing. The soft buzz of the microwave mixed with that ominous chanting I kept trying to ignore. It was too loud to ignore now and growing louder still. For whatever reason I didn't run screaming out of the house; instead, I followed the sound down the hall. I turned the corner as the chanting reached a roaring climax then stopped, sending the house into a suffocating silence, when I stepped into the kitchen. 

I peered through the window of the microwave but only saw a perfectly clean microwave plate, rotating in the yellow light. The timer only read 0s this time and I tried pressing Cancel. It didn’t stop, of course. That's when I noticed the microwave wasn't completely empty. A small pink colored glob was sitting in the center of the plate. I watched it spin for some time, mesmerized as it began to grow and expand. It looked soft but had form and structure. I felt like I’d seen that material before. 

It began to grow sideways, faster now, then curving back over itself to form an S shaped tube. That’s when it struck me why it looked familiar. It was human tissue, specifically, it was the tissue of a small intestine. I reeled back in horror as a slightly larger tube began forming from the roof of the microwave. Within seconds the entire microwave was filled with pinkish-red intestines, slimy and oozing. Gastric acid dripped from the lower seam of the microwave door when it suddenly sprung open. The bloodied glob of tangled intestines fell onto my kitchen floor with an awful slap, like a soaking wet mop. The end of the lower intestine had caught on the oven and dangled limply into the rest of the pile; my oven door smeared by blood and brownish-yellow gastric juices. 

If I hadn’t already thrown up something that looked similar, I’d have certainly emptied my stomach then. That’s when I looked inside the empty microwave. The inside of the microwave looked horrific. The walls and door were coated in a thick, pink membrane that oozed sticky mucus. It pulsated gently as small veins formed in it, dripping with blood as they formed then were quickly healed over and covered by another layer of pinkish-red membrane. 

The whole sight was finally enough to make me wretch, but nothing came up. I gagged on my own saliva and wretched again before noticing the feeling in my lower abdomen. It felt warm and empty. Hesitantly I looked down at my body and saw it. 

Attached to my abdomen was the microwave door; my skin woven into the hinge. Looking through the window of the door, I screamed at what I saw. In place of my organs was a perfectly clean microwave plate, rotating in the yellow light. 

I awoke in my bed with the same empty feeling in my stomach and drenched in sweat. My sheets were all tossed on the floor in a pile and my pillow had fallen off the other side. Gaining my bearings as the early morning sun peaked through my curtains, I realized it was just a dream and the emptiness in my stomach was only hunger now. 

I couldn't take it anymore; I went to the spare room and grabbed the one power tool I owned, a power drill. I stomped into the kitchen and used my newly acquired weapon to begin removing that damned microwave. I'm sure it was just a bad dream, but with everything that had been happening I couldn't take the chance. It was surprisingly heavy; and, in my sleep deprived, hunger induced rage I miscalculated the effort and manpower needed to remove it. Upon loosening the last screw, I lost what little grip I had on it. The microwave crashed into my stovetop with a horrible grating and cracking noise, shattering my glass stovetop. 

"Fuck!" I exclaimed. 

In my frustration, I quickly reached for the microwave to lift it off the stove and examine the damage. Unfortunately, my dumb ass tried to grab the microwave from the bottom edges, forgetting there might be shards of glass under it. Immediately I realized my mistake as a stinging pain tore through my palms. I reeled back from the pain and saw two large shards of glass sticking from jagged holes in each palm. I wailed in pain for what felt like five minutes, tears streaming down my face. 

Once I regained my composure, I realized they weren't deep enough to cause any damage to mobility, so I was able to remove the nearly two-inch long shards very carefully. I let out a pained squeak as I removed the shard from my left palm first and threw it into the sink, spattering blood around the metal surface before following gravity into the drain. I removed the second shard with a whimper and a moan then tossed it beside the other in the sink. 

I cleaned my wounds and covered them with elbow bandages. They stayed on and covered my wounds enough to keep them from bleeding much more, but I knew I would need stitches. Once I stopped shaking, I managed to call my mother and ask if she could take me to the emergency room. I hated asking her, but I knew I couldn't drive myself. After she finished berating me for my carelessness she agreed to take me. Six stitches in each hand and some pain meds later I found myself having breakfast with my mother at a small bakery. 

It had some dumb Gen Z naming conventions to the menu items like the Oh Lawd He Comin' Omelet and the Big Chungus Breakfast Croissant. My mother always tried to "stay hip with the kids" as she put it. Coming out of my medicated stupor, I realize my mother is discussing my love life, again. 

She's talking about a friend's son that is around my age and just moved back to town, suggesting he might be a good fit for me. For whatever reason I found myself agreeing to meet him. I was tired of being alone. So alone that I somehow made a broken microwave out to be a monster. If it wasn't my love life my family was critiquing, it was my eating habits. Perhaps this was just my mind telling me that I was getting too old to handle processed foods anymore. 

I listened intently as my mother described the man I'd be having over for dinner that night. She had it all planned out like a military commander. At a quarter to six my "date" would arrive with his mother and after initial greetings she would leave, allowing us to drink some wine and talk. The wine would be courtesy of my mother, and once we'd "gotten to know each other," mom said my stepfather would stop by around six with a fresh, hot lasagna and his famous seasoned vegetables. He was the only good cook in the family. It was partially why my mother married him. At the mention of my stepdad, my mother gets a call from him as if on cue. It seems while I was out with my mother he was helping clean up the mess I had left in my kitchen.  

I can't quite hear him when my mother answers the phone, so she relays to me that he cleaned up the broken glass and had his brother help load up and dispose of the broken oven. Mom promises she'll get me a new one if the date goes well, half joking. I shrug as she gets a confused look on her face. 

"What?! I'll try!" I mumbled defensively. 

She shook her head and said, "No, honey, I'm just confused." She paused then continued, "If you wanted to throw out the microwave, why did you put it back up?" 

I stared at her blankly at first then slowly contorted my face into confusion as what she said didn't make sense. I'd left the microwave on the stove; I swore I had. If I didn't put it up, then who did? 

My mother, as if reading my mind, scoffs, "Well you had to, no one besides your dad and I have a key." 

As soon as she says this, I feel a small shift in the back of my brain. It's subtle, but feels like a gate opened, allowing me to begin remembering what truly happened that morning. 

I woke up and decided to throw out the microwave, resulting in it breaking my stovetop and injuring my hands. That much I remembered. Then I began to remember more. After removing the shards of glass from my hands, I didn't immediately bandage them; instead, I carefully placed my hands, dripping with blood still, on either side of the microwave and lifted it back into place. Everything I did seemed robotic, like I was unbothered by the bloody and painful gashes in my palms as I mounted the microwave back on the wall. 

"I guess I did. I'm sorry. Can he still get rid of it?" I asked my mother hesitantly. 

She turned back to her phone and commanded her husband to take it to the dump. He agreed but needed to get his own drill since mine was coated in blood. In the meantime, I wasn't going home. I'm sure it was just some kind of delirium brought on by the injuries and blood loss, but it still wasn't sitting right with me. Either way, I asked my mother to go dress shopping with me until my stepfather was done. She agreed, obviously, and we spent a few hours shopping. 

Five-thirty came around and I was nearly ready to meet the man my mother had swooned over at breakfast. My mother texted me around this time to inform me that my date would not be arriving with his mother. I was told it was due to a "baking emergency." 

When my date arrived, I had already downed about two glasses of wine and was feeling quite comfortable for once. He was easy to talk with and very polite. He listened intently, even when I overshared and didn't seem to mind it. I truly felt like he might be worthy of a second date, given he found me agreeable. Just before my stepfather was due to arrive, I realized I had a bit too much wine and needed to empty my bladder. I politely excused myself as I dashed off to the bathroom. While in the bathroom I heard the doorbell ring. My stepdad, timely as ever. 

I heard the front door open then the muffled voices of my stepdad and date. After a moment I heard the door close and some shuffling as my date moved around. I washed my hands then stepped into the hallway and adjusted my bra in the hall mirror when I heard my date call from the kitchen. 

"Do I wanna know what happened to your stove?" He asked with a chuckle. 

I began telling him about how I was attempting to throw out my broken microwave until I turned into the kitchen doorway. His look of confusion stops me midsentence before I turn my eyes to the object hanging on the wall, right next to me. 

"Shit!" I hiss. "I thought my stepdad threw it out." 

My date smiled, concern growing in his eyes. "Want me to take it off your hands?" 

I scoffed then realized he was serious and straightened up, adjusting my tone. "You don't have to. Besides, my drill isn't uh... clean..." I said with a shy smile, showing him my bandaged palms. I explained the rest of the events of my morning as we ate. 

He found the story charming and even said my mother sounded like an alcoholic poser. This gave me a good chuckle before my bladder reminded me again that I drank just about as much as her. I excused myself to go use the restroom for the second time and darted down the hall. I hear my date moving around, probably cleaning up the dishes while I'm doing my business. How gentlemanly! Suddenly I felt a bubbling sensation in my stomach as it started to ache. At this point I'm beginning to think the wine has it in for me. I curled over in pain and began to push when I heard a knock on the door followed by my dates, gentle, yet deep voice asking if I had any coffee. I told him I had some in the cabinet over the coffee mugs. He thanked me then apologized for his intrusion before walking away. Mildly shellshocked, I returned to my business before realizing the key element to making coffee in my house. 

The microwave. 

I panicked and quickly cleaned up before darting down the hall and around the kitchen corner. I must have looked insane; dress hanging askew on my shoulders, face frazzled and red from the alcohol; my hair starting to come loose from my bun and hanging in my eyes. Blowing a strand of hair out of my face, I expected to see some horrific microwave monster, fearing it was all another dream. The man standing in my kitchen jumped at the sight of me then smiled, managing to somehow not spill the mug of steaming hot coffee in his hands. 

Realizing his mistake, he began to apologize, "oh, I'm sorry. I had thought you said your microwave wasn't working, but this coffee sure is hot." He nods gently and extends the coffee mug out in offering. 

I shift my face into a cheerful smile and thank him as I take the warm mug from him, "Well thank you. It's been causing me trouble, so I just stopped using it." 

I didn't dare tell him about my dream or the chanting I'd heard. I wanted him to think I was still somewhat sane.  Especially after I just sprinted into the kitchen like it was the hundred-yard dash from our schooldays. He didn't seem to mind though and after he heated up his own mug without any trouble, we sat together on my uncomfortably small couch. We discussed our youth and our least favorite class: math. I found out he was only one-year older than me, and we'd had the same horrible math teacher. After bonding over our shared hatred of the American school system and bullies, I began to feel my stomach rumble again. Was it the Olympic sprint this time, or the coffee? Either way, I excused myself for the third time that evening and trotted off to the bathroom. 

On the way back from the bathroom, I heard the microwave running. That low buzzing sound nearly turned my stomach again, but thankfully it was no longer accompanied by chanting. Steeling myself, I chose not to run this time. I figured my date was simply warming his coffee. We had lost track of time while talking and I had noticed my coffee beginning to get cold before I'd gotten up. That was the most likely answer why it would be running and assuring myself of this I sat back down on my couch and waited. I noticed my mug was not on my coffee table and smiled as I imagined the man in my kitchen politely reheating my coffee as well. I began to actually daydream about what to do for our next date when the microwave beeped. 

I jumped a little then turned towards the kitchen doorway with a smile, expecting to see that tall, blonde man of my dreams walk back in with warm coffee. I waited a second, then what felt like a minute. I finally realized I hadn't heard a sound from the kitchen since the beeping ended. It was dead silent; not the door opening as you'd expect, no feet shifting on the linoleum tile, not even a sigh. A chill ran down my spine as I began to fear I was alone in the house; or, worse, I really was dreaming again. I shook the thought away, replacing it with more likely possibilities. He was probably outside getting some air, or on a call. God forbid if he was smoking, but I figured my mom would have filtered out smokers. 

I sat in silence for another three minutes before standing and walking to my front door. It was locked so there was no way he was out front. I turned around and stared at the kitchen doorway. The house fell eerily silent. I broke it by marching into the kitchen, ensuring my footsteps made a resounding thump with each step; my arms pumping back and forth aggressively, fists balled tight.  Walking straight past the microwave I spotted a mug on the counter that was still steaming slightly. Curiously, my back door was unlocked but when I opened it and peaked out, I found no one there. 

I figured my date had simply had enough of my bowel problems and decided to run out the back door. Defeated, I locked the door and turned to face the sink when I noticed an empty coffee mug sitting in the drain. It was still slightly wet from having been recently washed. Small water droplets fell from the rim of the upside-down mug onto the plastic tray beneath, sounding like explosions in comparison to the still silence. As if trying to catch it off guard, I quickly turned to the microwave and whipped it open to find nothing inside. 

Nothing was inside, except for that damned glass plate. I shivered recalling my nightmare when I noticed the plate was still spinning slightly. 

"Faulty piece of shit!" I yelled as I slammed the door shut, then grabbed the power cable and yanked it from the wall. The plate stopped spinning and for a moment I felt some peace and calm wash over me. I decided to finish my coffee while eating Uncrustables and watching episodes of my favorite true crime podcast. Giving my date the benefit of the doubt, I began to hope it wasn't an emergency and someone in his family was in life-threatening condition. Odd as it may be, I was comforted by the thought and fell asleep shortly thereafter. 

While I slept, I had a dream of our next date. We were at some sort of theme park and playing all sorts of carnival games. It was at a ball toss game where my date won me a large prize. We were thrilled, jumping up and down when I turned to see the prize in the park member's hands. 

It was a brand, new microwave. That was when I realized the entire park was microwave themed. Toys, drinks, food - all microwave themed as the world seemed to spin around me, my eyes darting from microwave shaped collectible mugs in guest's hands, microwave plushies, merchandise, even shirts with microwaves on them. There were even clowns. Clowns in the usual multicolored baggy clothes and big shoes, except for one major difference. They all had large microwave masks on their heads. 

Sickly yellow lights emanated from horrific LED smiles and menacing eyes built into the window. The door of the mask opened and closed as they laughed, revealing only rotating, glass plates where their heads should be. I could feel my chest tightening as I began to breathe faster, panic setting in when I turned to my date and his face had been replaced by a horrific microwave mask. He began laughing at me behind the glowing yellow eyes and jagged teeth when the door of the mask swung open to reveal his severed head on the plate. It spun to face me as blood dripped from his neck. 

Expecting to see his cold, dead stare, I was greeted only by empty, bloodied eye sockets. His mouth hung agape; teeth still bright and white, a stark contrast to the dark red orbs that sat between them. A wooden stick was shoved through the orbs like a kabob. The microwave table stopped with his head facing me when his jaw loosened, and the orbs rolled onto the table. I didn't need to look, but I did. Aimed directly at me now; I knew what I was staring at, or what was staring at me. The man's gentle, blue eyes sat lifeless now, skewered on a stick like some kind of appetizer. 

I woke up screaming and realized I'd fallen asleep on my couch listening to my podcast. It was an episode about a well-documented cannibal and serial killer. It was no wonder I had bad dreams, honestly. I picked my empty coffee mug up off the floor and washed it. 

I looked at the microwave and told myself it was just a dream, but I couldn't shake that uneasy feeling it gave me. It had to go. Channeling every bit of strength in me and gripping it firmly I ripped the microwave straight out of the wall. A couple of screws and some pieces of drywall fell into the empty space on the floor where my oven used to be. I could feel my hands reminding me of yesterday's incident as I quickly transported it out my front door, fumbling with the lock and almost dropping the damn microwave in the process. Finally getting the door open, I burst out of my house and made a beeline for my garbage can. 

The lid was open, so I chucked it in and dusted my hands off over the device. Feeling like my problems were finally about to be gone, I closed the lid and looked up at the sky. I felt the early morning breeze against my face as first light showed itself through the fog. That's when I noticed it. A brand-new Lexus parked in my driveway next to my shitty Subaru. The same Lexus my date owned. The one he drove to my house, parked in my driveway, and hopefully was sleeping off a hangover inside of now. 

I walked over to the passenger side of the car and peered through the windows. It seemed empty, save for a grande Starbucks coffee mug with a bit of iced coffee still in it. It used to be iced coffee; the ice had all melted by now. There was no other sign of life in the car and when I pulled on the handle, it was unlocked to my surprise. I leaned in, not expecting to see anyone, and was unfortunately correct. I closed the car door and quickly went back inside, slamming the door shut and locking it. I practically jumped onto my couch then started to play another episode of my podcast, cranking the volume until it was so loud, I couldn't even think. I finally fell asleep again somehow and thankfully this time I didn't have any dreams. 

I was awoken by the ringing of my phone and saw I had slept until 2PM. I answered the phone groggily, managing to groan a, "Hello?" My eyes had glossed over the name, but I knew who it was by that sickly sweet voice. 

"Good morning, honey! Did you have a good time? I heard your date never made it home last night. Good news, I suppose?" 

I shook my head then realized what it would look like if I told her the truth. He disappeared and his car was still in my driveway. Before I knew it, the cops would probably be digging up my backyard while I sat handcuffed in the back of a cruiser. 

I lied and used my mother's own tone against her. I replied, "Yeah, we actually slept in. He's just hopped in the shower, so if you don't mind, I'm going to..." 

Thankfully I didn't have to finish the sentence, and my mother saved me the embarrassment by quickly agreeing to let me go, mumbling something about my stepfather needing help before wishing me good luck and hanging up. 

Relieved that my mother believed me for once, I leaned back and sunk into the couch slightly. I began to drift off when I heard it. A sound I thought I would never hear again. A sound that once brought me comfort but now brought only a cold shiver down my spine. A sound I knew now that I couldn't escape. A sound that would live with me forever. A sound that haunts me even as I write this. Echoing in my head for eternity. 

Beep! Beep! Beep!
