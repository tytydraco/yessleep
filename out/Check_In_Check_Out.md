I’ve stayed in a lot of hotels: hotels, inns, lodges, cabins, bed and breakfasts, and many other off-road overnighters. A few of them claimed to have some kind of connection to the paranormal. So they say to the tourists. Not that I had seen anything. I’d never seen anything of the supernatural. I’d never even seen a ghost, or a phantom, or a spectre. In fact, the only kind of spirit I had seen were the ones in the hotel mini bars along my journey. I mean, it’s not from a lack of trying. For the first couple of places I’d stayed at, I got nothing, not a peep. At least nothing that can’t be explained away. And after a while, after the many places I’d stayed at for many years, I was starting to wonder if it was me. And it’s not like I wanted to do it for a podcast, a YouTube channel or a bestselling book of top haunted places in the world, although I did quite handsomely writing one, regardless. But I was afraid of death. And I wanted to, I needed to, know if there’s something else out there that can’t be measured by any natural rubric. I imagined a lot of people do. So they do everything in their power to will things into existence. After the 20 or so places I went to, I was beginning to think these believers would psyche themselves into seeing and feeling things that simply… weren’t there. So I didn’t have very high hopes when I booked a weekend stay at the Worthington Hotel (name has been changed). 

The dates I was staying were planned around Halloween, somewhat of a tradition for me whenever I stay in these spooky sanctums. But at this point it felt like a chore, like putting on your shoes or brushing your teeth. But nevertheless, like Christmas with your relatives, I was determined to stay there and hopefully find some satisfaction along the way.   
This particular hotel had dozens of reported spirits, freak accidents, a few deaths, a couple of murders. Way too many for me to delve into when I was researching the place. But when I called up to book my room, I was surprised to find just how prolific this place actually was.

In my experience, there are two types of people that run these places: those that don’t advertise the place as haunted and would sooner bury its legacy so they don’t put off guests; and those who fully capitalise on it. The people that build themed rooms around tragedy. Like this hotel seemingly did. 

“There’s the suite where the woman drowned in the bathtub,” The owner told me when I called him about it. “Or the room where the fish wife killed her husband with an axe.” He told me this as if he were trying to remember what he wrote down on his grocery list that he left at home.   
“Any difference between the rooms?” I asked, writing down all the key details. “Activity-wise?”  
“Well, the bathtub suite is slightly larger, but there’s less activity, whereas the other room is less spacious, but yeah, you might see a woman hurling an axe towards you in the middle of the night.”   
“The bathroom one, is it drafty?” I asked.   
“Yes, it’s relatively drafty. But it’s a 200-year-old hotel, so it comes with the territory.”  
Because I’m quite fond of my bathroom time and not overly keen on a phantom axe getting hurled at my face at 2 o’clock in the morning, I decided to take the bathroom suite.   
“Be honest,” I asked. “It’s all bullshit, isn’t it?” Thinking that he would secretly agree.  
“Oh no, it’s absolutely real,” He claimed. “Even if you don’t see anything, you’ll feel like something’s watching you.”  
So, I booked a hotel down there on Friday.

When I got there, I was stunned by the exterior of the place. A giant behemoth of a building with a circular driveway out the front. In the middle of this circle stood an old fountain that looked like it's been out of action for as long as I’ve been alive. The building itself looked imposing, yet respectable.

As I walked through the doors, I saw a huge, grandiose entrance that looked like it was pulled out from the 70s. A one-man desk was on my left and a small lobby with various people sitting around on the couches to my right. Despite all the customers, though, it didn’t look like anyone was serving them. In fact, it didn’t look like there was anyone working there at all. I noticed that the people lingering around the lobby couches were taking pictures of the old fireplace in front of them, looking at the old paintings, talking into a tape recorder. Various things. They looked like those paranormal investigators you see on TV. I saw that they had the words “Ghoul’s Errand Paranormal Team” on their shirts. The name needed a bit of work, but what do I know.  
One of them came up to me and said, “Isn’t this place great?”  
I said, “It sure is something.”  
I got to talking to them and told them that they should make a YouTube channel of their findings. They looked at me like they didn’t know what I was talking about, and that was when I realised I’d reached a dead end in the small talk.  
Luckily, a guy came out and walked behind the reception desk. He looked like Tommy Chong from a bygone punk rock era.  
“Yeah, uhh, who’s next?” He asked. Evidently he was the owner, lead receptionist and occasional bellhop when need be. I never picked up his name, though.  
I walked up to the desk and told them that I had a room reserved.   
“Oh yeah. We spoke on the phone. You’re that guy! The ghost guy.” he remembered me from that. *I ask two questions about this drowning room and all of a sudden I’m the ghost guy.*   
“Oh yes, this is a very active hotel; it is.” He continued. “Every other room has had reports of *something* happening.”  
“You’ve seen these ghosts yourself?” I asked.   
“Oh yeah. Almost every day. In fact, at this point I can’t even tell who’s alive and who’s dead anymore, until they tell me their name or… where they’re staying.” He laughed. I thought he was laying it on a bit thick, but I laughed out of politeness.   
“I’ve never seen a ghost before.”  
“Oh, well then this will definitely be the place for you. In fact, they say this place is located directly on a portal to hell.”  
“Well, I should be in for a treat then.” I sarcastically replied as he handed me the keys. 

I took the elevator up. When the doors opened, I saw the hallway walls were painted in some kind of weird coffee cream colour, I thought that maybe these walls were white once upon a time, but have now succumbed to the elements.  
When I got to my room, I opened the door. A chill air came from the doorway the second I opened it; like I opened my fridge freezer. *It’s an old building,* I thought, *with bad heat ventilation that probably hasn’t been fixed in decades—nothing to suggest the supernatural.*   
I walked in and noticed the bathroom. The famous bathroom. No naked old women trying to lure me in like that one movie. No blood on the mirror saying, “*GET OUT.*” Just… a bathroom.  
The rest of the room was fine, albeit a little drab. I wasn’t creeped out by any of this, as one who’s been pretty disillusioned by the power of ghostly suggestion. It was still enough to make me not want to stay there for too long. So, I scattered my things to where I wanted them, and went out to sightsee.

After a night of drinking in the local watering hole a few blocks from the hotel, I made my way back up to the room. The elevator doors opened to the common room of my floor, a lavish alcove with a fireplace that led out to a balcony. Sitting at the tables and chairs that had been provided, I saw six or seven of the paranormal people, the ones I saw earlier that day; they were trying to contact *the beyond*. My room was on the corner of this area, which meant that I was only a wall away from these people.  
*Great,* I thought. *I doubt I’ll be able to sleep if they keep carrying on all night.* 

When I turned one of the lights on in my room, it started to flicker. At first I thought it was strange, but then I thought:   
*I should probably tell the guy downstairs to have it replaced.*   
Nothing abnormal, just needs screwing in a bit. 

I spent the next couple of hours in bed. Staring at the wall. But something was stirring inside me that wanted to come out. Something that’s been bothering me since I laid my head down. Then it dawned on me. I didn’t have my last smoke before bed!

So I got up and went out to the common room next door. I saw the paranormal people picking up massive amounts of energies on their readers, before realising it was probably me setting it off. They gave me somewhat of an annoyed look. Like, how dare I interrupt the sacred sanctity with human vices.

When I was out on the balcony, I could still hear them carrying on inside. I laughed to myself as I took another drag. One of their guys finally came out to join me for a smoke. I didn’t blame him.   
We got to talking. I found him interesting because he had an odorless cigarette.  
“Where’d you get that from?” I asked him.  
“Oh yeah, there’s heaps of them in my state.” He said through an exhale.  
But then he asked me, “So, you staying here for long?”  
“Just until Sunday. What about you?”  
“Well, with the amount of energy going on in our room,” he replied with a big grin on his face. “We might be here for a while.”  
I was doing my best not to ask him if he was sure his ghost equipment was the genuine article, but I left it alone.   
Then he asked me to join him, and a morbid curiosity inside me made me say yes.  
So he brought me in and said: “We’ve found another believer!”   
I was just smiling, saying *hello*. *How are ya*?* *

So, I sat down and just watched them in their habitat. Doing a series of different techniques to contact the outer realm. We probably spent about an hour with a little machine that picks up words and repeats the words with a little robot voice. All the while, I was sitting on the question of whether or not it picks up swear words.  
We got about three words out of it. One of them was “leek”; though we weren’t sure if it actually meant leak, as in something that’s leaking, and it was just spelling it like the vegetable, or if it actually meant a leek.   
The other word was “gas”. Someone began laughing as one might when one hears the word “gas.” By that point, I decided that this was all the lack of proof I needed to finish the last of the wine in my glass and head to bed.   
*No ghosts tonight,* I thought as I got back into bed. I put the TV onto a channel that played 90s sitcoms all night, and then I passed out. 

The next day I woke up and felt great. Had a fantastic night’s sleep. I thought I’d go out and get a huge breakfast and wander around the town that the hotel is attached to. A mile walk, but why not. I needed the exercise, plus I didn’t want to leave my parking space in case somebody decided to take it while I was gone.

So I went into town, bought a few novelties, had a great breakfast. By the time I got back, it was like 2 pm, and I was wondering where the time went.   
As I walked through that grand and prestigious door, I decided I wanted to explore this place, so I walked down all the corridors. And, walking down one, I noticed a huge bar and lounge in front of me. A place from another time, a ruggedly decadent place. A place where you could smoke in one of the chairs and not get in trouble. In fact, with how loose they were with this hotel, I would’ve been surprised if you couldn’t. On my left lay a few brown chairs, a fireplace, and yes, a cigarette machine, coin-operated and ready to kill you. On my right, I noticed what looked like a library. Two giant bookshelves filled to the boards and old photographs hanging from what remained of the walls. And in the middle of all of it, there was a book sitting on a wooden table. This place was a sort of hotel museum—a shrine for the history of the Worthington. I noted the bar for later patronage and decided to walk over to the table. It was a scrapbook. A giant one too. A firsthand account of all the accidents, all the murders, all the death. Written by what I could only presume to be someone who had seen the action, lived in it, experienced it.  
I laughed to myself and thought that they really did a good job of selling this thing. I grabbed a clump of the back pages and flipped them to the front.  
Last recorded incident. A woman got electrocuted in her bathtub. Was that my room or were there a lot of these? No, no. Mine was a drowning… that’s right. So maybe this was another bath-related incident? The woman in this case appeared to be listening to her Discman in the bath, not realising, before it was too late, that it slipped off the corner pocket of the tub and fell into the water. She was electrocuted and died. Dated May 1995.   
1995?   
Was that the last time anyone died in here? Is that when the death curse stopped? And what about the Discman? The strangest part of all this was that, from memory, I know that Discmen are battery-operated. So how in god’s name…? At this point I felt like whoever compiled this were trying to point to clues that weren’t there, or they were making some things up.   
*Nice try though.*

I decided that I would ditch my previous night’s watering hole (which was, again, a mile from here) in favour of this place. The bar was a little drab and depressing, but as long as the drinks were close, it suited me fine. There I drank for hours on end. However, I decided not to look at the scrapbook any longer, fearing that it would fill my head with suggestions, things and people being in my room that weren’t necessarily there. The power of suggestion is very strong, but if you have enough self-awareness, you can steer clear.   
It was about 10 at night when I went back up to my floor. The elevator doors opened and, as usual, the paranormal people were doing another experiment in the common room. I was wise enough to go out and have a smoke before, rather than after, I retired to my room. After that, I scooched past the spectral sleuths as I tried not to make too much noise and disturb them.  
“Is it time for bed?” One of them whispered to me.  
“Yeah, I check out tomorrow, so I don’t want to overdo it.” I replied.  
They mouthed the word “oh” and nodded in understanding. 

So I went into my room, turned the bathroom light on -'cause you never know- and crawled into bed.   
Quiet.   
But it didn’t last very long, though, because I heard a baby screaming and crying in another room nearby. It spooked me at first, but then it really annoyed me. This isn’t the ghost of a child. I told myself. Having said that, it is a little eerie. But even if it were a living, breathing, screaming kid. What kind of sick individual would bring their kid to a haunted hotel?  
Maybe they’re a lot more tolerant of the paranormal than the kid is. If so, hard cheese for little junior.  
Or maybe they didn’t tell the child that it was haunted?  
Or maybe they didn’t even know themselves. And this was just a strange coincidence? Eventually it passed. And I was drifting off to sleep. 

A draft in the room kept me slightly awake. An old hotel that’s prone to getting cold easily.  
The walls and shelves cracked suddenly and sporadically. An old hotel settling down for the night.   
A child was screaming from a great distance. An old hotel that is no place for a screaming child.  
As I closed my eyes, I drifted off. And, as I was dreaming, my mind’s eye saw a woman approaching me… like a princess or something exotic. As she came up closer, her gaze never left mine. Then her eyes turned to her right, my left.  
Suddenly, I heard a knock in the direction she was looking. I opened my eyes to hear it coming from the door, bringing me fully out of sleep.  
BANG BANG BANG. I froze. Who could it be?   
BANG BANG BANG. At this time of night?  
BANG BANG BANG. They seemed eager. But then I felt a chill. What if I opened it and there was a soaking wet fish wife ready to spend a night in the tub?   
*Better to just pull the band-aid off.* I thought.  
So I got out of my bed and slowly approached my door, slowly walking up to the little peephole to slowly see who, or what, faced me. I sighed in either disappointment or relief.   
The paranormal people. About three or four of them.  
I opened the door, rubbing my eyes.   
“Jesus, what time do you call this?” I mumbled.   
“We’re so sorry to bother you. But our readers picked up some massive energies, and we followed it. Now don’t be alarmed, but it went directly into your room. Mind if we take a look?”  
“It’s 2 am.” I stated, hoping their reader would pick up on how annoyed I tried to come across.  
“It will only take a minute.” They insisted. “Normally we wouldn’t do something crazy like this, but this is huge.”  
I shook my head and, without saying anything, stepped aside.   
They all piled into my room. Scattering around the room like ants when they see their home walked on.   
So they examined, and examined, and examined. And after about 15 minutes of excessive investigation, and even going so far as to rifle through my unmentionables, I had put my foot down.   
“Alright, you’re not getting anything now, and I really need to sleep.”   
I walked over to the front door and opened it so they understood what I meant.   
“But we were picking up great readings.” One of them protested as they all walked out.   
“Well, you can get them again tomorrow, I’m sure, ‘cause I won’t be here.” I grumbled like a scolding parent.   
They all muttered to themselves and walked out. One of them turned around and had the cheek to tell me, “Are you sure you don’t want to stay for longer? Apparently, there’s a ghost tour tomorrow night!”  
“I’m sure you guys will love it. Bye now.” I closed the door.   
As I turned all the lights off, I saw what I thought was a figure in the corner of the room. I had heard about this once.  
A medium once told me that you won’t see spirits in a traditional sense. Spirits are hidden in the dark; if you want to see them, you just have to look closer, deeper, look for the shadows within the shadows. That will be when you see them. So I looked. And I realised what I saw. It was my own eye floaters. You know, the ones you sometimes get when you see something that assaults your visual senses. It was just them, moving around like crazy, trying to play tricks on me. 

But as I got back into bed, I laid in it for what felt like an hour, just thinking.   
*I could live here. I could honestly live in this hotel. Just pack up and leave without telling anyone. Then I could join every investigator who goes here. And then \*I\* become the annoying one.*   
But then I started having, like, an existential crisis.   
*Let’s say ghosts did exist, for the sake of argument. And what if they get stuck here, do they just… exist here for the rest of their unnatural lives? Trying to find company? Any reason to communicate with the outside world, even if it means getting annoyed by the slew of ghost hunters that bother you?*  
*Would that be worse than just atheistic blackness? Both are heavy concepts to play with. What fate is more depressing? An eternity of nothingness or existing as a spirit for the rest of your days. Watching things come and go, seeing your friends and family get old and die. Never being able to make new friends before they go too. Lost and confused. Over and over, till the end of time.* With that thought, I fell asleep.

The next morning, the last morning I was there, I woke up feeling really groggy. Like I needed a century of more sleep. Nevertheless, I packed my stuff and went downstairs to check out. 

When the elevator doors opened, I saw that the old guy at the reception was attending to a woman holding her baby.   
*I knew that baby was real,* I thought, before realising just how weird that might’ve sounded if spoken out loud.  
“Hey man, you look tired.” He said to me as I walked up. “Did you see any ghosts this weekend?”  
I said, “I didn’t really sleep cause I kept getting woken up by other people in the hotel.”   
He asked me if it was that baby that was disturbing me.  
I told him, “No, no, no. It was more like those paranormal people that were wandering around. They were coming into my room at 2 o’clock last night. 2 o’clock! There should be quiet hours in this place.”  
“Hey?”   
I said, “Quiet hours. They have them in hotels for the express purpose of making sure people like them don’t wake me up.”  
“The paranormal people, right. What did they do, man?” Tommy Chong’s brother asked.  
“They were being loud and carrying on.” I leaned in and replied rather pompously, even for my taste. “They were treating this place like the temple of doom. Some of them even came into my room!”  
“Did you let them in? Cause the door won’t open without the right key, man.”  
The bastard got me there. I had to try to save face.  
“Yes, I let them in,” I confessed. “But they were rapping on my door like crazy.”  
I then leaned in again and whispered,  
“I’m pretty sure one of them stole my toothbrush!”  
“That’s really weird.” The old man laughed.   
“I know, right?”  
“No, like,” he corrected, scratching his head. “I don’t think we booked anyone like that near your room.”  
“Well, they may have been from a different floor; I don’t know.”  
“And like, come to think of it, I don’t think we checked in any paranormal investigators since you arrived.”  
“What are you talking about? Are you saying I let people, who weren’t guests, here into my room?!  
“Hey, it’s possible.” The guy laughed. “Did you find anything missing when you repacked?”  
I tried to think back over whether they actually had taken anything. As I looked around trying to think, I noticed a framed picture… on the mantle behind the old guy’s desk. It was a photo of the investigators standing outside the hotel.  
“That was them!” I told him. “They were the people that came into my room.”  
The old guy looked at me and stood back a little; he looked like he was posing for a photo at somebody’s funeral.  
“Are you sure you saw *those* people?"  
“Yeah, 100%. I bet my life on it.” I confirmed.  
“Because *those people* have been dead for over twenty years.” He declared.  
Suddenly, a sinking feeling merged in the pit of my stomach; alarms activated inside of me. Shock, confusion, frustration. All hitting me, all at once.   
“What?” I managed to get out.  
“Terrible circumstance. There was a gas leak in their part of the hotel, but the previous owners weren’t aware of it at the time. These investigators would come down describing the most bizarre things to me: ‘I saw this’ and ‘I saw that.’ Little did anyone realise was that all the things they were claiming to have seen may have just been hallucinations from the poison. They eventually put up a “do not disturb” sign on the door, something about reaching a breakthrough. And that’s the last we heard from them. We didn’t even find their bodies until a couple days later. The cleaning woman, she only comes in once a week, she comes up to me and says, ‘Can’t get into the room; they put the sign up; they’ve chain-locked the door,’ and then I noticed, ‘Hang on, they should’ve checked out days ago.’ Had they known it was probably carbon monoxide, they wouldn’t have stayed in there for as long as they did.”  
*Gas leak.*  
I looked at him, stunned. I said,   
“No, no. What about the scrapbook. Huh? That says, ‘the last reported incident was 1995.’”  
“Well, yes, the last *reported* incident. But the leak was the hotel’s fault. So to make sure this didn’t get out to the papers, the previous management handled it.”  
“Handled it?” I asked.   
“Yeah, they bought them off. The safety inspector. The families of the victims, they settled out of court so that it wouldn’t get into the news. It’s all fun and games when you see your name in the papers reporting on stories of self-inflicted tragedies or spectral encounters. But the leak was our fault. It’s horrible, I know, but I wasn’t around for it, and I got a business to run, pal.”  
“So you’re telling me no investigators have been here after them?”  
“There are, but they are far and few between. See, the owners didn’t let the papers leak it, but word starts to get around to more of these ghost hunters. They find out neglect was undertaken, and they figure no investigation is worth their life.”  
I was waiting for him to smile. Waiting for him to crack so I could say, I caught you. Nice try. Trying to get me til the very end.  
“What about the day I checked in!” I asked. “You didn’t see any of them wandering around?”   
“I only saw you.”  
“I can’t believe this. You’re trying to wind me up. This is a practical joke and a very poor one at that! I’ve possibly been robbed by people who weren’t even guests at this hotel, who weren’t even supposed to *be* here, and you're laughing about it. You’re still trying to scare… it’s all a big joke. You’re winding me up, you…”  
He put his hand up to say, ‘Wait a minute.’ I saw this and immediately stopped ranting.  
“Would you like to see the security cameras?” He asked. I agreed, and he brought me around to his side. That’s when he showed it to me. Something that I still cannot explain. There was footage of me talking to myself in the lobby; there was footage of me opening my room door and chatting to an empty hallway. And there was footage of me getting out of the way of my doorway before closing it, then fifteen minutes later opening the door and talking before closing it again. At first, I thought… *He must’ve enhanced the footage; he must’ve edited it in a certain way. But where would he find the time and energy to do something like that? Christ, some of this only happened hours ago.*  
And finally, he showed the last bit of footage. It showed me walking out from the balcony of the common area, sitting down at an empty table, and remaining there for two hours, by myself. No one else around.   
The old guy turned around from his monitor and looked at me.   
“I want a refund.” I whimpered.  
“I don’t like this. I don’t like this at all and I want a refund right now.”  
The old man looked at me, puzzled.  
“Why?” he asked.  
“What do you mean, ‘why’?”  
And he said something that chills my blood to this day. “Well you got wanted. You’ve finally seen a ghost.”

With that, I walked back around to the front. I dropped the keys off on the desk, and without saying anything, I got the hell out of there. I peeled out as fast as I could, and I drove as far away as I could. Even as I write this, several hours and hundreds of miles later, I still feel like something is still watching me. That something or someone had followed me back from the hotel, and is ready to see me again the second I go to bed.
