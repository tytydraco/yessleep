When I was eighteen, I had this wonderful friend, Sveta. She was gorgeous and a little fucking nuts. Actually, more than a little. The kind of girl you could never get bored with—the kind who regularly left you thinking, “Well, thank God we got out of that one without getting into trouble!”  
She was amazing. I say “was” because we lost touch a long time ago, and I haven’t been able to find her. I think I had a bit of a crush on her, too :)  
We balanced each other out. She was always coming up with wild ideas, and we’d carry them out together. But if I thought something was dangerous, or just had a feeling something wasn’t right, I’d say I wasn’t doing it. Usually, Sveta would drop whatever questionable plan she’d come up with and stay with me.  
She called me her “voice of reason.” Later, she started calling me her guardian angel.  
And this is why.  
One summer, we were staying at her family’s summer house in a tiny village. There were three of us: Sveta, me, and her little brother, Kolya. He was four.  
We all slept in the same room. Sveta and I shared a big bed, and Kolya slept in a little child’s bed. He always slept with a small teddy bear, and that night was no different. He fell asleep with his arms around it.  
Sveta and I stayed up chatting and laughing our asses off until about two in the morning, then went to sleep.  
A little later, I woke up to the sound of her laughing.  
She was laughing in her sleep. Her blanket had almost slipped onto the floor. Nothing particularly strange about that. It happens. I picked up the blanket, tucked it around her, and went back to sleep.  
At eight the next morning, Sveta woke me up. Normally, we’d sleep until ten or eleven, whenever Kolya woke up. But that morning, she woke me early and immediately started telling me about a dream she’d had.  
In her dream, she’d gone out into the yard during the night. There was a group of young women out there—beautiful, wearing short, sparkly dresses and bright makeup, their long hair loose around their shoulders. They were laughing and inviting her to come out with them.  
They seemed so cool and fun that she decided to go.  
Then she saw me standing in the doorway. She ran over and tried to talk me into coming along.  
I told her I wasn’t going anywhere. I didn’t know these girls, this could end badly, and anyway, let’s just go back to bed.  
The girls were waiting for her.  
Sveta stood there for a moment, torn. Then she said, “Fine, stay here. I’m going to have some fun.”  
“Fine. Off you go, then,” I said, and sat down on the bench beside the house, pointedly turning my back on her.  
Sveta ran over to the girls. They hugged her and whispered things to her.  
Then she turned around and ran into the house.  
When she came back out, she was carrying Kolya.  
He was asleep with his head on her shoulder, still clutching his teddy bear.  
The girls had opened the garden gate and were filing out, one by one. Sveta was the last in line.  
Just as she stepped through the gate, I jumped up and ran after her. I caught her by the arm as she was about to close it, pulled her back into the yard, and shut the gate myself.  
I started begging her not to leave.  
“What do you need those girls for? You don’t even know them. We can have fun ourselves.”  
I kept talking her into staying as I led her back into the house.  
We put the sleeping Kolya back in his bed and lay down in ours.  
Sveta said she was cold.  
I saw that her blanket was on the floor, picked it up, and covered her with it.  
Then I went back to sleep, too.  
That was her dream.  
She told it so vividly, with so much emotion, that I felt as though I’d seen the whole thing myself. Then she hugged me and said she was glad she had me.  
And then she asked, “Where’s my cross?”  
She wore a little silver cross on a chain. She always had it on. Never took it off.  
But now it wasn’t around her neck.  
We searched the bed and found it under her pillow.  
We were surprised, of course, but we came up with a perfectly reasonable explanation: she’d been tossing and turning in her sleep, and the clasp must have come undone.  
And now for the end of the story.  
A little later, Kolya woke up. We had breakfast and decided to go swimming in the river.  
We opened the gate and stepped outside.  
About five paces beyond it, lying in the grass, was Kolya’s teddy bear.
