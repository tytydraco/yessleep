

It was a quiet summer evening in their suburban American home. Ethan and Logan were completely alone, the rhythmic clicking of controllers filling the dimly lit living room as they stared at the glowing monitor.

"Come on bro, that's cheating!" Ethan said, his voice rising in anger as he threw his hands up.

Logan smirked, not breaking his gaze from the screen. "It's not my fault you don't know how to play."

"You are using a bug to speed up!" Ethan protested, pointing accusingly at Logan’s character model clipping through a wall.

"Not my problem," Logan chuckled, lazily spinning his joystick.

Ethan rolled his eyes, his stomach letting out a loud growl. "Whatever. Want to eat something?"

Logan grimaced, finally looking away from the game. "That day-old pizza? Nah man, keep it to yourself."

"So wait here while I grab some food," Ethan said, standing up and stretching.

"Not pizza again!" Logan groaned, attempting a sad but funny face.

Ethan laughed, heading toward the kitchen. "Okay bro, I will just grab some burgers."

While waiting for the food, Logan's eyes drifted to an online forum thread open on his phone: The 3 AM Candy Ritual. He read the instructions aloud as Ethan walked back into the room holding a plate of burgers.

"Hey, look at this," Logan said, his inner foolish bravery sparking up. "It says if you leave candies outside your front door exactly at 3:00 AM, two Black-Eyed Kids will come to eat it. If you stare directly into their eyes, they’ll run away... but they’ll remember you forever."

Ethan set the plate down, a chill creeping up his spine. "Dude, don't mess with that creepypasta stuff. It’s bad luck."

"Are you chicken?" Logan teased, already raiding the pantry. He pulled out a bag of leftover Halloween candy. "It’s 2:58 AM. Let's do it."

Despite Ethan's protests, Logan walked right up to the front door, unlocked it, and poured a small pile of colorful candies onto the welcome mat. He shut the door, leaving it unlocked just a crack, and turned off all the house lights. They waited in pitch darkness.

The grandfather clock in the hallway struck 3:00 AM.

For a few minutes, nothing happened. Logan began to laugh quietly, but his laugh died out when a strange, low scratching sound echoed from the porch. Scritch. Scritch. Like fingernails scraping against stone.

Through the narrow crack of the door, the sound of tearing plastic wrappers made both boys freeze. Something was eating the candy.

Driven by pure, foolish adrenaline, Logan lunged forward and yanked the front door wide open.

Standing on the porch were two small figures dressed in outdated, oversized clothes. They slowly raised their heads. Their skin was deathly pale, but it was their faces that made Ethan choke back a scream. Where their eyes should have been, there was nothing but pure, glossy, midnight blackness. No whites, no irises—just two voids staring back.

Logan, gripping the doorframe, forced himself to do exactly what the rules said. He locked his gaze tightly onto the pitch-black eyes of the child closest to him.

The air turned freezing cold. The black-eyed children stood perfectly still for three agonizing seconds. Then, with an unnatural, jerky twist of their necks, they suddenly sprinted backward into the foggy suburban dark, disappearing into the shadows of the tree line.

Logan slammed the door shut and locked every deadbolt, his chest heaving, a triumphant but terrified smile on his face. "See? I told you! They ran away!"

Ethan didn't smile. He pointed with a trembling hand at the bottom of the wooden door.

Scratched deeply into the paint at child-height were two fresh, jagged words:

WE REMEMBER.

The adrenaline that had fueled Logan’s foolish bravery vanished the instant he looked down at the freshly splintered wood. The words looked as though they had been gouged into the white paint by thick, jagged nails. A faint smell of ozone and damp earth lingered around the doorframe, heavy and suffocating. Ethan collapsed against the hallway wall, his breath coming in ragged, shallow gasps.

Logan stared at his own hands. They were shaking so violently he could barely pocket his phone. The triumphant smirk he usually wore was completely gone, replaced by a pale, hollow expression. The house, usually a comforting sanctuary of hums from the refrigerator and the soft glow of standby lights, suddenly felt vast, empty, and deeply unsafe.

Neither boy slept that night. They dragged a heavy wooden armchair across the hardwood floor, wedging it firmly beneath the front door handle. They sat shoulder-to-shoulder on the living room sofa, the television playing a mindless sitcom on mute just to keep the shadows at bay. Every creak of the suburban house settling made them flinch. Every gust of wind against the windowpanes sounded like a low, mocking whisper.

When morning finally broke, the bright summer sun did little to wash away the dread. The day passed in a blur of exhausting paranoia. When they walked to the local convenience store in the afternoon, Ethan kept pulling his hood up, convinced that the dark spaces between the neighborhood houses were staring back at him. Logan remained unusually quiet, his eyes constantly darting to the rearview mirrors of passing cars, half-expecting to see two small, pale faces sitting in the back seats.

By the time twilight purpled into midnight, the tension in the house was thick enough to choke on. The rules of the online forum had warned that they would remember, but it hadn’t explained what that memory entailed.

At 2:50 AM, the temperature inside the house plummeted. It wasn’t a gradual cooling; it was a sudden, aggressive drop that turned their breath into white mist. The digital thermostat on the wall clicked rapidly, its digital display flickering before dying out completely.

Ethan clutched a baseball bat tightly to his chest, his knuckles white. "Logan, we shouldn't have done it. We shouldn't have looked."

"We followed the rules," Logan whispered, though his voice cracked with uncertainty. "They ran away. That’s what the game said."

The grandfather clock began its heavy, rhythmic chiming. One. Two. Three.

A sharp, rhythmic sound shattered the silence. It wasn’t coming from the front door this time. It was coming from the second-story window directly above them—Ethan’s bedroom.

Tap. Tap. Tap.

It was the slow, deliberate sound of small knuckles knocking against glass.

The boys crept up the stairs, their bare feet freezing against the carpet. The air grew progressively colder with every step. Ethan’s bedroom door was slightly ajar, a sliver of pitch-black darkness visible inside.

From the other side of the glass window, a voice drifted through the walls. It didn't sound like a normal child's voice. It was flat, monotone, and lacked any human cadence, yet it carried an unnatural clarity that echoed right inside their skulls.

"Ethan. Logan," the voice droned, the pronunciation stiff and mechanical. "You looked at us. We remember your faces. We remember your names. It is so cold out here. Please open the window and let us in."

Logan stepped backward, his heel catching on the top step. Through the gap in the doorway, the clouds parted outside, allowing the moonlight to illuminate the window sill.

Two small, pale hands were pressed flat against the glass, leaving smudges of black frost. Behind the pane, the two children stood on the impossibly narrow ledge. Their glossy, void-like eyes were fixed directly onto the doorway, staring straight through the darkness at the two boys, waiting for them to break the rules.

The monotone voice ringing directly inside their heads was too much. Ethan dropped the baseball bat, his knees buckling as he collapsed onto the top step of the stairs. Logan’s chest heaved with terror. He scrambled to pull his phone from his pocket, his sweaty fingers slipping over the glass screen as he navigated back to the dark web forum where he had first found the 3 AM Candy Ritual.

With trembling thumbs, Logan typed a desperate reply into the thread: "WE DID IT. THEY ARE HERE. THEY ARE AT THE WINDOW. HOW DO WE GET RID OF THEM? PLEASE HELP."

He refreshed the page frantically. The seconds felt like hours. Outside, the rhythmic tapping against the glass grew faster and louder, mimicking a frantic heartbeat. Tap-tap-tap-tap.

Finally, a notification popped up. A user named VoidGazer99 had responded.

Logan squeezed closer to Ethan, holding the screen between them. The message read:

"You fools. You locked eyes with them, which means you accepted the contract. The rule about staring them down doesn't banish them—it just pauses them. They are bound to you now. They cannot cross the threshold without a verbal invitation... but they will stay outside your windows, tap on your walls, and whisper your secrets until you lose your mind and let them in just to make the noise stop."

Ethan choked back a sob. "Is there a way to break it? Look for a solution!"

Logan scrolled down. VoidGazer99 had added a follow-up reply, but the solution was far from simple:

"There is only one way to pass the burden. They require a soul to replace the one they are hunting. You must invite someone else into your home while they are still outside. The moment a new person steps across your threshold without knowing the rules, the Black-Eyed Kids will shift their gaze to them. The contract will transfer. You will be free."

Logan stared at the glowing text, a sickening realization washing over him. "We have to lure someone here," he whispered, his voice hollow. "An innocent person."

"No way, Logan! We can't do that to someone!" Ethan yelled, though he had to cover his mouth as the voice from the bedroom window flared up again, sharper this time.

"Ethan... Logan... your friend Marcus is trying to call you," the childlike voice droned from the darkness of the bedroom. "Answer him. Tell him to come over."

Right on cue, Logan’s phone began to vibrate violently in his hand. The caller ID flashed blindly in the dark hallway. It was their high school friend, Marcus, likely calling to check in after seeing them go offline mid-game.

The entities knew. They were waiting for the boys to make the choice.

Logan looked at the vibrating phone, then looked at Ethan. The freezing air in the hallway was growing heavier, suffocating them both.
