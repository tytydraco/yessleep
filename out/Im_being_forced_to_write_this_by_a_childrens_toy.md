I’m currently sitting at my friends, Alex and Jamie’s house. The former is unblinkingly flipping through an encyclopedia, the latter sitting on the floor, clutching a kitchen knife. It’s a strange scene, I admit, and a part of me actually finds the whole situation quite funny. We’re here because of a toy, something meant to amuse *children.* But then I glance at Alex’s bloodshot eyes, at Jamie looking back and forth from me, to his knife, to the door, and it all becomes deadly serious again.

I know you’ll laugh when I tell you the reason for all this. I know for a fact not one of you will take me seriously. Last night we found a Magic 8 Ball while smoking some prerolls in a park. Alex had just killed our last roach, and the three of us were bored of just sitting and talking. So, we stood up to pace around in the grass, desperate to do *something* but too stoned to form any coherent idea of exactly what. 

“So, what, are we just gonna hang at this park all night?” I asked, and at that exact moment I felt something knock against my foot. I cursed, startled, and turned on my phone's flashlight. It was an orb, a black orb. I picked it up, turned it over, and saw it was a Magic 8 Ball, its message starting out at me from the ink. *Very doubtful*. I felt a thrill. Maybe I was just stoned, or maybe just one look at the thing was enough to get its hooks into my brain, but somehow I knew night was about to get interesting.

“What’s that?” Asked Alex, and I held it up for my friends to see.

“Awww, some kid lost his toy,” said Jamie, and snatched it out of my hands. I felt a little pang of anger, even jealousy toward him that I didn’t quite understand. The thing was basically trash.  “Oh Magic 8 Ball,” he said theatrically, “I am really goddamn hungry, should we grab a Big Mac?”

*Concentrate and ask again.*

Now Alex snatched it, and Jamie's face twitched. “No, no, Popeyes, should we hit Popeyes,” and gave the Black Orb a shake.

“They close at 9,” I said, but Alex shrugged and showed us the answer. 

*It is decidedly so.* Without any further argument, the three of us began to walk to Popeyes.

I can’t say exactly what they felt, can’t determine what went on in their heads, but I assume it wasn’t so different from mine. It was like an itch. Like an itch in the pit of my stomach, and the only thing that could possibly scratch it was the cardboard-like flavor of fast food chicken. We made some casual conversation as we walked, but soon enough the words just fell away, and we walked through the darkness in silence. 

The itch filled the silence, and turned to a sickening churn. My heart began to beat faster and faster. The thought of a spicy chicken sandwich had grown in my mind until there wasn’t room for anything else. I didn't consider for a second what we would do when we arrived, and were confronted by locked doors.

“What if we just broke in?” said Jamie.

“I’m hungry,” said Alex. His urgent tone of voice belied the casual nature of his words “lets do it.”

When I’m stoned, I tend toward anxiety. I was anxious. Near panic in fact. But the source of that fear was the prospect of spending another minute doing anything but devouring some fried chicken. We picked up a few rocks and hurled them through the front window. As soon as it was remotely possible, the three of us scampered into the store,  vaulted over the back counter and toward the refrigerator in the back. None of us cared that it was cold. We just crouched on the ground like a pack of hyenas, gnawing meat from the bones. It couldn’t have been more than 15 minutes until we heard sirens.

Jamie snatched the ball from Alex and asked it if we would make it home. *Outlook good*, it said, and the three of us began, in unison, to run. My legs moved faster than I thought possible, as if something had forced itself inside of me and was using me as a puppet. A couple blocks from the store, I hurled myself into an alley with such force that I think broke my wrist. Not that I’ve been able to go to a hospital and get it checked. At that moment, a cop car turned the corner and drove past me. 

That ruthless, invisible force inside me wasn’t finished. As soon as the lights disappeared I was jerked upright and continued my mad dash home. I ran until my lungs were worn ragged, and passed out on the floor the second I stumbled through my apartment door. My last thought was of the Magic 8 Ball sitting in the grass. Of Alex and Jamie, who had brought the thing home. *I found it, they shouldn’t have just taken it.* Then all was black.

I woke up with the sun, my wrist throbbing. The frustration with my friends for taking The Ball was still gnawing at my brain. A part of me that didn’t really feel like part of me wanted to give them a call and demand it back. It was mine. I needed it. The most frustrating part of the whole situation was that if only I had the thing, it could decide the best course of action for me.

I’ve never been known for my intelligence, but it was clear to me these feelings weren’t natural. The Magic 8 Ball was exerting some malicious influence on me and my friends. My brain felt mossy, my thoughts were confused, but I knew I didn’t want to give in to whatever that *thing* had planted inside of me. I sent them a text.

*Yall doing ok?? What the fuck was that last night?*

Three dots. There, then gone. So I gave them a call. No answer. I began to feel that tug again, that itch. Like something was alive in the pit of my stomach. I thought of Alex and Jamie sitting in their apartment with The Ball doing God knows what without me. On the bus ride over, I told myself that I was heading to their house because I was worried about them, but I can admit it now. All I cared about was ridding myself of that horrible feeling. 

It was cold and drizzling when I knocked on their door. I immediately heard movement.

“What’s it say?” That was Alex.

“We’re gonna have to open it.” Jamie, sounding paranoid.

The door slowly creaked open, and I shoved my way in. Alex was sitting on their couch, slowly flipping through the dictionary. Jamie was sitting on the floor, and scooting awkwardly away from me. Both of them gripped a kitchen knife. The place was cluttered, but clean. Not a speck of dirt on the floor or smudge on the countertop, yet random stacks of DVD’s, books, and Lego sets were placed haphazardly across the floor. The two had a frantic demeanor about them. Their eyes were red. 

“Jesus Christ,” I said, “what the fuck happened here?”

But I knew. It was obvious. What else was there to say? Jamie, still sitting, pushed himself across the floor, while Alex slowly tore his eyes away from the dictionary in his hand to look at the Magic 8 Ball and give it a shake. 

“Should we tell Johnny?” they whispered to it.

Alex said nothing and looked back at his book, while Jamie glanced up at me and apologized.

“Hey man, nothing personal, but I really can’t tell you.” His face, his voice, was reminiscent of a child who, caught red handed and knowing no other way out, resorts to outright denial of reality to escape punishment. There was real fear in his voice. 

“Do I need to call someone?” 

“No! No!” they said, almost exactly in unison, “It told us not to.”

Alex blinked, winced, then returned to his reading. Jamie whispered something to the 8 Ball, and let out a pathetic whimper. “Come in, come in,” he said, and kept up the sickening charade that everything was just fine, despite the fact that all three of us knew the situation was deeply wrong, and knew exactly what had caused it. 

There was a moment where I hesitated. A moment where I felt something inside myself, something true, pulling me away. But there was The Ball, nestled in the crook of two couch cushions, pulling me forward like a black hole. I couldn’t resist it. I stepped inside. Jamie pushed himself toward the door faster than I would have thought possible, never moving from a sitting position, and slammed the door shut. The knife trembled in his hands.

“Fuck dude, what was that!”

“Sorry, sorry.” He said. There were tears in the corners of his eyes. “I wish I had a choice, but I can’t let you leave. It told me I should keep It a secret.” 

Alex flipped another page and let out a soft moan. I don’t think I’ve seen him blink once. Another silence settled over us. I didn’t know what to do. Nothing in my life had prepared me for something like this. Alex and Jamie needed a doctor, an exorcist,, but all they got was a fucking college dropout without the faintest idea of what to do with his own life, let alone how to fix… this. 

Jamie had let the Magic 8 Ball fall to the floor. His whole being seemed focused on me, observing my every moment. I did need to ask to know he wouldn’t let me out. The Ball lay facing away from me. The awful churning feeling in my guts was reaching a fever pitch. Every choice I saw was a bad one. So, I snatched the Magic 8 Ball. I don’t know. What else was I supposed to do? 

“Do I need to get help?”

*Yes - definitely*

“Like, ummm, the Police?”

*Cannot Predict Now.*

“A priest?”

*Very Doubtful.*

“What about posting online somewhere? Will anyone believe me?”

*As I see it, yes.*

So I opened my phone, and ended up here. But listen. Don’t try to find us. I know at least one of you will take me at my word. The Magic 8 Ball told me so. I thought about posting my location at the end of my story, of putting my faith in someone, somewhere out there having the ability to break us away from that evil black orb. But the look on Jamie’s face, desperately trying to fight off sleep by the door, and the twitching demeanor of Alex, reading through the *dictionary* like a zealot reading the Bible gave me second thoughts. There was one more question I had to ask.

“Can we be saved?” I whispered.

*Don’t count on it.*
