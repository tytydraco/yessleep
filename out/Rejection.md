Back then, my mom was around 17, or at least that’s what she told me. She was always happy back then. She graduated top of her class with highest honors and a stellar GPA. She traveled all the time and had a huge circle of friends who always had her back. Right after graduation, she went on a backpacking trip through Europe, hitting dozens of cities and countries, just living her life to the absolute fullest.

But when she came back, everything changed. Her parents started getting really sick because of their old age. That kind of heavy stuff didn't scare my mom though. She found the strength to work and pay for their meds, healthcare, and she even managed to scrape up enough cash for little things like spa days or massages. Day by day, their health stabilized.

Then she met a handsome guy. Right before that, she and my grandma had a long talk about how grandma desperately wanted to live to see her grandkids. So, mom decided to make a move. She asked the guy out, and honestly, things couldn't have gone better. He showered her with gifts and attention. They were practically inseparable.

He was her first. Her first in absolutely everything. She slept with him, even though she was far from his first. Then they did it again, and again. It was just a normal honeymoon phase. But right after that, something shifted. I never found out why, but he started drinking heavily, maybe even doing drugs. Still, their relationship held on, or so it seemed. One night, when they were both out of it, there was no condom around, and he decided it wasn't necessary. Mom got pregnant. With me.

About 5 or 6 weeks in, mom took a test. She had been denying it for a while, but her missed period was a dead giveaway. Unsurprisingly, it came back positive. She was terrified to tell him, but she had to. And guess what? A day later. Just one single day, and every single way to contact him went completely dead.

He ran. He ghosted her. He chickened out.  
What a piece of trash.

Mom didn't lose her nerve. She was carrying a child and refused to get an abortion. Her parents had her back, telling her not to despair and to just focus on the baby. During the ultrasound, the tech said: "It’s a girl."  
Joy hadn't abandoned her just yet. She and her friends celebrated the news together.

But after I was born, it turned out I wasn't just a regular baby. There were complications. They couldn't catch them early on, but the doctors said there was no need to panic. The absolute worst we should expect was a low heart rate and low blood pressure. But then, when I turned 3, I suddenly started suffocating out of nowhere. It turned out to be persistent, moderate asthma. They brought me back, stabilized me, prescribed an inhaler, and sent us home. There was just one issue: none of this was cheap. And as you can guess, a single mother didn't have cash flowing in, even with her amazing resume. A good job at 21 with a sick toddler on her hands was out of the question.

Welfare checks barely covered the basics, let alone my endless medical bills caused by a severely compromised immune system. My grandparents' savings were draining fast. Mom had stopped spoiling them long ago. There was only enough money left for food.

Four years later, things somewhat leveled out. Mom landed a job at a prestigious hospital as a surgical tech. I spent my days sitting in the hospital waiting rooms with my grandparents. Grandma was doing okay, but Grandpa was fading. Honestly, I don't remember exactly when, but about six months later, he passed away from a heart attack. This dealt a crushing blow to the already fragile pillar of our lives: my mother. After all, she hadn't studied that hard just to be an assistant. She dreamed of becoming a surgeon to save lives. And in the end, she couldn't even save her own father.

Our income plummeted again. The savings were completely wiped out by the funeral costs. We moved into a rundown house in some godforsaken place, a neighborhood that looked straight out of the slums. By that point, Mom and Grandma barely saw each other, and the money sent home dwindled with each passing month.

By the time I turned 8, life rewarded me with right kidney failure. Mom was in a total panic. She went down on her knees begging for a loan to pay for a transplant or at least a surgery to remove it. But the creditors didn't see her as someone who could pay them back and deemed it a bad investment. The doctors also said my condition was ultimately terminal, and it would only get worse from there. Mom didn't stop. She sold all her jewelry and all the family heirlooms we had kept for generations. She managed to pay for the surgery to remove my right kidney.

The remaining money was barely enough for cheap food and to pay the water bills we owed on. But fate didn't keep us waiting for long. Seven months later, my asthma began progressing into the severe stage, likely triggered by our toxic environment. Mom fell into a deep depression. She was at a complete loss. I needed at least one healthy lung. A mad idea popped into her head: she went to the hospital, begging them to transplant her own left lung into my body. The doctors said no because we had no money anyway. I remember the day she sat by the front door for hours. There were no tears left. She was completely drained.

Mom started spending more time in the basement. Strange equipment appeared down there, along with bright surgical lights and mirrors. A week later, I could suddenly breathe easier. I was jumping with joy, telling Mom that everything was finally getting better. She just chain-smoked one cigarette after another, as if the fact that she had never smoked before didn't matter anymore. The inhalers vanished from my room. I thought we were finally going to truly live.

Nine months later, I suffered a severe attack. My pulse stopped, and consciousness faded. The ambulance wouldn't make it in time. In total desperation, Mom carried me down to the basement, where the setup now looked exactly like an operating room.

The last thing I remember before losing consciousness entirely was her voice whispering: "If others won't save my daughter, I will."

When the paramedics finally arrived, they found me stabilized, but my mother was gone. The medical records later confirmed a highly unorthodox, desperate procedure had taken place right there in that dark basement.

I am 16 now. I was adopted. I live on, knowing the immense, terrifying sacrifice she made in that hidden room.

I love and hate her with all her heart.

I'm sorry, Mom, for being born.
