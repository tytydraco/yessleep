This part of the city was like a ghost town at night. The streets were empty with the only sound filling the air being the cool crisp gusts of wind that would blow from time to time. The outdated architecture and visible grime of the buildings made the borough feel ancient. The atmosphere subtly suggested that my presence there was inappropriate or out of place. 

I had parked about a block away from my destination due to *no parking* signs crowding the street of the home I was visiting. I traversed the snow littered sidewalks as the buildings became more clustered. It was clear that I was reaching the outer rims of the city; the trash ridden streets and weed infested home exteriors were a dead giveaway. The outskirts of the city were not particularly well kept and often served as a refuge for the homeless. I can remember wearing my dark blue coat with cheap fur lining the inside, as I had clutched it closed wishing I had worn a heavier one. 

With a few more crunches of matted snow, I had reached the tall and thin apartment building that was my destination. It appeared almost pitch black given the nighttime darkness against the worn out brick. I paused for a moment at the first step gazing up at the misshapen abode. I took a deep breath, a small cloud leaving my lips, and I made my way up the stairs. 

The door was a darkened navy blue with hints of mold lining the bottom. I reached out for the rusted golden nob before the door creaked open on its own. After a momentary pause, I pressed my hand to the door to open it the rest of the way. 

What greeted me was a dark and narrow first floor with a square spiral staircase leading upwards. I hopped in quicker than I probably should have but I was desperate to escape the cold. Snow left my shoes as I stepped on the welcome mat. After a brief look around I stomped my feet and headed upstairs. The old metal steps let out aching clanks as I made my way to room 501. 

The fifth and last floor was, oddly enough, dedicated to one room. At the top of the stairs I stood before a looming and claustrophobic hallway with plain gray walls leading to one door. The closer I got the smell of sulfur grew stronger. I had to knock on the door twice before I received an answer. 

A middle aged woman with short black hair and a warm smile greeted me on the other side of the door. It was Mrs. Morgan. She invited me inside and her demeanor made me feel more than welcomed. She wore a long sundress with heels. She appeared to be in a rush as she gave me a brief tour of her home. 

The apartment was small but cozy. The kitchen, living room and dinner table were all in the same room with no real divider. There were two doors next to the living room which presumably led to the bedrooms and one near the kitchen which was the bathroom. I was informed that Bobby, the little boy I was to babysit, was already sleeping in his room and was not to be disturbed. 

Mrs. Morgan confirmed my suspicions and said she needed to leave as she was in a hurry to meet her boyfriend for dinner. She thanked me for coming, I advised her to drive safe, and she left. I remember thinking her attire was more than inappropriate given the weather outside, especially since she did not grab a coat on her way out. 

I made my way to the kitchen in hopes of finding something in the fridge as Mrs. Morgan had insisted over the phone that I could help myself. I sat my hat on the counter and ran my hand through my hair. Its greasiness reminded me how I had planned to take a shower once I reached the place. 

The fridge was empty to my dismay so I opted to have some food delivered. I decided to shower while I waited for my food to arrive. The bathroom’s dirty tiles and walls gave it a yellowish tint. The lights were in desperate need of replacement which only added to the room’s outdated presentation. It wasn’t ideal but the thought of a hot shower after the trip I just had sounded more than delightful. I stripped, stepped in the shower, and cranked the handle to hot. It took the old faucet a moment but with a few rattles, water shot out at last. 

I finished up in the shower, pulled back the curtain and reached for the towel rack. Wiping the water from my eyes I realized I hadn’t verified that there were towels beforehand. Not sure why there wouldn’t have been. Annoyed, I hopped out of the shower, dripping water everywhere, in hopes to find folded towels under the sink. Of course, there were none. I decided to sit on the toilet and scroll through my phone while I waited to dry. For a moment I could have sworn I heard footsteps just outside the door. I wrote it off in hopes that Bobby was still sleeping. 

I threw my long sleeve shirt and jeans back on and headed out of the bathroom. To my relief, no sign of Bobby. Truthfully, I despised children. I was 18 at the time and little kids only irritated me. Not only that, but being a male babysitter left me a little embarrassed. Mannies had an awkward connotation to them, at least in my mind. Although I was anything but thrilled, I needed money much like anyone at that age. So when the job presented itself, I didn’t see the harm in making a few extra bucks. Especially when it was only one night. 

I sat down on the worn leather couch and switched on the tv which moreso served as background noise as I was on my phone for the most part. I must have fallen asleep there because I was woken up by a loud crash coming from the kitchen an hour or so later. 

I sprang off the couch and rushed to the kitchen. My heart was beating heavily, I felt every thud. I dug my fingernails into my palms as they accumulated sweat. I was worried Bobby had broken something or hurt himself while I was supposed to be keeping an eye out. I spun the corner to investigate the noise. A broken plate laid in the center of the dirtied white tile floor. It was shattered into several pieces and with no cabinets open or anything else being disrupted in the kitchen, I immediately looked up to scan the apartment for Bobby. I didn’t see or hear anything. My concentration was broken as that sulfur stench was suddenly reignited. My nose twitched and I scrunched my brow. The unpleasant scent stung my nostrils and led me to depart from the kitchen assuming that it was where the odor was originating from. After concluding that the kitchen was not responsible, I returned to pick up the pieces of the plate. 

After I finished cleaning up the mess, I attempted to sit back down on the couch and relax again. I could not shake the feeling of being uncomfortable in my own skin, especially when the bedroom doors were behind the couch and to the left, just out of my view. Admittedly I was a bit creeped out and the limited surveillance of my surroundings left me uneasy. There’s no way that kid could have smashed a plate in the kitchen and then ran back to his room without me seeing, let alone hearing him. I shifted my body and pulled a blanket close to my chest in an attempt to ease my nerves. 

There was no chance I was falling back asleep. Periodically I would turn my head back to check the bedroom doors as well as the kitchen which was just to my right and easily viewable. Eventually, my phone entranced me to the point where I was no longer focused on the phenomenon that had occurred in the kitchen moments ago. I was scrolling through instagram when a breath tickled the hairs on the back of my neck. I immediately shot up and spun around to see who was behind me only to be met with an empty room. My heart was beating fast, goosebumps bulged from my forearms, and I took deep breaths in a feeble attempt to calm myself down. My wide eyes darted around the apartment looking for any sign of another person. There was nothing.

I used my phone to check the time. It was 8:30 and Mrs. Morgan was to return home by 10 so I would only have to stay for an hour and a half, which I told myself would go by fast. I decided that the couch was a no go, so I migrated to the kitchen where I would have a full view of the apartment. 

A few minutes passed before I remembered I had ordered food and never retrieved it. I groaned as I figured my food had gone cold. I marched over to the door and pulled it open. There was nothing, however the food definitely should have been there by then. I looked out into the lightless hallway before stepping back into my apartment. I closed the door and pulled out my phone to see if I had received any messages from my delivery driver, which I had. He said he was having trouble finding room 501. He said he was at the address I shared with him but there were only four floors to the apartment. He must have gone to the wrong place I remember thinking. 

Just as I made my way back to the kitchen, the already dimly lit room lost power. The sudden surge of darkness made me jump. I stood motionless at the counter looking out into the dark void that the apartment became as my eyes slowly adjusted. The apartment was creepy enough as it were and although I realistically could have chalked up the power outage to the snowstorm, I failed to find comfort in reason. 

As the room grew more detailed, it seemed as though I could faintly make out the silhouette of a figure standing in the far right corner. My eyes watered as I squinted desperately trying to solidify what I was looking at. I remember how silent the apartment had been at that moment. For several seconds, nothing had broken the unbearable sound of nothingness until a high pitched screech, which sounded like a hefty chair being scooted across the wood floor, erupted only for a second. The sound startled me but I never took my eyes off of the thing in the corner. I was digging my nails into my palms so hard I could have drawn blood. My scalp felt prickly as sweat engulfed my hair. I turned on my phone flashlight and cautiously lifted it towards the corner. Before my light could meet the figure, a deafening groan eroded from the corner. The closest thing I can compare it to is the distressed bleating of an old goat. When my light illuminated the dark corner, any semblance of a being had evaporated. 

I was immensely disturbed and desperately checked the clock on my phone to see if Mrs. Morgan was to arrive soon. 9:00. Just sixty more minutes, I could wait it out, I remember telling myself. With the light from my phone guiding me, I hugged the wall until I reached the bathroom which I would lock myself in. 

No rational internal talk could calm me down. Something was very clearly wrong with the apartment. Everything was pitch black, the only source of light being my phone. I was grateful I had charged it beforehand as I had well over 60% left. I stared at the clock in the top left corner of my screen, waiting for time to move. Every minute felt painfully long. 

A soft hiss radiated from the bottom of the door. Shifting my light towards the noise, I could see that a folded piece of paper had been slipped under. I crouched down and picked it up. I unfolded it to see scribbles of ink. Upon further analysis it appeared to convey a message in a non English language. 

“Hello?” I called out. My voice cracked as I spoke. Almost immediately after, the hissing returned as another folded piece of paper was slid under. I instinctively pulled myself back before opening the note. This time it was in English. 

*God cannot hear you.* 

My hands began to shake more than they already were. My temples felt hot and my head started to ache. The nausea was overwhelming. The sulfur smell, although it had never left, grew stronger. The silence was deafening yet again, only broken by the growling of my empty stomach. 

The noise of sliding metal rang out as the shower curtain was yanked closed behind me. Immediately spinning around, I shined my light at the curtain which was faintly realigning itself to an idle state. I stared for a moment in terror, only deciding to vacate the bathroom once a faint whining began coming from inside the shower. It sounded almost like a dog whimpering. 

I stood up from my crouched position, swung the door open and emerged into the black apartment. Still utilizing my phone light, I traversed to Bobby’s bedroom door. I was no longer concerned with disturbing him awake as I refused to be in that place alone. I definitely wasn’t thinking straight but subconsciously I figured the company would ease my nerves. 

I worked my shaking hand towards the dull nob and opened Bobby’s room door. It let out a creak as it drifted open. I stepped in, shutting the door behind me. The sulfur smell had reached its unbearable peak. This time it caused me to physically recoil and move my unoccupied hand to my face, effectively guarding my nose and mouth. 

Bobby’s bedroom was small, leaving room only for his mattress and short wooden desk. His bed was directly in front of the door, with his desk, as well as the closet, to the right. I guided my phone light to his bed as I creeped over the left side to nudge him awake. In retrospect, that was probably a poor idea as he had never met me before as Mrs. Morgan never properly introduced us. 

I gently shoved him with my hand while muttering “Bobby…Bobby,” In a cautious whisper. I loomed my flashlight over his face which was tilted away from me. After failing to wake him up politely, I shook him a bit, causing his head to turn, revealing his maggot infested face. I physically recoiled and instinctively let out a gasp. I stumbled to the floor and scooted myself across the unevenly textured timber flooring to put as much space between me and the body. I packed my body against the wall and opened my phone in a panic, failing my password twice before successfully unlocking it. My plan to call the authorities was interrupted as I received an incoming call from Mrs. Morgan. After a slight hesitation, I answered. 

“Aaron! How are you honey? Is everything alright?”

I didn’t know what to think at that moment. The kid had clearly been deceased for days, there’s no way she was unaware. Something clearly twisted was occurring in that residence and it would have been more than reasonable to assume Mrs. Morgan was maliciously involved. In an odd way, her voice was comforting. The events that transpired prior left my mind in a state of shock and feeble helplessness. Perhaps conversing with a familiar person made me feel a bit better. 

“It’s… Bobby.” I trembled. 

“Bobby? Aaron, honey, what’s the matter?” 

“He’s dead.” 

“Oh Aaron, I know honey. I told you not to disturb him didn’t I? I really didn’t want you to have to see that.” 

“What? Mrs. Morgan, when are you coming home?”

“Honey, I’m already inside.” 

The call disconnected. I stared blankly into my phone which slowly began to blur as my eyes swelled with tears. The sound of rummaging in the closet grabbed my attention. I reluctantly drifted my phone flashlight towards the noise. The closet door was open and Mrs. Morgan was inside. Her once stylish hair was now ragged, her once vibrant sundress now engulfed with blood. She had a white glow about her, only emphasized by the darkness surrounding her. She stood there smiling from ear to ear. It looked as though she was straining her face as tears clearly developed in her beat red eyes. I knew I should have gotten up and done my best to make a run for it but I couldn’t. I had never felt that much fear in my entire life, it was paralyzing. I sat in utter awe as Mrs. Morgan and I held eye contact for what felt like forever. Her body was so still, her smile never fading.

 In a pathetic attempt to progress the evening, I called out, “Mrs. Morgan?” My voice cracked. 

A few seconds passed before she squatted down, her knees letting out cracks in the process. She crawled to the bed while panting heavily. She was then out of my sight as she was either up against the opposite side of the bed or underneath it, the dangling bedsheets did not allow me to see. 

A gluttural clicking eroded from underneath the bed.   
 My glossy eyes enlarged as I stared at the bed in anticipation. Various nightmarish scenarios inhabited my mind in an instant. I waited for her to claw her way out from under the bed to kill me the same way she killed her son. I waited for her to retreat back into the closet as I would endure more psychological torment as I sat in a bubble of paralyzed fear and anticipation. None of those came true.

Suddenly, the clicking halted as power returned to the apartment. The air was filled with the light hum of the ceiling fan above Bobby’s bed. The sulfur stench had faded and Bobby’s corpse was spontaneously nowhere to be found. With the returned comfort of the light, I broke from my paralyzed state and walked steadily towards the door. I didn’t run, I had developed the irrational thought that any sudden movements might set something off. 

As I cautiously opened the door, I could hear the sound of chopping and sloshing growing louder. I peaked out into the dimly lit apartment, just enough to see where the sound was coming from. It was clear it was coming from the kitchen, which I would have to pass if I were to leave. Swallowing my cowardice, I lunged out of Bobby’s room and bolted for the exit. My body crashed into the door and I desperately yanked on the decayed handle, only for the door to hardly budge. My hand had been stained with a bluish green residue as the splintery wood door had been engulfed in mold. It was as if the already dated apartment was withering away with every second I spent in there.   

Noises continued and going against everything in my body telling me not to, I looked in the kitchen. There sat Mrs. Morgan, facing away from me. She was on top of somebody, clearly a man. She clenched a dull kitchen knife in her hand, raised it above her head, and drove it into the man’s torso. The mass of blood on the floor and lack of retaliation coming from the man made it clear he had been dead a while; that didn’t stop her from hacking away at his lifeless corpse.

I quickly snapped my head away to focus on the door. The unbearable sounds of crunching and metal scraping echoed throughout the apartment. My mind wondered as the noises filled my head with terrible ideas of what the scene in the kitchen might have looked like. Trying to leave was futile; something was keeping the door shut, as if someone was holding the knob with all their might from the other side. 

Tears ran down my face, I gave up trying to hold them back. My whole body ached. My intestines were doing gymnastics in my gut. The fact that I didn’t vomit was a miracle. Despite knowing that my efforts were futile, I pulled on that door with everything in my power, even propping my foot against the wall to give me some extra power. Suddenly, the noises in the kitchen ceased. I reluctantly spun my head to face the it. Mrs. Morgan was standing there, facing me with the knife in her hand. She wasn’t smiling anymore, she looked angry. Her brow creased, her expression tightened and she pressed her lips together. Our tense eye contact was broken as she took a step forward, effectively sending me into flight mode. I bolted for the bathroom. I was just a few feet away from the door, her footsteps grew louder as she was closing the distance. Her gravely pants were heavy, seething through her gritted teeth. Her footsteps grew louder. When I finally reached the door, my shadow on the wall was met with hers as she was just behind me. Applying pressure to the handle, I shouldered the door with most of my weight to get it open. 

Tripping into the bathroom, I spun around to close the door, reengaging in eye contact with my pale tormentor. Before the door slammed, there was a brief moment where I got a good look at Mrs. Morgan. Her limbs were thin, only emphasized by her holding the knife above her head in preparation to swing. Her elbows and joints protruded through her slender skin. She looked as though she was decaying, her pallid skin was practically ready to fall off the bone. Blood dirtied areas of her face while the rest of her body was effectively drenched, further bringing out her red strained eyes. 

I locked the door and moved frantically around the bathroom looking for anything I could use to defend myself. The door shook as she knocked with an imposing amount of force, infecting the air with deafening bangs. There was a window in the bathroom, I had not remembered it being there before. Actually, I was positive it had not been there before. I didn’t have much of a choice, so I opened it planning to climb out. I propped the window open and stuck my head out. I was met with an unexplainable darkness. The void was darker than black, it was nothingness. I wasn’t sure what would happen if I leaped out into the ocean of shadows. She's just a scrawny middle aged woman for God’s sake, surely I could take her in a fight, what was I even scared about? I can remember thinking. That incentive changed in an instant as the door swung open revealing the closest thing I’ve ever seen to a demon. Her skin a dark gray, her eyes engulfed in red. Her limbs had elongated, her knuckles nearly scraping the floor beneath her. So I did the reasonable thing and took my chances lunging out of the window into the big nothing. 

As I descended, the apartment faded away while I fell deeper and deeper into the jaws of the abyss, not sure where I would end up. After minutes of falling, my screaming ceased. I thought that I might fall forever. I took in the dark around me while I fell faster. It felt like flying down a rollercoaster, the wind hitting your face making you squint coupled with the adrenaline rush rarely experienced anywhere else. I experienced that for several minutes while only gaining speed as I trickled through the air. 

I felt cold on my palms and part of my face as I lay face down in the snow strewn alley. When I came to, I was in a panic, stumbling to my feet panting. As I collected myself, I gathered that I was in the alleyway beside the apartment building. I didn’t spend much time pondering what had just happened. I was quick to dart out of the alley and make my way to my car. 

Since that night, I am still not sure what had happened to me with little evidence to prove that the night even transpired. Messages with Mrs. Morgan had been erased from my phone and there was no fifth floor to the building. No one with the last name Morgan had ever been a tenant.
