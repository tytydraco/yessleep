Update (08/29/26):

I am not in a good place, physically or mentally, to be typing at the moment, so I will keep this update short. I got a little too close, waited a little too long before I moved again and it almost cost me everything. Not that I have much to give up at this point but still. I heard my name. I can swear on it. It was an amalgamation of sound, unlike any voice or voices I have ever heard. One was apparent in the mess though. My mother’s. It was almost hypnotizing. A warm sense of comfort overcame me, a comfort I haven’t felt since this all began. I wanted to go towards it. Trudge backwards in hopes that I could maybe, just maybe, go back to my mother’s embrace.   
Seeing the black void as I turned to the sound broke the spell. I jumped straight into my car and drove until I couldn’t. I don’t want to rule this as a finite thing. I don’t want to believe that my mother, my family, is gone. This isolation is driving me to the point where I feel like I don’t have anyone at all. I know I will find someone again, or something, but that day seems to inch farther and farther out of my grasp. I should have just stayed with them. My time to write is dwindling away, I will update when I can.



Update (09/03/26):

  
I realize now looking back that I probably confused you. Ambiguity drives me crazy too. I did leave my family behind. I could say it was out of fear but that is just cowardly. Being fearful in a good way is telling them what I had seen and went through before I left, even if they didn’t believe me, and dragging them along with me. Not leaving them to find out for themselves. But with the sheer seriousness of the situation now, I’m sure more than just them found out. Who knows if they were smart enough to run like I did. All I know is that countless more lives are at stake and I am not going to get anywhere if I keep feeling responsible. I know that sounds selfish, but it’s also selfless to leave my problems in the past. And speaking of, I guess I should pick up where I had left off before.  
I was a mess when I arrived home that night. I drove around later than I had wanted to and got home around 10:30 pm. That thirty minutes was enough time in Rich’s head to grill me for being late to our call. I got onto my computer and opened the voice channel of our discord server to see Rich and Jackson in a call. I assumed Jackson had been updated on the situation by Rich in the time that I had been gone. My assumptions were correct when I joined the call to a screaming match between the two.



“Well, I don’t know what that thing is either, but you can’t expect me to-”, Jackson yelled, pausing for a moment after seeing my face in the call.



“Dean? Are you okay? You’re almost as pale as Ms. Jenkins.” Rich said with a shaky voice.

  
“April left me.”

  
“Oh no, now I feel bad for harassing you to get on here.”  
	

“Are you good, dude?” Jackson asked.  
	

“I will be. It’s honestly not the most concerning thing on my mind right now.”  
	

“What do you mean?” Rich asked.  
	

“It happened again.”  
	

“See, I told you Jackson! I wasn’t lying and I’m not crazy-”  
	

“Shhh, let him speak.” Jackson quieted Rich with a sharp interest in what I had to say.  
	

“I was driving to clear my mind. I guess I wondered out that way in my little fit and then I saw it.” My voice started to tremble.  
	

“What did you see?” Brittany asked, appearing in the background of Jackson’s face cam.  
	

“Does she really have to be here, Jackson?”  
	

“I can’t help that you dragged me into this call either, Rich.” Jackson replied sternly.  
	

I began to speak again when I noticed all of them staring, “It was a car. A sideways car. It was caught like your drone, but only halfway. Almost as if it swerved into it. And the person in it. It was worse than Ms. Jenkins. Them and half of the car were completely washed in white and grey. Even the headlight of the car seemed to just fade away.”  
	

“Did you try to help them?” Jackson asked.  
	

“You can’t. I got out to look closer though. Their head was out of their window. They were looking up at it, just like Ms. Jenkins was. And it just kept creeping towards me. I think it's getting worse.”  
	

“We have to see this.” They all said in unison. A clouded conversation proceeded.  
	

“Guys, we can’t go back.”  
	

“What, why? We just have to be careful.” Rich exclaimed.  
	

“And we haven’t even got to see it yet.” Jackson said in disappointment.  
	

“We can’t risk it. I have seen so much loss just within the past day. I don’t want to lose anything else!”  
	

“Well, did you tell April?” Brittany poked out again.  
	

“Asking the important questions, good job!” Rich said sarcastically.  
	

“I did, but there’s no telling if she believed me or not. She thought I was having an episode.”  
	

“Well I will make you a deal. We will believe you if you show us tomorrow. And, Brittany can talk to April for you. I’m sure you’d rather not.” Jackson said, nearly at the edge of his seat.  
	

“You’re right about one thing, but I don’t want to risk anything. So no.”  
	

“I can take you!” Rich piped up.  
	

“Sweet!”  
	

“Rich, please, don’t go.”  
	

“We will be careful, I promise.”  
	

“I can’t reason with any of you!”  
	

I shut my computer off in a blind fury. Every inch of muscle and bone in my body shivered as the thoughts of what could happen to them played out in my mind. A carousel that sped up faster than any safe for a child to ride. I sat with my fists balled on my forehead as I heard a couple of taps on my door.  
	

“Son, I know it’s summer break, but I don’t want to hear you yelling at that computer from the kitchen!”  
	

I turned to my father with tears rolling down my face.  
	

“Oh, what’s wrong?”  
	

“I’m just so scared.”  
	

“Did she break up with you?”   
	

Not wanting to tell him about the countless other things going on, I immediately jumped to the thing he had asked me.  
	

“She did.”  
	

“Why are you scared by it?”  
	

“I just don’t know what’s going to happen in the future. If there is a future.”  
	

“Son, don’t be so down. I know you “loved” this girl but it is not the end of your life. Like I said at dinner, there are probably so many girls waiting for their chance to get with you. You just have to take it one day at a time.”  
	

“Today was a very shitty day though.”  
	

“Well who’s to say tomorrow won’t be better? Lift your head up boy, everything will be okay.”  
	

“Okay.”  
	

“I love you, son. Now get some sleep. Tomorrow will come sooner.”  
	

“I love you too, dad.”  
	

I slid out of my chair and went towards my bed as my eyelids fast forwarded to daylight. The light had only met my eyes for five minutes before my mother came to my doorway to tell me that Rich, Jackson, and Brittany were outside waiting for me. Thankfully I was dressed this time so no hips would be bruised again. I stomped outside in a fit of rage and confronted them.  
	

“I thought I made it clear not to take them, Rich!”  
	

“I’m not taking them. We are taking them.”  
	

I looked in his car to see his passenger seat empty, waiting for me to sit down.  
	

“No no no, I’m not going with you. Are you crazy?”  
	

“You are going with us. You were the one to lead me out to it. We need you to lead us again! Plus I don’t know where the car is.”  
	

“Fine. But we are not staying for long. We are showing them and then leaving, got it?”  
	

“Sir, yes sir!”  
	

“I hate you sometimes, do you know that?”  
	

“I love you too.”  
	

We set our sites on the tattered vehicle not knowing the state that we would stumble upon it in.  
	

“Alright, where are we going?” Rich asked.  
	

“Just head towards Ms. Jenkins house, it’s pretty hard to miss.” I responded.  
	

“Great, so you’re taking us out into the middle of nowhere.”  
	

“Brittany, just hush. I know it’s going to bother you not being able to text your other boyfriends but you can wait for five minutes.” Rich spatted out.  
	

“Other boyfriends? Brit, what does he mean?” Jackson piped up.  
	

Rich turned to me, looking at me with wide, beady eyes, knowing the force that he had just awoken. I lipped, “What the fuck,” back towards him, side eyeing him with every form of anger and judgment that I could portray with my face. An argument consumed the cabin, and continued to consume the cabin as I pointed directions out to Rich, still not looking at him dead on.  
	

After a long, grueling five minutes, we arrived. Or at least got as close as we could. The thundering voices coming from the back seat halted in an instant. The “Grey Area” completely engulfed the car and trickled into the cornfield on the other side of the road. The stalks in the zone looked ready for harvest in contrast to the stalks on the outside. Some looked gradient like, the green seeping slowly away.  
	

“What the hell?” the two in the backseat said in sequence.  
	

“We weren’t lying to you.” Rich responded for me, as I was still too pissed off that they dragged me out here in the first place.  
	

We all stepped out and got closer to the area. Looking farther in, the person who hung from the car almost looked like they had rapidly aged. Their skin had sunk in, every vein in their body protruding from their skin. Their hair had lost its color and turned into a silky white. Their eyes looked like pearls sitting inside of pockets of pure white flesh. A mess of gained and lost detail.  
	

“I think I’m going to throw up.” Brittany whined.  
	

“Brit, just go sit in the car.” Jackson said.  
	

She responded with an eye roll and pranced back to the car.  
	

“Should we call someone?” Jackson asked.  
	

“It’s probably best that we don’t. I don’t want to attract any more attention to this.” I responded.  
	

“I mean I get that, but look at them!” Jackson exclaimed.  
	

“Do you really think we can do anything about that? She looks like a grocery bag.” Rich said, scoffing.  
	

“And THAT is why I don’t want to bring anyone else out here.”  
	

“That is fair, I guess. So this is coming from that?” Jackson said, nudging his head up towards the sky.  
	

“Well we think so, but we don’t exactly know. It seems like it just, I don't know, pauses everything, and takes the color away?” I began to fiddle in my pockets and pulled out a coin, “Watch this.”  
	

I threw the coin into it. At first it glided right through the threshold. But as it fell it began to slow, stopping inches from the ground. Jackson’s eyes widened.  
	

“Why is that person’s head stuck out of the window? There’s no way they could’ve done that in there.” Rich asked.  
	

“That part, I don’t know.” I responded.  
	

“Brit!” Jackson yelled, “Come check this out!”  
	

Reluctantly, Brittany propped the car door open and stumbled up to us, annoyed.  
	

“What! Wait, why is that coin floating?”  
	

“Did we not tell you about my drone, too?” Rich said, pointing up at the sky.  
	

We all turned towards his finger, seeing his drone in the distance. A startling amount of birds were strung up there like Halloween decorations.  
	

“This is insane. Jackson, I want to go. This is way too scary. Rich take us home!” Brittany rambled, walking up to Rich and grabbing him by the shoulders of his shirt.  
	

“Okay okay, we can go. Dean?”\`  
	

I nodded in overwhelming approval. As we began to turn to the car, a loud truck horn blared out behind us. We turned around to spot a semi truck, not showing signs of slowing down, heading straight for the area. As he got closer, he poked his head out of his window, presumably to examine the sight that lay before him. I bolted towards the semi, waving for them to stop. As he looked back at me a loud screech consumed our ears. And then…Bang! The front end of the semi skidded sideways and began to flip over. The mangled mess of blood and bones of a man flung out of the cab and into the area. His limbs suspended and bent in unnatural ways, like a tangled marionette. His blood froze in blotches and beads. If he somehow lived it, he wouldn’t last for much longer. The semi followed, tumbling into the back end of the car. The car came out untouched as the trailer of the semi seemingly balanced on top of it. A deafening screech turned into stillness in a matter of seconds, until.  
	

Brittany screamed at the top of her lungs. I could almost see them poke up through her throat. She turned and ran to the car.   
	

“Get me out of here!” She repeated.  
	

“We are going, we are going.” I said, motioning the boys to the car.  
	

We got back into the car and sped away. A deafening silence slithered into the air once again. Rich stared at the road as he had when we first saw Ms. Jenkins. Pulling up to Jackson’s house, Rich parked and locked the doors and nudged me. At that que I began to turn to them.  
	

“This is not to be spoken of, I cannot stress it enough.”  
	

“We have to say something. People are going to get hurt.” Jackson said softly.  
	

“We can’t, we don’t know how people are going to react. Shit, I’m sure that if that car wasn’t there, you would’ve tried to go in it.”  
	

“There are already dead people in it. We only know of three but there could be more. The longer we sit and stay quiet the more random people are going to stumble their way into it and…” Jackson said, abruptly getting cut off.  
	

“He’s right, Dean.” Rich said, Brittany began to sob softly in the back.  
	

“Even if we were going to tell someone, who would believe us?” I said to Rich.  
	

“We believed you. We saw what this thing can do, what it’s doing.” Jackson replied, “Now, can you let us out?”  
	

The click of the door locks sprung me into a state of dread, not knowing what was going to happen after they went inside.  
	

“Do you want to do anything?” Rich asked.  
	

“Just take me home.”  
	

Another quiet drive ended in my driveway. I went to get out but got the same treatment as the other two.  
	

“Everything is going to be okay, Dean. You’ve got to believe that.”  
	

“But what if it isn’t Rich?”  
	

“I don’t know, but we can’t sit and wait it out until it turns to that point.”  
	

These words resonated with me with all of the wrong reasoning.  
	

“I’ll see you later, Dean. Stay safe.”  
	

“You too.”  
	

Little did he know, he wouldn’t see me later. In my first update of this post, I mentioned that I ran away. This was the night that I would. After isolating myself in my room until midnight, I rummaged what I could into my suitcase and prepared myself for my selfish indulgence into the state I am in now.   
	

Before I left, I went into my sister’s room to give her a kiss on her forehead. Not even thinking about the consequences of not telling my parents about any of this going in there, she was the only one on my mind. I loved my family, still do, but a soul as innocent as hers, in my mind, should know that the brother that she looked up to growing up would never forget her. I worked up the courage and opened the door with a squeak. And as I stepped closer her eyes shot open.  
	

“Dean?” She said groggily, unsure that the figure that had stumbled into her room was her brother.  
	

“Yes, Sarah.” I said shakily, not expecting to wake her up.  
	

I knelt down beside her.  
	

“Why are you crying?” She asked, concerned.  
	

“I’m crying because I love you. I want you to know that.”  
	

“What’s going on?” She asked, startled after seeing the suitcase in the doorway.  
	

“I am going away for a while, okay?”  
	

“W-when will you be back?” She said, starting to cry.  
	

“I-I don’t know, Sarah.” I said, tears beginning to stream down my face.  
	

“Where are you going?” She asked.  
	

Not knowing what to say, I blurted, “I’m going to stay with grandpa for a bit.”  
	

“Can I come?”  
	

“No Sarah, I need you to hold down the house while I’m gone.”  
	

“What does that mean?” She asked, confused.  
	

“It means you have to be strong. Can you be a strong girl while I’m gone?”  
	

“Oh yes!” She exclaimed.  
	

“Shhh, I like your enthusiasm. But we have to be quiet. I love you, Sarah.”  
	

“I love you too, bubby.” She said overly softly in response.  
	

As I turned she whispered, “I’ll come visit you with mommy and daddy soon.”, and turned back over to go to sleep.  
	

“I hope so.” I said to myself as I left her room and walked out the front door. I got in my car and drove and stopped at a motel about fifty miles outside of town. And that’s what I have been doing since then, nor have I seen them since then. I haven’t seen any of them since then and I don’t know if I ever will. I juggled with the thoughts of what could happen to them the entire stay, and still do to this day. I wish I wasn’t so afraid of what could’ve happened. But as I had mentioned before, they most likely have found out by now. And not knowing in what way haunts me the most. That is all I have to say about that.   
	

Update (09/14/26):

  
You may sense a repetition in me saying this, but again, sorry for the delay in my updates. I have spent the past week or so gathering supplies and rummaging through empty shops for extra money. It is so weird, I have seen the news channels and they seem to be displaying the phenomenon, but it seems like it is not affecting people the way it has affected me. Everywhere that I go that isn’t isolated seems to be just moving along normally. People are just living their day to day lives. I don’t know if it is some sort of surrender, but I would think a surrender would consist of anarchy and chaos. I don’t know, I could be reading into it too deep but who knows.

  
Anyways, for some promising news, a familiar truck pulled into the lot of the motel I am currently residing at. I am not going to jump to conclusions and get myself excited just in case it leads to disappointment, but I think I saw Jackson. I’m not sure if he is with anyone else but I am going to keep an eye out. I can’t be a coward this time and leave myself alone for even longer. And even if it isn’t him I could possibly make a new friend to ride out this storm with. I will keep you guys posted as soon as I find out.
