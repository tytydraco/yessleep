It’s funny how easy it can be to dismiss an uncomfortable thought; that sound was just the wind, that shadow was just you seeing things, that watching feeling is just your nerves. Everyone does it, and I’ll admit I’ve been especially guilty of it. I, and likely many others, might refer to me as something close to skeptical when it comes to anything out of the ordinary. Friends purposefully avoided telling me their ghost story (everyone seems to have one for some reason), or about that time their grandmother saw angels in the room or whatever else. I just never really gave much credence to things that didn’t have a concrete explanation. And, at first, I tried to do the same with what had started happening to me.

I was on the daily commute to work when I first saw it. I work downtown and there’s always a bit of stop-and-start traffic along the way. I was in the ‘stop’ phase when my eyes aimlessly drifted over to the car next to me. I met the gaze of the driver, a brunette man with glasses who might have been my type if I was on the hunt. The look lingered a little past the threshold for awkward and I looked away. The sea of cars lurched forward slowly as I read a passing billboard. An advertisement for some new feature on Meta that promised to “deepen your connection with the global community.” It was the same kind of inflated BS the copywriters I work with might come up with; flowery, overdone, but apparently and unfortunately effective.

A car passed into the lane in front of me as we moved forward and through the back windshield, I saw a small pair of eyes peered at me from just above the backseat. A child, hands on either side of the half-face that was locked onto me. My gaze met theirs and I gave a small smile and wave. There was no response from the kid, other than to continue their staring, which they didn’t break until I pulled off on the exit away from them.

Working downtown, parking was scarce, so I always elected to park a little further out where it was easier and walk the rest of the way. Some days that was a great choice, others not so much, depending on rain, heat, those kinds of things. Today, it was a great decision; the sun was just pleasantly warm enough to heat the cooler air and the building windows glimmered in the light. It was still mid-morning so the streets weren’t quite crowded, but they weren’t empty either. About a third of the way into my walk, I passed someone else. I looked up and smiled at them, a gesture they had beat me to, and continued walking. I thought nothing else of it, until it had happened four more times. The same every time; I would look up, and, immediately, a face already ready to greet me before I had fully even registered the glance.

My first thought was maybe I had something on my face that day, or my shirt was inside out, or my underwear was peeking through a hole in my favorite jeans, I don’t know. I’m not an ugly girl, but I wasn’t cocky enough to think it was just ‘cause I’m pretty either. I only really started to feel genuinely uncomfortable when I passed my favorite local coffee shop and noticed, for the first time that day, that it wasn’t just a few quick idle glances. Every single face, some highlighted by the bloom of the sunlight, some dulled by the subtle tint of the windows, faced me as I walked by. I never even saw them turn to stare; every time I shifted my gaze to someone else, their eyes were already trained on me.

Their expressions differed; some seemed like they were trying to hide it, others were shamelessly gawping like a deer in the highbeams, some smiled. There was one woman who brought her hand to her chest like a mother in a moment when her child has made her proud. I stopped, trading gazes with strangers. My eyes locked on one table in particular, a couple as far as I could tell, sat near the edge of the patio fence. Had this been a regular day, I’m sure the intensity of the man’s leering would have elicited some harsh words from his partner about gawking or not paying attention to her, but that didn’t happen. The woman sitting across from him looked at me just as intensely. And the worst part was they seemed to continue their conversation like nothing was off, speaking with one another while jointly zeroed in on me instead of each other.

I picked up my pace and continued my stroll to work, avoiding meeting the looks of anyone else. I rushed silently to the bathroom and closed myself in a stall. My brain still struggled to provide a reasonable explanation for what I had seen, and I sat on the lid of the toilet, needing a moment to gather myself rather than any response to a call of nature. I took my phone out of my pocket, intent on opening the front-facing camera to see if I could figure out what everyone had been looking at.

Seventy-seven. I had seventy-seven notifications. When I had to work, I turned off any kind of notification alert, so I hadn’t heard it on the way, but nearly every person in my contact list had either texted me, called me, or both. I held the phone in amazement. The messages were all mundane greetings or attempts at initiating conversation, even from people I hadn’t spoken to in years. I only made it through about a quarter of them before the screen shifted to display an incoming call. My mother.

“Oh! Oh, honey, I finally got you.”

“Yeah, h-hi, Mom. What’s up?”

“Oh, nothing!” There was a somewhat awkward silence on the line.

“Right, I-well, I’m at work today.”

“Shoot, that’s right, I’m sorry, honey!”

“It’s fine…what did you need? Everything okay?”

“I just wanted to hear your voice.” Her reply came with the warmth only a mother could provide, but it unnerved me a bit. My mom…well she wasn’t really the sentimental type.

“Okay…well, anyway, I probably ne-”

“Oh, did I mention your father was here? Say hi, honey, it’s Alice.” There was a shifting sound as the phone rustled in their grip. It lasted longer than I’d expected. There was a light whispering, a little combative, that I swear sounded something like “give it here”, but I couldn’t quite make it out. When the rustling finally stopped, my father’s voice broke through the other end.

“Hey, sweetie. You doin’ okay?”

“Yeah, Dad, I’m-everything’s fine. I gotta-” my reply was cut off as another incoming call flashed on the screen. Tressa, my best friend, was calling as well. A chill overtook me; Tressa and I had been friends since high school and I could probably count on one hand the amount of times we’d actually spoken on the phone rather than texting. The small bit of silence from my end prompted a response from the other end.

“Honey, are you there?” my mother said worriedly.

“Sweetie! Where’d you go? Hello?” my dad’s voice echoed, the same tone present in his voice.

“Alice, Alice, talk to us!”

“Mom, Dad, Jesus!” I huffed.

“Oh, oh you’re there. Oh, thank goodness.”

“We thought you had left us, we-” my father said with a sigh.

“Guys, listen, I have to go, I’m at work.”

“Oh, uhh, right. Yeah, I guess.”

“Well, honey, can you at least call us when you get a moment?”

“I-you said everything is okay? Is there something you need to tell me?” I asked.

“Don’t worry, sweetie.”

“Everything is a-okay, honey.”

“We just-we just want to hear your voice.”

“Okay, I…I have to go.”

“Alice! Alice, wa-” I clicked to end the call, and just as quickly, the screen flashed awake again: six new texts popped up before another incoming call, this time from an ex I hadn’t even realized still had my number.

“What the fuck?” I said in a hushed tone before turning my phone off. As I went to put it back in my purse, a few harsh thumps rang out from in front of me, and it fell from my grip to the floor. Someone was knocking on the stall door.

“Alice? That you in there?” The voice sounded vaguely familiar, but not enough for me to place it.

“Yes, I’ll be out in just a minute.” I said with a purposefully annoyed tone to my voice.

“Okay, great, I can wait.” the voice responded cheerfully.

“I-what? No, no you don’t need to wait. I’ll be out in a bit.”

“No, no worries.”

“What-who is this?” I asked sharply.

“It’s Madeleine.”

I didn’t know any Madeleine. “Who?”

“Madeleine…Madeleine Turner. We worked on the Davidson account.” the voice responded, a somewhat disappointing turn to their voice. I still didn’t recognize the name, but the account mentioned had taken place months ago. By this point, I was starting to get angry.

“Okay, well I’ll be out when I’m out, can you please just give me a moment?”

“Of course!” The reply came with the previous cheerfulness, but wasn’t followed by any other sound. She was still just waiting.

“Okay, listen, can you please leave the room!? I just-I need a goddamn minute, okay!?”

There was a light silence before the voice replied with a new timidity to it. “Yeah…uh, yeah sure, I guess. I guess I can just wait outside for you. And you won’t be long?”

“What? No, God, can you just fuck off!? Who does this to someone while they’re in the bathroom!?”

“I…” the voice was somewhat hushed, and I struggled to understand it fully. What I thought I heard sent a shiver down my spine. “I just want to hear your voice.”

I leaned down slowly, my gaze on the red leather buckled slippers that stood pointed directly in front of the stall door. “Please just get out!” I yelled, emphasizing the last two words as best I could. The slippers shifted slightly before the voice spoke again.

“Okay…” the voice came soft as the slippers moved toward the door of the restroom. As it opened, another voice rang out from the lobby.

“Hey, have you seen Alice? Is she here yet?” a male voice.

“Yeah, she’s in there. She’s in a mood today.” Madeleine replied.

“Ahh okay, good, we can just wai-” the voice cut off as the door swung closed.

I felt bitter tears, a mix of frustration, anger, and fear, filling my eyes. This had been a bizarre morning, I was all kinds of worked up, but had I overreacted? Shit, I was at work and that interaction had been anything but professional on my part. The way Madeleine had acted was weird, sure, but maybe she was just an awkward person. And I had screamed and cursed at her. Rude, but functionally, that could come back to bite me if she filed a report or something. I gathered myself, adjusting my hair slightly and patting at the corners of my eyes. I exited the stall to an empty restroom. There was the gentle murmuring of conversation outside and I took a deep breath before pushing open the restroom door.

There was a crowd. As far as I could tell, every coworker I recognized, and many I didn’t, had gathered nearby outside the restroom, their faces already snapped in place looking directly at me. I watched a wave of varying emotions cascade over the crowd, each face locked and motionless, but shifting into expression. Fear dropped into my stomach, and I legitimately worried I was about to be the subject of a rush or swarm, but…nothing. They just stared. It honestly might have been worse than what I had anticipated.

Slowly, very slowly, I started walking forward into the building, the still and silent crowd trained on me as I went. I only got about halfway to the elevator before the first of them approached me, one of the others on my team, Erik.

“Alice, hey, great to see you!”

“So great!” came an unfamiliar voice from elsewhere in the room.

“Yeah…yeah, hi.” I said, averting my gaze as I continued to walk forward.

“So, how ya been? Anything new?” Erik’s voice continued behind me.

“Not since yesterday. I’m-I’m just gonna head to my office.”

“Oh, perfect, I’m headed that way too!” Erik’s reply was followed by several voices in the crowd that cheerfully echoed their destination and began moving forward toward the elevator.

“Okay,” I turned quickly in frustration to meet the sea of eager peering faces, “what is going on here? Is this a prank or something? Why the hell is everyone looking at me and following me and talking to me and-”

The reply was a static hiss of overlapping voices.

“Noo, nothing like-”

“Oh, Alice, you-”

“We just want to hear your-”

“Just want some company-”

Erik was closest and his reply stood out from the rest. “No, no prank! Everything okay, Alice? I’m here if you want to talk about it. You can tell me anything.”

“I am FINE! Everyone just needs to leave me alone! I just-I just wanna get through my day, can you all just leave me alone!?”

The voices didn’t stop, but their tone shifted; the pitch and volume dropped to a low drone with a hint of concern.

“You don’t seem fine, I can te-”

“Might help to talk to-”

“What is it you don’t want to-”

“I just want to be near-”

“Come on, Alice, let me-”

And that was it. My skepticism broke. I had thought, hoped, it might be something mundane, as most things are. Even against bizarre evidence to the contrary, I had tried to reason it away. But, in the very few scenarios that are not mundane, there comes a moment. A specific indicator that you have left Kansas, and things might be going in a way that most people will never experience. Looking at the crowd, an overwhelming flood of voices pouring from constantly watching faces both familiar and not, that moment came for me. Fuck work, fuck the social niceties, I couldn’t do this; something rooted deeply in me pulled fear from my core to every edge of my body, and I knew I had to get away.

I broke, wordlessly, into a sprint for the front door. I threw my gaze to the floor in front of me, desperate to avoid the intrusive stares. The voices raised in volume, their tone growing to one of worry.

“Alice, Alice, wait!”

“No, no, no, don’t-”

“Please don’t go!”

“We just want to hold y-”

“Alice! Come back!”

As I moved through the crowd, the swell of voices threw out physical weaponry; grasping hands reached for me, pulling at my clothes, my hair. I twisted to avoid the closing walls of the crowd. I felt a bolt of my hair lurch back as I pushed forward against it. A sound like ripping wet cardboard and a stabbing pain in my scalp as the tuft was yanked out by the root. My purse began to slide back off my shoulder, and a potent terror caused me to clench it. I thrust my hand into it and grabbed my car keys before it flung backwards into the writhing sea of hands. I was almost to the end.

The front door in sight, I lunged forward as my overshirt was wrenched behind me. I pulled out of it, the force of doing so sending me face first into the glass door as it opened. Pain painted my sense of the world, a ringing in my ears coupled with a haze-like vertigo. I shoved the door open with all my strength and stumbled out into the open air, the crowd behind beginning to move forward slowly.

Breaking back into a sprint, my pursuers followed one by one out of the door, shambling and shoving against one another. Ahead, those I had passed on my way previously were now standing, faces still trained on me. Indefinite and instinct voices raised from them as they joined the chase. I turned for just a moment to see a now-horde picking up their pace amidst shouting and intense, wide-eyed staring. Towards the front of the group, a solitary man ran pushing a stroller, which rippled and bounced violently against the street beneath.

My chest was heaving, my breath hot and quick as the view of my car finally, mercifully, came into sight. I clicked the key fob’s auto start function, and desperately hoped I would wake up to find this all a nightmare once I reached the driver’s seat. I stopped sharply, opening the door as the voices rose behind me, getting close, dangerously close. I threw myself into the driver’s seat, a single arm reaching for me as I did so. A large, shirtless man I didn’t recognize in athletic shorts, who breathed a deep inhale through his nose as he reached for me, a tense look in his eye like the stalking gaze of a wild animal.

“Let me come with you! Please!”

I screamed and pushed his arm away as I slammed the door. He stumbled backward and reached for me again. There was a wet crunch as the door closed and pieces of the index, middle, and ring fingers of the man fell into the floorboard at my feet. I pulled forward, colliding with the corner of the car in front of me as the swarm reached the back windshield. I floored the accelerator. The grinding, hollow screech of metal filled my senses, drowning out the thumping that was moving forward along the back of the car. A violent lurch sent me hard against the driver’s seat as my car pulled off of and away from the obstacle that had held it.

I looked into the rear view mirror, the crowd still in pursuit, faces still locked in place as if we held eye contact through both glass and distance. I sped down the road, slamming a palm against the steering wheel and giving vent to a noxious scream, my breath a distilled vapor of every negative feeling I had felt in my life. I swerved to avoid vehicles full of watching eyes and the obstacles of the city. To my surprise, not one vehicle gave chase; a few people got out of their cars, eyes still intent on me, but, at least for now, it seemed they had given up their pursuit.

Even now, my mind tried to form an explanation: they had tried to grab me, pull at me, but none had tried to full blown attack me. No tackling, hitting, nothing. I reached for the missing section of my hair, a dull burn as I touched it and brought back a smooch of blood on my fingers. It had been pulled out, but was that because I had sprung forward when they grabbed it? Were they trying not to really hurt me? As I thought, the low sound of weeping began to rise. Stark terror surged into my fingertips.

I wasn’t crying.

I threw my eyes to the backseat. To my relief, it was empty. It should have been a relief, at least, since I had confirmed that I was alone in the car. But my fear only heightened as the sound of weeping continued. It was a deep sound, thick and groaning in a way I hated. Gooseflesh rippled down my arms as the weeping shifted into what I could tell were definite words with no definite source.

“I’m so sorry, little one.”

I felt like I couldn’t breathe. Was I going insane? The voice continued.

“I had hoped this time would be different.”

My eyes darted around the car. I felt my ears, shifted the knob of the car’s radio, everything I could think of to try to explain what I was hearing. Nothing. A disembodied voice I had never heard was speaking words I wasn’t thinking. I was sure I had lost my mind, that maybe everything I had experienced so far was a product of hallucination or the morbid fantasy of a warped, unreliable brain. I set my vision to the road ahead and white-knuckled the steering wheel. The worst thing you can do for delusion is give in, and I wouldn’t do that.

“I understand if you do not wish to speak with me. I hope you will reconsider. I would prepare you for what is to come.” The voice was low and high, deep and shrill, the voice of my mother and my father and every person I’d met and never met all at once. It spoke so clearly and collectedly, yet still through sobs with all the weight of centuries of tragedy. Sweat mixed with the blood of my forehead wound and a dull red rivulet ran down the side of my face as I made eye contact with myself in the rear view mirror. “Alice.”

My name said in a tongue that couldn’t exist. I hated the way it sounded. My stomach churned with pangs of anxious dread. I was insane. I was hallucinating. I had gotten a concussion on the door. I was having a special kind of panic attack. I ran through every reasonable explanation I could hopelessly invent before a kind of venomous curiosity made me open my mouth.

“What…what the fuck is this?” I said coldly.

“It is a mistake. My mistake.”

“What does that-what are you?” There was silence for a moment.

“I am One.”

“I don’t know what that means!” I shouted angrily. “Can you just-what is happening!? What do I need to be prepared for!?”

The sobs felt more pronounced now amidst the swirl of vocal impressions. “They will continue to come. They have felt me and now it is all they are. They do not want to harm you.”

I struggled to swallow a lack of spit. “What do they want?”

“Absorption.”

“What-”

“They long to dissolve themselves into your presence. A need to feel my love.”

My chest constricted, a pressure like the weight of a mountain that made breath a miracle. “Love? How is this…what are you?”

“I am One. I have always been One. I longed for more. In one mote of the darkness of eternity, I felt others. I felt you. I felt those before you. I will feel others after you. Perhaps then, it will be different.” The voice was at once deeply regretful and hopeful.

“So, what, what is happening to me? If you’re doing this to me, just stop! Leave!”

“I am bound to you. My love pours from you as it had from those before. Your kind will seek to forever be in its presence. It will be their end. It will be your end. Then I will be unbound. Unloved. Then I will bind to the next. Until I am finally More.” There was something like tragedy ringing through its tone that brought unwilling tears to my eyes.

“Why me? Why did you do this to me!?”

“You were the first of your kind I felt.”

Searing anger ran through my blood. “So it was…random. You fucked my whole life on a whim!”

“I am sorry, little one. More than you could understand.”

“Oh, fuck your sorry!” There was a long silence. “So that’s it? This will be the end of everything?” I asked with a biting hiss to my voice.

“Yes.”

“How do you know?”

“It is what happened to others.”

“Well…” I didn’t really know what to say. I was still struggling against believing anything from this bodiless parasite, but it was hard, even for me, to explain anything that had happened to me today. In a moment of fearful rage, I said something I don’t think I really meant. “Well, what if I kill myself? Will that stop this?”

“No. They will pursue you beyond your death.”

“What if I drop myself into the ocean? Somewhere they can’t reach.”

“They will pursue you to their deaths. My love overwhelms them.”

I stayed quiet for a long time after that, with no reply from the voice. I drove as far as I could from the city, before the gas tank signaled it was near to empty. I pulled to the side of an empty backroad and put the car in park. Rage had settled into despair, and I clutched at the only straw I had.

“What do I do?”

“It is best to give in. It will be faster.”

“Won’t that kill me?”

“Yes.”

“So why wouldn’t I just-”

“I know that you are incapable. Death is inevitable. If you give in, I will be there in your final moments. You will feel my love, as have the others.”

My teeth grinded, the edges sanding down in scraping brushes I could feel in my skull. “So, what, I just lay down and…”

“Let them come to you. Let them overtake you.”

Tears began to form in my eyes once more. I had started this day waking up to go to work and now I was either completely broken from reality or choosing how humanity was going to end. How I was going to end. I hadn’t had enough time, hadn’t done so many things I wanted to, hadn’t reconciled some things with Mom, Dad. It all felt so unfair. I didn’t have my phone to say goodbye, I had spent what were apparently going to be my last few hours getting as far away from anyone who knew me as I could. It was justified, but that didn’t make it sting any less now.

“Okay. Say I’m thinking about it. How will it happen?”

“They will long to hear your voice. They will long to be in your presence. They will long for physical contact. They will place themselves as closely as possible until breath will likely escape you.” There was a thought that sent a jut of feverish fear through me. As if aware of what I thought, the voice responded. “No. Contact will be enough to placate them.”

“And…if you’re really aware of all this…you’ve been around this long…can you tell me what comes after? After, ya know…”

“No.”

“Why?”

“I am unlike you. I do not understand ending.”

I must have sat on the road for an hour in complete silence before I brought the car back to life. I pulled a u-turn on the road I had come through and drove towards a nearby town I had turned to avoid previously. By the time I started seeing others, who saw me first, and never stopped seeing me as far as I could tell, I had arrived at the edge of town. The tank began to sputter as I finally came upon a more populated intersection.

By the time I stepped out of the vehicle, people had begun to slowly trickle out of the surrounding buildings. They stared at me as they approached; men, women, children, all of them wearing that same intent, intense gaze regardless of their expression. The closest, and the first to speak, was an elderly woman I had never seen in my life, her gray hair striated with intermittent strands of blonde.

“Hello, there. How are you, dear?” I attempted to speak, but the words were choked by tears. She drew closer, surprisingly unthreateningly, and placed a hand on my arm as I lowered my gaze to the street. “You can talk to me. I just want to hear your voice.”

There was an undercurrent of fear as I heard the words. Was I really doing this? I looked up and suddenly, there was something unlike fear. Her eyes were fixed on me, but standing this close, I noticed, to my surprise, how much warmth was present in them. I sniffled slightly, the words finally emerging from my throat. “I’ve…I’ve had a really tough day.”

Sighs laden with sorrow rang out from the crowd that now surrounded us. “Oh, dear…I’m so very sorry to hear that. Would you like to talk about it?”

“I…no. No, that’s okay. Can I…I’m sorry, I don’t even know you.”

“That doesn’t matter…I can always lend an ear when someone needs it.” she said, smiling, her eyes still fixed on me.

“Can I…can I talk to you about something else?”

The crowd had drawn closer now, nearly only a few feet away on any side of me. I could hear scattered replies to my question.

“Let it out.”

“It always helps to-”

“You just need someone to hold y-”

“We can talk about anything-”

The old woman’s reply still cut through the rest. “Of course, dear. Anything you want.”

“Okay…do you mind if we sit?” Several reassuring no’s sounded throughout the crowd. I sat, every person present mimicking the action, with a loud shuffle sounding through the air. I did feel a strange semblance of comfort, but it was laced with a growing fear as the shuffling continued from around me, members of the crowd scooting forward as they sat. “Well I…oh I guess I should…my name is Alice.”

A loud contented sigh shot through the crowd as only the old woman replied. “Alice…that’s a wonderful name. I’m Evelyn. It’s so wonderful to meet you, Alice.”

“Thank you…”

“What would you like to talk about?” she asked, clasping my hand in hers.

“I…I guess my parents. There was some history we never really…or I guess, aren’t really going to get to…” my voice trailed off and Evelyn rubbed her fingers gently along the groove of my palm.

“That’s hard, I’m so sorry, dear. I’m a parent of three, though it’s only two now…I can tell you, it isn’t all about the history. I’m sure they feel the same.”

“I hope so…” I replied. The crowd was now close enough that I could hear breathing from behind me.

“Dear, do you mind if I hold you? I think it might help. You keep talking, and I’ll keep listening.” Evelyn said softly.

I don’t know why. There wasn’t a reasonable explanation I could find. Reason would tell me to run. To eek out whatever survival I could until the end. But the truth is, against everything that was happening and was about to happen, being held didn’t sound so horrible right now. “Sure…”

I laid my head in Evelyn’s lap as she began stroking my cheek. Looking forward, I could see the crowd ahead and around us slowly inching closer, some still seated, others crawling forward on their stomachs in an almost dragging motion. Every single eye was set on me. I closed my eyes against the sight as the sound of movement continued and Evelyn’s voice sounded above me.

“I’m here, dear. What else would you like to talk about? I just want to hear your voice.”

I continued speaking, talking about anything I could to take my mind off of this moment as I felt the first of the crowd’s hands make contact with my left leg. Evelyn continued gently caressing my cheek as another set of hands ran up my forearm. Tears had begun to well up in my tightly closed eyes, but I continued talking, saying all the things I wished I could to those I loved.

When I felt the first weighted pressure of a body laying itself perpendicular on top of mine, I let out a quiet sob. Evelyn let out a few gentle shushes. “It’s okay, dear. It’s all okay. I’m here.” The shuffling had grown louder and closer. The weight on top of me began to get heavier as I heard sounds of comfort intermittently ring out throughout the crowd. The heat of a multitude of bodies pressed down on me, their moist breath mingling with mine, the sweat bleeding through our clothes. The sticky rubbing of exposed arms, necks, and legs brushed against me and my breaths became labored, a pressure like stones laid on a wide plank pushing down on me as more climbed onto the pile.

I could hear Evelyn’s voice, but it shifted as the mass around me grew. Her high, gentle voice took on qualities that were low and high, deep and shrill. The voice of One.

“It is okay, little one. It is nearly time. I will help guide you.”

My brain shuddered. Where once had been fear, discomfort, pain, there was now a feeling of rapture; an ecstasy of comfort and contentment in degrees I had never felt in my life. The pain of my struggling breath faded, the heat and weight disappearing into the background of a relief that sunk deep into my being. It was as if I lived through every moment of every life that had given any hint of positivity or joy all at once. I was a child excitedly coming home to tell my parents about my first day of school. I was a young man kissing the woman he had just sworn his life to. I was a weeping mother holding her child for the first time, and I was the child, first opening eyes upon a world that greeted me with love. I breathed a heavy sigh that refreshed me as it left, and I heard a voice speak to me one more time.

“Sleep well, little one.”

I smiled, and blackness took me.
