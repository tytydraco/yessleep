It started a few weeks ago. The first poster caught my eye at the train station while on my way back from class. The white sheet of paper was held on by two dirty pieces of tape on the pole by the top and the bottom of the page. The wind pushed half of the paper up, folding in on itself but even then I could still see the bold red lettering. Missing. I scanned the paper, reading what info I could from where I stood on the platform, but didn’t look further into it as my train pulled into the station. The last thing I saw as I walked on was her face peeking out barely from the folded paper, curly black hair caressing her face as she smiled at me from the page. I redirected my focus back to my music before stepping onto the train.  

Then, I started to notice them more and more. They began to appear on stop signs, the sides of buildings, telephone poles, and across social media pages. It was like I couldn’t escape the posters. The more I saw them, the more I had grown familiar with the woman on the missing persons poster. It was a photo likely taken with a group of friends, as you could tell by the cropped off girl whose arm she held onto in the photo. She was smiling widely, almost in permanent laughter. Her black curly hair draped down her shoulders and back, long thick waves that caressed her collar bone and face. She was wearing a white crop top and mid rise black pants that showed off her navel ring and various tattoos scrawled across her body. She had intricate designs and flowers etched onto her stomach and hips and a snake that started up her right forearm and went up, disappearing into her sleeve. She had a Medusa and an eyebrow piercing that went through her eyebrow slit. She wore heavy makeup with large winged eyeliner and over lined lips. She was pretty in the face but not really my type.  

Mackenzie Davis. That was her name. Her roommates had reported her missing after she didn’t come home one night after her shift at a local bar. Her phones location was off and no one could get a hold of her. Her car was missing and it was as if she just disappeared off the face of the earth. Born July 17, 2004, 5’6”, black hair, brown eyes, and 150lbs.  

I tried to ignore how often I would see her. She was like a silent ghost that followed me from place to place, her frozen eyes staring into my soul from hundreds of portals that covered the entire city. One late night I stumbled across a video talking about the frequency illusion and it put my mind at ease. Maybe that was what was happening. I had seen the poster one time and now my brain was tuned into seeing every single one I came across. I finally felt relaxed that maybe I was just going a little crazy and that the city wasn’t going to be buried in a pile of missing posters by the end of the month.  

That is, until 3 days ago.  

I was in class, sat next to the window. The biology building gave a perfect view over the main court yard over the university and I lazily watched as tired students stumbled across the pavement trying to get to their next class. The old woman that taught the class talked so slowly I couldn’t ever focus for too long without my mind becoming numb, so I usually resorted to just staring out of the window and watching everyones days go by. That’s when I saw her. It was just for a moment, just the tiniest glimmer.  

But I know what I saw.  

In the window, across the way from the building, there’s a tall building will multiple large 4 paneled windows. While most had their blinds shut or the lights turned off with only the darkness of the rooms could be seen through them, there was one that I could see into. On the third floor of the building, I saw her. Just a sliver as she quickly ducked behind the wall, not even her face, just her hair and arm. I sat up, eyes widening as I tried to rationalize what I saw. ‘Come on man,’ I thought to myself ‘there’s more than just one girl with curly black hair.’ But every excuse or justification that came to my mind fell short.   

Because I swear I saw the intricate pattern of scales crawling up her arm, disappearing into the sleeve of her white shirt.   

I stared at the window for what felt like hours, hoping to see the same woman pass by. For it just to be another woman who looks similar. Or that my eyes were just tricking me and I really was just going crazy. Maybe it was hours because before I knew it, people were packing up around me and I had retained none of the information that was being taught to me that lesson. As I finally broke my gaze, my eyes had settled onto the teacher who returned a worried look. I got up and grabbed my backpack, trying to avoid her gaze but when I looked back up she was approaching me.  

“Are you doing okay?” She asked, her voice soft as to not alert the other people as they left the room, “you were spacing out all class.”   

“Yeah, of course.” I told her to ease her worries, “just have a lot of stuff on my mind.”  

“Well, you need to focus. This is your third time repeating this class and your academic advisor told me to keep an eye on you.” She scolded me, like a child. She always talked down to me like that.  

“Well, if she knows everything, then she knows why I failed those first times classes in the first place.” I scoffed and tried to find my way past her. She sensed my uneasiness and with a sigh, moved so I could make my exit. I spent the whole train ride home replaying the events in my mind. I decided that I just needed to just shake it off, go home and shake it off. It had to be the stress getting to me.  

But I saw her again today, while at the grocery store. I had just walked down to get some milk for me and my roommate when I saw the same thing. I had just entered isle when she rounded the corner on the other end. I couldn’t see her face again, just flowing black curls and a white shirt. I backed up quickly, trying to see if I can catch site of her in the other isle, but I ran directly into the shopping cart of an old lady, dropping my milk and nearly knocking myself down. The milk burst open, flowing onto the tiled floor in a big white puddle. It took 10 minutes to flag down an associate and get it cleaned up. By that time, she was gone. I searched the whole store, but there wasn’t even anyone who looked similar to her. But I saw it clearly that time, the floral pattern on her hips, covered by her black jeans.  

I had never seen this woman before. Her social media hasn’t been active in years and we have no friends or acquaintances in common. I’m even a frequent patron at the bar she worked at, but her face never looked familiar to me. But now, it stares back at me every day, as if she was just as physically present as she was before her disappearance. Why? Why specifically me?  

I try to distract myself with school work and hanging out with my friends. But I cannot escape Mackenzie’s gaze. I don’t know what to do. I asked a friend and he said to just call the cops, report that I’ve seen her and let them handle it. Call the cops? And say what? That I saw a glimpse of a girl that might look like the girl from the missing persons posters? It wouldn’t go anywhere. I’d be just labeled crazy or a liar and just be sent on my own way. That and me and the local police force don’t particularly get along.  

So, if anyone has any information about Mackenzie Davis, please get in contact with me. I can’t be the only person this is happening to, right?
