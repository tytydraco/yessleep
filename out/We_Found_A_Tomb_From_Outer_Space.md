Can you imagine what it’s like to see a dead body? Lifeless eyes. Rotting flesh. It was fourth grade. I remember the day vividly. The messy overcast was like a thick sheet of lint. Our school was in a horseshoe shape with a playground in the center. Extending out from the playground was a field surrounded by woods. From the third floor of the playground you could see Mount Creon, covered in pines.

His name was Randall. He was past the chain linked fence at the end of the field. This area was always so mysterious to me. A wooden bridge stood above a creek beyond the gate. What was beyond? What would happen if I just climbed that fence? Bees forced me to the spot. As a kid I had an irrational fear of them. I’d run away and swear they were following me. Running away along the field’s track I bumped into my best friend, Rory, who was wandering near the bushes. She joined me. We ran until we reached the fence, the forest beyond it. What little light that managed to sneak through the clouds and foliage shone on a sea of pine straw. Far to my left was old wooden exercise equipment no-one used. The gate was close to my right.

Randall was a macabre fixture in the environment which anchored your vision and dragged it to its own center. His head poked out from under the bridge. He did not die peacefully. Mouth and eyes wide open, his face was frozen in horror like a grisly wax figure. It’s not like we couldn’t comprehend gore. We knew what we were seeing, but we didn’t know how to process seeing it. All I knew is that I had to get a teacher. Rory stayed. When I returned, she was still staring at the body.

School was cancelled for the rest of the week. The following week we had an assembly. In the same gym we watched ET in during a sleep-in and played dodge ball, our short, balding principle had to explain to us what happened. The explanation was vague (for we were children). He gave the typical talk about mourning you’d expect. I can’t say that Randy was an unpopular or reclusive kid, but I also never spoke with him. He was in Rory’s class not mine. Regardless, I had to go to the councilor’s office every lunch for a week. It was two weeks for Rory. We were the only two kids at the allergy table, so for the week after they freed me I had to enjoy the company of teachers. Months passed, and Randall began to seem like just a bad memory.

Years later, I’d ask the town sheriff about it.

“Oh yes, I remember. We patrolled the school for two whole years. Didn't have anything better to do out here. Quite the brutal killing it was. There was a sort of grid of cylindrical holes going straight through him front to back. As if killed by an iron maiden – except the holes were perfect cylinders.

“Broke his parents’ hearts. Was an only child. He’d been missing a week. Think his head only popped up because of rising creek water. Think they moved away after that. “The killer? No, we never caught him. How or why he killed him, no one knows. It’s a cold case. In fact, we don’t even know where his missing flesh went. They had a closed casket funeral I remember…”

I would also get asked about it sometimes. Unfortunately, I could give people no more information than I’ve given you now. Later an ex-FBI agent confessed that trace amounts of radiation were found on Randy’s corpse.

This case has been a subject of interest for a few online personalities and podcasts. I doubt my account here will be taken into any serious consideration. I have no idea how to prove that I’ve seen what I’ve seen. Still, this story must be told.
