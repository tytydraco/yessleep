When I was 6 years old, I was playing with my toys in the street. Back then, the street was very empty—it was quite rare to see a motorcycle or a car pass by—so children took advantage of this to play outside for as long as they wanted. That day, while I was playing, one of my toys—I remember it was a little red and black car—slipped away from me and onto the road. Naturally, I got up and walked toward the asphalt where the car had landed. If my memory serves me right, the last thing I recall from that day is my parents calling out my name, followed by the sound of a motorcycle roaring very close to my ear. Yet, the very next moment, I was back on the sidewalk; the motorcycle had just passed by, and the little car was still sitting on the asphalt. My parents didn’t seem frightened—they didn’t appear to have shouted or even stood up from their chairs—but my six-year-old self paid little to no attention to that; I just kept on eating my Play-Doh.

Yesterday—exactly 10 years after that event—I went out shopping with my mother to pick up groceries and some things for my pet. On the way, I recounted the story, thinking she might remember it, but her response wasn’t exactly what I had expected.

"Mom, do you remember that time in that neighborhood when a motorcycle almost ran me over?"

"What?"

"You know, Mom, that time when I was little and I almost got run over?"

"I don't remember any of that"

"Really? I mean, you were there, both of you even screamed. I think I even saw my dad almost trip over as he came to pick me up and save me."

"Hold on, your dad?"

"Yes, Dad, do you remember?"

"Oh, of course, but that bastard abandoned you when you were 4 years old!"

I froze when I heard that—partly because I have photos on my phone of myself at age eight, painting a picture for school with my dad. I also have a drawing he dedicated to me, and I even keep a physical photograph of him. I decided not to ask any questions. But this wasn’t the only time something like this happened; it makes me believe that, for some reason, I cannot die. When I was 14, my whole family drove to a river not far from the city; we wanted to relax, fish, and swim. Once we were at the river, I remember accidentally falling into a deep pool of water. My swimming skills were very poor at the time, so I began to sink.

And yet, somehow, something or someone pushed me out of the water—something "saved my life"—but I am certain that I died in that pool. Every day since that moment—from that street to that pool—I have felt that something is missing, that something has been lost. And recently, it happened again. It was 3:00 a.m. and I was fast asleep; suddenly, I woke up, and the moment I did, something turned my head to the side—so quickly that I fell right back to sleep. When I woke up, my neck didn’t hurt, and I felt much more rested than before (for context, I’ve had significant trouble sleeping for as long as I can remember). That night, I lost something—I couldn’t say exactly what, but I don’t feel complete; I’ve felt as though something is missing ever since that early morning.

I’ve come across theories online suggesting that consciousness cannot die—that, in fact, it shifts to a different reality upon death. For me, the strongest evidence for this is that my family—even though they were present at the time—couldn’t recall what happened that day when I asked them about it; or, if they did remember, they mentioned details that weren’t there when I experienced it.

Can't I die?
