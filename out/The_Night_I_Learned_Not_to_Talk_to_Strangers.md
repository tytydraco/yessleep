When I was 9, my family went up to upstate NY for a weekend in October to attend my dad’s college reunion. We spent the day ice skating and swimming in the campus pool with the other kids of my dad’s college buddies, some of whom I was already good friends with from back home, so we were pretty tuckered out by the end of the day.

That night, all of the adults went out to some bar and had my dad’s best friend’s teenage kids babysit us - I think there were about 6 of us aged 4-8. We were just sitting around playing Mario Kart, so there wasn’t much supervising for them to do.

Around 10pm, they took us all down to the lobby to stretch our legs and get a snack. I remember it being pretty much empty, aside from a rather scruffy looking guy sitting in a chair watching a football game on the TV. He wore khaki pants, boots, and a work shirt with a name tag similar to what a mechanic might wear.

What intrigued me, though, was the big black bag at his feet. It was made of canvas and had a pattern of pumpkins and ghosts throughout and seemed to be about half full of what my then-child mind naturally deemed to be Halloween candy, so curiosity won out and I walked over.

“Hi…what’s in your bag?” I said, slightly nervously, knowing in the back of my mind my parents wouldn’t be overly pleased should they hear about this.

“Hey there son,” he said, in a voice that was deep and rather scratchy, like he had just smoked a couple cigarettes outside. My uncle smoked and, looking back, you’d think it would have stood out to me that the only smell was him - up close, he looked downright unkempt, as though he hadn’t taken a shower in days. His hair was greasy, and his face and arms looked like they perpetually had a thin layer of grime on them.

The man - Darren, according to the name tag on his shirt, which up close looked to be a size or two too big for him - picked up the bag and held it in my direction. “I’ve got lots of Halloween candy just for kind little boys like you. Wanna take a peek?”

Unfortunately, for Darren, the bottom of the bag, which had previously been on sitting on the floor, began to trip, and I noticed that there was a rather sizable wet patch on the bottom. He noticed, too.

“Oh, don’t mind that. It’s been raining the last few hours. Must have gotten a little damp sitting on the ground. No biggie”

What he failed to acknowledge was that the liquid dripping through the bottom was bright red. Or that the shapes in the bag, when seen up close, were large, bulky, and of all different shapes and sizes. A jagged end of something tried to poke out of the fabric.

Kayla, one of the teenagers watching us, then looked up from her phone and must have seen me taking a step back with a nervous look on my face, because she got up and walked over.

“Hi, sir. Thank you - you’re too kind. But it’s past my cousin’s bedtime and I don’t think his parents would be too happy with me if I let him eat candy this late,” she said as she cast a glance at the black Halloween bag, again cast to the floor.

“That’s quite alright,” he said, nodding. “I’ll be here all night if you and your cousin” - he winked at me, knowing a lie of convenience when he heard one - “change your minds.”

Kayla took my hand, leading me back to the other kids, who unlike me had not wandered off to try and score some free candy. Her twin brother, Kenny, sat at a table, his face set in an expression of mock effort as he arm wrestled another kid.

“C’mon, guys. Let’s head back up to the room.” As we made our way to the elevator, it was becoming increasingly hard to ignore the stare drilling into the back of my head - Darren, watching us. Looking back, I’m forever grateful he didn’t try to follow us.

As soon as we were all piled in and the elevator shut, Kayla pulled out her phone. Kenny asked her what was up.

“I’m calling 911. “

The muffled sounds from the lobby that ensued told us that wouldn’t be necessary. Shouts, followed by 3 sharp pops and a thump as something hit the ground. The elevator was silent. I looked up - Kenny was stone faced, Kayla silently crying.

Our parents got back to the hotel about 20 minutes later to a crime scene, and I learned in the years following what had happened, because there’s some things you just don’t tell a 9 year old boy.

There had been a string of murders in the state in the previous couple weeks, all unsolved. The man, whose name was not Darren - he had purchased the shirt at a Goodwill to try and throw off his scent - had entered the hotel about 5 minutes before we came down to the lobby. What his intentions were are, and will likely remain unknown.

The receptionist, who knew of the murders, was unsettled by his general appearance and demeanor. When she was unable to find a “Darren” under the checked-in guests, she asked him if she could get him a blanket and then went to a back office and called the police.

When they entered the lobby, the police ordered him onto the ground with his hands where they could see them. He apparently ignored the order and reached for the bag instead. They fired three times.

Rather than candy, the bag contained various…remains, and a hunting knife wrapped in a blanket.

Obviously we won’t meet again, but looking back, especially in his interactions with me (all of his victims were adults), it’s…interesting to say the least.

I don’t know what would have happened had I taken him up on his offer to look in the bag. I think it’s a question best left unanswered.
