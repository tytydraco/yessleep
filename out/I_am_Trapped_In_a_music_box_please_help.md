It started three days ago when I bought an antique, mahogany jewelry box at an estate sale. It was beautiful, lined with faded velvet, featuring a tiny porcelain ballerina that spun when a brass key wound up the clockwork mechanism. The melody it played was Tchaikovsky’s *Swan Lake*.

That night, I woke up around 3:00 AM to the sound of that exact melody playing softly in my bedroom.

I sat up, freezing. The box was sitting on my dresser, completely closed, but the music was spilling out of it—distorted, slowed down, and heavy. I got out of bed to close it or stop the gears. But as I approached the dresser, a wave of intense vertigo hit me. The floor tilted. The walls of my bedroom rushed backward into a crushing, suffocating darkness, and a massive, deafening *click* echoed through my skull.

When I opened my eyes, I wasn't in my room anymore.

I am standing on a massive, circular brass plate. The floor beneath my feet is cold, polished metal, and everything around me is painted in a deep, suffocating crimson. I looked up and nearly lost my mind. Towering over me, hundreds of feet in the air, is a colossal, curved mahogany ceiling. I am inside the jewelry box.

But the true horror isn't just the entrapment. It's the mechanism.

Every few hours, a sound like a localized earthquake tears through this place. It’s the sound of a giant key turning. The gears beneath the brass floor begin to groan and churn, sending violent vibrations through my legs.

And then, the music starts.

When *Swan Lake* plays in here, it is not a delicate lullaby. It is a booming, industrial nightmare. The brass plate beneath me begins to spin at a sickening speed. If I don't move, the centrifugal force will fling me into the velvet walls, which feel less like fabric and more like coarse, skin-tearing sandpaper.

I have to dance. Or rather, I have to mimic a dance.

There is a giant, grotesque pillar in the center of the floor—the porcelain ballerina. From my perspective, she is a colossal, faceless marble statue that rises into the darkness. When the gears activate, a massive iron rod drives upward through the floor, forcing her to spin. Because of the magnetic pull or some sick law of physics in this place, my body is violently jerked along with her. If I resist the rotation, my joints feel like they are going to snap out of their sockets. I am forced to sprint in a breathless, agonizing circle, mimicking her pirouettes just to keep my balance and stay alive.

The song lasts for exactly three minutes. By the end of it, my lungs are burning, and my feet are bleeding.

Then, the music stops. The world plunges back into a terrifying, dead silence.

But it’s getting worse. An hour ago, the giant mahogany lid above me creaked open a fraction of an inch. A sliver of blinding, artificial light cut through the crimson gloom. I looked up, hoping for rescue, but what I saw shattered whatever sanity I had left.

Leaning over the edge of the box was a face. It was *my* face.

It looked exactly like me, but its eyes were completely hollow, and its smile was stretched entirely too wide, baring too many teeth. It stared down into the box, watched me shivering on the brass plate, and then gently reached down with a massive, pale hand.

It didn't reach for me. It reached for the brass key.

I can hear the gears grinding to life again. The colossal ballerina in the center is starting to twitch. The first, deafening note of the music is vibrating through the floor. The imposter outside is winding the box. It wants to watch me spin.

Please, if anyone knows how to break an object-bound curse, or if anyone sees me walking around my town acting strange—help me. Don't let it wind the key again.

The music is starting. I have to run.
