Hi everyone. This is my first time posting on Reddit, so please bear with me.

My husband Adam and I are currently trapped inside a gated community somewhere in the English countryside, and I am writing this from my phone while he sleeps. I’m posting here because I don’t know who else to turn to, and I think something deeply sinister is happening to us.

Let me explain how we got here.

It all started a few months ago when Adam and I decided to start trying for a baby. We were crammed into a tiny, damp one-bedroom flat with barely enough room to breathe, let alone raise a child. We were desperate to move, but with the housing market being what it is in England, everything was completely out of our price range.

One evening, our two best friends, Diane and John, came over for dinner. As we were clearing away the plates, Diane noticed the stack of property listings on our kitchen counter.

"Still no luck?" she asked, setting down her glass.

"Not unless we want to raise a child in a glorified shoe box," Adam sighed, leaning against the counter.

Diane smiled in a strangely knowing, almost rehearsed way. "What about *The Commons*?"

I paused, looking at her in confusion. "The Commons? What's that?"

"Oh, it’s this gorgeous little private estate hidden away in the countryside," Diane said, her tone bright and airy. "The best schools, wonderful sports programmes, beautiful green space, and the homes are absolute perfection. You two would fit right in."

Two days later, driven by curiosity and sheer desperation, Adam and I took the four-hour drive down into the country. The route took us off the motorway, down narrow, winding back roads bordered by dense, suffocating woodland. Eventually, the trees parted, revealing a massive, Victorian-style iron gate set into a high stone wall.

There were no security guards or intercoms in sight—just smooth, expensive-looking ironwork. We pulled up to a stop, and I was just about to unbuckle my seatbelt to look for a keypad when the gates silently glided open on their own.

We drove through into a neighborhood that felt unnervingly pristine. Every lawn was a uniform, vivid emerald green. The houses were identical—large, upper-middle-class executive homes with dark oak detailing, flawless render, and immaculate driveways. But there were no cars parked outside. No kids' bikes on the lawns. No stray weeds. Everything was far too perfect.

At the center of the estate sat a neat, medium-sized brick building with a dark wooden plaque: **The Commons Reception Building.**

We parked and stepped inside. Sitting behind a heavy, dark oak desk was a man waiting for us. He looked like he had stepped straight off a luxury estate runway: immaculate 2x2 double-breasted pinstripe suit, perfectly buttoned, a purple and white striped tie, and leather shoes so highly polished they reflected the overhead lights. He rose to greet us with an effortlessly smooth demeanor.

"Welcome," he said, offering a firm, practiced handshake. "I am Mr. Wagner. You must be Adam and Sarah."

After a few minutes of polite small talk, he led us down the street to show us the house we’d be viewing. The moment he unlocked the front door, my breath caught in my throat. It was stunning. High ceilings, rich hardwood flooring, a modern open-plan kitchen and living area, a massive wall-mounted television, and five spacious bedrooms upstairs.

"Did I mention that the previous owners were unable to take any of their furnishings with them?" Wagner said smoothly, flashing a smile that showed impossibly white, perfectly uniform teeth. "Everything you see here comes with the property. Pictures, furniture, electronics... it's all yours if you take the house."

Adam leaned in close to me while Wagner stepped into the kitchen. "Darling, don't set your heart on this place just yet," he whispered quietly. "Look at it. We can't afford a place like this. It’s a multi-million-pound house."

When Wagner strolled back in, Adam put his hands in his pockets. "Mr. Wagner, it's incredible, but... what’s the asking price?"

Wagner smiled warmly, resting his hands behind his back. " three hundred  thousand pounds. That covers the property outright, as well as lifetime community contributions for bills and council taxes."

My jaw almost hit the hardwood floor. £300,000? For a five-bedroom detached house with all utilities covered?

"What's the catch?" Adam asked, his tone instantly guarded. "Nobody sells a property like this for 300 grand. What's wrong with the structure? Or the land?"

Wagner's smile didn't flicker. "No catch, my dear boy. The developers behind The Commons aren't interested in market speculation. We simply require young, lovely, respectable couples like yourselves to enrich our community. Crime doesn't exist here. You can raise your future children in a completely safe, loving, and controlled environment."

It sounded like a dream. Too good to be true, obviously, but when we told our parents, they were so thrilled for us that they pooled their savings to gift us the £300,000 cash.

Within a week, the contracts were signed, and we moved in.

On our first morning, as we were unpacking boxes in the kitchen, there was a sharp knock at the door. I opened it to find a couple in their mid-fifties standing on the porch holding a baked pie.

"Hi! We're the Fabers, your next-door neighbors," the man said warmly. "I'm Phil, and this is my wife, Shannon."

"Oh, look at them, Phil," Shannon cooed, gazing at us with an intense, unblinking smile. "Aren't they adorable? Do you think we looked that cute when we first arrived?"

"Well, *I* certainly didn't," Phil replied with a chuckle. They both laughed, though the sound was dry and hollow.

"So, how long have you two lived at The Commons?" I asked politely, trying to break through the strange vibe.

Phil’s smile instantly vanished. His eyes went flat. "12 years August. Worst twelve years of my life."

The silence that followed was suffocating. He didn't sound like he was joking at all. His eyes held a look of sheer, exhausted horror.

Shannon slapped his arm, her voice tight and high-pitched. "Phil, stop it! They don't know your sense of humor yet!"

"Oh, come on, honey," Phil said softly, his voice dropping an octave as he looked directly into my eyes. "They know I'm kidding... *I'm not kidding.*"

Adam quickly stepped in, forcing a laugh to diffuse the sudden tension, and invited them in for tea. They stayed for twenty minutes, making tense, rigid small talk before abruptly leaving the moment the clock on our wall struck 2:00 PM—as if adhering to a strict, unspoken schedule.

That night, Adam was completely exhausted from the move and fell asleep early. I couldn't sleep. The quiet out here wasn't peaceful; it was heavy and dead. There were no night birds, no traffic in the distance, no wind through the trees. Just total silence.

I sat downstairs in the living room and turned on the television to keep myself company. Browsing through the channels, I noticed the TV didn't have standard British broadcasting. There was no BBC, no ITV. Just numerical channels.

I flipped to Channel 04.

The screen flickered, showing a low-resolution, raw video feed—like unedited found footage or a security recording. It showed a bedroom identical to our own upstairs. A woman was standing by the bed, talking to a man. Suddenly, another man burst through the door, screaming at them.

*"How could you be so stupid?!"* the man shouted, his voice distorted through the TV speaker. *"Do you realize what you’ve done?! Do you have any idea what they do to people who try to break the code?!"*

A scuffle broke out off-screen, accompanied by a horrible, muffled crashing noise, followed by silence.

Genuinely unsettled, I grabbed the remote and jammed the power button, plunging the room into darkness.

As my eyes adjusted to the dark, a tiny, dull red light caught my eye.

It was coming from the upper left-hand corner of our living room ceiling, mounted right near the crown molding.

I stood up, walked slowly toward it, and reached up to touch it. It was a high-definition dome security camera. And as I stepped closer, the small motorized lens inside **swiveled silently**, tracking my movement across the room.

It’s pointing directly at me right now.

I went back upstairs, but when I tried to look out our bedroom window, I noticed something else. The front gate—the one we drove through—is closed. And mounted along the top of the entire outer perimeter wall are heavy, industrial rows of razor wire slanting *inward*, designed not to keep people out... but to keep us in.

I’m terrified. Adam thinks I’m just overwhelmed by the move, but I know what I saw. If anyone on Reddit reads this, what should I do? How do we get out?
