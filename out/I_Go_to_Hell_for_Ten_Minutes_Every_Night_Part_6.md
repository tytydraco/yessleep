[**\[Part 5\]**](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1xyhm9a/i_go_to_hell_for_ten_minutes_every_night_part_5/)

So I didn’t go to Hell at 1:18 for either of the last two nights. That’s new. It’s been a part of my life for so long now that I can’t imagine what it’s going to mean for it to be over. Maybe I’d get to be normal again. It would be nice not to have to follow a different set of rules than everyone else.

It does create a problem, though. Maia’s cure is waiting for us there, in the Marble Hell. How do we get to it if I can’t flip over there anymore? Even better, how do we know that Phil wasn’t lying?

Her curse is getting worse. Most of her left thigh and her whole right foot are stone now. The colored part of her eye—I can’t remember if it’s the iris or the cornea—is a light tan color instead of her normal brown. I tried to talk to Maia about the changes, but she’s more delirious than ever. She called me Ben earlier, her cousin’s name. It’s like I got her back just so she could forget me.

Shugart and Hugh approached me yesterday morning, right after I dropped that last post.

“How’s she doing?” Shugart asked.

“Fine, but it’s farther up her leg, and did you see her eyes?”

“Yeah, they’ve gone pale. Me and Hugh was talking about it and our other problems. We may have some solutions.”

“That’s great. I mean, we’re grasping at straws here. We need to move, don’t we? DL was pretty clear about it.”

“That’s part of it,” said Hugh. “Shug thinks we should call his captain, or the FBI. Try to get some protection.”

“And you don’t?” I asked.

“I don’t think we should,” he said, more to Shugart than to me. “We know there’s at least one Ring spy at the precinct. I’m sorry, man, but Melody’s dirty, and it’s dangerous for you to think any different. What if they have someone at the Bureau? It’s not that far-fetched. I know of at least one senator, and there’s gotta be others in the fold.”

“Alright, so where do we go?”

“I know a couple of places,” Shugart said. “I don’t know if I completely agree with Hugh’s take on it, but I’m not willing to put all of you at risk over my faith in the system. There’s a couple of safe houses the department hasn’t used in years. One of ‘em’s completely off the books.”

“That sounds solid; let’s go for it.”

“Yeah, but first we’ve gotta do something else,” said Hugh.

“This is the part I ain’t so sure about,” Shugart said.

“I’m not either,” Hugh agreed. “But we’ve still gotta. It’s the only way to save Maia.”

“Well, don’t slow-roll me, Hugh. If you know a way, spit it out.”

“I think Sarah’s the way.”

“How’s that?”

“Phil told you that the sea around the Marble could cure Maia, but you can’t get to it. Sarah can. She did when she saved you. It makes perfect sense, actually, as the reason he went after her. Think about it, Will. What if he wanted her because she could go there? Those are the kind of people the Ring is always looking for. I don’t even think the place is really one of the hells. I mean, if it was, he could probably escape it. I think it’s something else entirely, and she put him there that night when you got your powers.”

“No,” I said. “She would have told me. That’s a bit of a stretch, man.”

“Maybe, except for one thing,” Hugh said. “You told me she took control of the stone there, that she made it grab Phil right before he slashed her. That’s the detail that locked it in for me.”

It did it for me too. She had basically said she needed to talk to me, and then she had shown up over there and saved my ass. Even though I still had a lot of questions, it made good sense. If she could go there, then she could take Maia.

“We’ve gotta go see her.”

Hugh glanced at Shugart, who shrugged and nodded at me before he spoke. “Yeah, kid. We thought you’d say that. But now you’ve gotta hear me out.”

“There’s more?”

“Just one thing that you need to consider. If the Ring is looking for you, for all of us, it makes sense for them to put the hospital under surveillance. It’s what I’d do if I was them.”

“He isn’t wrong,” Hugh agreed.

“So how do we do it?” I asked them, and I was happy to find out that they already had a plan.

An hour later I was sitting in the living room of Shugart’s tiny house. We had split up, two to a car, and Hugh had taken Maia with him to hide out at the safehouse. Shugart had a nice place, even though it was small. It was clean, but there was no sign that a woman lived there other than the large framed portrait of a much younger Shug standing beside a seriously good-looking lady. They looked really happy in it, but I knew from personal experience that wedding photos could be misleading. The big one in my parents’ living room had been fooling people for years.

There were also a lot of pictures of a little girl there. I watched her grow up right before my eyes as I looked around the room. She had her mom’s beauty and her dad’s captivating eyes. I was standing there, staring at her in her college graduation gown, when Shugart spoke up quietly from behind me.

“You might think you know hell, son. Yeah, you’ve seen it, but I don’t think you’ve felt it yet. I hope you never do.”

I turned around and looked at him, and the pain in his eyes shook me. Part of me wanted to know the memory that was fueling the haunted expression on his face. The better part couldn’t bear to think about what telling me would cost him. Instead, I just cleared my throat and asked him if he was ready. He nodded and zipped up the jacket we had come to get.

When we pulled into the parking lot next to the hospital, Shugart turned towards me. He was wearing his old, ill-fitting security uniform. There was even a patch on his arm with an emblem of the hospital. It gave him a sad, diminished look that didn’t suit him at all.

“Where did you get that?” I asked him. “It looks like you stole it from a bodybuilder.”

He laughed. “It was mine. Standard issue. Would you believe I used to work here?”

I shook my head, realizing I didn’t really know anything about his life. I had met him as a detective, so that was all I saw when I looked at him.

“Yeah, I had a bad stretch for a few months there. I let the bottle win after my dau…” Shugart cleared his throat. “If you get down low enough, the devil don’t have to reach far to poke you.”

He laughed to himself and looked around the parking lot. I stayed quiet. It felt like the right thing to do. The conversation he was having in his head was so heavy I didn’t want to interrupt it. I knew he was done talking about it when he finished what he said next.

“It’s why I’m going to see this thing through, Will. You and Maia are good kids, and you need somebody in your corner just like I had back in my dark time. I owe for that, and I’m going to make sure I pay my bill.”

“Thank you,” I gulped. The man had a way with words.

“No need for all that,” Shugart said as he opened his door. “Hell, son, I was bored anyway. If there’s not a hell where they make you fill out useless forms all day, there oughta be. Now come on, let’s see if this works.”

I got out, and he led me across the parking lot around the side of the building. We walked around to the back and up to a security door. Shugart pulled out his ID card and slid it through the scanner beside the door. It declined him twice. On the third failed swipe, he muttered to himself and typed in a code. The light turned green and the door unlocked.

“I guess I owe Ernie a case of Michelob.” He nodded at the door. “Let’s go before it changes its mind. I don’t want to do all of that again.”

I followed Shugart inside, and I had to quicken my pace to keep up with him. He took a circuitous route through the hospital, taking back hallways and stairs to avoid the more crowded areas. Twice we ducked into rooms when he heard approaching footsteps. Eventually we made it up to the third floor, where they had Sarah.

“They shouldn’t have anybody up here,” Shugart whispered. “Security is pretty tight.”

We walked through the doors and into the ICU. It was a high traffic area, with a lot of nurses moving quickly, in and out of the patient rooms. No one really looked at me; Shugart’s uniform caught their eyes and they didn’t linger on us. For all they knew he was just doing his job, guiding a visitor in to see someone. I played along and tried to emote as much sadness as I could. No one cared—they were all too busy to notice.

When we got to her room and I looked at Sarah, I didn’t know how to react. I couldn’t reconcile the bandage-wrapped woman in front of me with the sarcastic, larger-than-life girl I knew. Why did it bother me so much? Her wounds had been much scarier when I thought she was dying back at the motel. Maybe it was the beeping of the machines they had her hooked up to, or the various tubes they had in her. All I know is that, at that moment, I became more scared of losing her than I ever had.

She raised a feeble hand towards me as I approached her bed. I looked back and saw Shugart standing beside the door. He just shook his head and made a quick shooing gesture before turning back to watch the hall. He was right; we didn’t have time to waste. I certainly couldn’t afford to waste time on my worries.

Sarah smiled when I was finally standing beside her bed. She tried to raise her hand again, but the tube running out of the back of it got tangled up on her sheet. She shook it loose and reached up to slip her finger into the end of the tube in her throat.

“I must… look terrible,” she said breathily. “Even my b…best friend wants to run away.”

“You’ve looked better,” I said, and I tried to stop my voice from trembling.

“Yeah, bu…” She lost her wind for a moment and had to release the tube to take in a rattly breath. “Can’t talk long. Hurts. Ha-how’s Maia? Did you g…get her out?”

“Don’t talk, Sarah. Let me tell you everything first. Tell you what I need.”

I told her everything—the phantom, the Knotted Ring, all of it. She cried when I described what was happening to Maia, and looked worried when I told her what Phil told me to do about it. She shook her head and said, “Can…can’t trust him, Will.”

“What else can we do? Put her in here with you? The doctors won’t have a clue.”

“He killed all my friends… destroyed their world.”

“Huh? Are you okay, Sarah? You’re not making any sense.”

“Muh-marble isn’t a hell. It was my playground, Will.”

“That place! It’s terrible. The void and all the statues. They were people.”

“Wasn’t bef… before I put him there. He got out… destroyed it all.”

I stared at her, less sure in my disbelief. How did I know so little about her that I didn’t know about that? What else was she hiding from me? I almost became angry, but the sight of her crushed it. Whatever she hadn’t told me, whatever she had done before, she had taken those wounds for me and Maia. I forgave her without any of the details. I wouldn’t let secrets she needed to keep affect how I felt about her.

“So the Marble was yours? 137 is right. And you put Phil there because he was trying to take you or hurt you? Is that what caused me to go to the hells every night?”

She looked away for a moment, but only a moment, before she turned and looked me right in the eye. “Yes, aw—all of the above. So sorry, Will… his curse hit you… meant for me. He wanted to send me to hell.”

“That… that’s fine, Sarah,” I said. “It’s not your fault. He’s a monster, and whatever reasons you had for not telling me… fuck ‘em. I love you. You’re my best friend, and I trust you with my life. You’ve done pretty well on that front. I’m still standing.”

She smiled and put her hand on mine. “You’re a lot of work. Ha-had to get wrecked to… to take a vacation, man.”

“Maybe the beach next time,” I said, wiping my runny nose with the hand she wasn’t holding.

“Think my swimsuit days are done… sorry, Will. I’m so sorry… tell you everything when I’m not half-dead.”

I shook my head and gave her a thumbs up. It was fine. It could wait, but there was something that couldn’t. “There’s one more thing, Sarah. Trust him or not, Maia can’t wait. I think she’s losing her mind. Can you send her there?”

“Too weak… has to be you,” she said, and frowned before saying, “You have to kiss me… only way… fuckin’ boy germs.”

“Kiss? I mean, I don’t really—”

“Only way to… to give it to you… only way to get her there.”

“Uh, okay. What, like on the lips?”

She smiled. “Thought you fi-finally got some… don’t know how it works. Poor Maia.”

I leaned forward to cut her off, but she squeezed my hand, stopping me an inch from her face. “Eyes open, and be…beware the hospital breath.”

I kissed her then, and it was magical.

As our lips met, I felt my pupils turn on their own to sync up with hers. A connection was formed then, one that was deeper than anything I had ever felt. It wasn’t love or in any way emotional. It was a totality. I felt like I was part of everything else in that moment, and all of it was part of me. That’s dumb, but it’s the closest I can get to saying it.

I suppose a better phrase is that I felt complete.

Through Sarah, I saw the Marble Realm for what it really was, what it once was, and what it was to her. I was aware of every blade of grass and chunk of stone that existed there, and everything that ever had. It was as overwhelming as it was fulfilling, and I was less afraid in that moment than I ever have been.

As I pulled away from her I noticed that I still felt it—that connection. It was an itch that I could always choose to scratch. I knew that it would only take me a few moments of concentration to turn around and be there. It gave me a sense of freedom that is hard to describe, and it made me understand her better. It also worried me, though.

She had given it to me, and I couldn’t help but wonder what it cost her to do it.

“You’re a lousy kisser,” she wheezed.

“Hey, I wasn’t giving you my best stuff,” I said. “That’s for Maia only.”

“Say hey… for me. You gotta go… tired now.”

I reached down and squeezed her hand as gently as I could.

“I’ll come back and let you know how it went.”

We were both silent during the ride to the safehouse. Shugart had gotten us back out of the hospital without any issues. When we got to the car, I expected him to ask me about my talk with Sarah, but he didn’t. He just sat there, behind the wheel, and left me to my thoughts.

Traffic was pretty tame. We got across town in twenty minutes, and we ended up in a neighborhood I had never seen. It was a seedy area, and it was probably dangerous after dark. Shugart told me that the people there made a habit out of minding their own business.

He brought us to a rundown building that used to be a HunkaBurger. We pulled around to the back, parked, and I followed him in through a door that said EMPLOYEES ONLY. Hugh was waiting for us just inside, and he asked us how it went. I asked him where Maia was.

“She isn’t good, man,” he said. “I was just in there, talking to her, and I don’t think she even knew who I was.”

“Then we better hurry.”

The three of us walked into the tiny room that was once a manager’s office. The only piece of furniture in there was a shabby old couch that was missing all but one of its seat cushions. Maia was sitting there with her stiff marble leg stretched across it to the opposite armrest. She didn’t glance up as we approached her, and I could hear her singing softly to herself.

“…*the streets of fair gilded Dolomé, make every joyous day a holiday…”*

I crouched beside her and stroked her hair.

Maia turned to me and asked, “Are you the one who was promised?”

Her voice was odd, more refined and articulate. The tone was somewhat deeper, totally unlike the normal sweetly melodic way she always spoke. I shook my head. “It’s me, Will. I got the cure, Maia.”

She stared at me for a moment, and I saw that those strange tan-colored eyes now contained flecks of glittery silver. I was so afraid for her at that moment that I almost grabbed her. I wanted to shake whatever it was out of her. But when she spoke again, her voice was normal and it quivered with fear.

“Will, I-I don’t know what’s happening. Please…”

“I’m going to make you well,” I said, and I gently lifted her face so she could see me. “I promise, Maia, but we have to go back there to do it. Can you handle that?”

“I-I don’t know.” She looked pleadingly over at Shugart, who leaned down to reassure her.

“We’ll both be there with you, darlin’,” he said in a soft voice. “I won’t let anything happen to you.”

“Yeah, I’ll come too,” said Hugh, and I felt his good hand grip my shoulder.

“See, we’re all in this together. Now let’s go over there and get you better.”

As I stood up, Hugh turned me towards him to ask, “How? Did something happen at the hospital?”

Shugart was still with Maia. He whispered something and I saw her smile. I nodded at Hugh.

“I don’t have time to go into it, but you were right about a lot of it. Sarah gave it to me so I could save Maia.”

“Hey,” he said. “I don’t need you to explain it. I’m just glad we can help her.”

“Are you sure about this? With your arm?”

“Yeah, I think she needs all of us. I can’t promise I’ll be any actual help, though.”

“We ready?” Shugart asked us, and I nodded.

“You two, grab my arm.” I took hold of Maia’s hand and I flipped just as soon as I felt them do it.

It was completely different from what I had experienced before. When I was pulled into the hells at 1:18 every night, it was like being thrown into another room. It felt unavoidable and a little jarring. Using Sarah’s gift to travel to the Marble seemed more like it was something that came out of me. I’d compare it to taking a step and feeling a new world take shape under your foot. I could tell by looking at the others that they had the former experience.

“Sorry, guys,” I said. “It takes some getting used to.”

The island was mostly the same as it had always been, but it seemed more vital than before. As we walked among the statues on our way to the shore, I could feel them in there, those who had succumbed to the Marble curse. Every stony face I looked upon was a snapshot of someone, and all they had remaining to them were thoughts and memories that they had no way to act on.

I had to quit looking at them. Maia was my only priority at that moment. Knowing what I knew from what Sarah had told me made me want to act. I wanted to find a way to make it right, to restore what Phil had brought to ruin, but it would have to wait.

Shugart and I stood on either side of her, holding her up so she could hobble along with our steps. Hugh followed right behind us. As we passed beneath the ruins of a shattered arch, I saw the broken wall where Sarah had held off my attacker. It felt like a place of power, where the memory of her dominance and sacrifice would be played out over and over until the end of time. I knew that I could see that moment again if I wanted to. I could see all of them. It was a lot for my tired mind to process, and the scope of what it might have meant baffled me.

No one said a word before we got to the shore. Shugart and Hugh were too busy looking at everything around us, and Maia’s face was tight with pure dread. Her strange, pale eyes took in nothing from our surroundings because she simply wasn’t there. She walked apart from us even as we held her up because I had forced her to revisit a past she wasn’t ready to face yet.

It made me worry that restoring her body might destroy the rest of her.

When we came to the shoreline, where the airy dark sea drifted against the stone, I stopped them. I handed Maia off to Shugart and turned to walk out among the waves. The black water—if that’s what it truly was—chilled me to my core and dimmed my vision.

“Alright, Shug, I think we walk her together, slowly. I’ve got to warn you, this shit is cold.”

He nodded and began to guide her out to me. As soon as they were close, I took her arm again and the three of us moved forward together. We continued until it was deep enough to cover her shoulders. I stopped them and stepped in front of her.

“Okay, Maia,” I said. “This is the last part, and then we can leave. We have to dunk you. I don’t know how it works, but I think we’ll know when it does. Are you ready?”

“I… oh, Will. I can feel something there. It’s bitter and it hates us.” She looked up at me. “Are you sure about this?”

“We have to,” I said. “I’m sure of that. I won’t lose you to it. If this doesn’t work, we'll find what does. I love you. I know you don’t trust this, but I need you to trust that.”

She hugged me, and I held her tight. The warmth of that connection was enough to make me forget all the cold that surrounded us. For that moment, we were all that was. Maia eventually pulled away from me and nodded. Her lips were pressed together tightly in a determined look I knew all too well. It was going to work because it had to.

“Alright, deep breath,” Shugart said. “Let’s get this over with.”

Maia took in a lungful of air, and we pushed her under. She lay there, staring up at us from beneath the surface for nearly a full minute. He and I exchanged glances, and I wondered if we needed to let her up for air. Were we doing it wrong? Her hand had been wrapped loosely around my forearm the entire time. When it happened, I saw her eyes widen in sudden terror, and her nails sank into my skin.

She squirmed and buckled, and I saw a dark shape rising up below her. Shugart yelled angrily and tugged at her arm. I heard Hugh call out from behind us and the splashes that accompanied his approach. I was also trying to pull her up, matching her piercing grip with one of my own. After a few seconds, Hugh had joined us, and he also grabbed hold of her. The three of us strained together, but we seemed to be no match for whatever it was that was trying to take her away.

I didn’t know if the cure had worked or not, but I knew that she couldn’t stay down there much longer. I took one hand off of her and held it out to them.

“*Hands!*” I screamed.

They both took my arm as I squeezed my eyes closed and tried to focus on getting us out of there. I was nearly yanked under a moment later, and a quick glance told me that I wasn’t the only one. The thing that had her had almost torn her away from us. The shadow had risen even more, and it was wrapped around her like a massive hand. As it shifted in front of her face, I watched her features change for just a moment. The only thing that remained the same were her wide, pleading eyes.

I focused on them, on getting her away, and when I closed my eyes again, I felt the connection.

We all arrived back in that tiny office of the old HunkaBurger a second later. The couch broke beneath us when we landed on it like a jumbled knot of waterlogged rag dolls. I was aching all over from the exertion of that last flip. My journeys through the various hells had never worn me out like that. I was quickly learning that my new custody of the Marble was not without its costs.

The four of us slowly untangled ourselves and I asked if anyone was hurt. It looked as though I had popped a couple of stitches, and the wound on Hugh’s arm was bleeding again. While Shugart went to grab the first aid kit, I shifted my attention over to Maia to see if it had worked.

Where the stone was once a creamy white, it had become as dark as ebony. It was also warm again, and it felt like skin. As I watched, the color gradually faded and the texture began to soften until it became like the rest of her. I looked up to her face, smiling in our little victory. Her eyes met mine briefly before turning to study the rest of the room.

“How does it feel, Maia?” I asked her. “Better?”

“Yes,” she said. “I feel great.”

She stood up then and turned around. There was a thin smile on her lips that slowly widened to reveal her teeth. They parted too as she began to laugh.

I just sat back and watched her—Maia was finally healthy and happy again.

Her enjoyment of her restored mobility was evident in the way she glided around the room. Her personality had also changed; she was no longer distant or muddled. She was curious about everything and chatty as hell. Seeing her like that made it all feel worth it. I was filled with a sense of hope that everything was going to work out, just like it had with her.

When we were finally bandaged and wearing dry clothes again, Shugart left to get some food. While he was gone, I lay back on the ruins of the couch and let myself nod off. I listened as Maia asked Hugh a thousand questions about his life, the Knotted Ring, and all the different hells he had seen. I snapped awake a little while later when Shugart came running into the room.

“The hospital,” he said breathlessly. “Captain called me. He said something strange is going on there. They sent some units, and they said it was like the place had changed. Like something out of a horror movie.”

“Sarah!” I sat up quickly.

“Yeah, I think we’ve gotta go. You good to stay with Maia again, Hugh?”

He was already nodding when she interrupted him. “I’m going with you. We have to save Will’s friend.”

I still wasn’t sure about her being there, even as the four of us crept through the security door Shugart and I had used earlier. It was like stepping back into one of the darker hells.

The tiled floors were slick with mud and filth, and most of the drywall lay in sodden piles that stretched along either side of the corridor. The exposed cinderblocks were sweating a grayish ooze that trickled down over everything, and they seemed to throb softly in the red glow of the flashing emergency lights.

Shugart walked in front at a half crouch with his pistol and a Maglite held in his crossed hands. The shotgun from his trunk hung from his shoulder. Maia and I walked together behind him. He had handed me a small revolver from his glove box after making sure I knew how to use it. Hugh took the rear, and he had brought his own gun.

We traveled the corridors at a swift but cautious pace. Shugart constantly made us hold position while he moved forward to check each junction. We passed so many bodies—nurses, patients, doctors, and security. None of them had died peacefully, and many were missing whole chunks of flesh as if it had been torn or bitten away.

The stairs were nearly blocked with piles of the dead. We were forced to climb over them in several spots. Shugart said it looked like they had all been trying to flee. Some had been trampled underneath the panicked mob, and the rest had been ripped apart.

Maia was strangely at ease. Her eyes never lingered on any of the horrors we encountered, and she moved quickly and deliberately to follow Shugart’s orders. She was no longer the scared girl she had been just a few hours before. It seemed like she was handling it better than any of the rest of us.

The detective was keeping his cool, but there was something about his darting eyes and the tight set of his jaw that told me he was feeling it, like I was. I’d seen a lot in my ten-minute jaunts across the many hells, but none of it had ever felt as real as what I was seeing then. Even Hugh was affected by it; I saw him sweating more than usual, and his face was paler than before.

Shugart started firing his gun just as soon as he opened up the stairway door to the third floor. He shot several times before retreating to where we stood.

“Hall’s full of ‘em,” he said. “Chained freaks. They look like drowning victims.”

Maia moved past him to steal a glance, and I rushed to follow her while Shug reloaded. She was almost completely out in the hall when I got to her, staring in the direction he had fired. I tried to pull her back in, but she wouldn’t budge. She glanced at me before looking back at the creatures in the hallway.

I saw them for myself when I followed her gaze. They were bloated, shambling things that barely looked human. Chains hung off of them like bayou moss, wrapped around their necks and limbs at random, and secured by hooks or studded rings buried in their corpulent flesh.

They didn’t seem to notice us at first, even though he had shot at them. Suddenly, though, the closest one looked up and saw Maia. It shrieked and began to trudge towards her, moving a lot quicker than I was comfortable with. I took aim and began firing at it. A moment later, Hugh and Shugart passed behind me and joined in the barrage.

The bullets tore into the charging monster. It was soon covered in holes that oozed black blood, and entire chunks of its body were ripped away. There were three more of them, and they also began to move towards us. As the first one went down, my gun was empty. I moved back inside and dragged Maia with me.

Hugh ran out a few seconds later. He hadn’t brought any extra rounds with him when he came to see us the day before, and we couldn’t exactly go to his apartment with the Ring after him. Shugart lasted longer. He had a couple of extra magazines, but they were soon gone as well. At the very end, he switched to his twelve gauge and fired off shots while he yelled at them.

“Still two left,” he said when he rejoined us. “We may have to backtrack, boys and girl.”

That was when the lights went out completely, trapping us in total darkness.

“What the fuck?” I heard Hugh hiss.

“Even my Mag’s dead,” Shug whispered from beside me. “Shh… listen.”

No one said anything for several moments, and I felt Shugart’s hand when he found me. A moment later he pulled Hugh over to us. That was three of us accounted for, but where was Maia?

“Come on,” she called to us from the general direction of the doorway. “They can’t see. We can slip past them if we hurry.”

“That’s useful, baby girl,” Shugart said. “But we can’t see either. Won’t do no good to try to sneak by if we end up tripping over them.”

“I can,” she said, her voice right in my ear. I felt her warm hand slide into mine. “So follow me.”

We did, and Maia guided us through the pitch-black hall. Her footing was sure and perfect on the slippery floor, and she never slowed or even faltered. She moved so silently that I couldn’t hear her, and I only heard the wet scuffing of my footsteps squishing on the disgusting, muck-covered tiles. Shugart and Hugh walked behind me, completing our chain.

Those bloated things in the dark were very close. The sounds of their tinkling chains, sliding feet, and their gurgling moans seemed to come from every direction. Twice I felt like one of them was standing right next to me. I’ll never know for sure, but I am certain that the memory of our walk through the ICU hallway will remain with me for a long time.

I think the thing that disturbed me the most was Maia. How could she see without any light, and how had she become so fearless? I’m only the occasional fool, so I knew it had something to do with her dip into the dark sea. Our journey through the darkness made me wonder, though. Had that result been what Phil had wanted?

Had what I thought was a cure actually been a baptism?

All of a sudden I emerged into the red flickering glow of the emergency lights, and Maia let go of my hand. I looked behind me and saw Hugh and Shugart there. They were just as confused as I was. There had been no gradual change, no dim light emerging in the distance. We had all been effectively blind one moment and then we could see again. I started to ask her about it, but I let it go when I saw Sarah’s room number ahead of me.

The area we were in was clean and normal-looking. Nothing had really changed since Shugart and I had left. The door was open, though, and even from where I stood, I could tell that something was wrong. I started walking towards it, but Maia stopped me for a moment by placing a gentle hand on my chest. I looked at her.

“They’ve taken her, Will,” she said. “But we’re going to get her back.”

I nodded and stepped away from her to enter the room. She was right; even the bed was gone. Someone had come in and rolled her right out. Sure, she could have been taken for tests or whatever. It was possible, but I knew it wasn’t so.

They had her.
