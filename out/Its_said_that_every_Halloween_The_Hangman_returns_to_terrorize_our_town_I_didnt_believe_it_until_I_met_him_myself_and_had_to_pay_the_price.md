“Be kind to one another this All Hallows’ Eve, lest you wanna suffer at the hands of The Hangman!” Daniels said with all the enthusiasm of a circus’ ringmaster. “What does that mean, Ackland?”

“Well, Daniels…” Ackland answered his co-host. “Some 200 years ago, The Hangman was the warden of the town of Badger’s Hollow. If there was ever a criminal in Badger’s Hollow, The Hangman would sniff them out. It didn’t matter it was for theft, murder, or assault; the punishment was always the same. They would be hung from the sycamore up on Hangman’s Hill, just outside of the town. Story goes that The Hangman would put the noose around the criminals’ necks, throw the other end of the rope over the thickest branch of the sycamore tree, and, by sheer muscle force, pull the criminals up and not let go of the rope till the bodies went limp and their skin became purple. Now, on every Halloween, when the veil between worlds grows thin enough, it’s said that The Hangman returns to his hill, watching over the town once again, eager to deliver punishment wherever he can!”

“That’s a curious story, ain’t it? We’ll return to this story, and many others, after this short message from our spons…” Daniels’ voice was cut off as Brandon paused the podcast and closed his laptop.

“It’s just the same fucking story as always.” Brandon said.

“Calm down.” I said. “It’s the 31^(st) of October; they’re gonna tell a story related to Halloween.”

Brandon sat down on his bed, crossing his arms and looking away from me. He was already crashing from all the sugar he’d consumed that evening.

“Can’t we just go out and trick-or-treat?” He pouted. “It’s not like it’s a crime.”

“C’mon, man.” I answered him. “We can just buy candy; we don’t need to beg for it.”

We were 18 years old. It was embarrassing enough trick-or-treating the year before, AND the year before that. This year, I’d finally managed to convince Brandon to stay home for Halloween. It’d taken a ton of convincing and a mound of candy, soon to be empty; even still, I could tell that it was going to be a rough night regardless.

“Hmph…” He turned his head away. “It’s not the same if you buy it.”

He grabbed the last mars bar from the box and stuffed it into his mouth.

“Dude!” I said. “Couldn’t you have saved me just ONE of those?”

“Christ, Mike… I’m having a tough fucking evening.” He answered. “Can’t you spare a bit of sympathy? Is it so bad that I want a mars bar when I’m down?”

I sighed. Brandon. Brandon. Brandon. It was always about what he wanted to do, and I always had to be the pleaser and follow along to keep the peace. He was the kind of friend who possessed the remarkable ability to make you feel like a side character in your own life; the kind of friend that you’re only around because it feels familiar, not because you particularly like them.

“Hey, let’s go down to the 7-eleven and get something more.” He said. “I’m still craving.”

“Sure… Why not.”

He opened his drawer and pulled out the pair of zombie masks we’d worn the year prior. I put on one of them and Brandon took the other. I didn’t mind. It would make him happier, and it would hide my embarrassment when we walked outside. We got out onto the street and began making our way towards the 7-eleven. There was no shortage of kids out on the street. We passed several vampires, scary clowns, and small devils, and, although he was wearing a mask, I could tell how jealous Brandon was. Then, up ahead of us, there was a bedsheet-ghost coming our way. They were swinging a pillowcase, obviously filled to the brim with candy. As we came closer, Brandon suddenly grabbed me around my wrist.

“Are you ready?” He whispered in my ear when he was within an arm’s length from the bedsheet-ghost.

Before I could even process what he was talking about, he grabbed the rim of the pillowcase, ripped it out of the hands of the bedsheet-ghost so hard that they fell backwards and tumbled onto the asphalt. Then Brandon ran, pulling me along by my wrist.

“WHAT ARE YOU DOING?!” I yelled as we ran away.

“Relax,” He yelled back at me. “Live a little!”

Then something happened which neither of us had foreseen.

“HEY, stop right where you are!” A deep voice shouted from behind us.

We came to a halt and turned around. Further down the street a police car was parked, blinking red and blue. One officer was comforting the bedsheet-ghost on the sidewalk, and another officer was slowly approaching us.

Without any further hesitation, Brandon began running again, and I was pulled along with him. Not even five seconds had passed before we could hear the police sirens pursuing us. It was madness. We couldn’t outrun a police car; I knew that much. Then Brandon suddenly took a sharp turn into an alleyway between two houses on the street, slim enough that the police car couldn’t drive through it. The alleyway was lined with fences leading into backyards. The police sirens were nearing the entrance to the alleyway as we ran through it. Brandon sprinted towards one of the short fences, saw no one in the backyard, and then we jumped over it. Once inside, we pressed our backs up against the fence, holding our breath. Soon enough, we heard the steps of the policeman as he ran through the alleyway, continuing past the fence that we had just jumped. Brandon let out a sigh of relief, overjoyed that he was safe. He pulled off his mask and opened the pillowcase to see what he’d gathered.

“Why the fuck did you do that?” I asked him as I pulled off my own mask, soaked in spit and sweat.

“Come on.” He said, pulling out a handful of gummy bears and showing them in his mouth. “It’s called trick-or-treat, right? It was just a trick.”

“Stealing from a kid isn’t much of a trick!”

“Pfft. Whatever, Mike.” He said, filling his mouth with another handful of gummy bears.

Then something dawned on me.

“Brandon, we can’t go home now. The police are probably looking for us. If we just walk back to the house, someone’s probably gonna recognize us.”

“Then I guess we gotta hide.” Brandon eyes shifted away from the candy and up towards Hangman’s Hill. “Somewhere no one’s gonna look.”

“No way.” I said. “Can’t we just give the candy back to that kid and apologize?”

“C’mon, man. We can’t just give it back like that.” He dug into the pillowcase. “Here! There’s something for both of us in here?” He pulled out a mars bar and slid it into one of the pockets of my jacket. “Anyways, you don’t wanna snitch on your best friend, right?” He said with all the complacency of a rotten politician.

“For fuck’s sake.” I answered as a smile formed on Brandon’s face.

We pulled our masks back on, Brandon stuffed the candy into his pockets, and then we began making our way towards Hangman’s Hill, crouching behind bushes, jumping over fences, and running through backyards, managing to stay out of sight of everyone.

We finally made it out of the town and didn’t stop running till we reached the top of the hill. Brandon grabbed onto the branches of the sycamore tree and pulled himself up. I followed along and we climbed up into the tree crown. From there, we could see all of Badger’s Hollow.

“This is perfect!” Brandon exclaimed. “We’ll just sit up here until midnight, and then there won’t be anyone on the streets to recognize us.”

I remained silent, still trying to catch my breath from climbing.

“…You’re welcome by the way!” Brandon said before leaning back and closing his eyes.

I was about to answer, but kept my mouth shut. It wasn’t even worth answering him. I just followed his lead and leaned back on the branches of the tree, closing my eyes.

“Mike, wake up! Something’s wrong!”

I almost fell from the branch with how much Brandon shook me.

“Relax, man.” I answered. “What’s the matter?”

Brandon just pointed down towards the town, and I instantly understood.

A great mist had swallowed up the entirety of Badger’s Hollow; a mist so thick that we couldn’t make out anything within the town: no buildings, streetlamps, people, or any source of light.

“God damn. What time is it?” I asked.

“I don’t fucking know man… My phone won’t turn on.”

I pulled out my own phone and faced the same problem.

“Maybe we should just head back home.” I said.

Brandon nodded, but I could tell he wasn’t feeling confident. I couldn’t blame him; I didn’t feel particularly secure myself. We climbed down and started walking towards the town. With every step we took on the way, I was filled with more and more dread; this only worsened when we arrived at the border of the town. Even though the mist was thick as a pea soup and we couldn’t see further than ten feet in front of us, it was obvious that the town was in a blackout. We walked further into town and regardless of the direction we looked there was no light, not in any of the houses nor any of the streetlamps. Though we had lived in Badger’s Hollow all our life, the town was unrecognizably lifeless on that misty night.

“HELLO?” Brandon yelled at the top of his lungs, careless that we should be keeping a low profile. “Is anybody there?”

The silence of the streets prevailed until, suddenly, there was the sound of thundering steps, slowly approaching us.

“Hey, over here!” Brandon was ecstatic.

The steps kept approaching, but no one exited from the mist. Instead, we began walking towards the source of the sound. Then the steps suddenly halted. We stopped dead in our tracks.

“Hello?” I said cautiously.

Silence fell once again, until the sound of the steps suddenly returned, this time coming from behind us. Still, there was no one coming out of the mist. I was stricken with the feeling that whoever it was, it wasn’t someone coming to help us. I grabbed Brandon by the wrist and began pulling him with me in the opposite direction of the sound.

But the sound followed us. I could feel my heart pounding in my throat. Brandon and I picked up the pace trying to get away, but it didn’t matter; the sound was still tailing us. It was then we started sprinting. Even so, the sound was still right behind us. Even worse, it was getting harder to breathe. It felt as if something was constricting my throat, not letting me take in a full breath of air. I tried to keep the pace, but my legs were burning, and my lungs were screaming for all the air I couldn’t provide them. I was slowing down and Brandon was struggling to pull me along. Suddenly, without any warning, he ripped his arm free from my grip and ran away screaming for help at the top of his lungs. The last thing running through my mind as he disappeared into the mist was the mere thought that Brandon really was a waste of a friend. I fell to my knees and slumped down onto the pavement, wheezing for air. As I lay, I could hear the steps steadily approaching. I looked behind. As I finally saw something take shape inside the mist, I felt the mars bar in my jacket. I dug into my pocket, pulled out the candy bar, and threw it in front of me with all the force I could gather.

“I didn’t want to be a part of this …” The lack of air was getting to my head. “Please… I’m sorry…” The last thing I remember was seeing the silhouette of something big emerging from the mist before I passed out.

“Hey, wake up!”

I could feel something poking my chest. I opened my eyes and sat up. I was sitting on the sidewalk. A policeman was standing over me, holding a baton, looking at me inquiringly.

“So, it’s you who’s stealing candy from little kids, huh?” The policeman said, grabbing my shoulder and pulling me up, looking at me with a satisfied grin. “We’re just gonna ask you a few questions down at the station, understood?”

I just nodded and got into the car. Sitting down, I pulled off my mask and looked out of the window. The streets of Badger’s Hollow were once again alive with kids trick-or-treating. I leaned back in my seat, letting out a great sigh of relief. Down at the police station, I was asked about the incident with the bedsheet-ghost and where my “partner in crime” was. I told it like it was.

“My friend, Brandon, was the one who stole the candy and knocked the kid backwards. He just pulled me along, but he ran away from me at some point. I don’t know where he is!”

There was no point in telling them about my experience. At best, they’d think I was trying to foist a story upon them to get out of trouble, and, quite frankly, I found myself not really caring much about whatever happened to Brandon, wherever he was.

Brandon’s parents were of course very concerned when he never made it home that Halloween. The authorities could never figure out where he’d run off to. For all they knew, he’d vanished into thin air. The only trace anyone ever found was a zombie-mask soaked in his spit, lying beneath the sycamore tree up on Hangman’s Hill.
