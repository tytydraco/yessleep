I've lurked here for years and never thought I'd ever be posting here. I'm not going to name the place or the country. If you've been there, you'll know. If you haven't, please don't go looking for it.

My partner (I'll call her M) and I have been travelling for most of this year. Money got tight. M found a work exchange: a few hours of work a day for a bed and three meals, at an old château out in the boonies. I didn't want to go. I want that on the record.

Before they accepted us, the host had us fill out a long questionnaire. I'll call him W. Then he emailed us a form he called a "pattern reading". It asked for our birth times down to the minute, and the towns our grandmothers were born in. I asked M if that was normal. She said it was probably an astrology thing. I filled it in anyway.

I keep thinking about that.

The château looked exactly like the photos, which somehow made it worse. Stone walls, towers, fields in every direction. A cellar too of course...though not pictured. There were a few other volunteers. Most of them seemed to have been doing this kind of thing for a long time, moving from place to place. One of them, an older guy I'll call P, smiled at everything.

W doesn't own the château, by the way. An old man does. In my time there I never saw him once. But W talks about the place like it's already his. "When it's ours." "After it's ours." He says it casually, like a weather forecast.

**The Circle**:

Every morning at nine we sat in a circle around a candle. W lit it and asked the question of the day.

*What are you carrying that isn't yours? Who did you used to be?*

I answered. Everyone answered. It took 45 minutes, sometimes an hour, and it didn't count toward the volunteer hours. Neither did meals. Nobody ever said you couldn't leave the table early, but if someone stood up, the talking stopped until they sat back down. So nobody stood up.

By day two, I realized I hadn't been alone for more than ten minutes since we arrived.

P told me the first week is the hardest. "*Your old self fights it,"* he said, smiling. *"Then one day you stop counting."*

I asked counting what. He just kept smiling.

**The Cellar:**

On day two, W gave us a tour. He saved the cellar for last.

The room at the top has no working light. W held up his phone. There was a narrow bed, and on it was a mannequin of a young adult, maybe 18, lying face down with his wrists chained to the frame. A small rat figurine sat in the middle of his back. A bat hung from the ceiling on fishing line, right above his head.

"*The owner put this here," W said. "We don't touch it."*

M asked what it meant. W said nobody knows. The owner has never explained it, and nobody has asked twice.

Then he turned off his phone light while we were still standing in there. Just for a second. I don't think it was an accident.

**The Bicycles:**

My job was fixing up a shed full of old bicycles so guests could ride them. I like bikes, so honestly it was the best part of my day. There were eleven of them, all rusted. The smallest was a teen bike, red, with a bell that still worked.

When I took the saddle off to replace it, I found tally marks scratched into the metal underneath. Rows and rows of them, small and neat, the way you'd mark days on a calendar.

I counted and stopped at 200.

**Night 3:**

I couldn't sleep, so I went down to the kitchen for water. The stairwell to the tower is right off the main hall. The door at the bottom was open, and there was candlelight coming down the stairs.

I heard W's voice. Soft and patient, the same voice he uses in the circle.

*"Who did you used to be?"*

Nobody answered.

*"That's alright,"* he said. *"You'll remember."*

I went back to bed and layed there until it got light. I didn't tell M. I told myself he was just practicing. He was talking to himself. He was on the phone. Something.

The question of the day the next morning was...you guessed it: *Who did you used to be?*

**Day 4:**

This morning, M shared in the circle. She said she's the kind of person who never needs to look for motivation when she has a purpose. But here she just feels flat. She said she thought we'd be helping work towards something, and instead it feels like we're cleaning rooms so a rich old man gets richer.

Nobody said anything.

I mean nobody. Not a sound. I watched the candle, and the flame was completely still, like there was no air in the room at all.

Then W smiled and said, "Thank you for sharing," and moved on to the day's tasks.

After breakfast, one of the other volunteers took M aside. M told me later what she said: *"It feels like that at first. It gets easier once you stop counting."*

That's the same thing P said to me. Almost word for word.

**Tonight:**

At dinner, W leaned over and told us our pattern readings were done. *"Very interesting,"* he said. *"You both have the same pattern as the others."*

I asked what pattern. He said we'd talk about it in the circle.

After M fell asleep, I went up to the tower. I'm not sure why. I wanted to see it again with my own eyes. Curiosity got the better of me I guess.

The boy's head was turned toward the door.

I know what I saw on day two. He was face down. His face was in the mattress. Now it was turned to the side, toward the stairs, like he'd heard someone coming.

The rat was gone from his back.

I found it when I got back to our room. It was sitting on M's pillow, an inch from her face. She was still asleep.

I'm writing this from the kitchen. It's almost 3am. I put the rat in my pocket because I didn't know what else to do with it, and I can feel it there while I type.

The worst part is M. Before bed she told me she feels better. Lighter. She said she's stopped keeping track of the hours, and it's such a relief. She wants to stay another month.

I'm going to try to get us out of here tomorrow. If I can't convince her, I don't know what I'll do.

W  passed me on the stairs on my way down just now. He wasn't surprised to see me. He asked if I'd thought about this morning's question.

I said I hadn't.

He said, *"That's alright. You'll remember."*
