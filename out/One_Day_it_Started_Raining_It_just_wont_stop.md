At first I thought nothing of it, just a sudden downpour during the ride home. I recall running straight home after getting off my stop and getting soaked all over. And by the time I reached the apartment lobby my clothes were already dripping puddles in a matter of minutes of being outside. That was how bad the rain was. I stood by the elevator waiting. Then I got on. There wasn't anything worth noting afterwards. The important stuff happened on the next day.

I checked the windows next to my bed after getting up; one hell of a shower out there. It didn't really matter whether it rained or not today since I didn't have anything planned so I just continued on with my day doing morning chores and taking a breather in front of the TV. Around noon I checked the weather app out of curiosity. If it had rained this heavily all night then surely it would go away sometime soon I thought.

Contrary to my expectation the app stated "Sunny" with rather warm temps and a low chance of precipitation. My first instinct was to check if I had the location correctly set, and it was. Then I double checked the date. That too was correct. Afterwards I took a second peak outside. There's no mistaking the sight of tiny drops racing to strike the ground with a faint *plat* that sounded almost like TV static when they were this abundant. 

There must be an error with the app I thought. But boy was I wrong. It just gets weirder from then on.

I was in the middle of grabbing everything I needed to make a small pot of Pho for tonight. Normally I'd have everything required but I had neglected to stock up on a few crucial ingredients.

Luckily for me, there was a market around the corner that required only a five minute brisk walk. I got myself dressed with only a light coat on top and equipped with an umbrella from the hanger. My first steps on the sidewalk were as hectic as I imagined. The rapid strikes against my umbrella, navigating around numerous puddles, and finding my socks soaked in mere steps. I was practically jogging just to get through.

Taking shelter in the entrance area, I was shaking my umbrella dry when I noticed a young woman entering. I admit staring at her as she walked by empty handed and without an ounce of care for the fact her clothes were leaking a trail behind her tracks. My mind couldn't believe anyone would be wearing jeans and only a t-shirt in this sort of weather. It was rather chilly too, but then again girls wore skirts even if it was winter when I was back in high school.

While picking out ingredients, I was humming an unfamiliar tune that got stuck in my head the night before while I was still riding the bus. I don't know what it's from but it was a rather solemn melody. Almost as if it was from a tragedy of a bygone era. Not sure why it stuck with me so much, but it really resonated with me.  

I tried making some small talk with the cashier, starting off with the rain as you'd expect with how feral it was. She looked confused, but then laughed it off as a joke after glancing at the glass pane.

"We really could use some rain around here," she said, while weighing the onions.There wasn't any humor behind her words. Her smile persisted until she caught my rude expression, and sheepishly finished scanning the rest of my items without us attempting to mend the awkward moment. She behaved in the same manner a scolded child would with their parents.

Looking back at it, it was my fault. I was just so taken aback with how she didn't acknowledge that it was clearly storming out there. That was when I started to question the situation. 

I grabbed my groceries and started moving along, stopping a step before the rain, feeling the cold air brush against my skin as the door opened. Earlier, the umbrella was in the way so I didn't notice it at first. And now that I had a proper look, everyone seemed unusually indifferent to the rain and greyscale skies. Paying no mind to the overflowing puddles that overcrowded storm drains, nor wearing the proper attire that fitted the uncomfortable coldness.

Was I the only one seeing this downpour? Feeling the raindrop brush against their skin and dripping off of them? The emotions I felt were... odd. Concerned more for my self than anyone else caught in the rain. I thought I was losing it at first. But when my hand reached for the rain, it was as real as it could be. Flowing out of my palms and down my elbow, dripping onto my shoes.

I hurriedly went home, into my kitchen, and laid my bags on the table. Then I stood still, lost in thought as I stared out the window once again. My mind was racing. Coming up with some kind of rational thought that explained all of this wasn't easy. In the end, I could not come to a single conclusion, even after pouring a portion out for myself.

Eating took me longer than usual. My appetite wasn't really there. By the time I finished half of it, the broth became lukewarm, the noodles all mushy and every attempt to grip it ended up breaking it. Then eventually, I ended up just picking at it with my chopsticks while scrolling through my feed.

From then on everyday started being monotonous. Going to work. Heading straight home. And being stuck here away from the rain. I can't stand feeling like a wet cat. At my workplace, my coworkers started throwing peeks at me, coming up one time to ask why I started wearing longer sleeves and bringing an umbrella every time I clocked in.

The only excuse I could come up with was that "my skin started being extra sensitive and prone to sunburns." They started being understanding, but I couldn't shake off the feeling they were gossiping behind my back.

Everything's the same a month later. My complexion has gone pale and it still continues to rain relentlessly. I've been keeping myself busy by keeping my abode nice and tidy, a little distraction from the melancholy lately. There hasn't been any real reason to be outside but, I wouldn't be lying if I said I missed the sunlight. 

Everyday I'd look out to the despondent skies through the torrents dripping down my windows. And down below, the flood had risen to around ankle length. It wasn't much but it still bothered me, having to walk in a pond. Now I've been wearing rubber rain boots and bringing my sneakers in a bag. And yes, others started to think I look ridiculous. There really wasn't an excuse I could come up with. It's whatever if they think it's weird.

There was one notable thing that happened however. One day, when I was walking towards my bus stop there was a hooded man standing around; soaked as per usual like everyone else. I didn't think anything of him at first cause it must've actually been raining. He adorned himself in the holiest rain coat I've seen. And by holiest, I mean riddled with holes like swiss cheese. Haha... always wanted to use that reference.

Anyways, as I was saying. I had a couple minutes to wait, most of that time spent waiting beneath the bus stop shelter while that odd fellow remained outside. Silent too. Everything was normal, even as we entered the bus. 

I scanned my phone on the barcode next to the ramp. *Beep.* The bus was practically empty, with only two others already sitting down further back and seemingly dozing off. I sat down on the rigged plastic seats lined up along the inner wall facing the back exit.

On the other hand, the guy who was waiting with me was slow with it. I don't mean this disrespectfully, but the way he crept up to insert his bus ticket felt unnerving. I've seen my fair share of addicts with their trembling hands and their disheveled appearance. Felt bad for them at times. But this guy? Not a single twitch as he lifted his arm. He had confidence in his hunched stride.

I had only a glimpse of his soulless eyes and pale complexion, barely visible beneath the wet hair that stuck to his face like streaks of rain. He immediately sat down on my left, a couple seats away, after inserting his card.

He was normal during the ride, that's what I'd like to think at least. It was a rather long bus route so I ended up letting my guard down and falling asleep. I woke up a couple stops before mine and noticed that the guy was already gone. 

Once again, I thought nothing of it. That was until I approached the back exit and caught a glimpse of him at the corner of my eyes. He was there, gazing through me from the end of the bus with nothing between us. It was palpable, the way he smiled at me with his crooked teeth showing below his thin pale lips gripped my chest with unease. Then he mouthed something. My instincts irked me to get home a soon as possible. And so I did without bothering to pull out my umbrella.

Along the way to my apartment entrance, I could barely see him through the windows as the bus rolled forward, still peering back at me with that uncanny smile of his. Pressing and waiting for the elevator felt tense when it really shouldn't be. The next bus stop after mine was a considerable distance for him to catch up and follow me home. If he even wanted to, that is. It was entirely unrealistic for something like that to happen.

*DING!* I could hear my neck snapping into place, facing towards the entrance. Contrary to my expectations, it was just a young woman. Her family was one of the more recent residents in this building. Helped them settle in. The apartments themselves aren't actually all that bad being only a decade old. Nothing seemed run down, carpets had a blue dim to them, and it smelled nothing like dust and mildew.

"Mrs.Bélanger! How are you?" It was just a light greeting. 

"Doing very well, Avery. How's your parents?" I responded with a simple, yet fragile smile. "Haven't seen you guys in a while."

This wasn't the first time she'd seen my current state. I assumed that she assumed it's just life taking a toll on me. That isn't necessarily wrong. I've already became accustomed to the rain by now.  
We merely chatted for a bit as we rode the lift. That interaction really calmed me down. Her floor was below mine so we parted ways earlier than I'd like.

Returning home, my doors were locked immediately. I usually neglect turning the dial, aside from heading to bed of course, but I still couldn't shake off any imagery of that guy. Not very befitting for someone who's usually so composed. Honestly, I just wanted to be in the comfort of my own couch with the curtains closed, and the TV playing whatever show caught my attention.

Eventually my eyes caved in and I started envisioning sheep bouncing around me. I'm pretty sure I passed out with that same melody reverberating in my head. Like any other time.

I was suddenly startled awake by a steady *knock knock knock.* 

It was a built-in reflex for my legs to make its way towards the door. My hands reached for the locking mechanism, lightly gripping the dial and turning it ever so slightly. 

Not sure if it was paranoia that kicked in at that exact moment, but my fingers hesitated. I reluctantly peered through the peephole and my heart raced right away at the sight of a brown, dreary raincoat. *Click!* My lock returned to its fully closed position as soon I registered the sight.

I could see it clearly as he moved closer in. Those hyaline eyes of his were edging to submerge me with its frigid grasp. That fraction of a moment made me fall back and stumble onto my palms. 

He knocked again, sounding desperate as he cried out, "H-h-hey, I just want to talk. Yea talk. Just a small talk about it. I followed your trail." 

I did not utter a single response.

His tone shifted and he started laughing. "You. Yea you. You can see it too, right?" It was hard hearing his babbles through the other end of the door.

My brain was screaming at me to pick up the phone and call 911. But my hands stopped in their tracks within my pockets when he said this,

"That the tears wouldn't stop. I'm sure of it. Y-you sang it. Or at least heard it since I heard it from you." His punches were swift and yet had weight to them.

My hands exited my pockets with nothing in them and I took another look. I was still terrified, however my curiosity overpowered that safety mechanism of mine. He was still leaning inwards, with those abyssal eyes now seeping a steady stream of tears. And that uncanny smile again. This time, wide open and collecting those same tears, overflowing out from his lips. He expressed only agony as his cheeks twitched.

Of course I was taken aback and pulled away. There was also another thud. When I had my third look, he was already on the ground coughing relentlessly and grasping his chest.

Security came moments later. I wasn't the one who requested them. Someone must've heard the increasingly worrisome ruckus and called. I was briefly questioned before the ambulance arrived. I really didn't know what I could say besides the fact that he somehow followed me. 

I was later told I'd have to file a statement and maybe some follow ups. And yes. Even a day later, the rain rages on. And I still ponder over what he said about the tears. I'm just writing about my experiences cause I hope someone can help me out. I know that I sound a little too calm about it, but it really is tormenting me. I'll follow up with another post if anything comes up.
