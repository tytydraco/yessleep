I’ve never loved corn. Boiled, grilled, sweet in a salad. Hell, I don’t appreciate it. I like seeing green fields in all directions, stalks waving in the wind, and all that. But when it’s ripe and yellow, it gives me a case of vomiting.

My old man is from Iowa, just like all his kin. I often used to stay at my grandmother’s farm. A few years back she and my father had a huge fight about something and were not on speaking terms. My trips to the farm were reduced to twice a year. Today something strange happened. As is traditional after his Sunday sermon about how a fifteen-year-old kid should live these days, my father and I set the table. We were eating breakfast in silence. He boiled a pot of you-know-what. A couple times a year, my grandma sends a wooden crate full of fresh ears. Dad doesn’t forbid pampering her grandson with homegrown corn. Weirdly he eats almost all of it himself. I only manage a couple of ears and only eat them under threat of withheld allowance.

More oddly, I was liking that batch, but something else was off. During breakfast, my father got an audio message. For about three minutes, after setting aside his silverware, he took it in. I managed to overhear the end, “In a former life.” And I’m not sure if I heard, “In a former life” or just “Former life.” I’ve heard this line before, but never seen him react like that. He looked weird or crazy or something. Dad put his smartphone on the table and froze, seeming to sleep with his eyes open. My sense was that he was being reminded of something that he should have remembered but forgot anyway.

I shouted at him twice. He snapped out of it and slowly turned his head to me, “We’re driving to Iowa to see Grandma.” I don’t know why but right then I assumed she was dead so we’d have to wear black suits. I asked if everything was OK, but he was quiet. For a moment, I thought he was smiling. Leaving the living room, he turned on his feet and said I needed to bring my good mood along. This was witty but not like him at all.

As I finished munching the last of the first cob, I bit down on something hard. Fuck! That shit broke my tooth! It didn’t end with a normal *ow*. After I spat out the chunk of molar straight onto the plate along with chewed kernels, my roar filled the house and I collapsed, clutching my jaw. Good thing the rug was at the cleaner’s. Blood and spit wash off laminate easily. My dad ran into the room and stared at me doubled over in pain, then, completely unfazed, he wondered aloud if I maybe needed help. I told him to throw out the rest of the corn and feed future batches as scraps to pigs at a local farm. At that, he smiled and left the room. Once I stopped screaming, I picked up the cause of the agony. It looked like a tooth but it wasn’t mine. It was shaped just like a yellow kernel. I spent a long while trying to make sense of what it looked like most, then went to the mirror and lined the corn tooth up against my front right tooth—their size and shape matched. But what was it? And how did it end up in my cob? I slipped the artifact into my pocket.

The drive took just a few hours, all in silence. I wanted to talk about that hellish breakfast, but my old man seemed lost in concentration. Before long, I gave up trying to chat, pulled out my headphones, and pressed my face against the window. The sea of corn was beautiful but mind numbing, and today I hated it. Endless green depression. Then a thought hit me - think about it: why do aliens always pick cornfields to do art? It’s simple: the poor bastards look down from above and see smooth soccer fields! It must suck how disappointed they are as they come in for a landing and realize the fields are nothing like what they expected. The trick is how to draw something here in a green thicket with a yellow surprise inside.

When we pulled up to the farmhouse, Jack, an old, one-eared border collie, barked at us. Usually, after a long sniffing, he’d recognize us and start licking our hands, tail wagging. It has just been a few months since I was last here; the dog couldn’t have forgotten me that fast. Old age, most likely. Grandma appeared on the porch and hollered at Jack, who retreated to the garden, his tail between his legs, over to the shade of his favorite apple tree. We greeted Grandma. Dad went inside, while I decided to take a long stroll around the farm. Every time I come here, I can’t help but be amazed that she manages on her own. Sure, she has a few farmhands, and things get done somehow, but my grandfather used to be a force. He passed away nine years ago, and Grandma changed drastically after that; I could not think of a time since then when she was this cheerful and excited.

I headed over to check on Betty, the only horse on the farm. As I approached the rundown barn, I noticed a metal vat that hadn’t been here before. Flies swarmed over it. The contents reminded me of rotten eggplants mixed with animal hides. I almost threw up. I forgot my plan to visit the mare and hurried back to the house. Grandma explained that the stuff in the vat was just corn fertilizer, a secret formula to boost yields, or something like that. I had to binge on fruit to get rid of the memory and the lingering taste in my mouth.

When I walked over to the window, I spotted Jack. He was sitting stock-still, staring right into my eyes. He wasn’t angry, snarling, or barking. His face just said: *Hey man, I know everything, so don’t you kid yourself that you’ve fooled everyone*. Just then, my chubby cousin’s hand came down on my shoulder out of thin air - he’s a fan of stuffing his face and telling old jokes. Who even cracks jokes anymore?

We sat down around the table, and my grandma called on everyone to hold hands and do evening prayers. I don’t mind praying, as long as it doesn’t drag on a half hour. Thank God, today’s was brief. We started in eating. In the center of the table sat a large platter piled with boiled corn on the cob. Everyone took an ear. It was an unstated family tradition for victuals to start off with corn. After studying the yellow mountain, I finally found the tiniest ear and, satisfied, put it on my plate. My grandma, sitting right next to me, smiled and grabbed the pot of mashed corn. She dished me out a double portion. Generous compensation. Everyone dug in; the clattering of utensils got louder. I tried to chew on my left side, my broken tooth’s dull throb still issuing reminders.

A knock at the door interrupted dinner. In walked a gentleman who looked like Tim Blake Nelson playing Buster Scruggs. The kind of slick, dead-sure guy who’s about to close a deal—no question. Everyone shook his hand as he fired off quick witticisms like revolver shots. But he didn’t offer me his hand. In trademark fashion, he tipped the broad brim of his cowboy hat, smiled, and said, “We’ve definitely met in a former life.” It didn’t sound like a question, more like the first half of a secret code, like he was expecting me to supply the second part. I smiled and tried to stand up from my chair when a sharp pain shot through my temples. My ears started ringing, and I could hear my heart racing. My cousin rushed over, babbling something. The horror was plain on his face. Grandma fetched some water. And Dad helped me upstairs. I decided to finish dinner in that bedroom but before I knew it, I was asleep and slept straight through until late in the evening. When I woke up, I went downstairs to join everyone else. My head was still throbbing, but it was a different kind of pain. Our original plan was to stay the night, but Dad announced that we needed to head home. I didn’t object - with a headache like this, I had nothing left to do here anyway.

I slept the whole way. Right inside my apartment door, I felt the old pain attack me with double the force. No joke, I was scared. I decided: either I’ve been poisoned or even better have caught one of those country diseases. I went upstairs to my room and showered. Then my memory kind of has a hole in it. Next thing I knew I was looking in the mirror, with a razor in my hand and my face lathered up. But I had no beard growth to shave. I get that from my father. I rinsed, toweled off, and wandered over to my laptop to put my whole story on here while my noggin still works. I’m writing with a nasty hangover. My mouth is sour from drinking. I reek of whiskey just like Grandpa Bill in his prime. I am remembering picking corn with my grandma and handing her ears….But wait! I’ve never been to a harvest. Did someone get me drunk? Why??? Damn. My head feels like it’s about to crack open. I’ll stop there and go take aspirin.

I see my reflection in his eyes. He is me. We parted ways but are still an intact whole. We can talk to one another without opening our mouths. We keep the same time as we breathe. Tomorrow we’ll visit several farms and fertilize the corn fields. Ripe corn on the cob will reach your tables, and we’ll definitely meet up.
