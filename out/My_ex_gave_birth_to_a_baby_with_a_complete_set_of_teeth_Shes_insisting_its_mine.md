“That ain’t mine.”

She looked… great.

Better than great, actually. For someone who’d recently been pregnant, she looked stunning. I hadn’t seen Darlene in about a year, and though I welcomed the break from our decades-long, on-and-off fling, romantic distance was against her nature. 

“Uhh…congenital something-or-other. She’ll need a dentist, but otherwise, nothing to do about it.”

*That* look.

*This is how it always starts* \- I reminded myself.

*She ropes you in with the promise of a few sleepless nights, and before you know it, you’re back in her bed, under her goddamned thumb…*

“Last fuckin' time I answer one of Darlene’s texts,” I whispered.

*Darlene <3 XOXO (11:09 PM): “I promise, she’s yours. A mother knows. If you spend more time with her, you’ll see that.”*

*Darlene <3 XOXO (1:01 AM): “Come back. She’s crying for you."*

*Darlene <3 XOXO (3:46 AM): “I miss you. If you’re here within the hour, I’ll* ***\[ insert the lewdest series of words you can imagine \]*** *“*

I was going to need that paternity test.

Well, almost everything.

That was fifteen years ago.

And felt nothing.

*Maybe after another fifteen years, I’ll finally be ready -* I thought. 

*Until then, there are bills to be paid.*

Nearest commercial paternity testing center was near Tampa. The out-of-pocket cost wasn’t pretty. Even worse, I’d need to procure a DNA sample from the kid. *Will I even be able to fit the cheek swab into her mouth with those godforsaken chompers?*

I chuckled.

Nearby, my phone screen lit up.

My chuckle morphed into a scowl.

It’d been hours since I last received something unhinged from Darlene. In a sense, I was overdue. I cracked my neck, threw back another swig, and grabbed the phone. 

***EMERGENCY ALERT***: *Flash flood warning for this area until 12:30 AM EST. Please exercise extreme caution and avoid driving unless absolutely necessary…*

“Huh,” I muttered. Didn’t know a storm was coming.

A faint sound was floating through the pub. 

A clicking. Mechanical, maybe.

A violent heartbeat shuddered through my body.

I couldn’t tell how many, but I could tell they were staring at me, and I could tell *that’s* where the noise was coming from.

I looked right. 

No sign of whoever had been peeking through the door.

No sign of anyone, actually.

Main Street was vacant, though that wasn’t completely out of the norm; destitution had stripped the once thriving marketplace of life. Potholes of varying depths littered the asphalt. Monsterous weeds sprang through the fractured sidewalk. Most of the storefronts were derelict, boarded up and hollowed out, the graffiti-stained carcasses of long-dead ambitions. The only businesses left standing were the pub and the dollar store, and judging by their darkened windows, they’d closed early for the day. 

The clicking noise curled into my ear. It was closer. 

Right behind me. 

I flipped around. 

Shadow-cast figures were disappearing into the alleyway. 

They rounded a corner, and then they were gone. 

I couldn’t move. 

I stood frozen, staring down the narrow, trash-laden alleyway, listening to the wind rush and whisper, until a boom of thunder broke my stupor. 

I closed the pub without calling my boss and bolted to my car. 

Rain pelted the windshield in furious sheets. I careened down the highway, my mind that panicked kind of empty, my foot a lead brick against the accelerator. As the minutes ticked by, however, my fear began to wane, and a numbing rationality crept in. 

*What was I so afraid of?* \- I asked myself. 

*Strung-out teenagers making strange noises through the pub door? A baby with a terrible deformity? Darlene, a flawed but beautiful woman - another casualty of this dead end place?*

My foot eased off the pedal.

My phone vibrated against my thigh. 

A single unread text message illuminated the interior of my sedan. 

*Darlene <3 XOXO (11:05 PM): Last chance, Jacob. Come over.* *Now.*

Heat swelled behind my eyes.

*Me (11:06 PM): This is insane. U need to get a fucking grip*

*Me (11:08 PM): listen up Darlene. Im getting a paternity test. I’ll be over tmrw to swab that kids cheek. Ill mail you the results whenever i get them. After that im done*

*Me (11:14 PM): for real this time*

I sat in my car a while, watching the screen, skin tingling with nervous electricity, but five minutes passed, then ten, then twenty, and still, no response. 

That was unlike Darlene. 

I scoffed and rolled my eyes, portraying false bravado - a show for no one but myself - and stepped out onto the driveway. I paced through the downpour. Shut the front door and deadbolted it behind me. I peered through the door’s frosted glass window and thought I saw something. Shapes navigating the blackness, darting through the evergreens that encircled my home. 

That *was* my last chance, I think. 

I needed to run. 

I did not.

Instead, I scolded myself. 

Convinced myself I was imagining things. 

I splashed some liquor into an unwashed mug, drank it all in a single gulp, and collapsed onto my bed. 

Sleep came quickly. It was thick and dreamless.

My eyes scanned the darkness.

I searched for the danger, for whatever had set my nerves on fire. The storm had slowed to drizzle, gently pitter-pattering against the shingles. The bedroom was still. The night outside my window was murky and impenetrable, and I reminded myself that was normal; no neighbors meant no ambient light. Everything was fine.

My eyes drifted back to the rain-streaked window. 

I squinted. 

There was something odd at its center. 

A small puff of condensation, fading, then expanding, fading, then expanding. 

I slipped out from the covers and planted my feet on the floor, phone in hand. 

It was probably nothing. No, it *had* to be nothing. But I needed to be sure.

Inches from the glass, I still couldn’t see anything, but I struggled to shine my phone at window, dread surging up my spine.

Lightning split the sky.

Darlene’s baby, face pressed to the window, eyes bloodshot and trained on me. Hot breath spilled through the slits in their perfect teeth, fogging the glass. Someone was behind the child, holding them by the armpits with gloved hands and extended arms. They wore a mask made of rough burlap, loosely fitted over their head and neck, with no slits for their eyes or mouth. 

No one moved. 

The flash quickly dissipated. 

A quiet darkness descended around me. 

For a moment, I felt detached, outside myself, a passive observer to the obscene circumstances, and that’s when it hit me: the baby...it had changed, grown, tripled in size, don’t think I would’ve recognized it if it weren’t for the teeth, and it didn’t look like a girl. 

It looked like a boy.

One hand flailed in the darkness, the other gripped my cheek. Another blow landed on the small of my back. A repulsive *crunch* filled my ears. I buckled. The taste of blood flickered across my tongue as I fell to my knees. My attacker stomped on my calves, pinning me down. They laced something underneath my nostrils and yanked it taut around my scalp. Felt like a ribbon thin enough for air to move through. I took a sharp, ragged inhale. A pungent aroma glided into my skull, intensely floral and sickly sweet.

My muscles relaxed. 

My vision narrowed, growing dimmer, and dimmer, and dimmer. 

I wasn’t asleep, nor was I unconscious.

**“HELP! PLEASE, SOMEONE, HELP ME!”**

“*Heh-lep. Plee-se, som-on, heh-lep me”*

Told myself this wasn’t happening.

Clicking.

Like teeth.

A school-aged boy.

A key.

My excised fingers.
