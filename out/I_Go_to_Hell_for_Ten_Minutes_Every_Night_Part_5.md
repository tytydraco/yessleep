[**\[Part 4\]**](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1xwtdlw/i_go_to_hell_for_ten_minutes_every_night_part_4/)

It’s definitely spreading. I marked the edges of it that first night, and it grew up to her heel and past her knee by the following morning. She said it didn’t hurt, but I don’t believe her. Maybe it was just upsetting her, but something in her eyes looked pained to me.

I told her we’d figure it out. I’d gotten her out of there and I wasn’t going to let it take her. She knew I meant it, or at least I wanted to. Maia has been able to read me cold since day one, and a little over a week in hell couldn’t change that.

She asked me about the old man again, and that’s when Shugart arrived with breakfast. We had gone over all of it with him the night before and he was still sitting with it, trying to decide what he could allow himself to believe. I told them both that I didn’t know. The guy was familiar-looking, but I couldn’t place him.

I called the hospital while Shugart went outside to talk to his captain. They said that Sarah was no longer in critical condition, but that didn’t mean she was completely out of danger. She hadn’t woken up yet, and there was significant internal damage. Basically, we’d have to wait and see. The woman told me to try back the next day, and I told her that I would.

I was checking my email when Shugart came back inside. I could see that he was angry, so I left him alone. If I needed to know about it he’d tell me. I had one new message from 137. It said, “I think it’s time we met. Send me an address and I’ll be there in the morning.”

I sent it to him, and that was the last high spot for the day.

Maia was in and out all afternoon and slept through most of the evening. When she was awake, she didn’t act right. She said weird things that didn’t make sense, like asking Shugart if he brought the flowers when he came in with lunch. He was sweet about it; he told her he put them in water so they wouldn’t wilt. Maia thanked him and fell back asleep.

I slept beside her for a few hours until my alarm woke me. Shugart was sitting out front when I went out. I joined him on the curb where he was smoking. He looked over at me. “Heading out?”

“I’ve gotta,” I said. “Too big a risk. I shouldn’t be long. Will you watch her?”

He nodded and tossed his butt. “Don’t go far. We still don’t know who hit the precinct, but we know they’re interested in you.”

“Yeah, I don’t know. 137 mentioned some people, but—”

Shugart raised a dismissive hand. “Regardless. They know about you, so be careful and hurry back. She needs you.”

I drove back to the same spot as before. It was dumb if we were being watched, but at the same time, anybody following me was going to see where I went anyway. At 1:18 I flipped over into the Choking Hell. It was a jungle of seeking, twisting vines. I had been there many times and I’d seen the shambling, mossy things the tormented became. I didn’t fear the phantom anymore. He was hurt or hopefully dead, and if he came for me I knew how to fight him. So I just sat down and waited for time to pass.

When I got back to the motel, Shugart was still outside. He was leaning beside the door. I asked him if he wanted to take a nap, and he just shook his head.

“Nah, go lie down with her. It might help. She’s had nightmares.”

I was really tired, so I wasn’t about to argue, but I did linger a moment. He was an older guy, and he hadn’t slept much since the night before. Maybe a couple of hours. His eyes were red and a little droopy. I tried to look away when he turned and caught me staring.

“Don’t worry about me, kid. I’ve been through worse than some missed sleep. We’ll swap out in a couple of hours.” He smiled. “Now move, you’re eating into my shift.”

I did as I was told. When I got inside and lay down in the bed, Maia found me immediately, latching on like I was her life raft. Every time I was about to drift off she moved a little, and I heard her nails scratching at the edges of the growing stone.

After a while I finally managed to fall asleep. I found myself once again dreaming of the dark place with the hanging chains. Maia was with me this time. We lay together on the damp ground and shivered as she opened her eyes.

“Where are we, Will?” she asked me.

“Shh, it’s just a dream,” I said. “Go back to sleep.”

I lay back to close my own eyes, but I felt her stir beside me. Her breathing was panicked, and when I looked over at her, she pointed up. I saw them there above us then, damned souls threaded into the links. They shifted and wailed in their agony, causing the dangling chains to rattle and sway. Maia shuddered, and it felt like she was trying to burrow into me.

“Shh…”

I reached down and pinched my leg. It had to be a dream. It couldn’t be—

It was real. I heard the beast roar in the distance and I stood up immediately.

“We’ve gotta run!” I told her.

“I can’t, Will. My leg. I can barely walk.”

Maia was right. There was nothing else for it; I’d have to carry her. I bent down and told her to climb on my back. I could hear the creature charging towards us through the clanking chains, and she had barely wrapped her arms around my neck when I started to run.

I didn’t run like my life depended on it; I ran like hers did. The weight of that stone leg was a burden I couldn’t carry for long, but I somehow did. My wild dash ignored the stings of those iron links and the bites of their barbs as I moved through them. Nothing mattered but the next step, the next ten feet, staying as far away from the horror behind us as we could for as long as we could. I saw a pale monolith ahead of us, a gleaming clearing in the void, and I made for it.

I could hear the thing getting closer. It was gaining, and I couldn’t run any faster until Maia screamed in my ear and I found a little more wind. It pushed us forward, out of the sea of lashing chains to stand in the light. As we passed into its glow, I heard the beast roar from much farther away than I thought. I stopped running and turned around to see an enormous dark shape retreating into the darkness.

As I leaned over, my elbows on my knees, I couldn’t hear anything but my deep panting breaths and my heart beating like it was a machine well past its limits. I didn’t notice the tingling feeling until it was fully upon me, and we flipped back into that terrible motel bed with an audible pop. I rolled off of Maia and she clung to me sobbing while I fought to breathe.

Shugart came in a moment later and asked what was going on, but I wasn’t ready to talk about it yet. I needed a minute and he understood. As I climbed out of bed and grabbed my shoes, I heard him speak softly to Maia, and he sat down to hold her.

“Shh, breathe, Maia. Tell old Shug what happened to you.”

I didn’t go back to sleep until it was nearly light out, and I had been sleeping for a couple of hours when Shugart shook me awake and told me that I had a visitor. I said okay and started to get up, but Maia clutched my arm and trembled against me. I told her she was safe again for the hundredth time and kissed her on the forehead before I left her.

When I went outside, Shugart was talking to a skinny, bearded man who might have been in his forties. It didn’t sound like a friendly conversation, judging by the tone of his voice. The man turned away from him as I walked up and held out his hand for a shake.

“Hey, Will,” he said. He had a deep voice. “I’m Hugh. How’s your girl? The detective said you got her out of there.”

“Well, okay, I guess, all things considered. She’s gonna need some time.”

“That’s an understatement. What was she there? Ten, eleven days? Double that twice and that’s how long it was for her. I can’t imagine what she went through. I wouldn’t want to.”

“She won’t really talk about it,” I said. “There’s so much I don’t understand. Like, why didn’t that man hurt her? I mean, I’m glad he didn’t, but none of it makes sense.”

“It does if you know him.” Hugh frowned. “She’s the bait, man, and dead bait don’t draw.”

“What do you mean, *know him*?” Shugart growled.

“Yeah, I knew it would come up.” Hugh looked at both of us. “There’s no easy way to say it, so I’ll just spit it out like the wad of shit it is. He’s your old neighbor, Phil, the one that… tried to hurt Sarah when you were kids. He’s my father.”

I stared at Hugh as it started to come back to me. I remembered our neighbor, the creepy old man dad didn’t like. They ended up voting him out of the church for something. And Sarah, she had never mentioned anything about it either.

“What did he do to her?” I asked him.

Hugh was looking at me, and it took him a moment to answer. “What… no, nothing like that. Wrong flavor of creep. He’s more of a bastard anyway. Phil’s big on the dark side of things. You know, occult shit. He’s part of a group called the Knotted Ring. They’re obsessed with the, what’s it called? The Perditious Cluster, from the book that asshole wrote.”

“What about the girl, Sarah?” Shugart asked.

“I don’t know all of it, but she’s special somehow. She can flip on her own to somewhere they needed to get to. They take people, a lot of them. They’re using them to make their own realm. It’s a dark place with a demon made out of the souls of the dead. Dreadful shit, it’s all cha—”

“Chains,” I interjected.

“Yeah,” Hugh said as his eyes studied mine. “You’ve seen it? What, did you flip there?”

“In my sleep last night, it took Maia too.” I could feel Shugart’s eyes burning into me and I heard him gritting his teeth.

“Fuck that,” he said. “Dude, you don’t go there if they don’t want you to. Someone made that happen. Believe me, I’ve been there once and I haven’t been able to get back since.”

“How did you go?” I asked him.

“I’m in the Ring,” he said, and he backed up when Shugart reached for his gun. Hugh spoke a little faster then. “Easy, cop. They think I work for them, but I’ve been working against them all this time. I’m the one that called Will about the raid, and I even called in the warning about the attack on your precinct. It’s not my fault they didn’t listen.”

“How did you know about the raid?” Shugart said. He wasn’t pointing the gun at Hugh yet, but I could tell he wanted to.

“Fuck! Look, man, I come in peace. The raid… the Ring has somebody on the inside, I think his name is Melody. Uh… Sergeant Melody?”

Shugart glanced at me for a second before locking back onto Hugh. “Bullshit. You’re trying to tell me one of our own… a cop, works with you scum? Jesus, Will, do you even know who you are talking to here? This guy might have been the one behind it all, behind Maia—”

“I know him, Shugart,” I said. “He’s been helping me since I was fifteen.”

“Helping?” he laughed bitterly. “Grooming you, maybe. Maybe ol’ Hugh here thought he could get in good with his precious ring if he turned you over to them down the line. Is that what it is, daddy’s boy? You getting ready to betray this kid?”

The bullet that was meant for Shugart’s head sailed past him and into the wall when he lunged forward to push Hugh back from me. A lot of things happened all at once after that. Shugart pivoted and steered me to the door just as another shot struck Hugh low on the side. When the detective turned to return fire, a bullet clipped his ribs, but his vest caught it. Hugh tackled Shugart, shoving him behind the car before the next shot got him.

I saw it all from the doorway, and I was too shocked to move until Shugart turned and growled at me to get the fuck inside. Hugh had drawn his own gun and he and the detective were coordinating their shots to conserve ammunition. I was about to close the door when another bullet brought Hugh to the ground. A moment later Shugart crawled through it and slammed it shut behind him.

“He went down,” he said quietly to no one in particular. “I couldn’t get to him.”

Shugart pointed to the far side of the bed, and I nodded. I grabbed Maia and pulled her down with me to lie beside it. He crouched below the window and risked a glance to peek under the curtain.

“Where you at, boy?”

The phone on the desk started to ring and he spun around so fast I thought he was going to shoot it. Instead, he crawled over to it and snatched it down by its cord.

“If you’re a telemarketer you better call for backup,” he cackled. He listened for a few seconds and said, “No, fuck you, you talk to me.”

A few minutes and several colorful catchphrases later, Shugart pressed the speaking end of the old rotary phone to his chest and turned to me. His face was flushed, and there was a wildness in his eyes that I would never have imagined there. When he spoke to me it was in a whisper.

“This asshole wants to talk to you, Will. He says you know him, that you read his book?”

I crawled over and Maia came with me. Shugart handed me the phone, and he reached over to brush the hair out of her eyes. He looked over at me with steel in his gaze. “No deals.”

I nodded at him as I put it up to my ear. “Hello?”

“Will, remarkable as always. I see you have surrounded yourself with useful allies.”

“Is this you, DL?” I asked. “Did you do this?”

“I’m afraid so, old chum,” he said without an ounce of regret. “I came for a favor, something only you can do. And you’re going to do it, or I’m going to tell my men outside the hospital that you no longer need Sarah in your life.”

“Why are you doing this?”

“I’m just righting a wrong and saving my friend,” he said. “Shouldn’t you do the same?”

“What do you want me to do?”

I listened to him as he explained what he wanted me to do, and I didn’t say anything until he was finished. Shugart was staring at me with pleading eyes. He didn’t want a negotiation and I didn’t blame him. I was learning to hate these people, and I was surprised that despite my Christian upbringing it was really easy to do. When DL finished talking I said four words and hung up the phone.

“I’ll do it tonight.”

Shugart was livid when I told him what they wanted me to do, but when I told him what DL was threatening me with, his tone softened immediately. I went outside by myself to check on Hugh. Shugart didn’t like it, but I told him that if they needed me, they couldn’t shoot me, and that settled it. Luckily, Hugh was still breathing and was only feigning unconsciousness. I helped him get inside with us, and I kept my body between him and the shooters just to be safe.

Hugh’s wounds weren’t quite as bad as I would have thought. The side wound was just a graze and the other one tore through the outside of his upper arm. He and Shugart worked on both of them, cleaning and packing them with fair skill. They said that Hugh would need to wear a sling for a while, but he should be fine as long as he kept them clean.

After that we waited. Since the Ring knew that Hugh was with us he couldn’t really leave. Shugart talked about relocating. He said that there was nothing stopping them from coming back and finishing the job. It sounded like a pretty good idea to me, but I worried that they would get in the way of what we had to do.

In the end, we decided to stay put. If they were still watching, they would just follow us, and it wouldn’t change anything. I had made a deal with them and we would just have to see how it played out. Shugart had smoothed things out with the motel owner after the shooting happened, so the guy was more than happy to rent Hugh the room right next to us.

I spent the last two hours alone with Maia. She had become even quieter after the attack, and sometimes when she spoke to one of us she was confused about who we are. We played along and tried to keep her calm. I told her I loved her and that she was safe, and before I left her I told her it wouldn’t always be this way.

Shugart offered to come with. He didn’t like what I was doing, but he hated for me to do it alone. I told him I needed him to stay with Maia, to keep her safe, and he accepted it. A minute later Hugh came out of his room carrying a bag. He said he was going to take me to be there when I came out, just in case.

I drove us out there since he was down an arm. With minutes to go, I asked him if he wanted to join me. He said no immediately, and that he hoped to be a surprise if things turned treacherous. But before I got out, he gave me some advice, something I hadn’t thought of. I’m happy to say I took it.

At 1:18 I cleared my mind and focused on the marble isle that drifts within a void. I don’t know how I knew it would work that time, but I somehow did.  Maybe it was my connection to Phil.  Maybe that’s what it was all along. I recalled every feature of his face, and when I appeared before him I knew that he’d been waiting for me.

“Come to bargain?” he said, and it incited a nasty cough that obviously hurt a lot. Phil was barely clothed. Many of the rags I saw before had been stripped away to be used as bandages. It didn’t look like they helped much. From the pallor of his skin, I thought I’d only beat the reaper by hours.

“No, DL sent me. He thinks I should free you.”

“Well, he always was a pragmatic sort. What’s he giving you in return?”

“That’s my business. I’m here to ask you what you’ll give me.”

He laughed before he winced in pain again. “I’d play your game, because I don’t think I have much time left. What do you want?”

“I want to cure Maia.”

“And you’ll take me back if I tell you how? That’s an easy deal. I bear that girl no harsh thoughts, or you for that matter.”

“You didn’t act like it. I nearly had to kill you to get her back.”

“The actions of a desperate man. Here, I’ll prove it by giving it for free. That leaves me at your mercy. So, here it is. She’ll need to touch the dark sea to remove the curse. You have to lower her into it. Can you do that? Could she stand to return?”

When it was time he took my hand and we flipped back exactly on the nineteen minute mark. As it happened, I felt a strange feeling at the base of my skull. It didn’t really hurt, it was more like something wet and slimy slipped down my spine. It made me sick, and I threw up just as soon as I landed in front of the car.

“Easy,” Phil said. “Happens to the best and worst of us.”

“Hands off him,” said Hugh. He was leaning against the car door with a gun held up in his good hand. “You good, Will?”

“Fine,” I said, still gagging.

“He really is. Fine young man, our Will.”

“Just shut up and get in.” Hugh gestured with the gun.

We drove back in mostly silence. Phil tried to talk to his son and Hugh shot him down. When we got back to the motel there was a long black car waiting for us there. Hugh climbed out of the car, doing his best to keep the gun visibly on Phil the entire time. I got out too.

The passenger-side window opened and DL smiled out at me. He looked over at Phil and winked, and he frowned when he saw Hugh and shook his head.

“Hop in, Phil,” he said. “We’ve lots to do. As for the rest of you, the truce stands for the night. If you’re still here tomorrow… Well, let’s just say we might not be quite as friendly.”

“And Sarah?” I asked him as Phil hobbled over.

“A deal’s a deal. She’s fine.”

As Phil got in the car, he turned and looked at me. “Pleasant dreams, Will.”

And then they just left. We haven’t been attacked and Sarah seems fine. She’s awake actually, and she wants to see me tomorrow. I asked Hugh about it, and he said there has to be a reason, because the Ring doesn’t like loose ends. Shugart said we should call in the FBI. Maybe it’s not a bad idea, but I told him we should sleep on it.

And that’s what we did, because we were long overdue. I spent the night snuggled up to Maia and there were no dreams.

That’s good enough for now.
