I’m writing this as a warning to everyone. If you ever see a sign for a town called Mercy, never stop there.

That was a mistake I made. Well, not by choice, my car broke down.

I had just gotten a new job a couple of states away, and I was making my way through the southern half of the states. I was driving on this narrow, backcountry road, choked on both sides by the endless wetlands. I had been driving about four hours that day when my car’s engine started to knock and sputter. I pulled over on what little shoulder there was and lifted the hood.

I don’t know what I expected to find when I did it. I’m no mechanic, and even if I was, it wasn’t like I was going to pull a bunch of spare parts out of my trunk. After about thirty minutes of staring at it and trying to get some cell signal, I finally saw a truck coming down the road. I frantically waved my arms, trying to flag them down. When he pulled up, a pot-bellied man in overalls stepped out.

“You need some help, fella?” he said, spitting a mouthful of tobacco onto the grass.

“Yeah, my car broke down and I can’t get any signal.”

“Well hell son, must be your lucky day,” he replied, gesturing to his truck. “I can give you a tow back to town. It’s just down the road aways.”

I told him thank you and tried to help however I could as he hooked my car to the back of the tow truck. I climbed into the cab, and we started towards town.

“The name’s Jon,” he said as he put in a fresh lip of chew.

“I’m Travis. Nice to meet you,” I replied.

“Likewise.”

“Where are we heading anyways?”

“Mercy.”

“I haven’t ever heard of Mercy before.”

He just laughed. “Most people haven’t. We like to keep it that way, the best we can. It ain’t much, but it’s home.”

After about five minutes on the main road, we turned onto a small single-lane side street that snaked further into the wetlands. The trees were tall and thick, their canopy blotting out the sun as we drove. Fifteen minutes later, a single sign broke the otherwise empty tree line.

Welcome to Mercy.

Jon wasn't kidding about it being small, but he definitely undersold it.

The town was like something from a postcard. Picturesque houses with picket fences dotted the streets. Older but well-maintained brick storefronts ran along the main strip, complete with hand-painted signs. In the center of town stood a beautiful white church with "Mercy Chapel" in bold, calligraphic font. Soon, we arrived at Jon’s garage and moved the car into the shop.

“Gonna take a day to figure out what your problem is. Then another one or two after that to get your repair done. Nothing to worry about though, I’ll get you fixed up,” he said.

“So I’m stuck here for a few days?”

“Yep. Rome weren’t built in a day and all that. There’s a motel in town though. I’m sure they can put you up there in the meantime.”

With no other choice, I grabbed my luggage and started to walk down the street towards the motel. I passed a small diner on my way that looked like it was straight out of the 1950s. It even had a sign for malted milkshakes. I finally arrived at the motel and rang the bell. Then a short balding man shuffled out of the back office. The man fixed his glasses and gave me a once-over.

“Well, hello there. How can I help you?”

“I need a room for a few nights. I’m having some car trouble.”

“Jon working on it?”

“Yeah.”

“Well, then you’re in good hands. He ain’t covered in grease for the fun of it. Now then, let’s see here,” the man said as he pulled out a form. I gave him my name, phone number, and driver’s license information. As he started to copy everything down, he looked up and asked,

“What brings you out this way?”

“I’m moving for a new job.”

“Sounds interesting. Is your family moving with you?” he asked.

“Oh no. I’m not married,” I replied.

The man handed back my license and a room key. “I’m Walter. If you need anything, the office number’s on the back of your key. Hope you enjoy your stay with us.”

The man smiled and shuffled back to his office as I turned and walked to my room. The room wasn’t much but appeared clean. It had a double bed, a desk adorned with a small lamp, and a small refrigerator in it. I threw my luggage into the corner and plopped myself on the bed. I lay in bed for a while before getting up to shower and change. After my shower, I headed out to the diner.

The second I walked in, I was immediately greeted by the blonde waitress behind the counter.

“Hey hun! Sit wherever you like.”

I picked a booth by the window and sat down. She then walked over and dropped a menu in front of me. I sat there for a few minutes and looked it over and that’s when something caught my eye. A big banner hung across the street that said “Mercy Annual Founder’s Festival” in big letters. The waitress then came back.

“Do you know what you would like hun? You look like you haven’t had anything but gas-station food all day,” she said with a chuckle.

“I haven’t,” I replied. “I think I’ll need another minute.”

Before she walked away, I asked her when the festival was supposed to be held.

“Oh that’s in a couple of days. You thinking about going?”

“I might. Looks like I’m going to be here for a few days anyway.”

“Oh, we heard.”

“Wait, what?” I asked. “How did you hear that?”

“Jon came through earlier. Said we had a visitor. We don’t get many of those here in Mercy.”

For a moment, Jon’s words replayed in my head. Mercy really must be tiny.

“So when do you start?” she asked.

“Two weeks. I wanted to get there and get settled before immediately getting into work.”

“That’s smart. Do you still need to find a place there or did someone help you find one already?”

“No, no. I still have to find one. I don’t know anyone there so I’ll probably be in a hotel for a few days.”

“Well you’ll have plenty of time to check out our festival then. I hope you show up.” She smiled, then turned and went back to the counter.

My food arrived and I sat there eating while I watched through the window as people were busy preparing for the festival. I could see a large stage that was almost finished, and decorations being hung from streetlights. People carried tables from the church while a couple of men stretched another banner across the stage. For a diner in the middle of nowhere, the food was excellent. The waitress came back over to check on me and even gave me a milkshake on the house. Said it was for “Mercy’s newest guest.”

After finishing my food, I walked back to my room, ready to settle in for the night. I checked my phone which still had no service, then turned on the TV. Eventually I drifted off to reruns of black and white TV shows. I was woken a bit later by the sound of a knock at my door.

“Who could that be?” I wondered as I walked to the door. When I opened it, there was no one there. When I looked down, there was a flyer for the festival, just lying on the ground. I picked it up and started to look it over when I noticed the hand-written message across the top.

Welcome to Mercy, Travis.

I woke up the next morning around 9 am. I decided I wanted to go check on my car, then get breakfast.

It was clear to me on the walk that the rest of the town had been busy for a while before I got up. Kids were playing in the grass near the church. Neighbors were laughing across picket fences. Every single storefront had some type of display for the festival. Everyone was happy and smiling. They all waved when they saw me, big smiles on their faces. Honestly, the whole town seemed excited. I finally reached the garage, its roll up door already open.

“Morning, Travis,” Jon said, bent over and halfway submerged in my engine compartment. He started to explain what was going on when a young man walked into the shop. He paused when he saw me standing there.

“Travis, this is my boy, Caleb. Caleb, this is the fella I was telling you about.”

He stood there for a moment before letting out a quiet, “Nice to meet you, Travis.”

Jon then continued explaining what all he needed to fix my car. He told me the part would take a couple of days to arrive and that he would get me taken care of as soon as possible. Caleb just turned and went back outside. I thanked him for the update and headed back to the diner.

The diner was just as busy as the day prior. I wasn’t sure if there was any other restaurant to eat at, but with the size of the town, it seemed unlikely. I was about halfway through my meal when I was startled by a pat on the back.

“Travis!”

I turned around and found a towering, older man standing behind me, dressed neatly in a button up shirt and black slacks. A clerical collar poked out above his shirt.

“Sorry, didn’t mean to startle you, son. Reverend Jones. Mercy Chapel,” he said, hand outstretched.

I reached out to shake the reverend’s hand. His grip was like putting my hand in a vice.

“Mind if I sit down?” He slid into the seat across from me before I could answer. The waitress came by and dropped a coffee off in front of him.

“How are you liking our town?”

“Umm, it’s great. Everyone’s been very nice,” I said, trying to swallow the bite of food I had in my mouth.

“Good! Good. You know hospitality is a big deal to us, here in Mercy. You don’t get many chances for first impressions.”

I sat there quietly as he continued.

“I really hope you’re going to make it to the festival. It’s a big deal around here. Biggest day in town every year.”

“Yeah, looks like I’ll make it. My car isn’t supposed to be ready for a couple of days.”

The reverend just smiled, “Well then maybe there’s a reason Jon found you when he did.”

“What’s this festival for anyways?” I asked.

“It’s a celebration for the town’s history. My great great granddaddy founded this town many years ago. See he believed that the world was becoming corrupt, so he and some others founded our little slice of heaven here. Free from the corruption, violence, sinning of the outside world. Somewhere we could protect faith, family, community and our values.”

“You’ve lived here your whole life?”

“Yes sir. The Jones family has been the reverends of every generation since Mercy was founded. Bit of family tradition, I guess.”

“It is a really nice town,” I said.

“I think we’ve done well for ourselves. The town is clean and her people are happy. We protect our own out here.”

Before I could say anything else, the reverend got up from his seat. “I best be going now. I got a lot of things to oversee for tomorrow. It was a treat to talk to you, Travis. Enjoy your time here in Mercy.” And without another word, he walked out of the diner.

The rest of the day was pretty uneventful. I wandered around town and eventually made my way back to my room. The night went on as I continued watching TV until something caught my eye. The light outside my window was flickering. At one point it went out for a few seconds and when it came back on, I thought I saw a silhouette cast against the window. Shocked, I got up to look out the window and see if my eyes were playing tricks on me. 

When I looked, I saw the dark figure of a man, staring at my room.
