The first thing they teach you in the hidden sect is that gravity isn't a force.

It's a lock.

Not on your body—on your perception.

Every cathedral, monastery, ruined chapel and forgotten shrine was built over the same fracture in reality. Religion was only ever camouflage. The paintings were never acts of worship. They were restraints.

The secret isn't hidden in the saints, the halos, or the scripture.

It's hidden in the cracks.

Not cracks caused by age.

Cracks that grow.

Each one is part of a pattern no human hand could paint. Follow them with your eyes in the correct order, and for exactly one heartbeat the lock opens. Gravity doesn't disappear—it forgets you ever belonged to it.

That's when you see the place pressing against our world.

It has no sky.

No ground.

No distance.

Only people standing in impossible directions, staring back through the opening as though they've been waiting longer than history itself.

The sect insists you must blink before they do.

No one has ever explained why.

I didn't.

I proved it to Michael.

He laughed the first time.

He stopped laughing when the painting moved before either of us had.

Not the figures.

The cracks.

They rearranged themselves while we watched, like veins growing beneath living skin.

Michael whispered that someone behind the canvas had just copied his breathing.

Then he grabbed my arm hard enough to bruise it and begged me—not to run—but to stop looking.

He said the final symbol wasn't appearing.

It was remembering us.

I wish I'd listened.

The police call him a missing person.

There are posters.

Search parties.

Phone records.

CCTV.

None of it matters.

Michael didn't disappear.

He fell sideways.

Sometimes I hear him where space folds incorrectly.

Behind mirrors.

Under staircases.

Inside walls that sound hollow only after midnight.

His voice always arrives upside down, every sentence struggling as though language itself has become too heavy to lift.

He never asks for help.

He only asks what day it is.

The dates never make sense to him.

Yesterday he insisted it was sometime before fire existed.

The sect warned me never to answer.

They said gravity remembers every conversation.

I thought they were speaking metaphorically.

They weren't.

Every time I answer, something in my house becomes heavier.

Doors take more effort to open.

My reflection lags behind by half a second.

Dust settles upward.

Rooms are slightly different sizes each morning.

There are now thirteen steps leading to my bedroom.

There used to be twelve.

No one else notices.

The paintings have changed too.

Not dramatically.

Just enough.

The saints no longer look toward heaven.

They look directly at me.

Not with anger.

Recognition.

Their expressions aren't painted anymore.

They react.

Sometimes they seem relieved when I enter the room.

Sometimes terrified.

Last week one of them was covering the eyes of another.

Yesterday they were all pointing.

Not at me.

Behind me.

I refused to turn around.

This morning I woke with dried paint beneath my fingernails.

My bed was surrounded by tiny flakes of centuries-old pigment.

Every painting in the house had fresh cracks that hadn't existed the night before.

The pattern was almost complete.

Almost.

There was also another name written across my ceiling.

Not in English.

Not in Latin.

Not in any language I know.

Yet I understood it instantly.

It wasn't someone's name.

It was mine.

Not the one I was born with.

The one they call me there.

I have begun finding photographs of places I've never visited.

I'm standing in every one of them.

Facing away from the camera.

In each photograph there's another version of me standing closer.

Neither of us casts a shadow.

The latest picture arrived this morning.

I don't remember taking it.

In the background, Michael is smiling.

He's pointing at something just outside the frame.

I've made the mistake of looking.

There wasn't anything there when I first checked.

There is now.

If you ever enter an old cathedral and notice a figure in a religious painting looking directly into your eyes instead of beyond you, leave immediately.

Do not look back.

Do not count the cracks.

Do not finish the pattern.

And if, as you're leaving, someone quietly says your name from inside the painting...

...don't make the mistake of answering.

Because the voice won't be asking whether you want to come inside.

It will be asking why you ever believed you had already left.
