When I came rolling back down the road to the Red Manor, I could already see two people waiting on the porch. The glow of a cigarette cherry told me one was Kait, and I could tell by the nervous pacing that the other had to be Lacey. I cursed under my breath, thinking that mild bad news about something while I was gone might be a preferable reason for their loitering compared to the ass-chewing I was about to get.

“Why did you need us to call the sheriff?” Kait called plainly before my foot even stepped out of the truck. Spoken with the venom of a mother trying to get their kid to confess to breaking her vase.

“Are you okay?” Lacey tacked on, “Was there some kind of trouble?”

Pacing closer and seeing their stern faces beneath the dull glow of the porch light, I didn’t want to be honest, but with Kait already pissed, I decided it was the best option, “I had an idea about the house on my way out of town and I needed to check the police archives. I needed a distraction.”

Lacey didn’t exactly look like she knew how she wanted to react, and it wasn’t until I saw her judgmental surprise that it dawned on me how risky of a plan it had been. I really hadn’t been giving ‘getting arrested’ as much weight as it deserved, but then again, I suppose the edge might have been dulled after facing far greater risks.

Kait, on the other hand, knew exactly how she wanted to react, “You were *supposed* to be taking a break,” she growled.

“I was,” I shot back, holding up my bag of groceries, “It only took a few moments. I stopped by Alma’s for a cup of coffee just before.”

“Yeah? And how’d the stress of almost getting arrested make that coffee stay down?”

I threw my head back and sighed. I still felt bad about our blowout earlier, but I wasn’t exactly in the mood to continue it, “Look, Kaitlyn, I’m sorry, but we need answers.”

“It wasn’t your turn to ‘look for answers’, Jess. Your body is going to give out if—” Whatever thought Kait was about to finish died on her tongue. I’d stepped up onto the porch, and in the light, she had begun inspecting my face. I was confused what the problem was until her own softened and she stepped forward.

With one hand, she plucked the cigarette from her lips, and with the other, she reached up and brushed her fingers across my cheek. I didn’t think that the mark from my mother's slap would have even still been there, but the way pain tingled across my skin told me that it was very much still raw.

“Who did that?” The girl asked softly. Lacey stepped forward and took the bags from my hands before I could protest.

I plucked the cigarette from Kait’s hands with my fingers, hoping stuffing it between my lips would give me an excuse to give a short answer, “My mom said ‘hi’.”

I could almost feel the heat steam off Kait’s skin; shame for coming in so intense. She made up for it by letting her fingers cup fully over my cheek, taking me in her palm and pressing softly on the bruise. For all the time it lingered there, the tingling pain was turned to bittersweet, mending kisses.

“What was the idea you had?” Lacey asked after Kait pulled back and retrieved her cigarette.

The coffee that Kait just mentioned gave a lurch as I recalled the stacks of missing posters on the station wall. I swallowed to keep it down and gave them a grave headshake, “Let’s get everyone together. It’s a rough one.”

Lacey clearly wanted to know now, but she nodded, “That would be best. We’ve got something for you too.”

“What is it?”

Her and Kait swapped stares, “We found the journal you were talking about.”

A cold wind swept up the cliffside, and the rickety manor shuddered.

Carly and Bryce suddenly appeared behind the screen of the porch door, pressing it open to come out and help.

“Dude, what the hell happened to you out there? I had to do my best old man impression for like, twenty minutes on the phone with the Sheriff just so he wouldn’t think it was a prank call.”

The thought brought some mild whimsy back into my quickly dying heart, “Thanks, man, I appreciated it; you worked wonders. Help me get the lumber unloaded, and I’ll tell you?”

“No, *we’ll* get the lumber,” Kait told me, back in commanding parent mode. She snatched her cig from me and pressed it back into her lips, “*You* take another fifteen to make up for your clever little recon mission.”

I wasn’t about to push my luck with her after our tender moment of reconciliation, “Alright, alright—yes ma’am.”

Her eyes fixed me for a moment as she tried to hide behind her cigarette, but it wasn’t enough to conceal the small smirk that turned at her lips. That was enough to bring one to mine too as I turned and entered the house.

It didn’t stay there long. Ahead of me, Lacey had already begun bringing in the groceries, leaving the two of us alone. My police department heist had distracted my mind from it on the way back up here, but I’d nearly forgotten I had another piece of information to share. One that was only for her.

My steps were timid and slow as I trailed her down the hall towards the kitchen, trying to buy myself enough time to figure out how to tell her about my talk with Anna. By now, each of our sanities were as paper thin as the brittle flakes of paint chipping from the Manor’s wall, and telling Lacey that her lover was practically going insane in her absence might just push her to snap.

None of that was even factoring in what the anger would do to her when she found out what I’d done to her brother’s name…

I had to weigh the options, knowing we had another attack from the door potentially drawing near. Give her hope that her girlfriend was waiting faithfully for her return, or crush her spirits with the very same notion and make her more vulnerable?

Ultimately, I decided to hold my tongue for now until we had shared all of our information. I’d be able to get a better idea of her mood once we were absorbed by the full gravity, and if any new horrifying truths came to light, that might be enough to break us anyway…

I was dying to know what secrets were held in the leather-bound journal, and I’m certain they wanted to know what tidbit I’d discovered while I was out, but first, we needed to get the lumber and scrap prepared. I’d already been away two hours, which meant the door was in the clear to belch out another denizen of the underworld.

When we had it all unpacked and moved by the door, Bryce and Kait set to work rigging a trap and listening over their shoulders while Lacey and Carley stood near me. I picked up the journal and laid it in my lap, eyeing them carefully.

“Did you already read it?”

Nobody's eyes would meet mine as Carly nodded grimly, “Yeah. We found it in a box hidden beneath the head lady’s bed.”

“Is it good news or bad news?”

That earned me her eyes in the form of a humorless stare, “I’m sure you can guess. What about you? What did you figure out?”

“Bad news,” I returned, bracing my emotions as I eyed the red door, “Honestly, I’m not even sure it’s ‘news’; I think we had already figured, but… This place—the kids I’ve seen so far? I checked the missing person’s database for them and guys; we haven’t even scratched the surface. There’s a board on the wall by the front desk *filled* with missing children posters that they don’t even know are there. Entries from decades ago that never got opened again after they were made.”

Kait and Bryce paused in their ropework to stare over at me, and Carly and Lacey shared a glance before the latter swallowed and dared to ask, “How many were there?”

The staid air of their silent exchange made me afraid to answer, but I did so anyway, “A lot. So many that they had to be taking multiple children a year, which means if nobody noticed, the door was doing its job before it even existed.”

There was another round of looks to one another, and a soft, shuddering, “Oh God” from Lacey under her breath.

This time, my curiosity of what they knew that I didn’t was too much, and I cautioned to ask, “What did you guys find in this thing?”

Everyone just stared, seeming to wonder if telling me would be better than reading. Eventually, I made the decision for myself and popped it open.

What lay within was a lot of incoherent nonsense, at least by my understanding. Drawings and notes and journal entries speaking of spirits and words that my brain didn’t comprehend. When you see a spell book or tome in a movie, the idea always seems so fantastical and absurd: that there could exist instruction manual that allows you to cast magic and brew potions. It’s almost fun imagining the power you could have with it—all the spells that could aid your life or brews that could enhance your abilities.

Holding a real-life, honest-to-God spell book in my hands, though? There was no childlike whimsy any longer. The scrawling and etches on the pages weren’t fantastical, they were surreal to see, knowing that they weren’t just made-up symbols and that the abilities that they held were by no means ‘fun’. It felt more like I was holding a loaded gun in my hand; a harmless pile of metal and mechanisms on its own, made dangerous only when a human picked it up.

A month ago, I would have never thought I could believe such dark magic existed. Now I just wished it had stayed that way.

“Is… it in here?” I asked slowly, “The ritual to seal the door?”

My eyes darted up hopefully to Carly and Lacey, but I quickly remembered they said this was ‘bad news’, and their solemn head shakes confirmed this.

“To seal it, no,” Carly told me, leaning forward and helping me turn the pages. She stopped a few weathered sheets before the end, “But to open it in the first place? Yeah. She wrote all of that down.”

I could feel the blood thrumming through my entire body as I let my eyes fall back to the pages. What I found there beyond the first page were mostly journal entries, a few spots in the walls of words carved out to make way for sketches and diagrams. On that first page was a ritual, its components and ingredients listed out in detailed points beneath a large, important, calligraphy scrawled title.

*‘Loom of the Fates’.*

My palms felt cold when I skimmed over to where the entries started, and I noticed a drawing there that accompanied it: the distinct shape of the pentagram made of threads and candles that Adeline had been sitting in during her ritual in the library.

The first words of her diary made me feel even more sick:

*It worked! By the stars, it actually works! After months of preparation, I was finally able to conduct a pure, unfettered run of the ritual. While I did not have the skill or practice to yet see beyond the veil as many sisters of the past have claimed the spell would allow, its potency at a base level must be unmatched. Instead, I heard beyond it.*

*I was beginning to believe that the ritual we’d been given was flawed or completely untrue. After all, an incantation of such power seemed almost too good to believe. My first attempts of the process only yielded a mild feeling of connectivity, but without a sensory connection to confirm that I was actually entangling with the threads, there was truly no way to know for certain.*

*But then, just now, a voice spoke out behind me, one that was not of a spirit or otherworldly force. I could sense it; this was a fragment of another time—past or future I am uncertain, but they were undoubtedly a product of the chords all the same. The act of peering through the threads—of conditioning a mortal shell to be in tune with them; it is possible after all.*

*I am admittedly unsure of where to go from here, or how to deepen my connection to the greater loom even further. The scraps that the ritual consists of are nearly as old as the ancient world, and were passed down for many centuries through only verbal accounts; hushed whispers in secret halls between the brotherhoods of conjurers and diviners. To even know for certain if its capabilities are what is actually recorded is a near impossibility.*

*If they are, however, then I must find a way. My sisters and I have been searching for potential such as this for countless moons, and now that I have one of these cosmic threads tied around my finger, I shall pull on it until I find my way forward.*

My head spun as the information I’d just read sloshed around within it, refusing to permeate into my brain like oil and water. Some sort of eldritch secret that my mortal mind was rejecting. There was one aspect of it that I could digest, however; a fact that stung and took the breath from my lungs.

“That ritual she was doing…” I said aloud, “If she hadn’t heard me…”

“Jessie,” Kait stopped me before I could even finish. She must have been anticipating the reaction, “Whatever this was, this was big for them. There’s no chance they would have stopped at that one ritual—they would have done it again, and found the connection they were looking for eventually.”

“I was the only one in there, though,” I shook my head, not buying it, “If I hadn’t even been there, she never would have heard me, and she wouldn’t have wanted to try again.”

“What about your friend?” Bryce asked, “That little girl, she’s wandering around in there too.”

“Yeah, or any of the other dozens of memories that you’re able to see,” Carly pointed out, “We don’t know *what* they were looking for that first time, Jessie, so you’re not allowed to jump to conclusions that this was because of you.”

I still didn’t buy it, but her wording of ‘not allowed’ and the stern, relentless glares of my friends told me that my argument was done. Even so, the thought still made me dizzy as I reread the words. Ink that had been scrawled decades ago referring to a moment that only happened earlier today.

What were these threads that she and her sisters were trying to access? I mean, I knew what they *did* by now, but what was their *nature?* She’d said whatever they were doing was as old as the ancient world, and though that still didn’t answer my question, it did mean that they weren’t a creation of Adeline herself. It also brought to mind an idea.

“The labyrinth…” I pondered aloud, looking to Kait and Lacey, “We were theorizing that this was somehow where that myth came from, but maybe it wasn’t the only one. This spell is called the ‘loom of the fates’, and those three famously dealt in threads of life.”

Kait nodded in agreement, “I noticed that too. Greek mythology isn’t the only culture to represent lives as threads either—it’s mentioned all over in other cultures we read about.”

“Well, our threads are barely ‘representing’ life,” Carly said, “The way you describe seeing them, Jess, it sounds like these things straight up *are* people’s lives, or at least remnants of them.”

“So we’re dealing with the Fates then?” Bryce asked with an incredulous chuckle, the idea seeming absurd, “That’s what these three ladies were?”

“At least wannabes,” Lacey guessed, “Sounds like they were witches before they even knew about this spell; this was just the newest temptation.”

“What makes it so valuable, though?” I asked, “Why were they trying to influence these things? Was it just some convoluted way for them to see the future? Was all of this shit just for that?”

The group went hushed again, and Lacey nodded to the journal, “You should probably just keep reading. It might answer more questions…”

I was afraid of what I might find should I continue to do so, but obeyed anyway.

*The strides I made last night were monumental.*

*I conducted the ritual again, but this time, I set up the séance in the basement. I had Natellie and Margret help me clear everything out so that there were no disrupting auras, and I sealed myself away to meditate for an entire day in order to completely purge my mind.*

*My thinking was that if the threads binding us to this earth have a better connection anywhere, it would be the part of our home that creates the perfect crossroads. The meeting of man’s creation and the dust we were born from—a place far less muddled with sentimentality and elevation like the library.*

*My theory turned out to be brilliant.*

*I felt the threads again, but this time, their tautness was higher; their contents so much easier to read. There were voices that faded in and out of my mind, and though I tried to cling to them, my hold was not strong enough to their plane, and they quickly slipped out of reach.*

*I began growing frustrated by this—all the extra steps taken possibly in vain, but then, salvation came. I heard a new voice call out, but this one was not like the others. It was not vague nor abstract; it was loud and spoke directly to me. While its tongue was not one I recognized, its intention struck at my being in ways I could fathom no matter what language I spoke.*

*It knew what we sought. What we had been searching for all these years. It showed me that it was not a part of the threads—it was what kept them—something akin to time itself; part of the very wool they were spun from.*

*Since the dawn of man, it has loomed over the chords, weaving indifferently through them as a being above our plane, the way we move over dirt without regarding its nature. Even so, when enough earth has been collected in one place in such a specific way to form a mountain or vista, we humans are occasionally inclined to call it a ‘beautiful sight’.*

*The being told me that a select few of us manage to be like this to it. Something fascinating to their dull, enlightened existence. I was one such creature, leaving them impressed that such a lowly animal could climb to their equal height. Because of this, they shone their favor upon me.*

*I have been given a gift; a tiny scrap, I’m sure, to the likes of them, but an invaluable treasure in the presence of us mortals. They gave me insight into what it is my sisters and I seek. The answers that we’ve sought ever since the tales of the Fates were sewn in our heads all those centuries ago.*

*Our dreams are achievable. There is a way to strum and weave the threads of creation in the fashion that we’d hoped, but we had been attempting to do so entirely incorrectly all this time. Only with this cosmic clarity could we have ever hoped to achieve such immense headway, and now that we have it, the only thing that can cease our progress is our own apprehension.*

*I have never backed out of an endeavor yet, and even with how barbaric the path forward may be, it shall not stop me. For what seems wicked and unfathomable to our human eyes is but an inconsequential yawn to those above us.*

*I’m sure my sisters and I will look back with the same indifference once we reach their heights.*

My eyes traced off the final period, then did a lap around the pages, looking for any traces of what this abhorrent ritual Adeline discovered might be. Whatever it was, though, it seemed that this otherworldly secret must have been something the witch kept close to her chest. The only steps and diagrams listed seemed to be for the base ritual that I had already watched her perform.

That meant even with all the secrets the woman wrote, there still was no answer to what truly brought about the red door. Maybe thinking of it ‘being brought about’ wasn’t quite right, though. Adeline said something called out to her—gave her the knowledge to reach whatever plane she was trying to climb to.

The essence behind the red door must have always been there somewhere; they just carved out the hole.

There was a lot I could have discussed with my friends after that entry, but by now, the words on the page had me in a morbid trance, and my swirling head needed its curiosity instantly satiated about what vile deeds Adeline was referring to. I turned the page and read on.

*It has been many months now, but the knowledge our patron beyond the wall imparted on us proved not to be a farce. We achieved a connection with the threads unlike the bond made before, but this time, what caused us to falter was my own negligence.*

*The sacrifice I chose was inadequate.*

My fingertips went numb, and my eyes froze on that last word like pressing on the brakes just before a cliff. I was too scared to let myself fall over…

*The sacrifice I chose was inadequate,* I read again once I was ready. *We had chosen a man from town; a drunkard vagrant whom all in the area cared very little for. Anyone who even bothered to notice he was missing most likely assumed he either wandered too far out into the woods and died, or hobbled off to a new town.*

*I should have known by the quality of the man that his blood would be impure and his thread too filthy a thing to produce any real results. Still there was not yet a guarantee that I could trust what the voice had told me, and to waste a perfectly virginal soul on an endeavor that may not yield results would be a shameful pity.*

*What was returned to us for our half-hearted efforts was exactly what we gave, but mistakes are what we learn and adapt from, and as I said, the experiment served to at least confirm the hypothesis. Threads can be taken and spun into something new under the right conditions.*

*Again, it was the quality that caused the spell to fizzle. For as powerful as the chords may be, they are delicate, fickle things, not meant to be roughly yanked around by our barbaric, mortal hands.*

*A thread as ragged as our first volunteer could never have worked in the state we sacrificed him in, but it has led me to wonder if perhaps rehabilitation on a person could correct this issue. If threads can be worn down by the passage of time and the strain that is put upon them by the world, could they not also be repaired in this way? After all, metal is refined and strengthened from its raw form through the fiery furnaces we scorch them in.*

*Humans often feel pride and hope upon overcoming adversity; almost a spiritual transcendence upon looking back at the darkness they once came from. If we take those at their lowest and help to mend their threads back to a state of peace, then the chords may be even riper than what one could harvest from a normal vessel.*

*However, even if all of these new theories prove to be true, the state of the drunkard's thread wasn’t the only issue. At his age, his spool has already been spinning for many years, which would not leave much room to work with once extracted anyway. We would need thousands like him to achieve the distances we need to weave our loom, and I fear that even with the youth we’ve attained, that sort of time is not the kind we care to spare.*

When I read the next sentence, I had already known it was coming. This was a book I already knew the ending to, and based on where its plot was going, I knew how we got there. Even so, it didn’t stop my eyes from welling when I finally reached it.

*This would make children the ideal targets going forward.*

*While adult strings are clogged and cluttered by years of memory, a child is unburdened by such things. They carry limitless potential, and their quality of thread would be virtually untouched. This isn’t even to mention the heights of wonder and imagination that an adolescent contains—our loom could be completed and thriving in mere decades under such quality.*

*This, of course, is riskier. Children are often watched more closely than their older counterparts, and while we may be able to dodge being found out with the right enchantments, they do not make us invincible to mistakes. So long as we tread lightly, however, I believe all of these variables will combine to resolve themselves.*

*The town is riddled with negligence and people much like the first soul we used. Parents so lost and tangled in their own vices that they leave their children to fend for themselves. Children whom I’m certain dream to be anywhere else. Children that hardly anyone would notice are gone. At least, not until it is too late.*

*We could bring them here to stay with us one by one, giving them their heart's desires until their threads are fully nourished; it would be easy to repair them beyond what was broken with guardians as wretched as theirs. When the time is right, we would harvest and sew them into the loom, and by then, anyone who might be looking for the child would no longer be able to find them; not even any traces of where they might have gone. This is thanks to a property of the threads that I have neglected to mention.*

*Our first subject—the man who we stole from town—he no longer exists. It took some time to realize, but Natellie has confirmed it; all traces of our vagrant, even ones that remain in plain sight, are neglected by the normal eye, as if completely invisible. It is a curious effect, but I think that the explanation is quite clear.*

*By removing his thread, we have also erased all things tied to it. If all of our spools are woven into a tapestry that we see as life, and that tapestry is what our minds perceive and operate through, then by tugging it out, we have left the world unable to view his story. He is sentences in a long book that have been erased; only still legible to us who remain fixed to the loom he is newly woven into.*

*Perhaps some might see this as a cruel fate, but I view it as quite the opposite. Unlike the taking of a normal life, there is no more pain or unrest for him. There is no one to remember to grieve him or to lie awake at night, wondering where he has gone. His miserable life has been remedied by pure indifference, a much better outcome than the tragedy he wallowed in.*

*And the children that we seek? It will be the same for them. Salvation from a life of pain inflicted upon them. Deliverance from the hell this world has made for them in the form of removing them cleanly from it. In a way, we are sparing them.*

 *The cesspool they come from—the ones who watch over them? It is all a cycle. Just like they are betrayed by their own parents, so were theirs by their own, and so on and so forth back into generations. Their strings would be wasted on a life turning into the very things that ruined them, all so the merciless cycle could continue.*

*But we would not squander them. We would not let their threads go to waste. Their essence would instead be taken to create a new paradise. One where they can be recognized and celebrated for who they were before their innocent souls were ruined. And in return, my sisters and I will finally have what we’ve spent generations seeking.*

*The voice agrees. He tells me with amusement almost, and I can sense he is impressed by the drive of such lowly creatures. To him, we must be nothing more than entertainment; as words upon the pages of a book are to us. Perhaps it is that entertainment which has led his curiosity to fancy us. To see if we would really be able to make it among the same fields he dwells.*

*He offers more aid to us now, reading the threads from his side of the mirror to show us who it is we should seek first. He’s able to see the lives in town—to tell us who would be the best mark. I will listen for the time being. He did not have to offer us his initial insights to lead us this far, and yet he did; I have no reason to suspect that his actions are malicious yet.*

*The journey ahead of us is long. There will be plenty of time to learn who our new patron is and what he sees in us. If time proves him an adversary, then we shall simply carve him out of our way once we complete the loom.*

*Everything we have ever done—all we have ever worked for—none of it is as important as this. This world was always nothing but a wicked playground for the corrupt and clever to run, and if that is the game we must play to escape it, then so be it.*

*Once we are done, we will be gods, and beyond the door we step through, we will look back at what we left behind like the one who speaks to us: Beings that see how little our petty lives truly are in a grander scheme.*

It took all the strength I had to peel my eyes from the journal’s pages when I was finished. It wasn’t like I wanted to linger there, but it felt as though while I’d been reading the world had hardened around me. It would hurt too much to tear myself loose from that shell and confront what was waiting for me outside the pages. So much easier instead to just sit there perfectly still and pretend that time was doing the same thing with me.

I understood my friend’s reactions to what I’d told them now. This book had just gone into explicit detail as to what had been done to every poor, innocent child that had been brought to this place, and I’d just told them how many it had happened to. Adeline had specified that their goal would take decades and an ungodly amount of the threads ripped from their sacrifices to complete. If they’d actually pulled it off in the end only for the door to turn into this, then it was all for nothing anyway.

That wasn’t even the part that made the words on the pages blur beneath my vision, however. What struck me the deepest was the way the witch had talked about them. So casually speaking of how she planned to betray them each. To use their vulnerabilities to her advantage and raise them like cattle to the slaughter.

Those poor children all thought they were being saved, and in reality, all they were was a means to a wicked, depraved end.

The feeling of nausea urging me to double over shook me from my vacancy, and I sat up with a deep breath, trying to bring myself back into the room. This didn’t help when I saw the surrounding concrete littered with blood and bits of flesh. The chamber in which the ‘barbaric ritual’ must have taken place time and time again. I wondered how many times in the past it looked like this before being mopped up so that the next victim could be brought in.

At the end of it all, only one word came to mind that summed it up pretty succinctly. I could barely even manage it as a whisper, “Fuck…”

“Yeah…” Kait agreed, another cigarette already lit between her fingers in preparation.

“Whatever that ritual was, do you think it even worked?” Bryce asked with bitterness dripping off his tone, “They did all that shit for this nightmare?”

“This can’t be it,” Lacey shook her head, “They said they thought it would be some sort of paradise, and if the creatures coming from beyond that door are from where they were going, I don’t think they found it.”

“I don’t even get what any of this accomplished for them,” Carly shook her head, “What was any of that even talking about? They talk in so many vague phrases—how would it make them gods?”

I looked back down at the journal, then banished it shut before I could do so for too long. I think I already had an answer, “The first flash I ever got in those hallways; they weren’t rundown like they are now. It was beautiful in there, and there were windows that looked out over a cliffside like the one above. That would be impossible for the underground that it's built in. Hell, the whole space is impossible. Magic, even.”

“What are you saying?” Kait questioned.

“I think we’ve been right about a lot of things, just in the wrong order,” I told my friends, rubbing at the scar across the back of my knuckles, “The door didn’t happen because they messed up the spell; it worked exactly as intended. I think they got their paradise, but for whatever reason, it didn’t stay that way…”

“Yeah, but Carly had a point,” Bryce began, “How? What even are these ‘threads’ that they were ripping out of people and how did it help them make it?”

“Souls,” Kait said plainly, a wisp of smoke leaking past her lips. Her eyes were glassy and focused just beyond my shoulder, “I mean, that’s gotta’ be it right? If the threads are what records everyone’s memories—all of their trauma and their joy and every action they do or don’t take—that’s basically what the soul is, right?”

Lacey slouched beneath the weight of the concept, “If we’re still going with the ‘Fates’ theory, that would explain why cutting a thread ended a life in the myths.”

“It also explains why it would make Adeline and her sister’s gods,” I said. My jaw clenched in anger at the idea I was about to suggest—at the selfishness of the ones who bred it, “Adeline wrote that the threads make up all of life. That all of our stories and energy is what weaves it together. If they were trying to make a new paradise that they were the goddesses of, all they would have to do is rip loose enough threads to get their own tapestry started.”

“They could weave a new world…” Carly pondered aloud.

Bryce scoffed aloud at the idea, half because it probably sounded so absurd, and half because I’m sure he believed it, and his mind simply couldn’t fathom such a concept, “B-But how would that even work? How does *any* of this work? Jessie can’t manipulate the threads like that!”

“I guess I’m just not on the same level as they were,” I said, “She said it took a lot of practice to interact with them—I only see them.”

“No, she said she needed a lot of focus to even see them too. You just stick your hand in a hole and suddenly you’re there, man. What do you have that they didn’t?”

“Whatever it was, they found it eventually, and we already know what it was,” Kait jumped in, her eyes burning against Bryce as hot as her cigarette cherry. She was still clearly not ready to talk about my relationship to all of this. I returned the favor by not calling into question hers.

Kait continued, addressing the group now, “They had a higher insight—knowledge that an outside force gave them.”

“Something *outside* of all life and time,” Carly shuddered, “how is that even possible?”

“Whatever it was, it doesn’t seem like her plan to suss them out as an adversary worked,” I said, nodding to the door, “Unless this was always the end result, I’d wager things fell apart because they didn’t know what they were getting into when they dealt with that particular devil.”

“Or they didn’t listen,” Lacey offered, “She said that whoever it was, if she didn’t like where they were taking her and her sisters, they’d cut it out of the picture. Maybe they didn’t know how to fly on their own.”

“Wouldn’t be the first time man’s hubris got in the way,” Kait snickered darkly, “Probably what put us on a rung lower than whatever is out there.”

“Whatever the case, I’m not really sure that it matters,” Lacey said, taking Kait’s cigarette to everyone’s surprise and pulling a long, labored drag. “We know what they did to bring about the door now, which means…”

She pulled another huff before the first cloud of smoke had even left her mouth, and her eyes fixed on me.

“Jessie, we need to know exactly how the ritual was done. If we can see it firsthand, then we’ll know how it is that they wove this thing together. Maybe we can somehow reverse the process and unravel it all.”

“Lacey, they *killed* people to make this door,” I argued, already knowing why she was singling me out, “We can’t exactly unkill someone, and even if we could, I doubt it would make it go away.”

“I know,” she said, softly brushing my sorry counter aside, “I know you said that little girl was too afraid to bring you down here, but… this is the most information on what’s happening that we’ve ever gotten; we can’t afford to stop here. If you could just convince her—”

“Lacey,” I cut her off desperately. I knew she was absolutely right. It was the best way forward, and the next logical place an answer would lie, but picturing Lucy’s innocent, sorrowful face, my heart ached too badly.

“I’ll look for a thread on my own, but I can’t ask her to do that. Her whole life was ripped away from her by these monsters—she was betrayed by the people that promised her a new life in one of the worst ways imaginable, and based on what we just read, whatever happened to her down here…” I swallowed, “I can only imagine what scared her so badly.”

“Jessie,” Carly beckoned, resting her hand over mine to stop me from digging my nails into my scar. I hadn’t even noticed I’d begun slicing the border of it open with how rough I’d been pressing. My eyes met her as I took a breath and blinked blurriness away.

She continued, “I know it’s a hard request, and I know that you can imagine better than most what it would be like to be in her shoes. I think we all can…”

Around the circle, I saw Lacey and Bryce’s heads drop solemnly. Kait pulled her cigarette to her lips and lost herself out the window.

“Lacey is right, though. We’ve been given a massive lead here, and this is the best way forward. If we can finally figure out how to destroy this stupid piece of wood from hell, then maybe whatever it is keeping that poor girl trapped in the first place won’t have a hold on her anymore. We can end her suffering once and for all.” She gave my hand a squeeze, “And we can finally end ours too…”

My body ached heavily in that moment, but it wasn’t with fatigue or pain. It was a mental aching. One that I’d been carrying a long, long time. I was so tired of that weight. I was so exhausted from the lack of dopamine in my brain and the constant anxiety in my chest, and the endless screaming of self-loathing that my thoughts played on repeat.

Carly was right; everyone around me felt the same. The sad thing was, no amount of monster slaying or dismantling the red door was going to fix what was wrong with us. As I sat there staring at the sinister crimson barrier, however, Carly’s hand over mine and looking at my friends, I didn’t care about fixing it. I didn’t care about justice or righting the wrongs that had been done to the innocent.

I was just angry. Years of rage and pain boiling deep inside me for a system we’d been born into that was already broken. Vitriol towards the ones who were supposed to look after us when we were small, but who only ever used our vulnerabilities to do us harm.

The tingle still lurking across my bruised cheek gave a soft murmur, and the thought of all the lives taken in this basement made my muscles tight. The scar on my knuckle itched, and echoes of Lucy’s trusting laugh toward Adeline and her sisters twisted at my insides.

It could have been any one of us. In a different time, we were all vulnerable enough at some point that it could have been one of my friends who ended up on this basement floor, their souls ripped out to fuel a selfish adult’s twisted vices.

We had survived. We had become hardened and strong. We had earned the right to live, and I wasn’t about to let the hags that brought this upon us get a second chance to take that from us.

“The clocks about to chime,” I told the others, “Let’s fuck this next monster up, then I’ll see what I can find.”
