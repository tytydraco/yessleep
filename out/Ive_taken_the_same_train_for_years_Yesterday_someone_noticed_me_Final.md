I’m on the train right now, and I’m not sure I’ll be able to post again after this. I’m writing on my phone, and either the battery or the phone itself is dying. Either way, I want you all to know what happened, and what to do if you ever encounter someone like the Grey Man.

I had to go back one last time. I couldn’t just leave without knowing more. After I left yesterday, nothing else seemed to matter.

The sewer grates actually lined up with the doors today.

When the doors opened, I was ready.

Two steps up. Three forward. Left turn.

Four steps up. Slight left. Three steps ahead.

I sat down in the seat. It had the same creaks and contours, but it felt unfamiliar. I looked unfamiliar too. No backpack, no work clothes, no coffee order ready. Only my phone in one pocket and the keychain in the other.

I turned to the Grey Man, ready to talk. He spoke first.

“*You came back,*” he said.

He was exactly as I left him yesterday.

“You look like you never left. How long have you been on this train?” I asked.

“*Long enough that I stopped counting.*”

“And the others? Do they ever leave?”

He looked at the Accountant first.

“*He knows all the answers. He’s known them for years, written them all down hundreds or thousands of times. He just can’t let them stay written, because that would complete the puzzle.”*

He turned to look at LSS.

*“She’s been saving that seat since the morning she decided it was easier than going home and telling him she wanted to leave. So, she never did.”*

He looked back at me silently.

“What about the Kid?”

He looked over at the Kid but said nothing. He turned back to me and stared silently. His eyes burned into my soul, silently demanding something of me. The silence crept around me, enveloped me, suffocating me until I was ready to scream just to end it.

Then it clicked.

I reached into my pocket and felt the keychain. I ran my fingers along its entirety, building a mental image with my touch. 

I took it out and looked at it.

Part of it was a classic keychain, a key to our house hanging off a small ring. The rest of it was her, a small flat silver heart. Engraved on it were our initials and a date. Our wedding day. A small blank space remained underneath it, with enough room for two or three more dates.

I ran my thumb across it, feeling the grooves of the engraving etched into its face. 

That was when I finally remembered. When I allowed myself to remember.

It was an unremarkable morning. 

She was well into her treatment, and the side effects had fully materialized. I woke up earlier than usual, well before my alarm was set to go off. I didn’t have enough time to go back to sleep, so I got up and got ready.

Before I left, I stood by the door. I watched her sleep, and listened to her breath.

In that moment I made a choice.

“I couldn’t bear to see her like that, so I left. And then I did it again the next day. And the next. Until it became the routine, and the routine became my escape from facing her. From facing reality. To disappear.”

*“Now, you know.”*

I stood up and walked toward the doors.

My legs carried me easily. My mind was clear as I got closer to the doors.

The train lurched and the brakes squeaked as it slowed. The doors opened.

I stood in front of the opening, the steps beckoning me to freedom.

I stood there as the chime dinged and the doors closed. I turned around and made my way back to my seat. Four steps up, slight left, and three steps forward.

I sat down. The Grey Man was gone.

I looked at my hands. They looked duller than usual. My clothes looked faded in a way I don’t remember.

I took out my phone and looked at the screen. I didn’t recognize the date, and the screen started to malfunction.

A new passenger, someone I’ve never seen before, sat down in the seat pod next to me. He looked around, learning this section of the train and enjoying the view outside.

He looked around until he made eye contact with me.

I looked back for a moment longer than I meant to, and looked away.

An announcement came on the overhead speakers.

*Now departing Hornbeam station.*
