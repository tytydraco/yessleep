I’m writing this because if even one of you tells me they’re experiencing the same thing, I’ll know I’m not imagining it.

And I really, desperately need to know I’m not imagining it.

I’m socially anxious, sure, not insane though. At least I don’t think I am. But whatever’s been following me for the past three years is starting to make me question that.

If you’re going through something similar, I wish I could tell you what to do. I wish I could offer some advice, some reassuring explanation, anything.

But I’m barely holding it together myself.

It started in November 2023.

Or maybe that’s just when I first noticed it. For all I know, it could have been following me for years. There’s no way to tell when something is always on the edge of your field of vision.

I remember that evening clearly.

I’d had a long day at work and was waiting for the metro, standing in a queue with at least twenty people ahead of me (average Rajiv Chowk crowd, am I right?).

Everyone was tired, some were staring at their phones, and some were talking in groups.

Many of them were familiar faces. I know where some of them get off. I know where some of them work, either because of their lanyards or because I’ve overheard some of them curse their companies for being horrible.

If I weren’t socially anxious, I would’ve loved to join them in their conversations.

There were times when I had the perfect story to jump in with but I couldn’t get myself to do it. Anxiety won every time.

All I could do was overhear the same conversations every single evening.

I know what you’re thinking. They should just quit and work somewhere better.

Some of them are sole breadwinners in their family. They don’t have the luxury of hopping jobs, not in this economy.

So they’ll bitch and they’ll moan, but they’ll work their asses off for the same company.

It’s sad, but that’s how it goes for most of us.

That evening, one of the regulars was not present.

I thought he must’ve skipped work or taken a cab, but I overheard his group mention that the guy passed away in his sleep.

My mind went through the many first-hand and second-hand memories I had of him.

I vividly remember the time he got a big promotion and handed over sweets to his clique. That was when I learned they were all working in different companies and were more of a commute group.

Or the time he came in on crutches because he couldn’t afford a cab and his office denied him leaves or the flexibility to work from home.

And then my chain of thoughts was broken as I noticed someone (or maybe something) standing near the edge of the station.

What struck me was how immediately I noticed them.

There were dozens of people around, and yet my attention drifted toward this one particular spot.

I turned my head slightly and they moved.

Not in the way someone shifts their weight or takes a step.

They were simply somewhere else. Still in the corner of my eye, still occupying that strange little space where I could almost see them.

I looked again.

Nothing.

I told myself I was tired. Maybe I’d caught someone moving out of the corner of my eye and mistaken it for something else.

But then it happened again.

And again.

During the entire ride home, I kept noticing them.

At the far end of the carriage. Heck, even outside the metro when that was at the corner of my eye.

Every time I tried to look directly at them, they were gone.

Or somewhere else.

By the time I got home, I’d almost convinced myself it was nothing since I couldn’t see them anymore.

I even stood in front of the bathroom mirror, checking my eyes for dust or irritation.

I blinked a dozen times. Rinsed them. Checked again.

There was nothing wrong with my eyes.

And, well, you can probably guess how that turned out.

Because I’m still seeing it three years later, still in the corner of my fucking eye.

Over the years I’ve asked my friends to look for it.

I’ve pointed out the places where I see it. I’ve tried to explain exactly what happens when I turn my head.

They don’t see anything.

Some laugh because they think I’m messing with them. Some feign concern. A few have suggested I see a psychiatrist.

The worst part isn’t seeing it. I barely see it at all.

It’s knowing that wherever I go, whatever I’m doing, there’s always that part of my vision where it could be.

Even when I’m home. Even when I’m lying in bed, with the doors locked and the lights off.

I can’t see it then.

But I know it’s there and it’s getting closer.

I don’t know how to explain that without sounding completely fucking insane.

It’s not like I can measure the distance between us. I can’t even look at it properly.

But I know.

It’s always in the same part of my vision. Just closer every day.

I’m not asking you to believe me blindly.

I just want to know if anyone has experienced this.

If anyone has noticed something that only exists at the edge of their vision, something that moves whenever they try to look at it.

Something that gets closer.

A few months ago, there was still a few feet between us.

But as I’m writing this, I can feel it just behind my right shoulder.
