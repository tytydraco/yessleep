You see, I was not raised in this cult. Not in the traditional sense. My mother found them when I had turned fourteen, and something about their doctrines and rituals appealed to me then. They were not reluctant about the term “cult” either, and I found that kinda disruptive. If anything, I don’t blame myself for those first years; I was a weird kid who wanted to shock my classmates and went along for my weird mother’s ride. This is to say that no, I wasn’t raised to believe in their shit by fanatical parents like many folks around the world; I *wanted* that.

At least at the start, it felt like a new-age cult of Mother Earth and everything, but with a twist. To them, the planet Earth was the true omniscient, omnipotent, and omnipresent goddess, the one that was here before anyone else, the one that will be here before we’re long gone. And when I say planet Earth, I mean just the huge geoid we live on, the soil, the earth; that’s the real deal for them. And well, it was for me too. Every landscape, every animal, all of this will change, but our rock womb? This one is forever. And along with this, they put a lot of their praise on minerals, especially the radioactive ones, which carried “a raw power of changing”. Well, they were not lying here either.

I could say that the minerals were to the cult what the idea of the Holy Trinity or saints are for the Christians, that concept that nobody truly grasps completely, but smiles and nods just to look polite. How can three people be one? How can you say you’re a monotheist when you have a plethora of people you pray to? See, these things are confusing and require Olympic mental gymnastics, Nadia Comaneci style. But I’m not here to judge; I just want to point out that all religions have these inherently difficult concepts that we understand but also don’t understand. Mine had the concept of “fragments of god”.

These fragments were minerals. Literal fragments of the planet Earth, and the truly powerful ones could communicate with us humans and give us the infinite knowledge of… everything? As long as you *could* communicate with it, that is.

Like many people out of cults and fundamentalist branches, I now see how the whole plot was full of holes, but then I was deep in that shit.

The cult was rather discreet; nothing changed in my daily routine aside from the gatherings and teachings. Our reunions took place in caves. It was peaceful. They stuck with large halls within caves, so it had a "being-wrapped-in-mother-earth's-womb" feel to it, not the "fat-worm-stuck-in-small-hole" feel some cavers enjoy. My hometown was encrusted in an area with lots of caves, and caving is still a rather popular activity in that region to this day.

When I was graduating high school, the cult was eager to recruit new girls around my age, not teens in general, just girls. The majority of the members were academic folks in their forties and fifties. The younglings were rare; just me, a girl two years younger, and a person in their twenties, who took me a long time to find out was a girl too. We had three leaders, two men and one woman. One guy was the straight white male prototype; his name is Doran. The type of guy I imagine would be bullying my old man at high school back in the day, god bless his soul. The other guy was rather androgynous and flamboyant. His name's Dane. African American, long braids, quite handsome if you ask me, but unbearable. And then, the woman. Man, how can I describe Leila? She is, was, the most beautiful woman I had ever seen. Okay, maybe she had a dab at why I chose to stick to my mother's gang of weirdos. She was forty, with a small but toned frame, beautiful brown eyes, and long dreadlocks she dyed white. Man, she could easily star with those Velaryon girls in that House of the Dragon show. I would be trained under her wing later, and I had an obvious crush on her. She was married to Dane; Doran was just… there.

The three were geologists working in an excavation when they recovered a fragment of god. A large druse with yellow translucent spikes flourishing from the middle, but the yellow colour shone, like it had an inner glow.

At some gatherings, Leila put her hand over the druse and entered a trance, then she would give us a message. I don't know if that stone spoke to her (although my posterior experiences make me inclined to believe so), but what I know is that as the time went by, the hand she put over the stone started to show blisters and an unnatural rubor, like a strange burn. In her last days alive, Leila's right hand had a bandage wrapped around it most of the time.

I continued to visit the cult throughout my college years, slowly rising in rank. As a young adult, I saw it grow and establish itself in my city. We were fifty by then. The cult bought a piece of land near a particular cave system and started developing a small neighborhood. I still lived with my mother when she moved to that neighborhood, so in time all members became neighbors too.

I was almost done with my BSc when the leaders approached me, demanding that I take lessons in childbirth and labor. I was designed to bring forth the next fragment of god. I was confused; were not the fragments stones? How can one simply birth stone (please keep kidney stones jokes to yourself; we're being serious here)?

Leila smiled at me, a cryptic smile.

"Well, H., it's time for us humans to evolve. We have to balance ourselves with god if we're to survive all this mayhem in our world. It's time to bring a new form into life, the new humanity". Her voice was serene but reeling a little under the grandiosity of what was said.

Not gonna lie, I shed some tears. Who can blame me? I was in it for good. I immediately put my whole life on hold so I could dedicate myself solely to being the best DIY obstetrician that ever existed. That's when things took a turn to weird. Really weird.

Remember when I told you the cult was looking for young ladies? It happens that this "prophecy" to bring forth the new being was rolling since back then. But this type of thing takes time, research, and all: there's a method to madness. Apparently, a recently impregnated woman should bathe herself in a cave deep in a rather dangerous room inside the complex in my town we called The Lady's Eye, the cave that coincidentally was part of the cult's lands. I don't know how they came to that information. Remember, I was just a member, probably chosen for my long time in the cult and my mother's connections with everyone. I knew what the leaders thought I needed to know. I was taught to seek for nothing more.

Since my high school years, three more young women joined the cult; some with parents, some alone. The androgynous lady left at some point; I met her at the last Pride parade and told her it was the best choice she ever made in her whole motherfucking life. The girl younger than me, Eleissa, was not so lucky. She was the first one chosen for the task, along with Sarah, a fresh-out-of-teens girl, and Fabrizia, a schoolteacher in her thirties.

Fabrizia had a husband, but Eleissa and Sarah did not. Not even boyfriends. So, of course, Dane and Doran took the heavy burden to try and impregnate these girls every day, two, three, four times a day, while Fabrizia’s husband did the same in their home.

The single girls were relocated to a stop inside the cave system, one we used for most of our gatherings, and I was there too, to assist them with food, medicine, and whatever they needed. Which also meant I was forced into the same space where those things took place.

Doran looked especially excited when it was his time with Sarah. She was barely eighteen, man. And her parents saw it as a blessing. She never made a sound, except when it ended. Because then, she cried.

I'm not gonna say I fought for her or that I did anything brave. I didn't, and it eats me slowly every day. Neither did Leila, who just stared blankly while her husband fucked two girls nonstop, clutching that egg-like yellow stone. I thought it was normal, at least on a conscious level. But I now trace my first moments of doubt to that time, like Sarah's quiet sobs started cracking something inside my rock-solid faith.

Eleissa was the first one to show signs of pregnancy, so she was the first to bathe in the cave's waters.

The passages were narrow and dangerous, and the cult leaders only had a handmade map to the actual location. Once in the right spot, the whole walk felt worth it. The room was breathtaking, the wet dream of every cave explorer. The hall was round with a ceiling so high it disappeared in the darkness, and a natural pool in the middle. Gray, serene water, gently lit by unseen sources of exterior light. The place smelled like soil after a summer rain, and the air was surprisingly warm, with a strange warmth also emanating from its walls.

"The mother's womb," Leila whispered, hugging Eleissa with excitement. We, the members, surrounded the pool, our bare feet almost touching the water. Pairs of expectant eyes in the dim light waited while Eleissa undressed with the help of Leila and entered the water. She walked until her waist was completely underwater.

Then, silence.

A foul smell, faint at first and later unbearable, began to waft from the pool. Eleissa opened her mouth, her eyes crazed, and let out the most unhuman sound I had ever heard. A shriek so loud I felt a vibration in the walls. The second scream was muffled; Eleissa had covered her mouth with two tight hands, tears flowing violently from her face.

Dane urged Eleissa to leave the pool, acting methodically and calmly to receive the girl at the shore. The three leaders looked stern, but not surprised. Not like us. They expected that kind of thing to happen.

"H. please assist Eleissa," Doran commanded, practically tossing the girl into my arms. She was cold, too cold for the warm environment, and stiff. She was completely paralyzed in the same position she reached the shore: right leg slightly bent, left leg stretched in a walking stance, and the two hands still holding the mouth tight. A flesh statue, that's what she was.

The walk back was silent, heavy.

Leila guided us in the front, with Doran and Dane in the rear of the line. Sarah was not inside the room, and she looked expectantly, eyes searching for Eleissa. She was faithful too, in the end. Ever shielding us from Sarah’s view, the leaders took Eleissa and me to another stop in the cave system.

"It's better to keep the girls separated from now on. Any stress can hinder a pregnancy in Sarah." Doran pointed at Eleissa like a discarded thing.

"Ah, this is bad. Real bad." Leila turned her fingers anxiously. "The god rejected her. What will we do?"

"Now, we see." Dane sighed. "H. will take care of Eleissa. Let us see what will happen".

*What will happen?* I was confused. I thought we would see the birth of a new being, not the display of ungodly suffering. I looked at Eleissa, her eyes still wet with tears, looking straight ahead to an unknown spot. I did not know then, but her eyes were paralyzed too. I held her body close at an awkward angle. In a moment, I saw my mother's face poking at one corner of the spot, but the leaders dispatched her quickly with serene, soft-spoken words.

Even they eventually left me too, to bring back supplies. There, in the dark, I bawled, my tears mixing with Eleissa's. I noticed a warm liquid in my leg; too thick to be urine, the liquid flowed freely from Eleissa. "A miscarriage", my first thought. But then, the sewer smell that filled the pool room started to spread. No mistake; the liquid was not blood but… leachate? I called Eleissa, but she was completely still. Her eyes were now dry, while that foul liquid pooled beneath us.
