I live in a cul-de-sac that’s in the first stage of an entire neighborhood development. It’s out in the countryside surrounded by miles of dense, overgrown forest. Driving on the main road, you wouldn’t expect the random dirt path off to the shoulder to lead you to here.

My father and I moved here a month ago after he got a huge pay raise and could afford something a bit better than the duplex that we called home. 

He’s always wanted to live near the woods too so at least he can enjoy it temporarily before it’s torn to shreds. 

I, on the other hand, don’t prefer the surrounding nature one bit. It creeps me out but I don’t really have a say and once again it’s only temporary. 

This house has everything I could‘ve ever wished for: a living room with a three-bay window, three bedrooms, a beautiful kitchen with a marble island and granite flooring, a two-car garage, and a decent-sized basement, which is where I stay. 

There’s something about having my own sanctuary under the house that’s always appealed to me. Maybe it’s the aesthetic, old shows that I’d watched when I was younger, or maybe even the feeling that it’s my own little space away from everyone. Granted I only live with one other person who’s never home.

Our last basement was packed from floor to ceiling with old junk dating back to when I was born. A half-working freezer that smells of rotting meat,  polyester clothing with corny one-liners that I’d get every Christmas, or even a stack of random 2x4’s that my dad swears he’d use someday. Basically everything you wouldn’t want in plain sight around the house.

There are four houses, including ours, in the neighborhood. All of them are built the exact same, down to the smallest detail. They’re all even painted the same color, a pretty off-white shade.

We had the most stereotypical but diverse neighbors. I’ve never met them because I’m not the talkative type, but my father has and he says they’re all pretty normal people. 

Well, most of them.

The people living in the home next to ours are a higher middle-class family that consists of a mother and a father with a son, daughter, and of course a toddler. The American dream. I don’t know any of their names but I know their last name is Chesterfield. They seem happy. A little too happy. Like some biochemically engineered “humans”. 

Next to them is a younger guy who lives by himself. Around the 25-30-ish range, finance bro, the type of good-looking dude that you’d consider to be a jerk but is actually down to the core. Always outside for some reason. Despite all that he has a simple name, Bob. I aspire to be as successful as he is in a few years.

And last but not least, the one and only Edith and Harold Ultra. Yes Ultra. At least that’s what was printed on their packaging label for things like orgonite pyramids, EMF blocking paint, and concerning amounts of tin foil that accidentally got delivered to our doorstep on multiple occasions. I’ve never really seen them leave the house. They’re probably living off of their retirement or something like that. Very interesting people.

My eyes shot open and my body stiffened. It was pitch black.

I peeled the sleep mask off my face and shifted my gaze toward my nightstand. It was my dad calling at 4 a.m.

“Hello?”

“Hey Salem! Sorry to wake you but they let me off work early so I figured I could come home to catch some Z’s!”

“Hello? Salem? 

“Sorry about that. Long night. Why’d you call?”

“Remember? Yesterday I asked if you could scavenge some new batteries from one of the moving boxes for the garage remote. I left it right on your nightstand before I left for work and you said you’d get to it ”

“Oh yeah. My bad. I was distracted. I’ll get to it.” 

My eyes darted over to my nightstand, the top drawer left slightly ajar, the last 4 edible gummies staring right back at me. 

“Be quick. I’m 5 minutes away.” 

“Okay okay.”

I let out a heavy sigh and scooted myself off of the bed, my feet landing on the cool tile floor.

I pulled the attic cord down and climbed up the steps, fiberglass and dust filling my lungs.

At the end of the hall was a beautiful stained glass window which was blocked off by a stack of boxes piling up to the ceiling, most of them toppled over at this point, bringing in pretty thin streaks of light to fill the dark void that is my attic. 

To the right of the entry, there was a torn-up box labeled ‘ELECTRONICS’ beneath a vintage coffee table that my grandparents gifted us as a housewarming gift. It was cluttered with old VHS tapes and DVDs dating that predated me.  

Old phones. Old gaming consoles. A singular Polaroid photo?

“Salem? Are you done yet? I’ve been here for 5 minutes now and it's raining real bad.”

I looked down at my phone, realizing I hadn’t ended the call before I started rushing upstairs.

“Yes. Almost done.”

The high really started to hit me now. I knew 15 milligrams was too much for me. 

I dropped the photo and scrambled through the box for the coin shaped batteries. The ones you use once every few years and forget all about the next day.  

A growing tapping noise ran along the ceiling and the glass stained window in perfect harmony.

“Hello? Are you there?”

My eyes shot back at my phone. We’d been on the phone for 10 minutes at this point. 

“Yeah. Sorry dad.” 

I felt the cool, metallic touch of either a pair of loose batteries or spare change. Lifting them up to the light, a heavy-lidded grin plastered across my face and I lifted myself off of the floor.

On the way downstairs, I pressed the remote repeatedly and was met with silence. The batteries didn’t work at all. 

On the other side of the garage door I could see my dad cupping his hands, scanning left and right, looking for me to emerge from the darkness. He looked to be relieved yet annoyed when he saw my silhouette emerging from the darkness.

“It won't work!” I shouted through the window pane.

“What do you mean? Did you search in the box that says-”

“Electronics? Yes I did. These are the only ones I found.”

“I bought those new. You must’ve put them in wrong. You know what? Just open the garage manually”

“How do I-”

“Are you serious? Go over there and pull the emergency release cord!” He pointed through the windowpane toward a huge red cord dangling in the center of the garage.

I gave it a pull and lifted the garage door.

My dad stood mere inches from my face and was soaked head to toe.

He took a step in the garage and looked down at me, his chest almost touching mine. 

After a moment he pinched the bridge of his nose and let out a sigh before running back to the car, disappearing into the now even heavier rainfall.  

He shifted the car in drive and pulled in, closing the garage door with a loud slam. As he walked over to me, a gut-wrenching feeling started to eat at my insides.

 I should’ve eaten something before eating those gummies.

“I’m sorry for getting like that. It’s been a long night. May I please see the garage remote?” His demeanor did a whole 180. 

I passed him the garage remote, not even saying a word. Hoping he wouldn’t be able to see the glossiness in my eyes. 

“Thanks. Goodnight son.” He walked into the house before pausing and looking over his shoulder.

“I guess you ended up putting them in the right way.” He waved the remote at me with a sheepish grin splattered across his face.

“That’s on me. I’ll check out the motor in the morning before I leave for work. Goodnight”

I nodded my head and we split off in different directions. Him walking up the stairs towards the kitchen for a late-night snack before most likely falling asleep on the couch and me heading back down the hallway towards my room to forget all about tonight. 

That’s when I noticed something as I turned to close the garage door.

My dad didn’t back in. He always backs in.

Maybe I’m overreacting. I mean it’s his first time back in days and the downpour was pretty bad. 

He’s always been specific about his routine. Even with parking. 

I turned back and thought about asking him about it but my body overpowered my mind and I was back to bed in seconds.

My alarm clock rang and I snoozed it once more.

Closing my eyes didn’t really do anything for me at this point. 

I flipped on my back and stared at the ceiling, thinking about life as I always do in the morning.

Breathe in. Breathe out.

After a couple minutes of contemplation, I stood on my bed and pushed the hopper window shut, turning the hinge clockwise.

I put on some underwear and jogged up the stairs past the living room, straight to the kitchen. It feels like I haven’t eaten in days.

What met me on the fridge was a note written by no one other than my own dad:

“Good morning sleepy head, I tried working on the motor and ended up messing around with the electrical panel till I realized there’s no power. It most likely went out last night around when our phone call ended. I asked Mr.Chesterfield about it as I was on my way out and he said he was dealing with the same thing. 

I guess some lightning hit a light pole last night so there won’t be any power for a couple days. I had to turn my car around because the construction crew is blocking off the path leading to our neighborhood. 

Try to open the fridge as little as possible. I’ll try to bring you some lukewarm takeout from the food court if I can make it home tonight. Have a good day and please do something productive.”

Just how can I keep myself busy?

I’ve been inside for so long that I completely forgot that my router provided my only source of Wi-Fi. The cell towers didn't even reach here so I can’t even use my phone. I knew I shouldn’t have deleted those dumb mobile games that took up more than enough storage. 

 It was just me and the house.

I opened the fridge and grabbed a tub of Greek yogurt, popping the lid open and cringing at the sour smell protruding from it. I might as well try something new to keep me entertained.

After a cold shower, the last thing I wanted to do was put on damp clothes from the dryer. Better than wearing dirty clothes.

I opened the blinds in my living room and sat on the couch, resting my legs on the coffee table and crossing my arms as I soaked in the sun.

That's when I came up with an idea.

I walked downstairs and opened up the top drawer on my nightstand. 

Opening the ziploc baggie, I popped 3 stale edible gummies in my mouth.

Minutes later the gummies started to take effect and it felt as if a weight was lifted off my chest.

I walked back up the stairs towards the living room and melted into the couch, staring up at the ceiling. 

“HUZZ HUZZ” 

I quickly stood up and looked around, wondering where that noise was coming from.

“HUZZ HUZZ”

The noise came from behind me, outside of the window.

I kneeled on the couch and peeked through my blinds to see Bob shadow boxing in his front yard. 

He was shirtless and shredded to the gills. A physique that I tried to acquire on multiple occasions but gave up on after realizing the commitment that it takes. Maybe I can practice that in my free time.

I stood up and started throwing punches in the air, feeling like I looked stupid.

Maybe I can both start to exercise and make a friend on the same day. 

I put on some old beaters and was out the door.



The Chesterfields were playing in their backyard. Bob was on his front lawn. And Mr. and Mrs. Ultra were in their house as usual.

My feet shuffled against the sidewalk, making just as much noise, if not more, than the Chesterfields. They looked to be playing catch in almost complete silence. 

I was about to pass their house when I decided to take a leap of courage. 

While I have it in my system, I’m gonna say hi. 

I shifted my gaze off of the sidewalk and up at them, cupping my hands around my mouth as I yelled out a nice neighborly “good morning”. 

The father, along with his son and daughter, all turned in almost perfect unison.

Like a delayed echo, Mr. Chesterfield went from a serious look to a perfect smile in seconds.

“Hi! Hello! Good morning! Are you enjoying the weather?” He waved at me in a perfect rhythmic motion, like a metronome ticking left and right.

“Pretty good!” I shot him an awkward smile and raised an open palm in the air.

He was standing there with his arm still waving, perfect posture and all. 

His children just stood there with their arms by their side, the wind blowing against their short sleeves and shorts.

The breeze picked up and I brushed my hair from my face and broke the awkward silence. 

“Well have a nice day!” I waved back at them one more time before going on my way.

I don’t know how my dad interacts with people like that. 

As I approached Bob’s front lawn, the smell of expensive cologne and musk filled the air. 

He turned 90 degrees to face me and gave me a welcoming smile.

“Hey I’m Salem, nice to meet you.” I offered my right hand for a proper handshake. 

“Hey I’m Bob!” He gave my right hand a firm squeeze and leaned forward. 

“Your dad’s told me some things about you! Congrats on graduating this year! Are you going to college?” 

“Thanks. I don’t know what I plan on doing yet. My dad says I’m allowed to take a gap year though so I’m making the most of it.” I scratched the back of my neck.

I sound like a bum.

“Nice. Nice. I remember being your age. Partying, hanging with girls, smoking pot…” He knudged my shoulder with the back of his sweaty hand and started laughing.

I gave him a confused look that probably looked more constipated than confused.

“I know you’re high man. It’s pretty obvious.”

“Wait really? I thought no one could tell.” My face burned hot.

“Trust me. Judging by your eyes and your reaction time, it's pretty obvious. You’re a bit awkward from what I’ve seen from a distance but now it's even more obvious!” 

It’s true.

I’m surprised he noticed me though. I’m barely outside.

“You’ve seen me? I mean you’re always outside.” I stammered. 

Very slick.

“Yeah.” He picked up a towel from the grass and slung it over his shoulder. 

“So what sends you over here?” He wiped the beads of sweat from his forehead.

“I was hoping to get some boxing tips. I’m really trying to get into shape.” I shot a practice jab towards him. 

 He ducked and shot a jab back at me, stopping just short of my face.

I tripped on my own leg and landed on the grass with a quiet thump. 

“My bad I didn’t mean to make you fall over like that.” He let out a laugh and offered a hand.

I gripped his hand and he pulled me up. 

He’s really strong. But at the same time, I don’t really weigh much. 

“You wanna come inside? I’m making protein shakes and have nothing to do with the power outage.” 

“Sure, why not.” I tried to sound as nonchalant as possible.

I followed behind him, noticing every detail of the muscle striations in his back. 

We went straight to his familiar yet unfamiliar kitchen and he pulled out a swivel stool for me, offering me a seat. 

I sat down, swaying my body left and right as we got to talking.

“How’re you liking it here?” He poured the last droplets from a gallon of milk into a protein shaker bottle with his back to me.

“It’s been alright. It’s pretty different here compared to where we were before. I’m not complaining though.” I looked down at my hands, fidgeting with them to keep myself distracted.

“Oh yeah?” He started to shake the bottle at a rapid pace.

“Your dad told me all about how much he loves it. How’s he been? I haven’t talked to him in weeks.” He pulled out a mug from the dishwasher and poured out half of the protein shake before sliding it across the granite countertop towards me. 

“Ehh good I guess. I never really know how he feels to be honest. He’s always at work.” I took a long sip, trying to find my words.

“I understand he has to save lives and all but with this new promotion I feel-” I looked back up. Bob was staring right into my eyes and listening to every single word I said, a foamy protein shake mustache sitting over his top lip.

“Sorry for getting too personal, I'm just really high.” I stammered. 

“It’s fine, don't worry man. Shit happens. I’m an open book myself.” He drank the last of his protein shake and wiped his face with the back of his hand.

“I’m assuming he’s a doctor right? So that means you’re gonna be a doctor?” A slight smirk spread across his face.

“My dad wants me to go into something in the medical field but it’s just not my style.” My foot started tapping the floor.

“That’s why I’m taking a gap year.” 

It felt good to say that. To finally talk to someone about how I feel. Even if it’s just a little bit.

He crossed his arms and gave an approving nod.

“Now tell me about yourself. How’re you so successful at a young age?” I crossed my arms back at him.

We went on for hours as we talked about everything. 

We’re surprisingly the same in many ways. Same worldviews. Same music taste. Even the same zodiac sign. I just don’t like working out, but I’ll make it there soon.

Our stories were very different though. He grew up in foster care and would often run away for long periods of time. Even then, he worked hard in school and ended up becoming the successful man that he is today. I learned so much life advice from him. Maybe I’ll try out a college out here, or even better, online. I guess he also got a raise from his job and found his house on the market around the same time we did. 

Once it got dark, I excused myself and we agreed on meeting tomorrow morning to do some training. 

I looked over my shoulder and gave one last wave to the freakishly tall silhouette before closing the door.

 Now that the gummy wore off I can see that he's way taller than I originally thought.

The watch that Bob gave me as a “graduation gift” read 10:30 p.m. 

I decided to walk on the road rather than the sidewalk this time.

I looked left and right as if there were any cars that would randomly appear out of nowhere. 

There was nothing to worry about, though. Everyone was stuck in the neighborhood unless they wanted to walk or bike for God knows how long to reach the main road. 

I walked while spinning in slow circles, thinking about the productive day that I had. 

Everything felt so still. 

Mid-spin, something caught my attention from one of the homes. 

Through the bay window, a pair of eyes was peeking through the white blinds, looking straight at me as I stood in the center of the cul-de-sac.

At first it was one pair. Two. Three. And then four. Focusing on every single step that I took.

Maybe I made too much noise with the sound of my feet dragging across the rough terrain but even then, the chirping of crickets and other wildlife in their backyard definitely outweighs a couple of footsteps. 

Also why would they all be sitting in their living room of all places in the dark?

I stopped in place and turned my body to wave at them just as I’d done earlier.

They just stared at me. No emotion in their eyes.

I quickly jogged the rest of the way home, looking over my shoulder.

Just what did I do to them?

I entered the house and shut the front door behind me with a soft click.

Looking up the stairs, I caught myself reminiscing on times back then where I’d come home to my father. He’d finish work before I got home from school and stopped whatever he was doing to greet me at the front door and ask about my day. Every single time. 

Before the big move he truly was a man of routine.

Snapping out of my daze, I noticed the attic ladder still hanging open. I completely forgot to close it. 

I guided the attic stairs back up the chute, coating myself in a layer of dust, sending me into a violent sneeze. 

Upon opening my eyes, the Polaroid photo from the night before fell onto the hardwood floor. 

I picked it up, lifting it above my head to get a good view. 

My blood ran cold.

It was a photo of me. 

Not me from years ago. Not even me from a few months ago. 

It was a photo of me last night. It was taken from my open window.
