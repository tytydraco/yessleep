The man standing opposite me on the reception desk avoids eye contact while the outdated room booking software takes his payment. When it finally gives us the all clear, I hand him his card and tell him,

“We hope you enjoyed your stay!”

I smile and try to act like nothing’s weird, but he just ignores me and hurries away. I don’t blame him. The night before, I’d been checking out a noise complaint when I bumped into him coming out of his room. He was wearing nothing but a leather harness that made a big X across his pale chest and a ball gag worn loose like a necklace around the throat. Soon as he saw me he gasped and slammed the door shut, while I yelped like I’d just seen a ghost. I almost knocked his door straight after, ready to apologise for being unprofessional. But that’d only make things worse. Besides, when I got close, I heard him crying and then I really lost my nerve.

I cannot help but feel bad for him as he leaves reception, the doors closing behind him just as Callie comes out the back office. She’s pulling on her blazer, ready to take my place at the front desk. 

“I feel sorry for the cleaners,” she says. “You ever ask ‘em about some of the shit they deal with?”

“No.” I shake my head. “Maria’s nice. Reminds me of my Mom.”

“Maria’s a good Christian woman.” Callie smirks. “She isn’t gonna tell you about wiping loads off bathroom mirrors. Bella? Now she’ll give you the gory details.”

I wince. “Not sure I want them,” I tell her.

“Probably for the best.” Callie approaches my computer and leans across me to grab the mouse. “Go on, you go home,” she adds. “I’ll sign you out.”

“Thanks,” I say, not bothering to point out that I have to wait for my Dad no matter what. The hotel is an island built only for cars. If I tried walking I’d be forced along gravel banks and concrete ditches, flinching each time a truck barrelled past at nearly 90mph. So Dad’s gotta pick me up and drop me off and sometimes he’s nearly an hour late. The staff room isn't an option. If the manager sees you, whether you’re clocked in or not, he’ll give you work. So after saying goodbye to Callie I go sit on the edge of the parking lot and blink my eyes clear in the morning sun. The whole time I’m out there, I’m trying to get the image of that guy outta my head. It was his eyes. When he saw me, they went so wide that they seemed to almost bulge out of his skull like little marbles. He looked downright scared. Not *I’m embarrassed to be caught dressed like this* scared.

No.

He was more like *this is it, this is how I die* scared.

His room was 408, so I look up at the hotel and start counting out the rooms and floors trying to spot the right window. My eyes reach the fourth storey when I see a flurry of motion among one of the room’s blinds. It’s probably just the housekeepers moving things around. Then again, maybe someone was up there looking down at me, stepping back quickly out of sight when I turned their way?

I tell myself that’s a silly thing to think, but I stare a little closer at the hotel anyway, with all its windows like eyes. Each one a room. Most empty. A few occupied. Lights off. Lights on. Some beds neatly made. Others with duvets pulled back in a messy tangle. Motes of dust drifting in the light as unseen figures walk around. Getting dressed or undressed. Pacing anxiously towards the window, then using two fingers to pull apart the blinds and spy on me from the shadows.

I’m alone in this lot. There *is* a parked car nearby. The only other. But it’s stationary with neither a passenger or driver. I’m pretty sure it belongs to the man in room 408. 

*Where is he?* I wonder. *Why hasn’t he left?* When I look for some sign of him, the world behind me seems to hold its breath. There’s nothing here except an empty road—its cracked tarmac dotted with some half-dead weeds—and a small stretch of overgrown and neglected grassland. I’m looking that way when I hear a dog whine somewhere in its depths. It’s a sad sound full of pain and fear, and I wait anxiously hoping to spot some sign of the animal. A wagging tail. A concerned dog owner. But there’s only waist-high grass that rustles dryly in the wind. 

People sometimes dump unwanted dogs around these parts. It’d be real easy for one to get clipped by a passing car and go running for shelter. When whole minutes pass with no sign of the animal except its pained and plaintive cries, I go and explore the small wilderness on foot. 

It’s quiet, but the dog’s whining is persistent and ghostly. Hard to track by sound alone. Eventually, I stumble across a small ditch that’s just a couple feet deep. It looks like it’s used to drain excess rainwater into the wilderness. I step down into it and hear the dog’s cries more clearly than before. There is a storm pipe on one end of the ditch, likely helping to dispose of the water gathered by all the storm drains along the nearby road and highway. But it’s been a dry couple months and the entrance is choked with weeds and pitch-black shadows. I walk towards it slowly but when I’m a few feet away, the whining stops and is replaced by a low growling. It doesn’t sound like a dog. Not anymore. So I stop and focus all my attention on the inky darkness, hoping my eyes might adjust and let me see a little more clearly without having to get any closer.

There’s something in there. 

I see it. I see fur. It isn’t a dog…

*“What are you doing!?”*

I cry out in terror and jump away from the storm pipe. Standing at the top of the ditch is the man from room 408. He’s looking down at me, angry but afraid. Gut instinct makes me step away from him and his wild furtive eyes. 

“I’m sorry can I–”

“What are you looking for?” he asks while stepping down into the ditch. He walks towards me and my heart races. I pray to God that I’ve somehow just misunderstood a confusing situation. He doesn’t look like he’ll hurt me, but I don’t know him and I don’t know what he wants. He looks scared, which is somehow worse than if he was just plain angry. Thankfully, before he has a chance to get any closer, I hear a familiar voice. 

“Lisa?”

It’s Dad. He’s shouting for me. The man from 408 and I both look away and I take the opportunity to run. Within seconds I’m back at ground level and in Dad’s line of sight. 

“Lisa what are you doing over there?” He shouts when he spots me coming from the grassland 

“Nothing,” I cry while hurrying towards him. When I look back the small man is still in the ditch and looking at me. 

His expression is that of someone whose world is ending.

The next day I come to work and see a dozen police cars in the lot. Callie is being led out of the hotel by her Mom who has her wrapped up in a big coat. Behind them, reception is full of police, paramedics, and a couple guys head-to-toe in what looks like hazmat suits. I hurry towards Callie and begin to speak when her mom interrupts and tells me all about the day’s events.

“They think there’s been a murder on the fourth floor,” she says. “They’ve spent all day asking Callie about it, but she only found the room after the gentleman who was staying in it didn’t check out on time. Eventually had to go in using the master key. Poor thing. She says it was just *awful.*”

“Room 416. It was a fucking bloodbath Lisa,” Callie says with a sniffling sob.

“There there now,” her mom says while patting her on the shoulder. “Try not to think about it.”

Callie manages a dark laugh. 

“It’s all I *can* think about, Mom.”

Her mother nods sadly then continues telling me: “They’ve spent all day speaking to Callie. But really, honey, it’s you they want to speak to.”

“Why?” I ask. “Why me?”

“Well it happened during your shift, ” She answers. “During the night. And they’re pretty sure you’re the one who checked him out.”

“What?” I snap like I’ve been accused of something. “Who?”

“The murderer,” she tells me like it’s so obvious. “They kept asking Callie about this one guest and eventually the officer slipped up and revealed they think they know who did it.”

“It was that creep you told me about,” Callie says with another sniff. “The one in all the leather. Apparently he came here to meet some guy he’d been chatting to online. They think it went wrong and one of them ended up dead.”

“Anyway,” her mother says in a comforting tone. “You should go on and speak to them. I wish there was something I could do to make this better. But the police were very good with Callie. Just remember to be honest and tell them everything you remember and if you’re lucky, Mr Morrison will let you take the rest of the day off for stress.”

She smiles, hugs me, then goes to leave but stops just before pulling her car door shut. 

“Make sure you wait for your father *inside* today,” she tells me with a gentle but firm tone. “They haven’t actually arrested anyone yet, and they’ve got cruisers going up and down the highway this very instant looking for him. He could be anywhere, okay? So stay safe.”

“You sure you’re okay doing this?” I ask Callie as she lingers by the reception desk. It’s been a while since we got the noise complaint, and she still hasn’t followed up.

“Yeah,” she replies with a hurried nod. “Of course I am. Why wouldn’t I be?”

“It’s the fourth floor,” I say.

“So?” She replies. “It’s been like three weeks. It’s not like I found a body or anything.”

“I mean, Cal, you basically did,” I reply. I never saw room 416 myself, but I did catch a glimpse of some of the sheets getting taken out by the police and they were dripping wet and red like someone had tipped cranberry juice all over them.

Technically, we’re allowed to rent the room out as usual now. Our manager even paid for proper crime scene cleaners to come out and take care of it. But the hotel’s never that busy, so I’ve avoided checking anyone into it. I couldn’t bring myself to let someone stay in that room knowing the whole place was a blood bath just a couple weeks ago.

“Why don’t I go?” I ask. 

“No you did them all last time we worked together, including that drunk guy throwing up everywhere,” Callie says, but I can tell her heart isn’t it.

“Don’t worry about it,” I say while grabbing the room key from the back and making for the elevator. “I’ll go check it out. You stay here.”

When I reach the fourth floor I step out of the doors and look at the nearest room number then slap my head and quietly mutter, 

“Dumbass.”

I march half-way down the corridor towards the nearest service phone and dial reception. Callie quickly answers:

“Lett’s reception how may I–”

“I forgot the room number!” I cry and both of us immediately burst into laughter. 

“Jesus Lisa, you took the complaint!”

“Just check the notepad by the desk,” I tell her. “I wrote it down.”

“Okay okay two minutes…”

I hear some shuffling through the receiver but that’s not all. There’s a loud thump from the North end of the corridor. I’m still facing that direction when Callie returns.

“Room 402,” she tells me. 

I look back towards the elevator and the South end of the hotel. That’s where Room 402 lies. 

“Oh,” I say surprised. “You sure?”

There’s another thump from the North end, and this time it’s so loud that I jump a little and my hands clutch the phone so tight the plastic squeaks.

“Uhhh you’ve written, c*omplaint from 404 about next door neighbour*,” Callie answers. “Only occupied room that’s sharing a wall with them is room 402.”

“Alright.” I shrug, then say goodbye and head South. 

The noise complaint is easily resolved. The tired looking man in 404 tells me he’s been kept up since 5am by the shenanigans in 402 and sure enough, when I knock that door I’m faced with a young couple laughing nervously and wrapped in bedsheets. I tell them there’s been some complaint of a disturbance, and the guy goes bright red but his partner just laughs at his embarrassment.

“We’ll keep it down,” the young man says with a nodding head that just promises they’ll be on their best behaviour. When he closes the door I overhear the woman ask him:

“You don’t think they heard *everything*?”

Compared to how noise complaints usually go, this one was surprisingly easy. And after reassuring the man in 402 that things should be better now, I go to leave. 

That’s when I hear another thump from the North end of the hotel. It’s like someone’s rearranging furniture down there. No way the other guests aren’t being bothered by that. And if it’s those bumps and scrapes that the man in 402 has been hearing all along, then I may have just embarrassed that poor couple for nothing.

The elevator arrives with a ding but I don’t enter. Instead, to figure out what’s making those bumps, I go to walk the North corridor. On the left side are the odd numbers, starting at 409 and ending in 419. On the right are the even numbers starting with 408 and ending in 418. I stick to the left and count my way down, stopping at each door to listen carefully.

409

411

413

415

417

I hesitate on the last one. Behind me is 416, which Callie had once described as a *fucking slaughterhouse*. The room where a man had been stabbed or cut or maybe even dismembered by another man I’d spotted the night before, sauntering around in a leather harness and not much else. Neither victim or killer have been found, although as one police officer pointed out to me with strange enthusiasm, this was a rare case where they wouldn’t need a body to charge someone with murder. They had all the evidence they needed soaked deep into the mattress, sheets, comforter, carpet, curtains, and pillows of a two star hotel.

I feel it. I feel it *behind* me. That room. And when I close my eyes I see it as Callie must have seen it that early morning when she opened the door. Sheets of blood dripping from the ceiling in an impossible current. And I swear that the more I think about that room directly behind me, the more I feel as though it might be thinking about me in return. It’s like an itch between my shoulder blades. A pair of eyes burning a hole in my back.

*It hates me*, I think and then quite suddenly–so suddenly that somehow my own thoughts scare me–I correct myself. 

*It wants me.*

I turn, ready to give up on my search, when there is the unmistakable sound of something being dragged from within that room. It is loud and deliberate. I can not convince myself it is coming from the pipes or a nearby faucet. I cannot even convince myself it is coming from rooms 414 or 418 no matter how much I want to. It is coming–it can *only* be coming–from room 416.

There is another dragging sound and this time it is followed by a heavy but soft thump. A new image plays in my mind like a videotape. One I cannot stop or pause or eject.

*A body sliding off a blood soaked mattress and landing on the floor…*

I blink this image away and laugh out loud. These thoughts are childish and stupid and completely irrational but the feelings they inspire are real. So real they could be used as a kind of torture.

Surely there’s nothing in that room. Least of all *that*. Of all the things it might be it can’t be *that!* A blood soaked corpse wrapped in sheets sliding off a mattress to land on a carpeted floor. A skull, rapidly cooling to room temperature, bouncing unexpectedly like a dropped coconut. 

No, it can’t be that.

I take a deep breath and use the master key to open the door, but I do not enter. I examine it from the safety of the corridor where I see that Room 416 is clean and vacant. Another empty hotel room. One of millions all over the world. But that’s only how it *looks*. It doesn’t feel empty or safe. It feels like the cupboard under the stairs of the house I grew up in. The one that scared me senseless as a little six year old, because even though Mom was always in the kitchen nearby and Dad was usually in the yard, a part of me worried that if I went too far into the dark the door would slam shut behind me and I would scream and bang and kick and cry, but the monster in the shadows would find and eat me long before my parents ever came to my rescue.

As a child, those feelings made a strange kind of sense. I mean, I really did think there was a monster under those stairs! Why wouldn’t I be scared of it? But I’m an adult now. And I don’t believe in ghosts. Or monsters. Or much else, really. An empty room can only ever be an empty room. It can’t be haunted or cursed. 

I try to find *something* to help make sense of this drug-like fear. I lean just a little further into the room and stare at random spots hoping to see something I’ve missed. The TV. The bed. The wardrobe. *Nothing.* It’s an empty room, right? And yet it’s like I’m standing on a big red X and there’s a piano dangling over my head only I can’t see it. Or I’m a caveman holding up a chunk of uranium not knowing why my hand hurts. Every second in that doorway is torture, and in the end I get so fed up of being scared of nothing that I barge right in and walk over to the window because I need to find something to do so I can justify finally leaving. Only when I reach the blinds, I part them and look down into the lot and see a man waving at me. 

He is dressed like a dog. 

A brown furry dog. Head to toe. He almost resembles a bear, matter of fact. And even though he is far away, it’s clear he’s got a strange looking mask on. It’s pale brown. Maybe plastic? Maybe leather? I don’t know. But the eyes. Even from all the way up here on fourth, I can see those awful creepy eyes. They are staring right at me and he is waving like a school boy.

Once he is sure he has my attention he jumps up and then crouches down and proceeds to crawl around on his hands and feet. There is something mocking about him. He’s laughing at me isn’t he? He knows I’m up here confused and (yes) *scared* and he finds it funny. Enjoyable. I can’t just look away and he knows that, doesn’t he? He knows that I can’t tear my eyes away from the strange man in matted brown fur, stagnant and stinking and coated in blood so old and dark that it has turned nearly black from weeks spent festering. And still he is prancing around on all fours, stopping every few steps to twist his head to suddenly stare up at me even though he surely can’t see anything except the sunlight bouncing off a distant window.

I snap the blinds shut. 

My hands are shaking as I step back and hurry out of the room. This has been like a nightmare but Callie is real. Reception is real. Those lights are so bright and there’s always music. That place belongs to the real world and I am so desperate to reach it that I actually jog out of room 416 and down the corridor towards the elevator. 

I am passing the service phone when it rings and I let out a yelp so embarrassing my cheeks flush hot and red as I answer it. It must be reception. It must be Callie.

“Callie?” I ask. “Is everything okay?”

“Lisa?” She sounds *terrified*. “Lisa? Did you just leave room 416?”

“Y-y-yes,” I stammer. “I went in because I heard–”

“You were taking your time so I checked the camera,” she says. “I saw you go in and… Lisa, I think room 410 is empty. That’s the one right behind you, yeah?”

I look that way, then reply:

“Yeah but Callie what the hell is going on–” 

“Lisa, you need to go into room 410 right now and lock the door. I’ve already called the police and they’re on the way but honey, please you’ve gotta get somewhere safe and lock the door.”

“What?”

“Lisa you need to get inside and lock the door!” she says firmly, and now her voice is so shaky she can barely get the words out*.* “I can see him on the camera, Lisa. Some guy. He’s been lurking in the corridor and as soon as you left he ran off and Lisa...

“He’s in the elevator waiting for you to come out.”

A shape comes towards me from the end of the corridor. 

He must have heard me. He must have heard the hesitation or anxiety in my voice. The fear. A twitch in the rabbit’s nose that lets the wolf know the chase is on. There are footsteps fast approaching with a steady rhythm but I do not look as I rush for the nearest door. Sweat pricks my skin as a shiver races from the nape of my neck and down past my shoulders, back, chest and stomach before settling in my ankles. My heart skips several beats and I feel like I could fall into the great big void they leave inside my chest.

*How close is he?* My body screams. *How much further? How much longer do we have?*

My hand fumbles for the door handle but it is locked, and grabbing the master key from my back pocket is not easy with my damp and nearly useless hands. After the third time, I let out a desperate and inarticulate cry. A sort of strange and wordless wail that might be funny under other circumstances, but feels tragic and pathetic in the moment.

When I finally get the door open and stumble inside, he is already there and in my periphery. A shadow. A monster. Violence on two legs. I am sobbing like a toddler with a scraped knee when I slam the door shut just in time for him to barrel into it unsuccessfully. The distance between the two moments–the clicking of the latch and his fist pounding against the wood–is terrifyingly close, and I will never feel safe again. Not for the rest of my life. Not knowing how quickly a man walking briskly but quietly can cover a couple dozen feet.

I step back. Breath hitching. Hands shaking. I have peed myself. Only a little, but when I realise my skin flushes hot with anger and embarrassment.

There is an electric hum as the lock begins to open. I throw myself at the door just in time for the handle to depress. It doesn’t matter *how*, but the man has a key. Probably one of the master ones stolen from reception. Thankfully, the intruder is not prepared for every single ounce of my body to be pushing back from the other side. I buy just enough time to slide the chain into place and then shriek in revulsion as his fingers grope hungrily through the open gap.

He shouts and it is monstrous. A dreadful groan full of desperation and rage and terror. But no matter how hard he tries, he cannot reach the chain. After several long seconds his hand slides away and he presses his face through the gap, cheeks turning beetroot red as he squeezes his head as far as it can go.

I recognise him as the man from room 408. The killer who has surely returned to tie up some sort of loose end. I am about to reach for the phone when he speaks. 

“You’ve seen him, haven’t you? The man in the dog suit.”

His face is stained with tears and snot, and his nose and cheeks are so pink I think of a squashed tomato. But when his eyes meet mine my hand freezes. My throat dries up. A different kind of dread begins to settle on me now. It is cold and terrible. A puff of breath exhaled from cracked and drying lips.

“What do you want?” I ask.

“You have to help me,” he moans.

“Why would I help a killer?”

He laughs wetly as he processes some great irony. 

“Yes,” he says. “I *am* a killer, aren’t I? But Marcus was worse. He was always a bastard. A terrible handsome wonderful bastard. But I thought that’s what I deserved and it’s why I went along with his weird little games. You know, one time, he had me dress up as a cat and he actually sent me out into the yard and he chased me wearing…” His face goes suddenly awash with shame. Eyes darting left to right like a typewriter. “Never mind all that,” he says, correcting his line of thinking. “I always thought Marcus was just a sonnofabitch, but then I found these photos with these *people*, all tied up. All… cut up. *Jesus, was I next?* He was so cold and calm when he caught me looking. I don’t think he even thought I might fight back. I think he got so used to hunting prey he’d either drugged or bribed that he didn’t think the real thing would fight back. Isn’t that crazy?” He laughs and it is sad but just a little manic too. “But I *did* fight back and… well, it got out of hand very quickly.”

“I don’t know what you want me to do about any of this,” I tell him.

“After the fight,” he says. “I panicked. I didn’t know what to do so I went to get cleaned up in my room although I didn’t have any fucking clean clothes except that stupid outfit and then I went to check on Marcus and *that’s* when I saw you! You scared me as I was coming out of my room, so I hid and by the time I came back out and went to find Marcus he was gone and he’d taken the photos with him. I *need* those photos. No one will believe me. Marcus had a yacht. A fucking *yacht*. You know what I have? Track marks and Hep C. But if I had the photos, maybe they’d believe my version of events. I just need to know where he took them and you must have seen something! You were there that night and then you were sniffing around that field. Do you know where he is? If I get those photos, maybe then–”

There are sirens in the lot outside and the man falls quiet. There is a muted thump as he leans against the locked door and slides down onto the floor.

“Please,” he says in one final desperate plea before a parade of armed police arrive to arrest him. “It isn’t just the photos. I killed him. I *killed* him dead. But now I keep seeing him over and over and over! He finds it fun doing this to me. He knows they’ll fry me unless I get those photos and he thinks it’s so fucking funny! But it isn’t just me he’s been keeping an eye on, is it?

*“His stink is all over you too.”*
