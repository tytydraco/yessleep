I don't know how to explain any of this without making it sound ridiculous, so I'm going to start with the parts that were normal. I'm writing this from a guest room I'm not allowed to leave without permission. The Wi-Fi here is better than it ever was in our apartment, which is the only reason I can post this at all.

My three friends and I shared a place in the city. We weren't particularly adventurous people. We had jobs, places to be, people to meet, and the usual problems that come with being young adults sharing a living space. Mira lived two floors down from us. She was part of our little group, although she was probably the most reserved among us, and we'd known her for a few years.

One evening, she told us that someone had invited her to visit. She showed us the card. It was heavy, cream-coloured and completely blank except for an address in the old part of the city and one line printed underneath: "Upper residence. Your visit has been approved." We laughed at first, because we assumed she was joking. When we asked who had sent it, she wouldn't tell us much. She just said it was an opportunity she couldn't refuse. We asked when she'd be back, and she smiled before saying she didn't know. Then she left.

We didn't hear from her again. At first, we assumed she was busy, but a few days passed, followed by a week, and eventually we started getting worried. Her apartment was empty, her phone didn't work, and nobody seemed to know where she'd gone. We called the number we found on the back of the card, which none of us had noticed at first. A woman answered and told us that Mira's visit had been approved and that everything was fine. That was it. Everything was fine. Then she hung up, and the number never connected again. We went to the police, who took a report and clearly didn't think much of it. After a few more days of getting nowhere, the four of us decided to go to the address ourselves. In hindsight, that was probably the stupidest decision we ever made, but we were young, and one of our closest friends had disappeared. We weren't going to sit around waiting for someone to tell us she was fine.

The address was an apartment block I must have walked past a hundred times without noticing. Twelve storeys, grey concrete, a lobby with mailboxes and a dead plant. Nothing about it was strange except the elevator. The panel had buttons from one to twelve, and above them, a column of unlabelled buttons that ran almost to the ceiling. One of my friends pressed the top one. Nothing happened. Then he pressed the one just above twelve, and the doors closed.

The elevator went up for far longer than it should have. When the doors opened, we weren't on the thirteenth floor of anything. We were looking out at a street. There were apartment complexes, shops and roads, and people going about their lives, but there were trees everywhere. Enormous trees grew through old buildings, their roots breaking through concrete and their vines covering abandoned structures. There was a sky above it. Not a ceiling. A sky.

We stood there for a long time. A woman walking past glanced at us, then at the elevator, and kept going as if people stepped out of it all the time. Later, we learned that the people who lived there called the place the Tower, although I'm not sure that was its actual name. It stretched so far upwards that nobody had ever seen its roof. The building we'd walked into was, as far as I can tell, just one of the doors at the bottom of it.

The strange thing about the Tower was that every floor had its own environment. I don't mean different decorations or architecture; I mean entirely different ecosystems. The people we spoke to on that first floor told us that some floors had forests that stretched beyond the horizon, while others had enormous bodies of water, strange vegetation and creatures that looked like something out of a biology textbook written by someone who had never seen Earth. Even the skies were different. Some floors had perfectly ordinary blue skies, others had permanent sunsets, and one floor supposedly had three moons, although I never saw it myself. Nobody knew how many floors existed, and nobody seemed particularly interested in finding out.

There were elevators everywhere, and they told us that not every floor was safe. There were stories about people getting into elevators and never coming back. Others returned with injuries, strange illnesses or stories so unbelievable that nobody took them seriously. Most people simply stayed where they lived. The wealthier populations occupied the higher floors, where they had better infrastructure, better facilities and, apparently, considerably more knowledge about the Tower. They rarely interacted with anyone from below. When we showed a man Mira's card, he looked at the words "upper residence" and told us, very politely, to go home.

We didn't. We found an elevator that could take us considerably higher. The first few floors were uneventful. We passed through what looked like an industrial district, then a residential area with architecture unlike anything we'd seen before. Eventually, the elevator opened into a forest, and I remember stepping out and just standing there. The trees were enormous, their trunks wider than houses, and their branches disappeared into a sky that looked almost purple. There was a faint smell of rain, and we could hear something moving in the distance. We decided not to stay and continued upwards.

I don't remember the exact number of floors we visited. There were too many, and eventually the floor indicators stopped making much sense. Some elevators had buttons that weren't labelled with numbers, while others had no buttons at all, just a small screen that displayed a destination after the doors closed. We began to realise that the Tower was considerably larger than we'd imagined, and we were nowhere near the top. The first real problem started when we entered a floor that appeared to be abandoned. There were buildings, roads and what looked like an old shopping district, but there wasn't a single person around. The vegetation was strange, too. It wasn't like the overgrown streets on the first floor we'd seen. Thin, pale tendrils covered the walls and ceilings, almost like roots, and they seemed to grow in places where there shouldn't have been enough light or moisture to sustain anything.

One of my friends suggested we turn back, but before anyone could respond, we heard a metallic scraping sound coming from somewhere above us. Three slow scrapes, a pause, then three more. At first, I thought it was a person crawling through the ventilation ducts. Then I saw the ceiling bend slightly beneath its weight. Whatever it was, it was large, and it was moving towards us. We started walking faster, but the scraping followed us. When we reached the elevator, the doors wouldn't open. We pressed the button repeatedly, but nothing happened. Then something slammed against the inside of the ventilation shaft, and one of my friends shouted that we needed to run.

We ran through the corridors, looking for another elevator, and I remember the sound of metal tearing behind us. When I looked over my shoulder, I saw something emerge from a ventilation opening. I can't adequately describe it. It had too many limbs, and they didn't seem to bend in the right directions. Its body was pale and narrow, almost skeletal, with a head that seemed too small for its size. It moved incredibly quickly, and whatever noise it made sounded like metal being dragged across concrete. We eventually found a maintenance entrance and climbed inside. The passage was narrow, dark and filled with cables, but at least it gave us somewhere to hide. We crawled for what felt like an eternity, listening to the thing move outside and scrape along the walls. One of my friends was breathing so heavily that I thought he was going to pass out.

At one point, the creature stopped directly above us. I could see its limbs through a gap in the ventilation grille, barely moving as it seemed to listen. It stayed there for several seconds before finally moving away. We didn't move until the sound had completely disappeared. Eventually, we reached another elevator shaft and managed to get inside a functioning lift. Nobody spoke for a while. We were terrified, exhausted and completely lost, but we were also closer to the destination. We knew because the elevators had started behaving differently. The floors were cleaner, the architecture was more elaborate, and the people we occasionally encountered wore clothes that looked impossibly expensive. Nobody seemed surprised to see us. They just looked at us with mild curiosity, as if four exhausted men climbing out of a maintenance elevator wasn't particularly unusual.

Then we reached a floor that was unlike anything we'd seen before. There were no forests, no abandoned buildings and no strange creatures wandering around. Everything was immaculate. The streets were enormous, the buildings impossibly tall, and the sky was a brilliant shade of blue. There were gardens, fountains and structures made from materials I couldn't identify. It felt like an entirely different world, almost as though we'd stepped into a place that had been designed to exist independently of everything below it. We asked someone where we could find the residential estate associated with Mira's invitation. The man glanced at the woman beside him before asking who we were. We explained that we were friends of someone who had recently arrived from below. He didn't ask any more questions. He simply pointed us towards a building at the end of the street.

It was an apartment building, technically, although calling it that felt wrong. It was taller than anything back in our city, and the lobby alone was bigger than our entire apartment. We walked through its entrance and were immediately stopped by security. We explained ourselves again, but this time something changed. One of the guards recognised Mira's name. He looked at us for a moment before speaking into a small communication device, and a few minutes later someone came to escort us upstairs. A private elevator took us to the top of the building and opened directly into an apartment that took up the entire floor. We were led past enormous paintings and rooms that looked like they belonged in a museum, and into a living room with windows that filled an entire wall. And there she was. Mira was standing beside a woman who looked human, at least at first glance. She was tall and dressed simply in white, in the kind of plain, expensive clothes that made everything we were wearing look like rags. The guards who had escorted us stopped at the door and wouldn't come any further. None of them looked at her directly.

Mira looked at us, and for a moment she seemed genuinely surprised. Then she smiled. We started asking questions immediately. Where had she been? Why hadn't she contacted us? Why had nobody told us where she was? She tried to answer, but the woman beside her raised a hand, and everyone went quiet. She looked directly at us and asked, "These are the friends you told me about?" Mira nodded. The woman smiled, although it wasn't a particularly warm smile, before saying, "How wonderful. I was beginning to think you wouldn't come." We didn't know what to say. One of my friends eventually asked who she was, and Mira looked at us before saying, "This is my mother."

Nobody spoke. In all the time we'd known Mira, she had never once mentioned her family. We'd always assumed there wasn't one, and none of us had ever thought to ask. The woman approached us and looked at each of us individually, as though inspecting something she'd ordered. Then she said, "You've travelled quite far. I hope the journey wasn't too unpleasant." I wanted to ask her why Mira had been living so far below. I wanted to ask why she hadn't told us who she was. I wanted to ask what that thing in the ventilation system had been. Instead, I asked the only question that seemed important at the time.

"Why didn't she come back?"

Her mother looked at Mira, and for the first time since we'd arrived, her expression changed. It wasn't anger or sadness. It was something closer to disappointment. "Because," she said, "there is something you don't understand about the place you call home." She walked towards one of the enormous windows, beyond which I could see the city stretching into the distance. Then, far above us, I noticed something. Another floor. Not a ceiling, not clouds, but another world suspended above ours. She continued, explaining that the few people from our side who ever found their way in always assumed the same thing: that the Tower was something separate, a strange place hidden behind a few elevators in a few old buildings, and that our lives down there had nothing to do with it. She turned towards us and said, "That is not entirely true."

One of my friends asked what she meant, but Mira's mother didn't answer immediately. Instead, she looked at Mira and said, "Tell them." Mira looked at us, and I swear, for the first time since we'd known her, she looked frightened.

"Every floor holds up the one above it," she said quietly. "Not the building. The people. The bottom one is yours. Your city. Your street. Our old building. All of it holds this up. Every generation, someone from up here is sent down to live with you, to find out who down there would come looking for them." She swallowed. "The invitation was never for me. It was for whoever followed it."

"You shouldn't have come looking for me," she said.

I remember feeling a chill run down my spine, not because of the creature we'd encountered or because of her mother, but because of the way Mira said it. Our journey hadn't been a rescue mission at all. We'd walked into something that had been waiting for us.

Her mother smiled again. "Well, now that you're here, I suppose there's no point sending you back." I looked towards the entrance. The guards were still standing there, and none of them had moved. Then I noticed something on the wall behind her. It was a map showing the floors of the Tower. There were hundreds of them, perhaps thousands, and at the very top was a section that had been deliberately left blank. I asked what was there. She followed my gaze and remained silent for a moment before replying, "Something even I have never seen."

That was the last conversation we had before being given rooms in the building. We were told that we were guests, that we were safe, and that we were free to explore the floor. There was only one instruction: we were not to use the elevators without permission. When I asked why, the guard escorting us simply said, "Because some floors know when you've arrived."

I haven't slept properly since. I still haven't found out what happened to the thing that chased us, and I don't know what Mira's mother means to do with us. But last night, while I was lying in bed, I heard something scraping inside the ventilation system. Three slow scrapes, a pause, then three more. I tried convincing myself that it was just the building settling, or some maintenance worker moving through the ducts.

This morning, there were thin, pale tendrils growing out from the edges of the vent above my bed.
