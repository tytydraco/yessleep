CW: Domestic Violence, Self harm, Suicide and child abuse. 

Damien Telle let each of his family member’s mouths lowly foam up. He had poisoned the chicken soup they were having with a near fatal amount of rat poison. After each of them had passed out and he assumed they died, he walked onto his front garden, called the police and slit his wrists.

His suicide note was 50 pages long.

I didn’t read it. I think only his wife and the police did. Put it was next to his corpse when the police arrived. 

I write this as plainly as possible as the horror damien put his family through should be expressed in the most simple wording possible. As in every conceivable way, Damien ruined his families lives. Burdened them with the trauma that will way on them for years to come and by taking his own life took away his children’s father. 

*However.*

The three final times I interacted with Damien, have played on my mind for the four weeks since the incident.  I assumed he didn’t have any prior difficulties in mental health, I still believe that to be true. But I think there was something else going on. 

We spoke on Monday. 

The town’s post office often had a large line, you could spend hours waiting to send the smallest of packages. That’s where I saw him, at the end of the line, fidgeting and flipping through half a dozen brown packages.

“Damien, how long have you been here for?” I asked tapping him on the shoulder.

His head whipped round in a frightened yet accusatory way.

“Oh…” He said his expression settled.

“I haven’t been here long.” He continued, he held his gaze a little too long. 

“Ah, cool.” I tried to end the conversation, It seemed he wasn’t in the mood to talk.

“I’m not just waiting around here for no reason. Okay?” He said with an air of sterness, he wiped snot from his nose with his sleeve.

“Oh I know. It’s a post office. You’re cool dude.” I assured him.

“I’m sending important stuff. Okay?” He told me that I wouldn’t believe him.

I chuckled a little. “What are you sending?” 

“Newspapers and…” he looked out the post office’s window before continuing in a lower tone. “Photo of these cars.”

“Oh… Uh… Thinking of swapping out the station wagon?” I asked, I shouldn’t asked.

“Hah, no. No. I’m not.” He ripped open one of the packages, pulling a page of a newspaper it’s headline reading “OUTBREAK IN SINGAPORE" and a small stack of paper with photos of cars printed scattered across them. “Look.” He pointed at a grey ford fiesta. “This one. It passes by my house and my office.” he pointed to another car on the paper. “This one- always has five people inside. And it’s like if they were a family then maybe you’d see three or four. But it’s always five.” His manner had become a little more composed, like he was ahead of the curve and I wasn’t.

I just nodded my head in an admittedly blunt attempt in ending the conversation. He huffed. Throughout our conversation the line slowly progressed with him finally making his way to the counter.

The clerk looked up from her monitor and sighed. “Sir, I don’t know what to tell you. They’ll bounce. Or you’ll get another one of those letters.”

“I’m paying. Send them. Okay? It’s my right to use the postal service.” He snapped back. She rolled her eyes and took the packages. He paid and stormed out, coughing a beleaguered drained cough into his hands.

The Telle’s had a motion sensing light in their back garden. It’d only switch on when someone was in the garden for a prolonged period of time. If they got out in the middle of the night the light would permeate through my blinds and keep me awake. 

But it wasn’t the light that kept me awake that night. It was the fighting. Muffled by our walls I could faintly hear shouts of:

“...AND YOU’RE THE WORST OF THEM. MY OWN FUCKING WIFE!”

And a woman’s calming assuring tone.

“Honey, please calm down, you'll wake the kids.”

“ARE THEY EVEN MINE!?” Damien responded.

The light switched on and I left my slightly groggy state. I got out of bed and peaked through the blinds. The Telle’s had a workshop/bar Damien had put together over the past summer, I saw him walk into it and slam the door behind it.  
I headed to the bathroom, my head was killing me and I needed to take an aspirin. The light was already on and the window was open. I could’ve sworn I shut it. I ran the faucet and filled a glass of water.

I looked out the window, it faced the teller's window that looked onto their staircase which led to a landing with three doors. I saw Damien’s wife, Nancy walking up the stairs in her pajamas. She did have an expression on her face, it didn’t look like she was in thought or even had the kind of face that would show she was used to this kind of thing. It was just emotionless.

She got to her bedroom door and tried the handle, flopping her hand against it. She tried again, eyes fixed on it. It just bounced back, there wasn’t enough force to press it down fully. She stopped. Turned her head and looked at me.

She smiled and shrugged in a “One of those’s nights” kind of way. Then opened the door and walked into the room.

I took the aspirin and went to bed.

The morning after I had the day off from work so I planned on doing some maintenance on my car. I opened the hood and got to work. I saw Nancy and the children call into the house, shut the door and walk to their car.  She drove away, not long after so did Damien in a coughing fit as he walked to his car. He nodded at me as he left with a slightly more composed look to when I had seen him last. 

I continued working on the car. As I closed the hood I saw Nancy pull into the top of our road. It had only been 2 minutes since she had left. I could make out their eldest son sitting in the front seat, he tugged on her arm and said something to her that I obviously couldn’t hear. 

She stopped the car dead in it’s tracks. 

I looked behind me, no one else was on the road. Just me and the telle’s. I felt out of place so I went back inside. Moments after I shut the door I heard them pull into their driveway. I stayed by my front door and looked through my peephole, I could just see Nancy leaving. She returned seconds later with five ruck sacks? Five rucksacks? Why the hell did they need five ruck sacks? And they were all full!

I’d get like one or two if the kids had forgotten them.  But five? There were only three people in the car?

The fighting continued that night.

I didn’t hear Nancy’s side. Only Damien’s. 

“What do you want from me. Huh?! What! 10 fucking years down the drain!”

“You’ve got some fucking nerve.”

No more shouting. I slowly drifted to sleep.

***KNOCK KNOCK.*** 

My eyes snapped open.

**DING DONG DING DONG DING DING DING** 

Was he at my front door?

I slowly walked downstairs.

A silhouette against the front door’s window.

“Hello?” I called out. 

“Jack? You up?” Damien’s voice came from behind the door.

“Yeah but y’know. I got work in the morning… Early start… I should get back to-” I begun but Damien cut me off.

“Please man. I know we don’t speak often. But- I’m going through some real deep shit right now. I need to talk to someone.” He said his voice raspy.

I opened the door. He was sweaty, soaking through his shirt. His eyes where red but I don’t know if that was from crying.

“Thanks man.” He said quietly as he entered, he had a pile of newspapers and scrap paper.

I led him to the living room, offered him a beer which he finished before I sat down.

He began plucking out newspapers from the stack, carefully, like he was making a one of a kind conspiratorial collage on the living room floor.

I don’t remember a lot of it, believe me. I wish I did. But when someone’s in that kind of state you care more about how their feeling than what their saying.

He made illusions to outbreaks of a flu in Singapore. suicide and domestic violence rates skyrocketing in Malaysia.

He told me about his trip to Malaysia. How he felt everyone watching him, black vans emptying men in jumpsuits into buildings neighbouring his hotel.

I looked up from the makeshift conspiracy board.

“My brother, he’s yanno kinder than me, he’s a therapist.” I said in the most reassuring tone I could muster.

Damien rubbed his face in disappointment, sweat wiped off but was soon replaced.

“He’s good, really good. And he takes things seriously, deals with stuff people are going through. He can help.” I continued.

“I dunno man-“ Damien said, suddenly interrupted.

**RATTLE RATTLE**

“The fuck was that?” He snapped his head round to the garden.

The noise did come from the garden, as it happened I noticed our shared rather flimsy fence shake. I didn’t want to set him off, I tried to ignore it.

“I can call him now. He can come over, you can talk him through all of this.” I said.

“Shh- you hear that” he put his hands out to call for silence.

“Listen it’s probably one the neighbourhood cats.” I started, he stopped me.

“Shutthefuckup” he shot in a whisper.

It was the quietest room I had ever been in, I heard wind shake the branches of a tree.

And the unmistakable sound of footsteps on gravel, 

Something was coming down the gravel pathway between my house and the fence.

“Again, it’s probably one of the cats-“ I whispered.

He didn’t take his eyes of the window that led to the garden. Neither did I as a figure emerged from the shadow.

Nancy.

“OhnoOhnofuckohno.” Damien rambled.

I got up to let her in from the back door, slowly.

“Don’t, please, please man don’t do it” he begged his voice dripping with fear. 

I opened the door, to a remorseful Nancy she spoke.

“Hey.” It was quiet, shameful.

“She’s fucking part of this man. They’re fucking part of this! She’ll-you jumped the fucking fence didn’t you. Your so fucking weird!” He was frantic, shakey, no ounce of calm in him.

Nancy blinked back tears. “Jack. Is your brother still a therapist?” She steeled herself.

“Yeah, I do.” I answered letting her in.

“Could you maybe grab your phone? And me and Damien will talk and let you know what we want you to do?” She looked to Damien he was near hyperventilating his chest rising and falling till he was interrupted by a cough.

“Please don’t go upstairs Jack.” He begged me like it was the thing he’d ever do.

I walked out the room and went upstairs but waited at the top. I listened through snotty sniffs.

I heard talking, low quiet, whispers. 

I grabbed my phone and headed back into the living room. 

They where both sitting again, Damien wiped his tears.

“We think we’re gonna leave it for tonight Jack.” Nancy said like a politician.

I nodded. 

They left through the front door. Damien looked back at me like I was sending him to death row. 

That was the last time I saw him.

I hope his children get better, I hope Nancy can move on.

I hope Damien was wrong, that his wife could be trusted. 

That he was just another person who lost his mind in a sea of so many who already did. 

I hope so, as Jesus man I’m feeling sick.
