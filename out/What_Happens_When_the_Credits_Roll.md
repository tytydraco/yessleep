*Meet me at the place of our first dance tonight at 3:13AM. You need to see something. Yours Forever - Alice*

The handwriting was unmistakably Alice’s, complete with her signature flourishes and the scent of her favorite perfume. There was only one problem: she had been dead for a month.

Days after her 30th birthday, she wrapped her Jeep around a tree. Because of our secret tryst, I couldn’t even visit the hospital before she died, forced to mourn from a distance at her funeral. Yet here I was, standing at the rusted gates of the old abandoned gymnasium of our high school.

It’s where we had our first dance. Young and in love, we thought we would always be together, then college happened. She left, and I stayed behind.

Feeling like a fool in the empty lot, I turned to leave. Suddenly, a deafening screech of feedback came from inside. “I’m glad you could make it,” Alice’s voice crackled through the speakers.

I pushed the rusted doors open and entered the dilapidated gymnasium. The basketball court was littered with beer bottles, and the walls were covered with crude graffiti. The bleachers sagged under dozens of televisions, every type from the ones you see in pictures from the 1960s to ones that I could buy at a store today. They looked like they could collapse at any moment.

But something else caught my eye.

The back wall, towards the locker room, had a tower made of these TV sets. Each one had her beautiful almond-shaped eyes and that smile which made my knees tremble for over fifteen years.

As I approached the tower, I could almost feel her staring at me. “Come closer. I want to see you.”

“I don’t know what sort of sick joke this is,” I screamed. “Show yourself!”

“It’s me,” she cried out from inside the televisions. “I just want us to be together.”

Something about the tone of her voice, even through the crackling speakers and glowing screens, made tears well up in my eyes as I collapsed to my knees staring at the tower. “We can’t be together, Alice. You’re dead.”

A cigarette burn flashed across the screens, and the scene shifted. Alice, her jaw nearly ripped off, hung limply from the steering wheel of her Jeep Rubicon, blood pouring down her like a waterfall, her eyes dead. I staggered to my feet, knees locked, staring at the woman I had fallen in love with. Seeing her in such a condition made my stomach turn. As I stared, longing for her touch, her eyes moved, cutting quickly over as if she was staring at me.

“Who the fuck would do this?” I screamed. Then the screen flickered again. Happier times. The night we kissed again for the first time, behind my bar, quite passionately. I could almost taste her.

It made me pause before cigarette burns appeared on the screen again, burning through the scene, revealing weird symbols and words I couldn’t even begin to pronounce. I moved closer to the tower. Dozens of orange extension cords and surge protectors snaked down its sides.

I looked up again. She was smiling at me from every screen. “Tell me, do you still love me?”

I pressed my hand to one of the screens and felt the static coming off of it. “Of course, I do.”

“Then you’ll do whatever it takes to see me again.”

My eyes wandered around, tracing the cables. One loose orange cable seemed to be the source of power for all of it. I wanted to follow it. It seemed to be heading back into the locker rooms.

As I grabbed it and started to follow, a loud piercing noise came through every television in the gym. It sounded like an emergency broadcast with every speaker in the room maxed out.

It was painful. I covered my ears and stared at the screens, which had all turned to static, the old cracks and pops growing even more unbearably loud. I could feel my ears ringing and pressure building in my head as I backed away from the tower.

But it suddenly stopped. Every screen went dim as I stood in the dark gym.

Then the tower flickered to a new scene. Alice and I were embracing outside my bar.

“The divorce will be finalized after the holidays,” she promised on screen, rubbing my face. “Then let’s get the hell out of town.”

The camera panned back, revealing her husband watching us from a parked car. My heart sank. He knew. The film cut rapidly to him handing her a gift, then to Alice driving, followed by the sickening sound of screeching tires and twisting metal.

Suddenly, the screens flashed white again. Alice’s tear-streaked face appeared in a close-up, staring directly at me.

“It wasn’t an accident,” she sobbed. “He planned it and killed me.”

I stumbled back from the screens. “What do you want from me?” I shouted.

“I just need one thing,” she whispered.

“What is it?” I yelled.

The feed cut to live footage of me standing in the gym, mimicking my every move. Every time I turned my head, the man on screen turned his a half-second before I did.

It had to be a trick. This cruel joke had gone too far. I marched back to the tower and grabbed the extension cord, following it into the dark locker room.

I tugged harder and felt it go slack. I pulled the end free. It had been cut clean through. There was no way this could have been powering all the televisions.

I heard a groan from somewhere inside. I wandered around, unable to see anything, until a buzzing sound and the flicker of another television cut through the dark. The dim light poured down to show Alice’s husband sitting in a circle made of the same symbols that had briefly flashed across the screens.

Her face appeared through the static. “You know what to do.”

He was barely conscious, his breathing labored, but I noticed a knife placed close to his body. As I looked back to the small screen, her beautiful face slowly faded away into the darkness.

Then credits with all our names started to roll.
