It was a chill, Autumn evening in our small Midwest town. The sky illuminated in the twilight. Blood red and golden orange leaves scattered the ground while trees stood barren in remembrance. It was after school, and our neighborhood ran back and forth in a game of hide-and-go-seek. Unfortunately, I was never good at this game. I’m just not very sneaky. It sucks always losing, but I didn't want to be left out, so I played anyway. 

Today, I was feeling pretty good about my chances. My energy was high and I wanted to impress. Filled with anxious adrenaline, I rushed to find a spot. I decided I would hide in the tree line that bordered the Westside of the neighborhood. The woods were dense, so I knew I could easily find a hiding spot there. It *technically* wasn’t out of bounds; it was just a bit of a grey area. I felt this would work in my favor. All I had to do was pass through a couple old properties to get there. 

One of the properties was owned by an old man that rarely left the house. The other he owned, but he had abandoned for quite some time. The abandoned house was heavily weathered, paint stripped from most of the outside. It leaned, separating from its foundation as the earth beneath it slowly shifted. Generations of foliage surrounded the property. Overgrown or already dead, the grass and weeds towered high overhead. It wasn’t well-kept, like the house the man lived in, and I had never seen it occupied. It was strange how drastically different the two were. It was as if he had entirely given up on the abandoned house and left it there to crumble, but year after year he allowed it to remain intact. To stand but be forgotten.

The old man was rather senile. No one really cared about the eye-sore he let manifest. In fact, no one really cared about him at all. He made it obvious a few years back to stay away. He threatened to shoot anyone that'd step on his property. It was his way of warning us. Because of this, our parents told us to stay away. He was crazy and we didn’t want any trouble. He left us alone and we left him alone. That was the unspoken rule. Until tonight. 

I KNEW it was a bad idea, but truthfully, the excitement was too much. The amount of respect I’d get for passing through the old man's property would surpass all the embarrassment of my losses. I’d be a neighborhood legend. “*The balls on this guy, huh?!”* I could hear them cooing already. I figured I’d wrap quickly around the abandoned property, avoiding the old man’s house. The weeds were high enough to hide me as I ran through, so I didn’t think much of it. How dangerous could it really be? 

The game begins, everyone running in opposite directions. There's 12 of us, but there was more than enough room for us to hide sporadically throughout the neighborhood. I split away from everyone, heading towards the tree line. Heading towards the abandoned house. As I approach the tall grass surrounding the property, I glance over at the old man’s window, next door. The lights aren't on in the house, but it's just light enough and just dark enough for me to peer inside. I can see him, sitting in a reclining chair, drinking a beer and watching TV. I hurriedly sneak into the tall grass.

I'm quickly surrounded as I wander. I realized I had underestimated how badly it would block my vision. It was nearing dark and I couldn’t see more than a few inches in front of me. The property was big enough to have difficulty navigating in this case. I slowly began to panic. I felt claustrophobic and lost at the same time. I was walking in circles and being swallowed by the grass. On top of all of this came an irrational sensation… It felt like I was being watched. I couldn’t explain why, maybe I was just paranoid, but it felt like I was a rat in a laboratory maze, being studied. Observed. Like the old man was laughing at my foolish mistake. There was no laughter though. It was dead silent besides my heavy breathing and the movement of weeds as I pushed them out of the way.

I began to increase my pace, as my fear of being found had escalated to dread. If I can run in one direction, I have to be able to find my way out… I knew the house lay west of the powerlines, so I had to go that way. As I periodically looked up for reference, I began practically jumping through the weeds when, *THUD*. I hit the ground. Hard. My foot had been snagged by what appeared to be an overgrown root. Upon falling I put my hands out to protect me from the sharp, uneven and rocky ground. “AEGHHH!”. I cried out in pain. Crows dissipated from the powerlines hanging above… My hands were severely cut. One wrist felt possibly broken, as it folded under the impact. I needed to get out, but I couldn’t move. My body, still aching and weak, struggled to regain strength. As I try to stand up, my ankle recoils, still caught. Pulling with urgency, I could feel the grip tightening. Not a root, but a rope. I was caught…and that's when I heard the door open.

   
I hoped it was my imagination, but it wasn’t. I had never been surer of anything. I heard the door creak. I was fucked and I knew it. I wasn’t sure what to think at that exact moment, my brain just stirred with different outcomes. *Would the old man yell at me? Would he tell my parents? Would he call the police for trespassing? Would he really just kill me*? I was frozen in fear and wincing in pain. I have to lay low. I have to untie my ankle. The grass is overgrown, so hopefully he won’t be able to see me, and I can hide until he leaves. It was my only chance at escape; I just needed to remain quiet.

So, I sat there concussed, silently trying to untie the rope. Listening. Now hearing the faint screams and laughter of my friends in the distance. I missed them. I sat watching the sun fade into the dim-lit sky. I hoped they had heard my scream and would come to my rescue, but that hope quickly faded. I feel a slight tug. I froze entirely, like a deer caught in headlights. Then a pull. The rope snapped taught, yanking my ankle from under me, slamming me on my back. I was being dragged. I grabbed ferociously at the rope to fight my capture. I leaned back with force, my head to the sky, eyes shot in horror. I now saw myself sitting, facing west under the powerlines. I was being pulled into the abandoned house. I clawed at the ground as I screamed. 

It sat in the doorway, cackling like a witch. They were pale and lanky with a contorted face. Melted as a wax figurine would under a heat lamp. Eyes wide, bloodshot with adrenaline. They roared in ecstasy. It wore clothing that looked to be from ages ago. Unwashed and torn, with an unbearable stench. They towered over me, smiling. A devious, rotten tooth, half-filled smile. Drooling, it salivated in hunger. “LEAVE HIM ALONE!” Shouts a voice nearby. A wave of relief falls over me momentarily, in the hope that I might be saved... “I SAID GODDAMNIT SON, LEAVE HIM ALONE!” ... The gimp looks up for a second with some consideration, before quickly continuing and snatching me up off the floor. It nearly yanked my arm out of the socket, throwing me over its bony shoulder with force. I was dizzy with confusion and fear. It felt like a dream. That's when, for a brief second, I could see above the tall grass that once encapsulated me. It was the old man standing on his porch. A look of disappointment on his face. A look of, “I told you so”. 

It was dark inside. It smelled like dust and death, as if the walls were filled with animal remains. A few pieces of furniture sat in the barren house. The upholstery-filthy and battered. We walked through to the kitchen. Old bloodstains darkened on the broken vinyl tiles, and chunks of rotting black substance allowed a breeding ground for flies in the sink. In the corner of the kitchen was an entrance to the cellar. It led down a rickety wooden staircase. Into the pitch black, we descended. I could feel the temperature dropping as we got closer to the bottom. When we reached the final step, the man carrying me turned to an old pull switch. For one second, as he turned, I could see a dim lightbulb ignite behind a small window. A window placed on a door. A door hiding a room in the bottom of the cellar.

As I pleaded to be let go, screaming for my life, he replied only with shrill squeals. He was ecstatic to be taking me, like he had made a new friend. As he ripped open the heavy metal door, I felt an intense icy chill of air rush out. The sound of fans blowing. The metallic walls of the room behind the door let off a slight shimmer. He set my shaking and beaten body down onto the floor, before closing the door behind him. 

I hear a latch shut. He now sat on the opposite side, watching me through the window. It felt like he turned into a statue the minute the door locked behind me. He never left. Not even for a moment. Not to use the bathroom…Not to sleep. To eat. He sat smiling, and unblinking. The stale air dried and burned his shifty eyes as he watched, blood vessels dilating in his cornea. Grimacing, he smiled harder. His rotten teeth clicking and grinding.

We sat for what felt like days. At first, I screamed for help, but no one could hear me there. No one came, and I started to think no one was ever looking for me at all. Eventually, something changed and I could feel my heart begin to slow. The ice cutting into my skin, caused a warmth to pass over my burning blue hands like a soothing balm. It no longer felt like a thousand needles pricking my flesh but cushion-like. My thoughts and my vision became muddled, yet there was still a noticeable voice in the back of my mind. Still screaming at me to get help. It would come for them next. I knew that, yet... All I wanted to do was sleep.
