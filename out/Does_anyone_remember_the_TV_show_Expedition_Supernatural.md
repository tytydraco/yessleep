I am posting primarily to organize my thoughts. It has been a very rough month, or year depending on how you look at things. So much has happened. So much to sort through. 



I have looked everywhere. I have searched every article, blog, and YouTube video. I have looked through fringe forums and message boards. I have consulted physical TV guides and newspapers. I have asked anyone and everyone who will listen. But no one seems to have evidence that this show existed at all.



I used to have proof, but I left it smoldering at the bottom of a metal dustbin. It was a good idea at the time. Now I’m not so sure. It’s the reason for everything. It has to be.



Let me slow down. I’ll start at the beginning.



\---



It was a very hot day. Not a single cloud in sight. The heat rose in waves above the asphalt, and vibrant shades of green streaked by as I drove. Despite the perfect weather, I couldn’t focus on it. My mind wandered absently. My sister, we’ll call her Claire, had just finished her end of the paperwork, and now we were left to clean up reminders of better times. It had been sudden. Our father was already struggling with pneumonia when our mom was diagnosed with stage four. She fought valiantly for two long months, but it had progressed too quickly. I barely had time to make it to my dad’s hospital room before he followed after her. 



Everything since had been a blur of phone calls and condolences, leaving me no time to process any of it. 



I pulled into the crammed drive way, a U-Haul and Claire’s Prius taking up most of the space. The one story house looked the same as all the others in the copy/paste neighborhood, but this one held my earliest memories. It was different. Special. I closed my eyes and leaned back in the seat, breathing deeply. This would be hard. 



I fumbled for the keys, turning off the car. In my peripheral vision, I could see Claire standing on the concrete pad we called a porch. I grabbed my travel bag out of the passenger seat, put on my best smile, and threw open the door.



“Hello! You picked a good day to clean,” I called from my car.



“Yeah, perfect weather to clean out the basement. I made us a casserole for lunch, hope you don’t mind,” she said in her naturally quiet voice. Even growing up, she was never one to speak loudly, or even speak much at all. 



“I don’t! What about dinner? Can we get pizza and wings?” I flashed a smile. Dad had always said that jokingly when Mom spent all day preparing dinner.



There was a pause while I walked up the awkward steps. She watched me carefully before replying, “Sure, if that’s what you want.”



Now that I was standing in front of her, it looked like she hadn’t slept in weeks. Her long, black hair hung loosely over her shoulders, and her clothes were covered in dust. I probably didn’t look much better, not that anyone could blame us. We’d been through a lot. Too much. 



“I wonder if we’ll find the foosball table,” I asked her, grasping for some semblance of a conversation. She crossed her arms. A ghost of a smile crossed her lips before she smoothed it out.



“Maybe we will,” she replied, turning to the door.



As she reached to grab the knob, she hesitated, as if thinking about something. An internal argument I wasn’t privy to, was never privy to. When she faced me, I saw an instance of pain in her eyes. And something else, *anger?*  



“It took you… It took you too long. I- I don’t know why you…” she looked down as she fumbled over the words, scanning the ground around my feet as if to find them again. Inevitably, she didn’t. Finishing simply with “I missed you.”



I was surprised, Claire wasn’t one to show emotions like this, although it was clear she hadn’t said what she wanted to. I would have to coax it out of her eventually. That’s how she’s always worked. 



“I missed you too,” I replied, somewhat lamely. 



As we walked into the house, silence stretching out like a dark hallway, I couldn’t believe how empty it was. Most of the rooms had been packed up in neat boxes, ready to be auctioned or stored. It was almost unrecognizable from the distant memories of childhood. It felt too open. Too wide. Not claustrophobic enough. No excess furniture clogging the halls or last night’s takeout left on the dining room table.



I walked through the hall to my old bedroom. Four creme-colored walls and an air mattress. The bittersweet feelings of relief and sorrow swirled in my stomach. It’s like I never lived here at all. I sat my bag down and returned to the kitchen, waiting next to the basement door as Claire collected the cleaning supplies. 



In sharp contrast, the basement was *exactly* as I had remembered. Boxes upon boxes of god-knows-what covered the space, piling high in the corners. I could see the framework of what looked like shelves completely buried in the clutter. We had our work cut out for us. Claire brushed by me on the creaky stairs, carrying a stained box of old toys. Ok, point taken. I bent down and picked up the nearest box.



One by one, we cleared the whole damn room with not so much a word of conversation. Vinyl records that were never played, furniture that was never used, and paintings that were never hung. All of these and more were covered in layers of dust and cobwebs. We actually did find the foosball table, broken handles and all. A leaky pipe dripped rust colored water on its center, forming a mess of black mold. I felt a pang of nostalgia and sorrow. We had spent many nights playing game after game. I knew Claire felt the same through her slight curve of a frown. Without any bravado, she helped me lift the thing up the stairs, directly into the trash. 



We worked for so long that we ended up eating the cold casserole for dinner. Shortly after which, I stepped out for a smoke. The cold night air bristled the unkept grass. Remnants of a metal firepit surrounded by rotting chairs blemished the backyard like a sore. I didn’t know how Claire could stand to see it all. All those pieces from the lives before ours. I left her when she started organizing the old photo albums. The memories stung.



I took a long drag of my cigarette and walked to the firepit. It was stained a deep black from decades of ash. The center was beginning to sag from rust and neglect, small holes perforating the bottom. I vaguely remembered my parents sitting around it with their friends. They laughed and laughed, and got louder as the night went on while my sister and I… 



What were we doing? Playing foosball, probably.



I was about to take another drag when I heard Claire scream. I hesitated for a second, but then the cigarette was under my boot and I was already inside. I could hear the back door rattling in its frame as I barreled through it, into the living room.



Claire looked up at me instantly with her eyes wide. In a meek voice, barely audible, she said, “I’m sorry, I just found something, that was all.”



Why would she make me worry like that? “What is wrong with-” I started, but I thought better of it. Instead I asked, in a much calmer tone, “what did you find?”



She motioned to the box at her feet. It was smaller than the others, a shoebox, and it was filled with unmarked VHS tapes. “Do you remember these?” she questioned back.



It took me a second before I recognized the blank, yellowed labels. “Oh my god! I haven’t seen Expedition: Supernatural in forever!” I replied, frustration now replaced by excitement. “Let’s watch a few episodes!”



Claire looked at me. Really *looked* at me.



“That show was really weird. I don’t know why mom insisted on recording it,” she said finally.



“Yeah, but we never really *watched* the show, did we? I remember our parents would talk over it,” then, I had an idea, “Maybe we could do the same. You know, catch up?” Something was obviously bothering her, and this was her chance to say it.



She looked from me, to the tapes, then back to me. “I guess it has been a while…” she trailed off.



Before she could protest further, I moved to the living room where our parents’ CRT TV sat nestled in the entertainment center. It was the only thing left in the room besides a beaten-up leather couch. I switched on the TV and was met with the familiar buzz of static. Without looking, I pulled a tape from the shoebox and slotted it into the VCR. The static stuttered, then the title card of the episode flared onto the screen.



*Breaking Point*



I couldn’t remember this episode at all.



I took my place on the right side of the couch. Claire had already taken the left, bundled in a blanket. I wanted to wait for the episode’s introduction to finish before talking over the whole thing. 



*Open on woods. Two people stand in center frame: a man in a baseball cap and cargo shorts, a woman in a tanktop and leggings.*



*“I’m Oscar!”*



*“And I’m Jenny! And this time, we have a special episode for you! Kinda like a double feature!”*



*“That’s right! Today, we’ll explore the mystery of the Johnson twins: a tragic tale with an aftermath that haunts Oregon locals to this day.”*



*“We’ll venture into the mouth of Centurion Cave where the mystery took place. Then later tonight, we’ll explore the remains of a nearby town, where the twins are said to have lived. Two locations, an Expedition: Supernatural first!”*



I looked over to Claire. She had the blanket wrapped tightly around her. Was she nervous?



*The pair walk on a narrow path, winding through hills and across streams. Jenny begins to tell the story of Everett Johnson.* 



*“He was the boldest and bravest of the twins, always jumping into things head first and pushing the field of cave cartography to its limits. In his lifetime, he mapped 18 lesser cave systems solo, refusing any and all payment from expeditionary organizations. As he said, ‘the thrill of discovery was enough compensation.’”*



*“He was a true treasure, and his brother Carter knew it,” Oscar chimes in.*



“So… how are you holding up? I haven’t seen you in a while, we haven’t really spoken,” I began, tentatively. 



Claire seemed engrossed in the show, mildly startled by my words. I could see her thinking carefully before responding, “it has been a while.”



*Oscar was winded from the effort of hiking up the trail, but continues, “Carter, an avid speleologist, was much more careful. Under his supervision and planning, the discovery of Centurion Cave was made: a 15 mile long cave hidden in the Oregon mountains. Although he didn’t lead every expedition himself, that was Everett’s job, his research proved invaluable.”*



*“Unfortunately, their achievements are overshadowed by Ever-”*



*The episode cuts jarringly: a skip in the tape into a commercial for a toy out of production. A discontinued phone number ran the length of the screen.*



Claire stirred, wiping her hands on the blanket before speaking, “where were you?”



“I’ve been working mostly. I came here as soon as I could when you called.”



“You did?”



“I did what?”



“Come as soon as I called?” She fidgeted, picking at her nails.



“Yes, I had some things to tie up. Work has been a lot.”



“Has it?”



I was stunned but-



*Oscar points at the wide mouth, dark and jagged. It towers above him. The afternoon sun barely reaches a foot into the cave.*



*“And here we are!” Jenny exclaims. “Centurion Cave was by far the longest in Oregon, but now it’s a shadow of what it once was. A reminder of Carter’s failure and the subsequent tragedy.”*



“It has,” I managed to say, the words felt choked.



“I called five months ago”



“I-”



*The camera follows the duo into the cave. Cold rock under foot, deep-sounding silence, and meager light slicing the pitch black.*



*The audio cuts in and out as Oscar speaks:*



*“-was a great man. By some accounts, Carter did everything he could in the end. Condemning his mobile lab to the weight of the rock and dragging-* 



*-ver, most accounts agree that Carter was more selfish. Sparing the research instea-*



*-erhaps it was intentional. Afterall, he was the sole surv-”*



“-I’m sorry,” I looked over to Claire. Her cheeks were flushed underneath watery eyes. “I tried to be there.”



Her gaze fell upon the floor in front of her, then to the foosball table piled onto the trash. Her knuckles turned white as they gripped the seat of the couch. “You weren’t here.” She shifted her weight to face me, wearing a pained expression. “You. Weren’t. Here.”



*Jenny ushers the camera forward, illuminating the way with a thin beam of light. The cave ends in a steep drop. At the bottom, where there once was a grand archway is now covered in rubble.*



*The camera zooms slowly, towards the bottom. Damp rock and mud. Particles of dust play in the light that tapers off in the darkness.*



*And just at the edge of the light, the treads of an old leather shoe can be seen, sticking out of the rubble.*



Where was I, really? Work? Or somewhere else? Why didn’t I come back immediately? 



I thought back to my childhood. 



This house. 



I felt sick.



“I’m going to have a smoke,” I said, leaning against the armrest to stand. A hand stopped me. Claire.



“I thought you wanted to talk?” she said through gritted teeth. The grip of her hand on my arm was significantly weaker than the sharp tone of her voice, but coming from her, it was lethal.



I sat down.



*The audio distorted, cutting sporadically, louder than before.*



*Jenny spoke directly to the camera, “now we’re on our way to a special place. The town where-* 



*-lived. It might look like your traditional neighborhood, but some say it's deeply connected to-*



*-’s intentions.”*



Claire brushed away her tears, settling back onto the couch. In a small voice, she said, “if I could, I would’ve left too.”



Shouting. Crying. Arguments that lasted hourly, daily, yearly. That’s what I left behind. That’s ALL I left behind. 



“You should’ve left with me,” I told her.



“What?”



“You should’ve-”



*Static crackles, the image fades in and out.*



*Oscar crests a tree-lined hill, Jenny shortly behind.* 



“How could I have left? I had to do everything,” Claire raised her voice, enough to make my skin crawl and my stomach turn. Tears welled once more, despite her best efforts to clear them. 



*The camera pans over the hill, revealing an overgrown neighborhood.* 



*A modern neighborhood.*



*Vine-wrapped streetlights, sagging powerlines, and rusted cars parked in driveways.*



*In the farthest house on the right, a U-Haul with peeling decals and a battered Prius.*



My blood ran cold. I was confused. I turned to Claire, opening my mouth to speak, but she beat me to it.



Through rolling tears and a cracking voice, she tore into me.



“I did everything. I was here for them and where were you? Where *were* you? I held each of their hands as they died. I put together the funerals, and all the paperwork, and I called everyone I could think of for help when you didn’t answer my calls. I sold their cars, I cleaned out the house, and you can’t even show up to put a flower on their graves. And now you walk back into my life like nothing happened. Like it hasn’t been *months* since I’ve seen you. Like it hasn’t been *years* since you left me alone here. In this awful-” 



*They approach the house.* 



*Faded paint stripes the empty road. Weeds sprout between the cracks. Dry-rotted tires disintegrate into the Earth.*



*Cracked windows and broken homes.*



*Their mouths move, but there’s no sound.* 



*They step past the cars, up to the fractured concrete porch. I can see light from behind the shattered window.*



*The camera pans to the moss-tinted door.*



*Oscar knocks.*



I look over to Claire, can she hear it too? I can tell she is shouting now, the loudest she’s ever been, but I can’t hear it. I can only hear the knock.



The static on the screen rose and fell like a tide. 



*Jenny pushes Oscar aside and looks at the camera, speaking noiseless words. Words for me, about me, or to me, I can feel it. Suddenly, she pivots to the door, reaching for the knob.*



I tore myself off the couch and ejected the tape. The TV sputtered into static. In the corner of my eye, I could see Claire staring at me in shock and confusion. 



“Did you see it? They were at the door,” I spoke hastily, afraid.



She stood up slowly. “I- I don’t know what you’re talking about.”



“Oscar, I mean. Oscar and Jenny. From the show.”



“They were in some weird neighborhood, or something… I don’t know…” She took one step backward, lowering her voice to its normal level. “Did… You know what, nevermind.”



At that, Claire walked out of the room, leaving me alone with the tapes. A few minutes after she left is when I burned them in an old metal trash can. How could something like this happen? I can’t just be imagining it. 



I drove back home a few days ago, and I can’t stop thinking about the knock. Claire didn’t understand, telling me over and over that she didn’t hear it, that *I* wasn’t listening. But I was listening, and they were here. I can’t prove it, but they were *here*. If someone has proof of anything related to Expedition: Supernatural, please *please* let me know. I *can’t* be the only one to know about this show.
