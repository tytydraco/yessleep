It's been too long since I've enjoyed myself outside the house. Work has been piling up left and right, and my friends have dwindled since graduation. It was in spite of these that I decided to try, to make myself go out and exist in the world without any purpose. 

Funnily enough, my first instinct was to get groceries. Though I had to talk myself out of it. Instead took myself across the street to the new cafe, Apres La Vie. After much deliberation on what I should get (I was never one for cafes, I always had coffee at home), I settled for a mocha and a croissant. Both of which were much, much better than I anticipated. I did my best to savor them but that was never likely, as it was nearing noon and I had not had anything other than a glass of water. The cafe was peaceful, almost the exact thing I was looking for. There were people chatting and others working, soft jazzy instrumentals playing just loud enough to be heard over the barista calling names and the bell on the door. 

I must admit, one of my hobbies was people watching. Playing a guessing game based off of observations and nothing else. I stared at a man biting his nails every so often as he typed on his laptop, three and a half empty cups occupying what little space the table had. He looked too old to be a student, but still too young to be of great import to most businesses. I settled for a recent graduate, hoping that his job offers would pour in that day.

I played this game with everyone in the cafe. The two girls who seemed like long time friends reuniting after months. The old man on the patio feeding crows. The woman who read Wuthering Heights in the corner. I don't know how long I was there, inventing stories about these people, but it was fun. More fun than I had in ages and entirely in solitude.

As I sat guessing what Erich, the name I gave to the old man, did before his retirement I heard a voice from my periphery. It was low, soft and smooth, the kind you develop after a life of speaking in public. 

"Excuse me, is this chair taken". It said, emanating from a man I felt I recognized. Every feature of his face felt like an amalgam of everyone I had ever knew. Of everyone in this shop right now. His skin was sun kissed, but bore no moles or blemishes. His arms leaned towards skeletal while his legs leaned towards an ox's. His grey shirt gently hugged his torso, which was neither that of a athlete nor a sloth. His hair was cut short and styled just enough to be presentable, as was his facial hair, which covered all but the philtrum and chin.

I gestured towards it and as he sat I asked "have we met? You seem familiar?"

"Guess" he said, smiling. His eyes trained directly on mine. I felt as though he was about to sell me something. 

"Where did you go to college?"

"The same as you"

"And where would that be?"

He smirked and turned to the old man, "Erich, or as he's known by everyone else, Anthony, comes here for three hours every day to feed the crows. Susan, the woman with the book, comes twice a week to wait for laundry next door." He turned back to me, and then to his mug which he sipped out of, letting the moment linger.

"I'm sorry I don't understand, we have met?"

"Once, only once."

I was growing tired, for all I knew this was a stranger's game that he played. The same I played with the others in the cafe. I wanted to avoid a scene, but I did try to glance at the barista as though I felt like something was wrong. He only looked confused at my attempts.

He continued. "You work, no?"

"You tell me,"

He smirked again. It was beginning to bother me. There was a gentle warmth in his smile, as though he truly was a friend. Yet his eyes told me he was smiling for another reason.

"When you work, you feel a sense of accomplishment. The more you work the prouder you are. But what else do you have to say for yourself? A lonely man growing older each day" he tisked as looked down at the table. My annoyance was growing into rage. Whatever sick joke this was began to grow older and older by the second. 

"What do you want?" I asked.

"What do you want, Howard? Did you come to this cafe for the coffee, the atmosphere? Or did you come because you had no other choice on where to go?"

"I was bored"

"And you could be bored no longer. If you would only indulge me a little more." His smile faded and now his face became even harder to pinpoint. It was as if the only concrete detail was that smile, and everything else was subtly changing as I focused on each part. What color were his eyes? Green, blue or gray. Or were they always brown?

"I thank you for the offer but I must-"

"-be going back home, you have things that need to be done. Be honest Howard, you're scared because you don't understand." he paused for a second, gathering himself. He spoke again.

 

"I would like to show you something, it's here on my phone. When you see it you'll understand, and it explains it in ways better than I or anyone else could. It's the truth Howard, it is purpose. It is the answer to the boredom and the loneliness. Thousands have already seen it and all have said it changed their lives for the better." he pulled out a phone that bore no markings or buttons, no camera or charger port. Not a single fingerprint or scratch like it was fresh from the store. He placed it on the table and I took a look. 

The phone was heavy, thought it was slimmer than any I had seen and without a case. The display was peculiar as well. When viewed on the saggital plane, it appeared to be less than a quarter of an inch. However when you looked at the screen, if you could even call it that, felt like gazing into a room from the ceiling. The images within it looked three dimensional, no, were three dimensional. I saw a man in black and gold robes sitting upon a chair made of sticks, he stood still. I was impressed by the artist's control of perspective, but it was too real to be art. If it was a photo, then the camera could only have been the human eye. I stared at it, just trying to process what I was seeing and how it worked. It must have been five minutes when I realized that the music was no longer playing, and the chatter of my fellow patrons fell mute. I looked up to see that I was in deep darkness, yet in about a three foot radius everything was lit as it normally was. As if a spotlight shone only on the stranger, the table, and me.

"What is this? Who are you? Is this one of those prank shows?" 

"Look" he said sternly, his face was angry now, and the details of it even more out of place. It was not the same man who sat next to me. I tried standing but the man shot up impossibly quick, almost as if he had always been behind me, and pushed my head towards the screen. The black and gold robed man was standing now, and the stranger pushed my head closer to it so it was the only thing within my focus. The robed man reached out, and I saw his finger leave the box and touch my forehead. I resisted, the finger felt white hot and ice cold. My brain immediately aching and vision blurry. Time froze, it felt like an eternity that he had kept me there. The more I resisted the harder me head was pushed into the finger. Then the nail broke skin, and I could feel blood trickle down my brow and nose.

I realized now that the finger itself was skin and bone, black as the night. I tried to peer into the robed man's eyes but the shade from his cloak obscured him. Then, I felt all the weight behind me disappear and my head shot back. 

I let out a scream, clutching my forehead and crying. Music played, and when I looked up it was light outside. No time had passed, the patrons and employees looking at me with intense worry. I left immediately and returned home. In the mirror I saw nothing that indicated I had been harmed in any way, except for the faintest scar between my brows. This might be hallucination, but there lays a small spiral, counter clockwise. I stayed home the rest of the day, and when night fell sleep took me immediately.

Now, I have dreams. I see the man in robes. I see his throne of sticks. He is impossibly tall, and even motion he makes creates a gust of wind. I wait for him, mute and neutered. He never gives me orders, but I cannot disobey. I know this. We receive guests of all races, sexes, and ages. All speaking a tongue I have never heard, one that in my waking hours I cannot find. The worst part is that my time asleep is pure bliss. A serenity that no man has ever felt. I serve him, my King of Sleep. I'm proud to. My hours are spent waiting for the night, dozing off at dusk and waking at noon.

I have neglected all my other duties, I could not tell you the last time I worked, or left my home. The last time I ate or drank. I embarrassingly admit that I even relive myself in bed. This waking world holds nothing for me, and that is why I write this.

Tonight I will sleep, and this is the last I will ever be awake. I will serve him forever, in his court in the forest clearing. In the place it is always dark and not even stars or the moon illuminate us. I have found my purpose, my joy. And I leave this as my last testament. When the stranger shows you his phone, take it.
