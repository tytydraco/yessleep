​I made a radical decision.

​Spending the last few days locked inside, staring at that damn brass key and waiting for next month's tithe to completely consume me, was destroying my mind. Every night, the voices outside the wall grew more aggressive. The monster wasn't just mimicking my mother anymore; it started using the voices of childhood friends, old neighbors, all of them crying, begging, or screaming for me to open the gate. My mind was on the verge of snapping.

​Yesterday afternoon, while the sun was still high and the men in gray weren't in the garden, I decided I wouldn't be a passive victim of this cursed inheritance. If my family's blood is the only thing keeping the seal functioning, I needed to understand exactly what I was dealing with. I wasn't going to wait 26 days to find out what they meant by "demanding more than just your palm."

​I grabbed the brass key from the kitchen table. My hands were shaking so badly I almost dropped it. I also grabbed the kitchen knife and a powerful flashlight.

​I walked over to the hatch. The silence coming from below was suffocating. I brought the key close to the lock. The exact moment the metal of the key touched the keyhole, a violent shiver ran down my spine. I turned the key. Clack.

​The latch gave way. With all my strength, I pulled the heavy wooden lid back and propped it against the wall.

​The darkness down there seemed solid, as if the light from my flashlight was struggling to cut through the heavy, cold air of the basement. The stench of iron and wet dirt was almost unbearable. I stepped down the creaking wooden stairs, one by one, holding the knife out in front of me.

​My grandfather's basement wasn't big. The walls were made of rough, damp stone, covered in a dark substance that looked like slime but had the texture of dried blood. 

In the center of the dirt floor, there was what the journal called "The Mouth." It was a narrow fissure in the ground, from which a faint black mist rose continuously.

​Around the fissure, massive chains were bolted to the walls, but what caught my attention was something my grandfather had hidden in the corner. On a worn-out wooden table, there were several clear plastic IV bags—the kind used in hospitals for blood storage—all empty and dry, along with needles and rubber tubing.

​My grandfather didn't cut himself every month. He used that equipment to drain and store his own blood gradually over the days, preparing the tithe in advance so he wouldn't have to mutilate himself in front of the cult. He was draining himself piece by piece to maintain control.

​But as I examined the table, a sound made me freeze.

​“João... did you come to see me?”

​The voice came from inside the fissure. But it wasn't my mother's voice, nor my grandfather's. It was my own voice. Exactly the same tone, the same timbre, but dragged out, as if something was learning to use my vocal cords at that very instant.

​I shone the flashlight into the fissure. Two long, grayish, skeletal arms sprouted from the darkness of the Mouth, clawing at the edges of the dirt floor. The thing began to pull itself out. Rows of sharp teeth clicked in the dark, and the black mist began to rise harder, freezing the air around me.

​Panic told me to run, but rage and desperation made me act. Instead of fleeing, I took a step forward. I took the knife and, in one swift motion, sliced a deep cut into my right arm. I let the blood gush directly onto the creature's skeletal arms.

​The effect was instantaneous. The moment my blood touched the monster's skin, it let out a piercing roar—my own voice screaming in extreme agony. Its skin began to smoke and burn, as if familiar blood acted like pure acid 

on its body. The creature let go of the edges of the fissure and plummeted back into the deep darkness underground.

​I ran up the stairs, slammed the hatch door down, and locked the brass key with three full turns. I collapsed on the kitchen floor, panting, as I bandaged my arm with a clean cloth.

​I understand the dynamic now. Our family's blood isn't just a passive offering; it is the containment weapon. It is what burns and weakens the prisoner. My radical decision gave me an answer, but it also brought me a massive problem.

​When I looked out the kitchen window at the dead garden, the men in gray weren't there, since it's still daytime. But right in the center of the dry lawn, their dark wooden box has reappeared on its own. And the lid is wide open.

​I anticipated the tithe by attacking the prisoner directly, and the caretakers of the Order already know. The air inside the house is getting so cold I can see my own breath.

​Did I break their rules by going down there? What happens when you use the tithe early to attack the prisoner? If anyone has ever dealt with entities that respond to this kind of protocol breach, please answer me quickly. I think I just heard the front gate swing open, and it's still many hours before 2:45 AM.
