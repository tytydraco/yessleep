This was supposed to be just another fossil dive, something I had done hundreds of times.  A boat ride 40 miles out off the coast and three dives spent digging through the mud and rocks, 95 feet underwater.

I never could have expected what would happen.  Despite what movies might show you, recreational scuba diving isn’t some dangerous activity.

But I’m getting ahead of myself, my name is John, and as long as I can remember I’ve loved the ocean, although now when I think back on what I had to endure, I can’t imagine ever going back on the water.

My buddy William and I had boarded the dive boat before the sun was even up, both of us still half asleep.  This was standard for these boats as we needed to travel so far out into the ocean to reach the fossil ridge.

William and I had both grown up in the city nearby, he had gotten to explore the world with the Marines, something I still regretted not joining him on.  He loved to talk to me about his travels and how had been stationed on a base in Australia up until a few years ago.

We had been joined by four other divers, and before long we were all resting in a partially covered portion on hard metal benches, but we each knew to bring sleeping bags from the listing.  It was not a large boat, but it had room for at ten divers at total.

A young couple, Brian and Hannah, who mentioned they had been here once before with a friend, but were clearly still very green to this kind of diving.  I’d have been tempted to dismiss them as tourists, but their earnest excitement at collecting million-year-old fossils had won me over.  While lugging their gear onto the boat they had talked incessantly about all the megalodon teeth and whale bones they hoped to find.

In between us and the couple were two more divers, towards the stern of the boat.  An older man named Ulysses who we had ran into on other dives in the area, he was a retired doctor from Texas who was using his newfound free time primarily to dive.

Finally, I had the misfortune of being closest to our sixth diver, Ian, who was apparently some sort of aspiring lifestyle/scuba diving/MMA influencer.  He had started vlogging the moment we all laid down, so loud we could hear him over the engine hum without much effort.

He continued for far too long, did he not realize how early it was?  But eventually I was able to drift off to sleep, the sun still not having risen over the horizon, as our little boat cut across the ocean.

I was woken by William tapping me on the shoulder. The boat had noticeably slowed, the engine humming quieter and at a lower pitch.  The sun now above the horizon, setting the sky ablaze with brilliant reds, the ocean was still a deep black.

“We’re getting close, we should start eating our gear ready”. William said.

I nodded, looking around for my gear while the deck hand, Sean stood across from me, waking the others.

I stood up taking in the sun rise, in every direction there was nothing but the sea, still dark, imperceptibly deep, concealing anything that may be swimming underneath.  

“ Never seen the sun before John?” William said punching me on the shoulder, “don’t forget to set up your BC”

“Yeah, just hoping I’ll find a good one today, could really use a cash injection or some rent money, not to mention Edgar— is that a dolphin out there?” I said, interrupting myself.  Far out on the horizon ahead of us I could see something surfacing on the water.

William now approached the edge of the boat, “That’s so far out I can’t say, not with this lighting, maybe a grouper, or a sunfish?  From this far its just a silhouette, would love to see whichever it is on the dive”

As if on cue the boat now slowed further, the engine getting even quieter, before becoming only a dull hum, apparently idling.  The crew of the boat now got to work setting the anchor and preparing the diving platform.  I still found myself staring off toward whatever I had seen, it had long since disappeared back below the surface.  I was normally so good at identifying sea life, even at this distance, but the outline had seemed so strange.

Because of my distraction, I ended up needing to rush my gear setup, William laughed when he noticed that everyone else on the boat was fully ready to go and I was still connecting my regulator, sorting out weight packs, awkwardly climbing into my wetsuit.

Once the boat was stopped the deckhand got right into the dive briefing, we would be doing three dives with a max depth of 95 feet, our tanks should be nitrox around 34%, 3300PSI, but it was on us to know exactly what we were diving with.  And with steel 120s almost certainly our limiting factors would be our NDLs rather than air supply.

Through it all, I couldn’t shake this growing feeling that something was wrong, I had no reason to call the dive, I even ended up triple checking my equipment and everything was working fine, but I had the knot in my gut would not leave.

Even William had noticed, apparently my worry showing on my face.

“Hey if you’re not feeling up to it theres no shame in calling the dive, especially since these are semi-solo dives, its not like you’ll interrupt my day”, He said.

“As if I had that choice,” I responded,  shaking my head, “I owe Edgar a lot of money after last week, I need to find some decent teeth today for my wholesaler or it won’t end well”

“John, if you’re short on money you should’ve just taken another shift, how many good teeth do you need to offset the charter cost?” William said, clearly a disappointed.  “You’re basically gambling this money too”

I looked him dead in the eye, “Why, other than gambling, do you think I even owe Edgar so much”.

“Fair enough mate”.

It was then time to enter the water and start the first dive.  I was the first to drop in, immediately descending into the cool ocean water.  I was soon surrounded by a world of grayish blue.

The visibility was decent thankfully, I could not see the bottom, but it looked to be about 30 feet with minimal current.  

I followed the length of the boat about twenty feet down, glancing back to see William right behind me, and further back Ian had followed us in, distinguishable in the water by a red helmet on his head, mounted with a hint flood light and a GoPro.  Once at the bottom we would split off somewhat, each conducting our own searches for fossils, the distance necessary since our digging would create giant clouds of silt.

Up by the surface I could Brian and Hannah floating, probably sorting through an issue with their gear, Ulysses was floating a few feet away.  I knew from past dives he was the type to deliberately stay near them to make sure they were ok, it was easy to make simple mistakes when fossil diving.

The three of us having reached the stern, began following the anchor line down, hitting about 60 feet before the bottom came hazily into view.

Once there William and I made eye contact, both signing that we were both ok, and then clipping in our wreck reels to the anchor.  These could become necessary once we dug into the mud, and silted out the area, without the lines it could be very easy to get lost.  

I’d reached the bottom with 3000 PSI more importantly, my dive computer showed I was at 93 feet, and had a NDL of 24 minutes.  

I shouldn’t just assume you all know what an NDL is, so for the uninitiated, its your no decompression limit, the dive computer runs a fancy algorithm, that gives you the amount of time left before the nitrogen build up in your blood means you will be required to do a decompression stop on your ascent to avoid getting bent.

Before taking off towards the location the deck hand had recommended for best finding teeth, I took a moment to take in my surroundings.  I knelt next to the rusted anchor, the water still that murky grayish blue, although a bit darker at this depth.  However the sun still shone down clearly, even if it was distorted and everything was a bit greyscale.  The bottom was a mix of seagrass, sand, mud, rocks, with a few boulders spaced randomly around.  I was also acutely aware of my breathing, every inhale lifting my body a little bit, sinking on every exhale, feeling the bubbles leaving my regulator tickling my stubble.

I took a quick heading, the recommended fossil site was about a hundred feet north, and started my swim towards it, unspooling my wreck reel as I did so.  William was already ahed of me, having not waited at the anchor, I could see him clearly as he was only about 10 feet up.

I glanced back to see Ulysses, Brian, and Hannah were following the anchor line down, Ian still at the anchor holding a second GoPro on a selfie stick, where had he kept that second camera?  This was hardly a picturesque dive site.

I continued swimming forward though, with map precise kicks, adjusting my buoyancy slightly as I went along, although this would be thrown out the window once I had the weight of fossils in my net.

“Wait, what is that in the mud?” I thought.

I’d nearly missed it, right beneath me sitting atop the mud, there was some sort of tooth.  It wasn’t shaped like any kind I had seen before, certainly no megalodon fossil, looked more like half the jaw of a grouper if I had to make a guess.  An elongated, curved shape, although it was clearly a tooth from the serrations.

And it was white, if it was a fossil it was miraculously well preserved, we’d expected the recent storm to shake up the bottom, but you normally needed to dig for anything in this good condition.

Well, I told myself it must be a real change in luck, if I had never seen it before it was probably rare and therefore probably valuable, so I stowed it in my net.

I continued on, reaching the clearing where there was very little seagrass as the deckhand had described, over on my right, William had started digging through the mud creating a massive cloud of silt around him.  My plan was to go out past the clearing though, just to get more potentially untouched fossil areas, and dig through the mud as I followed my line back in.  

So I continued pressing forward, quickly reaching the edge of the clearing, already I could only tell where William was from his cloud of silt and a column of bubbles stretching up towards the surface.

Soon, even that had faded away into the cloudy water.  I continued probably another 50 feet, deciding to stop at another smaller clearing of the seagrass with a very large boulder on its edge.

My computer showed my NDL was sitting at 20 minutes, air at 2700 PSI, depth 95 feet.  As I reached the edge of the rock field,  a few feet away from the giant boulder, this would be as far as I would go.  I dropped my reel in from of the rock, I would leave it here between dives and would reel it back in on the final dive.

I started my fossil hunt, slowly working my back down the line, fanning away the silt on the ground, pulling rocks out of the way to get deeper, throwing anything that looked vaguely triangular in my bag, I would sort through it later.

It was a pattern I had grown quite used to, fan the dirt away, wait for it to clear, sort through the rocks, and move a foot down the line.  There were already a few promising finds, some definite meg teeth, but I didn’t stop to give them a good look, that could be done later.

I’d made it only a few feet down my line before Ian swam right by me, a bit over my head and to the side, I’d barely seen him swim by with the tunnel vision from my mask, but the motion had made me look up.

He was no longer pulling along his wreck reel, clearly planning on using mine, still holding his GoPro, apparently getting plenty of shots of me digging through the mud.  Probably as B-role for his own video, it didn’t bother me but I still wish he’d asked.

He continued past me though, swimming past the end of my reel, and over the top of the rock.  I had paused my search to watch him, as if he was not carrying his own reel he could pretty easily get lost in the silt.  He floated above the boulder, keeping himself steady with one hand touching the stone, staring down at something on the other side.

I figured there was probably some critter on the side of the boulder, maybe a nurse shark or a turtle if we were lucky.  No matter what it was though, we were here for the teeth, I needed to get enough good ones to offset the cost of the day, I couldn’t spend my time looking at a fish no matter what was there.

That being said, he then slipped by the boulder, maybe to look at whatever he was seeing, maybe to swim off in a random direction.  I couldn’t risk the latter, no matter how much I needed to meet my quota, it was deceptively easy to get lost out here.

So turned back and followed him over the boulder, floating over top and looking down to see where he’d gone, what he had been looking at.

And It was something, I’d never considered seeing out here.

There was a hole in the ground, up against the boulder, about four feet in diameter, the narrow tunnel went down at an angle, obscured from my vision.  Ian knelt beside it pointing his helmet flood light and both of his cameras down into it.  As I lowered myself next to him I could see further down into the hole, seemed to widen out into a larger cave.

The light illuminated silt down in the darkness, it was hard to tell whether we were seeing five feet into the cave or fifty.

Ian shifted, getting off of his knees and began to descend face down into the cave.  His light gradually illuminating further in, even though we were yet to see anything but blackness still.  I couldn’t let him do this alone, this sort of situation was incredibly, dangerous if the space got silted out it could become impossible to find his way out.  And as far I knew he had no experience with any kind of cave and cavern diving.

Fuck fuck fuck, please just turn around.  Of course he didn’t hear me, continuing to lower himself into the hole, fins rubbing against the walls silting up the passage behind him.

Following him into this unknown cave could get us both killed, I didn’t even have any kind of light, I had to stop him and hope he’d listen to reason.

His fins were a bit out of reach and I didn’t want to risk pulling one of and having it fall into the hole.  I inched down into the hole, avoiding his kicking fins, it would also be a shame to lose my mask into this deep cave.

My dive computer began to vibrate on my wrist, and I stole a glance down at it, I was at my maximum operating depth for my tank of nitrox.  For all you non-divers reading this, oxygen itself becomes toxic at a certain (partial) pressure.  Since we were diving enriched air it could happen much shallower.  He’d probably not bothered setting his MOD since we’d ordinarily never be able to get there on these dives with a solid bottom.

If he went any deeper he was risking an oxygen toxicity seizure, which was dangerous at any depth, but a death sentence in a cave one hundred feet from the surface.  

I squeezed down along side him, trying my best to avoid touching the wall and flooding the passage with more silt.  Grabbing his shoulder, but he continued to look forward, shining his light down into the chamber.

I had to squeeze his shoulder for him to look away, I pointed, to my computer which was flashing with its MOD warning, buzzing violently on my wrist, we were now down at 106 feet.

He took a moment to understand, it was hard to see his face fully between his mask, reg, and the silt, but he had a drunken look.  God, he was probably experiencing nitrogen narcosis now that we were down at this depth.

His eyes seemed to focus with the blinking computer, he pulled up his own console and saw that his dive computer was doing a similar buzz.

Nodding with understanding he went to turn around, but his head turned back towards the cave chamber sloped down ahead of us, opening up only a few feet further. 

I followed his eyes looking down into the chamber, we still could not see any walls, it seemed to open up larger than I had expected.  And down, at the very edge of the light, were two dots of light.

Two reflections shining back from his light, staring up at us, eyes.

I met them and stared back for a moment, 

It was hard to judge the distance, but it was something decently large, probably a nurse shark using the cave as a den, I told myself.

We both turned away from it, it was a bit awkward, in such a tight space so I gestured for him to go first, I had to move a bit further down the passage to make space for him to turn.  Leaving me facing down into the cavern, this time without his floodlight shining into the space, only a wall of darkness and clouds of silt.  I could no longer see the eyes, but I could feel them staring up at us.

I turned away and followed Ian back out of the passage, glancing back a few times down the passage.  But now we had left so much silt coming back up we couldn’t even see into the opening.

We went back up and around the boulder that had marked the entrance of the cave.  Now that we were further up Ian seemed to have much more focus, his eyes no longer cloudy and out of focus.  It seemed he’d only been narced when entering the lower depth of the hole.

Well, there was teeth to be collected, we both returned to where I had left off sorting through the sand.  He knelt on one side of my reel, I on the other, he seemed a bit shaken at his onset of narcosis, I’d been narced once or twice before and when you realized you’d basically been intoxicated and unaware of it, it unsettled you.

I remembered to check my computer and gauge, I was down to 1900 PSI and a NDL of 11 minutes.  Shit, that excursion to a lower depth had burned through a lot, without some luck I’d have some progress to make up on the later dives.

Ian continued to inch along with me, although we left enough space that we didn’t constantly silt each other out.  Getting back into the rhythm, fan, wait, search, fan, wait, search.  A few whale bones, a few smaller megalodon teeth caked in mud and growth, but nothing that would cover the charter cost, never mind my debts.

I had focused in entirely on my work, deep into tunnel vision, when a shadow passed over me.  For a moment I was worried Ian was skipping past me, to explore something else now.  But no, he was still behind me, I looked up towards the sun, and it was visible like normal, so there hadn’t been some cloud passing over.

Maybe a boat going by?  It wasn’t a guarantee we’d hear it this far down, and it wouldn’t be the first time someone hadn’t respected the dive flags.  Thats what I told myself anyway.

Whatever it was, it was gone now, just a passing shadow.  But looking up made it me realize we had made our way pretty far, nearing the edge of the secondary, smaller rocky clearing.

Swimming over, along my line was William, probably coming to see where I had gone off to, I was relieved to see him if it meant it wouldn’t be me alone keeping Ian from killing himself.  When we made eye contract he signed to me, asking if everything was ok.

I signed back that I was ok, and he nodded, glancing at his own gauge and gesturing toward it.  I looked down at my own, 1600PSI, 9 minutes, and I signed to him as such.

The three of us made our way back toward the larger clearing, it was the obvious call, Ian was a bit worse off than me on both fronts, and we were pretty far out from the anchor.  In an emergency we could surface where we were, but with a surface current and poor visibility that could mean a long difficult swim back to the boat.

Off in the distance there was a giant cloud of silt, concentrated in the larger rocky clearing which was steadily coming into view.  Three separate bubble columns were visible streaming out of the cloud, Ulysses, Brian and Hannah were all there, although they seemed to be at the far end of the clearing.

We now reached the edge ourselves and spread out from my wreck reel line, each finding our own promising spots and resuming our dig in the mud, clouding the water with silt around us.

I found myself, beginning to tunnel vision back into the search, some more whale bones into my bag, one decently large tooth, although it was severely damaged to the point that I doubted it would be worth much.  My air was low though, lower than it normally would be at this point in the dive, forgetting that could be very bad.

For now I would resume my search though, and keep an eye on my air and my NDL.  Until another shadow moved over me, more than that I felt the swell of water as something passed right over me.  I instinctively flinched, too many dives where I was hit by the stray fin of an unaware diver had conditioned me as such.  But no hit came.

I looked up to see who it had been, but there was no one there.  I looked around me though and on my right I saw the caudal (rear) fin of something pass disappear into William’s cloud of silt.

The water churned around, and the silt became even more opaque as clouds of red blood spilled into the water.

There were flashes of that large caudal fin, whipping back and forth, before it darted fully through the cloud.

I stared in shock from my knelt position, before swimming upwards to see if I could spot William, he’d clearly been bit by the… shark?  It had looked like a shark fin but it was proportioned differently and the tail had seemed to have weirdly placed segments that I’d never seen on a shark.

I hovered in front of the cloud, craning my eyes to see if I could spot William through the swirling pattern of red and grey.  It seemed whatever had attacked him had swam off, there was a floating trail of blood and silt going off to the side.  There was no sense in trying to enter it and ending up blind in the cloud.

Well unless he was gravely injured, but I’d be unable to help until I could actually see.

I continually looked around to see where the thing had gone, silent cursing the tunnel vision given by my scuba mask, now that I was frantically searching for whatever had attacked him, I could only watch a relatively small angle around me, leaving me feeling horribly exposed..  Hopefully it was just some weird shark that had given him a nibble and swam off once it realized he wasn’t a snack.

William emerged from the cloud of silt looking frantically around.  His wetsuit was torn in multiple places, blood oozing out from the cuts.  And his BCD vest was torn along a shoulder strap so that the was stuck keeping it in place with one hand.

It was a relief to see him though, even though his injuries seemed worse than I’d hoped.  He made eye contact with me and he didn’t have to signal to me that we’d calling the dive.  I gestured him to go away, I’d alert the other divers that we needed to go in, since it would mean we aren’t delaying the boats return trip.  It was hard to know for sure but given the amount of blood in the water, William would certainly need stitches, maybe a med evacuation since the boat ride was awfully long.

I swam up ahead of him, keeping an eye on him in the corner of my eye, he was capable of swimming but was clearly slowed by his injuries, once I’d made Ian aware, he could alert the others and I’d drag William along to get him in sooner, it was critical to get him out of the water if the injuries were worse than I had initially thought, but to get everyone in get the moat moving ASAP.

Approaching from the side, I grabbed Ian on the shoulder making him jump, I signaled to him that something was very wrong and we needed to end the dive immediately and surface, I pointed towards Brian, Hannah, and Ulysses, hoping he’d understand I needed him to alert them as I brought in Ulysses.

He shook his head, seemingly ready to brush me off, before his eyes focused on something over my shoulder.  I turned to follow his gaze and William had come in to view, continuing his slow swim forwards.  My vision lingered on him for a moment, he was only partially visible between the drifting silt clouds, and he kept glancing around him.

I looked back towards Ian to make sure he now understood the danger of the situation, but instead of looking at me he continued to stare towards William, pure terror in his eyes, visible even through his mask.  William was still swimming at his slow pace.  And then I saw where Ian had been looking.

A dark shadow was hovering in the nearest silt cloud, a long shape barely visible, following William, stalking him.

Before I could make any movement towards him and faster than I could react, the shadow darted out in a flash of movement.  It grabbed William, yanking him out of view as they passed out of view into the distant haze.

I jumped back nearly spitting out my regulator, bumping into Ian in the process.  Frantically looking around, I swam upwards for a better view.  I had to help William somehow.

As I reached over the cloud they’d passed through I saw William still being dragged away rapidly by the creature.

In a moment he’d had been dragged out of view, off into the haze.

Below me, Ian had bolted the opposite direction, already passing the rest of the group.  Fuck, he wasn’t going to bring them in, probably scared out of his mind to think straight.

I swam along my line back towards the boulder as quickly as I could, trying my best to control my breathing, not even bothering to check my air although I knew I was probably getting dangerously low.  Deliberately swimming a bit higher in the water column, to limit the air consumption a bit if I could.  

It had been too fast to get a good look at it, but it had looked like a giant fish, with a bulky body, like a grouper, but it had been maybe 15 feet long, and narrower.  The face had looked strange too, I’d only gotten glimpses, but it had looked so alien, almost like a grasshopper with massive canine teeth.

The visibility seemed to be receding, maybe from all the silt in the water or the new current that was starting to sweep across.  I didn’t get far swimming along the line before I glanced at my computer, I was at 700 PSI and 4 minutes of NDL, though my deco limit would rise with depth so that was less concerning.

There was so much blood in the water off ahead of me, but there was nothing I could do.

I flipped around and swam as fast as I could back along my line, I would alert the other three that there was something seriously wrong and then get the hell out of the water, fuck a safety stop.

I made my way back to the larger clearing, flinching at ever movement of water around me, every shadow at the edge of my view, fearing that I would be pounced on like William had been.  As that clearing came into view, the silt clouds had mostly dispersed and I did not see any bubble columns.  Whether they had decided to come in on their own or Ian really had alerted them of the danger I couldn’t say, but at least it let me continue at my breakneck pace.  Although it dawned on me that I may now be completely alone down here with that thing.  One way or the other.

There it was, the anchor, I would follow that line up to safety and make it out of here.  It was just on the edge of my vision, edging closer.

There, on my right, floating alongside me barely perceptible, probably 30 feet over.

Kicking as hard as I could, my legs and fins straining against the water, I swam left, no reason to bother hiding I had spotted it, I was so exposed down here and that wasn’t going to change upon reaching the anchor line.  I frantically searched for anything that could help me.

But there was nothing, I was still 95 feet down, I wasn’t going to magically find a speargun or even a knife down here randomly, and the idea of being in the middle of the water column with that thing stalking me was a no-go, even if I was dangerously low on air.

I could still feel its eyes on me, there had to be something I could do, it had stalked William for less than a minute so I didn’t have much time, and it was so much faster than I could swim.

And then off ahead of me emerging from the haze, I saw a narrow ravine, running along the ground, I’d barely be able to squeeze into it, it wasn't much but there was aa chance the thing would be unable to reach me inside.

I immediately changed course, swimming toward the ravine, taking off my bcd as I did so, my regulator shifting awkwardly in my mouth as I swung the bcd and tank around.  Out of the corner of my eye there was a flash of movement as it darted forward.

With one desperate push of movement, I delved into the ravine turning on my back and holding my bcd and more importantly my steel tank out in front of me.  Just as I held it up, the creature smashed into the tank.  Its jaws gripping onto it but unable to break the steel, thrashing about violently churning the water around me as I held on to the tank for dear life.  It effortlessly pushed the tank past my arms, knocking my breath out of me, and my regulator.  I could feel one of my ribs break as it continued to push into against me as hard as it could, continually snapping its jaws at different places on the tank.

Salt water poured into my mouth and into my lungs as the violent movement and lack of air making it impossible not to take a few breaths, my regulator floating limply in front of my face.

I was sure that this was it, even if it didn’t kill me directly, I was about to drown down here.

But just as my vision began to darken, from the effort and lack of air, the creature stopped its effort.  It floated backwards and stared down at me, I immediately grabbed the regulator and put it into my mouth, coughing into it and taking as many deep breaths as I could.

All the while, it continued to stare down at me, and I finally got a good look at the full thing, along its scull there was black, exposed bone.  Its face looked almost insect like, but with smaller eyes and a massive mouth with huge incisors.  Its body was like a wider, armored shark, with more of what looked like the exposed black bone.

Turning away from me, it peered off into the distance, and then it swam out of view in an instant.

I laid there for a while, still trying to catch my breath, glancing down at my computer though I was at 300PSI and 2 minutes of NDL. Shit, that would barely be enough to reach the surface.

I poked my head up around, to see if it was waiting for me just out of sight, ready to duck back under the tank if necessary, but it wasn’t anywhere around me that I could see.  And the anchor was on the edge of my vision, so at least I was still close.

Except, there right next to the anchor, were Brian, Hannah and Ulysses, swimming up to it casually.  Ian must not have alerted them like I’d hoped, he’d probably just swam past and they had just gone to explore another section for fossils.

My eyes widened as I spotted a shadow that was trailing along behind them, the creature it was now stalking them.  I had to warn them.

But as I tried to swim over to them, I found my tank was stuck in the ridge.  The creature had pushed so hard against it, it had been fully wedged in between the rocks.

And so I could only watch as the three of them reached the anchor, beginning their ascent, Brian and Hannah swimming side by side, Ulysses a little behind them, floating a bit higher too, right in their blindspot.

I strained against the tank, jolting it against the hard stone, it budged a little as I did so but it was wedged tight.

In a flash the creature emerged swimming up from the side and grabbing Ulysses in one continuous movement, dragging him off away from them and out of sight, probably back towards its den.

Brian and Hannah didn’t even notice, Ulysses screams wouldn’t carry through the water.

They continued their ascent, reaching the edge my vision before stopping to look back for Ulysses, only then noticing he was not there. They looked around and shrugged to each other, probably assuming he’d spotted some fossils.

I pushed against the stone, the tank holding tight, but every jiggle seemed to get me a little further out.  One last good push got me there, the weight of the tank now being my only resistance from pushing it out of the crevasse.  

My air was now at 200 PSI and my NDL had fully ran out, my computer flashing about a mandatory decompression stop. That was obviously not going to be an option though, I could deal with the bends on the surface.  That was clearly better than the alternatives. I threw the BCD back on and swam as fast as I could to the anchor and back up the anchor line.

At shallower depths I would use less air, but the bends were unavoidable at this rate, and far from my biggest concern.  I’d get on the boat as quickly as possible, have them send a distress signal and put me on oxygen.  Above all else I’d make sure Brian and Hannah get out of this.

100 PSI now, not much to work with at all.  Normally I’d at least do a decompression stop under the boat with the tank they leave hanging underneath but with the creature in the water I couldn’t take that chance.

Further up the anchor line Hannah was floating holding on to the anchor line frantically looking around, Brian no where in sight.  She glanced down at me and I could see her eyes wide through the haze and her scuba mask.  Bubbles flowed from her regulator constantly, as she was probably close to hyperventilating.

I couldn’t be sure whether she had actually seen the creature of if she Brian had just been pulled away without her notice but I knew I needed to get us onto the boat even if it meant dragging her.

Somewhere out in the haze around us was the creature, just out of view, it could be watching both of us at this very moment.

Even as I swam up along side her, she was still in her frantic state, I had to grab her shoulders just to hold her steady, her eyes darting around through her mask.  I signaled that we needed to surface immediately ,that something was very wrong.

At first she started to sign something back, her hands a mess of different symbols, but she seemed to recognize how serious I was from my expression and her pace slowed.  She looked down a little, and I became aware of how i probably looked to her, my wetsuit torn all over the place and body covered in cuts and scrapes.

I pointed towards the boat and she seemed to understand now.

We both continued up the line, the ship looming above us the front of it clearly visible while the back hidden in the shadow.  We reached the same level as the underside of the boat and swam along underneath the hull.

As we swam along I kept looking around us expending to see the shape dart out and grab one of us, but it didn’t come.  There would be nothing we could do if it decided to attack us here, only the hull above us and open water surrounding us.

Only the grey haze in every direction.

About halfway across the boat I felt my air begin to run out, each breath gave more resistance until there was no air left to pull.

Fighting the urge to bolt for the ladders ahead of us, I could still feel that thing’s eyes on us.

Somehow I knew if I panicked it would be on me in moments, although I couldn’t say for sure whether it was really even watching.

Hannah and I reached both ladders sticking down into the water, swinging ourselves around them and hoisting ourselves up, out of the water.

As I burst to the surface I gasped for air spitting out my regulator.

Hannah surfaced alongside me, “That thing, it, it got Brian, I-”

Before she could get out another word she was pulled off of the ladder by her leg, splashing into the water.

I could only watch as she was pulled further beneath the surface, flooding the ocean water with blood.  In a flash she was gone.

Bubbles rose up around me, coming from deeper and deeper as she was dragged down.

After standing on the rungs in shock, watching those bubbles, I became painfully aware of my own legs still beneath the surface, on the lower ladder rungs.  I continued the climb up and the crew helped me aboard.

Ian was there, sitting wrapped in a towel, it seemed he really had just booked it for the boat.  But as I reached the deck I stumbled, my strength leaving me as the symptoms of decompression sickness washed over me.

The crew rushed to get the boat on the move, alerting the coastguard and trying to get me in, it seemed they were aware no one else was coming up after seeing Hannah get dragged away and whatever Ian had said.

But as we got moving I heard a crew member cry out, and someone pointed off behind the boat.

I craned my neck from my position on the floor, and saw the head of the creature peeking out from between the waves and sunlight glint.  Its black eyes staring towards us, seemingly right towards me.

I’m writing all this from the nearest decompression chamber, they kept me on my back the whole ride in, although my memory of getting here is quite blurry as my body fought through stages of exhaustion and DCS.

That thing looked ancient, unlike anything I've ever seen while diving.

It seemed to enjoy picking us off one by one, pulling four innocent people down into the depths never to be seen again.
