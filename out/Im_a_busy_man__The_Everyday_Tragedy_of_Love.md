I am a busy man. My wife and I have been married for over 35 years. We met at a lecture, started seeing each other, fell in love. She anticipated my every wish. She stayed home and raised the children, giving up herself and her dreams completely. She was perfect. I, however, was busy with my company. I work in finance. No, I live finance. You want to take out a mortgage? I'm your man. A loan with the lowest possible interest rate? Pfft, no such thing, but hey, I'm happy to pretend the terms are fair while I bend you over the table and bleed you dry like a Christmas goose. I'm just a busy man.

My day begins promptly at 5:30 a.m. My wife Elisabeth loves having breakfast with me, eggs with salt. Soft-boiled, of course. Today I take the time to set the newspaper aside. On other days I'm already leaving the house by then, eager to get to the office as early as possible, with plenty of time to pay my attentions to one of the many trainees I find rather appealing. I tell my wife I need to prepare documents for clients. But today I take the time to have breakfast with her. For far too long she had already felt alone at the start of the day, especially after the children moved out, the children who had kept her company in my place, more than ever.

I pour her coffee, lots of milk and lots of sugar, gentle and sweet, just like her nature. But she doesn't drink.

I turn on the radio, tuning it to her favorite station. After a commercial break, it plays the song we used to dance to so often when we were young. But today, we don't dance anymore.

Whenever she used to talk to me, telling me about her favorite television show, her thoughts, or her fears, I brushed it all aside with empty platitudes. I never listened. I only ever thought about the things I had to do. About myself. Today, I listen to her. I hang on her every word. But she doesn't complain. She remains silent. Almost as if, for the first time in her life, she is content.

After breakfast I do the dishes. It has been years. She should relax, she should rest. Two stories filled with the appearance of happiness; she always keeps the house spotless and fulfills my every wish. But today, today I should lighten her burden, repay her for all her hard work.

I kiss her on the cheek. Every day, though I never noticed, she made herself beautiful. She put on makeup, blush, mascara, powder. She wore expensive perfume. Yet it was never enough to distract me from my affairs at work. I loved the thrill of the forbidden more than the constancy and loyalty of true love.

Today, I drive into town and buy lipstick, makeup, and nail polish. All from her favorite brands. She deserves the attention. She should be beautiful, as beautiful as her soul. And I should truly see her with both my eyes, her real beauty, her unclouded truth, the one that everyday routine kept hidden from me for far too long.

The telephone rings. When my wife used to call, I rarely picked up. Today, I let it ring. Because everyone who truly matters in my life is here in this room with me. I don't care about work.

I take my time and paint her fingernails a deep red. I apply lipstick, but she doesn't kiss me. I spray perfume onto her neck, behind her ears. I kiss her, one last time, before the undertaker comes to take her away.

She was the glue that held everything in my life together. Now, nothing does.
