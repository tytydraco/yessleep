I can’t tell if I’m going insane, or Clark is.

Okay, so long story short, I changed my phone passcode in a fit of paranoia after that girls’ night, and I feel like he’s been acting weird ever since. He was really happy to see me when I got back to his place (he had gone back to his place and fallen asleep there, so one of my friends dropped me off) and we cuddled for an hour before falling asleep.

But then when I woke up the next morning, he’d gotten up before me, and he was in a sour mood.

“I think I’m coming down with something,” Clark said, pacing around. “I had a sore throat when I was going to sleep last night and I woke up with a pretty bad headache.”

“Oh no,” I said. “I’ll make you some tea or something.”

“It’s fine,” he said, waving me away and starting to make his coffee like usual. “I just hope you didn’t bring the flu back from the club.”

“It was hardly a club,” I said, rolling my eyes behind his back as I started microwaving some oatmeal for breakfast. “It’s a bar with a dance floor. And I feel fine.”

“I just think we should wear masks if we go out today. We were gonna get lunch at that place this afternoon.”

“You don’t want to stay inside and take it easy?” I asked. “We can check it out another time.”

“No, we made a plan, we should do it,” he said. “It’s got an outdoor spot. I’ll pay for everything.”

He coughed politely before drinking his coffee.

“Oh, that hits the spot. I think I have some extra masks somewhere around here.”

He immediately got up again. I sat and ate my oatmeal as he rummaged around the kitchen. Tammy paced around his legs and stuck her nose into the open cupboards near the floor.

“Does it make sense to wear masks if we’re still gonna be eating lunch there?” I asked.

Clark stopped and looked at me. “I mean, it’s a little ad hoc, but it’s better than nothing.”

“I feel fine, though.”

“You might be an asymptomatic carrier,” Clark said, going back to his searching. “You should wear a mask just to be safe.”

I glanced over at the chessboard on the kitchen table. It was packed up, but it’d remained where we’d left it after I’d beaten him once. Clark had never asked me to play with him again after that. I kind of missed it, to be honest, even losing to him was fun since he was mostly a good sport about it, and spending the afternoon doing that would be better than sitting in a restaurant wearing a mask. But what did I know.

It was a nice day when we went out for lunch later, at least. It was finally starting to get cold, and although the trees weren’t completely bare, there was a nice combination of skeletal branches and fallen red leaves on the ground that hadn’t fully lost their color. I felt a bit self-conscious next to him, both of us wearing masks and waiting in line to order at the counter, but his arm around my waist assuaged some of that. He’s so much warmer than me. We always joke that I’m his corpse girlfriend. He had stopped coughing when we’d headed out (I let him drive us, because I still felt bad about last night) and I wondered if he was feeling better. I hoped he wasn’t just holding it all in to look healthier in public.

Anyway, we were waiting in line, I was scanning the menu board behind the counter, Clark was fiddling with his phone like always, when he said it.

“Can you see if your texts to me are going through?” Clark asked. “I think I might be missing texts from people, and I just want to see if it’s a problem with my phone, or someone else’s phone, or like, my phone carrier, or what.”

“Sure,” I said. “Right now?”

Clark nodded. I pulled out my phone to text him, but since I was wearing a mask, the face recognition unlock thing didn’t work. Annoyed, I pulled the mask down to my chin for a moment, but enough of it was still obscuring my lower face and jaw that my phone stubbornly refused to unlock. I brought up the keypad and my finger hovered over it as I paused to remember the new code I’d set.

The new code I’d set, in a fit of absurd paranoia. *Assume your shit’s already compromised.* My friend raised her glass to me from across the bar. Clark had a sixth sense for when I was making plans with my friends.

He was standing right next to me, looking down at my phone screen expectantly.

“I actually really have to go to the bathroom,” I said, letting my hand fall to my side.

“But we’re about to order,” Clark said, confused. “It’ll just take a second.” He nodded to my phone.

“I know, I’m sorry,” I said. “Can you just get me the breakfast burrito? I’ll be right back.”

Clark shrugged and turned back to the counter as I pulled my mask back up and hurried to the bathroom around the corner of the restaurant. I had to make sure I wasn’t crazy, and in order to do that, I needed to eliminate all possibilities.

I checked the logins to my cloud account. There weren’t many, just a few from a web browser in my neighborhood, and a few from a phone and web browser in Clark’s area. That would make sense, because I’d brought my laptop over to his place a few times. Still, though.

I changed my password for my cloud account, too. I don’t even know why I did it at that point. It said it’d sign me out of all other devices, and I didn’t look forward to having to log in again and do the authentication stuff on my laptop, but I was just feeling weird about the entire thing. And I think you’re supposed to change your passwords every once in a while too, just to be safe.

*Testing*, I texted Clark. The message showed as delivered, and then as read an instant later. At least that was working.

We ate our lunch in contented silence. It was later in the afternoon by the time we’d gotten our food. The shadows of the trees stretched over the outdoor area. There was a chilly breeze blowing, so we were the only people out there and we’d finally taken off our masks.

I’d been hungry still since I hadn’t had dinner the night before, because the disagreement with Clark had ruined my appetite. But I was feeling better in the fresh air, with a burrito in me. I laid my head on his shoulder.

“How are you feeling?” I asked.

“Better now,” he said, after a moment. He cleared his throat experimentally. “Yeah, much better. Thanks.”

The lighting was good, so I took out my phone and took a photo of us together. Clark barely noticed me do it until he saw the screen light up, and he gave me a very genuine smile. Seeing him like that, I felt like such an idiot for being paranoid about the passwords. Maybe the conversation about the car stuff the previous day was still reverberating around my brain. This whole thing still felt so surreal. We were barely a month in, at least on my time.

“Cute,” he said. “Love you.”

“I love you too,” I said, and kissed him on the cheek. “Thank you for lunch.”

“Of course,” he said. “I’m glad we still went out.”

We decided I’d spend the night at his place again. He dropped me off at my apartment so I could hang out with my cat, and then grab my laptop since tonight was Sunday and I still had some emails to send before tomorrow. I got to his place around dinnertime and we ate and then settled down on the couch together.

Around that time was when I felt like an idiot again, but for a different reason. I had been working on the couch while he was watching TV next to me, and then I closed my laptop and got up to use the bathroom. Then when I came back, Clark was still on the couch watching TV. I opened my laptop and saw some existing text disappear from the login screen, as if it’d been waiting there and just timed out right as I was reading it. But I’ve always been a fast reader.

*Your account is locked. Try again in 1 minute.*
