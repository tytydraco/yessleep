I'm writing this on my bathroom floor with the door locked and my phone at 31%. I don't know how long I can keep typing, so I'm going to tell you everything fast.

NEPA took the light at 9:40 tonight, like it does almost every night. My generator is out of fuel, so I did what everyone in my compound does. I sat by the window to catch some air and watched the road.

We have one working light on our street, a solar lamp the landlord put up after the robberies last year. At about 10:15 I noticed a man standing just outside the circle of that lamp. Not under it. Just past it, in the dark. Tall, arms hanging straight at his sides, facing my building.

I thought nothing of it. People wait outside when the light is gone. I went to find my torch.

When I sat back down, the solar lamp flickered. Off for one second, then on again. And the man was under it.

I didn't see him walk. I'm sure of that. He was in the dark, then he was in the light, and his head was tilted up toward the fourth floor. Toward my window. The lamp was behind him, so his face was only a shadow.

I told myself it was a trick of the dark. At 10:48 the lamp flickered again, and he was at our gate. At 11:05 it flickered a third time, and he was inside the compound, standing next to Mr. Okafor's car, still looking up.

That is when my phone lit up. Our block has a WhatsApp group that has been dead for months. A message came in from Mama Chidi, who lives in 4B, right across the corridor from me.

"Please nobody should go near the window. Please. Do not call out to him."

I almost felt relieved. Someone else could see him too. I wrote to her privately: "Mama, are you seeing this? Are you okay?"

She replied right away. "I see him. I am afraid. Open your door, let me come and sit with you."

I was already standing up. Then I realized something, and I'm still shaking. Mama Chidi doesn't type in English. She has told me many times that she can't read or write it. Her son always sends her messages for her. Her son is in Dubai.

I went to my door and put my eye to the peephole.

She was standing in the corridor. It was pitch dark, but I could see her because her phone was in her hand, glowing. She was in her nightwear, with her wrapper loose. Her face was turned up toward the ceiling, and she was smiling. It was a wide, wrong smile, the kind you make when you are holding it for someone else's photo.

My phone buzzed in my hand. "Why are you not opening? I'm right here."

I watched her hands. Her thumbs weren't touching the screen. They weren't moving at all. The messages kept coming anyway.

I backed away from the door so slowly that I didn't make a sound. That was at 11:40. I locked myself in the bathroom, because it's the only room in the flat with a lock on the inside.

There's a small window above the bathtub. It looks out over the compound and the road. I'm lying on the floor beneath it, and I'm not going to look. I already know the man isn't standing next to Mr. Okafor's car anymore, because the solar lamp flickered a minute ago and I saw the flash of it on the ceiling.

The knocking at my front door started a few minutes ago. Three slow knocks, a pause, then three more. Now there's a voice too. It sounds like Mama Chidi, calling my name softly in Igbo, over and over, the way she does when she brings me food. She hasn't stopped to take a single breath.

Her voice is still coming from the corridor. But the sound I just heard didn't come from my front door. It came from above me.

Three slow taps on the bathroom window, on the fourth floor, from the outside.
