 I heaved a sigh of relief at catching the subway at the nick of time. As usual I was unusually late to leave my friends Jasmine due to my long tongue. We girls are not notorious for being a chatter box in vain or because of a foolish conspiracy of men, because I, myself am a proof. A biggest proof. Pulling my auburn hair into a pony tail I rushed to get a seat and didn’t have to worry for that because nearly all the seats were vacant. In fact the compartment was quiet empty with just five more passengers. I glanced at my wrist watch. Being out of town and now out of time, I felt jitters because my sister Maya was going to give me a tough time. I knew that because ever since I came to New York to college, it seemed to me that Maya and my mother had swapped their souls. My small town parents, back in New Haven, were always frantic with worry for their only two daughter’s safety. I wished Rick, Maya’s colleague and lover, to hop in tonight. It would buy me time to sneak to my bed room and not having listened to her tantrums. So what if I went out of town! For Heaven’s sake, I was twenty one and the big city was not going to eat me. I gave a timid welcome smile to the old woman sitting in front of me and got a grimace back. *Uh,* I thought. What has happened to the night courtesy to the polite strangers! I shook the thought of nice behavior roughly away and stole a look at my right, and swiftly turned left.

 A very burly man with big biceps and numerous tattoos was playing with his I-pod. His every piercing screamed *dangerous*. That was the kind of people that had made Maya and my mom twined together. A woman was sleeping with her baby cooing and playing with her brown curly hair. A very peaceful sight. I found my lips smiling again and I saw the man reading the newspaper. He was middle aged and seemed to have found something in the paper that had a very intense value to him. Which reminded me that I had a reading stuff too. I rummaged through my enormous bag and took out the battered copy of my favorite serial *The* *Vampires Chronicle*. I lost the count of the following seconds reading the bloodshed and the devil’s attires until I couldn’t control myself. How could I ever explain my love for the supernatural life!  I nearly squealed with delight, ‘Oh, I love horror’, I frantically turned the page.

“Well that’s not a good thing.”

I started and jumped. There sat, a man, just besides me all of a sudden. When I looked up at him, he smiled. I felt a chill that went all through my body trembling my spine and raised my hair all over my body. He was a big man, though he was sitting and I wasn’t sure of his exact height but he was sure taller because I had to crane my neck to watch his face. He had a pale white complexion─ pale with dark probing eyes that seemed sarcastic with his thick arched eye brows. His lips were almost white and turned up in one side as if he was smiling politely. But I couldn’t find anything polite about him. He had a large built with raven black tuxedo on and he appeared to me very formal. Formal and frightening. At the same time.

He was handsome; no doubt, but still I in took a huge breathe for calming myself.

 “Um, what did you just say? When did you come here?” A very rude question but my mind was perplexed.

“I am there all the time.”

“But *I* didn’t see you.”

He laughed, and his teeth sparkled. My eyes widened at that. “That’s because you were too busy reading this horror fiction. And also enjoying it very thoroughly, I must add.”

“Yeah,” I was senile. Maybe he came after I started reading. I shouldn’t be so blind not noticing *this* man.

“And to your first question: I said it’s not good for lovely young lady like you to indulge their minds in the complexity of bloodshed and frights. It’s bad for their mental well-being.” His voice was grim but soothing. I immediately felt relaxed despite of his eerie looks.

“Have you read this before?” I put the book aloft.

“No. But I know what these horror fiction writers broods over: Vampires and demons falling in love with humans and starting a war for their defense.” He wrinkled his long nose. “As if such creatures don’t have any better things to do. Really amusing.”

I went defensive. I’d always liked horror fictions and when there was romance involved with the supernatural─ it always become my favorite. I could speak about them for hours without stopping once. “It’s not *that* amusing. It’s, I think, very romantic…”

He cackled. “*Romance?* Oh, yes. Why of course, our maiden’s ideas of romance always roams around a damsel in distress and a super hero who could solve all their problems with alien powers.”

“You think it’s absurd?” I hissed.

“Yes. Very foolish to even dream about the possibility of demons loving humans and then constructing their lives closer to humans. Suppose you meet a demon, what would be your reaction? Don’t try to be callous about it; answer it truthfully.”

I took his question seriously. “I may take a swoon if I meet one. But…”

“Not fall in love with them.” He countered.

“Yeah, may be….” I stammered. “What do you prefer? Action” Comedy? Thriller? Typically men’s choice. But all these are out dated and out of world efforts. Such things happen all the time in reality. So why not explore something that’s doubtful?”

He nodded. “But I don’t like all this stuff. I take interest in History and Philosophy. Facts and exploration towards facts. If you’re doubtful about something then go and look for the signs that lead to the darkness of an unknown quest. Why make fun and comic the inspirations of other creatures?”

“May be these theories matches with the real ones.” I was suddenly earnest. It was just five minutes ago that I met this fascinating frightening man and already I was arguing with him about …ghosts and demons?

“Impossible.” He scoffed.

“So what are your theories? What are these horrific creatures that haunt us and still don’t reveal themselves?” Then I added after a thought: “Do you even believe in them?”

“Yes, I do.” He was uneasy. “But my theories won’t interest you.”

He smiled then, flashing his teeth. “There’s no romance involved in my logics but it’s closer to reality and facts that I could guess. If there’s existence of another force among us then there should be difference between them and us. If there’s not then this discrimination is irrelevant.”

I had to admit I was rather impressed. Like most mature people, he wasn’t avoiding saying these were childish and fantasy issues. He wasn’t making fun of my absurd fixation but he was ready to share his own views. So, I wanted to hear him but differently.

“Okay, then. Let’s interview you!” I exclaimed.

He already was pondering over something. “Interview?”

“Yes. I am a student of journalism in college. You didn’t ask my name. I’m Carol Frank. And because you said so yourself that your theories are rather going to be dry and devoid of interest for a romance lover like me then let’s make it fun.”

“How?” He smiled. “But keep your voice down. You don’t want audience, don’t you?” I looked at the way he was pointing, towards the old woman. She was staring at me with her mouth open.

“Has she heard me?” I whispered to my partner.

“No,” he used the normal volume. “But she’s getting a gist of our conversation. So speak low and try to look casual when you talk.”

“Sure.” The woman looked away now that I caught her staring at me. I turned toward the man and put my book inside my bag. I was excited to pass the rest of the minutes this way. You don’t find gorgeous looking vile stranger who alluringly agrees and disagrees with you, every day.

“Now what’s in your mind, Carol.”

“Do you know how to act?”

“No.”

“Well, then. Are you ready to share with me your theories?”

“Yeas, of course.”

“OK. But you gotta do it in a unique way.” I told him while hiding excitement but it was hard to suppress. He was going to tell me *horror stories*! “You actually have to act like demon while telling me and I’d interview you. And tell me exactly a way a demon can tell like you know that stuff. Agreed?”

“Ridiculous.”

“Yeah, I know.” I laughed loudly and he hushed me. I stopped because now the old woman was gaping at me with a strange expression that I didn’t guess first. Anger? No, it was confused. She was confused and afraid. And why not? The man beside me was frightening but not any more to me. So I turned my attention to him again.

“But it is fun!” I insisted. “I don’t know if I’ll get a chance with a real demon but we could pretend it for my sake. I mean for fun’s sake.” I corrected myself. He gave me a knowing look. “For time pass?”

“But our passengers won’t think its fun to discuss such things at such late night.” He was watching the woman with an intense look. The woman was not watching me now. Her face was turned toward the left like she was careful not to watch us.

“Don’t mind them….” I realized that this man must have a name. How rude of me not asking! So, I asked him.

“My name is Damon.”

“Oh,” he was already playing his character.

“And how old are you?”  
”I’m hundred and fifty-seven.”

“So you think that demons could live that long? Not a normal life span like ours?”

“I don’t think so, I know so.” He said grimly. “We all are immortals.”

“Really!” I wanted to punch myself for acting stupidly and jumping out of my self-designed story. I was such a bad interviewer.

“Move on,” he urged softly.

“Are you married?” I blurted out.

“No. We don’t have such courtship rituals.”

“Then how do you produce offspring?”

“We don’t reproduce at all. After every fifty years, our kinds sit at a full moon night and use the power of darkness. It depends on your skills and strength. Those of us who are capable, creates an image that reflects us in capability and character: A shadow, as we call it. This shadow wanders it on its own and gains strength eventually. It might take a century but that’s how it’s done.”

A very philosophical theory. I was captured by it. “How does this, this shadow gains strength? By drinking blood?”

“No,” he declined. “That’s vampirism. And they don’t even exist. Only myth and fiction.”

“Then by eating raw meat? Like Cannibals. *Jack, the ripper*?”

“Nothing that dramatically.” He shook his head. “The shadow sucks the soul out of living beings.”

There was a sudden silence. I felt color draining from my cheeks. His matter-of-fact voice was so sharp like it was blatant truth. He searched my face and his own face became worried. “Have I frightened you? I am sorry but you wanted to know. I must say you were looking for more imaginative than this.”

I tried to breathe normally. “You’ve really freaked me out, Mr.?”

“Damon.”

“I’m asking for your real name.”

“So I’m telling you.”

“Damon?”

“Yes, you didn’t believe me when I told you?”

I ignored his question. “Like in *Vampire Diaries*?”

“Yes, but I’m not a vampire. I told you that vampires don’t exist and neither do those half-breeds you call as werewolves. We, demon, though are the facts of the true creations.”

This self-fabricated story was getting too out of control.

“What do you eat?” I whispered.

“Lives.” He whispered back, and his eyes darkened some more.

I flinched and turned my head away. Why did it feel like if he was telling the truth? Suddenly his presence their out of thin air took on a different meaning.

I glanced at the old woman. She was still giving me quizzical looks like I was…mad?

“Yes, she thinks you’ve lost your mind.” Damon confirmed that and I started. How could he answer a question that was inside my head? How could he hear a thought?

“Why?” That was a crucial question.

“Because she thinks you’re busy in soliloquy. A first sign of losing your head, according to layman’s point of view.”

“She’s thinking I’m talking to myself? But why?”

“Because she can’t see me. I am only visible to *you*.”

That explains why. I felt like suffocating.

“You are lying.” I gulped, trying to appear brave.

“Ask her if you don’t believe me.” He shrugged and gave me an open invitation. And I couldn’t accept that. If I did…. Then what? Was he a *demon*? A true demon? What should I do now?

“Nothing dear,” he was again using his mind reading. “You can’t do anything against me. Just stay relax.”

“Are, um, you a mmind reader too like *Sookie* and *Edward*?” My voice trembled as I said it by looking at my numb legs. Who could blame me?

“No,” he said softly. “Your face expresses each query very visibly. It’s all written on your face. Human’s mind is a very easy thing to see and read. Their thoughts and actions─ no matter how much they claim is complex─ is clear as mirror.”

“Ah,” I could just say that. *Help me! Help me!*  I was afraid to move an inch. Afraid that one vibration and he’d chew me apart.

“You can carry on with your interview, Carol.” He cajoled sweetly like nothing was happening.

*My last interview*, I thought.

“Where do you live?” My tone pitched higher at the word live. Surely, I wasn’t going to live too long, that was for sure.

“Every where.” He answered. “Arboretum, vacant buildings and shacks. Humans have assumed our habitat correctly─ we like seclusion.”

“Where do you prefer to live?”

“Tree trunks.” He saw my confusion and explained. “It’s like living in nest or a tree house.”

“Oh,” I lamented. “Is this…  Is this way…you look….is it real? Do demons resemble humans?”

“Yes, but our essence is altogether different from yours.”

“Do you guys have souls?” I hushed.

He smiled and a malicious pleasure sparkled in his eyes. “If we had, then why would *we* suck *yours*?”

I looked away, trying to calm my pulse that seemed to be beating from every inch of my body. I was trapped in a net and there was no way out for me. But still my survival instincts kicked in, urging me to find a way to stay alive. There must be a method to kill this monster too. The world and its creature should have maintenance in the balance of life and death’s circle.

Oh, dam all my favorite horror fiction. Why couldn’t this demon fall in love with me and leave me alive like in most movies? I wasn’t a self-lover but I knew I was beautiful enough─ like Rosemary, or better than *Bella, Elena, Sue, Sookie*, and so many others. Apparently this immortal was immune to me…

I sighed in frustration at ruminating over stupid characters while my life was in precipice.

I should be like…*Buffy*, like *Mina*!

“Can you be killed?” My tongue slipped.

“Yes,” he wasn’t careful at all by my question.

My heart raced like a mad thing. “How? Can you tell me?”

He shrugged. “If you must. We could be killed at only one time─ when we are very vulnerable and that is our shadow’s duration. If we’re not presented with enough living bodies to feed then death could become our shadow.”

I couldn’t believe that he was stupid enough to provide me a weapon.

“I’m telling you the truth.” He was reading my face. “Whether you trust it or not.” I nodded shakily. “But don’t think about it; I’m not a shadow any more. I can’t be killed by any bullet or amulet.”

“Never?” I asked timidly. My last hope had diminished.

“Ever.” The man with the I-pod, took out his cell phone and began to check something in it. Messages perhaps. I was squirming inside by outside remained stolid.

Oh Mommy, Daddy, Anna….Good bye. Every one was right. Horror is bad. Horror is *very bad.*

“Do you want to continue?”

My stop was ten minutes away. Ten minutes to live some more. So, why stopping the interview and give this demon a pleasure of influencing me and proving his point about staying away from horror right? I won’t die before death. It was my childhood wish to talk to a devil. So, here it is. Wish granted. No regrets.

“Have you ever felt pity on your victims?”

“Pity!” He was incredulous, and ran his hand through his neatly coiffed hair. “Don’t insult me. We never feel pity on any one. We never play with our food. Involvement with our victims is a violation of code of our nature. Speak for yourselves, humans eat meat. Do you feel pity on those animal while you rip them apart and bleed your hands and tear out their flesh while spray pepper over them while eating. You can find it barbaric from animal’s point of view but there is never any emotion involved with the food?”

“But I am a vegetarian.” I felt like crying.

“To each its own.” He smiled and there was cruelty in it. “How often do you eat?”

“Once a year.”

Oh My God! I am his annual victim!

“Does it hurt?” My voice broke.

“What?” He was astonished.

“Dying! When you take the soul out…does it hurt…to the victims?”

“Not at all. Not a mew; but why do you ask?”

“Casual curiosity for time pass!” I snapped sarcastically.

The old woman was relaxed on her seat, her face lolling to the ceiling. Surely, she’d written me off as a wacko and crazy. That gray-haired man was still immersed in reading. Would my face appear on tomorrow’s paper? Would this man remember my face? Tears began to drench my cheeks…The last five minutes of my life…

“Why are you crying?” He leaned toward me.

I hiccupped, no longer afraid of our proximity. “Why do you think?”

“Oh, I know.” He nodded. “The train has almost reached its destination and its time to part. It’s all written in fate. These days adolescents doesn’t believe in kismet but always remember dear Carol, that tonight our encounter was written in the pages of fate. You’re a devil-lover and your sub conscious desires in its extremity brought interest in me that I never show to humans. It’s not every day that I reveal my true identity and admit my secrets. But frankly, it was awesome to be interviewed by you.” I said a quick prayer in my mind. He was saying the farewell words. “You’re good at Q and A, but what you lack is grip. Your emotions and enthusiasm ruins your command on a normal flow of interrogation that must follow in an accurate pattern.”

*Oh great*! I thought. *Before killing me he’s criticizing!*

“But meeting with a lovely girl who not just loves supernatural but also expresses true courage and joy at meeting with one is very rare. Dear, I am very impressed with your valor and vigor.” He smiled affectionately.

“My honor!” I turned my head in his direction. The train slowed down. My heart nearly shattered when he leaned towards me and opened his mouth. I closed my eyes, embracing death with the compliment and his thick breathe caressed my face. So much for the lovers of demons…

“Take care, Carol.”

The train stopped.

I waited.

And I waited.

Nothing.

Nothing?

I opened my eyes slowly. The light out there blinded my pupils momentarily. Was I in the other world? I couldn’t believe it that it was so quick. I felt…alive…normal. Not dead, for sure.

Then my head scanned the cabin. The train! The seat next to me was empty.

The demon was gone.

I stood up and my legs shook violently. How could he leave me alive? Has he taken my soul?

But I was living. I was standing. I was alive! Thank you! Thank you!

“See? I am alive! He left me alive!” I screamed with mirth and trotted toward the woman. She was still looking at the ceiling. The so-called dangerous boy was sitting with his I-pod, his face lolling down now. The woman with the child was still sleeping…her chest wasn’t heaving.

The newspaper had fallen from the old man’s hand.

Nobody was breathing….

I was the only living person in the compartment.

My mind went numb, my senses felt cut. With heavy steps I quickly left the train, unable to think or react. I didn’t want to think at all.

I took a taxi and reached home. A wished, Rick was with Anna in their room, so I went straight to my own and wrapped a blanket around me while convincing myself that it was all a nightmare.

One question that I missed─ and he did criticize me on leaving certain questions in a row─ was crucial: How many souls you pick per year?

Answer was: five.

The next day’s headline told the confused world about the five mysterious deaths of passengers with no disease, infection or a scratch on the body.

I switched off the TV, my face white with a strange dread, and threw all the horror stories outside the apartment. The secret of my first interview remained buried in my head. But they *did* save me. My absurd fixation on the devil brought interest in Damon that compelled him to break his rules and left me out of his fifth victim.

Because he said it himself: “Involvement with our victims is a violation of code of our nature.”
