The Man in the Halloween Mask  
I was around ten when this happened. My younger brother was still pretty little, so my mom came trick-or-treating with us. I had a friend from school with me, and my mom mostly stayed on the sidewalk with my brother while we went up to the houses.  
Our neighborhood was always busy on Halloween. Almost everyone handed out candy, and people from other neighborhoods would drive over because some of the houses gave out full-size candy bars. There were always teenagers dressed up trying to scare people, too.  
I first noticed the man outside a house that had a bunch of decorations in the yard.  
He was wearing a rubber Halloween mask. It had a pale face, a big nose, and this weird smile with crooked teeth. It looked like something you’d find at a Halloween store, except it was older and kind of dirty. He had on a brown jacket, jeans, and work boots.  
He was standing beside a tree near the sidewalk.  
I thought he was going to jump out at us when we walked past, so I kept watching him. But he didn’t do anything.  
We went to a few more houses, and I forgot about him.  
Then, probably twenty minutes later, I saw him again.  
We were on another street, and he was standing near a stop sign. I recognized the mask right away.  
I pointed him out to my friend, and we joked that he must be following us. She thought the mask was funny and wanted to go ask him where he got it, but I told her not to.  
We kept going.  
The next time I saw him, we were waiting in line at a house giving out full-size candy bars. There were probably fifteen kids in the driveway, and I was looking around while we waited.  
He was across the street, standing between two parked cars.  
I watched him for a minute, trying to figure out if he was with anyone. There were plenty of people walking around, but nobody seemed to be paying attention to him.  
I told my mom about the guy I’d seen a few times, and she looked across the street.  
“Where?”  
I pointed, but he’d moved. I couldn’t find him anywhere.  
She told me it was Halloween and people were probably just trying to scare kids.  
That made sense, so I didn’t bring it up again for a while.  
But I kept seeing him.  
Once, he was standing in someone’s driveway. Another time, I saw him walking down the opposite sidewalk. I even saw him cutting between two houses, which I thought was weird because he didn’t seem to live at either one.  
The thing I started noticing was that he never seemed to be with anybody. He wasn’t carrying candy, and I never saw him go up to a house. He wasn’t even trying to scare anyone.  
At one point, we passed a group of older kids who were messing around with a guy dressed as Michael Myers. They were daring each other to get close to him, and he kept pretending to chase them.  
I remember thinking that was what the guy in the brown jacket was supposed to be doing.  
Instead, he just kept showing up.  
I started looking for him every time we turned onto another street.  
My friend noticed and told me to stop because I was making her nervous.  
A little later, we were walking past a house with a bunch of people sitting outside around a fire pit. I saw the brown jacket on the other side of the street and immediately grabbed my friend’s arm.  
He was walking in the same direction we were.  
I watched him pass under a streetlight, and for a second I got a better look at the mask. The mouth was open slightly, like it was made for somebody with a bigger head. I could see a dark space underneath the chin.  
I remember thinking I should be able to see some part of the person’s neck, but I couldn’t.  
Then a car drove between us, and I lost sight of him.  
I asked my mom if we could go home.  
She said we were almost done anyway, and that we could do one more street.  
My brother was getting tired, and I think my mom was ready to leave too.  
We were heading back toward our neighborhood when my brother dropped part of his costume. My mom stopped to help him look for it, and my friend and I walked a little farther ahead.  
There weren’t as many people on that street. A few porch lights were off, and most of the kids had already moved on to the busier blocks.  
We stopped near a driveway to wait for my mom.  
My friend was digging through her candy when I noticed someone standing farther down the street.  
It was the man in the brown jacket.  
He was about three houses away, standing near the end of a driveway.  
I didn’t say anything at first.  
I watched him for a little while, hoping he’d turn around or walk up to one of the houses.  
He didn’t.  
My friend asked what I was looking at.  
I pointed, and she looked up.  
“Oh, it’s that guy again.”  
We both watched him.  
Then he stepped off the driveway and started walking slowly along the sidewalk toward us.  
I remember my friend saying, “He’s probably just going somewhere.”  
I said, “Then why does he keep stopping?”  
Because he had stopped again.  
He was standing beside a mailbox now, facing us.  
My friend called out, “Nice mask!”  
He didn’t respond.  
She said it again, a little louder.  
Nothing.  
I wanted to go back to my mom, but I also didn’t want to look scared in front of my friend. So I stayed there, pretending to look through my candy.  
Every few seconds, I’d glance up.  
He hadn’t moved.  
Then my friend said, “I think he’s looking at us.”  
I told her he probably couldn’t even see very well through that mask.  
She said, “Then how does he know where we are?”  
I didn’t have an answer.  
I looked back toward my mom. She was still crouched beside my brother, trying to fix something on his costume. I could hear them talking, but they were farther away than I’d realized.  
When I turned around again, the man had moved closer.  
I hadn’t seen him walk.  
He was standing at the next driveway now.  
I remember being annoyed with myself because I kept looking away at exactly the wrong time.  
My friend stopped messing with her candy.  
“Let’s go back,” she said.  
I nodded, but neither of us moved right away.  
The man tilted his head a little.  
Not dramatically, like someone trying to act scary. More like he was trying to get a better look at something.  
Then he raised one hand.  
He waved.  
It was a small wave, just his fingers moving.  
My friend gave a nervous laugh and waved back.  
I didn’t.  
I was trying to look through the eyeholes of his mask. I wanted to see if it was somebody I knew, somebody who might be playing a joke on us.  
But I couldn’t see his eyes.  
I could see the edges of the holes, and the rubber around them, but nothing behind them.  
Then he started walking toward us again.  
My friend grabbed my arm, and we ran back to my mom.  
I was crying by the time we reached her. She kept asking what happened, and I told her the guy in the mask had been following us all night.  
She looked down the street, but we couldn’t see him anymore.  
My mom decided we’d had enough Halloween for one night and took us home.  
She called my friend’s mom later and told her about it. I remember hearing her say that it was probably some older kid messing around, but that she’d rather be safe.  
I didn’t really think about it much after that. Every once in a while, I’d remember the mask, but I figured he was probably just some teenager who thought he was funny.  
A few years later, my friend and I were talking about Halloween when she brought him up.  
She remembered the whole thing, including the brown jacket and the weird mask.  
I told her I’d always wondered why he kept showing up wherever we went.  
She said something about how creepy it was when he waved at us.  
I laughed and said, “I know. I thought he was going to chase us.”  
She looked at me and said, “Do you remember that lady standing behind us?”  
I had no idea what she was talking about.  
She said there had been a woman standing a few feet behind us when we saw him in the driveway. She remembered because the woman had been wearing regular clothes and didn’t have any kids with her.  
I told her I didn’t remember seeing anybody.  
She said, “Well, I thought he was waving at her. That’s why I waved back, because I thought he was just saying hi to somebody.”  
I asked her what the woman looked like.  
She couldn’t remember much, except that she’d been wearing a long coat and had her hair pulled back.  
Then she said, “Actually, I don’t remember seeing her after we ran to your mom.”  
I asked why she’d never mentioned any of this before.  
She shrugged and said she hadn’t thought it was important.  
I still don’t know who that man was, or why we kept running into him that night.  
And I have no idea who the woman was supposed to be.  
But for years, I’d been thinking that man was following us.  
Now I’m not even sure we were the ones he was following.
