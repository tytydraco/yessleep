“They're people,” Jacob said. “Those voices.”

It confused me initially, the way he said that. As if I hadn't known that already.

“Some of them know each other,” he continued. “I've heard the same names come up in different places. People thinking about the same things, mentioning each other.”

“How many have you listened to?” I asked.

He walked away and returned with a notebook. He leafed through the pages.

“32 of them.”

I was taken aback. When did he find the time for that?

He put the notebook in my hands. I felt nauseous as I flipped through the pages.

*The Blakes (presumed family)*

*Names repeated across three different Alethophone. Cathie thinks about Roland and Angel. Roland mentions Cathie and Angel. Cathie recalled a school notice addressed to Angel Blake. Assuming shared surname.*

*Cathie – Mother?*

*Thoughts: debt, spiders, a tree falling on the house, the crack that formed in Angel's bedroom wall (Something inside?), Roland needs to work more.*

*Roland – Father?*

*Thoughts: stuff about “the team,” clean that fucking window, Angel is getting older.*

*Angel – Daughter?*

*Thoughts: repetition of different children's shows, an image of something moving in the wall, her family standing on the porch but they're blurry.*

There were similar entries for each Alethophone.

How could he reduce these people to words on a page like this? These weren't just little scraps of information. There was so much more to them than their names and whatever happened to be troubling them. I thought about the woman I'd heard arguing with herself about a lake house, how her thoughts had wandered and doubled back, sometimes contradicting themselves. None of that could be captured in a list.

I flipped through the pages until I found 15A.

*15A – Unnamed. Lives alone?*

*Repeated thoughts about returning to an empty apartment. Anxious about meeting people at social events, worried about making friends. Seems preoccupied with herself and how others perceive her. Frequently replays conversations. Worried someone will “find her out.”*

I recognized the thoughts he was describing, but I didn't recognize the person. Reading it this way made her seem petty, self-absorbed. That wasn't how I'd heard her.

I did not read all the entries.

I grabbed the map, of which Jacob had already made copies, and left for the tunnels. I needed to hear her again. I didn't want to lose that sense of connection to categories and labels.

15A. What was her name?

Jacob didn't try to follow me. If he said anything as I walked to the garage, I don't remember what it was.

15A has thoughts like me, or I have thoughts like her. Once I made my way back into her chamber, I lay down on the bench. I could feel the impression of a metal tongue on my spine, small fangs against my arm. Familiar sensations, as if my skin had learned the touch of the bench.

Supine, I allowed the Alethophone to crawl into my brain. The darkness around me took on life, a kind of sentience, before settling into a comfortable fog. I breathed deep. Let my heart settle. I fully submerged myself in the thinking of 15A. Drifting into pools of black ink, into a worry that I’d left open my front door and anyone could wander in. They’d touch the coffee table, pick up an empty can of pop and trickle the last drop of liquid down their throat; they’d be so intoxicated by violating the sanctity of a stranger’s home.

What it’s like to be part of someone else without their knowing.

\[From Jacob’s Journal\]

Chambers correspond with houses?

The three voices I've identified as the Blakes all came from the same chamber. Cathie thinks about Roland and Angel. Roland thinks about Cathie and Angel. All three mention similar events. I've found other groups like this.

Could explain the different numbers of Alethophone in each chamber. One for each member of a household? What determines the number? Some benches have no Alethophone at all. What does that signify?

I've started comparing the tunnel map with a map of the neighborhood. Using the garage as a reference point, I made an overlay and tried to approximate the directions of the passages. There may be some correspondence. Several voices have mentioned familiar streets and landmarks, and some of the chambers appear to fall roughly where I'd expect them.

The distances don't make much sense. Passages that seem to run in the right direction don't always end up where they should. I can't tell if my measurements are wrong or if I'm trying to find a pattern that isn't there.

I need more reference points.

One of the Blakes, Angel, thinks about something moving inside her bedroom wall. Cathie has mentioned a crack in that same wall. Could be unrelated. Neither seems particularly concerned about it, though Angel's thoughts are difficult to follow.

The tunnels stretch further than I'd imagined. The other night, I woke up restless and felt compelled to continue mapping. I walked along a familiar route until I came upon new branches. They were much the same, with metal benches, Alethophone, and chambers, but there was an older quality to everything the further I went.

It was subtle at first. Some benches flaked with rust, their silver coating chipped away to reveal scar tissue and jagged lines like the teeth of a saw. The wooden supports were discolored. In places, the walls appeared soft, almost swollen.

I found a patch where the surface gave beneath my fingers. I pressed harder and felt my hand sink into the wall. There was little resistance. I pushed past my wrist, then my elbow, until my arm was buried nearly to the shoulder.

I don't know what I expected to find. I wanted to know how far it would let me go.

When I pulled my arm free, nothing clung to it. No dirt, no moisture. I shone my flashlight into the cavity and put my face close. There was a subtle pulse inside, the throb of veins. I moved the light away, then shone it back to see only dirt in the wall.

I guess I shouldn't look away.

Jacob is sleeping on the wrong side of the bed, so I try to push him over to his side. His skin is cold. It's just storage space, a root cellar. He whispers things in his sleep, but he's not asleep.

I couldn't say how much I'd slept that night. I remembered Jacob lying beside me, though I wasn't sure whether we'd spoken or if I'd only dreamed it. By morning, I felt foggy and unusually sensitive to touch. Birds pecked at the feeder outside the window as the sun came up. I pulled off the sheets and wandered into the kitchen.

“Where's…” Jacob trailed off.

“What? Where is what?” I tried not to snap at him. Maybe I snapped at him.

He gestured to the ground, moving his hand back and forth.

I grabbed my coffee creamer and slammed the fridge shut. “Ruffle?”

“Yeah. The cat.”

I gestured toward the living room, under the couch.

Jacob glanced in that direction, then looked back at me without saying anything.

“It's under there?”

Picking up my coffee, I walked away. Jacob stood there for a moment before making his way out to the garage.

I settled onto the couch. A moment later, Ruffle emerged from beneath it and scurried toward the bedroom.

Tried recording an Alethophone with my phone. Placed the microphone near the opening and left it running while I listened. I could hear a woman's voice clearly enough to make out words, but on playback there was nothing.

Tried again. This time I could hear my own breathing, the rustling of my clothes, and the scrape of the bench as I shifted. No voice, though.

In the tunnel, Jacob scrawled on his map, making ever-branching paths that fractaled into lungs, roots, and cracks of glass. His pages had become crowded with lines and annotations, some extending beyond the margins. I passed by him on my way to 15A without speaking.

There was little we shared besides this space and, even then, he tended to crawl the halls, tag chambers, and write down pathways. When I tried to bring up the thoughts I heard, he wrote them down but clearly felt nothing about their content.

He had begun exploring further into the tunnels, returning with pages of new passages and chambers. I couldn't understand what he hoped to find. There were more people to listen to, more voices to hear, but I had little interest in them anymore.

I made my way to Chamber 5A. I knew the bench well enough that I could lie down and fit the speculum into my ear without needing to adjust myself. The metal pressed against my back in familiar places. I closed my eyes and listened.

*“I can hear your breath.”*

It's the only thought I can recall.

I'd just come topside and still had a tickle in my ear. Before I'd closed the door, Jacob called out from the living room.

“I think I figured out where 15A lives.”

He was sitting on the couch with his notebook open. The room had a cardboard quality to it, as though I could walk behind the walls and knock the whole scene down like a stage set.

“Where is that?” I hadn't given much thought to where her body was on the surface, that her voice had a habitat.

“About four blocks away, I think.” He turned his notebook toward me. “She's mentioned a park a few times, and something about the apartments across from it. I compared what she said with the neighborhood map. There's a building that seems to fit.”

He handed me a piece of paper with an address. I recognized the street name but couldn't remember ever driving down it.

“How do you know that's where she lives?”

“I don't. Not for certain. But I think we can find out.”

He explained that he wanted one of us to watch the building while the other listened at 15A. If they were the same person, perhaps something happening outside would correspond with what he heard. He could write down her thoughts, and afterward we could compare them with what I'd seen.

“There’s a park across from the building,” he continued. “You could sit there and watch the entrance. If you see someone who might be her, do something conspicuous. Something she'd notice. I could listen for her reaction.”

“And ruin it all? Fuck you.”

Jacob looked at me. “Ruin what? I'm just asking you to get her attention.”

I started toward the bedroom.

“Fine. Forget that part. Just watch the building, then. We can compare what you see with what I hear. If she's leaving for work or coming home, there might be something we can match.”

I stopped. The thought of seeing her made me uncomfortable, though I couldn't say why. I considered the address in my hand.

“All right. But I'm not approaching anyone.”

He nodded and returned to his notebook.

I remembered what I'd heard at 15A.

*I can hear your breath.*

I considered telling Jacob, but I didn't. He'd want to know exactly what she'd said before and after it, how long I'd been listening, whether there had been any other indication that she knew I was there.

I couldn't remember any of that.

I wish someone would listen to me.
