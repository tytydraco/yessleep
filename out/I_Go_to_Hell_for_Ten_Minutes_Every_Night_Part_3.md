[**\[Part 2\]**](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1xskjvv/i_go_to_hell_for_ten_minutes_every_night_part_2/)

I’ve got to apologize to you all.  I meant to get back on here, but I kept waiting for the next thing to happen so I could post about it.  Really dumb.

I’m so tired right now, and I’m not thinking straight. It’s crazy. My friend is bleeding out in the bathtub, and I’m trying to remember how long it’s been since I took Tylenol. But I’m going out of order. Let’s try this again.

Where’d we leave off? Yeah, I was lying on my bedroom carpet with claw marks in my arm, and it was 1:30 am. Two things had changed, and I didn’t know how. I think I do now, and we’ll get to it. We have a lot to get to.

The wound won’t close. I cleaned it and bandaged it when it happened, and I woke up Tuesday morning with a very bloody bed. I’ve tried glue, which hurt like hell, and I even attempted to stitch it up, but I was doing something wrong there. My last effort was a staple gun. I didn’t even know I had one until I found it in a drawer, but I used it. It worked for a little while, but then they started falling out. I just keep it wrapped up now with a lot of padding. It’s all I can do for now; if I go to the ER they’ll have questions and Shugart has big ears.

The next worry added to the list in my last post was those two extra minutes. It’s always been ten,and now it’s twelve. Well, actually now it’s fifteen. It seems to go up by one every night. I try to find the bright side, though. It gives me more time to look for her, and now more than ever that’s the most important thing.

I haven’t given up, Maia. I won’t.

Tuesday was pretty chill, all things considered. I really miss that. After I posted that morning, I mostly spent the day dealing with my arm and finishing that book I was talking about. I had a meeting with the author for lunch the next day—I’ll call him DL—and I really wanted to be prepared for it. I also called and talked to my friend Sarah for a while. We’ve been tight since before this stuff started, and she’s the only person in the world who knows about my little condition.

Sarah is cool. She’s like a year younger than me, the same as my sister. I actually met her through June. They were doing that middle school thing where they thought they were girlfriends. It didn’t work out because June figured out she wasn’t a lesbian, but I got a best friend out of it. Maybe experimentation is healthy.

Anyway, we hadn’t talked in about a week so I had a lot to tell her. She was really worried when I told her about Maia and she wanted to know everything I could remember about the phantom that attacked me and the Marble Hell. I don’t think I had ever told her about that one. There’s so many of them that I forget. Before we hung up, she said she’d swing by my parents’ house and get my old PC. So that was one less thing to worry about.

It was the Sand Hell that night. Not to sound like old wooden Anakin, but I really hate the place. It’s all one big desert, and the damned souls that roam it are slowly eroded by it. But they don’t die. None of them ever do in any of the hells. Instead, they become part of it, just little grains in a sea of sand. You can hear their screams when the wind blows or as you walk across the dunes. I can’t express how disturbing it is. It’s why I used to wear earplugs.

When I popped back into my room, I was met with another new escalation. There were a few inches of that moaning sand layered around me. I swept that shit up. I don’t know what I’m going to do with it. It’s the first time something has come back with me, and considering how bad some of the hells are it had me worried.

Oh, and I was there for thirteen minutes.

I’m being kind of wordy. I need to watch that or this thing is going to be massive. A lot has happened and I don’t want to skip over too much of it. Let’s move on to Wednesday.

I didn’t have anything going on so I left to meet up with DL. It was a pub he had suggested; a place he knew of that was halfway between us. I was easily an hour early, and he was already there. I recognized him from the picture on the back of his book and I walked over to introduce myself.

The first thing I noticed about DL was how paranoid he was. He was constantly checking the entrance, scanning the room, and he made me answer a series of questions to prove that I was the person who emailed him. I endured it all because I hoped that he had the answers I needed. By the end of the meeting, I wasn’t so sure he even knew what he was talking about.

DL didn’t tell me much that I hadn’t already read in his book. He talked a lot about his precious pomegranate and the different hells he wrote about. When I asked him about the man he mentioned in the final chapter, he just clammed up and didn’t say anything. He just told me that he wouldn’t betray his trust. I mean, okay, I can respect that, I might could even admire it if Maia’s life wasn’t on the line.

Even though most of it was useless. I think he did tell me two useful things. He said there is a group called the Knotted Ring. They’re basically a cult, but he didn’t use that word. He talked about their obsession with the Perditious Cluster and told me to be wary of their symbol. It honestly explains a lot about how he acts, but I can’t really do that. If I start flinching every time I see a circle, I won’t be much use to anyone.

Then he taught me a trick that might help me find the Marble Hell. DL gave me a small piece of marble and told me to keep it with me. He said that the feel of it in my hand, combined with focusing on my memories of the void sea and Maia’s voice, might allow me to do it. I took it and put it in my pocket. It was worth a shot.

After I left him, I got a text from Sarah telling me she had the computer and was going to come by Thursday to help me. She also said she wanted to talk about some things. I said okay and headed home. That night’s flip took me to the Bottomless Hell.

The pit has always scared the fuck out of me, and I don’t know why. There are much crazier ones out there. It’s just an endless fall through a stony hole into darkness. The entire time you are hurtling downward, you see thousands of the damned clinging to those walls, trying to hold on for as long as they can. They are naked, of course—it’s a common theme—and hundreds of bats and other creepy fliers constantly swarm around them, feeding on their defenseless backs. I have nightmares about it, and I guess that’s why it never occurred to me to try DL’s theory while I was there.

I’ve taken to wearing a stopwatch every night, and I was at the twelfth minute when the shadow rose up from below me. Those purple eyes lit up the tunnel, and I knew that it was the same phantom as before. I barely turned quick enough to get out of its path and I watched it collide with a few sad souls as it struck the wall. They went falling past me and the thing came up right behind them. It grabbed me and slammed me into the stone. I couldn’t break its grip on me and I worried that it was going to be pulled along with me into the real world.

In the end I got lucky. It turned out that three of the damned were hanging onto the phantom for dear life and I guess they started annoying him. When he let go with one hand to deal with them, I was able to kick off the wall and out of his grasp. A moment later I fell face first onto my bedroom floor.

I slept poorly; my arm was killing me.  It was red and pissed off but at least it wasn’t bleeding.  I had a little fever from it.  When I woke up the next morning every joint on my body ached.  I saw that there were purplish lines coming out of the wound, tracing the veins in my arm all the way to my elbow and the back of my hand.  I was considering what I should do about it when my phone dinged with a text message.  It said, “Police are coming, there's a camera in your hallway.”

It was from a blocked number, and it included an image of me and Maia standing outside my apartment door. The alert went off again, “In your building now.”

I knew I had only moments, so I just grabbed my workbag, wallet and my laptop and ran out into the hall. I could hear them coming up the stairs—the elevator sucks—so I went up too. Back when I smoked I used to practically live up on the roof, so I knew a few good hiding places. In the end I spent most of the day squeezed inside an old maintenance hatch. It was a little funny, because none of them ever even came up there. Double funny really, because if they had checked the camera again, they would have seen me.

I almost felt clever until I realized I had left all of Maia’s things behind for them to find.

When it started getting dark, I sneaked out of the building. I stopped a few blocks away and bought some food and a cheap phone. I called Sarah and she agreed to come get me. An hour later we left the city.

I was in rough shape when she picked me up. I didn’t have any meds, and I was in and out of consciousness for most of the drive. I crashed hard when we finally arrived at the cheap motel. When my alarm went off at 1:00 am, Sarah was sitting across the room from me. She had my old desktop hooked up and was deeply engrossed in whatever she was doing.

“It took a bit of doing, but I may have a lead,” she said. “Eat first, though, and take some of those pills. That arm looks terrible.”

“I can eat on the way,” I said. “I need to borrow your car.”

“You mean you need to wreck it? Dude, have you looked at yourself? You were mumbling in your sleep. I was worried that you’d fried your brain.”

“I gotta. I can’t risk anyone else.”

“Should I drive you?”

“Nah,” I said. “I won’t go too far. Just need a little distance to keep you safe.”

“Well, the room will smell better,” she said, and tossed me the keys.

I didn’t have to drive very far to be safely away from her. There was a wooded area about a mile from the motel. When I got there, I pulled off on a gravel service road and drove until I found a good place to stop. I got out of the car at 1:15 am.

I walked around for those last few minutes, rubbing the piece of marble in my pocket like it was a shooting star begging for my wish. I blinked and the next thing my eyes beheld was the agony of the Eaters’ Hell.

It is an awful place that I have passed through too many times to count. Blood-soaked soil and grass surrounded me as I watched the hopeless gluttons consume one another. To be eaten there is to merge and become part of an ever-evolving host. Those immense and grotesque abominations roam that realm: bloated, conjoined things that live only to hunger.

Once I would have believed myself safe from those multi-limbed ravagers, but the past few days had robbed me of that surety. To protect myself from them, I ducked down into the cover of the tall grass and gripped the stone. I began to imagine that black sea and the rippling of its waves. I remembered her laugh, rather than her voice, a thing of melody that had shamed all other songs for me.

Slowly the smell of copper and hay grass was replaced by the scent of salt and clay. I opened my eyes and there she stood, my Maia. She had draped herself in scavenged, threadbare cloth, and she looked just as lovely as she ever had. We walked towards each other, and the surprised smile on her face was like a mirror of my joy. It had worked, and I had been brought to her. Finally I was going to take her home.

But of course it didn’t go that way, and that tiny, glowing moment fell under the dusk of that plaguing shadow. Our fingertips were allowed to touch for a brief second and then I was yanked back and slammed against a marble wall. The dark shape of a man stood over me. Its violet eyes shone violent burgundy as the fingers of its left hand elongated, becoming long, tapered claws.

“Let him go!” she screamed as she threw a chunk of stone at the phantom. It swung its right arm dismissively back and it snapped at her like a finger flicking a fly. It sent her tumbling.

It leaned in close to me and spoke in a wispy tone that was more echo than voice.

“Would you give yourself for her?”

“Yes, just let her—” I was interrupted when the column next to us suddenly bent down and twisted itself around my enemy. The phantom shrieked and raked its claws against the unyielding stone that held it.

“You’ve got to learn how to negotiate a little better,” Sarah said. “You hurt?”

“How?” I asked as I took her hand and let her help me up. “How are you here, did you follow me when I left?”

“No.” She frowned. “I wanted to tell you about it but you fell asleep. You need to know because this has all gotten out of hand. How many minutes do you have left?”

“Maybe two.”

“Get her and let’s go. I’ll explain back at the motel.”

I nodded and ran past her to do just that. Maia was slowly getting up; she was a little dazed from her fall.

“Come on!” I called to her. “Hurry. We’ve got to go.”

She had just started towards me when I heard Sarah scream. I turned around at once and saw her lying there against the stone. One hand lay across her stomach where the phantom had slashed her. It looked deep. Her other hand was upraised, and as she clenched her fist, I heard the creaking of the stone. Blood ran freely from her mouth, and I saw that the creature’s claw had also struck her in the throat. When she tried to speak, only a breathy wheeze came out.

I turned back to Maia, hoping she was close, but her steps were slow and unsure. Looking down at her legs, I saw the reason: the left one was solid stone from the knee down. She stopped, out of breath, and waved me away.

“Save Sarah, Will,” she said. “She’s hurt bad.”

I started to protest, to tell her we’d find a way. My alarm began to buzz, though, and I knew I had maybe thirty seconds left.

“I love you, Maia. I’m not giving up.”

I saw her answering smile as I spun on my heel and turned back for Sarah. My hand closed around her ankle without a second to spare. As we flipped back, I heard Maia’s reply.

“I love you, Will.”

We both appeared right in front of her car on the gravel road. I got her inside and I couldn’t tell you how I managed to drive us back while putting pressure on her wounds. She was fully unconscious by then and for a moment there I thought we was gone.

I brought her in and put her in the tub so I could tend to her. The damage was beyond my meager first aid skills, but I did manage to slow the bleeding. I don’t believe it hit an artery, but there’s no telling how bad it is on the inside.

She woke up for a few minutes and helped me improve the work I had done. I told her I should call an ambulance, but she shook her head and used her blood to finger paint “will be fine” on the bathroom wall. I don’t believe her. I think she’s just trying to protect me and that’s not worth her life.

If she blacks out again I’m biting the bullet and driving her myself. Fuck the consequences.

So that catches you all up. I’m sitting here in the motel typing this while the sun’s still asleep. I have no home and I’m hiding from the law. Worse than that, all of my efforts have failed, Maia is still over there, and now my friend is hurt really bad.

I don’t have a clue what to do next, but there’ll be another 1:18 tomorrow night and I’ve got to figure out how to be ready for it.
