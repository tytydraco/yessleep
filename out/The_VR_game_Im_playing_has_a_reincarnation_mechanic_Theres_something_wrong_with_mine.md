I bought *Re:Awakening* on sale because I wanted an RPG I could sink a weekend into. The days were getting shorter and I didn't have much of a reason to leave my apartment after work. The reviews were decent enough and the screenshots looked good.

The character creation was basic. Humans were the only available race, so I moved on to the fighter class. I gave him messy chestnut hair and light eyes, and tried to get his face vaguely right. When I loaded into the game, I found myself standing in a stone chamber with a wooden door, a narrow window, and a mirror mounted on the opposite wall.

I walked over to the mirror and laughed. The reflection looked surprisingly close to me. It even had the faint scar beside my left eye, an old injury I received after falling off the monkey bars in elementary school. I didn’t remember adding it. I wasn’t sure the character creator even had an option for it.

I leaned closer. My character leaned closer. I touched the corner of my eye, and he did the same.

Then I smiled.

My reflection took a moment longer to smile back. It was probably a rendering delay. I’d seen stranger glitches in VR. I shook it off and tried the door.

It wouldn’t open.

I checked the controls and searched the room for a switch, trying every interaction I could think of. Eventually, I noticed a line of text floating above the mirror.

**TUTORIAL INCOMPLETE**

I figured I’d missed something. I quit and relaunched the game twice. Both times, I could leave normally. On the third attempt, the option to exit was grayed out in the system menu.

I held down the headset’s physical button. Nothing happened.

I wasn’t worried yet. Games crashed. Headsets froze. I assumed I’d have to let the battery die or force a restart somehow. I could still access the inventory and pause menu, so I returned to the chamber and looked at the mirror.

The text had changed.

**YOUR FIRST DEATH IS REQUIRED**

The wooden door opened.

Beyond it stood a skeleton holding a rusty sword. A combat interface appeared, explaining that fights were turn-based. I selected **ATTACK**. My character swung, the skeleton lost some health, and then it raised its sword.

I tried to dodge.

The blade struck my chest.

At first, I thought the headset had malfunctioned. Then the pain hit, white-hot and sharp, spreading through my ribs. My tunic darkened around the wound. Blood ran down my stomach, dripping onto the stone floor and pooling beneath my boots.

I tried to pause the game. I tried to shout. The skeleton struck again, and I fell forward, my hands pressed against a wound that felt wet and impossibly deep. My character’s health reached zero.

The screen went black.

**DEATH ACCEPTED: REINCARNATION IN PROGRESS**

I woke in the original chamber with full health and no wounds.

For a few seconds, I couldn’t move. I kept my hands against my chest, half expecting to find blood on them. Then I checked my inventory. My sword and armor were still there.

I told myself it was a scripted sequence, some elaborate tutorial meant to demonstrate the game’s reincarnation mechanic. The pain was harder to explain, but I was panicking, and panic could do strange things to your perception.

I went back through the door.

The skeleton was waiting.

This time, I knew what was coming. That somehow made it worse. I tried to fight, but my hands shook as I selected each action. When the sword struck me again, I screamed. I watched the blood spread across my tunic and begged the game to stop.

I woke in the chamber.

I remembered everything.

The first death, the second, the pain and the smell and the awful sensation of trying to breathe with a crushed chest.

I reached up to remove the headset. I could feel the pressure of the device around my temples, and my fingers found something solid where the edge should have been. But when I tried to lift it, my hands seemed to slide away from it. In the mirror, my character’s hands remained at his sides.

I tried again with no luck.

I looked at the mirror and raised my hands. My character copied me perfectly. I lowered them, then reached toward my face again. The reflection followed, but I couldn’t make contact with anything outside the game. I started to hit the mirror. My fists passed through it without leaving a mark.

Above my reflection, the message returned:

**TUTORIAL INCOMPLETE**

I died six more times. I won’t describe them all. I remember crawling away after the skeleton cut my leg. I remember watching it approach while I lay on the floor, unable to do anything but wait for the next turn. By the eighth death, I had learned its attack pattern. I thought I could beat it.

Then it changed its pattern halfway through the fight.

Its sword caught me beneath the ribs. I folded over, trying to hold the wound closed. Blood slipped between my fingers. I started to choke out a desperate laugh. The laughter turned into sobbing before the skeleton finished me off.

When I returned to the chamber, I stood before the mirror and stared at my character’s clean face. The game wasn’t resetting me. It was resetting my body, and the mechanic ensured I was the part that carried over.

Eventually, I defeated the skeleton. Its bones scattered across the floor, and a door opened at the far end of the room. I didn’t feel triumphant. I just wanted to know whether the next room would let me leave.

It led to a ruined chapel. Stone benches sat in crooked rows beneath a ceiling painted with faded stars. At the altar knelt a man in a gray robe.

He rose as I entered the chapel. His outline kept shifting. Thin black lines crawled across his shoulders and face, vanishing and reappearing a few inches away. Parts of him looked perfectly normal, others were covered in tiny numbers.

His face was the worst. One eye sat too high. The other flickered between a brown eye and a socket full of darkness. His mouth appeared, disappeared, then reappeared slightly to the left of where it belonged. When he turned his head, the rest of his body took a moment to catch up.

For a fraction of a second, his face became a grid of binary code:

01001000 01000101 01001100 01010000

Then it snapped back into place. I took a step backward as the man tilted his head. His neck stretched, broke into horizontal bands, and reassembled. His robe remained perfectly still while the body inside it shifted through several positions.

A combat interface appeared:

**ENEMY ENCOUNTER: THE PENITENT**

The name flickered.

**NULL**

The Penitent raised his head. His mouth moved, but the sound came a moment later.

“You have already –”

His voice skipped.

“You have already –”

A burst of static swallowed the rest. His face stretched into a wide black smear, and for a moment I could see the stone wall through it.

Then he was standing normally again.

“I know you,” he said.

I selected **ATTACK**. The Penitent didn't react. The sword passed through him. The health bar remained full. Then he reached into his chest and pulled out something that looked like a handful of tangled roots. They writhed between his fingers, twisting around one another.

A horrible pressure built beneath my ribs. I dropped my sword. My character’s mouth opened, but no sound came out. The Penitent pulled the roots away from his body, and I felt something inside me stretching with them.

My chest flickered. For an instant, the wound was a hole where my torso should have been, filled with broken white characters that crawled over one another like ants.

Then the flesh returned, and the pain hit. I died before the turn ended. When I woke in the chamber, I fell to my knees. I could still see those characters moving beneath my skin. I looked down at my hands, expecting to find them there too. There was, however, nothing. I went back to the chapel. The Penitent stood at the altar, his face flickering between several different expressions. I tried defending this time. He pulled the roots out of his chest again, and I fell clutching my chest. 

On my third attempt, I noticed the roots trailing into his shadow, not his robes. The shadow moved a second before he did, stretching toward me when he attacked.

I waited until his next turn and struck the shadow. The Penitent screamed and his body folded inward, collapsing into a heap of gray cloth. The roots shriveled into black threads and the combat interface disappeared.

For a moment, the chapel was silent.

Then a message appeared above the altar:

**REINCARNATION IS NOT MERCY**

I stared at the words until the door at the back of the chapel opened. I knew I didn't want to go through. I wanted to stay in the chapel, where I knew what was waiting for me. But the room offered no other refuge, so eventually I moved forward.

The rooms grew stranger after that. Enemies had human faces but walked as if they had too many joints. Other enemies spoke in long hisses that echoed through the room, occasionally resolving into words. The combat remained turn-based, and the rules remained consistent. I could learn their patterns. I could win.

During the fights, sometimes I would stop thinking about escape. I focused on the next move because it was the only thing I could control. Between fights, though, I kept looking for a way out. My character always returned clean and healthy. I didn’t. I had to escape and the only way forward was through.

I began flinching before attacks landed. Sometimes I heard a sword scrape against stone and ducked before an enemy appeared. I could still feel wounds long after they had vanished from my character’s body. Once, I spent several minutes staring at the floor because I was certain there was blood on it.

I found the game’s Steam page through the virtual browser in the pause menu. The screenshots showed fantasy landscapes and ordinary enemies. Nothing from the stone chamber appeared in them. One review complained that the tutorial was impossible to complete.

I tried to contact the developer, but the support page wouldn’t load. I attempted to write a review explaining what was happening, but the page froze before I could submit it. I stared at the store page for a while. People were still leaving reviews. Someone had posted a screenshot of their character standing beside a river. Someone else was complaining about an inventory bug.

Their problems seemed so ordinary that I nearly cried.

When I returned to the game, I went straight to the mirror. The scar beside my character’s left eye looked deeper. I leaned closer, turning my head to catch the light. The mark was there, just as it had been when I started.

Then my reflection smiled at me. I jerked back so quickly that I stumbled. My character stumbled too, but the smile remained for another second before fading into the expression I was actually making.

I tried to remember what my living room looked like. I could picture the couch and television. I remembered sitting down to put the headset on, the light coming through the window. But small details kept slipping away: the design on my coffee mug or what color the curtains were. The stone chamber was easier to imagine. I could recall the skeleton’s sword in perfect detail, down to the dull glint along its edge.

I began repeating my name, my age, and the date aloud. I said them over and over, afraid that if I stopped, I would lose them. Sometimes I reached the end of the list and realized I’d started again without noticing.

The next time I stood before the mirror, I saw my living room behind me. Just for a second: my couch, the coffee table, the cracked blinds, a picture of my dad and I on my graduation day. It looked so ordinary that I almost turned around expecting to find myself back there. Then the reflection shifted and the room was gone. The wooden door opened behind me. I heard something dragging its feet across the stone.

I didn’t turn around. I knew what happened when I went through that door. I would fight, or I would refuse to fight, and eventually I would die. Then I would wake up here again with every second of it still in my head.

I reached for the headset one more time. My character’s hands rose to his temples in the mirror. I couldn’t find the straps. I couldn’t find an edge to pull. I couldn’t even tell whether I was touching the device or only imagining that I was.

Above the mirror, the words appeared:

**TUTORIAL INCOMPLETE**

I opened the browser. The page was blank. I tried the address bar, but the keyboard wouldn’t appear. I closed the window and opened it again. Nothing changed. I pressed the virtual keyboard button once more. It appeared, flickering at the edges. I typed as quickly as I could.

I don’t know if this message will reach anyone. I don’t know if the browser is connected to the internet or if it’s just another part of the game. I can’t take the headset off. I can’t shut it down. Every time I die, I come back. And every time I come back, I remember.

I can still feel the wounds, smell the blood, hear the things in other rooms speaking to me. I'm not sure how much time has passed. I don’t know if I’m still sitting at my desk or if my body is slowly deteriorating in my apartment. I don’t even know if anyone can see this.

The thing in the next room has stopped moving. I can’t hear its feet anymore. When I look at my reflection, it is standing perfectly still. Its face is covered in tiny white characters. They crawl across its skin, gathering around the scar beside its left eye.

The reflection opens its mouth.

I hear my own voice come out of it.

“You have already –”

The sound skips.

“You have already –”

The door behind me begins to open wider. Please...if anyone can read this, help me. I can’t take the headset off.

I don’t want to die again.
