A delivery request popped up on my app from a nearby restaurant. I glanced at my watch. It was eleven at night. I wanted to decline the order and finally call it a day, but instead of displaying “Order Declined,” the screen said, “Order Accepted.”

I stared at it for a moment. I was certain I’d tapped the decline button, but it didn’t matter now. Apparently, my workday wasn’t over yet.

I checked the address. Fortunately, the restaurant was only about a ten-minute drive away. I wasn’t very familiar with that area, so I had to park my car and walk a short distance to reach it.

The restaurant was tucked away in a corner behind a row of apartment buildings. I couldn’t imagine anyone finding their way here, let alone voluntarily coming here for a meal. The door was peeling, and the only letter still clearly visible on the sign above it was an R.

I stepped inside, and a disgusting smell immediately hit me. I looked around. There were dirty, empty tables everywhere. On one of them sat a plate of something that could barely be called food, even with a vivid imagination. Flies swarmed around it.

I’d seen plenty of things during my time as a delivery driver, but even I was taken aback by this place.

After a while, a man emerged from the kitchen. His greasy hair hung in clumps, his face was covered in scars, and a strand of drool ran from the corner of his mouth, landing directly on the food container he was holding.

“Hello. I’m here to pick up an order.”

The guy let out a hoarse, muffled chuckle and handed me the container.

“Thank you. Have a nice evening.”

He didn’t respond.

I took the container and headed for my car. I had no intention of staying there a second longer than necessary. I kept glancing around as I walked. Just before the restaurant disappeared from view, I noticed the man had stepped outside and was watching me.

I quickened my pace, got into my car, started the engine, and drove to the address displayed in the app.

I parked in the lot and called the customer. An old woman with a tired-sounding voice answered.

“Hello, I have your order. Could you come downstairs to pick it up?”

“Son, could you bring it up to me? I’m old, and I can’t manage the stairs. I live on the second floor. It’s not very far.”

“Of course. No problem,” I replied with a smile before heading inside.

A few minutes later, I was standing in front of a partially open door. A woman’s voice came from inside.

“Son, could you please bring the food in here?”

I couldn’t understand why she couldn’t come and get it herself, but I stepped inside anyway.

“Please put it right here on the table.”

I followed the voice for a few steps and entered the living room. It looked exactly how you’d imagine an elderly person’s living room would look. It was cozy and inviting.

A very old woman sat in an armchair. She looked utterly exhausted, too. Her white hair fell across her face.

“Good evening,” I said with a smile, handing her the container.

“Good evening. Thank you so much. I’m glad you brought our meals.”

I froze.

“What meals?”

I explained that the order had only included one meal.

The woman looked at the container and opened it.

In that instant, every question in my mind disappeared.

“Jesus Christ, what is that?”

She looked at me.

“Pork liver.”

“I can see that, but it’s raw!”

The woman didn’t answer. She simply reached into the container and began devouring the liver.

That was too much for me. I turned around and headed for the door.

“Wait!” she called after me.

I turned back.

“More food for him.”

“Ma’am, I told you the order only contained one meal. You’ll have to take that up with the restaurant. It’s not my responsibility to deal with missing items.”

The woman laughed.

“But he doesn’t eat food out of containers.”

I wanted to ask her who she meant, but just then, I heard a noise coming from the next room.

I turned toward the sound. At first, all I could hear was shuffling, followed by something that sounded like fingernails scraping against wood.

The door creaked open, and pale hands began reaching through the gap. Their fingers were longer than the palms of their own hands. They gripped the sides of the doorframe, and then the whole figure emerged from the room.

Its body was twisted, its limbs bent at angles that made them look as though they had been broken. Looking up, I saw a long neck bent at an unnatural angle.

Then I saw its face.

Where its eyes should have been were empty black sockets. It had nothing but two holes where its nose should have been, and its mouth was split almost all the way to its ears.

I screamed and began backing away.

“W-what is that?” I stammered.

The woman looked at me calmly.

“My grandson. And he’s hungry.”

I didn’t wait for anything else. I bolted for the front door, but the figure lunged at me with incredible speed and grabbed me. I could barely breathe. All I could hear was my heart pounding in my ears.

It grabbed my hand and sank its teeth into my fingers.

A wave of pain shot through me, jolting me into action. I kicked the creature as hard as I could. It let out a startled shriek, and I didn’t waste a second. I took advantage of the opportunity and ran.

I took the stairs three at a time. I threw myself into my car and tried to start it with trembling hands. Before I drove away, I noticed a pile of empty food containers lying off to the side of the apartment building.

Exactly like the one I’d brought with me.

I finally managed to start the car and drove as far away as I could.

After driving for about an hour, I pulled over and called the police. I looked down at my hand. Several chunks of my fingers were missing.

When the police arrived, they discovered that according to the app, I had never accepted an order that night. There was no record of the address, the restaurant, or the apartment building where I’d delivered the food.

They tested me for alcohol and drugs. To their surprise, both tests came back negative.

Eventually, they took me to the hospital, where my injuries were treated. The case was eventually closed, and the attacker was never identified.

I tried to keep working, but every time a new order notification came through, I felt something tighten inside me. Eventually, I had to quit and find another job.

But even that didn’t help. Every time my phone rings, I feel sick.

I want to forget everything that happened, but there isn’t a single day when I don’t find myself thinking back to that night, wondering what actually happened to me.
