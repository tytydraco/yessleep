It sounds crazy, I know, but I swear that it was his idea. Here’s what happened: I left work two days ago and spent my evening working on transcribing posts. Then, after I had finished writing, I looked back over everything and realized how crazy I sounded. Seeing it all on the page, I wondered if any of you would believe me. I decided to call it a night, and post the story tomorrow once I looked it over with a fresh set of eyes. I went outside, lit a cigarette and looked up at the great fowl outside my apartment, still staring through my window.

“506,197”

“What would you do if you were in this scenario?” I asked, exhaled smoke making swirling shapes in the beams of the parking lot’s street lights.

He flicked his head, almost pondering my question as I took another long drag from my cigarette. 

“506,186”

I shrugged, “I don’t expect you to respond with anything helpful anymore. But there are a lot of times I wish you would be a little more specific with me. When was the last time you gave me a cryptic feather?”

Now, I had no expectation of him actually doing anything in response. I just needed the smoke break, and talking to a giant duck that only I can see while doing so seemed practical enough in my head.

That’s why, when the massive mallard craned his shiny green head down and began preening for a feather to give me. I was shocked to find it had an address on it. I read it over and over, wondering if maybe there was something hidden in the way the address was written, but it was just an ordinary set of numbers, on an ordinary street. I put out my finished cigarette, and looked back up at the fowl. 

“I guess I’ll go look this up?”

“506,097”

When I got back inside, I found that the address was for the Church of Cogitology. Not having been to church besides on holidays with my parents when I was too young to say I didn’t want to go. I felt like it wouldn’t hurt to at least see what the place looked like. So I sent a message to Linda telling her I’d be at work at noon tomorrow, posted the story,  and went to sleep.

“485,455”

I slept in a little, lavishing in my slow morning, then eventually got ready for church. I wasn’t sure what to wear, since most of the things I had in my closet were ratty shirts and pajama pants, or worn out jeans and shirts with “Large Lou’s” printed on them in big letters. I grabbed my least worn out pair of pants, and AN AC/DC shirt that didn’t have any holes in it. A quick sniff check said I’d probably be fine, but I wished I had taken a shower the night before. I grabbed my clothes for work, and then got in the car and drove to the church.

It was only a mile from Large Lou’s. The only reason I hadn’t seen the place before was because it was a mile the opposite way from the direction I take to work every day. It was mostly an unassuming building. Actually, it was in a strip mall. The church was nestled between two buildings, a butcher labeled with a sign saying, “Meat ‘n’ Stuff” on the right, and a soda shop with the same logo as the one on the soda fountain at my job. The church’s name was in white, Ariel font, letters.

“**The Church Of Cogitology**”

I had tried to do some research into what their religion was, but the name happened to also be the name for the research of the human mind and thinking, so I didn’t have much luck. Once inside, I realized the whole place was like any other “modern” church. The lobby had exposed ceilings with the plumbing and air conditioning pipes painted in a dull blue-grey accented by the white walls which were balanced out by being decorated with square boards of reclaimed pine and glass vases filled with pebbles that held up stalks of wheat.

Service had already started, and the worship band was an odd mix of lutes, cellos, electric guitars, and one grand piano. Three women, who were dressed only a bit more formal than I was, were at the front of the stage singing songs about freedom, being happy, and loving others.

The stage itself was a basic wooden platform, with a projector screen the size of the stage at the back, with two smaller ones of the walls on either side. The same color scheme of grey, beige, and white from the lobby, carried through into the service room. Save for the maroon red pews. I found a mostly empty seat in the back and watched the service.

In spite of the gaudy interior design. I felt very comforted. Nothing in the building was too “in your face” or obnoxious. While it all boasted a certain level of sterility, it also felt stable, and calm.

When the music stopped, The pastor stepped up. He was quiet for a moment, simply gazing out upon the congregation. With a move of his hands that were merely a suggestion, the audience sat. 

Finally, the pastor spoke.

“Men and women of the congregation. I have come here today to speak on such matters which you all know of, but find little time to properly utilize in your own personal lives. The world we live in is a messy and complicated place.”

Murmurs of agreement rippled through the space.

“Just a few days ago, I found myself looking at the people on the streets, those asking for something they did not have, because they were down on their luck. It grieved me to see all of those people struggling. But it reminded me of what we search for in this church. The energy that flows between all of us, but goes to no one. I’m speaking, of course, of heaven and hell.”

I let out a heavy breath. Preparing for the usual words that followed after saying such a statement, and the condemnation of people who weren’t here. The stuff that made people feel a moral superiority over nonbelievers.

The pastor continued, “How we, ourselves feed both those places every day. How we, everyone in this room, equally contributes to those planes. We are nothing to those realms, and conversely, those are nothing to us. When I was younger, I cursed those places. It felt like a cruel joke that we would only be able to engage in those realms after our death. Yet, our choices here somehow decided where we would spend our time there as if we were engaging with those realities the whole time.” The pastor took a moment for what he said to settle.

This was new. I thought back on my life before working at Large Lou’s. How I never found any interest in doing anything because what would it bring me in return. The only reason I did finally get a job was because I was living out of my car.

He went on, “You, me, we shouldn’t let these things exist only outside of our current mortality. Now, this doesn’t mean I want to send you out into the world and ‘be good people’. That mindset is lackluster, and narrow minded. I’m calling upon you people to take the energy that was meant for the heavens, and redistribute it upon those around you. 

“One of the great prophets, Nikola Tesla, once wrote, ‘Pray not to honor those above, but to be your own engine of ethereal energy. You are not a lost soul searching for repentance, but a driving body that enacts perfection via holy engagement.’ We are not meant to let this escape us. We are meant to be good people for our own sake. 

“Freedom. Love. Happiness. You do these things out of the kindness of your soul. But then what does your soul gain for those actions in return?”

Again, the pastor was silent. I was taken aback by his eloquence. He was dressed so casually that I almost didn’t expect it.

The pastor ended his short sermon with a conclusion to his question, “You take that energy back yourself. These realms exist to parse our energy. When you pray, you gain only what is good. When I looked at those hurt people, I wanted to grab each one by the shoulders and say, ‘YOU’RE NOT ALONE, leave your hurt behind. Take hold of the overflowing energy we all share, and do something with it!’... Now, I want each of you to take a moment, and call upon that heavenly battery, and bring it back into your soul.”

The pastor bowed his head, and many in the crowd joined him. After a few moments, the pastor let a short prayer come from his lips.

“Oh power, how great is your current. In the midst of this sanctity, I demand your force flow back from your banks, and into our hearts. Be not a latent field beyond our reach, but a wonder in which our souls can delight. Let luck, good fortune, and joy be not something that comes for just those who have the will to do so, but those who have lost hope in it being given to them at all. Let our freedom be one of constant engineering. To the heavens and to the hells, may our peace be something of our own manufacturing.”

The pastor stepped away, and then the band began to play again. I could hear mutterings from those around me as they each spoke their own prayers. Some demanded the healing of their family. Others spoke of getting a better job. Most simply requested happiness. Then, as each finished their prayer, they stood up and sang with the band. The weirdest thing was the feeling in the room. It felt palpable. Like the air was thicker. Like the energy the pastor spoke of was really coming down from above, and lingering in the space around us.

As I absorbed this all, I realized I had wanted these things too. When most of the people had finished praying, I too stood up, and joined in the singing.

Eventually, people started to file out and back into the lobby. I stayed for a moment, reminiscing on how it was some stupid duck who got me to do this in the first place when the pastor suddenly walked up to me and broke me from my deep thoughts.

“Charlie, right?” he asked, sticking his hand out to me.

“Yeah…?” shaking it.

The pastor smiled warmly and bowed a little, “We are very happy to have you here.”

I started looking around the room for “we” and if “we” had given the pastor my name, but there were only a few stragglers left, and they certainly weren’t part of the pastor’s “we”.

“How do you know my name?” I finally asked.

The pastor, still smiling, answered, “I have my ways.”

The white walls suddenly felt much less spacious.

The pastor, seeing the obvious expression of discomfort on my face, laughed heartily, “I’m kidding. We have several members of the church who frequent where you work. Stan, who… I don’t think is here today - stout fella with no hair - is even friends with Large Lou.”

“Wow. I work in the kitchen, that’s crazy you’ve heard of me.” I said, trying (and failing) at keeping the sarcasm from dripping off the sentence.

The pastor laughed again, then added, “I *am* the pastor, it’s kind of my job to know who people are.” Then laughed some more.

I could feel the corners of my face start to twitch upwards. I also didn’t know *why* my face was twitching. The pastor hadn’t said anything funny. Yet, I was enjoying my conversation with him. Not to mention his laughter was a bit infectious.

The pastor finally introduced himself, “Bartholomew. I came to talk to you because I was wondering if you wanted to join our meeting tomorrow for new members?”

“What time?” As the words left my mouth I realized I genuinely wanted to know if work would get in the way of this event.

The pastor handed me a pamphlet, “9:00. It’s late, but I really hope you can make it. We plan on having the event catered, and I’m worried we’ll have way more food than people to feed it to.”

I looked at the pamphlet, and read the same thing as what the pastor was telling me. The pamphlet itself was in a minimalist modern design. With the date, location, and white text on a grey background saying “New Members Meeting”.

I said goodbye to the pastor, and left for work. The HomeLess Man was doing his usual mutterings behind the dumpster, but I paid him no mind. Brent was outside smoking a cigarette. He offered the pack to me, but I declined, deciding to just take in the atmosphere with fresh air in my lungs.

“Must be nice to not work until noon.” Brent grumbled.

I smiled, “Yeah…” I said, with an air of wistfulness, “that was pretty good.”

Brent looked confused, but didn’t ask any further. Then, “Could You Be Loved” rumbled across the black top and we both watched Marley approach us. Brent stayed outside because he still had a cigarette to finish. I stayed because I felt less animosity towards Marley than I usually did.  

“What’s up fellas?”

“Sup Marley”

“Hello Marley”

Marley looked a little shocked at first when I said the full “hello”. I guess somewhere in his monkey brain, he had registered that we didn't really like him that much. Or, the obvious difference from my usual tone was so great it made Marley realize: up *until* this moment we had not liked him very much. No matter the reason, and to Brent’s relief, this completely shut Marley down. He then lit a cigarette, and started scrolling on his phone while humming “Buffalo Soldier” to himself.

Work was slow, I prepped pizzas while Brent did the dishes. I  was getting pretty decent at stretching the dough, and I was almost ready to toss it in the air, but I knew I wasn’t completely ready yet. So I didn’t attempt it, because no matter how enlightened I had become at a random church, I was not ready to be humiliated in front of Brent so soon after he scared the shit out of me.

I did come to a conclusion while I was stretching, though. Before working for Large Lou’s I was a poor and irresponsible person. Who knows how much negative energy I had cultivated in that time without even knowing. That energy had gone somewhere, and I was going to live without it. But why not create new energy that was positive and worked in my favor.

I made a prayer. Not for anything in particular. I wasn’t really sure how all of this worked, and I thought maybe a little soul energy could put some pep in my step. 

When I clocked out for the night, I brought the trash bags out with me to throw them away. As I tossed them in the dumpster, I almost thought I could hear the HomeLess Man whimpering. 

I drove into the parking lot of my apartment complex. Once I got out of my car, I thanked the duck.

“469,255”

I laughed, “I didn’t know you had the ability to actually be helpful. I really, honestly, feel so much better about all this other crap going on.”

The duck picked through his feathers again. Given the luck I had with the last feather, I felt excited, hoping this next feather might be winning lottery numbers or something. I was smiling when he dropped the feather in my hand. I stopped smiling when I started to read it.

“When paths converge. Your knowledge will find the cracks in a damaged road.”

Maybe I should try to kill the duck.

The next day, I was still in a good mood. The duck followed me to work, but I wasn’t bothered. I happily waved at Shirley when she pulled into the parking lot soon after me. I even decided to try Woof’s again. It was still gross. But I was okay with that.

When the meat delivery showed up, I decided to just let it go. Let whatever negative energy the MeatBoy and the meat delivery man had be their own burden to bear. I did, however, pray before they arrived. Yesterday’s trial run felt pretty good, and I wanted to give this a more honest to goodness effort. So I closed my eyes as the meat delivery man arrived, and focused my prayer on the truck. The HomeLess man was just beginning to scurry away as the truck pulled in. The meat delivery man stepped out and handed me the invoice.

“Hey, sorry about that whole *thing* that happened a few days ago.” 

I handed the signed invoice back to him and smiled, “It’s alright. I forgive you.”

We both spoke over the roaring of the MeatBoy in the truck. For reasons I couldn’t begin to imagine why. It was most likely due to great negative energy emanating from the thing in the truck, and the delivery man. So I did my best to maintain a positive output to outweigh them. 

To my surprise, when I received the order, everything was there. I picked up the product, and went inside, thanking the delivery man before he hopped back in his truck and drove off.

It was Brent’s turn again to prep pizzas while I did the dishes. Which went fairly smoothly. I even got to have a delightful chat with Max about the weather, while Brent kept muttering a delighted “wee” every time he tossed the pizza dough in the air.

Then, once work was finished, it was finally time for me to go to the new members meeting at church. I was very excited. I felt kind of like my old self before all the weird creepy shit started happening, but… happier. 

I pulled into the parking lot and my green-headed friend landed behind me. I stepped out of the car, and looked at the church’s glowing white sign illuminating the sidewalk in front of it. I looked at the duck, and smiled. 

“No matter how much you upset me. Thank you for showing me this.”

“428,312”

Bartholomew came out of the front doors of the church and called to me, “Charlie! It’s so good to see that you made it. How has your day been?”

I turned around and greeted the pastor, “Pastor, nice to see you too. My day’s been great so far.”

As he approached, I realized he wasn’t wearing the same casual t-shirt and jeans as he was yesterday. Instead, Bartholomew was dressed in a kind of dark red hooded robe. I’d like to reiterate, it had been almost a decade since I had done anything religious. So I was very out of the times with what was a cult and what was just another religion. I mean, the Catholics wore very similar garbs and nobody was giving *them* any flak for being a cult.

Bartholomew put an enrobed arm around my shoulder and guided me towards the doors, “Excellent, excellent. Say, do you like oatmeal?”

As we talked about oatmeal, I looked back at the duck, who watched with a cocked head as I went inside the church. His eyes bore the same look they had before I met the HomeLess Man, but I ignored it and continued on inside. 

The strange thing was. I could have sworn I saw the pastor look back at the duck as well.

The lobby was empty. Save for a few signs that were up saying, “**New Members Welcome**” and “**Please Proceed To The Elevator For Orientation**”. 

I raised an eyebrow and looked at the pastor, “Elevator?”

Bartholomew laughed that same infectious laugh, saying, “Yes, Charlie my good man. I’ll show you.”

He walked to the right end of the lobby, and pushed a button behind one of the vases with stalks of wheat.

A few moments later there was a ding, and the wall beside the vase slid open to reveal the interior of an elevator. Bartholomew smiled at me and gestured for me to follow inside. I started to step forward when suddenly, the front doors of the church swung open frantically, and I spun around in surprise to see a man in similar robes to the pastor but white. Once inside, he doubled over and started catching his breath.

After a moment, he stood back to full height, and looked at me and Bartholomew, “Sorry I’m late, I was having some issues with closing up the store.” He said between shallow breaths for air.

Bartholomew waved his hand dismissively, “It’s alright, just get in, it’s a good thing you got here before the ceremony.”

When he got closer, I realized he was none other than the delivery man who brings meat to Large Lou’s Pizza. It seemed that he recognized me as well. His face changed from apologetic, to surprised. He nodded to me, and I nodded back. Then both entered the elevator after Bartholomew. The doors closed in front of us, and then we began our descent.

“It’s going to be a minute,” Bartholomew explained to me, “our underground portion is a ways down. Can I say Charlie, we usually don’t show members so new everything that happens in this area. But, given you’re part of Large Lou’s, I discussed it with the board and we’ve made an exception for you.” He followed this with a mischievous wink.

I thought it was odd, but then got a little too in my head about feeling special and forgot to ask anymore questions. While the elevator descended, Bartholomew stood at the front, patiently waiting, while me and the delivery man stood at the back. Bartholomew coughed. The delivery man cleared his throat. I sneezed.

“Bless you” said the delivery man, and the pastor.

“Thank you.” I responded.

The delivery man cleared his throat again and then said, “Hey, I just wanted to apologize for that whole thing again. I thought you had already met him, y’know, Large Lou’s being the establishment that it is and all, and that was my fault for not asking first.”

I shook my head, “It’s okay, I can let it go, man.”

Bartholomew piped up, “Oh, so you’ve met Ford already?”

I laughed, “I sure have, I hadn’t gotten his name yet though.” I stuck a hand out to Ford, “Nice to meet you, Ford.”

The delivery man shook my hand, but said in a confused tone, “My name’s Hadrien.”

Now I was confused. But then the elevator dinged and the doors slid back open while Bartholomew said in a voice like an announcer, “Welcome, to the Temple of Cogitology.” 

My jaw dropped as I stepped out of the elevator and into the lobby of the temple. The room walls were dark with intricate designs and symbols covering every inch of them. Two amber orbs hung from the ceiling, illuminating the room in a warm, and comfortable light. Men and women in the same robes as Hadrien were chatting and laughing with each other. While eating bowls of oatmeal from an expansive oatmeal bar at the back of the lobby.

When Bartholomew stepped out from the elevator, the whole crowd began to clap and cheer, happy that their leader had come to partake in the festivities. He opened his arms to accept their gratitude, and then bowed to them, before they returned to whatever conversations they were having with each other.

Bartholomew then turned back to me and said, “Come, I will show you what this temple has to offer.”

At the left end of the lobby, there was a large wooden door with a book engraved into the door. Above it, was an oddly familiar symbol.

Bartholomew explained that this was the library. Many of the books of the prophets were stored there, and members were allowed to come and go from the library as they pleased.

“All the greatest minds tended to also be members of Cogitology: Newton, Edison, Tesla, Franklin, Einstein...” As he explained the books and the authors, I noticed the architecture.

The library had the same aesthetic as the main room did, With the addition of copious bookshelves. It seemed like more than just religious studies as well. There were books on zoology, biophysics, biochemical engineering, and psychology.

The next door, which was opposite the elevator door, was the lab. Bartholomew simply gestured to it, saying he would show me the inside later since we will be heading in there once the ceremony gets under way. However, I did look at the door for a bit. It had the words LAB written on it, but then the same symbol higher up as the library did. 

Finally, the third and final door was the temple’s sanctuary. Bartholomew explained we will be opening the ceremony there very soon, so I would get to see it then. This time that *was* no other symbol or text. This sanctuary door simply had the symbol on it that was giving me very odd deja vu.

“That’s just about it. Have you got any questions for me?” Bartholomew asked, then checking his watch and saying, “Cuz I gotta scoot soon to get on with the proceedings.”

I pointed at the symbol on the door, “Yeah, what is that?”

“Oh, that? That’s our church symbol,” he told me with the same nonchalant tone somebody uses when telling you the artist of a painting, “First, we use our minds in this church. Thought and scientific research happen to be at the forefront of our mission here. Then the brain, instead of having the normal wrinkles, has circuitry lines on it. This is because of our philosophy on holy energy and such. We wire our brains to something greater than our own strength.”

Somewhere around “this church” and “scientific research” I stopped paying full attention to what the pastor was saying. Because while it was interesting to hear about what it meant, I couldn’t get over the fact it was the exact same symbol on the meat delivery truck and soda fountain at Large Lou’s.

A bell rang in the room and everyone fell silent. Then, at the door to the sanctuary, one of the robed members stepped in front of the door and addressed the group.

“Ladies and gentlemen. Please proceed into the sanctuary as the ritual will soon begin.”

I want to open this portion by removing any images you’re building in your head about sanctuaries. Sure, in the sense of what this room does, it is a sanctuary, but that is where the similarities stop, and I want to keep the delineation clear for you as I describe what this room looked like.

The sanctuary was filled floor to ceiling with odd beeping machines. Some with screens that showed numbers that were constantly changing. Others were arms that moved about with spinning rods at the ends of them, pointing at other machines that did other things. The sanctuary was even larger than the main room, but all of the machinery and electronics gave a very cramped and hectic environment. Colored stripes on the floor encouraged members to walk to what looked like a viewing area, with a large glass orb in the ceiling above us pulsed in muted shades of blue and grey. On the other side of the sanctuary, opposite the viewing area, there was a ten foot circle that seemed to be the main area of focus for all of the machines. It looked kind of like an over engineered metal detector.

The viewing area had the same maroon red pews as the main church area upstairs, and once everyone was seated, Bartholomew stepped out from a section of the room that had an exceptionally high amount of screens and turned to the crowd.

“Hello, friends. I don’t have much time, since I’ve been informed that connectivity levels are spiking, so we will be rushing this whole event ahead of schedule to catch that influx of energy. We have a new member joining us today, his name is Charlie-” Bartholomew laughed a bit as he said this, but then regained his composure, “why don’t you stand up Charlie?”

He looked at me, and held out a hand gesturing that I stand up. When I did, the whole room started to clap. It felt awkward, and once it was done I was happy to sit back down. Then, Bartholomew spoke to the crowd a little more.

“Now, let’s cut to the chase. After some tweaking of numbers, and fine tuning of the machines. We believe we’ve finally got it. So, as I do at every one of these meetings. Please, bow your heads, and focus your energy as best you can. Like last time, chant the words: ‘sikh faht mram’. It got us close, and I think we really have it. No blood sacrifices today, we’re simply too low on resources and don’t want to risk another accident. With that all out of the way, let’s begin with the ritual.”

Bartholomew signaled to the men in the screen corner that Bartholomew had walked out of before. One of them, whom I realized was the delivery driver, and as he pushed a button, the machines behind Bartholomew started to make more noise, things that weren’t moving before started to move slowly about the circle, and things that were moving already started moving at a faster pace.

From the sanctuary doors, a stainless steel cart holding 7 squirming bags wheeled into the room. The man, who wore a lab coat instead of the robes that many of the other members were wearing, took each bag, with much difficulty, and placed them in seven different cases, each labeled with different names on the glass doors of the cases: Lem Lim, Bubbles, Cerberus, Mountain Storm, AS, ASZ, and Yorg Drizzle.

Once the cases were closed, the bags were quickly blended up with what was assumedly blender blades at the bottom of the cases. Red viscera and slime sprayed about, until the bags were all properly blended. This was scary to say the least, but given the lack of reaction from everyone here, I assumed I probably wasn’t openly witnessing animal cruelty.

Then, as more around me began to pray, the orb above us shifted from blue and grey to a dark black. I watched the machines get louder and move more quickly until the ten foot circle began to glow bright white. So bright, in fact, I had to shield my eyes.

The sanctuary grew even louder, something that made me realize why they had this whole operation happening underground. However, the increasing noise sounded not like the mechanical whirring of machines. Instead, it sounded like deep guttural roars, and tortured screams. The whole room actually got quite hot as well.

This continued for several moments. The machines whirred faster and faster, until a loud **POP** sounded, and the light in the circle snapped off suddenly.

While I waited for my eyes to adjust to the sudden shift in brightness, I could hear the machines begin to die down, returning to a much more tolerable volume. However, there was still something that was groaning and growling. Once my eyes did eventually adjust to the light. I discovered the source of the sound.

An amorphous heap of flesh with two eyes near the top sat in the circle. It seemed to slide around like a slug, and continued to growl angrily at the church members. As the other members realized what had come out, I expected to hear screams of fear and horror. 

Instead, they were groans of disappointment.

A large tube descended from the top of the ceiling dropped down on top of the blob. Then, a few moments later, it started to rise back up, taking the blob with it. The man in the lab coat grabbed a bunch of plastic bags from the cart, and dispensed the gooey contents of the cases into the bags via nozzles. Each of the cases now had different colors of goop inside them: Lem Lim, and Bubbles had become clear, almost see through. Cerberus, AS, and ASZ were now dark brown. Yog Drizzle had turned beige. And Mountain Storm had turned a neon green.

I seemed like the first part of the ceremony was completed, as members began to file out, but once people were in the lobby, nobody seemed to want to go into the lab. After some hunting, I was able to find Bartholomew and asked him about this.

“I’m sorry, Charlie. I thought we were gonna be pulling something better. This is where the ceremony ends. I can still show you the lab though, if you want to see it.”

I nodded, and we started to walk over.

“So… what do you mean you thought you’d pull something better?” I asked.

Bartholomew sighed, “It’s complicated. See, given you work at Large Lou’s, I thought your being here would help kickstart the activity in hell, and finally give us what Cogitology has been searching for. Unfortunately, we have been ending up with most of Sloth’s underlings, since they’re the easiest to catch.”

We stepped into the lab, and the blob was now hooked up to a series of machines, and there were a few people in lab coats writing furiously on laptops, then looking at the monitors on the machines hooked up to the beast, and then typing more things down.

“This is the lab,” Bartholomew said while gesturing defeatedly about the room, “Here, we study what we’ve pulled from hell. With enough luck, we get to learn from our mistakes, and then further correct the machines in the sanctuary and try again.”

Nearing the end of their note taking, the lab members began closing their laptops, and disconnecting the demon from the machines it was hooked up to. Bartholomew stepped back and tapped my shoulder, suggesting I follow suit.

After the machines were all disconnected, one lab technician typed into a computer connected to the platform the monster lay upon, and then they all joined me and Bartholomew in the back of the lab as blue flames engulfed the creature.

My eyes were wide with shock, and Bartholomew must have noticed my expression, as he said, “Yeah, we have to do that with every single one now. We used to have cages on the sides of the lab, but after one of them got loose, we decided it was easier to just destroy them as soon as we were finished studying.”

Once the flames died down, the research team swept up the charred remains, and disposed of them.

As we walked back out of the lab, I turned to Bartholomew, and decided to ask him a question that was still burning in my mind, “Still, one hell of a welcome ceremony.”

Bartholomew laughed when I said this.

“This isn’t the new member’s ceremony. That was just a joke I thought I’d pull on you. I know you’re already well acquainted with our religion and what we do.”

“What?”

“You take so much of our leftovers from the demons, I really am happy that one of you finally decided to see how it’s all made. Having Large Lou’s be a shelter to so many has really brought a great amount of good energy into the world.”

Shelter? What was he talking about?

“The pizza place?” I asked?

“Oh! Is that what you folks serve? Well, that explains how you were able to make good use of Ford.”

My brain was spinning. All of a sudden looking for religion was not the best idea I’ve ever had.

“I’m tired,” I told Bartholomew, “I think I’m going to bed, now.”

Bartholomew nodded his head understandingly, “Right, I’m sure you have work tomorrow. Thank you so much for coming, Charlie, and we hope to see you at service next week.”

I barely heard that last part, as I was already half way to the elevator.

Once I was outside, the duck still stood beside my car, and looked at me as I walked out the front doors.

“424,795”

In a fit of rage I flipped him the bird, then felt a mix of shame, and embarrassment as I pulled my hand back and instead spat “Kill yourself” at the bird before getting in my car, lighting a cigarette, and driving home.

I don't want to go to work anymore.  I’ve been sitting in my apartment and playing games, while the duck continues to count at me through the window.

He just said “135,130”, and I couldn’t care less what he’s counting to at this point.

I was supposed to get a paycheck last Friday that I never received, and since I’m kind of done with working at Large Lou’s, I’ll just go in tomorrow, pick up my check, and tell them I quit. But once I do this, I will never EVER even step foot near Large Lou’s pizza ever again for as long as I live.
