When I was about ten, my siblings and I used to spend almost every summer at our grandmother's house in the countryside. There was a huge forest not far from her property, and we were strictly forbidden from going too deep into it. My grandmother constantly warned us about the swamps, saying that people could easily get lost or step into a patch of ground that looked solid but wasn't. Of course, being kids, we never took her warnings particularly seriously.

One afternoon, the four of us decided to go exploring. We weren't planning to go far, just walk along the edge of the forest and maybe find some berries. Eventually, we came across a patch of wild strawberries and spent a while picking and eating them. I remember how excited we were because there were so many. After that, someone suggested playing hide-and-seek. I volunteered to count, closed my eyes against a tree, and started counting to fifty.

When I opened my eyes, everyone was gone. At first, I thought they'd found really good hiding places, so I started searching behind trees and bushes. I walked around for what felt like ten minutes, calling their names and laughing because nobody was answering. Eventually, I got annoyed and shouted that I was giving up. Still nothing. That's when I decided to head back toward the road and wait for them there.

Except I couldn't find the road.

I turned around, trying to retrace my steps, but suddenly nothing looked familiar. I couldn't recognize the tree I'd counted against, the strawberry bushes, or even the direction we'd come from. The forest seemed much darker than before. The trees were so tall and densely packed that barely any sunlight reached the ground. I remember looking up and realizing I couldn't even see the sky properly. I started shouting my siblings' names again, this time much louder, but there was no answer.

I don't know how long I wandered around. It felt like hours. I kept walking in what I thought was the right direction, only to find myself surrounded by more trees. At one point, my shoe sank into soft, wet ground, and I remembered my grandmother's warnings about the swamps. I pulled my foot free and backed away, terrified that the next step might be my last. By then I was crying so hard I could barely breathe. I knew it would be dark in a few hours, and I couldn't stop imagining spending the night alone out there.

Eventually, I just sat down on the ground because I was too scared to keep walking. I remember closing my eyes and silently begging for someone to come and save me. I promised that if I ever made it out, I would never set foot in that forest again. I don't even know who I was making that promise to. I just wanted to go home.

And you know what? About fifteen minutes later, I heard someone calling my name.

I jumped up and ran toward the voice as fast as I could. I didn't even stop to think about where I was going. Somehow, the trees started thinning out, and suddenly I was standing on the road near my grandmother's house. My brother was there with our grandmother and two neighbors. The moment they saw me, they all ran toward me. Apparently, I'd been missing for almost four hours.

Later, my brother told me something that still doesn't make sense. He said they'd been hiding for less than a minute when they heard me screaming. They immediately came out to find me, but I was already gone. They searched everywhere around the spot where we'd been playing, but there was no sign of me.

I still don't understand how I managed to get so lost in such a short amount of time. But I kept my promise. I never went into that forest again.
