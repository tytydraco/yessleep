I live alone in an old farmhouse twenty minutes outside a town that barely qualifies as one. The nearest neighbor is over a mile down a gravel road that turns to mud every time it rains. Cell service dies at the end of the driveway. The landlord lives in another county and only comes out if something is actively on fire.

At first the quiet felt like the whole reason I moved here.

Then the house began settling in ways it never had before.

It started with the pipes. A soft, rhythmic knocking that traveled through the kitchen wall, into the narrow hallway, and stopped directly behind wherever I stood. If I moved, it waited. If I stayed still long enough, it answered with three slow taps, as if confirming I was still there.

I blamed the age of the place. Expanding wood. Temperature swings. The usual explanations people invent when they live this far from anyone who could tell them they’re wrong.

Then it learned my habits.

I talk to myself when I cook. Small, useless comments that used to disappear into the empty rooms. One night I muttered “Where did I put the salt” and, from inside the wall beside the stove, something tapped twice; Exactly where the salt canister sat on the shelf!!

Two days later I was searching the linen closet and said, without thinking, “I know I have more in here somewhere.”  
Three light, evenly spaced knocks answered from the precise shelf where the extra towels were folded.

I stopped speaking out loud.

It made no difference.

The house began answering the things I only thought.

I would reach for a glass and hear a single tap behind the cabinet a second before my fingers closed around it. I would hesitate in the hallway and the floorboard three feet ahead would creak once, as if the decision had already been made.

Last week I woke at 2:14 a.m. to the sound of something large shifting its weight inside the wall that runs along my bed. Not scratching. Not animals. Something patient, testing the plaster from the other side. When I held my breath, it held still. When I exhaled, it moved again, matching the rhythm of my breathing exactly.

I pressed my palm flat against the wall.

Something on the other side pressed back.

The pressure was warm.

In the morning a perfect handprint remained in the dust on the outside of the wall, level with where my hand had been. The fingers were longer than mine. The palm was wider. It looked as though the plaster itself had softened just long enough to take the impression and then hardened again.

I called the landlord. He told me he would send someone “when he could.” No one came. The nearest hardware store is forty minutes away. The nearest person who might actually listen is farther than that.

I started sleeping in the living room with every light on.

It does not like the lights.

When the bulbs are burning it retreats deeper into the walls, but I can still hear it relocating—soft, deliberate rearrangements, the sound of something heavy being dragged through spaces too narrow to hold it. Sometimes it simply waits just behind whatever surface I am closest to. The plaster there feels slightly warmer than the rest of the room.

Two nights ago I did something irreversible..

I stood in the center of the living room and whispered, “What do you want?”

Silence stretched until it hurt.

Then a single, clear knock answered from the wall in front of me.

I asked again!

This time the answer came from the wall, the floor, and the ceiling at once—three knocks in perfect unison, so tightly layered they almost sounded like one sound.

Since then the house has grown busier.

I hear it measuring the last rooms I have avoided. Soft presses against the plaster. Careful shifts of weight. The low sound of something slowly unfolding itself into cavities that were never meant to contain it.

Last night I noticed the first real change.

The original handprint on the bedroom wall is gone.

In its place is a second print, smaller, the exact size and shape of my own left hand. It is pressed deeper into the plaster than the first one ever was, as if whatever is inside has begun practicing the fit.

This morning I found a third print..

It was on the inside of my bedroom door, at the exact height where my hand rests when I close it. The fingers matched mine perfectly. The only difference was that the print was made from the other side of the wood—from inside the door itself.

I have stopped sleeping!

Every time I close my eyes I feel the walls breathing in time with me. The rhythm is no longer matching mine. Mine is matching it.

An hour ago I tried to leave!!

I unlocked the front door, stepped onto the porch, and immediately heard three slow taps from the hallway behind me; Exactly the same pattern it has always used to confirm my presence!

I turned around.

The hallway was empty..

But the front door was already closed..

I do not remember closing it.

Outside, the gravel road disappears into fog so thick the trees look like unfinished sketches. There is no sound of cars. No porch lights in the distance. Only the dark and the house waiting behind me.

I am writing this at the kitchen table with my back against the only wall that has not yet answered.

The rest of the house is no longer quiet.

It is listening.

And I finally understand what it has been measuring all this time.

It was never learning the shape of the rooms.

It was learning the shape of me.

The final handprint appeared five minutes ago, directly in front of where I am sitting. It is the exact size of my right hand. It is still warm. And when I place my own hand over it, the plaster softens just enough to let my fingers sink in to the exact depth of the print already waiting there..

as if the wall has been holding the space open for me the entire time,and has only now decided I am ready to fit...

What should I do..?
