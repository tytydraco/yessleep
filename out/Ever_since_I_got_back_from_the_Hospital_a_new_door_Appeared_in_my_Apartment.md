Everything's felt different since I got back, and I've had some trouble remembering things and collecting my thoughts so. I'm going to start keeping this digital journal to hopefully help with all that. 

A couple of weeks ago I fell at work and woke up in the hospital. I had been up on a ladder when I lost my balance doing something stupid, and before I knew it the back of my head was smacking the ground. 

On the way to the hospital I suffered a stroke, they kept me for almost a week, monitoring me and running tests. I had suffered some minor brain damage, but compared to what it could have been I was extremely lucky. I wouldn't consider any amount of brain damage lucky but the doctors assured me I could make a full recovery given time. 

I've been back home in my apartment for a few days now, and things have felt off. I was told to expect some mental fog and headaches, and I was experiencing those. But this was different, I felt confused and kept seeing movement out of the corners of my eyes.

I wish I could say I had someone to tell about this, but I really don’t. I never kept in close touch with my friends after school ended, and they never really reached out either. Sometimes I wonder if we would have all still been friends if not for my pride, or more accurately my fear that we weren't really all that close. But it seems in hindsight that the fear that kept me from reaching out in the first place to try, ended up isolating me anyways. 

Funny enough we all still have each other added on facebook, and I watched through the digital glass as most of them met up for a recent wedding. I guess I missed the invite. And I haven't exactly been putting myself out there as far as making new connections, I think somewhere along the way I became comfortable in solitude.

I don’t have any siblings or cousins. I was the sole child of our aging family tree. And now I'm the sole remainder of our family all together. Grim I know but it's the truth, and necessary to explain why I'm sharing my thoughts here. Or maybe it's self indulgent, woe is me and all that.

But today when I woke up, something was different. As I emerged from my bedroom I spotted a red door with a brass knob sticking out from behind my couch. And no I don’t mean propped against the wall I mean screwed into a frame that never existed. I pulled my couch aside and tried for the knob only to find it locked. I tried knocking fruitlessly before stepping back and examining the wall around it, there was no way someone could have just installed this while I was sleeping. 

I decided I was going to take the damn thing off its hinges, one way or another I was going to find out what was inside. And hopefully prove I hadn’t just lost my mind, I mean this isn’t exactly what I would call a hallucination. I could run my fingers across its surface and feel the imperfections of the paint. I felt the cold brass of the knob and the clicking of its internal lock. It was here, it was real, I just needed some tools.

I just need to drive down to the store and get a screwdriver set and I could pop it right out of the wall. On my way out I noticed a small stack of bills and mail by the door. I have to remember to check on those later, they look important.

When I returned from the store I headed straight for the red door, and that's when I spotted it. On my coffee table sat a brand new screwdriver set, the exact same set that I had just purchased. 

In front of the red door was a single curved scrape along the floor that outlined the inward swinging of its path. It had opened while I was gone, and even possibly left me a screwdriver set. This all sounds crazy but I don’t have any real explanation for what's going on, but maybe I can get one. 

I got to work on the hinges, and one by one I freed the screws from their frame. Finally I took off the doorknob and pulled away the door, I held my breath as I peered behind it. 

There was nothing. Well not like an empty nothing more like a wall, only a quarter inch from the doorframe. It was covered in wallpaper, a different one than the rest of my apartment, and it looked a fair bit more worn. It had an ornate floral pattern that blended seamlessly into looping textures, the paper had yellowed with time but still held faded colors. It looked familiar, but I couldn't place where I had seen it before, nor could I confirm I actually had seen it before. 

I felt relieved that nothing was on the other side, but then why did it open when I was gone? I spent an embarrassing amount of time trying to find a way to open the wall on the other side. I became convinced it was a false wall or had some sort of trapdoor mechanism, but I found no such thing. I debated just trying to smash my way through, but I was afraid that I might end up busting into someone else's apartment. 

I plan on returning the screwdriver set to the store tomorrow seeing as I have two of them now. Hopefully now I can put this whole door thing to bed and feel like a normal person again.

Last night I woke up to the sound of knocking at 2 am. 

I stumbled out of bed and started dressing, wondering who could be here at this hour. But as I opened my bedroom door out to the hall, I stopped in my tracks. 

Directly across the hall from my bedroom was the red door. This time with the addition of a brass mailslot three feet up the door.. Through the cracks of the mailslot poured light, a glow that made me squint with pained eyes. From behind the door came faint sounds, and I leaned in straining to hear. Wind chimes and birds and the sound of branches rustling in a calm breeze, but just before my ear met the door's surface the knock sounded again. 

The same knock my grandfather did every time he visited my childhood home when he was still alive. 

Thump, thump, thum-thump, thump. Shave and a haircut. 

And just like my grandfather it never finished the rhythm, instead waiting for a knock back.

“Grandpa?” I whispered out toward the door. 

Again it repeated its half of the knock.

Thump, thump, thum-thump, thump. Shave and a haircut.

Cautiously I reached out to knock back. 

Thump, thump. Two bits. 

I completed the knock and held my breath, but nothing happened. And after a moment I reached out for the knob, unsure what else to do. To my surprise it clicked, and I pulled the door open.

On the other side was a screen door between me and a flood of sunlight. Somehow through the doorway was the front porch of my childhood home, and beyond it the well kept lawn and our tall oak tree adorned with a thick-roped tire swing. 

The cool morning breeze met my face, the sound of chirping birds and windchimes now in full. This was impossible, I was 6 floors up, there was another apartment on the other side of this wall. None of this could be real. 

And yet, the sun felt warm on my skin through the screen, the breeze was cool and the air smelt of fresh grass and sidewalks wet with morning dew. It was exactly how I remembered it, and it only made it more terrifying. The street, the neighboring houses, the trees and well kept lawns and classic cars in driveways. It was all in there, but I couldn’t step through. 

Afraid the world itself was coming undone I shut the door, and the light that had been visible through the cracks before, disappeared as the knob clicked in place. This time I need help, I have to be sure this is real. First thing in the morning i'll call the property manager and ask for maintenance to come check out this door. In the meantime I'm going on a walk to clear my head. On my way out I noticed a small stack of bills and mail by the door. I have to remember to check on those later, they look important.

Another sleepless Night.

Last night the door woke me up again, or maybe it was a dream I can’t even be sure. It was right above my bed, already hanging open. Through the door extending straight upward was a familiar dim hallway. It was my grandparents house, their own hall stretching up impossibly into the sky. At the end of the hall I could see the hue-shifting glow of a tv, the muffled sounds of a sitcom drifted down the hall. Intermittently I could hear their laughter, a warm familiar sound. 

As my eyes adjusted to the dark, I got up from my bed to rummage around for a stool. But when I returned the door was being pulled shut from within by something, an even smooth pull followed by the clicking of a lock. 

Instead of returning to bed I got to work with my tools and once again pulled the door off its hinges. It fell to my bed with a thud that I worried might wake the neighbors, but I soon forgot seeing the place beyond the door had changed. 

On the other side was a white louvered closet door, its slats letting in morning sunlight despite the real time. Between the slats I recognized the room, my childhood bedroom sat beyond the closet door on my ceiling. But beyond the slats everything was rotated parallel with the doorframe, as if gravity kept different rules just beyond the threshold. This time I had to be sure, I reached up and pulled the closet door open before climbing inside. And as my feet left the stool, I felt my weight suddenly lurch horizontally and nearly fell backwards into my apartment. 

For a moment I struggled on all fours to orient myself as I looked back at the closet door, seeing my bed straight ahead appearing as if it had been stood up against the wall. The stool looked like it had been placed directly into a wall, I forced myself to look away as dizziness swept over me. 

I was here, like my entire childhood bedroom had been placed in a time capsule. I was practically standing in a memory, I dug through my old toy chest clutching a plastic body in my hand. It was real, it was all real. I could smell breakfast being prepared, the aroma of bacon causing me to salivate.

As I stepped toward the blinds-covered window I heard the sound of heavy footsteps coming from beyond my bedroom door. As it got closer down the hall I recognized the sound as my fathers work shoes. Realizing they were nearing my door, I reached for the knob only to find it locked. 

“Hello?” I called out hesitantly.

The footsteps stopped just behind the door, the shadow of two shoes casting beneath the crack but no response. 

“Please is someone there? I don’t understand where I am.” 

“Whos out there?” I tried one final time.

And my fathers voice replied.

“Your mom wanted me to remind you you’re grounded before I leave for work, but instead I'll just remind you to take out the trash before I get home. I guess its my fault you’re a disobedient fuck up, my gear of becoming my own father poisoned my efforts in raising you. But I couldn’t trust myself, if I could hurt the people I love the most, even as a punishment, what would that make me?” 

I felt a turning in my stomach as he talked at me, words my father had never spoken. But about things that sowed doubt into my image of the man he was, things that I almost feared were true.

“Instead I ignored anything that upset me, unwilling to react in fear of blowing up. And your mother, your poor mother, left to be the bad cop, the jury and the judge. You resented her, and she resented me in turn, as it was like many things, all my fault.”

“D-dad?” My voice cracked as I spoke.

“Before I leave for work, your mother and I will kiss with the same transactional formality of a public bus making its route. In the parking lot I’ll take off my wedding ring before entering the office and hitting on the receptionist that drunkenly kissed at last year's christmas party. I still remember the feeling of her clumsy fingers running over my zipper as our lips met, only pulling away after seeing your judging face staring up at me from my desk. Now I place the photo facedown instinctually when she comes by my office, how monstrous is that?” he finishes nearly emotionless. 

Stunned at their words, and unsure if any of that could be true I took a step away from the bedroom door. I watch the shadow beneath the door intently, frozen in place. 

Suddenly the violent banging and rattling of the door breaks me from my fear and I dart back toward the closet. The banging becomes louder and louder, the door practically jumping in its frame with each blow. The frame and hinges begin to give way just as I cross the threshold back into my apartment. But as I tumble out, again my weight and inertia shift so suddenly I find myself twisting through the air.   
I fell for a half second in terror, before I felt myself smack against the bedframe and everything went dark.

Or at least move somewhere else, If I could even figure out what was wrong with my bank. I even considered selling items from behind the red door if I could physically bring them back with me. A crushing silence enveloped me as I sniffled back tears, and for a brief moment we stood in the darkness facing one another.

Her tone softening she whispered “I should be sorry too.” before my light came back on, and she was gone.

I looked around the room, remembering my plan, I pulled a quilted blanket off the corner armchair and tucked it into my bag. It smelt like her perfume. Her words played over and over in my mind, then my fathers. Is this really what was going on? Had they really kept it in till their death beds? The more I considered it the more overwhelmed I felt. But I had to keep moving.

A rising terror overtook me, the thought of being stuck in that place nearly sending me into a panic. Shakily I produced my tools and began working on the door, constantly checking over my shoulder and jumping at every noise as I worked from the bathroom floor. Finally I pulled the door away from its frame, and my breath caught in my throat.

A second door just behind it, a half foot of space between them. 

Frantically now I knelt down again, working the screwdriver on the doorknob and hinges. From my childhood living room I heard the sound of our clock begin to chime, over and over. I removed the second door, to find a third. 

With rising frustration and panic I roughly tossed the doors into the hall to make space before getting to work on the third. When the sound of a loud crash causes me to stop and listen. The chiming of the clock stops as I hear the bang and shattering echo through the house. 

I redouble my efforts as more crashes begin to sound, a few pieces clattering down the hallway as the destruction continues. The third door comes off, and as I turn to push it out of my way a heavy thudding comes down the hall.

It stops just before the bathroom doorway, but I can hear one of the discarded wooden doors creaking under its weight. 

“Son” the voices of both my mother and father overlap. “Don’t leave”

I try to speak but nothing comes out, fear holding me hostage. 

“I love you” it’s voices overlap.

“You're not them.” I managed to say.

The wood creaks as if it shifts its weight, a heavy silence hanging over. 

“Stay” it hisses.

I glance over my shoulder seeing my apartment through the doorway, no more doors blocking my path. I wonder for a moment if this thing will try and stop me, or if I could even resist. 

I dart for the exit without a word, and I hear the sound of heavy snapping wood behind me and the resulting shower of splinters striking the tiled bathroom. As I tumble out into my apartment I pull myself over my couch and whip around to see the doorway. From within the darkness I start to hear whistling, a familiar tune unmistakable from any other in my mind. One my father used to whistle to me as a kid, one he used to calm me down as a baby. His own version, misremembered and out of tune, came out of the doorway as if plucked directly from my memory.

And slowly in the darkness I watched as the shadow of an arm reached across the doorway and pulled my apartment door shut from within as if it had never changed. I stared blankly at my front door, unsure if the apartment really still lay behind it, or if I really ever escaped the red door in the first place. For all I know I could be in a perfect copy of my own home, and how would I even tell.

I doubt I'll get any sleep after this, I don’t know how much more of this I can take. I don’t know what the fuck that thing in there is, but it clearly knows me. I’ll try and update if something happens, for now I just need to rest. I noticed a small stack of bills and mail by the door. I have to remember to check on those later, they look important.

I stepped toward the garage door, the tall windows flashing with light as distant fireworks boomed in the sky.

“I remember you were afraid of the pop-its, scared they’d blow pieces right off your hand. So I sat for a while and watched the fireworks through the garage door, and listened to the sounds of a happy family. And at some point I heard the sound of the car door opening, and without protest, I climbed inside, expecting to die.

I woke to find myself in an unfamiliar bed, and felt cold metal around my left wrist. I blinked as my eyes adjusted to the bright room, and found myself in a small hospital room. I feel drowsy, hardly able to think I try to rise from bed and find my back in pain.

After a while someone comes along and spots me awake, and eventually someone enters the room. But I can hardly make heads or tails of the conversation, mumbling at responses as they speak at me. 

She shut the blinds for me, and that helped a bit. She talked about care plans, and something about my insurance, and asked about family. At some point she asked me if there was anyone the hospital could call to inform, and I couldn’t think of anyone. I wanted to see my grandfather, so I asked if he could come and see me. She said that she would see what she could do, and I hoped that was enough. 

But he never came, and neither did anyone else. And at some point I remembered he was dead, and he had been for a long time. 

And as I looked around and realized that my fear had come true, and that I had indeed lost my mind, and was now sitting in a hospital bed like I was sick.
