I'm someone who enjoys exploring abandoned places, wandering through spaces consumed by time, where you can see the marks left by everyone who's passed through, from a clumsily done graffiti to a simple broken glass bottle. I've always loved being in a place that keeps me on edge, where the cold caress of the wind sends shivers down my spine.

The smell of plants, growing, devouring the foundations of something built before I was even born. Those hints of dampness, tobacco, and alcohol, penetrating my nose until they fill my lungs.

All of this accompanied by the creaking of the floorboards along the hallways beneath my feet, sometimes even the light touch of tree branches against the windows and the police tape being moved by the wind.

But beyond that, I'm drawn to learning about urban legends and exploring the places where they've been documented. I never take photos or record videos, nor any kind of audio. I don't want the atmosphere to be ruined by my comments. One of these days, after a night of exploring, I woke up wanting to scour the internet for more legends or places to discover. Time passed slowly; nothing stood out in the forums amidst the poorly plagiarized texts repeated over and over. Everything was too flat, lacking any spark to ignite anything in me. It felt artificial, even manufactured. All I could do was long for the stories from years ago, the ones that led me to do what I love so much.

That's why, after taking one last look at my trusted websites, an idea occurred to me: what if I created my own urban legend based on one of the nearby places? Perhaps that way I could satisfy this hunger for new thrills and at the same time stir things up online with something new. It's not that I'm a great writer; I consider myself rather mediocre at the craft. I have no awards and rarely participate in contests of any kind, at best reaching fourth place, and even then, I tied with someone else for that position.

Even so, I tried. I grabbed my laptop, put it in my backpack, along with some energy drinks, my pocketknife, and finally, my phone. With everything ready, I left my house for the nearest location; no car, motorcycle, or public transport, just my boots against the rocky path.

My heart was pounding in my chest. I was eager to create my own urban legend; a multitude of ideas were battling each other in my mind, vying to be the winner: murder, disappearance, an unsolved crime, paranormal activity, and if so, what kind. So many possibilities, and none of them seemed enough. I wanted to innovate, to leave my mark as a pioneer of this genre.

The journey was anything but short; when I arrived, it was dusk, and the owls were beginning to hoot. I quickly entered the abandoned radio station, greeted by the smell of old oil and grease, and the sound of the mechanical doors creaking as they struggled to open. I walked until I reached one of the control rooms; The place still had some natural light, but I knew it wouldn't last long. I put my backpack under one of the desks and took out my laptop.

Then I placed the energy drink cans next to it, away from my dominant hand, to avoid any accidents that might ruin my plans for the afternoon.

I took a breath and exhaled slowly. I could almost hear my heart climbing the walls of my throat, searching for fresh air. The sunlight had been completely delayed, and the soft chirping of crickets had settled around me. Everything felt calm. With another sigh, I reviewed several of my drafts, looking for ideas to get started, many of them inspired by places I had visited. But among them, I noticed some that didn't quite fit; they mentioned radio or television broadcasts, recounting paranormal events that occurred shortly after being broadcast, like macabre predictions, some of them coinciding with each other.

One of them was about a strange interference in the local weather forecast in the city of Toronto. It was a fairly normal day, a Monday morning like any other, with low temperatures and the sun streaming through the windows. Many people tuned their radios to listen to the local news on their way to work, on their way to drop their children off at school, or even on their way to the shops. At first, everyone had a little trouble tuning in to some stations, encountering unusual static or choppy transmissions that were simply background noise. After several attempts, everything seemed to be working; everyone who experienced the event said they enjoyed a good few minutes of entertaining commentary and their favorite music, with the occasional interruption of the transmission. But while the weather forecast was being given, talking about the possible rain that would occur throughout the week, an extra beep interrupted the transmission, catching the attention of all the listeners.

Then, a soft, professional voice seemed to be speaking to the presenters.

“Hello, good day to everyone,” the voice exclaimed with great joy, almost leaping from the speakers.

“Thank you for tuning in to Radio MGS, where every day we bless your ears with the freshest and juiciest news.”

At that moment, some people tried to turn off the station, but it appeared on every channel they selected, unable to escape the presenter. The power buttons remained motionless, untouchable.

“For you, our most loyal listeners.” He cleared his throat. “Today we bring you a very special story, especially for those of you who are fans of rail travel.”

The sound of a train whistle was heard, followed by more laughter.

“As many of you already know, our beloved Lower Bay station is known to both locals and foreigners who enjoy exploring such… unusual stories.” He cleared his throat again, his voice becoming somewhat raspier.

“And we love such daring people, don’t we, my dear audience?” Another round of applause followed, but their cheers quickly morphed into somewhat mechanical noises. “Today’s story focuses more on a very special woman, the nameless lady who haunts said eerie place.”

“El día de ayer recibimos un testimonio de uno de nuestros oyentes. Pero no se preocupen, no les narraré sus palabras como un loro, no como otros presentadores de pacotilla.” Una risa salió del presentador: “No, en su lugar les hemos traído a nuestra maravillosa oyente para que relate su historia.”

At that moment, the broadcast cut out, filled with static and strange words overlapping each other. Everyone who heard it reported feeling dizzy and nauseous, only to quickly hear soft music and applause.

“We’re back, my dear listeners, and with our guest today.” More loud applause followed: “Without further ado, I present to you Emma.”

After those words, the rapid, shallow breathing of a woman could be heard nearby.

“Hello, dear Emma, ​​are you ready to tell your story today?” the host asked, his expression cheerful.

Where am I? Who are you? The last thing I remember is typing on my computer when a similar beep interrupted his words, sending another wave of nausea through the listeners.

“Come on, come on, dear, you’re the one who asked us to invite you to our beloved station, MSG, don’t act so surprised.” A chorus of boos erupted. “Now, why don’t you tell us about today’s protagonist, the ghost woman of Lower Bay?”

Emma’s rapid breathing was heard again, along with her attempts to form coherent sentences.

“Come on, dear, the audience is waiting,” the presenter retorted, his voice dropping several tones until it was enveloped in static. “Don’t… make us… waste… any more… time…”

The sound of someone swallowing was heard.

“I-it was just an ordinary night, and I was on my way home on the subway, s-as usual, when I fell asleep on one of the benches.” She swallowed. “When I opened my eyes, I-I was in a completely different car than the one I'd gotten on; it was older, metallic, and smelled strange, and the lights were flickering like they were old.”

“So what was it that made you see our dear ghost lady, darling?” the presenter asked, his voice returning to its soft, gentle tone.

“N-not exactly. I walked around the car a bit to see where I was, but I didn't find anything s-that would give me any clues,” she replied, her voice a little shaky. “I kept seeing the same thing in every carriage; when I got back to my seat, I felt someone sit next to me. When I looked, I saw a beautiful, tall woman with pale skin, wearing a pretty dress and with long black hair. I don't remember what I talked to her about, but I do remember that she was always kind to me; she made me feel a little calmer at that moment.”

“I see. And what color dress was that woman wearing?” the presenter asked, coldly cutting off the woman's story. “That distinctive and striking color that many people remember.”

The place fell silent for a few seconds.

“H-her dress, i-it was…” Emma stammered, trying to find the words. In her inability to respond, murmurs emerged from the broadcast, along with a small laugh in the background. “Red! It was red, that's why she's called the Lady in Red.”

“Very well, Emma, ​​that's the color all the announcers say they saw.” The audience applauded again and cheered the woman's response. "There's just one small problem with your story."

The audience's laughter returned, growing louder and more frequent with each passing second, and above them, footsteps could be heard—dry, heavy, and patient. And in an instant, a snap silenced everything.

"Our dear lady in red has never been spotted going up to a lower level; she always stays on the entrance platform, waiting." That sweet voice had turned cold and sharp, and between her words, the creaking of wood could be heard. "So that leads me to believe you've lied to us, Emma." Those words brought even more static.

"I didn't..."—but she was immediately interrupted by the presenter.

"Liar, liar, LIAR, LIAR, LIAR, LIAR, LIAR, LIAR, LIAR!" The presenter repeated it over and over, his voice distorting until it sounded inhuman. “YOU HAVE TARNISHED THIS WORLD THIS WONDERFUL PROGRAM WITH YOUR DISGUSTING LIES.”

At that moment, a frantic, heart-wrenching scream ripped through the airwaves, tearing through the speakers, while the word “lie” echoed repeatedly in the background. The sounds of flesh and bone being torn apart followed, the dripping of blood against wooden planks, the rapid breathing of a woman before she screamed again.

For the third time, a strange interference interrupted the broadcast, and then there was only silence. Everyone who heard the transmission was too terrified to touch any buttons or dials on their radios.

“My sincerest apologies, dear listeners. It seems my producers forgot to thoroughly investigate our esteemed guest. I assure you this incident will not be repeated. Without further ado, I bid you farewell and wish you a good morning. Until next time.” The presenter explained, his expression calmer and almost emotionless.

With those words, all the witnesses to the strange phenomenon claim to have seen their radios return to the stations they were tuned to before the bizarre incident, vowing to themselves to uninstall their car radios and never use one again for the rest of their lives.

When I finished reading the draft, I checked the time on my computer and noticed it was almost time for me to go home. I sighed and leaned back in my chair. A chill ran down my spine, almost causing me to collapse, my teeth digging into the rusty control panel to my left. It was then that I realized I hadn't had any of my energy drinks while I'd been reading, and my laptop was about to die. Without thinking twice, I grabbed my things, stuffed them back into my backpack, and retraced my steps home. When I arrived, I put everything back in its place and I lay down on the bed staring at the ceiling.

In my mind, voices echoed, slowly forming a clearer sentence, and a smile spread across my face. I got out of bed and sat down at my laptop, opening one of my browser applications. With a mischievous grin, unable to resist, I spoke them aloud; I could no longer keep them confined to my mind: 

"Welcome back, dear listeners, to MGS Radio. Here, from our new headquarters, we'll bring you more of your favorite and most exciting news. Stay tuned; you won't want to miss anything we have in store for YOU. This is your wonderful and handsome host speaking."
