Scouts aren’t really a big thing here in Ireland. People in these parts are deeply superstitious, and only 1% of our ancient forests are still standing, and trust me, there’s a reason for that.



Geologically, our mountain chains are part of the exact same ancient rock system as the North American Appalachians. The land holds that exact same heavy, unsettling energy.



I was never a scout, but I’ve always loved survival and the outdoors. So, when I heard about an available spot for the two-night camp, I signed right up.



The campsite sat right on the edge of the dense, old woods. Beside the three-story recreation hall was an old stone well everyone avoided.

Fifty years ago, a girl vanished during a camp here. It took two days to find her body inside that five-meter-deep well. She died from blood loss, but without a single broken bone according to the coroners report. The most baffling part is that the stone lid covering the well was far too heavy for a child to move or replace, and the soil around it was gouged by boot prints that sank unnaturally deep into the dirt.



Around the campfire the first night, they told us the local legend. They claimed that on nights when the moon gives off a cold, bluish hue, if you sit by the fire at a specific time, you can see a faint shadow of the girl walking around the hall toward the well.



I played the macho kid who wasn't scared of anything, but the story rattled me.



While everyone was cleaning up, I hung back to get a better look behind the building. I could have sworn I saw a flicker of movement in the shadows, but before I could step closer, a scout captain barrelled into me. He nearly knocked me over, giving me an absolute earful about staying in sight of experienced members.



That night, after the floodlights died and our tent leader started snoring, I locked eyes with a girl my age and we burst out laughing.



Thump.



Something heavy smacked the outside canvas to shut us up. We froze.



A minute later, we can see a large shadow looming towards us. The smacks hit again despite our deadening silence. Convinced it was one of the older leaders playing a prank, I grabbed a spare metal tent pole and waited.

When the canvas bulged again, I swung with everything I had.



The pole tore straight through the fabric, connecting solidly with something dense outside with a sickening thud.



Chaos erupted. The tent was ripped open, waking half the camp. The experienced leaders gave me a proper kick in the arse and ordered me outside to make sure I hadn't just killed someone.



The tents were pitched about 100 meters across open grass from the rec hall, surrounded entirely by the perimeter of the dark forest.

 

I popped my head out of our tent.



No one was running. No footprints crossed the grass. All the other tents were zipped tight.



I sprinted the 100 meters to the rec hall to check on the captains. I found them sitting inside in front of the fire, sleeping bags pulled up to their waists, looking half-asleep. They couldn't possibly have made a 100-meter dash back across open ground in time.



They denied having anything to do with our tent, and ordered me back to bed. When they asked who was out with me, I panicked and lied, saying another experienced guy was in the toilet, giving myself a cover story to take a cheeky look at the well and the perimeter.



As I stepped back out into the dark, a whisper drifted from the treeline.

I could swear it was calling my name.

Hypnotized, I started stepping toward the dark trees. But a sudden instinct made me look back toward the rec hall.

Standing directly behind the building was a tall, motionless, man-shaped shadow.



The hair on the back of my neck started to rise with the paralysing feeling of hundreds of pins and needles. The feeling of impending doom made my feet feel as if they were anvils.



Then my gaze was drawn upward by that unexplainable feeling of being watched. Three stories high, on the pitched roof. Silhouetted against the pale blue moon, a figure stood completely still, staring down at me.

I screamed and sprinted for the hall. 



In my stunned state, I foolishly began running directly beneath the figure on the roof because I was too terrified to head for the tree line.



Mid-stride, on the same flat path I had walked just sixty seconds prior, my boot snagged into a deep hole.



Everything went dark, and in that moment I thought… “well”, this is it…

I wasn’t in the well thankfully, I had slammed face-first into the freezing mud.



Before I could think, I scrambled up, and burst through the hall doors hysterically.



The captains, now three-quarters of the way into their sleeping bags, jumped up in a panic. One ran outside while the other dialled the police.



An hour of frantic searching with flashlights and dogs followed. They found nothing on the roof, and nothing in the trees. I officially earned the title of "the boy who cried wolf," and the entire camp was forced to sleep inside the hall. 



I know what I saw.



I didn’t make any friends that weekend.



The next morning, as we gathered our gear near the carpark by the well, I located the exact spot where I had tripped.

It wasn't a natural dip in the ground.

It was a boot print, identical in shape to a standard boot, but crushed so violently deep into the hard earth that the sole pattern had dissolved into the soil.



If you ever find yourself camping near those woods in Ireland, listen to the leaders. 

Stay in sight.



And if you hear your name whispered from the treeline.

Run !
