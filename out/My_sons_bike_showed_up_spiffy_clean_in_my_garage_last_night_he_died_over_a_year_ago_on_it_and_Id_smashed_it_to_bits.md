The whole thing didn’t even last a full minute. I was preparing dinner and my boys were playing outside. I could see and hear them through the small kitchen window.

They were tossing a baseball when I could hear a neighborhood kid walk up. I didn’t recognize the voice but it sounded like the boys knew him.  
First, I heard my oldest raise his voice in a panic. Then, my youngest cried out my name. I looked out and saw both of them disappear around the corner, my oldest on his bike and my youngest on foot.  
We’re on the second floor so I sprinted outside and down the stairs. By the time I rounded the corner it was already done.

My youngest had fallen and I scooped him up unconsciously. With him in my arms I fell to my knees. I could see my neighbor, a police officer, at the side of my oldest.

I don’t even like to think about it but somehow writing is cathartic. I’m not going to describe anything yet, just know I basically blacked out from grief. Making sure my youngest was taken care of was the only thing that saved me from a proper meltdown right there.

Here’s what you do need to know. Another boy had talked his way into my youngest offering up his bike for a spin. Pretty quickly my oldest realized the kid had his eye on stealing the bike.

They took off on bikes, my youngest running behind.

My downstairs neighbor is a lovely woman and grandmother. Her family has been trying to take away the keys for a few months now, unsuccessfully. She insisted on one trip a week to the supermarket. The only really dangerous moment was a blind spot as she backed out of her steep driveway.

She never saw him. I’m told he died instantly.  
So that’s what I tell others. And myself.  
But I don’t know for sure. I don’t know much of the days following either. My youngest went to stay with my sister. My ex was abroad and making their way home.

Somehow, months later, I wandered into a Church near where I live and sat in the back pew. Empty, quiet and mostly dark it was just a place to rest my mind.

The week had been a non stop assault on my senses and emotions. I hadn’t stopped to feel much of anything yet. I definitely hadn’t opened up to anyone about how crushed I was, how broken.  
I didn’t see anyone that night, I heard someone cleaning I thought but didn’t see them. The next night I met the Janitor.

For three nights we spoke after my quiet moments. The second night I broke down, but somehow by the third night I felt like I was actually going to make it through this in one piece. Our conversations are another story but it wasn’t just the words that had healing power, there was something else about him. He had tapped into some wavelength of my suffering and seemed to be able to make it still.

Or at least quiet down.

From the beginning I was insanely curious as to what subliminal powers this church janitor was hiding. After I spoke to the Priest on night four I just felt insane. 

Again.

The Church doesn’t employ a Janitor.
