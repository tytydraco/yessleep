I told myself it was a glitch. The world let the matrix glitch accidentally showing its under belly of the spirits and the dead souls who walked beyond us.

A prank, it had to be.

A mistake, because I’m not crazy.

Anything besides what I knew it to be.

Then the phone stopped ringing.

A message appeared.

**1 NEW VOICEMAIL.**

I don't know why I listened to it.

I wish I hadn't.

At first, there was only static.

Then the sound of water dripping.

Then footsteps.

Then Frank's voice.

"Hey, Lyla."

He sounded perfectly normal.

Warm.

Friendly.

Almost amused.

"I found something out."

A pause.

"I know where I am now."

I held my breath.

"I know what the best part of Hell is."

Another pause.

Then he laughed.

"You."

The voicemail ended.

I haven't listened to it again.

I haven't told anyone.

And I haven't slept.

Because I know what I heard.

I know what he said.

And I know that if Frank is telling the truth, then I have a problem.

A very big problem.

Because I remember the little girl.

I remember the woman with the scar.

I remember the man in the field.

And I remember the house, the blue door, the dog sleeping by the fireplace.

I remember standing at the window and watching someone walk toward the house.

I remembered smiling, opening the door, telling them they could stay as long as they wanted.

Because this was the best part.

This was the part where nobody hurt you.

The part where nobody left you.

The part where you could finally rest and be whole.

The part where you could forget.

I remember all of it.

I just don't remember when I started to.

And that is what terrifies me most.

Not that Frank is dead.

Not that he might be trapped in Hell.

Not even that Hell might be real.

It's the possibility that I've been there before.

That I've been there so many times that I've forgotten the difference between a memory and a dream.

That every life I've lived has been leading me back to the same place.

That somewhere, in some other version of myself, I'm standing in a hallway beneath a flickering light, listening to water drip onto the concrete.

That somewhere, a little girl is holding my hand.

That somewhere, a man with my face is standing at the end of a field, waving me home.

And that somewhere, behind a blue door, a voice is waiting to tell me that everything is going to be okay.

I don't know if Heaven and Hell are real.

I don't know if we live once or a thousand times.

I don't know if time is a line, a circle, or a hallway full of doors.

Are we all really connected as one? All of the people in the world are each other. There is no separation between one another, because consciousness is smaller portions of our soul split into smaller bits to experience every ounce of life that is possible.

But I know this.

If you ever find yourself somewhere familiar after you die, somewhere warm and beautiful, somewhere that feels like home, don't trust it.

If you see someone you love standing in a window, don't wave back.

If you hear a voice calling your name, don't answer. Especially if their eyes are black.

And if you find a blue door at the edge of a field, no matter how tired you are, no matter how badly you want to rest, no matter how much you miss the people waiting on the other side...

Don't open it.

Because the best part of Hell is the part that makes you forget you're there.

And the worst part?

The worst part is when you remember.
