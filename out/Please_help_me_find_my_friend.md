Have you been having those dreams again?” He’s apparently been dead since sophomore year of high school, which can’t be the case because the Polaroid of our high school graduation, where we’re posing by each other, is right on my desk, his left  eye staring right at me (he was winking for some reason). To make things more complicated, people are recalling the same events, but this time the stories feature me, perhaps someone else, but not him. And get this, he has a girlfriend, right? Minute he goes missing, I learn that she’s my fiance. Apparently in high school, my friend and her were dating while she was just a friend, but his death drew us together. A real Spider-Man situation. Thankfully we had decided to “cease for now and resume on our wedding night” because, well….I feel guilty as hell kissing her as is.

I decided to play along after it sunk in that he was not returning, because the last thing I need is to be institutionalized.

Sorry for rambling so much, shit has been extraordinarily weird and I only have the faintest idea of what’s going on. So we became friends in elementary school and rode bikes and played video games, mostly. Sometime around freshman year, we decided that hiking would be fun, and at the time, it was. We would hike into the wilderness, at which point it stopped being an uphill climb and became a simple stroll again. It was around this point that we would turn around, simply because I had asked for us to.   
To put it simply, forests scare the hell out of me, regardless of whether they have predators or not. There aren’t any in this state, but there are mighty trees that adapted to the unyielding and unforgiving soil of the mountains, their very existence almost a spite against God Himself, creeks and pockets of water on the trails that are 34 degrees regardless of the outside temperature, and warnings by other experienced hikers to stick together and carry loads of water because people have gotten lost and/or gone missing.

This causes me to respect the forests with utmost seriousness….and also to be terrified of them. Following the beaten pathways will get you there and back just fine, but God help you if you’re adventurous. 

The darkness is those mountainous trees are absolute. Even during the day, the trees will block out enough sunlight to mistake it for evening. By nightfall, your eyes will never adjust to the dark. The moon and stars hanging above serve as ornaments rather than a true light source. 

You can call someone that you lost and it’ll echo for miles, but they’ll never hear it. You may hear a voice in the middle of your hike telling you “don’t go left” only to find yourself either alone or your companion thinking your crazy….best case scenario. At worst they tell you they heard it too. 

There was one evening we went hiking. This time, he wanted to go into the forest. We were both in college at this point. I explained to him my superstitious fears, but he insisted. I told him I would wait by the entrance, to which he shrugged and went “I’ll be back in ten minutes.” 

Twenty minutes passed and I tried calling him. Straight to voicemail. Becoming more fearful that he had hurt himself than of any ghosts, I went into the forest.

I was initially deceived by the trees. This was no forest at all. It was a meadow. I scanned the area, noticing how quickly it was getting dark and realizing that I had no flashlight except for my shitty phone (checked the battery percentage. It was at 80%, thankfully). I shouted his name. No response. I walked through the grass and the flowers. There were plenty of flowers that grew here that supposedly didn’t grow native to our state. My stomach twisted in a knot at the implication. 

But the strangest flowers that I saw growing wildly that I knew for certain our climate couldn’t support was the corpse flower and the black baccara. Although the black baccara usually has a tint of redness, in the twilight, they looked pure black. I called out his name once more, to no avail. 

Right when I was about to turn around, I felt a tug at my shirt. I turned around and saw a girl about my age, wearing clothes that were outdated by a hundred years. A cosplayer has gone hiking? Whatever, that wasn’t the strange part. It was the fact she was approximately ten meters away. Shaking my head of these odd signs, I ran up to her. I got a better look at her. For whatever reason, despite the disappearance of sunlight and the encroaching darkness, I could see her clearly. She had brunette hair that almost looked black in color and brown eyes, with incredibly pale skin, skin as pale as mine. I guess she went outdoors as much as I did these days.

“Uh, have you seen my friend anywhere?” I asked stupidly. 

She shook her head sadly. She then told me: “go back from whence you came. You don’t know how to handle what’s out here.” 

She didn’t have to tell me twice. I nodded, then did as she said. I guess I was filing a missing person’s report. I hiked back down the trail, sighing in relief when I saw civilization peaking behind the trees. I walked there, stepping past said trees….

Then I was back in the Meadows. I was too confused to even panic. It was completely dark now. I pulled out my phone and turned on the flashlight to look around. The familiar flowers and the scent of death emitting from the corpse flowers (I hoped). No sign of the cosplayer anywhere. She must’ve gone back as well. 

Not knowing what else to do, I hiked back down again, saw civilization peaking behind, car lights and all, and made a run for it, hoping to outrun whatever was keeping me here.

Then I was back in the meadows. Despite my phone battery being high, it fucking died on me right at this moment. Reality began to set in and I started hyperventilating. I vomitted the contents of my stomach, then checked my bag for remaining supplies. Four water bottles and a whole bag of trail mix. Better than nothing. 

I tried thinking of a rational explanation, none came. No matter how I looked at things, my worst fears came true, and I could no longer doubt my superstitions anymore. My friend suddenly was the last thing on my mind as saving my own skin took priority. 

That’s when I heard footsteps approaching me. I hoped desperately it was policemen, which is uncharacteristic, because I never hope for them in fear they may punish me for possession. (I usually carried a joint in my cigarette holder). Yet I didn’t see light. Policemen usually carry flashlights, right?

I fell into despair as whatever was coming after me was gonna have me no matter what I did. I looked up. Illuminated by the moonlight (since when did the clouds part?) and giving me a good ol’ sigh of relief was that same girl. This time, she was only a foot away from me. I wanted to hold her, but refrained. 

“You’re stuck here, too?”

Stuck here too?

“You’re strong.” I said dumbly.

“I know.” She said. Must’ve been a farmer’s daughter or something. There’s a few of those in my college, I see them moving furniture sometimes.

“I’ve been trying to figure that out for a month.”

“You’ve been stuck here for a month?”

“You don’t need to worry about supplies up here, at this place, we feel neither hunger, thirst, nor even fatigue.” She said matter-of-factly.

“I see. Except I feel all of those things currently.”  
“Give it five minutes.”

“Weird.”

So we set off. The flowers underneath our feet wouldn’t crunch and would instead spring back as if nothing disturbed them at all. The meadows were now completely illuminated by the full moon, which looked abnormally large in the sky and lit our path a little too perfectly.   
“So uh, what school do you go to?”  
“I haven’t gone since high school.”  
“I see. I take it you graduated?”  
“A couple years ago.”  
“Oh neat.” The conversation died there. Silently, we made our way, until we came across trees. One of them had fallen long ago, with sprouts growing from the stump. I looked down and saw footsteps leading into the dark passageway. That gave me hope, but also inspired anxiety. Was I gonna face more darkness?  
“Check your phone again.” She said, almost commandingly.   
I did as she was told. It was back on! At 60%! With renewed hope, I turned the flashlight back on, and we went passed the trees. 

Despite my flashlight, the darkness around us was oppressive. I could hardly see five feet in front of me. I looked over towards her. She looked as bright as ever. 

“Oh lord, what have I done….” I heard my friend’s voice echo. I frantically searched for the origin, calling his name out once again. No answer. I understood though I shouldn’t have at that moment that he hadn’t called out just now, but whatever it was simply had echoed back what he had said hours earlier.

Shaking uncontrollably, my mind losing its grip on sanity, I regressed back to childhood and reached out for a hand to hold onto. I clasped onto one, I looked down to see whose I was holding onto. “Let’s get out of this cursed place.”

Going to the meadows won’t save you.
