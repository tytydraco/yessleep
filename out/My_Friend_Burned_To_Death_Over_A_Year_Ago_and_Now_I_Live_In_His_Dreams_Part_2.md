I should have never opened that damn book.

I should have just tossed it in the trash, forgotten about it, or burnt it so that no one else could make the mistake I did of reading it.

But my curiosity got the better of me, I wanted to know more. So when I went to bed that night I drifted off thinking about what my friend wrote, about that place, The Dreary.

Then, I woke up in my bed.

But it wasn’t my bed, and I wasn’t in my home. I awoke in The Dreary. The smell of Smoke and Frost violated my senses, like the way the scent of diesel would seem to stick to the cold autumn air. 

I peered out my window and watched the slow and gentle fall of a mixture of Ash and Snow. The world felt still with no winds to whip through the empty streets. I remember looking for something in particular, and I remember something looking for me. 

Attempting to exit my home was an endeavor on its own. The door had been welded shut by the cold, or something else, and try as I did I just couldn’t open the door. I started bashing at the door with my feet, desperately trying to kick it open. I felt such a strong compulsion, a strong desire, a need to go outside into this world. But for what?

Eventually my tenacity proved to be too much for the barrier as it gave way with a thunderous crash. As my foot made contact the thin barrier of gray powder that coated the floor outside I felt instant, and immense, fear as a deafening crunch echoed across the land, far louder than that of the door I’d broke through but a moment ago. I felt as if I had just broken into someone’s house, had just bashed in a window and made myself at home, as if something knew I wasn’t supposed to be here. 

As if to answer directly to my worries, the howling began. An agonized, painful, a deliriously hypnotizing chorus of wailing and howling washed over the snow from beyond as a strong breeze crashed into me. Now the road and the skies and the ground were alive with whirling flurries and frigid gales whose blades of frost cut deep into my body, far deeper than any knife, wounding my very soul. I was cold, freezing, no, something far more frigid and agonizing than that. It was as if the warmth of life I held within me was being leached away, cut off, or siphoned by the air as it moved around me. 

I fell to my knees, the burning cold of the fresh snow searing my palms as they dug in to the thin layer beneath me. My breath was hot, the puffs of vapor emerging from my mouth bordered on smoke, as cold as I was, I was burning up inside; I felt none of it. 

Then, like an angel from the heavens themselves, or like an omen for what was to come, a shadow loomed over me, fuzzy and indiscernable in the haze of this place. 

The wind grew stronger, the choir of agony crescendoed, and I so desperately wanted to join them. As I craned my head upward, ready to call out to the void just beyond the fog, I saw him.

My friend.

He stood before me, his face partially obscured by a massive and torn golden cloak that clung to him like plastic in a breeze. It showed no emotion, but his eyes, his eyes were full of a focused rage I had never known in him, nor seen in any person in my life.

“Aiden?” I called out to him. 

He flinched at the sound of his name.

“Fuck me,” he uttered in astonishment, “it really is you, Alex.”

For a moment we stood in eachother’s gaze. The world around us held its breath as we held ours, fearing that even the slightest movement might shatter this dream and whatever solid ground we may have finally found within it.

“Aiden- I can’t-“

Before I could finish my sentence he punched me straight in the jaw.

“What the fuck are you doing here!” He shouted. His panicked, shrill voice betrayed fear more than anger. 

“What am I-“ I started, stumbling back up to my knees, “you’re dead! The fuck are you doing here? And the fuck was that for?”

I held my jaw as I waited for his response. To my surprise, I felt no pain, save for the slow erosion of my soul from these freezing winds.

“Yeah, I’m aware,” he replied as he extended his hand, “thats why I’m a bit shocked to see you here.”

“Am I dead too?”

“I fuckin hope not, cuz this isn’t hell,” he snarked with a quick chuckle.

I laughed after giving him a gentle shove. For a moment, a brief moment, it felt like old times again. Before us, before the incident, before this.

“So then what are you doing here?” I jabbed back. He didn’t laugh.

“I… Uh…” 

He was searching his brain for an excuse, a lie. He was always terrible at lying to me.

“Well I’m dead so I can go to any fancy spirit world I want to.”

“Is that why you sent me that book? So I could come and visit you?”

For how gray and colorless this place was, I swear I saw his face grow even paler. 

“What book?”

“You know, the black one? Your dream journal?”

Immediately after the words left my mouth Aiden had pounced on top of me, pinning me against the ground, my back pressing into the cold layer of snow on the ground. I felt the icy melt seep into and soak my clothes as it burned its way into my body. 

“Describe that book to me. Exactly. What was it like? A black book? Was it made of leather?”

His face was close, dangerously close. I could see the details of his face that hid behind the golden cloak. He looked older, not by much, but worn down. His eyes had bags under them that told me he hadn’t been sleeping well again, if he’d still been alive, I’d have given him a lecture about proper sleep. His eyes silently and gently shook within his skull, his pupils were wide with a rabid fear I’d only seen in an abandoned dog. The heat of his breath caressed my face as I became disoriented, more than dreams usually do. 

“Wha- I… Yes it was black, cover was made of leather, I dont know, it was totally blank other than what was written inside it-“

“What. About. The. Spine.”

“What about it?”

I was afraid, afraid that the wrong answer would set him off again. As bad as Aiden spiraled I’d never seen him like this. Even when he drank heavy after the first few weeks, even when he’d-

“Did it have anything on it? A logo? Symbol? A sigil?”

At the time I couldn’t much recall the details of the book beyond that basic description. A fine detail like a sigil on the spine was just not something I could have remembered at the moment. 

“I don’t remember,” I replied sheepishly, cowering under Aiden as I had done many times before. 

“Listen to me and listen good,” he started, his voice was cold, colder than the winds that were eating away at me, “when you wake up, destroy that book. Burn it. Shred it. Throw it into the fucking sun if you can and forget about it.”

“But I-“

“And forget about me, never ever think about me ever again. I am dead. I burned to death over a year ago. I screamed in agony as my skin bubbled and melted off of me. I watched as my muscles charred and snapped in the flames. My soul watched as everything I was become nothing more than dust and ash.”

I felt tears welling up in my eyes. His face was so close, he was so close, so real, I wanted to reach out and touch him. I wanted to hold him tight to me and bury myself in his chest and never let him go again. No matter what. 

“Please Alex, forget about me.”

He then reached into is back pocket and brandished something dark.

“This should wake you up.”

For a brief moment I caught the glint of silver and the muted finish of polymer plastic.

A loud crack woke me from my slumber, which was briefly followed by the worst migraine I’d ever had. 

The fucker shot me, in a dream.

To be honest, I would have discarded all of that, all of this as some weird dream. But out of pure curiosity, pure stupid arrogance, I opened the book.

On the page where once I saw Aiden’s handwriting, I now saw the unmistakably illegible script of my own handwriting. It detailed the very dream I just had, and I know for a fucking fact I didn’t write it.

My mind is screaming at me, begging me to destroy the book like Aiden said I should, and I want to, but I can’t. Not anymore. This book, these dreams, they’re all I have left of him, all I have left of us. Even without that, I feel the ethereal winds tearing away at me whenever this book isn’t in my immediate possession. No matter how many clothes I wear or how hot the shower is I feel myself freezing solid from the inside out. Like a brain freeze but for the whole body. 

Like it or not, I’m stuck with this book for now. I should probably call someone, maybe a priest, but I think this was something meant for me and me alone to solve, or at least to experience until the end. After all, it is a book, right? Maybe what I need to do is just see it through to the end.

Though, maybe just as easily I’m gonna get myself killed. Maybe those things howling with the wind want to kill me and eat me for sustenance. Though Aiden could kill me in the dream and I woke up mostly fine.

Speaking of, theres something about what he said that made me curious. So i looked it up to confirm. I knew he died in a house fire, and I know burning is a horrible way to go, but not everything gets destroyed in a fire. Human bones can survive fires pretty well, and by all accounts, they should have for his apartment’s fire. I mean, they identified him off of dental records. My point for saying this is that, I knew his bones were intact. But he doesn’t. Matter of fact, that whole story he told sounded just like his lies, over exaggerating certain points and reiterating them to try and convince me. Maybe I just knew him so well I dreamt up a convincing copy of him.

Or maybe it actually was him, maybe it was his ghost and now he lives on in this book, like a curse, and maybe I’m supposed to free him. 

Whatever the case may be, I need to see this through. For him, for myself.
