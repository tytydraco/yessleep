Another business trip, another long haul. Sometimes I think I hate my job. Deciding to take a smoke break, I pulled over near a small residential area with a couple of houses. There are people around, so it's safe.  
   
Probably.  
   
Stepping outside, I pulled a lighter from my pocket. A small tongue of flame illuminated my face. In the darkness, it looked like a tiny star. Bringing the cigarette to the flickering flame, I watched the tip catch. Taking a drag, I exhaled a wisp of gray smoke into the cool night air, watching it slowly dissolve. Finally relaxing just a little, I heard... Footsteps? They were getting closer. Who the hell needs something from me this late at night? With an annoyed sigh, I turned around. Standing behind me was a guy in his early twenties, drilling me with an anxious and slightly tense stare.  
   
"Leave this place if you don't want any trouble."  
   
I raised an eyebrow. What kind of threat is that? Deliberately blowing smoke right into his face, I threw my half-smoked cigarette on the ground and put it out with my boot.  
   
"Are you threatening me? What could I have possibly done to you already?"  
   
The guy winced slightly and crossed his arms over his chest, continuing to stare at me sternly.  
   
"No, I'm not threatening. More like warning."  
   
He looked around, lowering his voice to a whisper.  
   
"It's not safe in this village. Recently, some psycho has been operating around here, leaving human remains on people's doorsteps. For example, yesterday a family found a human hand on their porch, with a note next to it that had a smile drawn on it in blood. And it's not an isolated case. Some 'Bloody Martin' leaves these 'gifts' almost daily. The police don't want to get their hands dirty, so they keep postponing their visits under various pretexts. So the locals are extremely hostile towards newcomers, fearing this lunatic. I understand it sounds crazy and you have no reason to trust me, but..."  
   
I cut him off. It really did sound crazy. But this young guy spoke so seriously about it that even without proof, I wanted to believe him.  
   
"Alright, don't make excuses. I get it. So you're not a local, then?"  
   
The guy seemed to calm down a bit, realizing I wasn't going to accuse him of lying. He let out a sigh of relief.  
   
"Yeah, I was just passing through. My car broke down like in a cheap horror movie, and I had to work in some old lady's garden in exchange for a mattress on the floor. And she has dementia, so from time to time I have to explain who I am on top of everything else. Now I just stand on the side of the road all day after work, trying to hitch a ride."  
   
On that last sentence, the guy deliberately put on a maximally pitiful tone and a miserable expression.  
Getting the hint, I rubbed my face, realizing I was now going to have to drag another soul along with me. Well, at least I'd have someone to talk to on the road.  
   
"Alright, get in. But keep in mind, the road is going to be long. Very long. Until I reach my destination, you're stuck with me. Hurry up. Before I change my mind."  
   
The guy's face instantly changed, giving me a grateful smile as he quickly climbed into the truck. I climbed in after him. A few moments later, we were moving. Driving along what seemed like an endless highway, the guy chattered away without stopping. About everything. Starting from stories about that old lady, ending with updates in his favorite game. I just silently nodded from time to time, or gave a positive grunt in response to something. His voice was already ringing in my ears, how has his tongue not dried up yet? Oh, how I didn't appreciate the silence before he showed up. But at the same time, his meaningless conversations didn't make me feel like something creepy was about to jump out onto the road. My "favorite" paranoia just had no opportunity to show itself. His rambling threw it all off. Well, on the one hand, that's good.  
   
That's how I, or rather we, spent the next two days.  
   
Another night. The headlights illuminated the road ahead of me. On the sides, a dark, dense, seemingly pine forest. Kai, that was the guy's name, had slept all day, so at night he was chattering away again without stopping, taking breaks to eat my sandwich. Ugh, what a brat!  
   
Suddenly, the sharp screech of brakes.  
   
Right out of the forest, a person jumped onto the road, looking straight at me. In his hands was... A kitchen knife, its gleam reflecting in the headlights. Kai stared at him in fear.  
   
"That's... That's him. That's Martin. Bloody Martin."  
   
My heart skipped a beat. Was it really true? And what should I do now? In a panic, I couldn't think of anything better than to drive around him. But the road is narrow... Alright, there are no other options. Shifting back a little and moving left, the truck went off the road, and the front turned towards the forest.  
   
"Damn... We're stuck..."  
   
I needed to call a tow truck; I couldn't get out of this myself. I called. They'd only be there in hours. That meant being stuck in this middle of nowhere for a whole day, alone with some maniac. Just my luck. Martin had disappeared from sight in the meantime. Well, nothing to do. I'll lock the truck, and we'll go to sleep. After telling Kai about my idea, he raised an eyebrow, looking at me incredulously.  
   
"Are you sure sleeping in the middle of the forest with a maniac outside is a good idea?"  
   
I just snorted.  
   
"We're locked in anyway. Don't be a pussy, it'll be fine."  
   
That night I tossed and turned for a long time, looking out the window. Sometimes it seemed to me that someone was looming out there, looking straight at me.  
   
Or maybe they weren't?  
   
I slept in until ten in the morning. But when I woke up, I didn't find Kai. Maybe he went outside to get some air? After all, the maniac doesn't operate during the day. I decided to go out and check on him. And check the cargo while I was at it.  
   
Kai wasn't by the truck either. Well, maybe he went to the bathroom? Alright, now for the check. Opening the compartment with the barrels, I noticed one was slightly ajar. Must have opened during the hard braking. I walked over to see if everything inside was intact. But opening the barrel, I almost passed out from what I saw.  
   
Inside lay a disemboweled body floating in its own blood.  
   
I instinctively jumped back from the barrel. What a surprise. But... Where did all this come from? So this Martin actually managed to get inside the truck somehow. Then why didn't he touch me? Strange. Alright, but the question of where Kai was was still open. Closing the compartment, I returned to the cab. My throat was so dry from what I saw, I needed some water. Opening the mini-fridge for drinks, I saw...  
   
Kai's head lying on the shelf, with a note next to it that had a bloody smile drawn on it.  
   
Shivers ran down my spine, my pupils dilated. What the hell?! And what am I supposed to do with all this now? My heart was still pounding wildly, my hand reached for the phone. I needed to call the police before he got to me. And as luck would have it, I suddenly lost service. I had to wait for the tow truck. Then, I heard footsteps behind me. I swallowed, guessing who it was. He put the blade of the knife to my neck, pressing down slightly. I froze in fear. My brain frantically tried to figure out what to do, but nothing came to mind. Then, Martin started muttering something under his breath. I thought he was just going to stay silent. But no, he spoke.  
   
"You shouldn't have picked up that boy. All the residents of that village have a chip implanted in them, tracking their location in case anyone tries to escape. Smart, right? I know, you don't have to answer. But if you helped one of them, it means you're a resident of my village now."  
   
Pressing the blade harder, I felt a drop of warm blood roll down my neck. A little more and I was done for. And he continued his story:  
   
"That was your biggest mistake, and it cost you your life."  
   
And my vision went dark, my body went limp, my head fell onto the steering wheel.  
   
"In long trips, it's better to ride alone."
