It was a *long* shift at the plant. 

We clocked in at nine and didn’t get out until around seven. My back was killing me. The bright lights had been chewing on my retinas since lunch.

Pedro and I walked home, side by side, like we had since we were kids. He was explaining the alien races of *Stargate* again for the hundredth time. I told him, for the hundredth time, that wasn’t the kind of sci-fi I was into. He rolled his eyes and asked where my sense of whimsy had gone.

I hit my pen. The wax tasted burnt. I grimaced.

“You down to stop at Paul’s for smokes?” I asked.

Pedro squinted those beady eyes of his. “God, no. Isn’t he *super* racist?”

“No?” 

Well. I thought about it. My upper lip folded and I bit the inside of my cheek. 

“Toward Guatemalans. Not *us* if that makes you feel any better.”

“It does not, homie.”

We kept walking. Up ahead was Boot’s corner.

He’d been sleeping on that same black futon since the ‘90s. Nobody knew his real name. We just called him Boot because during rainstorms, he’d pull one of his old work boots over his splotchy head like a protective covering. 

He used to work at the university, or at least that’s what the older guys at the plant say. These days he existed on the corner with long, rambling cardboard signs and a rusty lockbox he never let out of his sight.

*“PLEASE HELP I AM MISSING MY CICERO GOD BLESS”*

Tonight he was all curled up, both arms wrapped around the box. He never let it out of his sight. Most of the time he thought it would quite literally grow legs and crawl away. For once, though, he seemed peaceful. 

I tried to steer the conversation back toward weed.

“We can be in and out. You don’t even have to talk to his pasty ass.”

Boot snorted as we passed. One leg kicked. His nose twitched. A dreaming dog.

Pedro shook his head. I kept pushing.

“He’s right down the block. He’ll smoke us up and we can veg and watch *Saint Seiya*. We can probs finish it tonight, even.”

Pedro sighed, then held out his fist. We bumped. I turned us down Paul’s street.

Under the entryway, a large cobweb stretched from the porch light to the railing. A fat, spindly-legged spider was planted in its center, working on a twitching fly. 

My whole body recoiled. I’ve never liked those fucking things. I shrunk, stepped wide around it. Felt my skin crawl just looking at the thing. Nearly split my knuckles knocking on the door.

“Come in!” sang a hoarse voice. 

Paul was inside, shirtless on the couch. His beer gut spilled over the waistband of his pajama pants. A three-foot bong sat on the coffee table, a glass obelisk. The whole living room reeked like heaven.

“What’s happening, Salamander? Who’s your friend?”

“This is Pedro. I’ve told you about him.”

Paul’s bearded face lit up. “*This* is Pedro?!”

Pedro gave a shy wave.

“Well get in here, you son of a bitch! Catch a buzz!” Paul waved him over, the flab on his arms jiggling. 

We sat. Paul packed me an eighth in a glass jar while we talked about work. Pedro stayed hunched and quiet at first. A few hits loosened him up enough to get into a rant about the Goa’uld. 

After a bit of talking, Paul put on *Requiem for a Dream*. We’d zoned out hard enough that we forgot about our earlier plans. 

Somewhere in the quieter stretch of the movie we heard it.

*Screaming.*

At first we thought it was the dreary-ass film. We were stoned and immersed. 

The screaming kept going between scenes, though. It sounded a bit too real.

Paul paused the movie, muttering a string of *unfathomable* slurs, heading for the door. His hand was on the grip of a pistol he never bothered to conceal. Pedro and I followed. *Carefully*.

Boot was thrashing on his futon three houses down. His spine arched hard enough that the metal frame left gashes in the cheap sidewalk.

Paul holstered the gun and started jogging as fast as his old legs would take him. We caught up just as Boot’s eyes snapped open, bloodshot, sickly.

Pedro and I grabbed his arms to keep him from cracking his own head open. The lockbox lay open beside the futon. I hadn’t seen him open it.

“It’s out,” Boot gasped. Thick spit flecked his lips. “It’s out! Someone let it out!”

He kept chanting it, finding the strength to throw us off of him. He grabbed both of my shoulders, and rolled off the futon. I hit the concrete, wind knocked from my lungs. 

Boot’s face was inches from mine. Foam hit my cheeks while he tried to force words out. Something orange and jointed shot from between his lips. Then another. Then a whole, fully-grown camel spider shoved its way out of his mouth and landed on my face.

It bit my eyelid.

I screamed and squeezed my eyes shut, but I could still feel the legs. The sheer *weight* of it. The damn thing moved around like it knew *exactly* what it was doing. More followed suit.

They poured out of Boot’s every orifice. Ears. Nose. The corners of his eyes, too, until the eyes themselves popped free from the pressure. They chewed through the softer parts of his face and kept falling in tandem with facial gore. I felt them on my neck, my collarbone, the skin of my scalp. A few tried to dig. I felt tiny mouths testing my jugular and the swollen lid of my eye.

Paul opened fire. Six sharp cracks. The body on top of me jerked. Pedro finally managed to shove what was left of Boot away.

The swarm finished what they started in under a minute. The old man was mostly gone—clothes and bloody scrap atop a spreading red stain. The spiders scattered in every direction like someone had kicked their nest, slinking into the shade.

I made it to my knees and vomited until nothing came up but stringy spit. My face felt tight. Tight and hot. My eyelid was already puffed closed. My face was swollen all over. 

Pedro picked up the lockbox. He stared into it for a long second, then turned it so we could see.

Inside the lid, written in pink marker on a strip of masking tape, was a name.

***Cicero.***

I called off work for tomorrow. Pedro and I are going to the campus library in the afternoon. 

We’re going to dig up whatever we can about Boot and his tenure as a professor. Figuring out what he was trying to accomplish feels like the least we could do for him.

I’ll update when we know more.
