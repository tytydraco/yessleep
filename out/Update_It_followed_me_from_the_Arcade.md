Part 1 - Something in the Arcade: [https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1tmjipb/something\_in\_the\_arcade/](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1tmjipb/something_in_the_arcade/)

I didn't sleep; I just sat on my bed with every light in the apartment on, staring at the door and listening. The humming stopped around 3 AM, which should have made me feel better, but it didn't, because I kept waiting for it to start again. I called out sick the next day, told my manager I had food poisoning, and he said to take the day off. I hung up and sat there in my apartment, and the quiet felt wrong.

By that night, I'd almost talked myself into believing I imagined the whole thing, no sleep, stress, a dark building, your brain fills in the blanks with whatever scares you most. I read that's a real thing, and I almost believed it, almost, until my phone buzzed on the nightstand.

It was a text from myself. My own number.

"Hey buddy, we're closed. Where are your parents?"  
  
I dropped the phone, and my hands were shaking too badly to pick it back up right away, and when I finally did, three more messages were sitting there, all sent one second apart.  
  
"turn around"

"turn around"

"turn around"

I threw the phone at the wall without even deciding to; I just watched it happen, and then I sat there breathing like I'd sprinted a mile. I don't really remember falling asleep, because the next thing I knew, it was morning, the sun coming through the blinds, and my phone was sitting on the floor where I'd thrown it, screen cracked in one corner. When I picked it up and went looking for the messages, they were just gone, no notification, nothing in my call log either, as if none of it had ever come through. For a second, that was almost worse than the messages themselves, because now I couldn't even prove to myself it happened.

I quit the arcade that morning. Called my manager and told him I found something else, and he didn't push it, and I told myself that settled it, whatever it was, real or not, I never had to go back there again.  
  
I decided to concentrate on my schoolwork and forget about it, telling myself that I'd quit the arcade, and things would get better. I had a project due, and the lab on campus had the equipment I needed, so I decided to go in early afternoon, get a few hours in while the building was full of people, and be home well before dark. A school in broad daylight is about as safe as anywhere gets, so I packed my bag and drove over, not thinking twice about it.

The lab was busy when I got there, a handful of other students spread across the benches, someone's music leaking out of a pair of headphones a few seats down, and the sun still coming in through the windows. I set up at my usual spot, put in my own headphones, and lost myself in the work the way I always do. I didn't notice the room emptying around me one person at a time, didn't notice the sun going down, didn't notice any of it until I looked up from my laptop, and the windows were just black rectangles reflecting the room back at me. The clock on the wall said 9:40, and I was the only one left.

Almost done with this last section, I muttered to myself. A second later, I heard it come back at me from somewhere behind me in the lab, just the two words, "almost done," flat and a little delayed, like an echo that took its time catching up.

I turned around slowly. The lab was empty, every workbench dark except for mine. At the far end, near the supply closets, the door was slightly open, even though I was sure it had been closed. I sat there, trying to convince myself it was nothing, a draft, the building settling, anything. Then I heard it again, this time closer. 

"Come on, almost done," a voice called out, cracking midway through and dropping into something deeper and more unsettling that no longer felt like my own. In that moment, every light in the lab went out at once.

I grabbed my phone to use the flashlight, but I couldn't turn it on. In the dark, I heard something small moving between the benches, its feet dragging against the tile in a slow, unhurried way that felt worse than if it had been running, as if it already knew it didn't need to hurry. When my flashlight finally flickered on, it caught the figure, a kid standing at the end of my row. He was about my height if he had been standing on the bench, wearing a school hoodie that I didn't recognize. His head was tilted at an unnatural angle, and he was smiling in an unnatural way, with his teeth visible but no lips surrounding them.  
  
"Come on," he said, in my voice, smiling wider than a jaw should go, "almost done."  
  
I bolted for the exit and heard him behind me immediately, not footsteps, but that same clicking joint sound from the arcade, except closer and faster. I hit the stairwell door so hard it swung all the way into the wall, and I didn't stop until I was outside in the parking lot under a light with my keys already in my hand. I sat in my car for ten minutes before I could make my hands work well enough to drive.

I'm home now, door locked, chain on, and I just heard my own voice, very quietly, say "almost done," from somewhere inside my apartment. I haven't moved from this chair since.
