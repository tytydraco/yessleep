The first time I saw it, I was brushing my teeth.

Our bathroom has a separate little room for the toilet. There’s no door, just a narrow doorway beside the vanity. I was looking down at the sink when I got that very specific feeling that someone had entered the room. You know it. Nothing moves, you don’t hear anything. You just suddenly know you’re not alone.

I looked up and something was peeking around the doorway. I only saw part of a face: one eye, a little forehead, maybe an inch of cheek. Then it was gone.

It happened so quickly that I didn’t scream. I actually laughed. That involuntary embarrassed laugh you make when something scares you and your brain immediately starts trying to explain why you’re an idiot.

I called for my husband, then checked the toilet room and bedroom. Both were empty. It took me another few seconds to remember he wasn’t even home. He was working late.

There was a chrome towel hook beside the doorway, and eventually I convinced myself I’d caught some weird reflection in it. That explanation made absolutely no geometric sense, but it was almost midnight and I wanted to sleep.

I told my husband the next morning. He immediately leaned around the kitchen doorway with one eye showing.

“Like this?”

I told him to fuck off. He disappeared, then slowly peeked around from the other side.

“Or like this?”

I threw a dish towel at him.

That became a joke for a while. I’d walk into the bedroom and half his face would be sticking out of the closet. I’d open the garage and he’d be crouched behind my car with one eye visible over the hood. Once I woke up in the middle of the night and saw his eye peeking around the bedroom door. I screamed so loudly that he fell over laughing.

After that, the original thing stopped bothering me.

Until I saw it again.

I was downstairs watching TV alone. Our kitchen is behind the living room, and from the couch I can see the edge of the refrigerator through an opening in the wall. Something moved beside it.

There was a face peeking from behind the refrigerator.

This time I had enough time to actually look at it. It was low, maybe three feet off the floor, and it looked completely human. Nothing distorted or obviously wrong. Brown eye, normal skin, a few strands of dark hair. It blinked once while I stared at it.

Then I realized our refrigerator is almost flush against the wall. There are maybe three inches behind it.

The face slowly moved backward. It didn’t turn or duck away. It just slid sideways into that three-inch space until the eye disappeared.

I went upstairs and locked the bedroom door.

My husband came home twenty minutes later, and we pulled the refrigerator out together. There was nothing behind it. No hole, no animal, no weird reflection. Just dust and an old bottle cap.

That was when the joke stopped.

I tried to sketch the face the next day. I’m terrible at drawing, but I had this stupid idea that if I could get it right on paper I might understand what had bothered me about it. I couldn’t. Every version looked too expressive, too obviously creepy. What I remembered was much more ordinary than that.

Eventually I drew only the part I’d actually seen: the eye, the forehead, the edge of the cheek coming around the wall. I’ve attached it. The eye still isn’t right. I remember it looking less frightened than my drawing does. More curious.

For the next few weeks, nothing happened that I could point to and say was definitely strange. A cabinet we’d left open would be closed later. We’d hear a floorboard upstairs when both of us were downstairs. The dogs would occasionally stop in the hallway and stare toward an empty room. Our downstairs camera, which we use to check on them when we’re away, started sending more motion alerts than usual. Every time we checked, there was nothing there.

One night we heard three knocks from upstairs while we were watching TV. My husband went to check and found the chair from my office sitting about six feet out in the hallway. Neither of us remembered moving it. He rolled it back, and the next morning it was in the hallway again.

We blamed the floor. We tested it, couldn’t make the chair roll on its own, and eventually stopped talking about it.

A few nights later my husband woke me because he’d heard a floorboard creak outside the bedroom. We listened together and heard another creak, closer this time. After several minutes he checked the hallway and the rest of the house. Nothing.

When he came back, I tried to lighten the mood. I leaned halfway around the bedroom door so only one eye was showing and whispered, “Like this?”

He didn’t laugh.

I did, but even as I did it I remember thinking the joke didn’t feel like ours anymore.

After that, things became strangely normal again. I didn’t see the face for almost a month. There were only little things that were easy to explain individually. Once my husband asked why I’d walked into the bedroom and immediately walked back out without saying anything. I hadn’t. Another time I heard him coughing downstairs while I was getting dressed, but when I went down he was outside with the dogs.

We both got very good at telling ourselves things.

Then one morning my husband asked why I’d been standing in the hallway the night before.

He’d woken around 3 a.m. and seen someone peeking around the bedroom doorway. He could only see one eye and part of a forehead. He thought it was me, so he said my name. The face disappeared behind the wall.

Then he rolled over.

I was asleep beside him.

I asked why he hadn’t woken me up. He thought about it for a surprisingly long time before saying, “I don’t know. I think I was pretending I hadn’t seen it.”

That sentence bothered me more than anything else he’d told me.

We started closing the bedroom door at night. Nothing happened for almost two weeks.

Then my husband had an overnight shift.

I was home alone. I worked late, ordered food, watched TV, took the dogs out. Around midnight I took a shower.

When I stepped out, the bathroom mirror was completely fogged. I wiped it with my hand and saw the face behind me, peeking around the bathroom doorway.

It didn’t disappear this time.

For the first time I could see the eye clearly. Brown iris, wet lower lid, a few eyelashes. Completely, ordinarily human. It blinked.

I turned around. Nothing.

When I looked back at the mirror, it was there again, except slightly more of the face was showing. Enough that I could see the corner of its mouth. It wasn’t smiling. Its mouth was closed, but it had the expression of someone trying very hard not to laugh.

Like a child hiding during a game.

I ran downstairs, grabbed my keys and called my husband. I told him the face was back and that I was leaving. Then he remembered the camera.

We have one downstairs so we can check on the dogs. He opened the app while I walked toward the front door. I could hear him tapping through it, then he suddenly stopped talking.

“What?”

“Are you by the couch?”

“Yes.”

“Go outside.”

Something about his voice made my stomach drop. I asked what he was seeing, but he just told me to keep walking. Then he added, “Don’t run.”

That scared me more.

I was almost at the front door when he told me not to look toward the kitchen. Of course I looked. It was empty. I asked again what he could see, but he only kept telling me to walk.

When I reached the door, he made a tiny sound, like he’d stopped himself from yelling.

“What?”

There was a pause.

“It’s closer.”

I opened the door and went outside. I sat in my car until he came home, and neither of us wanted to go back inside. We watched the camera footage together in the driveway.

At first I couldn’t understand what he’d seen. There I was standing beside the couch with the phone against my ear. Nothing behind me, nothing in the hallway, nothing in the kitchen.

Then my husband paused it.

Around the kitchen wall, a face was peeking at me.

He pressed play. On the video, I turned toward the kitchen and the face disappeared. I remembered that moment. That was when he’d told me not to look toward the kitchen. I had looked anyway, and there had been nothing there.

The second I turned away on the recording, the face appeared again, except it wasn’t in the kitchen anymore. It was peeking around the hallway, several feet closer to me.

I kept walking toward the front door. It disappeared from the hallway and then appeared over the back of the couch. Closer again.

There was no blur or shape crossing the room. It was simply behind one thing, then behind another, always while I was looking somewhere else.

Then, on the video, I stopped walking.

Neither of us remembered me doing that. I was facing the front door, but my head tilted slightly like I was listening to something. We turned the volume all the way up.

Very faintly, a voice said, “Like this?”

Neither of us spoke.

My husband rewound it. The voice was unmistakable this time. Not loud, not distorted. Almost conversational.

“Like this?”

It was the exact thing he’d said the morning after my first sighting.

He rewound farther. I was walking again. The face was behind the couch.

“Or like this?”

The other thing he’d said.

I hadn’t remembered hearing either phrase while I was in the room, but the person on the recording clearly did. Every time the voice spoke, I stopped walking for a second. And every time I stopped, the face moved closer.

We kept watching.

When I reached the front door, the face disappeared from behind the couch. For several seconds there was nothing.

Then something leaned into frame directly behind my shoulder.

There was no doorway there. No furniture. Nothing for it to peek around. So it had made one.

It bent sideways at the waist until only one eye, part of its forehead and a little cheek were visible beside my head.

Like it was pretending my body was the wall.

Its face was inches from mine.

I opened the front door and it straightened. For the first time, we saw its whole face.

It looked like a man.

That’s all. A normal man. Average age, dark hair, a face I could pass in a grocery store and forget ten seconds later. There was nothing about him I could describe to the police that would sound unusual.

Except his expression.

He looked delighted. Not smiling. Just delighted, like someone whose turn had finally come.

On the recording, I stepped outside. He didn’t follow. The door started closing behind me, and at the last second he leaned around it, peeking through the narrowing gap.

Then the door shut.

I asked my husband why it hadn’t followed me. He didn’t answer. He was already rewinding the footage.

We watched the sequence again. Kitchen. I turned and it hid. Hallway. I turned and it hid. Couch. Then the voice. “Like this?” I stopped. It moved closer. “Or like this?” I stopped again. It moved closer.

My husband looked at me and said, “It isn’t hiding from you.”

I understood what he meant before he said anything else.

It was playing.

And we’d taught it how.

I wanted to leave. My husband wanted to know how long it had been happening, so he started going backward through the camera history. There had been motion alerts earlier that night at 9:14, 8:02 and 6:47. I’d ignored all of them.

The first two showed an empty living room. At 6:47, my office chair was downstairs, sitting in the hallway facing the camera.

We watched for almost a minute. Nothing happened. Then the chair rolled slowly sideways about six feet and stopped behind the hallway wall.

Neither of us said anything.

We both remembered where we’d found that chair weeks earlier.

Six feet outside my office.

He went back further. There were alerts from that afternoon, then that morning, then the night before. Most showed empty rooms until he opened one from 3:12 a.m., when we had both been asleep upstairs.

Nothing moved in the living room. My husband almost closed the clip, but I noticed the television. It was off, and the black screen reflected the hallway behind the camera.

Someone was peeking around the hallway corner.

The next alert was at 3:41. Same place, except a little more of the face was showing. At 4:06 there was more. At 4:38, more again. By 5:17, almost half the face was visible.

Then there were no more alerts.

We checked the history. The camera had been triggering for weeks. We’d assumed it was the dogs.

We opened one from twelve days earlier. Nothing in the room, but an eye in the reflection. Another from eighteen days earlier showed an eye beside the kitchen. Twenty-three days earlier, behind the couch.

Then my husband reached the night he’d woken up and seen someone peeking through our bedroom doorway.

There was a motion alert at 2:58 a.m., three minutes before he’d woken up.

We opened it.

The living room was empty. No face. No movement. For a while there was nothing at all.

Then we heard something through the camera microphone.

Upstairs.

Very faintly, my husband’s voice said, “Like this?”

A pause.

Then my voice: “Fuck off.”

We stared at each other.

That conversation had happened months earlier, the morning after I first saw it. Not that night.

The recording continued. His voice again: “Or like this?” Then me laughing. Then silence for almost thirty seconds.

Finally another voice spoke.

It sounded almost exactly like mine.

Almost.

“My turn.”

The clip ended.

Before either of us said anything, his phone buzzed with a new motion notification.

We were both sitting in the car.

He opened the live feed. The downstairs camera showed the empty living room. Nothing moved for about ten seconds.

Then the camera shifted slightly.

It shifted again, slowly, like someone was turning it by hand. The view moved away from the living room, past the couch, past the kitchen, and toward the front window.

Toward us.

It stopped.

Through the glass, I could see our car in the driveway. I could see myself in the passenger seat and my husband beside me holding the phone.

Then something whispered directly into the camera microphone.

“Like this?”

My husband dropped the phone.

I didn’t.

I was looking at the house.

At the downstairs window, a face was peeking around the curtain.

One eye. A little forehead. A little cheek.

It wasn’t looking at me.

It was looking at my husband.

And he hadn’t noticed yet.
