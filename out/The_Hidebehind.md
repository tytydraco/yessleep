I am, even now as I type this, being hunted. Normally, around this time of year, my dad and I go out and take an annual camping trip to the Sawtooth national forest. And I don’t mean in the designated spots with the RVs and the barbecues. My dad and I were boondockers. We’d go off the beaten path.

I’m well aware that, especially without the creature comforts of an RV or an air conditioned resort, camping is absolutely not everyone’s idea of a vacation. But, when you’re growing up in a state with more trees than people, there isn’t much else to do with your free time besides go to the local church on Sundays and explore the near-endless forest of pines. 

You see, forests in Idaho are sort of like liquids. That’s the best way I can explain it. They flow slowly, creeping through every valley and over every mountain, swallowing up everything in their path. Even in the places where people lived, you could not escape it. At least not without the help of a couple chainsaws.

People do their best to hold the waves of greenery back, but whenever a place stopped being lived in, stopped being maintained, or *loved*, it would take only a few years for it to be fully entrenched in the sea of conifers. In some places, the forest could become so dense that it was basically impossible to walk straight through. As if you were trying to walk straight through a wall, or a mountain. The pines above you would become so thick that you wouldn’t be able to see the sun in the middle of the day.

My dad’s a pretty experienced outdoorsman. He was aware of how dangerous these places can be, in both the natural and *supernatural* ways. He had made sure that I knew how to read a physical map and quote the Bible by the time I had stopped wearing diapers. For my seventh birthday, he gave me a waterproof compass. I guess you could say that he was a hard-ass about safety. 

And about faith. 

To be honest with you, he’s always been a bit of a hard-ass about everything. Maybe it’s a side effect of having to raise me alone, I don’t know.

I know that it made him paranoid.

Earlier, when I said that he knew about both the natural and supernatural dangers of the forest, I meant it. He'd always tell me stories to make sure that I was committed to safety. Especially when I was a kid. If anyone reading this has ever been camping, you can probably guess the sorts of things that he used to say; Clean up all the trash you leave, never go anywhere outside at night without a flashlight, don’t feed any of the animals. Pretty normal camping safety stuff. 

The thing was, I was the kind of kid who would go around overturning stones to see what kinds of spiders would appear from underneath. I was the kind of kid who would see a dark train tunnel in the side of a mountain, and say;

“*Woah! Can we explore it, dad?*”

I probably wasn’t the brightest kid, either.

I guess, because of that, my dad would tell me stories.

Stories about the Hidebehind.

“The Hidebehind lives in the forest. We’re his guests here, y’understand?” My dad would say, “He ain’t too friendly, but if we’re polite guests and take care of ourselves, he’ll leave us alone.”

He would tell me about how the Hidebehind could suck his own gut in, and crack his own bones to stay hidden behind even the thinnest of branches. 

He would tell me that the Hidebehind was as tall as a tree and loved to walk on his hands.

He would tell me about how he loved to follow people, staying directly behind the back of someone’s head, where he couldn’t be seen. He would tell me how the Hidebehind was one of the many forms God took to test us.

He would tell me that the Hidebehind loved to eat disobedient children.

It scared me. I mean, I was a kid, of course it scared me. So, at least when we were camping, I tried my best to be obedient. But I wasn’t perfect. I remember one summer, I must’ve been eleven or twelve, we ended up having neighbors. The most adorable little family of raccoons was living in a tree not twenty five feet from where we had set up our tents. After two days of cutesy eyes and pairs of little begging paws, I eventually broke. I fed one of them a piece of bacon, and after he found out about it, my dad wouldn’t even look at me for the rest of the trip. 

When we got home, he told me how dangerous that had been. How if the Hidebehind had been there, and if Jesus hadn’t been looking out for us that day, that I would’ve been eaten on the spot. I think he ended up making me cry. 

I know that he was just trying to protect me from the very real dangers of the forest, but I don’t know. It seemed cruel. 

I wish that my dad was less cruel with me.

When I grew older, and stopped believing in the Hidebehind, I stopped being so afraid of breaking the rules too. I started drinking. Partying on weekends. Sleeping around.

It led to predictable results with my dad.

But, regardless of how rocky our relationship was at times, I managed to keep my life mostly in order. I studied for every damn test that high school threw at me, at least. I kept my head down. I tried my best to ignore the strange sense of… *un*\-belonging that had begun to grow in me. A dull, black emptiness in my chest that seemed to follow me around wherever I went. 

The partying and drinking didn’t make it go away. So I swore it all off and started going back to church. That didn’t make it go away either. 

It felt like divine judgement. 

One night, after my dad had gone to sleep and I had turned out all the lights in the house, I stayed up. Laying awake in my bed. I just sat there, in the darkness of my room, and stared at the window. Even with the curtains closed, I was still able to see the dark shapes of the trees in the backyard. Those endless, inescapable pines. It made me hopeless, in a way. I was stuck. Trapped in a life, in a body that I felt no familiarity with or longing for. 

Why go to church? I could start partying again but what would be the point? It would only be for the sake of noise. 

I didn’t know what else to do, so I prayed. 

“Why did you make me this way?” I spoke, barely a whisper, into the cold air. “Why put me here, God? Why not let me be a deer, or a tree, or a raccoon instead?” 

Nothing answered me.

“Why make me a man in a world with so many beautiful creatures?” I said, my eyes welling with tears. Silence in return. And then;

*“Gaw. Gaw. Gaw. Gaw. Gaw. Gaw. Gaw.”* 

A noise rang out. From the direction of my window. A strange sound. Like wood knocking together. 

*“Gaw. Gaw. Gaw. Gaw. Gaw. Gaw. Gaw.”* 

Louder this time. It took me a moment to finally realize what it was. 

It was a laugh. A strangled laugh coming from the window.

*“GAW! GAW! GAW! GAW! GAW! GAW! GAW!”*

I leapt up from my bed. Adrenaline flooding my body. 

“*Dad! Help me!*” 

A dark shape, shadow outlined by the moon, just behind the curtains. Gigantic, and furry. The shape of a man? I couldn’t tell.

*“GAW! GAW! GAW! GAW! GAW! GAW! GAW!”*

It kept laughing. I covered my ears but couldn’t force myself to run away. This void in my soul that I was feeling, was that its presence? 

The Hidebehind?

“God, is that you?” I murmured… “Please… please stop… *please* stop laughing at me…” I murmured, shaking. “I’m *sorry…* God… I’m sorry…” 

My dad ended up breaking the lock on my door when he kicked it in. He was holding a shotgun and wearing a crucifix. But, by then, the laughing had stopped. 

There was nothing outside my window anymore. Fifteen years old, and I had wet myself.

“What the hell happened?” My dad had asked me. 

A night terror, I had told him. I started to believe in that lie myself, after a couple of years. I didn’t hear it again. I didn’t stay up late. I slept with my back to the window.

Eventually, all of my studying in science class paid off, and I got my acceptance letter from CU Boulder. My dad had never gotten to go to college, he was a lumberman, and with only him to raise me, I was going to be the first in my family to go.

I was getting a bachelor's degree in environmental science. Plus a minor in astronomy. It was my first time outside of the sleepy little town I had grown up in, and for the first time I was surrounded by people that were… well, *not* from Idaho. to say that I experienced a culture shock would be putting it extremely mildly. 

Don’t get me wrong- I’m not complaining about that. I met all sorts of amazing people, and I learned that the world was infinitely more varied and interesting than I could’ve known. I made new friends, and I gained more informed opinions. I guess you could say that I got to learn that there was more to the world than endless forests. Even better, it was the first time in my life that I had the freedom to do what I chose. To dress how I wanted to. To sound, and *say*, what I wanted to do.

By the time that my second year of college was finished, I had been able to take a long look at myself. At my own identity. And I decided to make a decision.

He was packing the last of our equipment into his old pickup when I told him.

“Hey, lend me a hand with these tent poles, would you?” He asked, and I obliged, helping him stab the last few of them through the cracks in our supplies. “Thanks, kiddo.” 

“Dad, there’s something you need to know.” I said, when I had finished.

“What is it, Harry?” He asked me, partially playfully, and partially sternly. I wonder if he thought that I was going to tell him that I had a girlfriend. Or that I had accidentally gotten some girl pregnant. He probably would’ve preferred that to learning that he actually had a daughter. 

“Dad, I’m trans.” I had thought it would be better to be direct. “I don’t want to be called Harry anymore. I want to be called Heather.” After I told him, I remember that he just… stood there. Staring at me. I’ve never quite seen a look like that on anyone before. It was as if I had become a stranger in an instant.

“What?” He managed to say, after a small moment.

“I’m trans, dad. I’ve known about it for a long time. I know, it’s a lot to take in. It took me so many years to realize it, and even more for me to stop hating myself for it. But I’m done doing that. I’m ready to live my life on my terms. And I hope that you can accept this part of me.” 

Accept this part of me. God. I can’t believe I said that.

I think that I knew what sort of reaction he was going to have. I think that’s why I was so… calm about the way I told him. So clinical. Like a veterinarian euthanizing a sick dog.

As much as I wanted him to say something different. As much as I had hoped, part of me had already known what the outcome was going to be. It didn’t make it hurt any less, though.

“I don’t understand.”

“I can answer any questions you want.”

“Why are you doing this?”

“Because…” I paused for a moment, “Because it’s who I am, dad. I don’t have any other reason besides that.” He stared at me, hard.

“It was that damn college, wasn’t it? All those silly people putting these ideas in your head.” 

“No dad, I’ve felt like this for a long time. I just…” I tried to choose my wording carefully, “I didn’t have the vocabulary before. I never knew how to explain the way that I felt. Now I do.” 

“So what, then? You think you can change who you are because of what some people told you?” 

“Dad- Dad I’m not changing anything. I’ve *always* been like this.” 

“Do you’re smarter than me? Is it that? You know how much I sacrificed to get you into that school?” He practically spat out the words. I sighed. “Why do you think I put in so many hours at the lumber mill?”

“No dad. I don’t think I’m smarter than you. I just want to be called Heather from now on. That’s all.” I said. 

“No.”

“That’s all I’m asking for, dad. You don’t even have to call me she/her, if you don’t want to. Please just don’t call me Harry.” He studied me for a moment, before turning and briskly walking up the driveway. 

He opened our… *his* front door, and slammed it behind him. Barely a minute later he returned with something. A necklace with a wooden crucifix on the end of it. 

“And here’s what I want you to do from now on,” he said, shoving the necklace into my fingers, “I want you to take this. Take this and never come back here.” I think that it was the first time I had ever seen my dad cry. I was about to throw the crucifix right back at him, when he said something I never could have expected.

“You know, the *Hidebehind* eats disobedient children.” He told me.

“Excuse me?” 

“Just…” he muttered something under his breath, “Just take this and get out of my sight.

I did what I was told. 

Fourteen hours of driving later, I had pulled into my apartment building’s parking lot. Home sweet home.

Now my only home. 

I grimaced, my legs hurt from sitting in one place for too long. I pulled out my phone, and scrolled until I found a contact.

*‘Avery - Roommate’*

I called him, and he didn’t pick up, so I left him a message telling him I was home. Afterwards, I scrolled to a different contact, a good friend of mine.

*‘Houston’*

*“Hey, it didn’t go well. I’m back at my apartment now. Thanks for your support.”* I texted him, before getting out of the car.

When I walked into the apartment, I heard the shower running. The bathroom door had been held ajar with a ski boot, and steam was pouring out onto the wood floor. 

“Hey Avery, I’m home!” I called out. “I told you to stop showering with the door open!” 

“You weren’t here! And I hate it when the mirror fogs up!” He said back, voice slightly muffled by the running water. “Sorry dude!” 

I had told him before that I didn’t like being called that, but I guessed that it was my fault for not reminding him. I went to my room and closed the door behind me.

Just me and my crappy mattress. I reached into my purse, and took out what my dad had given me. The crucifix. It was heavier than expected, considering its small size and being made out of wood. Carved by hand with a knife. Christ’s agonized expression had been tailor crafted.

I wondered to myself if my dad had originally made it as a coming-home gift for me.

He didn’t even try to save me. 

Not that I *needed* to be saved. 

Damn it, was I crying? I knew this was going to happen. Why was I crying?

I stood up, and opened the window to my room. The blast of cold air wasn’t a great replacement for splashing water in my face, but it beat going out there and talking to my roommate again.

The mountains lay ahead. Small headlights from distant cars slid down it like beads of sweat. Crickets chirped in the bushes two stories below.

*At least,* I had thought to myself, *I finally understand what the Hidebehind was.* 

The truth was, the Hidebehind wasn't for me after all. It was just for him. It was his way of controlling me. That’s all it ever was. Turning the crucifix over in my hand, I noticed something. The wood was not naturally dark. It had been stained that way, with some sort of fluid. It didn’t smell, and it didn’t leave any sort of stains on my hands. Maybe he left it out in the rain. I put the crucifix on my nightstand, and laid back down on my bed. 

My phone went off. A text message.

*“Damn. I’m so sorry, Heather. That sucks. Let me know if you need anything.”* A text message from Houston. 

I was about to text him back when I heard something. 

*“Gaw. Gaw. Gaw.”* 

My blood ran cold. I turned to the open window, and rubbed my eyes after a moment of staring. I couldn’t believe what I was seeing. 

A set of clawed hands, clinging to my windowsill. 

At first, I thought someone was dangling from my window, but the arms attached to those hands didn’t hang beneath the window. 

Instead, they went *up.* The arms, a set of dark, leathery things, five feet long, no, *eight* feet long, stretched further and further upward until the window ended and I couldn’t see them anymore. 

Did they go all the way up into the sky?

*“Gaw. Gaw. Gaw.”* One hand left its resting position and grasped blindly. It was trying to grab at something. Grab at me. 

I gasped, cursed. And fell out of my bed. 

“Jesus *fuck-*“ I cried, a splitting pain erupted from my elbow. I managed to crawl away from the window, the first hand now blindly trying to rip my mattress out of its frame. The second hand was searching for me now, too. 

I found my ankle, and began to pull. 

“*Help! Help me!*” 

I kicked at it with my free leg, looking desperately around for something to hold onto, I grabbed the leg of my desk and ended up pulling it down with me, causing a loud crash. 

*“Gaw. Gaw. Gaw.”*

It kept laughing at me.

Then, someone knocked at my door.

“Are you alright in there?” My roommate, Peter. 

“*NO!*” I shouted, but even as I did, the hands were slinking away. Back out the window. They were completely gone when Peter opened the door to my room. 

“Jesus, your room!” He said, when he came in. “You really ought to clean up in here, dude.” 

I was still gasping for breath, terrified. 

“W-w-w-what?” I mumbled.

“What’s going on with you?” 

“I… hands… the *hands…* I…” I tried to get the words out. Honest to God, I tried to get the words out. But how could I? How could he even believe me? 

I wouldn’t believe me. 

“Huh? What?” He said, confused.

“I… I’m going to a friend's place.” 

By the time I had finished explaining everything to Houston, I had finally managed to stop shaking. 

“You don’t… do you believe me?” I asked him. We were sitting at his kitchen table, and after a moment, he looked away and sighed. “God dammit, you don’t.” 

“I believe that,” Houston said, getting up and heading to his freezer. “That you need some ice cream. Want some?” He asked me. After a moment I nodded. 

He opened the freezer and took out two tubs of ice cream. Minty chocolate flavor. 

“Please tell me you don’t think I’m insane.” I told him. He handed me one of the tubs. 

“I don’t think you're insane, Heather. I just think that you’ve been through a lot this week. I want to be here for you, but like, what you're telling me is pretty unbelievable.” He explained, opening his tub. 

“I know. But I swear to you, I swear to you it’s true. This thing is following me. I heard it when I was a kid and now I’ve seen it. Or *part* of it.” 

“Right, yeah. The dream you had when you were a kid.” He said, and I stood up in frustration. 

“I don’t think it was a dream!” 

Houston was about to respond when the front door suddenly swung open. A girl came running in. 

“Hey! Heard you were feeling bad!” The girl, Petra, was giving me a soft smile, and then a big hug.

“Wait, what? Really?” I asked, trying to awkwardly hug Petra while keeping the still-cold ice cream from touching my neck or her face. 

“Yeah, really. My brother texted me!” She said, and Houston nodded.

“Petra and I were going to head up to Vail, to our family’s ski home, but after you showed up, I figured…” Houston trailed off.

“We figured you could come with us. Take your mind off of everything.” Petra said. She gave me another hug. It was getting slightly excessive now. “I’m sorry about your dad.” 

“Don’t worry about it.” I said, prying myself out of her grip.

“So, what do you say? Do you want to come with us?” Houston asked.

I shouldn’t have said yes to going with them.
