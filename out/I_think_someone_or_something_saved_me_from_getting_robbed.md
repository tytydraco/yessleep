I'm a nurse, and this happened a few months ago after a particularly exhausting night shift. I got home in the morning, barely able to keep my eyes open. I had a couple of grocery bags with me, dropped them in the kitchen and collapsed on the couch without even changing out of my scrubs. I live alone, so I wasn't worried about anyone disturbing me. I fell asleep almost immediately.

I have no idea how long I'd been sleeping when I suddenly heard someone say, "Get up. Lock the door."

It was a woman's voice. Clear, loud and close enough that it sounded like someone was standing right next to the couch.

Now, remember, I live alone.

I didn't just wake up. I practically jumped off the couch. My heart was pounding, and for a few seconds I was convinced someone had broken into my apartment. I looked around the living room, checked the kitchen and listened for any movement. Nothing. The apartment was completely silent.

Then I remembered what the voice had said.

I walked over to my front door and almost had a heart attack. It wasn't locked. In fact, it wasn't even properly closed. I could literally push it open without touching the handle.

I guess I'd been so exhausted when I came home, with my hands full of groceries, that I'd completely forgotten to close and lock it.

I immediately locked the door, checked it twice and went back to the couch. I just sat there trying to understand what had happened. I was completely awake now, and I couldn't stop thinking about that voice.

It didn't sound like a dream. I know how strange that sounds, especially since I'd just finished a night shift and was probably sleep deprived. But I've never experienced anything like that before.

I called my best friend right away and told her everything. She suggested I might have heard something in my sleep because I was exhausted. That seemed like the most reasonable explanation, so I tried to convince myself she was right.

But the next day something happened that made the whole experience even stranger.

I was leaving for work when I noticed police cars parked outside the house across from mine. There were officers talking to the people who lived there, and I later found out their house had been burglarized the previous day.

Apparently, their back door had been left unlocked.

Thankfully, nobody was hurt, but someone had gotten inside and stolen some of their belongings.

I couldn't stop thinking about my own front door. The fact that I'd left it open on the very same day someone had broken into a house right across the street terrified me.

Maybe the voice was just a dream. Maybe some part of my brain remembered that I hadn't locked the door and woke me up.

I really want to believe that's what happened.

But I still can't explain why I heard someone speaking so clearly, or why the first thing they told me to do was lock my door.

And I can't shake the feeling that someone was looking out for me that day.
