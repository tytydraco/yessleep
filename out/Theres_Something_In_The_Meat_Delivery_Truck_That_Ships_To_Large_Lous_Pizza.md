I was originally just going to make this post compiling “interviews” I do with the employees about Large Lou’s Pizza. But then something happened today, and I am now genuinely considering quitting.

We had gotten basically everything together and sat down for lunch. Salads were prepped, toppings were stocked, and the dough was rising. Unfortunately, the meat delivery still hadn’t arrived. 

Me and Brent could barely appreciate the chicken tacos Cezar’s wife had prepared for us as we repeatedly would stop and listen at the slightest noise in hopes it was the sound of the truck arriving, or the delivery man knocking at the door.

was only after we had finished eating, and were well into the lunch rush when three pounds came from the back kitchen door. Brent and I immediately looked at each other, sharing a look that said, “Neither of us has time for this, but one of us has to do it”.

I was closer to the door, and I usually picked up the order anyway, so I was the one to go outside. The first thing I saw as I went out was the delivery man, disheveled and breathing heavily. There were also no boxes for me to carry inside.

if it were an excuse, he said, “You need to help me with the meat man.” Then gestured for me to follow as he walked around to the back of his box truck.

Now, this should have been something that would make me ask more questions before nodding and following the man. But I had misheard him. In my head, the surly delivery driver had said, “You need to help me with the meat, man.” His accent blurring my ability to delineate between the presence of a comma or lack thereof.

The truck doors swung open, and the first thing I saw was a rotund, almost engorged, purple stomach.

The rest of the truck was empty, save for a box of grocery bags, and some empty cardboard boxes with the company’s logo stamped on them. However, empty didn’t mean clean. Every part of the truck was splattered in coagulating lumps of what I can only assume was blood. This all centered around the red beast laying in the back of the truck. Its stomach was easily the largest part of its body. Its arms and legs looked more like twigs wrapped in leathery skin as they flailed hopelessly under the weight of its great abdomen. What I could only assume was its head bore a closer resemblance to a frisbee balanced haphazardly on top of a plunger. As air flowed in from the open truck doors, the thing groaned painfully while swinging its limbs and raising its malformed head. 

The groans, to my horror, were familiar. The delivery man hopped into the truck, his shoes squelching in the mess as he stepped. He berated the thing a bit, even throwing a few angry kicks as it groaned and flailed in tortured pain. Its thin arms slapping wetly against the sides of the truck. The driver came back to where I was and held out a hand to help me get in the truck. He noticed my stricken face as he waited for me to take hold.

“Yep,” He grunted, while looking back at the creature, then examining the walls of the truck, “He’s made quite the mess. Feistier than usual. I just need you to put the meat in the boxes while I do my thing.”

I don’t know why I stepped in and helped. I think I was too shocked to do anything else. The delivery driver handed me a box and started pushing in on the creature’s stomach while telling me where to hold the box.

the top of its head, there was a two inch hole in the center of the maroon disk. As the delivery man pushed, the creature flailed, and red goo sprayed out of the hole. As both of us were sprayed with goop, the man stomped down on the creature’s leg and said something I couldn’t make out in a loud, angry tone. The beast whimpered, and the goop dribbled to a stop. Then, the man pushed again on the bulbous stomach and a meaty tube protruded from the hole.

Morbid curiosity took over me, and I watched the red log as it pushed further from the hole. After it got to about six inches in length, the man told me to start sweeping the meat into the cardboard box I had forgotten I was still holding. I tentatively pushed on the tip and realized that it was not one solid piece, but multiple disks stacked on top of each other. Disks that bore an uncanny resemblance to the pepperonis we put on our pizza.  
This continued for thirty minutes or so as different meats slid forcefully out of the hole: ham, salami, ground beef, bacon, sausage, shredded chicken. All the same dark red tone as the skin of the thing they pushed out from.

I placed it all in the box until the thing’s stomach reduced to at least 50 percent of its original size. Then, it was finished. The delivery driver took a sharpie, and labeled each of the boxes by the excrement that was contained within, and then took a big sigh of relief and looked back at me.

“Thanks, bubba. I don’t know why he makes such a fuss sometimes, but you were a huge help.”  
I stepped out of the truck with a box labeled “Sausage” while he said this to me. When the bright sun struck my face, it was like I awoke from a coma, and the events which had only just transpired registered in my brain like a truck hitting a possum.

The box fell from my hands, and onto the ground, crimson protein bursting onto the warm asphalt. 

“Hey!” Exclaimed the delivery man, “We just filled that box!”

I shouted, frightened and furious, “WHAT THE HELL IS THAT THING?”

The man, staggering back at my sudden change of tone, was at a loss for words. His mouth opened a few times before the last thing I wanted to hear in response came out.

“It’s just the MeatBoy. You know he’s not *that* bad.”

Know? KNOW!? What meeting did I miss where I was supposed to "*know"* about the large meat creature that sat in the back of the delivery truck?

I brought the meat inside, told Linda I was sick, which wasn’t much effort since all blood had drained from my face, and went home for the day. Which leads to me writing everything down right now, and letting Brent deal with the lunch and dinner rush on his own.

I still plan on going into work tomorrow. I just needed a break from the bullshit. Since I had loads of extra free time today, I also wrote all the interviews (more like, guided conversations) into transcripts from what I recorded on my phone. I originally planned on just uploading the audio files themselves, but decided this made more sense since there were multiple moments where the rustling of my pocket made it practically impossible to hear what the other person was saying.

I gave up trying after this. Marley’s actually the stupidest person I think I’ve ever met, and I was getting dizzy smelling his body spray.

I have never seen a “bright-eyed” or “chipper” customer in the whole restaurant. For reasons I’m not sure, it seems like Shirley is entirely oblivious to everything that’s happening here. I’m not sure why, or how I didn’t notice it before. Does this mean she’s in on it, or did something happen to her? It really feels like every time I dig deeper into the shit happening here, I turn up with more questions than answers.

However, I’ve caught him frowning at the meat when I bring it in sometimes, or scrutinizing a vegetable. So I think he’s aware of *something* being wrong here. Linda threatened to fire me after I mentioned something about the homeless man in passing to her. So I did not try to have a whole interview about the subject.

I’m starting to get really overwhelmed with all of this. The stress of knowing all these creatures has gotten to me so bad that I barely noticed that the duck is under a million now. At the time of posting he’s looking at me through my bedroom window, and is at 506,525.
