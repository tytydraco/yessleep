I work as an inventory control associate for a big distribution center out of Denver, CO. We receive and ship a bunch of stuff like furniture, machine parts, appliances, and so on. My job is basic: I reconcile our inventory. If the system says there’s a blender at a specific location in the warehouse, I find it, scan it, then the system is happy. If there are too many or too few things, I update the system with the correct amount. One of the more painful aspects of this job is working with a system that received its last update in the 90s, when some 80-year-old programmer changed the color scheme to ‘exceptionally gray’ and walked away thinking, “It’s perfect. It’ll never need another update.” So, it’s common that the system spits out faulty results and errors that we click through mindlessly every day. Recently, though, I encountered an error that nagged me like a lunch lady. No matter how often I’d try to reconcile with the system, it wouldn’t stop appearing. It was strange, too. It showed as:

ITEM: NEWSPAPER DISPENSER

QTY: 1

STATUS: ALLOCATED

I couldn't remember us ever stocking newspaper dispensers, but who knows. The issue seemed to be the location of the item. 4300 W COLFAX AVE. I assumed the system was being itself and catastrophically failing to work properly by putting some address we ship to as the storage location. I deleted the location and modified it to our facility to clear the issue.

ERROR: ITEM ALLOCATED.

I nodded aggressively to myself. The item was already assigned for shipping. No changes could be made. Great.

I left work late and without a resolution. While driving home I noticed a street sign. Colfax Avenue. With vengeance against antiquated software in my heart, I turned down Colfax. It was a wide, worn-out street full of convenience stores, restaurants, and fading street signs. I drove along and scanned for 4300 Colfax. I found an old-looking sandwich shop with a tattered sign. In front of the sandwich shop, I noticed a faded blue newspaper dispenser.

**\*\*\***

The dispenser had a disintegrating advertisement in the window for some lawyer whose faded skin made him appear like some ghoulish European noble. I thought about punching the little ghoul in the glass pane, imagined my hand shattering the glass, my blood dribbling all over the newspapers, then just decided to kick the thing. It rattled a bit but remained intact. Then I noticed something white underneath it. I squatted down and recognized an inventory label, identical to the ones in our warehouse. I took a quick photo with my phone before heading home.

The next day at work, I compared the photo to the numbers in our system. Wouldn’t you fucking know it—they were the same. Now, that really didn’t mean much at the moment except that we, at some point, shipped newspaper dispensers or tracked shipping for some other distributor. Whatever. What confused me was, a few days later, the status for that newspaper dispenser was updated.

STATUS: RECEIVED.

Someone was actively changing the status for this old item, maybe trying to clear the error, same as me. Despite the obvious explanation, that message left my hands sweating.

When I left work that evening—by compulsion or something else—I felt the need to check on the newspaper dispenser. It was gone. Four spots of rust marked the crime scene, a chalk outline of the now-deceased dispenser. I stood there longer than I needed to. There wasn't anything else to look at, but I kept checking the sidewalk.

I went inside the sandwich shop and flagged down the owner.

“No public bathrooms, customers only. Okay?”

“That’s fine. Wasn’t there a newspaper dispenser outside?”

“I think there was. Not anymore though.”

It was clear he was becoming annoyed when I continued my line of questioning: Who took it? When? Where’d they take it? Did you see a truck? He didn’t give me any answers and shooed me out of the place.

When I was back at work, I decided to neglect usual duties and do some digging through the system. Dozens. I found dozens of weird location fields, objects that I felt we shouldn’t have, and some that made no sense at all.

**\*\*\***

DESCRIPTION: BENCH 11  
LOCATION: S. PLATTE RVR  
STATUS: RECEIVED

I found myself at a random park near the river. I couldn’t be sure if this was the right place. All I could hear was the thrum of frogs, rushing water, and the buzz of insects passing my ear. I walked behind a couple out for a stroll, scanning the area for a bench. I found a patch of concrete, out of place with the rest of the foliage. There were holes in the concrete and a faded-out gold placard lying loose on the ground. It read: In Loving Memory of Amelia – Gone before her time.

I looked around to see if anyone was watching me. I wasn't doing anything wrong, but I couldn't think of a good explanation for why I was standing over an empty patch of concrete.

DESCRIPTION: FENCE SEGMENT, 24 FT

LOCATION: WASHINGTON ST.

STATUS: ALLOCATED

I found the fence. A chain-link fence that ran alongside an abandoned office building. I was skeptical at first and felt a bit crazy. This fence is much longer than 24 feet, so do we only have ‘inventory’ of 24 feet of it? I walked along it, trying to decide where those twenty-four feet were supposed to begin. There wasn't anything to separate that section from the rest of the fence.

On one of the poles driven into the ground there was a white sticker with a barcode. At work, I confirmed that it was the same fence. What was ‘allocated’ though? The fence? And who the hell was allocating it? The owner of the abandoned offices years and years ago? Mind you, this fence was not new. In some parts it was barely even connected. We never tracked items after they left the facility. How was this considered part of our current inventory?

 

DESCRIPTION: EAST ACCESS

LOCATION: BRIGHTON BLVD

STATUS: RECEIVED

A sleepy suburb. There are houses to my left and right. I can turn right and go down 46^(th) where I see a stop sign or turn around back the way I came from. Straight ahead of me is a brick wall. Why would you need a stop sign if no cars would turn past each other in that spot? No crosswalk either. And there was this storm drain, too that looked like it continued beyond the wall, like someone built the wall after the drain.

I looked at maps on my phone, and it seemed like this road should continue. The rest of the suburb was laid out in a grid so, just beyond the wall, there would be another road. I turned around and drove through the subdivision until I came to the other side of the wall. I could see the missing segment of the storm drain. What a bizarre design choice. Both roads ended abruptly at the brick wall. Was this some sort of zoning thing? HOA? And why the fuck was this in our system? Never have I seen a single brick in our warehouse. Maybe the wall isn’t even what the order referred to. I searched for a barcode sticker and found nothing.

**\*\*\***

All night. All damn night looking for “MUNICIPAL DRINKING FOUNTAIN (SHIPPED).” And guess what? I found it… I found some shitass rusted-out drinking fountain. In a park. After midnight. I didn’t even bother to get a picture. Pretty sure there was someone sleeping on a bench nearby ready to hold me at knifepoint.  

It’s been two weeks and I’m not sure if I’m getting somewhere or just spinning my wheels. CORNER LOTS (RECEIVED) took me to a parking garage, and I discovered missing parking spots. The numbered spots went from 14 to 15 to 20. You’d expect there to be some gap between the numbers, but no, they’re all squished together. I walked the length of the row and counted them again. There wasn't room for the missing spaces. Not unless the whole row was supposed to be longer. I also noticed an Exit sign hanging against a solid concrete wall, the exit arrow pointing towards the blank wall.

Now, I did pull the structure’s municipal records and found there was a renovation some years back, but I couldn’t figure out exactly what was changed. Maybe a stairwell was removed, and they didn’t take the exit sign? But what about the parking spots? Maybe it’s all just fucking lazy workers. Is it all just fucking lazy workers?

Yesterday during my lunch break, I found an interesting record:

DESCRIPTION: CONCRETE STEPS, EXTERIOR

STATUS: RECEIVED

LOCATION: PEARL ST.

I got off work later than planned but decided to make the extra drive to check it out. Only 40 minutes out of the way. 40 less minutes of rotting on the couch at home, right?

So, I spotted a building that had a weird shadow on it. It reminded me of the newspaper dispenser. I’d checked back on that one—still gone. I’d also gone back to the fence on Washington. The 24-foot segment with the barcode had disappeared, and when I checked the system, its status had changed to RECEIVED. All that remained on the building in front of me was a silhouette outlining what looked like stairs leading up to a second-story door. A metal bar ascended the wall at a diagonal, the remnant of an old handrail. Now, anyone who went out that door from the inside would meet a nasty drop. I thought about going inside, a James Bond-esque break-in where I’d investigate the inside and find the mastermind who steals benches, newspaper dispensers, and fences. I didn’t do that, though. I just took a picture and headed home.

I got into work late the following day. Overslept a bit, stayed up a bit too late.

I sat at my desk and shuffled some papers around, stapled some stuff together, and dug into the staircase situation. I found a related record:

DESCRIPTION: SERVICE LANDING

LOCATION: PEARL ST.

STATUS: ALLOCATED

I assumed it was related to the staircase, maybe the interior portion, the landing around that second-story door. Even better, I was able to dig up an old interior photograph. A small landing with damaged yellow wallpaper and this nasty, worn-out linoleum. One of those lights hung on the ceiling, the one people will say looks like a boob, you know? I printed out the pictures and stapled them to a copy of the record, then I saw it: a white sticker on the underside of my stapler.

**\*\*\***

I scanned the barcode and immediately it flagged in the system:  
DESCRIPTION: STAPLER, BLACK

QTY: 1

STATUS: ALLOCATED

Allocated. It seemed that these allocated items were disappearing, so I decided to stage an experiment. I took the stapler home with me that evening. I did a photoshoot of the stapler (nothing provocative), weighed it, then placed it in the center of my kitchen table. I set up my phone to record, plugged it in so it wouldn’t die, pulled up a chair, and waited. I assumed whatever happened to these things was abrupt, so I had a meal ready, an audiobook playing, and a small pile of energy drinks. I think it was around 4 a.m. when I dozed off. I woke up to a puddle of drool under me and the stapler as present as ever. It was 7 a.m. now and I felt stupid at this point. The only thing I succeeded in was making myself part of some mundane, avant-garde torture-art. I checked the video. I watched the first few minutes at normal speed, looking for something I couldn't even describe. Then I started skipping ahead. It was as boring as one could imagine.

I lifted the stapler and inspected it again, sighed, and made some coffee. I was grateful for the weekend so I could recover but I couldn’t stop wondering: had the status changed? What if I was meant to receive it? I shook the thought away. My hands were clammy, my head hurt. What did I expect? Was the stapler supposed to evaporate? Or was some interdimensional logistics team supposed to bust through my door and take the stapler by force? Well, if it was meant for me, then the only thing to do was to keep it here and check the status next workday.

**\*\*\***

I arrived at work a bit early on Monday. I was interested to see if the stapler had changed its status at all. I logged into the system and found the stapler: ALLOCATED. Okay, so nothing changed. There didn’t seem to be an obvious timeframe for something ‘allocated’ disappearing—maybe they didn’t always disappear—and things ‘received’ are already gone (where to?). After the stapler letdown, I started to fall back into the rhythms of work. I ignored other strange errors and pressed on with actually doing my job instead of barely doing my job.

A couple days passed until, during a routine count, I encountered something strange. There was a notification that a pallet location was occupied. A couple other guys told me they attempted to resolve the issue but someone, or something, kept flagging it. I assumed someone failed to scan the right barcode, or some new item was incorrectly entered into the system.

The spot was near an exterior wall of the warehouse, off in a corner where old inventory that didn't see much movement was kept. There were a couple damaged pallets, loose packing materials—tape, shrink wrap, cardboard—nothing out of the ordinary. Then I noticed a strange scent. It smelled like a forest after rain, like damp wood and leaves.

There was no pallet in the location. I checked behind the rack and noticed something yellow against the warehouse's white walls. I pulled out my phone and shined the flashlight between the shelving. It looked like a piece of painted drywall, probably left behind from some renovation. I reached through the rack and gave it a push. It didn't budge.

I moved around to the end of the rack to get a better look. The yellow surface continued behind a pile of discarded cardboard, and when I pulled some of it away, I found that it met another wall at a right angle. I figured someone had walled off a small storage area back here. Strange place for it, but whatever.

I pushed the remaining cardboard aside and found an opening between the two walls. It was narrow, but wide enough to walk through. I stepped inside and shined my phone ahead of me. The yellow walls continued several feet beyond the shelving, farther than I would've thought there was room for. I looked back toward the racks, trying to picture where the exterior wall should have been.

At the end of the hallway was a door.

**\*\*\***

I began to sweat a bit and reached out for the handle. It opened to a narrow space, like the entrance to an old apartment building. There was a short stretch of linoleum flooring where it seemed someone started to tile then abruptly stopped. I stood for a moment, unable to progress. I chewed my lip and slowly placed my foot over the threshold. I stepped onto the linoleum and looked deeper into the hall. The yellow walls continued for a stretch, narrowing in until they terminated at a wooden door. I told myself it was an old storage room, some unrenovated space. I took another step. It was warmer than the rest of the warehouse. The air was thick enough to make me nauseous. Moving ahead, I could smell grease and thyme. It was humid. My shirt collar itched, and I pulled it away. It was hard to swallow. I must have been moving slower than I thought. When I looked back, I realized I'd barely made it through the doorway.

At a crawl, I approached the door. I noticed a strip of damaged wallpaper near the doorframe, worn-out linoleum, and a rounded light fixture. I recognized the light, and I thought back to the image on my desk: SERVICE LANDING.

A noise sounded from behind the door, a gentle click, like the flick of a switch. I paused, tried to calm my breath, and listened closely. I could hear a soft scraping sound, something dragging across the floor—furniture, a chair…a body. I froze, wanting to open the door and see what waited in this impossible corner, wanting to turn and run. I was more afraid of the space than of what I’d find behind the door. Before I could make a conscious choice, I noticed the door growing smaller. The yellow walls seemed to draw away from me. I looked down and realized I had been walking backward. My shoes were on the concrete warehouse floor.

I walked quickly back towards the commotion of the warehouse. I saw a co-worker about to get into a forklift and flagged him down. He shrugged at me, but I continued waving him closer.

“Yeah, what?” he said.

“I need to show you something.”

I took him back to the spot and pointed behind the rack. “There. Go…go look behind there.”

He chuckled with annoyance. “A fuckin’ rat or something scare you?” He peered around and let out a long sigh. “You going to tell me what the hell I’m looking for? It’s just boxes and shit, just junk.” He waved his hand at me and walked away.

I chanced a look behind the rack and saw nothing out of the ordinary, just the typical white concrete wall.

Back at my station I logged into my computer and searched for the service landing record.

DESCRIPTION: SERVICE LANDING

STATUS: RECEIVED

**\*\*\***

I sat at my desk and tried to put the pieces together. The Pearl Street stairs had disappeared. They were “received,” and the service landing was “allocated.” So, the physical item is…taken, I guess, from its original location and then received at the warehouse? Then the landing appears in the warehouse. Then it disappears. Clearly the warehouse wasn’t its destination, or at least not its final destination. So where did it go? It didn’t feel right being there, like some cosmic mistake happened. I mean, was it really in the warehouse?

I searched through the records looking for some explanation or any other mention of the service landing. It certainly wasn’t delivered by truck and there was no employee designated to receive it, of course. In any case, I wrote down the info associated with the service landing:

PALLET LOCATION: Q-11-03-29

ID #: 1229-03

The pallet location matched the spot I'd been sent to investigate.

A trip back to Pearl Street was in order, the place where the landing supposedly came from. I arrived to see the exterior staircase still missing from the side of the building, that same stairstep shadow burned into the brick. I took a walk around the building, part of me hoping to see a giant hole in the building and the landing conspicuously uprooted like a rotten tooth, but instead everything looked normal. I waited around until a lady walked by. She had shoulder-length brown hair and wore a cheap-looking pantsuit. She opened the door and went inside. Before the door could close, I hurried up my pace and slipped in the building. It looked like an old apartment. The entrance was bare, just a small mailroom and a staircase leading up to the second floor. The hallway split in two directions, both lined with numbered apartments. The brown doors were worn with time, their gold numbers a bit drab. Each hall was covered in faded yellow wallpaper, the flooring carpeted in a faded olive-green. The place seemed well-kept inside, if a little dated, and well-trafficked enough I could slip in. So, what was I? A home invader?

I walked down the rightmost hall until I reached a narrow corridor with linoleum tiling. Near the doorframe was a damaged strip of wallpaper. I looked up to see a dome light hanging over my head. On the door in front of me was a large “DANGER: DO NOT ENTER” sign. A free-fall to the concrete waited on the other side of that door.

I was lost in thought when the clinking of keys jolted me awake. I turned to see a man holding groceries, his pink-coated daughter clinging to his pantleg as he unlocked his apartment door. Neither of them looked toward the landing. I could hear the distant din of a TV set, the quiet muttering of a talk show host or some mindless reality tv show. I heard a familiar click and the scrape of something dragging across the floor from a nearby apartment. This was it, then, the service landing. How could it be here still? Everything else, the newspaper dispenser, the fence, the stairs, they’d all disappeared, never returned.

Maybe the locations are being copied somehow. An erroneous or intentional duplicate. Maybe these things can exist in two places at once.

Back home, I pored through some old inventory records I’d taken from work. The fence, parking garage, that weird intersection in that neighborhood, the drinking fountain. Was there something I missed this whole time?

**\*\*\***

Why was only 24 feet of the fence being accounted for when the fence was much bigger? What about that portion was important? When I saw it there was nothing there, just rusty chain link. No clear delineation between segments either. How can the system attempt to store a location? Service landing. East access, exterior stairs.

I made a comparison between all the inventory records, checked the location codes and item records to see if there was anything, any kind of hint of what was happening. Nothing. No pattern to what went missing. Just random shit from random places disappearing (or not disappearing) for no reason. What makes one thing a more likely target than another? Why couldn’t the chair I sit on disappear, my TV, table, the door that keeps people out of my apartment—why couldn’t my apartment disappear? I could be preparing a meal in the corner of the warehouse right now and not even know. A coworker could be outside my door now, hesitating to come in, confused that they stumbled upon the stretch of hallway leading to me in my kitchen.

I went on a walk and noticed a bricked window at an old corner store. Moving close, I inspected it, wondered if it was meant to be there. This was the only defunct store on the street. Maybe the store itself wasn’t intended to be here, it was shipped here or received. I walked on and stopped at a vacant lot between two houses, a set of steps missing half its middle step, a metal post missing its sign. I couldn't remember the last time I'd gone outside without looking for something that was missing. Where were these things? They could just be broken or demolished, or they could have been allocated, then received somewhere else.

I rushed back home and threw open the drawers in my desk, my kitchen drawers, I checked the coffee table—I couldn’t find that fucking stapler. Not under the couch or in the bathroom. I found it on my nightstand. Was it still allocated? I used it the other day, but I’d been keeping it by my bed… in case, I guess. I held it above my head briefly, ready to spike it into the ground and shatter it into pieces—would the pieces be allocated? Is it still a stapler if I beat it with a bat until it’s nothing but black dust and metal fragments? If I bleed on it, is it still a stapler? I put it in the drawer of my nightstand.

 

I wasn’t getting anything done at work. My boss sent me a message to verify a few items in the inventory. They were back near that pallet where I found the service landing. My chest felt tight. Procrastinating that task, I scrolled through some old records and kept finding these weird things in the system. A lot of times it was just normal bullshit but some of it… I guess I don’t know what it is. Showing my co-workers led to that “I just work here” response. I got hung up on one, though.

I’d fixed a couple routine errors that were simple to dismiss. Outlet covers in this side of the warehouse when they should be in that side of the warehouse. The one that led me down a rabbit hole was a rubber hose manufacturer in Utah:

DESCRIPTION: UTAH RUBBER HOSE

LOCATION: UTAH

I spent the day calling, leaving voicemail, digging back into the records—why did we track something so specific in another state? Eventually the company called back and confirmed that they had sent the order to us and, yes, we were keeping it in our warehouse. It turned out the location field was never updated. I verified where the hoses were stored, corrected the issue, and the discrepancy vanished.

I’m just tired. I don’t know what’s important anymore.  

I knocked on my boss’s office door. “I can’t do this anymore.” I looked at the floor while I spoke to him.

“Scanning boxes is too stressful?” He chuckled a bit. After realizing I wasn’t going to respond he said, “take a few days to think it over. The worst time to quit is when you’re most stressed. Can’t think clearly.” He sent me on an early lunch. I left and didn’t return.

About two months passed and it was good. I took some time away, burned through some savings, but it was time I needed to clear my head. I could even take regular walks without inspecting broken steps and vacant lots. I even met a girl and had a few interviews lined up. The whole thing was behind me. Really no use dwelling on it because I wouldn’t figure it out, I didn’t even have the means to figure it out anymore.

One day, I drove to the grocery store, chatting with my girl on the phone. I parked, told her bye, got out of the car, and snagged a shopping cart from the corral. I passed by a concrete wall and stepped into a deep puddle. This area of the parking lot seemed to have poor drainage. Ahead, water trickled from a runoff pipe into the parking lot. It collected in a puddle, then ran down the slight decline in little tributaries. On the pipe was a white label with a location code: Loc: Q-11-03-29. That was the pallet location where I’d found the service landing. I thought of stepping into the hallway that shouldn’t have been there. I could almost smell the grease and thyme.

I looked at the pipe again. I thought of a name:

PARKING LOT DRAINAGE PIPE.
