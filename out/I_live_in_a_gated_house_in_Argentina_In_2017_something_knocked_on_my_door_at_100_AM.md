I’ve always been a pretty quiet guy. Back in 2017, I was around 12 years old. I wasn't the popular kid at school or anything, just that quiet, likeable fella in the back who would occasionally say something funny, and they would either laugh at me or with me. Since I was kind of introverted, I spent a ton of my free time on the internet.

At the time, we lived in a normal house in Argentina. The neighborhood was mostly quiet, but it was right on the edge of a high-crime area. There was a public hospital about three blocks away, so hearing sirens at night was totally normal for us. Because of where we lived, security was a big deal. Our house was built like a fortress: a tall concrete wall with sharp iron spikes (rejas) on top, and a heavy front gate that my dad locked with a massive deadbolt every single night before going to sleep. Once that gate was locked, it was physically impossible for anyone to reach our wooden front door unless they had a ladder or an angle grinder.

This happened on a Friday night. My family was already asleep and I stayed up late in the living room, watching Season 7 of The Walking Dead. I was already completely spooked because earlier that afternoon, I’d fallen down a YouTube rabbit hole watching creepypastas. Specifically, I watched one about eyeless kids who knock on your door at midnight to steal your eyes. I was basically using a zombie show to try and distract myself from my own imagination, ironic as that is.

Then, right around 1:00 AM, the hospital sirens down the street suddenly cut out. The whole neighborhood went dead silent. And right in the middle of that silence, I heard it.

Three sharp, heavy knocks on our front door.

I completely froze. My chest got so tight I could barely breathe. In any normal house, you’d probably assume it’s a lost neighbor or a teenager playing a prank. But here, with a locked, spiked iron perimeter, it made zero sense. In all the years we lived there, no one had ever come to our house at that hour. Ever. It wasn't the house settling, and it wasn't the wind. It was clearly someone's knuckles hitting the wood. Multiple times.

I completely panicked. I started sweating like crazy, grabbed the TV remote, slammed the power button, and literally sprinted straight to my bedroom. I hid under the covers and just stared into the dark until I finally fell asleep.

I never told my parents about it. As the years went on, I honestly just stopped caring because life got busy, and I eventually convinced myself that my 12-year-old brain had just hallucinated the whole thing because of the scary videos. I’m 21 now, we still live in the same house, and I had completely buried that memory.

Until last week.

My dad and I were having a beer out on the patio, just talking about how the neighborhood has changed over the years. Out of nowhere, he looked at the front gate and sighed. He said, "You know, the only thing that still bothers me about this place is that night back in 2017. Right before the hospital expanded."

My stomach instantly dropped, but I tried to keep my voice casual. "What night?"

"I don't know the exact date, but it was a Friday," he said. "I woke up around two in the morning because I heard the front gate rattling. I turned on my phone's flashlight and went outside, thinking someone was trying to climb the rejas. But when I checked the gate, the heavy deadbolt was still completely thrown. It was locked tight."

He rubbed the back of his neck, and he had this genuinely confused look on his face that I've never seen before.

"But when I turned around to go back inside, I looked at the front door," he whispered. "There were no scratches or damage. But right in the middle of the wood, at about the height of a small kid's chest, the varnish was totally gone. There were three perfectly clean, round circles worn straight down into the bare wood. Like someone had spent a lifetime knocking on the exact same spot."

My dad thinks it was just some weird chemical reaction from the humidity. He has no idea that an hour before he went outside, I heard those exact three knocks from the inside of the house. He doesn't know the gate was locked the entire time. I’m sitting in that exact same living room right now writing this. It’s a Friday night, it’s almost 1:00 AM, and the hospital sirens down the street just stopped.
