This happened about a decade-and-a-half ago, in a rural county known for its university specializing in STEM. I was completing the final semester of my degree and living off campus in an apartment with my roommate, Brady. 

We were an odd pair. I had been a sloshed-on-a-weeknight frat boy and Brady was…himself. He didn’t say a lot, especially in groups. For my first couple of years, we only really knew him as “Side Shields.” He’d sit at the front of the class, nonstop tapping on his brick of a laptop, then send out a Google Doc of the day’s notes to the whole class roster, inviting everyone to, “pls add and edit, thx.” 

I was underwater on a two week transistor lab when I actually used one of Brady’s docs. I’d partnered with some do-nothing douchebag and had an unfortunate habit of smoking my transistors. It was a day out from the due date, and I was frantically posting stickies on how to take certain measurements. Brady directly emailed and asked me to meet him at the lab that night. 

That dude absolutely saved my ass. As repayment, I goaded him into letting me buy him a shitty steak from Applebee’s, and we sort of became friends after. As my junior year rolled around, and I got increasingly tired of riding the curve for every class, I had a little quarter-life, come-to-Jesus moment. I was sick of the frat house, didn’t really even like two thirds of my “friends,” actually wanted to be *good* at something. It happened at the time Brady was looking for a roommate for his cheap, off-campus apartment. I grabbed the opportunity.

I think I changed more that year than I would in the next ten. My advisor was so shocked by the improvement, he went overtime to help me secure a local co-op. Starting my senior year, I even volunteered with an after school program. That’s still probably one of my favorite things I’ve ever done.

It’s also where the most bizarre incident of my life begins. Every Monday and Wednesday, I’d head to the basement of a local grade school, where they’d corralled the students still left after 3:00pm into a multipurpose room lit like an interrogation chamber. I was instructed to help the kids with their math homework, or barring that, play “math games” with them: chess, Equations, Candyland (that it “involves counting” was the technical justification on that one). 

We were supervised only by a disinterested teacher's aide called Ms. Helen. Within fifteen minutes, I would usually be wildly off subject with my little hoard of glue sniffers. And I use that term with the utmost affection - as a former latchkey kid myself, those lil shits hit me right in the feels.

For kicks one day, I told a couple of kids eating Cosmic Brownies that the real Little Debbie was my great-grandmother, and I was the last scion of the Little Debbie snack cake empire. The joke kept building, until we'd weaved a shared fever dream where as a child I'd been forced to bake endless miniature cakes for my domineering relatives, in the process gaining both a virtuosity and loathing for the art of baking. Eventually, I ran away and vowed never to pick up my rolling pin again. Which, conveniently, was why I could never demonstrate my incredible skill.

As a farewell gesture, I had planned to bring-in an actual, from scratch cake for the kids; a few slick YouTube videos had given me an unearned sense of competency at baking. Unfortunately, their end-of-year was the same as mine, and given I was also pounding the job search pavement, I was a bit overleveraged. Which is a weasel way of saying I waited until the eleventh hour.

Literally. 11PM on a Tuesday night, there I was, trying to bake a damn cake without any baking powder.

I frantically knocked on Brady’s door, thinking by some miracle he might have some tucked away within his neat stacks of canned goods. No luck. Brady’s hobbies were a little too niche for baking. He did, however, offer to drive me on a late night grocery run, probably knowing I’d indulged in a few too many celebratory shots earlier that evening. I thanked him profusely for the offer. 

I was almost sure he’d ask to take my rustbucket of an Altima, but he grabbed his keys and asked where we’d be going. Brady had a sweetass car: a restored Chevy Chevelle he’d inherited from a dead uncle. Brady probably knew that car down to the last valve, but damn if he didn’t avoid taking it out on the road if he could.

Here was our predicament: all the nearby grocery stores were closed by that hour. The only place open would be the 24hr Walmart roughly 15 miles away. Usually you’d take Route 73 the whole way down, but that time of night there was always at least one cop car hanging around, jonesing to pull over any kids in hopes of finding even a sniff of drugs.  

When I mentioned Walmart, Brady sort of drummed his fingers on the table for a moment. He asked if I ever went the Twin Foxes Road way. I said, No, I’d never even heard of it. He explained it was a back road that ran pretty much parallel to 73, which you could use to circumvent traffic during the day. It wouldn’t save us any time now, but we’d avoid any harassment. 

It was close to midnight when we finally set off. A few turns down unfamiliar streets, and suddenly there was nothing but a vast field flanking the passenger side. I’m still jarred how, when you’re out in the American heartland, that comfortable, urban monotony can just disappear in a blink.  

I was lounging back in my seat, letting my arm hang out of the open window. It was one of those cool, spring nights where the rushing air feels like dipping your hand in a river's current. Brady was going at an irritatingly steady speed.

I was yakking at him about “opening ‘er on up” when we approached a stretch of wooded road. Brady must've switched his brights on, because I remember seeing everything ahead in an eerie spotlight. There was suddenly just this ominous aura. The car fell uncomfortably silent.

Brady craned his neck to peek in the rear view mirror, and I followed his gaze to see two headlights distantly behind us. Unlucky coincidence someone else happened to be driving that way. Brady glanced up again. The lights were accelerating towards us at an uncomfortable clip.

“Aw fuck, we just had to get some blitzed out redneck who doesn't wanna share the road,” I said as I hoisted myself towards the window. Buzzed me never missed an excuse to shout out of car windows. I leaned out, and there was…nothing. Just the trailing void and the wind against my face.

I turned to slide back into my seat when a flash from the side view mirror struck my eyes, followed by a violent *whomp* that sent me into the dash. 

“Motherfucker!” I let loose as I grappled back into my seat while whipping my head up to see Brady white-knuckle gripping the steering wheel, face completely frozen forward. “That methhead rear-ended us!” 

Instead of pulling off to the shoulder, Brady lurched the car into the opposing lane. The headlights followed us. Brady moved again. The headlights followed again.

At this point I was screaming at him to just pull the fucking car over, but it was like Brady was in a trance. The Chevy must’ve been pushing past 90mph, sidewinding through the road. The lights behind didn’t seem to move, like they were fixed five feet back from our rear. I felt sick and panicky, and in bewilderment I leaned half out of the window again to…honestly, I don’t know what I thought I could’ve done. Shame an already deranged driver? It didn’t matter, though. I was hanging out of the Chevy to witness only the red glow of its tail lights. What the fuck. How was this possible.

I *swear* I remember seeing the back of Brady’s car jerk forward as if something physically smashed it, but the whole incident is a blur. Suddenly I was being thrown backwards, then I was wrenched back into the car by Brady’s iron grip on my shirt. I didn’t have time to collect myself before I was eating the dashboard again. This time I stayed put, almost hugging the dash, bracing for the next impact as I felt the Chevy jostle over an uneven piece of road. 

Brady said my name in a flat voice. I looked over at him, but he didn’t say anything else. The Chevy was slowing down. I cautiously peeked at the side view window. The headlight was gone. As I eased back into my seat, I saw the night sky outside my window, and soon the glow of a shopping center was on the horizon.

Brady and I didn’t say anything until he had parked in the Walmart lot. We got out and surveyed the damage. The rear end of the Chevy was *fucked*. That shit wasn’t dented, it was crumpled. I started apologizing to Brady, offering money I didn’t have to fix it, but he just gave me this confused look and asked what I had to be sorry about. I never knew if Brady was oblivious or like the chillest guy ever. Regardless, we went into Walmart and got my stupid baking powder. At this point, why not?

I was nervous as hell to drive back home. I’d half-heartedly tried to convince Brady to call a ride, but he wanted to get his car home. Prowling cop cars be damned, we hopped straight on 73. 

We got pulled over pretty much immediately. It was approaching 1:00 in the morning, and we were a couple of kids driving around with our back end practically hanging off. The officer was a stern, middle-aged guy with a local accent. He gave us some smartass remark like, “You boys run into some trouble, huh?” as Brady handed him his information. Guileless Brady answered, Yes, in fact we had been repeatedly rear-ended by an aggressive driver on a nearby backroad while on our way to Walmart. 

The cop froze for a passing moment. Then he handed Brady back his license and insurance card and replied, “Twin Foxes Road?” Brady answered affirmative. The cop hemmed over his words for a second before finally saying, “Better to stay on the main routes this time of night, son.” Couldn’t have agreed more, officer. 

Long story short, I didn’t end up baking that homemade cake. Before volunteering that afternoon, I picked up a couple packages of Cosmic Brownies and adhered them together with canned frosting to make some sort of snack cake monstrosity. The children loved it. Of course they did - grade school children would lick a pile of glass for a sugar rush.

Brady and I never really talked about the incident on Twin Foxes, save for that night we got home. Neither of us could sleep, so we sat up on our crappy futon all night. I asked Brady why he didn’t just pull over. He said he didn’t know. “Survivor’s instinct” switched on and something in his lizard brain was telling him to get the hell off that stretch of road as fast as he could. 

“Once we hit that little bridge, the lights stopped following us,” he then explained to me. I accepted that we both made it out unhurt (same could not be said for the Chevy, RIP), it was too weird to tell other people, and that was kind of that.    

In a few weeks we were both graduated, got jobs out of state, and kind of fell off with each other. Not in a bad way, just the way things naturally go when you’ve got two guys with hectic careers who don’t much care for social media.

About three months ago, I ran into Brady at a work conference. He had a beard and an extra 30lbs, but he was pretty much the same guy I’d known 15 years ago. Good ol’ Brady. After a short catch up, we made plans to, for old times sake, have dinner at an Applebee’s. 

We talked about the usual things: relationships, kids, jobs, family drama, absurd housing costs, why I was drinking a Dr. Pepper instead of a beer (5 years sober, y’all). Eventually we got to the good stuff, our glorious college days. I asked Brady if he ever got that old Chevy Chevelle fixed back up. 

No, unfortunately, he told me. He’d moved to the East Coast for a job and offloaded it for a pittance onto some car enthusiast. “At least he probably took real good care of her,” Brady shrugged. I then got to what I’d really wanted to ask. “How the hell did you explain the damage to insurance?”

“Hit and run. They rejected my claim,” Brady said with a genuine laugh. I laughed, too, but there was still the question digging in my craw.

“So what the hell do you think actually happened to us that night?” I finally asked. “I mean, we both experienced it, so it’s not like it was a hallucination. Drones weren’t readily available yet. It doesn’t make any sense.”

“It doesn’t make any sense,” Brady agreed, “But I think the main thing is, don’t drive on Twin Foxes Road after midnight.”
