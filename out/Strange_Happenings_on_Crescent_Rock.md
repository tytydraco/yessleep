I suppose the end of my world began as many grandiose events do, with a seemingly innocuous, insignificant decision. A butterfly, whose wings flapped at just the right time in the right place to bring about the beginnings of a hurricane. That was how the end of my world began. With a simple yes.

*Lightkeeper needed, 45,000-dollar sign on bonus, one month tenure, live in position.*

That was just about the sum of the ad I saw, and the position seemed like a perfect way to cobble my life back together.

In my twenties, I worked as a park ranger and fire lookout. In that time, I quenched my thirst for adventure, experienced the majesty of the outdoors, and saw and heard a few strange and unexplainable things.

But this was not the most fulfilling course for my life. My degree was in marine biology, and frankly, it felt wasted in the park services. Once more, when I came home after my last stint as a fire lookout, I found that my house was no longer mine and neither was my fiancé.

To say I felt lost would be an understatement. My life had crumbled apart before me, and I was left to try and assemble pieces that would no longer fit.

That was when I saw the advert, and I had felt a renewed feeling of adventure, of mystery. It was sketchy to be sure, but what was an adventure without a little danger.

The interview was the first sign that I would be making a most terrible mistake. In terms of the content of the interview, it was pretty mundane and run of the mill. We made nice and he explained my role in the position. But when I looked at him, all I could see in his eyes were crazy. Wide, unblinking eyes shot me up and down as he methodically laid out every detail and fine point of the time I would spend on the lighthouse known as Crescent Rock. 

He told me that my role as lightkeeper ought to be relatively easy. My main responsibility was to make sure to keep the automated systems in the lighthouse running. If anything was to go awry, it'd fall to me to "unfuck" the situation, although he assured me that there would be maintenance manuals on sight. Apart from that, I would also be responsible for keeping the overall island in good condition, going outside to record or note any erosions of the rocky shore, and to generally note down any oddities or points of concern I may come across in a logbook situated within the lightkeepers house.

Still, I figured he was the same as me. Just a guy who fell on hard times trying to get back on his feet. I didn't want to mess this up for me or him.

Boy, how wrong I was.

I got a call the very next day saying I had gotten the position, my second red flag. I would take off for the lighthouse from a sleepy New England township up in Maine.

At the time, I was staying with my parents in New Hampshire, so the trip out to the sleepy hollow wasn't very far.

In no more than three hours, I found myself in this sleepy hollow that felt as though it was taken straight from the mind of Lovecraft. small farms on rolling hills gave way to a quaint seaside town adorned with a modestly robust main street along the pier, with maze-like streets, alleys, and avenues jutting and crossing from this main thoroughfare. 

I sat on the pier, staring out into the foggy haze that served to guard us landlubbers from what lay beyond the shore. But as I stared, it was as though something took shape within the cold fog. Something large, imposing a terrifying yet awe inspiring visage. But when the shape broke through, I sighed in relief as it turned out to be just a boat. And not just any boat, but *the Apostle.* The boat that would take me to my new home, my new adventure, and the end of my world.

The ship rocked onto the pier and docked, with the captain waving me over. I grabbed my luggage and ran to the boat, tossing my bags onto the small wooden boat and hauling myself aboard.

The boat churned on choppy waters as we sailed to the island, and as we made our way, I looked up out of the fog out towards the lighthouse. It looked like an anglerfish in the deep. Its bright light beckoned me further, but its looming figure, the jagged rocks that encircled the island made clear I was not welcome. Its bright light paled in wake of its ominous, almost malicious figure as it stood in solitary within the fog.

A chill down ran down my spine as we docked, and I set about to acclimate and familiarize myself with the island. I spent the day reading the maintenance manual, scouting the cliffs, and unpacking my things.

I would spend the next week cementing myself in the idle routines of my job, from the well-being of this ancient, dusty lighthouse to the catalog of odd, coal black seabirds' I'd never seen. Birds who spent their time simply staring at me with wide, knowing eyes.

It was around the end of my first week that they came. One day as I stared out to the unending foggy blue, I felt as though someone was looking at me. That's when I looked to my left. Maybe 80 yards away, stood a hooded figure. I could not see their face, but I knew they were staring me down with a hatred and vitriol that brought tears to my eyes.

From then on, I would constantly see them standing as the lighthouse stood. In solitude, exuding malice from them. They would be tall as oaks or as small as any man. They terrified me, but more than that, they drove me mad.

I could not know if they were real, I did not know if the danger on this island rested outside these old walls or within my fragile mind. So I turned to the logbook. Admittedly, I never looked at the logbook. There was nothing for miles, so I surely thought there was no use for such a thing. But I truly regret not looking when I had the chance.

Hundreds of pages filled with the ramblings of madmen, of the damned. The birds, the hooded figures, whispers, sounds, so many things filled the confines of the pages. My stomach turned and my bile ran acrid. And that night I dreamt it. As did all the others.

The altar.

Under this accursed island I knew there lay an altar. I dreamt it, and the vile things done upon it. And then I awoke to find them standing in a ring, the hooded figures stood in a circle around my lighthouse, my sanctuary. Whether by madness or courage, I grabbed a rusty axe and ran out of the lighthouse and into the cold and ever foggy island.

But there was no one.

No figures. No waves. No sounds. Just fog and obfuscation. The understanding that nothing was ever as it seemed.

Then my body began to move of its own accord.

I marched, axe in tow, towards an end I had no desire to meet. Tears welled in my eyes. I began to hyperventilate as I climbed down steps I knew did not belong. I reached the bottom of stairs leading to a path I knew was never there in my time on the island. A path that led to a cave.

Within the confines of this cave stood the terrible altar, bordered by seawater. The smell of rotten fish and iron filled my nostrils as this invisible force put me upon this altar. Against my will I raised the axe to my neck, and with tears now streaming uncontrollably, my hands made a cut into my neck. Just enough to draw blood.

My blood flowed through grooves within the altar, spilling into the seawater below. It was if that blood was grabbed, taken by some thing as it glided into the deep rather than dance statically in the water.

Then the call came. A hellish whale call vibrating my very soul, shaking the very island, the very fabric of my known reality. It was only then that I came back to my senses, and I ran right back to the lighthouse, only stopping to vomit on my way back.

It's been more than a month now. My relief boat hasn't come and I doubt they will, the hooded figures encroach more and more upon my shelter, and every night that horrid whale call rends my very being apart, shaking the walls and earth in that terrible song. It's as if the very souls I know now trapped within this lighthouse are calling to me. Begging me to join them in their hellish choir.

I will die here, I know this, but as I see the wicked figures romp within the fog, I feel it is my duty to warn the world.

No one should step foot on Crescent Rock. Not if they don't have a death wish.
