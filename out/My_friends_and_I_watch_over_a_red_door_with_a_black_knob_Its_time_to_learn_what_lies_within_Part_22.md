At night, Stillwater felt like a different town. You would think the streets paved with decaying leaves and litter would look sketchier than they already did beneath the blanket of shadow, but somehow, it had the opposite effect. The amber streetlights made it hard to see the trash in the gutters, and the golden glow dusted the walls of quiet homes, casting the crumbling monuments to a ‘suburbia long past’ in a warm, nostalgic light. Above, the sky wasn’t the black, looming abyss that made children think monsters had come out to hunt, it was a deep blue blanket of rhinestones and diamond dust lit by the gleaming midnight sun, the perfect backdrop for crickets and frogs to serenade the still air with their songs.

That was the best part. The silence. Beneath the light, Stillwater was alive like a roused hornet’s nest. Cars blared their horns at anyone stopped longer than a second at a stop sign, couples argued aloud in their front lawns about trivial things, and there was always a rhythm of angry dog barking rippling over the town. Night was bereft of that. Night was a place that, to anyone passing through, might be able to convince them that Stillwater wasn’t hell on earth.

That night, I had gotten a text from Kait at nearly 1 in the morning telling me to meet her behind the school, and that she needed to talk to me about something. We were only a few months out of high school, and I had work in only four hours, but I hadn’t been sleeping anyway, and admittedly, I would never pass up an opportunity to sit with her.

Only the crunch of dead leaves gave me away as I crossed town toward the school. When I made it, I trekked around the building's side and left the amber pools aiming to expose me. There among the dark valley between the imposing brick building and the looming woods, a single light glowed softly like a drawn out heartbeat, pulsing brighter and softer every few seconds.

I followed the cigarette cherry like a fire in the flood until I was close enough to see Kait’s face in its glow. She sat on a swing at the playground and used her tip-toes to gently rock herself in place. Her attention was bought by the stars currently, and she didn’t waver when I stepped closer and took the swing next to me. Instead, she just held out the cigarette to acknowledge my presence.

I took it to steal a drag, then joined her rhythm back and forth on the creaking chains. It was quiet for a while, and I reached out to brush her hair from the side of her cheek. She looked at me, and I gave her the cig back.

“You okay?”

She nodded, “Yeah… Sorry to call you out so late.”

“It’s alright, I wasn’t asleep anyway,” I told her, “What’s up? What’s the emergency?”

She shook her head, “There isn’t one. I just… I made a decision tonight, and figured it was better now than later to tell you.”

The creaking of my swing stopped, “Oh, um… yeah?”

Kait took a long pull from the cigarette, burning away nearly a quarter inch, “I’m getting out of here, Jessie. I have enough saved, and tonight, I—” she stopped herself, as if whatever was to follow didn’t matter to the grand scheme of her words. Nobody needed justification for leaving Stillwater, least of all me. “This week I’m going to find a place over in Melbourne. I need to be as far away from here as I can without leaving entirely.”

I swallowed hard. The claim made me ecstatic for her after all her years of yearning to escape, but there was one problem, “Isn’t Melbourne over an hour away?”

She nodded slowly and dug patterns into the sand bark dust with her toes, “Yeah…”

She didn’t follow that up with anything, and I knew why. She was waiting to see my reaction. She wanted to know my honest thoughts up front.

I tried to stall, “That’s great for you,” I told her, “Are you going to be able to afford that?”

She nodded, “Wages will be higher out there once I get situated with a job.”

“Damn, Kait. That’s awesome.”

She turned to me, and I could see her eyes pouring me over in the dying cherry of her cigarette.

“I’m not going to see you as much anymore…” I finally said, unable to hold it any longer. It wasn’t something that I had wanted to do. I knew if there was any reason Kait would have doubts, it would be if I sewed them, but I just couldn’t help it. Seeing those eyes that I knew were soon to slip away pushed me to the edge, however.

She burned the last of her cig to her fingertips, then dropped it into the grave she’d made with her foot before burying it. After a long pause, she said, “Come with me.”

The words were both everything I wanted and the last thing I needed. My stomach fluttered and my heart began to race and my hands gripped tighter around the chains keeping me afloat. At first, I just laughed nervously the way I always did to play things off. Once I gave it a few seconds and faced Kait once more, her expression hadn’t changed, still stone cold serious. There was no need for elaboration, the question was point blank.

Go with her, or stay here.

Suddenly, in a flash, all the anxieties and fears that lived in my thick skull roused, slithering down past the lump in my throat to coil in my guts, devouring the butterflies that had only gotten moments to live and making me feel sick. I didn’t even want to think about my answer. I wanted to just blurt yes and be done with it. It wasn’t so easy, though.

“I can’t do that to my mom, Kait,” I lied, the most valid excuse I could find.

“You don’t owe her anything, Jessie,” She’d returned. Already, her face had switched from its stark stare into something with just a hint of worry and desperation that the question might not be so clear cut after all.

“I’m all she has left, Kait. If I go, she’ll be buried by the expenses.”

“Those expenses aren’t yours. Your dad racked them up, and I’m sure she helped. You’ve had to live with their bullshit for this long, don’t give yourself to it now when your life is finally your own, Jess.”

“It’s not that simple.”

“Yes it is. You *know* it is.”

“Maybe if I had time to make sure she’s settled but in a week? Kait, I can’t just pull the rug like that. Can you just give this a little more—”

“*No,*” Kait said quickly, standing from her swing and placing her hand to her head, “You know how it goes in this town, Jessie. People say ‘tomorrow’ all the time, and that day never comes. If we don’t pull the trigger now, we never will, and we’ll rot in this place alongside everyone else.”

I couldn’t help but snicker fondly, “You say ‘we’ like you already figured I would do this with you.”

Kait didn’t seem so amused. She tossed her hands and stared me dead in the eye, “Well, yeah, I guess I thought that it was what you wanted too. Maybe I was wrong…” She slipped her arms around herself and looked off into the forest, clearly gripped by some sort of embarrassment.

I quickly stood and moved toward her, taking her arms and smiling down, “Kait, I do want this, it’s just so soon…”

She shook her head and wiped at tears that I hadn’t even noticed, “I don’t think you want this in the same way that I do…”

It wasn’t a pointed remark, it was a new statement. This was only punctuated when she lifted her face to look at me, and her gaze was full of fire and passion. My mouth felt dry, and the hands that were holding her tingled with numbness. This wasn’t just her asking me to leave with her. This was her asking if I loved her enough to take the leap at the same time. A level of love that the two of us had always known was there, but had never acted on.

She was wrong. I did want this exactly the way she did. If there was anyone in the world I wanted to run away with, it was Kaitlynn. I wanted to wake up holding her every day in a place far from our past, slowly building something new free from everything that had broken me in the first place. But that very brokenness meant my guts were still twisted with serpents instead of butterflies, and so instead of just telling her that outright, I held my tongue. I couldn’t bring myself to confess those stupid fears, and I just stood there like a silent fool.

I still wanted her to know though. How much I cared for her. How desperately in love I was. Her eyes were still glued on mine, and I found myself pulled into their gravity. I pulled her closer and leaned in, moving my nose to slip beside hers. Just before our lips could meet, however, she pulled away with a shuddering gasp.

“Please don’t. Please don’t make me stay.”

Her hands gripped my jacket like I was already nothing more than a memory too painful to hold onto.

“If you’re coming with me, I want nothing more. But if you can’t leave? If you aren’t sure?” Her head shook in a way that made her nose nuzzle mine, making my heart ache and crack. Her voice broke with a small sob, “If you kiss me, I won’t be strong enough. And I have to get out of this place, Jessie… I have to.”

I wish I would have kissed her in that moment, despite her pleas. I knew it wasn’t true—Kait was too headstrong to give up, and eventually her drive would push her to escape whether I went with her or not. I wasn’t enough to stay for, and I knew it.

No, I wish I would have pressed in because maybe it would have been *me* who changed my mind. Perhaps that small act would have been a powerful enough declaration to push through the cold, hard walls of my brain and speak reason to me. We could have left together that weekend and never looked back.

Instead, I did as she asked. I pulled away and bowed my head in shame with a nod. She let me hold her in my grasp for a few more seconds before pulling away herself. Neither of us had anymore words to say to one another, so we just stood for a while in silence, letting the crickets count the seconds away.

“If you change your mind, call me, okay?” Kait finally spoke. There was no more desperation or hope in her tone. The sentence was mostly out of courtesy. She knew I had made up my mind.

She left me there, and I never bothered to call. Come the following week, Kaitlynn was gone, and I had doomed myself to a life in Stillwater where I decided I belonged.

I felt a similar sting in my stomach as our caravan of cars headed down the mountain away from the Red Manor. The knife twisted when we hit the crossroads by the old mill bridge, and I watched Lacey’s car turn north and split away from us, taking her and Kait somewhere far away. Once again I’d let Kait go, but hopefully this time, it was the right decision.

Even so the memory was like a bad itch in my brain, incessant and irritating. It made my knuckles grip the wheel tight in frustration and the road blur beneath watery eyes. The weight of regret and hindsight was far too heavy on my shoulders, and it almost made it hard to do what needed to be done.

Carly and Bryce split off not long after, taking the next road through the mountains toward the nearest major city. In the rear windshield, I could see Bryce peer back to make sure I was truly staying my course toward Stillwater, or maybe he was just making sure I was still okay. I hoped that if I was remembered after all of this, he would be able to forgive me about lying on both counts.

I continued onward just long enough to make sure that they wouldn’t see me turn around in their rearview, then pulled off to the side of the road with a deep breath. I gave myself till the chimes of the first hour rang out over the mountainside to get the rest of my emotions collected, then I whipped a U-turn and started back up the mountain.

There was a lot to be done before I sealed myself behind the door. The first order of business was to get things ready for the sheriff. While my plan was no longer to take the corpse to his station, , I was still going to make sure it got to him. Once everything was ready, I would place the call for him to drive out to mile marker 55, where my truck would be waiting with everything he needed.

The body itself would be ample proof that there was something greater going on here than just a big hoax—especially if they sent samples of the thing to any sort of lab in the area. Based on the ink-blood of things from the halls, I was certain nothing on this earth would match its forensics.

I still needed insurance, however. Just dropping a body for them to find didn’t explain what was going on to the authorities, and the top priority was to make sure there was no confusion, and the door didn’t get opened again. That meant I needed to leave behind the right instructions and guidelines to ensure that, unless it was someone capable of containing the disaster, the red door was to be left alone.

To start, I pulled a sketchbook out of my glove box and pulled out a pencil. I flipped past the dozens of sketches and doodles I’d done through the years to pursue a dream that was never meant to be, then I began to write on a blank page every detail of our story that I could think of.

From the beginning, it truly all sounded crazy. If I wasn’t leaving a piece of evidence behind to back it up, I was certain nobody would ever heed such a warning, creepy mansion aside. Regardless, I explained how we found the door and all the rules that went along with it, then scribbled down its origins and what its intended purpose was. There was heavy emphasis in the log that the goal of ‘paradise’ it was used for had clearly ended only in agony. Lastly, I specified what happened if someone was taken into the halls by a beast, as well as what had happened to all the victims before us.

That last part was the hardest. With everything we’d learned, I wanted to leave some sort of legacy behind. For Casey and Mindy and Lucy. Even just their first names—I wanted some part of them to live on past the doors consumption, even if nobody would be able to know who they truly were. I refrained, however.

There couldn’t be any evidence of anyone who was already lost, not even myself. I made sure the letter was vague as to who wrote it, only that it was a person who had lived to tell the door’s tale. If there was a link to someone who’s thread had been erased, there was a good chance the officers might pick up the page, become seized by the witches spell, then drive off with their minds fogged over, unable to process any of the evidence that, as far as their minds could fathom, didn’t exist.

If left vague, however, I had a feeling that the spell couldn’t take hold. It needed a soul as a catalyst to stun the brain, and if there was no person attached, only words on a page and a demon in a trunk, they should be able to process the door and its functions even with no tie to it.

To make sure of this, I also pulled all of my information from the truck and burned it. Anything with my name on it, registration, insurance papers, leftover recipes—any evidence of my former existence—I reduced it to ash. I even stripped my plates in case the police ran them and tossed them into the woods where no one would find them.

That was the next task—the woods. When we’d first found this place, there was camouflage blocking anyone who was not linked to the threads from seeing the way through. To make sure that the police didn’t get lost looking for the place only a few feet from my truck, I grabbed all the rope I had for tying cargo, then laced it to my hitch, running the line up the driveway from tree to tree as far as it went before tying it off. From where the rope stopped, you could just barely make out the red manor through the trees, and it should have been more than enough distance to break through the spell.

After that, there wasn’t much else to handle. Everything that I needed to prep for beyond the trees was taken care of, and all that remained now was the house. I gave one last look down the driveway, through the portal back to the world I was leaving behind, then traveled up the rest of the way.

Before entering the manor, I moved alongside its chipped, rickety slats toward the garden shed. Overhead, dark clouds had begun to set in, and from them softly drifted down the first snow of winter. The flakes were delicate and quiet as they wisped through the cliff side breeze, and before I entered the shed, I took a moment to gaze off over the Appalachians.

For all the anxiety and sorrow I felt in that moment, there was a moment of peace watching that gentle mist fall over the rolling pines. The world hadn’t been very kind to me, but there was still so much beauty to it that I was happy to have lived to see, and my only regret was not living to see more of it. I found it so foolish that Adeline and her sisters would commit acts so heinous just to escape this place and create one of their own.

In the shed, I found what I was looking for: red paint used to touch up the house. Taking two cans of it, I moved back toward the porch just as the second hour’s bells chimed. Only one left before I needed to be on the other side of the door.

There were no brushes in the shed alongside the paint, so I had to get creative with my work. Hacking the lids off the paint, I dipped rags into it then started from the entryway, writing on the floors, walls, and furniture wherever I could fit: *DANGER! DO NOT ENTER! TURN BACK!*

The writing only looked more sporadic and insane thanks to my method of brush, and I made quite a mess of the stuff, slopping it all over myself and splashing it on the floor, but that only helped the effect.

By the time I was done, even I got chills of dread staring back and looking at the halls, its once fancy wallpaper now desecrated and screaming for guests to beware. I knew for certain that if I had entered the place and seen the messages on the wall our first time through, I would have had the good sense not to open the door.

In the basement, I continued my art show. It was harder with the floors already painted horrible shades of gore, but over the black stains, I was able to make the message clear: *DO NOT OPEN. NO MATTER WHAT. DANGER. YOU WILL DIE.*

By then, my cans were running low, and my hands were stained the exact same color as the coffin lid that’d soon close over me. I eyed it with malice and only the smallest sense of satisfaction, knowing that even if they were going to get their talons on me today, there would be no third chance for the sisters to escape it should their plan fail again. I hoped it did, and that they choked on the threads they’d string from my corpse.

And that was that. Only one last order of buisness. Moving back upstairs, I pulled a pack of cigarettes from my coat and lit one up, leaning against Mindy’s car and pulling out my phone. Already, the snow had increased and begun to stick, but thankfully nothing that would prevent passage up the mountain.

My paint crusted hands smeared my screen as I unlocked the device and dialed the sheriff's office, listening to it ring between puffs on the cigarette. My heart thumped as I realized this was the last conversation I’d ever have with another human being.

I was happy when the sheriff actually answered instead of Stella, “Stillwater Sheriff’s office, what can I do for ya’?”

“Hey sheriff,” I started. My fingertips burned, and I didn’t even realize I’d already smoked my cig to its butt in a few long drags. I flicked it to the snow and continued, “It’s jessie.”

His tone grew softer, “Oh, howdy, son. How are things going? You get everything sorted out with your insurance the other day?”

I smiled that he had remembered, “Yeah, um, I did. But, I’m actually calling about something else.”

“What is it?”

I looked off down the driveway, “There’s uh… an emergency up on the mountain. I was out driving at mile marker 55, and I found something. It’s, um… It’s a body.”

There was a long silence on his end, “Are you pulling my leg, son?”

I didn’t blame him for the skepticism. Stillwater never had anything newsworthy happen, and the last time it did, said news was promptly erased from existence. I hoped this wouldn’t be a rerun.

“Dead serious. I know the weather’s getting rough, but I need you to come up now. Whatever happened, it’s gruesome.”

“Course’. I’m getting my deputies rallied up, and we’ll be there soon. I know you only found the thing, but I’ll need you to stay put, alright, Jess?”

“Sure thing,” I nodded.

There was another pause, and it seemed like something dawned on him, “Jess, you did only *find it,* right? Nothing you got up to?”

The question made my eyes blur. Maybe it was just the lack of having to use this protocol that caused him to only think of it after, but it touched something inside me that the sheriff assumed without hesitation that I would have nothing to do with such a situation. After so many years living with scornful glances from people who assumed I’d wrong them the same way my father did, it was nice to know there was at least one person in Stillwater that didn’t see me as the same monster.

“No sir,” I told him, “Just found it is all.”

“Good,” He grunted, “And you’re alright?”

I nodded, as if he could see it, then shut my eyes tight. I knew it was a stupid thing to say in that moment, but what last words *are* good ones?

“Hey sheriff?”

“Yes, son?”

“I don’t think I ever thanked you back when my dad died, but… thanks for always looking out for me. After everything it… it meant more than you could know.”

There was a finality to my words that the officer clearly picked up on, but whether it was discomfort or worry that kept him from prying, I’ll never know.

“It’s what us old folk are supposed to do, Jess. Now hang tight. I’ll be up soon.”

I dropped the phone and hung it up, letting the tears on my cheeks dry cold against the snowy air. I lingered like that for a while before heading back up the driveway a bit, just enough to see my truck through the trees. It was growing dark now beyond the already greying sky, but the darkness of the forest didn’t intimidate me. I found it peaceful there in the snow, and besides, nothing could be scarier than what waited for me back at the house.

After about twenty minutes—only twenty left before the third hour chime—I saw light begin cutting through the trees, flickers of red and blue dancing with the bold white of the high beams. The sheriff's cruiser slowed as it approached the mile marker, then it rolled to a stop to park behind my car. There was another police car that pulled up close behind, and the lot of them got out, shining their flashlights around the area and then into the bed of my trunk.

“What the fuck is that?” I heard one of the deputies say loudly in surprise as they all crowded around.

I couldn’t help but snicker. Those five words were the perfect summary of everything.

With peace in my heart, knowing that the rest would unfold from here and that my friends were off somewhere safe, I could finally release a breath that I’d been holding for the last four weeks. Quietly, before the officers could read the note and follow the rope up to the manor, I dismissed myself to beat them there.

There was no fanfare to my reentry. I stepped back into the manor as casually as walking into a grocery store and headed up the hall that I’d vandalized to hell and back, tracking fresh crimson onto the soles of my boots. The boards echoed creaky laughs beneath me with each step, the manor gloating that even if I’d found a way to draw this game, it still had cost me more than I could ever afford. The stairs did the same all the way to the basement until I was stood before the red door, this time completely alone.

I eyed the thread in my chest that led straight into it, then let it reel in as I drew closer. Maybe it was even tugging me.

The knob was cold and foreign as I placed my hand on it. I had really only physically used the thing one other time. The first day when I’d unlocked it, by breaking Mindy’s seal. It was time to fix that mistake.

I threw the thing open to a gust of chilling wind, then peered inside. There was only ten minutes now before the bell chimed again.

“Call it off,” I yelled into the abyss. My voice came out confident, but inside I was trembling like a scared kid, “I’m giving it up. You win.”

Before me, something appeared in the darkness, but it wasn’t any of the witches henchmen. It was the familiar small form of Lucy, only the rotting, corpse version of her.

“Hey, princess.” I smiled softly.

She didn’t return it. Instead, she shook her head, pleading for me not to go.

“I’m sorry, Lucy,” I told her, “But I have to. This has to end.”

She dropped her head, and I pursed my lips.

“If you want, I wouldn’t mind some company for a little bit.” I wasn’t lying for her sake either. The thought of not being alone made it easier.

She was hesitant, but still seemed to like the idea if it meant not entirely abandoning hope. I smiled one last time, then turned to the door. Exactly as I’d seen Mindy do it so many times now, I took my thread and began stitching a cradle around the knobs and the latch. Back and forth, looped round and around, then pulled tight.

Once it was set, pulled the door shut before I could let my eyes slip beyond it again. I was sealed inside.

I turned around and stared at my tiny companion again. There was comfort to be found somewhere in the dark, sunken sockets of her eyes no matter how grotesque their appearance, but at the same time, they only served as a dark reminder of what I was to become.

If our theory that the hallways had once been a labyrinth, then our interpretation of Ariadne’s thread hadn’t been far off. The hallways, even with all of their branching paths and crossroads, were no longer a labyrinth. The Thread in my chest floated gently in the center of the corridor, its soft glow lighting my way and twisting down each turn I needed to take like a spectral GPS.

Lucy stayed close by my side the whole way and didn’t try to stall me further, but she was getting more nervous the further we went into the floral guts of the house. Despite all we knew, the place was just as strange and perturbing as the first time we’d been through.

I passed some doorways that were open like the room full of past victims’ belongings where we’d found Mindy’s camera. Some were as mundane as a pantry full of canned foods, others were odd and set up like museums. They were full of uncanny statues of things that looked like humans or animals, but they were simply off, having extra limbs or faces that looked just a little too symmetrical. I wondered what the purpose of the places were—was this all some kind of prep or testing area for the paradise they built on the other side? The statues' some kind of blueprints for their new denizens?

I pondered the possibility as I walked, but I didn’t stop to investigate. There was no time to slow down. The final gong of the clock finally came blasting down the hallway, and I held my breath while gripping my axe tightly. I was here to give myself up, but I was going to do so on my own terms. I hadn’t come this far and killed countless beasts just to meet my end to one anyway. Thankfully, it seemed like the witches had heard my call into the darkness earlier and had decided not to waste their time sending an envoy to claim me.

Either that, or they were saving their cards for something special once I reached them…

After a few more minutes of walking the shifting halls, I came to the end of one that let out into a room much larger than the others, and one that was darkly familiar to me. The grand foyer where I’d watched Casey get dragged off into the endless ceiling. From above the balcony at the top of the stairs, the portraits of Adeline and her sisters stared down at me. They weren’t painted to have smiles, but it felt like they were grinning nevertheless.

I checked my thread and saw that it lingered forward straight ahead beneath the balcony and into the cavernous corridor that waited there. Somewhere in the dark, I saw the glow of the string join into a massive set of double doors, its ornate details lined in intricate weaves of the spectral chords. For the last four weeks, the simple wooden red door with the black knob had been my ultimate source of anxiety, and I hadn’t thought anything could top it. Clearly, I hadn’t seen *this* set of barriers yet.

I moved closer, and beside me Lucy’s translucent form flickered in the light of my thread. Her restlessness had finally reached its peak, and she attempted to change my mind one more time by thrusting a hand toward mine to take it. It passed straight through.

I knelt before her and tried at my best smile, “It’s going to be okay, Hun. Maybe somehow this will make things better. Maybe if I can restore their paradise, there’ll be a better place for you than the loom.”

She shook her head vigorously, and I found my heart sinking. I didn’t know what to say to make her feel better, but there was no other option but forward at this point. The only choice was to let her down.

I was so caught up in the emotions and the pounding of my heart that I almost didn’t notice the thunder of footsteps coming from the hallway behind me.

I quickly jumped to my feet and turned to face the dark, only to find a bright light slicing through it. My nocturnal eyes squinted as the beam assaulted them, and I raised my axe, worried that I might have been wrong earlier and about to fight another flashbulb beast from so long ago.

I felt dread twice as strong than any beast could bring when I saw what was actually standing there.

Kait huffed like a madwoman, buckling over when she reached me and falling to her knees with relief. Her cheeks were streaked with tears, and her eyes shut tightly while she muttered prayers of thanks under her breath toward the ground.

I was stunned at first, then wary, unable to believe my eyes, “K-Kait?” Was all I could sputter out. For once, I actually hoped this was some sort of trick, and not truly my friend come to hunt me down, “What the hell are you doing here?!”

I knew it was really her the moment her eyes opened to meet mine, and a look of raw fury took stage, “What am *I* doing here?” she muttered between gasps. Her teeth clenched, she launched to her feet, and with little regard to the weapon in my hand, she charged and shoved me hard, “What the hell are *you* doing here, Jessie?! Are you kidding me? Are you *fucking* kidding me?!”

I knew I had no right, but I couldn’t stop the frustration from taking hold. If Kait was really here, then that meant that everything that I’d just meticulously pulled off and sacrificed was for nothing. The door was unsealed.

“Did you open the door!?” I accused, nearly ripping my hair out with my free hand, “Kait, tell me you didn’t unseal the door! There’s no time to—”

“NO!” Kait screamed, “NO! You don’t get to start with that shit! What the fuck were you thinking, Jessie?! How could you lie to us like that?!”

I wanted to immediately lash back, the anger was so strong, but Kait had already begun to break into sobs, and the guilt in my chest kept the words from coming out. I just stood there speechless.

“You were really going to do that to me?! After Mindy and Casey, you think I could live without you too!?”

“Kait, I—”

“I already told you I was ready to give myself for this—I don’t want anyone else to die over me, Jessie! You think I would be able to live with myself once I realized?”

My stomach’s knot got even more tight. It was worse than I thought. Kait had only discovered half of the lie. She only thought I’d come back to protect her, and that the witches still wanted her thread.

Kaitlynn sobbed even harder, “I guess I wouldn’t have realized though, huh? Was that what you were counting on? That I’d just forget about you?”

I swallowed hard and tried to banish the blur in my own eyes, “If I could have had it another way I would have…”

“Bullshit. This was always the plan. You lied and got me as far away as you could to buy yourself time. The plan you fed us was just a backup, wasn’t it?”

I opened my mouth to argue, but could make no sound. I’d only be digging myself a bigger hole.

“Well thank God you can’t vandalize for shit,” Kait hissed, pointing to my boots. I looked down to see that across the carpet, I’d been tracking a perfect trail of prints in glistening red paint.

“Did everyone come back with you?” I asked, dreading the answer.

She nodded, “Lacey and I got to thinking not long after we left. I just had this feeling, Jess—I *know you*. I knew you wouldn’t give up so easy, and you wouldn’t let anyone else die.”

“Kait, this isn’t what you think.”

“Why not just let me do it? If they want me and *this* was your plan, why not just give my life instead? It would have been so much less risk!”

“It’s not that easy.”

“Why? Because letting me go is hard?” Kait hissed, shaking her head. I knew it was anger possessing her to say it, but it still hurt to hear, “You already did it once. What’s one more time?”

The insult may have beaten me down, but it also made my anger flare higher, and with emotions so high, the last thing anyone needed was for me to explode. I held my tongue, but Kait kept pressing.

“I don’t get it, Jessie. Why are you always in such a hurry to leave?”

“I never left anyone,” I warned, “You all left me.”

“Yes, you did,” she snapped, “Staying in one place and letting the world pass you by is still a form of retreat. I know you wanted to leave Stillwater, Jessie. I know you knew how much each and every one of us would have been happy to have you still in our lives so why, after everything those assholes put you through, did you still choose to stay with your bitch of a mother instead of escaping with the twins, or moving in with Carley, or escaping with *me?*”

I winced away, unable to face her desperate gaze, “Kait, we don’t have time for this.”

“Then let’s talk about *now*,” Kait spat, “I finally get you back during this mess, and you come up with this plan where I think we might *actually* have a chance to live a normal life together after, and then I find out that, nope, it was all just a ploy for you to escape again. Run off so that you could erase yourself from existence. Is dying really better to you than being with us?”

“That’s not a fair accusation,” I huffed through clenched teeth, “You know that’s not why.”

My face was still turned away from her, so Kait moved forward, taking it firmly in her hands and yanking my head to look her in the eyes.

“Why can you not accept that we love you, Jessie? Why can you not accept that you’re just as important to us as we are to you? You may not love yourself, but it’s a torturous burden to put on the world to have to suffer your absence. It’s selfish.”

“It’s selfish if I keep living,” I told her.

“No, it’s not! You’re taking away one of the *few* good things in this world if you go.”

“I’d be making it better.”

“You’re the only one that thinks that!”

“It’s a fact, Kait.”

“How is that a fact?!”

“Because it’s ME!” I finally broke, shouting so violently in her face that it made her wince back. the reaction immediately iced the rage back down, but it only left me in crying shambles. “It’s me, Kait… you’re not the one the witches want. It’s *me*. It always was.”

I could see the girl processing the information I’d just given her, and her eyes flickered to the doors behind me.

“If I stay, I’m damning everyone else to clean up a disaster that could be solved with one life. One tiny, worthless soul in exchange for peace for everyone else.”

Kait’s tears had become silent. She shook her head, “That’s not peace. Not for us. Not for me…”

I shrugged, “Even if I stayed, Kait, it wouldn’t matter. If I had gone with you that night at the park? There is no normal life for you and I. For me and any of us. I’m *poison*, Kait. You said it yourself all those years ago on the roof of the school; I’m an animal bred by animals in a cage. I’m just as angry, I’m just as bitter, and I’m just as mentally fucked up as my dad was. It’s the only thing I know. And if I ever let that poison hurt you, Kait—if I ever left with you and ruined it? I wouldn’t be able to live with myself.” I took her cheek in my hand and wiped a tear with my thumb, “Look what I’ve already done to you…”

Kait lifted a hand to caress my own, then she shook her head, “People hurt people, Jessie. You can’t avoid it. We’re always going to carry what we were given, but it doesn’t define us. You’re nothing like your dad, and I know you would never mess it up.”

I shook my head, “That doesn’t change what’s happening now…”

“Yes it does,” Kait stated, pulling me close and standing on her tip-toes to press her forehead into mine, “Because the women that did all of this—they’re no different than your dad. They used and abused you, and none of it was your fault. *This* isn’t your fault, Jessie. And you can’t let it define your fate.”

Between us, the glimmering line of my soul coiled. In all my haste, I hadn’t even remembered to untangle the girl or any of my other friends. They were all still just as tangled and tied to me as I was to the door.

“I have a new plan,” she told me in a whisper, “Stay. The others are coming after they sort things with the sheriff. You made quite the mess for us.”

“*Kait*,” I desperately pleaded, as if the utterance might compel her to turn around and leave this horrible place.

“Once they’re here, we can go into that door together,” Kait said, “I don’t know what they have planned for you, but we’ll stop it. We’ll stop it, and then we’ll make sure they can’t hurt anyone else.”

“Kait, no,” I told her sternly, “We don’t know what’s in there. I won’t risk any of you dying.”

“It’s gonna be okay no matter what,” she said, “cause’ you know something? Every single one of us is willing to fight for you.” she pressed closer, her lips dangerously close to mine. I knew in that moment that if they touched, I would lose all willpower to leave her behind.

“I don’t want that,” I told her, pulling away “Please, Kait, just go back while you still can. Don’t do this. Please don’t make me sta—”

Before I could finish, she leaned in all the way, silencing my words and filling the cold, dark space around me with her warmth. My string drew cursive through the surrounding air, glowing brighter and humming with a soft, pleasant melody. For a brief moment, the world seemed to fall away; all the urgency and fear. It was just Kaitlynn and I, and in that ephemeral pocket, she held me so tightly and with such passion that somewhere deep in my soul—if only for a moment—I was able to glimpse the truth she’d been trying to tell me.

We were all each other had in this dark abyss, and whether it was before the red door or after, if we were to go on, that fact could not change.

For all the insanity of the moment, and for all the fear that I felt, suddenly, I felt temporarily invincible. Enough to charge beyond the doors behind us, axe raised in defiance, not surrender. Enough to trust that my friends might be invincible too. Enough to feel that I could take on a god.

Maybe even three of them.
