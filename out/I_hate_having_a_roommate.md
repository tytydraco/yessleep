My roommate isn’t bad. They’re alright. It’s just insufferable having a human invade your personal space.

I’m in my second year at a university far from my hometown, sharing a small apartment with a first-year student. They’re much better than my previous roommate, who had no hobby other than complaining about the bathroom not being spotless. My current roommate enjoys watching movies and collecting their merch.  
Despite sharing some interests in common, my roommate and I barely talked, except for the occasional hi. 

Today I came back from university exhausted. Upon entering my bedroom, I noticed that our trash can was overflowing with tissues. My roommate didn’t seem to have left their bed all day. They just lay there, facing the wall.  
I wanted to talk to them, but we barely knew each other. Maybe they didn’t want me interfering. Still, I couldn’t help but feel sorry for them. They appeared as lonely as I was. Besides, I understood how depressing life could get after moving away from home. 

As it became late, I switched off the lights and went to sleep.

At midnight, I suddenly woke up to the echoes of someone screaming. Was it coming from the window? Like, was it from next door? It sounded so close, as though it came from inside the building. At first, I thought it was the cry of a kid or a newborn, but slowly, I realized that these weren’t the cries of a kid. They were from someone older, an adult.  
It sounded like someone screaming in pain, in agony. I couldn’t stop thinking about them. What could have caused all this pain?  
After a while, it stopped, followed by the whooshing of an air conditioner. I lay in darkness, disturbed by the what just happened. Eventually, I fell back asleep without thinking to check on my roommate.

The next day, I woke up for uni my roommate wasn’t here. They probably left earlier.

Time passes like usual and I came back from uni exhausted with so many assignments to do. I went to my room, and as I placed my bags on the bed, I noticed something. 

My roommate was facing me, their eyes wide open. 

I awkwardly pretended I saw nothing and headed over to the kitchen to boil my instant ramen.  
I told myself, “I’m gonna start my assignments after this meal.” While the water heated, my door creaked open.

They must have gotten up. I listened for footsteps but heard nothing. This was weird. Once the ramen was ready, I headed to the living room to eat. On the way, I noticed my roommate standing still in the doorframe of our room. Their expression was blank. 

Were they looking at me or staring straight ahead? I moved to the left, and their eyes moved left too. 

What is wrong with them? I thought to myself.

I sat on the sofa, eating. My roommate joined me, turning their head to stare directly at me.

“Do you want something?”

Silence.

“Hello? Can you hear me?”

Nothing.

Were they asleep? Was this what a sleepwalking person looked like? I admit part of me has always wanted to see a sleepwalker. I was jealous whenever my family recounted their encounters with my sleepwalking self. Seeing me aimlessly roam the house, stare at them, murmur incomprehensible words, seeing me able to send gibberish texts to people but unable to open doors. They told me I never respond with any word but when someone says “get back to bed” I follow. 

My thoughts went back to what was currently happening.

I ignored my roommate. pulling out my phone, pretending to scroll while taking sneak peeks every now and then. A moment later, they pulled theirs out too.  
I finished my food as fast as possible and retreated to my room. 

My roommate followed me. 

They sat on their bed with a posture similar to mine and stared at me. 

I moved to the living room they followed again. God, just leave me alone. I rushed into the bathroom and locked the door. The handle turned once. Nothing happened after.

Ahh, finally some peace. After fifteen minutes of scrolling on my phone in the bathroom, boredom set in. I unlocked the door and left the bathroom.  
My roommate was pacing between my room and the living room. A random idea came up to me 

“Get back to bed” I ordered.

It did nothing but overall thank goodness they were not following me, but how long were they gonna be asleep? They must have not slept at all that night. I don’t blame them.

I tried to work on my assignments, but I couldn’t concentrate. Thoughts of my roommate kept distracting me. After a while, I gave up and did my laundry instead.

To hang the wet clothes on a racket I visited our balcony. The view there was nice during the night. Observing the sky, I noticed three stars aligned in a tilted line. This was Orion’s Belt.  
Seeing it brought my mind back to a memory I had. My roommate and I grabbed two chairs and a small table to enjoy the place during the night. Heh, I remember pointing out star constellations I recognized to look smart. That day, we ended up watching a movie both of us grew up with on their iPad. This was one of the few times we interacted.

Time passed getting bored I decided to get back to the apartment. But there was a feeling bothering me now.  
Staring at the balcony’s glass door. The curtains blocked most of the view,  
 There’s this feeling that someone stood behind it.  
I didn’t even want to know if they were. 

My eyes lingered one the buildings outside, the door behind me. 

I couldn’t.

I pushed away the curtains.  
They were standing there. Far closer to me than I expected.

“You scared me!” I snapped trembling while retreating to my room.

I tried doing my assignments. But I couldn’t do any, thoughts about my roommate distracted me.

They entered the room standing near the entrance standing straight doing nothing but watching me. My stomach churned.

“It’s been so long how is my roommate still asleep… are they asleep at all?” 

My roommate laid down on their bed closing their eyes.

“Maybe they are”

After turning off the lights, I crawled into bed. I was creeped out but another dread took over.

“I’m so tired, but I don’t want tomorrow to come. What will my professor do when she finds out that my assignment isn’t done?”

The next morning, my alarm woke me up.  my roommate was not in bed.

“I wonder where they went?”

I saw my roommate in the kitchen, washing their hands.

“Hi…” I said.

“Hi,” they said back.

Before I could bring up what happened yesterday, my roommate went to the bathroom, leaving me standing there, embarrassed. Was “hi” the right word? Maybe saying “hey” would have been better? I didn’t know. They looked disinterested in talking, I annoyed them

After getting ready as quickly as possible, I headed off. I rushed out and headed for the metro station. The train left me squeezed between strangers, clinging to a pole made greasy by the touch of countless hands. I made it to class in time. Shortly after class started, my professor confronted me about my assignment.

“Why haven’t you done your assignments again?”  
I stared at her, my mouth lacking any words to say. 

I had used the “I was tired” excuse too many times for it to be believable anymore.

“Answer me.”

My hands trembled, my gaze unable to meet hers.

“Are you just gonna stay silent like that.” she said, her face frustrated, then looked away.

“You can get back to your seat.”

My neck hurt from how heavy my head was. I heard before that deep breathing could help calm you down, but surrounded by these people, I just couldn’t… I couldn’t risk my breaths being too loud, too noticeable. 

Four hours later, class finally ended. Students left in pairs and groups. Some joined friends waiting by the doorway, while others stayed behind to chat. 

I have been taking the same classes with the same people for over a year. Somehow, everyone knew everyone. Everyone except me.  
They were all connected by some invisible tunnels, operating under unwritten rules. Subterranean creatures they all were.

I wasn’t alone in the classroom. Sitting on the other side was a group chatting, aware of my existence. I pushed over the chair as carefully as I could. It still made an attention-grabbing screech. I interrupted their conversation. They all watched me, every step I took. Were my steps too loud? Was my walking slightly uneven?

I walked across the hall, my arms wrapped around myself. So many humans here, so many. Every passing glance I felt someone watching me, even if only for a second. Desperate to escape, I searched anxiously for somewhere isolated. Then, I spotted a utility room.  
It was dusty and dark, with some sunlight leaking in. 

It was perfect since no one could watch me here. I felt my feet pressing against the soles of my shoes. With every movement, I listened to the faint sounds of my muscles shifting. I stretched out one arm, feeling the pull of my nerves and muscles. I could never move freely, not with someone watching.

Closing my eyes, slowly I raised my hand in front of my face, feeling the warmth of my breath brushing against my fingertips.

I’m human.  
I can only feel human, be human when I’m alone.  
The world around me moved when I moved. It spun when I spun. I watched my fingers curl and uncurl whenever I wanted them to. I can only be free when it’s just me, myself, and I.

Then, tears came, Tears held back for so long finally fell. Silently, I cried, fearful that someone might hear me. I didn’t want to return and have someone stare at me. I don’t want to lose this freedom. But I can’t stay here forever.  
I had to leave because my university was closing. I headed back to my apartment more energized than before.

After opening the apartment door, I saw my roommate standing face-to-face with me, so close. My whole body felt light. Not having enough air to breathe.

“What is wrong with you?” I snapped.

They responded, “Want something?”

“Why were you quietly staring at me?!”

“Get back to bed.”

What! I pushed them, screaming at the top of my lungs.  
“Please go away! Please, please stop what you’re doing to me!”

They pushed me back and screamed the same things word for word. I heard the desperation and loudness of my voice through them. Their voice was full of emotion, but their face remained blank.

Horrified, I ran to the bathroom and locked it. I sat there, fidgeting frantically. What is that thing? What does it want? I smashed my head against the wall. How do I make it disappear?! “That’s it!!! I’m leaving this place!”

I ran to my room, locking the door. Everything I could find, including my bags and keys, I picked up. On my way out, a cabinet opened in the kitchen next to me. I looked over.

My roommate opened my cupboard, took out one of my ramen cups, prepared it, and sat exactly where I sat earlier, scrolling through their phone.

I stared in shock.

They never liked ramen.

They told me so themselves before.

Who is this? What is this?

“Can you hear me?” I said hesitantly.

A long silence followed before they said,

“Hello? Can you hear me?”

They stared, watching me quietly with the same blank expression as yesterday, but I slowly realized that their face was never expressionless. 

It’s lost. Why hadn’t I noticed this sooner? 

I put my bags down and picked up my phone. I dialed emergency services as I hurried to my room, locking the door before my roommate could follow me inside.

“Hello? I think something’s wrong with my roommate. They don’t seem conscious. I mean, they are standing, but don’t respond when I talk to them…”

The operator asked if anyone got hurt. I said no. She asked if my roommate threatened me.  
“I don’t know. They keep staring at me for hours. I don’t recall them blinking.”

Then, she asked a few more questions before she told me someone would come check on them.  
After an hour, they came and found the apartment door unlocked when they arrived. 

 My roommate was standing, facing the bedroom door. They didn’t respond to any of their questions. After leaving the bedroom, they asked me questions. 

They told me I should have called them earlier. 

I messed up. 

They informed me that people from other apartments had reported hearing loud screams. 

Later, I saw them take my roommate to the hospital. They didn’t fight back at all. 

It was like they gave up on existing.

I haven’t seen them since.  
A few days later, a family member contacted me to inform me that my roommate wouldn’t be returning to university. When the family member came to collect their things, I asked them what happened. They told me they too had no idea and apologized for the situation. 

Later, I left the apartment too.  
It was expensive, and having to always find a roommate drained me. Fortunately, I found another apartment. It was far from university and much smaller, but at least I could afford to be alone. To this day, I think about that night. The memory of my roommate haunts me. 

I keep wondering what happened to them have they gotten better. Could I have stopped that thing from getting worse? Did I worsen it?

I know I messed up, but to my roommate… I’m sorry. I wish I had given you the help you needed. I wish I had talked to you more before losing you, wish I made you feel that there was someone here who cared about you. 

Maybe in another universe, we could have been friends.
