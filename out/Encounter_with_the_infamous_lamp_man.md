As the post title might suggest, I'd known about this "lamp man" figure before my inevitable encounter with him. But to understand the circumstances leading up to this moment, I need to give you some background.

My wife (25 f) and I (26 f) have been married for 4 years but have been together for 9. The two of us recently finished college with our respective degrees and moved in together on one of her parents' farms. Her family farmed with cattle and allowed us to take over one of them as almost a "wedding present". The two of us are very grateful and the farm is gorgeous. During the week she stayed at the farm or with me in our city home, as her job allowed her to work at home, while I spent the week in the city working part time as an archaeologist while I pursued my master's degree. Our weekends however we spent at the farm, keeping busy with chores, training our dogs as well as the new horses.

So this weekend was the same as any other, a day spent in the sun that led to a night spent in the living room watching our show and playing a few rounds of video games. Around 8pm I began -what I like to call- closing up shop while my wife went to bathe. This included checking the cameras, locking doors, closing curtains, setting the alarm and getting the pups ready to go in their crates. Max -our golden- was already sound asleep on the couch so I simply picked him up like a fluffy sack of potatoes and put him in his proper place for the night. While Sasha -our border, who was our newest adoptee- was being difficult as per usual, so I ended up playing with her for a bit until she got bored and went to lie down. By that point my wife was already in our room reading while she waited for me, so I took a quick shower before joining her in bed. By then, it was probably around 9 or 9:30. Still early for a weekend night, but the sun had just about drained the life out of us that day and my wife wanted to head to bed.

Once I was sure both our water bottles were filled I turned off the light so the two of us could turn in. It wasn't too long before I heard her breathing even out beside me, but I, for some reason, couldn't fall asleep. As my wife was a light sleeper, I didn't want to toss and turn too much in case I woke her up. So after a few moments of tossing and turning I let out a small sigh and quietly got out of bed.

Sliding my feet into my slippers I thought to myself what could possibly be keeping me awake. After all, I expected I'd be the first one to fall asleep as I'd spent most of the day in the lunging arena I built in the spring, working our horses until I was satisfied. I figured I must be hungry, even though I'd eaten dinner only 2 to 3 hours prior, it made enough sense that I found myself wandering to the kitchen. Sasha, of course, alerted by the sound of my shuffling, came trotting into the kitchen to come sit on my feet as I made a sandwich. Begging, with her presence, for a bit of peanut butter. The sandwich did nothing. If anything it made me feel even more awake, and with my growing consciousness, came unease.

I am not a sceptic, and neither is my wife. In fact I consider myself a witch, while she considers herself simply curious. I'm clairaudient and that's really about it, but I have had a number of "spooky encounters" to consider myself a believer in the paranatural. I'm telling you this so that you know I'm not an idiot. I'm not another "stupid white woman" running towards the source of danger without a plan. In fact, I pride myself in my ability to plan around the unexpected, and come up with split-second decisions in the moment that often lead to a more positive outcome than if I did nothing. Taking all this into account you must understand that when we moved in here, I went to work on this place. We didn't have a welcome mat, or any "welcoming" paraphernalia by our front door, back door or windows. I had sealed every mirror, and similarly reflective surface with black salt water (I do not play about safety). And the specific magic I specialize in is protection and cleansing magic. This place was the magical equivalent of Fort Knox. No spirit -especially those that mean harm- was getting in here. And that was why the unease was so... unsettling to me.

Since I didn't want to wake my wife with more tossing and turning, I decided to make myself useful and go sit by the security cameras. With the way the house was set up, the room we kept our security monitoring station in was basically this hole-in-the-wall closet-like space where two people couldn't fit comfortably inside at the same time. And often times I was the one manning the space as my wife was kind of banned from there, as she'd spend the whole night in there scaring herself about figures in the dark that were actually just a smudge on the screen. Once again, my faithful companion was Sasha, but this time sleeping on my lap.

It was still early in the evening and a Saturday so I wasn't too worried about going to bed late and waking up feeling like garbage. I still had Sunday to catch up on sleep so I wouldn't be a total zombie come Monday. So I sat, clicking through each camera view on the one monitor while the other showed every angle the cameras captured in smaller tiles. I looked for anything out of place, anything suspicious, anything that could explain the pounding in my chest despite how calm the evening was.

And then, I felt Sasha tense.

Her head flicked up from her slumber to look up at the window, where it stayed fixed, frozen. I couldn't speak, didn't want to in case my wife heard me and came in wondering what I was doing up. I glanced at the window, then my dog, back and forth for a while before clumsily clicking back through the cameras to find the one that captured the side of the house where the window was. Nothing. Had she heard something I missed? What could have made this little dog tense up so bad and then-

I heard it. Whistling. Lazy with notes missed in between like the person was drunk (or bad at whistling) but it was from so far away I could've almost brushed it off as the wind blowing through one of the jump training poles. And I almost, almost, breathed a sigh of relief. Wanting to reassure my dog that it was nothing, that she could relax. But something stopped me, and that something came in the form of a flash of light coming through on the monitor screen, and then across the window itself.

It was like someone was carrying a flashlight, then stumbled on a rock sending the light skittering. But it was much closer than the whistling had sounded, purely based on how direct the beam of light was. Like maybe, 20 meters away, maybe less. I couldn't breathe, I didn't want to for fear of alerting whoever, or whatever it was that there was someone awake in this house. My first thoughts were rational, realistic even. A burglar, a poacher, homeless drunk, stupid teen coming home from a party that got lost, murderer. But then I realized, no. That wouldn't be possible, as they would've triggered the alarm if they got within 200 meters of the house, let alone 20, so it made no sense. I knew I had set the alarm that night, and I could see that it was active with a quick glance at the monitors again. So what the fuck was that?

The light passed the window again, I ducked slightly out of instinct even though the curtains were closed, but this time it passed it, slowly. Like someone trying to case the house which wasn't possible but fine, that's where we were now.

I had to get a better look to see what I was dealing with, and as far as whoever that was knew, no one was awake. Like I said before, I'd closed up shop earlier in the evening, so I knew this place was locked down tight, so I wasn't worried about them catching me off guard and being able to come in just anywhere. Besides, if I could get a description (height, weight, race, build, clothing) it'd be more useful to the police than "help there's someone on my farm and I'm just a helpless girl".

So, I put Sasha down on the chair, gesturing for her to stay as I crept closer to the window on my haunches, creating a small gap in the curtains with my finger so I could peer out just as the light began to pass again. I felt my throat dry up when I recognized what I saw from a description my wife had given me when we were still dating (about 8 years ago). Tall build, dark figure, and most distinctively, a light for a head. I let out a small breath as I let the curtain fall closed again, praying to anyone and anything that he didn't see me and that he would just go away.

I sat on the floor of that shoe-box office with my dog quivering on the office chair for what felt like ages, while in reality it was probably only 30 minutes. Counting tiny, shallow breaths until I felt my chest begin to ease and Sasha eventually jumped off the chair and came to cuddle with me. Her tiny tail wagging. And then I knew, this "lamp man" was officially gone, and I could finally breathe again.

After that harrowing encounter I ended up breaking my "no phones after 8pm" rule to watch some cute animated short films to further calm my heart before putting my pup to bed, and going to bed myself. Feeling much safer than before, and having an interesting story to tell my wife in the morning.
