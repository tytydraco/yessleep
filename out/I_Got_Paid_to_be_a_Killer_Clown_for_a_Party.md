I unfortunately only had suspicions, but I didn’t air those out. Not even when the police later showed up in my room, once my arm was basically all cast. I mean, they asked me all the simple stuff,

‘Do you know who owned the house?’, ‘Do you know who hosted the party?’ I answered the best I could. And as I did, their questions seemed to get more off-track, ‘Did you notice the cameras?’, ‘What were you dressed like?’, ‘What was in the shed?’. Once more, I gave them what I knew, I didn’t cut out any facts, or anything. Then they asked, ‘Did you see who did it?’ 

I almost didn’t want to say yes, or no, I was technically not the one who did it, in fact, if anyone did it, it’d be Lake, but… “Did what?” I let out.

The officer nodded, “Who fired the gun?” He said it quite matter-of-factly.

I narrowed my eyes, not realizing I misunderstood the question, which made me ask for clarification, “Was… Wasn’t most of it just an accident?” 

His partner smirked, then smacked her lips, “Well, that’s for sure, I mean, there’s no two-ways about most of it. We just need to know if *you* saw them. Their accidents I mean. That's all.” 

I raised an eyebrow, “Yeah I… I was just hired to be… A performer… But I think the one that fired a gun… Was someone named Jack.”  I was still trying to piece things together myself, and they were just adding to the fog in my mind.

She nodded while the other one continued, “Alright, well… All I can really tell you is… Maybe don’t pick up random gigs as… Whatever kind of performer you are. You were in a pretty risky spot last night, but you seem to be all good now that you’re patched up. We won’t press charges, and there’s no record of anyone owning the home on that street… So just worry about getting better.” He glanced towards his partner, simply telling me to let them know if I remember anything else, and the two had left.

It’s been a week since then.

The news on the event went by quickly. It was just treated as a highlight for that one day, and then nobody else even mentioned it. Even less, it was just a single 5 minute segment on the local news.

‘This just in: Series of Remarkable Accidental Deaths Meets a Real Murder at Renard Rd. House.’

I really can’t ignore the details. The way they described everything, it’s like there’s this veil of secrecy… They didn’t mention the ‘break-in’ I had to do just to get in the back door. I wasn’t charged, but… 18 people *absolutely died.* Sure, 15 of which were an 'accident', 2 were part of a 'murder', and 1 died by misfire. But one extra person was saved… She had a name tag! If 'Lily' wasn’t intended to be at the party, why’d she have one? And what about afterwards? Why not mention *that?* They even kept any talk of me to the barest of minimums, simply saying I was ‘hired to perform, but broke my right arm by the end of things’. Not knowing what details were different from what I experienced wasn’t hard. Trying to figure out *why* it was changed *was*, and I’m still stumped on that front. So, I looked Lake’s real name up online. It took quite a bit of searching, and I could only find a few old newspapers on the site of a public library. They describe him as a ‘small-time set director’ who was ‘working his first big job’. It was this upcoming film that used the house at 'xxxx Renard Rd.', when a brutal accident had left his body split in two. There wasn't much of an investigation into it, and he wasn't all that well-known in the first place. The whole incident still left the neighborhood abandoned by the mid 90s, leaving just that last building standing...

By the way, I wasn’t even fired. *That* was simply a computer error. I am back on the clock this Monday. It will be tough flipping burgers with my non-dominant hand, but I'll get used to it. Hell, I might even *need to.* As for why I started writing this… I heard someone was collecting stories and mine had fit their odd requirements to a T...  And, well, that $5,000 *did* also come in as one last surprise.

Just yesterday, an envelope holding a written check with Lake's name on it slid underneath my door
