Saturday night was family movie night.

It had been for as long as I could remember.

My sister Ginny, my two moms, Mom and Mommy, and I would bring dinner into the living room, turn off all the lights except the lamp beside the sofa, and spend twenty minutes arguing over what to watch before eventually putting on something we'd all already seen.

That night it was Interstellar.

Again.

Nobody really minded. It was better than scrolling through Netflix for an hour, and Mom had made pasta bolognese and garlic bread, so I carried the bowls in from the kitchen in two trips, three first and then another two.

Mom and Mommy had already taken the couch. Ginny was sitting cross-legged on the floor, picking the burnt bits off a piece of garlic bread, and I dropped into my usual spot beside her.

Just as I sat down, a window slammed shut somewhere down the hallway.

Hard.

All of us jumped.

For a second nobody said anything.

Then Mom sighed.

"Can you check that?"

I got up.

For context, we live pretty far out of town.

Not completely rural, but close enough that the nearest house is almost five minutes away by car. During the day it's beautiful. Trees everywhere, huge gardens, birds, nothing but open land around us.

At night it's different.

There are no streetlights. No pedestrians. No sound from traffic.

Once it gets dark, it feels like our house is the only house left in the world.

Still, we've never really worried about anyone getting in. We have cameras, motion lights and a locked gate. You can't exactly wander onto the property without us knowing.

That's what I kept telling myself as I walked down the hallway.

The sound had come from Mom's bedroom.

Her door was shut.

I tried the handle.

Locked.

I frowned.

"Mom?"

Nothing.

Obviously Mom wasn't inside. I'd just left her sitting on the couch.

I tried the handle again.

Locked from the inside.

That was when I noticed the light beneath the door.

The bedside lamp was on.

Its glow spilled through the small gap along the floor.

Except part of the light was blocked.

Two dark shapes.

Feet.

Someone was standing on the other side of the door.

I froze.

For a few seconds, neither of us moved.

Then the shadow shifted.

Just slightly.

Like whoever was standing there had leaned closer to the door.

I backed away.

I don't know why I didn't call out.

Something in me suddenly felt very strongly that I didn't want whoever was inside that room to know I was standing outside.

I walked backwards until I reached the living room.

"Mom," I said.

She looked over.

"There's someone in your room."

Nobody reacted the way I expected.

Mom barely looked up from her food.

"Oh, it's probably Ginny."

Ginny stopped chewing.

I stared at Mom.

"Mom. Ginny's right there."

Mom looked at her.

For a few seconds she seemed genuinely confused.

"Oh."

She gave a small, uncertain laugh.

"Then it must be Mommy."

Mommy slowly lowered her glass.

"I thought it was you."

That shut everyone up.

The movie was still playing behind us, the music swelling loudly through the room.

Suddenly it felt unbearable.

I grabbed the remote and paused it.

The house went silent.

Mom looked at me.

Then Ginny.

Then Mommy.

Her eyes moved between us as though she were counting.

"Hang on," she said.

"What?"

"There are four of us here."

Nobody answered.

Mommy frowned.

"There should be five."

I laughed.

Not because it was funny.

Because what else do you do when someone says something that makes absolutely no sense?

"What are you talking about?"

Mommy didn't answer.

She was staring at the coffee table.

Then she pointed.

Five bowls of pasta.

For a few seconds I just stared at them.

One in front of Ginny.

One in front of Mom.

One in front of Mommy.

One in front of me.

And another one at the end of the table.

Untouched.

A fork beside it.

A glass of water.

A piece of garlic bread resting against the bowl.

Something cold moved through me.

"Why are there five?"

Nobody answered.

And then something even stranger happened.

Looking at that bowl gave me this horrible feeling that I knew exactly who it belonged to.

Except I couldn't think of who.

The knowledge felt like it was sitting just beyond reach.

You know when you're about to introduce someone and suddenly forget their name?

You can almost feel the word.

It's right there.

That was what it felt like.

Except I hadn't forgotten a name.

I'd forgotten a person.

Someone was supposed to be sitting there.

I looked at the empty space beside Mom.

For some reason, looking at it made my chest hurt.

There should have been someone there.

There always had been.

Hadn't there?

Ginny was staring at the same spot.

Her face had gone pale.

"I feel like I'm forgetting someone," she whispered.

Mom's expression changed.

She covered her mouth.

"Oh my God."

Mommy stood up.

"Don't."

We all looked at her.

"Don't what?"

"Don't try to remember."

Something about the way she said it made my stomach drop.

Before I could ask her what she meant, three knocks came from Mom's bedroom.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Slow.

Evenly spaced.

Nobody moved.

Then Ginny reached for the serving bowl.

She spooned pasta onto her plate.

A ridiculous amount.

I stared at her.

"Ginny?"

She added more.

"Ginny."

She picked up her fork and started eating.

Not eating.

Shovelling.

Ginny is the slowest eater I've ever met. She takes tiny bites. Mom jokes that everyone else finishes dinner before Ginny decides which part of her meal to start with.

Now she was stuffing forkful after forkful into her mouth.

Her cheeks bulged.

Sauce ran down her chin.

"Ginny, stop."

She didn't look at me.

I grabbed her wrist.

She fought me.

Hard.

Her eyes weren't focused on anything. She just kept trying to pull the fork toward her mouth.

"GINNY."

Three knocks came from the bedroom.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Ginny stopped.

The fork slipped from her hand.

She looked down at her plate.

Then at me.

There was sauce all over her mouth.

"What..."

Her eyes filled with tears.

"What was I doing?"

Nobody answered.

Then came another three knocks.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Mom started laughing.

At first it was just a chuckle.

Then another.

Then suddenly she folded over.

She was laughing so hard she couldn't breathe.

"Mom?"

She slapped a hand over her mouth.

It didn't help.

The laughter kept forcing its way through her fingers.

Her face went red.

Tears streamed down her cheeks.

"Stop it."

She shook her head frantically.

She couldn't.

Mommy grabbed her shoulders.

"Stop. Stop!"

Mom's laughter became higher.

Almost painful.

She was gasping between every burst.

Then the knocking came again.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Mom stopped.

Instantly.

She sucked in a huge breath.

Then she started crying.

"I couldn't stop," she whispered.

None of us spoke.

For the first time, I became aware of how quiet it was outside.

No birds.

No wind.

Nothing.

Just four people breathing in a room that suddenly felt like it had space for someone else.

Mommy picked up her phone.

"We're leaving."

Nobody argued.

"What about whoever's in the bedroom?" Ginny whispered.

"We're not checking."

Mom stood immediately.

"Keys."

"I'll get them."

"No."

Mom grabbed my arm.

"Nobody goes anywhere alone."

So we stayed together.

All four of us.

We moved toward the front door practically pressed against one another.

I couldn't stop looking down the hallway.

Mom's bedroom door was still closed.

The light was still shining underneath it.

And the shadow was still there.

Standing perfectly still.

We were almost at the front door when Ginny stopped.

I nearly walked into her.

"What?"

She didn't answer.

She was looking back at the coffee table.

Her face had gone completely white.

I followed her eyes.

The fifth bowl.

The one nobody had touched.

Half the pasta was gone.

There was sauce smeared around the inside.

The garlic bread had a bite missing from it.

For a few seconds, none of us moved.

I looked at Mom.

Then Mommy.

Then Ginny.

Then back at the bowl.

We had all been together.

I know that.

I'm certain of it.

Nobody had gone near the table.

Behind us, from Mom's bedroom, came three slow knocks.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

We're in the car now.

We just drove.

I don't even know where we're going. We're sitting in the car park of a petrol station because none of us wanted to be somewhere empty.

Nobody has said much.

Mommy keeps checking the mirrors.

Ginny won't eat anything.

And every few minutes one of us starts saying something like, "I swear there was someone..." and then just stops.

None of us can remember who that fifth bowl was for.

But that's the part that's really scaring me.

Because apparently I brought five bowls into the room and none of us thought anything of it.

Not one of us asked why.

Some part of us seemed to think there were supposed to be five people there.

And now none of us can remember who the fifth person is.

I don't know what we're supposed to do.

I don't know if we should call the police. I don't even know what we'd tell them.

There's still someone standing inside Mom's bedroom.

And there's one thing none of us has been able to explain.

Who ate that?
