I’m running out of time, so I need to write this fast. I’m sorry if it comes out messy.

I’m sure you’ve felt it before.

You’re the last one awake. Maybe you’re watching a movie in the living room, raiding the kitchen for a midnight snack, or leaving the safety of your bedroom just to pee. Whatever it is, eventually that last light has to go off, and you need to make your way back through the darkness alone.

And in that half second before your eyes adjust, your body just knows. Something is behind you. Something that was waiting for the darkness. You also, for some reason, know that if you turn around and look at it - you’ll die.

So you don’t. You run. You race back to your room like a frightened deer, slam the door, and don’t feel safe until you're back in bed... with your feet safely tucked beneath the blanket, of course.

Every kid feels it. Most of them grow out of it.

I didn’t. Because I saw it.

I was nine years old the first time I turned around.

I grew up in an ordinary two-story house with a basement. It doesn’t matter where. Wherever there’s a dark room and a staircase leading out of it, I believe this thing exists.

My bedroom was upstairs, at the far end of the hall. This was the mid-2000s, before smartphones had flashlights and apps could turn on every lamp in the house. If you switched off a light, you stood in the dark. Simple as that.

One night, I stayed up very late playing Resident Evil 4 on my PS2. God, just thinking about it makes me miss those days. Anyway, I’d eaten so much candy that sometime after midnight, I became unbearably thirsty. I needed a glass of water from the kitchen.

Which was downstairs.

To give you a clearer picture, we had a switch at the top of the stairs that controlled the lights in the hallway below. That probably sounds reassuring at first… however, I hated it.

To turn those lights off, you had two choices: stand at the top of the stairs and stare down into the darkness as it swallowed everything below you - or turn your back on it.

I always chose the second option, even if it meant exposing myself to whatever was there. I would always hit the switch and run like a bloody maniac to my bedroom.

But not that night.

I paused the game, left my room, and turned on the lights as I went downstairs. In the kitchen, I drained the first glass of water like I’d just crossed a desert, filled another, and headed back up. I don’t know why I hit the switch differently that night. I guess I was too busy thinking about how to beat Verdugo.

Whatever the reason, when I reached the top of the stairs, I turned off the lights while I was still facing them… and I froze.

There was a face at the bottom of the stairs.

A huge face, barely visible in the darkness, staring up at me.

And when I say a “huge face”, that’s exactly what I mean. There was no body beneath it. It didn’t have a neck. No arms or legs. There were just two enormous, bulging eyes and a wide grin crowded with pure white teeth grinning at me. I could only describe the expression it had as… patient*.*

I want to be very clear about what happened next, because I’ve spent twenty-five years trying to explain that night away. Was I dreaming? Sleepwalking? Was I having some kind of hallucination brought on by exhaustion and - fuck, I don’t know - too much sugar?

No. None of those explanations hold up. I know I was awake.

I didn’t scream. You need to understand that, because if you ever see it too, I’m begging you: Don’t scream. It was like some ancient part of my brain understood immediately that making a sound was the wrong move. Like an instinct left over from a different time... some primal part of me just knew that - whatever was starting at me - was a thing that only moved once I did.

So I stood perfectly still. 

And for what might have been ten seconds - or ten minutes - so did it.

Then I ran. God, I ran. 

The instant I moved, it came racing up the stairs after me.

There was no question of fight or flight. I could feel those huge, unblinking eyes burning into the back of my neck as it gained on me. Every nerve in my entire body gave me the same command: Run if you want to live. I reached my bedroom half a step ahead of it, threw myself inside, and slammed the door. Then I dove into bed, shoved myself beneath the blanket, and lay there with my heart pounding so hard I could feel it in my teeth.

I didn’t play any more video games that night. And for the next twenty years, I never walked a dark staircase again.

My parents used to fight about the lights. They didn’t know what I was afraid of. I never told them what I’d seen. As far as they knew, their teenage son had developed an irrational habit of leaving every light in the house on until morning… which drove my father insane. “It’s like we’re running a goddamn football stadium,” he’d say whenever the electric bill arrived.

One night during my senior year, he finally put his foot down. He went through the house at bedtime and turned off every light himself. Then he stood outside my bedroom and told me I was too old to be afraid of the dark. 

Honestly, I didn’t disagree with him. But I moved out the following year. I got my own place with my own rules. Today, I live two states away with my wife and our seven-year-old daughter. And here - the lights stay on at night.

My wife thinks my fear of the dark is unreasonable. She thinks it’s equally ridiculous that I know there are exactly sixteen steps in our staircase, and that they all make a specific sound. Our staircase is old, and I have spent enough sleepless nights listening to my family walk up and down that staircase to recognize every one of them. But she loves me, and I love her, so we compromised. We installed dimmers throughout the house and keep them on their lowest setting after bedtime. It took me a while to trust them, but eventually I learned that even the faintest light was enough to keep it away. 

It needs complete darkness.

Last month, my daughter started closing her bedroom door when she went to sleep. When I asked her why, she shrugged and said she felt “too exposed” with it open. Those were her exact words.

Too exposed… 

I wanted to ask if she had ever felt something watching her from the bottom of the stairs. I wanted to ask if she sometimes ran the last few steps to her bedroom without knowing why. But I was afraid of putting the idea in her head. Maybe that’s how it finds you… So I just told her she could keep the door closed.

That brings me to tonight. There’s a storm raging outside, and my wife is out of town, so it’s just me and our daughter. About twenty minutes ago, I heard her leave her room. Then the stairs began to creak. 

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Seven.

The power went out.

Not only in our house. The entire neighborhood. I can see the other homes through the window, and every one of them is dark. The outage map claims that a tree came down on the power lines at the end of our street - they won’t have them repaired before dawn. 

My daughter hasn’t taken another step. I can hear the tiny, frightened breaths she’s trying to hold back. And below her, barely visible in the darkness, I know it has its eyes fixed on her. 

I know what will happen if she runs back up the stairs.

I know what will happen if I run out to get her.

It’s waiting for one of us to make a move.

So it can make the next.
