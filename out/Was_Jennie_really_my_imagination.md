Not a very creepy story but still one that bothers me so I thought I would share it here. When I was a kid I grew up in a village in the countryside. I’m not going to describe too much of how life was like then but we were not very well off. My mom spent most of her days mending shoes and my dad opened a small convenience store. Both of my parents were always at work and I too had many chores.  

Other than a few neighbours visiting during the holidays, we usually never had guests. But mid-January the year I was seven, some family friends came over to visit and they brought along their daughter who was two years younger than me at the time. 

“This is Jennie, and she will be staying with us for a while,” the aunt and uncle had greeted me, introducing me to a kid with a poofy red jacket sitting on our living room couch after school. I’ve never seen these people come over ever, but I didn’t question things that much then because I was just happy to have someone to play with! “My new friend is the best new year’s gift ever!” I remember replying to them very happily. And she was! Before Jennie came around, I was pretty lonely. Jennie was a very pretty child. She had a mob of black hair, pale skin, and rosy red cheeks from the cold winter winds. Her dark pupils were large and innocent, full of hope and curiosity. Jennie never talked much but my parents treated her very nicely. Thinking back on it now, it was pretty surprising given how my parents were very frugal people; having another child to feed would mean spending more money during those times, but my parents never complained at all. My mother treated Jennie like her own child, bathing us together in those large wooden basins (because there was no bathtub) and feeding us food and snacks very well. Jennie left around two months later in March. All of this happened around 1990. I was sad to see her go, but I eventually stopped questioning and forgot about her as life returned to normal. 

A couple years ago, when my parents and I had already moved out of the country, something prompted my memory of Jennie so I casually brought it up to my parents and asked them whereabouts of the girl that stayed with us. Neither my mom nor my dad remembered or knew who I was talking about. For a girl that stayed with us two months and treated us both like their own children without complaint, I would say it would be pretty hard to forget her. I keep insisting that she wasn’t a figment of my imagination. But since I really had no proof of her existence or pictures other than my own memory I stopped pursuing the subject in confusion and eventually forgot about it.  

But then, this past summer the city wanted the land of the countryside to be cleared for buildings. So me and my parents went back in August to clear out the things in our countryside home which we hadn’t sold yet. We had this storage bunker on top of our regular floors, and I was scavenging through what to keep and what to throw when I came across a parcel wrapped in this old fraying brown wrapping. I tore it open and found a Takara Jenny doll still in its packaging. These were very popular then; I remember always wanting them as a kid. Initially, I threw it aside without a glance, but then something about the doll stood out to me. I carefully picked it up again and observed its features: the doll had black hair, pale skin and rosy cheeks. It was a holiday edition collection; she was bundled up with scarves, boot accessories and a poofy red jacket. I froze. 

Running downstairs, I asked my parents, holding the doll in my hand. 

“Oh! Those things were very popular then, weren’t they? I think my friend’s sister came and brought you that when you were like seven! We wanted to save it for your birthday so we hid it, but I guess we forgot to give you your gift…”
