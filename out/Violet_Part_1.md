I used to think I was crazy. I distinctly remember my 2nd grade class going over the visible light spectrum.

My teacher made an off-hand comment. "And then there's violet, but you can't really see that."

I looked around at my class, but everyone just looked bored or distracted by something else. I almost raised my hand, but the reaction from the rest of the class left made me afraid I was wrong. 

Could no one else see the giant, vibrant, piercing color at the bottom of the rainbow? 

Plus, how do you even explain a color? Violet is unlike any other color, but aren't all colors that way? Like how do you describe green versus yellow?

I tried telling people about my little visual quirk, but they usually just looked at me sideways. I even told a doctor once and they just sort of nodded, furrowed their brow, and kicked me out shortly after.

Call it a subconscious byproduct of my condition, but I generally avoided art. My talents usually lied in math...so much so that I got a full ride scholarship to a really prestigious technical college. 

I made a close knit group of friends while I was there, call us: Sam, Erin, Dylan and me, Finn. We all started out different majors, but our friendship that stemmed from our gen ed classes ended up having us try and coordinate classes. Eventually we all found venn diagram overlap of all of our interests in Material Science.

Senior year we all were able to pick our capstone group, where we got to work on a research product for a whole semester. Yes we were friends, but we also did a good job at different skills. I was math, Erin was science, Sam was engineering, and Dylan brought the creativity to bring it all together.

To start off the semester, each capstone group of four did an ice breaker, 2 truths and a lie. The other three went first and we all guessed and laughed, but we knew so much about one another it was hard to come up with a truth they'd never heard. So for the first time ever, to any of them, I threw out the visual quirk. My actual lie was innocuous enough that it kind of gave me an out where I could pretend it was true and that the visual quirk was the lie.

"No way!", Erin said with a chuckle in her voice.

Sam chimed in too. "Yeah he's BSing on that, reverse psychology, think of something so out there that we would have to think it's true even though it's clearly the lie!"

The only one who reacted in a way that made me hesitate was Dylan. He just sat there, head quirked to the side, eyes half slit closed.

I mustered up the courage and actually told them that it was true. Erin and Sam thought it was funny and sort of gave me the "yeah, sure..." that friends give when they know the other one is lying. But Dylan stood still like a statue the whole time.

"Hey man", I said to Dylan.

He shook his head and gave a plastered on smile. "Hey! Sorry, you know I space. Did we finish up that stupid icebreaker?"

Dylan became noticably stiffer from that point and became quicker to anger when discussing the capstone.

When we picked our research topic, he was adamant. "We need to infuse a new polymer, one that has never existed."

We all kind of looked around awkwardly and just kind of said okay, just to keep him from blowing up on us.

Our advisor was a stern, stone faced man, with a gut that gave him the geometry of an M&M mascot. "My thoughts? This is way too much of an undertaking for an undergrad team of four. If you want, I could guide you to...".

Dylan slammed his hand on the table like he intended to eviserate a fly. "NO! THIS IS WELL WITHIN OUR CAPABILITIES."

Our teacher, despite his stoney demeanor, eventually yielded to Dylan's insistance once he got confirmation that the rest of us were all onboard as well.

Dylan showed up that next week with scribbled shapes, diagrams, and honestly incoherent scratches of paper that he threw at our desk.

"This is what I'm working on. Uhhh....Sam you can check the notes, Erin just verify that we have the materials available, and Finn I need you in the lab with me."

Call us apathetic, but honestly none of us were able to keep up with Dylan. He worked relentlessly on this project. He spend every waking our in the lab like a mole rat burrowed underground.

I would show up to the lab when he called me, but he would usually just have me sit there and watch him work.

That was until last week. Sam corrected a couple of small errors on Dylan's diagrams and Erin was able to acquire the requested materials.

Dylan called my phone in a manic state. "I need you in the lab. I am shortly approaching the apex! The material is almost complete! Now! HURRY! NOW!"

I did hurry, in my own way. I meandered over, a little hesitant for sure...but Dylan had always been eccentric, so I hoped that this "apex" would result in an A and for Dylan to finally calm down.

When I walked in, Dylan greeted me in a panic, with owl wide eyes. "QUICK! SEE WHAT HAPPENS."

In front of me, lasers from three equidistant points burrowed into the material that Erin had got. They moved rapidly, almost matching Dylan's manic energy. I stared, unblinking as a light began to appear. Not any light, but a vibrant Violet. I almost didn't hear Dylan click the lock to the door behind me. My subconscious told me that there was danger here, but my body fought that urge and drove me closer to the growing Violet. 

On a table in front of me a Mantis Shrimp lay on the table, twitching weakly.

I heard Dylan's voice, a shout that sounded like a whisper. "Remember it all. Tell me what they want from us!"

And like that I dipped into a deep pool of Violet. Gravity left like I was in water, but the Violet gave less resistance than water. Enough where I could flail around and move, but not enough where it felt like anything surrounded me.

That was until I sensed it. Not in my periphery, but in front of me. An almost imperceptible change in the Violet. In encompassed the middle of my sight, I tried turning away and moving from it, but it stayed there. 

It had an elongated face further lengthened by a hollow mouth that stretched beyond that of a human jaw. The eye sockets were just as hollow, yet this creature stared at me in a way that was undeniable.

The rest came together like one of those magic eye pictures, you see one detail and all of the sudden everything morphs. The creature stared, unmoving, but behind it a sea of razor sharp teeth gnashed at these frail, shadowed beings. They screamed in my head, I tried to plug my ears but it did nothing.

My utmost focus was required to maintain the picture and even then, the images shifted just out of sight. The gnashing, the hideous face, and feeble beings all existed just out of sight at all times.

The face spoke. "All roads end here. To avoid is to succumb. You peer at the presipious like none before have."

The Violet closed in on me like walls of a cavern. Clausterphobic fear took hold of me and I tried to wiggle free. But then I felt beings behind and in front of me doing the same. Kicking, screaming, primal instincts accelerating the panic between us all.

It felt like it went on for years. The voice never stopped speaking, but few things stuck with me.

Something about parallel space. Worship through long lost text. Stuff that I couldn't concentrate on, believing I was in a perpetual clausterphobic space that I could not be released from.

Then all the sudden the voice laughed, signaling the end and the Violet rescinded.

What was left was Dylan staring with a notepad in hand.

"What did it say?"

I was completely devoid of thought. 

"TELL ME!", he slammed his hand against the table smashing the Mantis Shrimp.

I told him virtually what I told you above, but one thing did stick out to me. The Mantis Shrimp reminded me. There was a lot of talk about depth, about things beyond what we have seen. At first I thought of depth in a metaphorical sense, but the pressure I felt and the anti-gravity sensation made me feel as though maybe I was deep in the Earth. Deep in the water. Deeper than we have ever explored in the ocean.

Dylan nodded along as I told him all this, like he expected everything I was saying.

When I finished his wild eyes stared deeply into mine, a wide grin plastered across his face. 

"Well then, looks like I have a new goal. Get you deep into the Earth, deeper than we've ever been. The text is there, the answer to our salvation!"

He handcuffed me to a rail after that, as I write this he's shuffling back and forth in front of me...working with hooded figures on a schematic for what looks to be a box of some kind. 

He told me to write this. He wants the world to know. Because he wants help and he knows there are others who want to see past our visual limits, find what exists just out of our sight.

I was hesitant, but at this point I fear that the only way I escape this with my life is to get him the help he needs. So please, if you can provide anything to help get me into the furthest depths of the Earth I need you to reach out now before they toss me in a lead box just to see what happens!
