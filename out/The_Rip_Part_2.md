Update (08/26/26):

  
I apologize for the gap in my follow up. Things have gotten worse…far worse. Food and fuel, basically anything that costs money is running low. I don’t know how much time I have until I can’t do anything anymore. I feel like a stray. I only have myself to depend on. I feel so alone. I should be desensitized to what will eventually be my fate. It’s never the visual of it, only the sounds. The shrieks of horror and confusion only send me into a deeper spiral than I already am in now.



I will never forget the first time I saw “it” take someone. As I mentioned in my first post, which I would read first before you continue, Rich and I’s minds went to work the day after this all started. The weekend of May 23rd, 2026 will be a time that I remember for the rest of the time I have on this planet, and will forever be the most horrifying day of my life, at least for now. I still haven’t been able to contact any of my friends, or family. The day that I find out about them might take 1st place.



Rich was right on time, 12:00 pm on the dot. He came up to the door with a mighty roar. A few thuds hit the door, shaking every window in the house and waking me up. My mom opened it with a large thrust in annoyance.



“Yes? Oh, hello Richard. What brings you here?”



“Is Dean home?”



“Yes? At least I hope so? He hasn’t left his room. I’ll go check for you though.”



I hadn’t slept well that night at all. I slid out of bed and shuffled to my door and cracked it to see two blurry silhouettes. I cocked my head towards my alarm clock and noticed the time. My brain went on autopilot and immediately grabbed any clothes I could find to drape over myself. By the time my mom got to my door I was in full panic mode. A mangled mess of cotton and polyester.



“Honey, Richard is here for you.” She said with a couple taps on the door’s trim.



I was midway through putting my socks on, balancing on one leg as I tried pulling one up my ankle. And as she knocked, it startled me enough to knock me flat on the floor. As my vision came to me I could see Rich walking away from the door, hand over his mouth and as red as a tomato.



“I’ll be just a second.” I groaned, rubbing my hip as I pulled myself up off of the floor.



“Take your time.” My mom said, walking off joining the giggle party.



Only after a little bit of pouting, I eventually got my socks on and bolted outside to see Rich with what I would call a shit grin on his face.



“That floor has been so nice to you, man. What did you hit it so hard for?”



“Oh shut up, dude. You act like you’ve never fallen before.”



“Did it say something to you?”



“Dude!”



“You can tell me what it said.”



I punched him straight in his shoulder.



“Ah damn, dude. Calm down.”



“Should’ve stopped.”



“You should’ve told me you were going to treat me like the floor. Now I know how it feels.”



I hit him again in the same spot.



“Okay I’ll stop!” He said, wincing and holding his arm.



We got into his car and set our sights towards, what I call now, “The Rip”. It was larger, noticeably larger. It resembled a lightning bolt, with many dwindling branches spanning out. It was wide enough to almost see the inside of its nothingness. The sky around it looked ever so slightly lighter than the rest of the blue hue. 



The car ride towards the school was a quiet one. I am unsure what Rich was feeling, if our feelings were reciprocated, but I felt eagerness and dread. The thought of figuring out what the rip was and the sense of danger made a troublesome knot in my brain. Both of our sets of eyes didn’t leave the road until we got close, then shifted to the black microcosm in the sky. We parked on a side, gravel road. No matter how deep into the flowing corn fields the school was swallowed into, no matter how full or empty the parking lot was, the sheriff's office was always on standby to catch any risky vandals who would try to possibly deface the school. 



“Did you bring anything with you?” I asked Rich, being as I was very ill prepared.



“Let’s see.” Rich grabbed a backpack out of his trunk and began rummaging through it, “I have a couple of waters, some binoculars, my camera, and for the grand finale, this!”



“Is that a drone?”



“Hmm, you don’t say.” He said back in his normal, smart ass tone.



I rolled my eyes behind my eyelids but showed only a sense of impression towards his prep work,

“I’ll ignore the tone because that is sick! And smart too!”



“Thank you, thank you. Now let’s get to it. I have to work at four.”



My next twenty minutes were spent with me looking through the binoculars, for obvious reasons, while Rich messed with his drone. It was a pretty nice drone. It had a built-in 1080p camera and could fly to ranges of up to three miles before disconnecting. It was the perfect apparatus to fly to high levels in the sky. 



In this time I noticed some…strange things, to say the least. The blue sky of the day faded into grey the closer it got to it. And looking into it was astonishing. Empty black, the darkest dark I had ever seen. At the angle we were at it appeared to travel within it as far back as the mind could comprehend. Almost an empty expanse. And lastly, a single bird underneath it. It lay as still as concrete, and the normal browns and blacks of its feathers were faded into lighter shades.



“Have you got that thing connected yet? I can only stare at this thing for so long.” I said impatiently, not wanting to spoil to him what I had seen. I don’t think he would have believed it without seeing it.



“I think so? Ope!” He jumped back with the whirl of the blades, “We are up and running.



He mounted his phone to the controller and lifted the drone into the sky. It zipped straight up with a strong gust of wind that sent dust from the road into our eyes. The first thing he did was try to match its altitude. When he got to it though it disappeared. After bobbing the drone up and down a couple times, we at least figured out one thing about the rip. It was as flat and as thin as a piece of paper and could only be seen from below. 



“What the hell?” Rich said in shock.



“Go closer and look into it. I think it might be some kind of rift or something?”



He corrected its course towards it, the surrounding area. It was almost completely color washed. He eventually spotted the bird, confirming how still it sat in the sky. Looking closer, its head was arched straight up towards the rip.



“That shouldn’t be possible, should it?”



“There’s a black crack in the sky, I don’t know what is possible anymore.” I said back trying to match his previous snarky energy, “Keep going.”



As the drone headed closer, we noticed the camera feed was starting to fizzle out.



“This is such a scam. It said the range was much farther than this. Piece of shit, I’m bringing it back.”



But as he began to turn it around, it slowed and then stopped, displaying the ground. We both sat back starstruck as to what just happened. We looked at the phone to see an almost white washed house underneath with a woman’s head, cocked straight up like the birds, looking straight at us…or what we thought was us.and then the feed went out. Her skin was unnaturally white. Her eyes were cloudy, the look of advanced cataracts. Every ounce of color was wiped from each follicle of her hair. As a matter of fact, every piece of clothing, every strand of grass, every flower in the woman’s flower bed was an unsettling shade of light gray at the darkest.

  
	  
“Did you see that shit?” I said, the words almost gushing out of my mouth.

  
	  
“I did. You know, this is one time that I wish I didn’t have my glasses on.”

  
	  
And then it clicked.

  
	  
“Dude! That’s Ms. Jenkins’ house!”

  
	  
“And I’m supposed to care about that hag?”

  
	  
“She may be a hag, but you saw that. We have to go look.”

  
	  
“Fine, I guess she is just a person after all.”

  
	  
“Great observation. Now let’s go.”

  
	  
We got into the car and skidded off towards her house, not knowing what we were about to pull up to. We arrived, being careful not to get too close to the greys and whites of her lawn.

  
	  
“Ms. Jenkins’? It’s Dean, can you hear me?”

  
	  
Her head stayed stuck upright and her eyes stayed looking straight at the rip. Her hands sat slumped to her sides. On one hand sat a garden pale, stuck at the tips of fingers as if it were glued there. She sat knelt down on her knees as if she were praying. One eye broke free and shot its cloudy gaze towards us. Her jaw began to move slowly. Like a gear that hadn’t been turned for hundreds of years. 

  
	  
“Dean?” She said, almost unintelligible. Her tongue lay in the back of her mouth causing her speech to sound gurgly and the words coming out in slow motion,   
“Help.”

  
	  
Her mouth locked back up after the last sound of the word. Every letter almost sat stuck in the air in front of her face. Each syllable echoing faintly towards the sky.

  
	  
“What should we do?” Rich asked in a panic.

  
	  
I looked down at the ground, noticing the grey strands of grass start to grow closer to our feet.

  
	  
“We need to go!”

  
	  
“We can’t just leave her here!”

  
	  
“Yes, we can! Now c’mon! We will call the police on our way back, let’s just go!”

  
	  
“But what if this happens to them too?”

  
	  
“I didn’t think about that…It doesn’t matter, we still need to go!”

  
	  
Tears started to well up in Rich’s eyes as the realization of what had just happened, what was happening…what is still happening, set in. I grabbed him by his wrist and yanked him towards the car door. We both sat in silence as imaginary police sirens rang in my head, not knowing what could happen to them if they tried to step foot in the “Grey Area”. 

  
	  
“So, are we going to talk about what the hell just happened?” I asked in begrudging curiosity.  
	  
“I’d rather not.” Rich replied, sniffling and curled up against the steering wheel. I assumed he was grappling with what he had said prior as well.

  
	  
“Did it…did it kill her?”

  
	  
“I think so…”

  
	  
“Why was she so…why was everything so…and why was everything just stuck there?”

  
	  
“I don’t know, Dean!”

  
	  
The car went silent once more.

  
	  
“I’m sorry. I’m just…I don’t…”

  
	  
“No, it’s okay. I’ll stop talking about it.” I said, reassuring him.

  
	  
The rest of the car ride was a slog. Our minds raced, not being able to pinpoint any fathomable thing that could relate to what we had witnessed. After an eternity we pulled into my driveway.

  
	  
“We can talk about this later. I get off at nine so just call me around ten?”

  
	  
“Yeah, that works.”

  
	  
“Good, I just…need to get my thoughts together.”

  
	  
“I understand.”

  
	  
I walked into my house, the aroma of a crock-pot dinner souring my mood even more. My mom’s head peaked in from the kitchen.

  
	  
“Hi honey. Oh lord, you look like you’ve seen a ghost. Is everything okay?”

  
	  
“Well, you could say I have.” I murmured quietly.

  
	  
“What do you mean?”

  
	  
“Nothing…I’m fine. I’ll talk about it later.”

  
	  
“Okay? Just know if anything is bothering you, you can always tell me.”

  
	  
“I know...thanks mom.”

  
	  
“You’re welcome. Now go rest that hip of yours. I’ll call you when dinner’s ready.”

  
	  
All I could do was let out the slightest chuckle before my face went flat. I went to my room and laid face down into my pillow, until I heard a buzzing from my night stand.

  
	  
“Oh shit, my phone!”

  
	  
I had left it sitting there the entire time. I flipped the screen up to my face and saw ten missed calls from April. I instantly called her back. After three agonizingly long rings she eventually picked up.

  
	  
“Dean?”

  
	  
“Listen, I’m so sorry. I was out with Rich and I left my phone at home.”

  
	  
“Can we see each other tonight?”

  
	  
“What could this be about?” I wondered to myself with another knot tying above the first, “Yeah, sure. If you want. Is everything okay?”

  
	  
“Yeah, I just need to talk to you. Are you okay? You sound spazztic.”

  
	  
“Yeah, yeah. I just need to tell you something, too.”

  
	  
“Okay then. Can we meet at the lake at, say, six? And can we not invite everyone in town this time?” She said sarcastically.

  
	  
“Yes, that’d be perfect.”

  
	  
“Okay, see you then.”

  
	  
Before I could say another word she hung up. The thoughts of what I did to screw up this time meshed right into all of my other thoughts. What was this thing?  Why was everything around it just…stuck? Why is it taking the color from everything? Was it taking the life out of everything? If not life, then…energy? Or was it just a black hole, or a wormhole, taking everything into it? If it was a black hole it wouldn’t leave anything behind, so it has to be life right? Or energy? 

  
	  
All of these thoughts carried into a quiet dinner, on my part at least. Maybe I was just sounding everything out. I sat picking at my pot roast, not knowing how much of it I could stomach.

  
	  
“Son, you haven’t added much to the conversation.” My dad said in an almost stern tone.

  
	  
“I’m sorry. I just have a lot on my mind right now.”

  
	  
“The boy walked in as white as our plates earlier.” My mom chimed in, the word white making me flinch a little.

  
	  
“Are you okay? Did something happen today?” My dad responded.

  
	  
“It’s just…” At that moment, I remembered April, giving me the best excuse to stray around the actual problem, “April is giving me some off putting vibes right now, and I don’t know what to do about it. She said she wanted to talk later and I am scared about what she wants to talk to me about.”

  
	  
“Well Dean, you are a young man. And no matter what, if she breaks up with you, there are probably countless girls lined up waiting for their chance.”

  
	  
“Tom!” My mom exclaimed, smacking him on the shoulder, “What your father means is that we are here for you no matter what happens.”

  
	  
“And what I mean is that I never really liked that April girl.” My dad quipped back.

  
	  
“Thanks guys, that really helps.” I said, sarcastically,  going back to playing with my food.

  
	  
The rest of dinner was quiet, a common theme of the day that was likely to continue. By the time it was over, it was time for me to head towards the lake. I arrived a little before six hoping to get there before April did, but to no avail. Her headlights beamed off of the lake's surface, showing colors I was relieved to see. 

  
	  
“Hey honey.”

  
	  
“Get in.” She said, sternly.

  
	  
“Okay?”

  
	  
I sat down in her passenger seat.

  
	  
“Now, I’m going to let you say what you want to first because, from the sounds of it, it seems like it has you all over the place.”

  
	  
I began to tell her about what Rich and I had stumbled upon a few hours prior.

  
	  
“So she was just stuck there?”

  
	  
“Yes!”

  
	  
“And she was completely faded?”

  
	  
“Yes!”

  
	  
“And you think it’s because of that thing in the sky?”

  
	  
“The Rip.”

  
	  
“You named it?”

  
	  
“You don’t believe a thing I am saying right now, do you?”

  
	  
“It just sounds ridiculous.”

  
	  
“I saw someone, most likely, die today and you’re calling it ridiculous?”

  
	  
“Well, did you call anyone?”

  
	  
“We were too scared to. His drone froze up there. She froze. There’s no telling what could happen to them if we did call.”

  
	  
“This is a load of horse shit.”

  
	  
“I don’t want to risk anyone else’s life. Do you think it wasn’t hard not calling the police?”

  
	  
“No, I don’t. Because I don’t believe any of this.”

  
	  
“Well, let’s go there then.”

  
	  
“Right now?”

  
	  
“Yes, right now! Because I feel like I’m alone here. You probably think I’m crazy! I’ve struggled with this all damn day, and I don’t want you adding onto it!” I said, starting to cry.

  
	  
At this point, she began to be swayed.

  
	  
“Okay…I believe you. Please, just calm down.”

  
	  
“I’m trying!”

  
	  
After a few minutes of shedding tears, she began to speak again.

  
	  
“Dean, listen. I’ve been wanting to talk to you about us, or me specifically.”

  
	  
I lifted my head up from my lap.

  
	  
“I haven’t been feeling like myself recently. And I’m not saying I don’t want you to leave my life because you are my best friend,” she began to cry, “but I want to be the best woman I can for you if we are going to be together and I just don’t think I can be that woman at the moment. I’m sorry if this will hurt us, but I’m breaking up with you.”

  
	  
“I…I understand.”, was all I could say at that moment. 

  
	  
“I still love you, I promise. I just have to love myself more right now and I feel like I can’t.”

  
	  
“I…I love you too. I…” I said back, shakily, storming out of the car back into my own.

  
	  
“Dean, please, don’t go!”

  
	  
I couldn’t listen. All I could do was drive. And I drove. I drove on almost every road in the county. I passed by the blinding lights of cars and the dim lights of street corners. My speeding escapade was eventually stopped. I approached a car on the road, a loud screeching sound consumed the air with sparks and smoke flying from the back. The lights were angled out towards the surrounding fields that lined the road. This car was sideways on the road. As I got closer, my lights illuminated a color stripped wedge of the road. I turned my high beams on to inspect closer. 



As my eyes traveled upwards, I noticed that half of the car’s color was stripped, and the person’s head, arched up and out of the window, was as white as Ms. Jenkins' had been previously. I followed the person’s gaze upwards to see that “The Rip’s” size had doubled, including the “Grey Area”. The person across from me on the road got unlucky and stumbled a little too close to our discovery, becoming part of it. I got out to check on the person to see if I could hopefully get some sort of response, but as I looked down the dark black asphalt turned to grey at the tips of my toes. I bolted back to my car and turned it around in the middle of the road as carefully as possible and pushed my engine all the way home in anticipation to tell Rich what I had stumbled across.



I’m going to have to cut this update here. At least I could finally tell you what I am dealing with now. But thinking back on this is bringing up emotions I didn’t think would be able to resurface in this wasteland of agony. I think I have avoided it, for now, but I need to find a place to refuel. Hopefully I don't use my last dollars on this trek. I will update again when I have reached a good place to do so.
