I've got an easy job, not the easiest job, but it's not like brain surgery or anything. I'm a groundskeeper for a large collection of bougie condominiums associated with a retirement company. I don't really know the specifics of how people apply to get in, but I know how they leave.

Even if I tried to type in the name for you to cross off your potential lists, you won't remember it. Not until you're standing on the property itself hemming and hawing about carpet accents. By then, you won't care.

I got the job about fifteen years ago. I was a couple of months out of an undergraduate degree that wasn't taking me anywhere, so I just started interviewing at all the gigs popping up. New restaurants, shops, or homes. Somewhere was bound to be in need of a cleaner, which really turned out to be the case with the condos. Obviously it wound up to be more of the outdoor-cleaning-type than indoor, but tomahto tomato y'know?

They were buying up a failed condo project from a few years prior and were planning on aiming towards the usual retired crowd. It was a nice area to be fair, supermarket was only a five minute car ride away.

I'd spent most of my teenage years doing yard cleanup for my parents anyway, so the learning curve wasn't too bad. I just needed to prove I wasn't stupid and could go along with a background check. I initially thought they were concerned about hiring potential-robbers but now I now they didn't want to hire plant food.

The first red flag I should have noticed was that I was the only one hired to work the grounds. It's at minimum, during peak season, a ten person job. Like, it's a huge property once you look at in on a map. Even if you were to ignore the actual woods and just try to keep the grass and weeds under control around the houses, you'd still be way too behind on other problem areas.

Even with being the only one on staff in the department, I was told in no uncertain terms that I was only expected to work from sunrise to sundown at my own discretion. Monday through Friday, barring emergencies. (Like fallen branches or snow plowing.)

It was kind of weird at first. My everyday was just making sure that the surrounding wilderness looked appealing to people touring and also making sure there was nothing for the target demographic to trip over. I kept expecting to get hounded over how slow I was making progress, but I never was.

It was kind of satisfying to rescue all the bushes and flower beds from the invading ivy. I'd gently snip it all back and uproot as much as I could, then the bush will billow out with leaves after receiving its first taste of sunlight in years.

All in all, it probably took me about two years to beat back the overgrown ivy. That was the second red (well, more so weird than alerting) flag, the ivy was so incredibly overgrown but there were no weeds. It was all just the ivy and some overgrown grass.

That's just not how nature responds to being able to grow without restriction.

It did make the first few months of my job much easier. I was basically a glorified grass-trimmer who got to plant whatever native flowers I wanted in the designated areas. I think that's the most I ever felt really connected to any type work, like I was reading long winded textbooks and everything! I really do enjoy the job.

It was during my first shift in the actual woods on the property that I realized something was just, wrong. Like, I'm not a ecosystem expert or a botanist or anything, but the woods were too clean. Ignoring the almost complete lack of diversity in the local plant life, there was also no garbage. No beer cans, no cigarette butts, nothing.

I volunteered at a park clean up once. No matter how deep you got into those woods, there was litter. There was litter under the bushes, there was litter under the benches, there was litter in the trees, there was EVIDENCE that humans had been there.

These woods, with all the hiking trails that were supposedly one of the key selling points of living in the condos, seemed completely untouched. I distinctly remember the chill that ran down my spine when I went for a two hour hike and realized my trash bag was still empty. It should have felt like a good thing, but it really didn't.

It was after that joust through the woods that I became almost obsessive about never littering, accidentally or otherwise. I don't know how or why I got that idea into my head, but it felt right. Like I had finally had one of the rules of the land embedded into my mind. I know a lot more now, which is why I'm bothering to type this all out. As much as it feels good to talk to someone real for once, you'll never find me. I'm just your warning.

Regardless, there were several other little things like that that kept creeping me out. I don't think I've raked once while working here. Once I even sat and watched leaves fall from some trees on the opposite side of the street to the entry gate. Even when a large gust of wind came by and rustled all of them, none of them found their way onto the sidewalk bordering the condos. Again, it was just weird.

It took two years for me to realize where the ivy was coming from, which I'm a little embarrassed about. Once you see him for the first time, it's pretty obvious. I didn't even really notice at first when we had our first chat.

You can't really chart your way over to him, I only found his spot because he wanted me to dispose of some litter. I think some of it was actual bona fide trash, the rest of it was the clothes of the litterers.

The main reason I was initially uneasy about the lack of litter is the fact that there's a high school like, ten blocks south of us. Anyone with brain cells can understand why having unguarded property with a high senior population might cause some clash with the local teen crowd. It was the only part of the job that made me hesitate when I initially signed on. I really thought I'd be scrubbing half-assed graffiti off every inch of the grounds my first day. But again, nothing.

It's hard to remember how I first got there. I remember getting out of my truck. My goal for that day was to start the installation of a vegetable garden in one of the flatter fields. I'd already run it past management and everything, just needed to get my lazy ass in motion.

I'd hammered in the first post when I suddenly stopped, dropped my hammer, and walked into the woods. I don't remember hearing anything or even thinking anything. It was like my body went on auto-pilot. It felt kind of nice, like I was feeling nostalgic for something I'd never felt. I wanted to go home, but not to a home I recognized.

I remember walking for what must have only been about five minutes, when I came across this really tall oak tree growing in a large clearing. There were rabbits jumping about and birds flying from tree to tree. There was an incredible abundance of life. The sun was bright on my skin and the air was clearer than I'd ever tasted. I felt giddy and practically skipped to the center tree, like a trick-or-treater expecting candy. 

Anyone who actually knows me would understand how horrifyingly out of character this is for me. I don't skip nor do I chortle.

When I got right up to the tree, my eyes were drawn to all the moths writhing on it. It wasn't any specific type, just all of them. Any moth you could think of, pantry moths to rosy maple moths. Tiny ones were crawling over the larger ones, larger ones were displacing the smaller ones when they landed. It was harmonious chaos. You could hear them all fluttering once you got close enough. I don't know how I knew this for certain, but every type of them that exists was present. They just were. After a few more minutes of staring, I realized that they were all trying to squirm their way into a tiny crack in the bark.

I almost felt tempted to take a peek, but I knew it wasn't my turn yet.

After I finally peeled my attention away from the tree, I looked down to the ground. There was a neatly tied up recycling bag that contained a selection of beer cans and next to it, a stack of neatly folded, soiled clothes. I don't remember thinking anything weird about it. I just nodded, picked it all up, and trotted all the way back over to the dumpsters.

It wasn't until about three hours later when I'd finished assembling the general frame of the vegetable garden that I panicked. It was like my mind had finally dethawed from whatever had called to me and I was myself again.

This wasn't like the kind of panic that felt justified, it was the kind of panic that I felt could only be experienced prior to death.

I'm a bit ashamed to admit I just hid in my shed for the remainder of my shift. I don't think I could have left if I wanted to. No one came looking for me because there was absolutely no work that needed to be done on the land, but I was still terrified. Every time I saw the trees sway outside of the window I was terrified I'd see him, walking or something. I don't really know what I was afraid of happening, but nothing ever came of it.

The clock finally reached a time that I felt it would be reasonable for me to clock out. The sun had set a while ago, so I was ready to just book it home and move to a different country tomorrow. I was halfway to my truck when I remembered how many goddamn tools I'd just left lying out in the field. As much as I wanted to run, I knew I'd feel even worse if one of the old folks stumbled over my toolbelt and broke their neck.

I quietly drove my way back over to the field and quickly scooped all my tools up. It was well past nine PM now and the dead of night was truly creeping in. I glanced up at what I thought was the sound of footsteps, but didn't see anyone near me. Suddenly, I felt a tickle at my ankle.

I froze and looked down to see long strands of ivy growing smoothly through the grass, past me, towards the condos. I was too far away to see which direction they'd come from, but I just knew it was from him. I learned a long time ago to trust the thoughts that plant themselves in my head. He likes keeping me around for some reason, so I get hints every now and then.

But I stood there watching, ivy gently circling around my ankle, as it crept up and up and up and over the first house and kept prowling. I saw the floodlights turn on at the ninth house, then immediately shut off.

In that moment, I had just decided that enough was enough. It was all too weird for minimum wage, even if the hours were pretty good. I gently unwound the ivy from my leg as tears streamed down my face. I'm really surprised I didn't piss my pants that night, but I was definitely openly sobbing by the time I stormed into my manager's office.

I shouted something along the lines of 'being done with all this shit' and everyone being 'weird as hell' and that I was 'never coming back so you might as well fire me'.

She just blankly stared at me and then asked if she'd misheard me. I was about to tell her to go screw herself, when my peripherals caught movement outside the window.

I slowly turned and saw that everyone was staring at me, blankly. Every single one of them. From their yards, from their windows, and even from the front yard of my manager's office. Most of them were limp, like they were still sleeping. Their eyes were definitely on me though. I even saw the ivy curling in the night sky above the houses, highlighted by the moonlight. It looked like it was wet with something dark.

My manager asked me one more time if I was certain about what I just said, and I stumbled out an apology. I made up an excuse about being unsatisfied with my pay and having difficulty sleeping.

I got out of there in under five minutes and woke up the next day to an email proclaiming that I'd received a bonus for my 'spectacular behavior' and some other nonsense.

She also mentioned that a new family would be touring the ninth condominium, so it would be much appreciated if I could pull all the overgrown ivy down before the showing.

I know it's gonna sound stupid, but it honestly was a high enough pay-raise that I completely backed out of ghosting the job. If it makes you feel any better, I did hesitate a bit. I think I had a stronger back bone when I was younger though.

I've never really felt threatened while on site anyway. Frankly, I feel more like a stray worker ant that's being humored by a spider. A big scary spider that could crush me from hundreds of miles away, but whatever. I think its only settled near the condos because it's an easy feeding ground. I don't really think anything is actually keeping it here or restraining it in anyway. It's an absurd thought, but maybe this is its form of retirement.

I've been here for years now and have never actually seen anyone move out. No one comes to collect their leftover things either, it all just vanishes.

That's why I'm really concerned about the stipulations with my next raise though. It's an absurd amount, like don't tell estranged family members levels of money, but I'd have to move to living on site. The email said that a special spot has already been built for me, so at least it isn't in any of the cursed condos.

I kept trying to find the words to decline the offer, but no matter what I type it turns into grateful acceptance. Even while typing up this whole thing, my hands kept formatting the email within this post. I don't think I'll be able to put it off for much longer, I'll probably wake up having already written the response anyway.

I don't think I've been aging lately either. I don't get callouses anymore. My hair has stopped greying. I don't feel any degradation in my body from how much physical labor I've been performing. I think I'm being accepted into something larger than myself, my own little niche in the ecosystem. I just hope I still get to take my walks down to the local library.

I don't think I'll ever be allowed to quit now. Not that I'd ever want to anyway.

Regardless, don't come here. I think I'm just a lucky person. I've hopefully planted enough fragments in your head for you to recognize his presence when you're near here. Just be polite to Mary when she's showing you the property and never come back. 

The plumbing's finicky anyway.
