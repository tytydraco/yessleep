Skiing has become my hobby ever since my college made us go to a skiing resort for P.E lessons during winter. I live in Russia, and in Russia it is pretty common to make students practice skiing during winter months or as long as there is snow outside, since usually it starts snowing around in October. In my school we were just going around the field in the circle. But when I was at college, we had to go to the skiing resort. We would always go down the same path through the woods, and when we end up back at our starting point, we can go home. These P.E lessons were usually the last ones for the day, so it was fun to take a break from lectures and do something physical and relaxing.

The feeling of my first stroll was incredible. Nature all around, music in my ears, and also physical exercise made me addicted. I started skiing on my own, simply to relax and get my mind off things. I was never interested in any sports, but I enjoyed strolling through the woods and sliding down the ramps, observing the beautiful scenery of my town's forests. All the heavy stress and problems melted in that place; I felt free and unbothered.  Like I became hollow, in a good way.

After I dropped out, I had plenty of time to go to the resort as much as I want during winter, since I was also unemployed. So one day, I decided to go for a stroll on another route. There are different routes that are meant for either skiing or walking through the woods. I explored almost every one, except for one that was way bigger than my usual route, the 10-kilometer route. It's definitely meant for people who REALLY like skiing, which just so happens to be me! So I went to the ski-renting counter at the lobby, since I didn't have my own skis back then, and asked to give me the skis fit for my size.

"Want to change routes, boy?" - A man behind the counter asked me.

"Yes. I want to go for a long one this time."

"Alright, just be sure to follow the arrow signs so you don't get lost. It can get pretty confusing sometimes for newbies. Crossroads and all."

"I know, it's not my first time here." - I chuckled.

"Need a flare gun, just in case you get lost?"

The counterman liked to ask the new visitors if they would like a flare gun. They are not supposed to give flare guns to visitors, as far as I knew, which I always found odd, but I never thought of this as anything more than just a joke. After all, the forest was big, and you can get easily lost if you don't recognize various landmarks.

"Why do you ask people if they need a flare gun?"

"You want it? I'll give it to ya."

"..I don't want to buy it"

"It's free, just a safety measure."

Man pulled out an old-looking flare gun from under the counter. I raised my brows in surprise. I thought he was kidding, but apparently not. This looked like an actual flare gun. I actually remember firing one of those during a new year celebration. My grandpa on the mother's side of the family works in the military, and he wanted to fire a few shots for fun. It was a bit hard to fire for me since my ears are sensitive, and it felt like the grip was a bit tight. But anyway..

"Why do you have it?"

"Used to rescue people, but then I retired after I grew old. So now I work here. And I sometimes come to help people who got lost or went off trail by accident. I remember some couple went for a stroll and got lost in the woods. Went off the trail, and we had to look for them. Found the lady sitting in the snow, but no partner in sight; she was pale as snow she was sitting on."

"That's.. Terrifying."

"We brought the girl back to the lobby, but we still haven't found her boyfriend. She couldn't respond to us, like she had a brain freeze. She was delivered to the hospital, and I did hear that she had a full recovery, but she never went back again. I haven't heard from that lady since. We never managed to find the man, and people still search for him, dead or alive. Maybe it all could've prevented if they just took the flare."

"Well.. Guess I will take it then. Thank you very much. I will make sure to give it back."

"No problem, kid."

He smiled at me and gave me my skis and my ski poles. He gave me a holster for the gun and 3 flares just in case. I thanked him, but at the same time, that story made me a bit worried. He never specified which route they took. But I shrugged it off; I tend to overthink things, so I thought that maybe it's not that deep. I put my skis on, wrapped the pole's straps around my wrists, and put on my earphones, and I was on my way.

The road didn't look that much different from other ones at first; I just had to remember that I am going for a 10 km stroll on my own. Each road has its own arrow sign, marked by color so you know what path you should take when you are at the crossroads. The arrows for the 10 km path were blue, and for the 5 km path, they were red, for example. Both roads started at the same starting point. I could have always turned the other way and gone through the 5 km path; maybe I shouldn't go on this path alone. But in my head, 10 km shouldn't be a problem, considering how much time I spent at this place. You have to challenge yourself sometimes. So I took a deep breath and followed the path.

Nothing seemed out of the ordinary at first. I felt relaxed as always, with my phone playing my favorite songs as I strolled. I felt a strange brief vibration. Usually these are caused by military cannon trainings at the polygon, a term for military training grounds, basically, for those who don't know. I am used to sudden bangs and booms that happen out of nowhere. Although I didn't hear what caused the vibration, it was probably because of music in my ears. Speaking of that, my music stopped at that exact moment. I was confused and thought that maybe I accidentally disconnected my earphones from my phone, but they were still connected. I thought maybe music paused, but it was still going, and volume wasn't changed. Great, my earphones died, I thought. They are connected, but they went mute. I just kept going, resorting to humming my tunes. I could have turned my music on at max volume on my phone, but it would probably be muffled by sounds of crunching snow beneath my feet, and the speaker on my phone is terrible.

I found another arrow sign and splitting paths.  But, something was off. I remember the map and direction you have to go on certain points. At the point I was on, I should go left. But it was blocked by a bunch of fallen trees. The arrow was pointing to the right. I thought that maybe they made another path that goes around the fallen trees, so I followed it. After all, I was told to follow the arrows so I don't get lost. The strange vibration I felt hasn't left my mind, though. I was ready to turn around and go back the way I came, but I have gone this far; I might as well keep going.

The road ahead felt a bit darker, the trees were covering up the sun above, and they felt taller. I tried singing songs to distract myself before I ended up finding another arrow. Another splitting path. It was pointing to the right again. I decided to take a break and have a sip of my water. I squatted and started squeezing the water from the bottle straight into my mouth, looking around as I did so. But then I noticed something odd. Footprints. Or should I say, ski prints, because whoever left them decided to go walk without taking off his skis. As funny as the visual of some dummy walking in deep snow with two planks on his feet was, I couldn't help but question the point of this. Why didn't they just turn around? This made me wonder if it's even worth going forward; maybe there's another obstacle on the way or more fallen trees. So I grabbed my poles and turned around, pushing myself in the opposite direction.

But somehow, after minutes of walking, I ended up at the same place, but I came from another direction. I turned to the tree next to me. The arrow. I came from the path the arrow was pointing at; only now the arrow was pointing to the left instead. Was my mind playing tricks on me? I looked around. It was all the same. Even the bottle that I left on the snow by accident was lying in the same place right where I left it. Either God didn't want me to loiter and brought me back, or I got lost. But the arrows' direction confused me. Maybe I just didn't pay too much attention to it, and it was always like that? I get distracted sometimes. Felt more confused and frustrated by whatever is going on. I had a feeling that trying to go back would be impossible, and walking in the snow back is not a good idea. I am not going to be like that weird person who left the prints over there. Guess I had to follow the arrows. It's not like I had anything else to do. But before I went, I put my bottle in my pocket.

Things only got stranger when I kept going. As I looked up, the sun was fully covered by massive trees around me. I didn't know pines could get this big; they made me feel small. When I tried to check the time, my phone was dead. It did get unusually cold, so it probably died due to temperature. I couldn't even get an emergency call. I had no way of contacting my friends and family. Damn, I knew it was a bad idea. I knew I should have been more careful; I knew I shouldn't go there alone. My brain began to hurt, and my heart pounded in my chest like it was replaced with the heart of a colibri. I felt like I was about to panic. I stopped in my tracks and took deep breaths. I pushed forward, telling myself that everything will be okay. I will make it out alive, I will come back home, and I will see the sun again. Of course, the idea of me not making out alive was too distracting. I was so distracted that I didn't notice a gap on the road. I nearly fell, but thankfully I managed to stop before I could have fallen into what looked like a deep snowy ravine. When I looked in front of me, all I saw was an arrow sign on a tree. Pointing downwards. I couldn't jump across; the gap was too big. Does the arrow want me to go back? Or does it want me to go down?

I was so shocked I laughed to myself. You are telling me I have to go into the ravine!? Since I couldn't turn around, I guess I had to follow. I tried to get the skis off me, but for a mysterious reason, I couldn't take them off; my hands felt too weak to do that. I tried to get them off me, but it was like they fused into my boots. I yelled and punched the ground; it helped me cool down. I got up and looked into the gap. I had to jump down. Taking a deep breath, I prepared myself for a jump.

One. Two. Three.

I jumped in and landed on my feet, hearing a crack in my legs. It hurt, but I did not break any bone. I was in the ravine. The walls around me were tall. I completely forgot this was supposed to be a skiing trail. I almost forgot why I came here. It's like my memory was slowly fading as I went deeper and deeper into these wretched woods. As I walked, I felt them; the vibrations have returned. But now I can hear them. It sounded like a loud hum. It was definitely not a sound of polygon cannons. What caused this? What am I even doing? Am I even getting to my destination? So many questions, so few answers. I just walked on autopilot.

The ravine's walls slowly got smaller and smaller until I finally saw familiar trees standing around me. As I turned around, I felt like I went through an arc of sorts. The trees above me were curved, apparently. I was in a more open area. I felt hollow, now in a bad way, because I felt like I was losing hope. My hands were clutching my poles, and my feet connected to the wooden planks. The poles were my arms, and the skis were my legs; they are now part of my body. I walked forward; my head was spinning, but I could still thankfully see what was in front of me. I looked around for the last time. Thousands of arrows on trees. It felt like they were on every single one of them, with each tree having a dozen of them. Where am I? Where the FUCK am I?!

I walked on autopilot before I tripped into the snow. I came to my senses. Afterwards, I looked at whatever I tripped on. And I saw him. A man, in a red-colored jacket, flat on the ground, covered in snow. That man was dead. Crushed even. His head and body looked flattened. It wasn't fresh; it lay there for a while. This isn't real. It must be a hallucination. A dead body in the woods that no one found that had already decomposed. Who is that man? Is it a man? I couldn't tell the head was crushed, and it was lying face down, and I doubt I would be able to turn it over; it practically fused to the ground.

But then, I looked closely at my surroundings. And there were more. Women, men, and of all ages. Covered in snow. Some were deep in it; some laid next to trees. They were old. I started to panic. I am at the mass graveyard. I wish my phone was charged because I would have taken pictures, but I doubt that even a camera could work in this hellhole. Then I started to notice similarities; some were crushed like the man I tripped over. But the others looked like they were bitten. Some had lost limbs, and some had no legs. But a few had something odd. Massive holes in their bodies. I was ready to think that an animal or maybe some weird psycho cannibal serial killer was roaming around, but a round hole in the chest is something I had never seen.

But suddenly, the ground began to vibrate slowly and rhythmically. The trees were shaking with each vibration. And then I heard the hum. It sounded louder, closer than before. It wasn't a hum... It was a roar. A roar of something approaching from the woods. Suddenly, the arrows all turned and pointed in the same direction. I turned my head and saw something terrifying. From the woods, a silhouette of some creature stepped out. With every step, the ground shook. It was hairy and massive; it looked bruised and scarred. I stood in fear, as it got closer and closer to me. It had big tusks that were covered in fresh blood, blood coming out of the person that was impaled on one of them. The person... was alive. Barely clinging to life, but still alive. A man, about a few ages older than me, trying to push himself towards the end of the tusk in a futile attempt to escape, grunted and screamed as the beast interrupted the attempts by lifting its head up, forcing the victim to scream and go backwards. I was faced with a monster of mythical proportions. Its head was massive, and on top of its huge head was a crown made of differently colored arrows. Whether it was put on its head or someone else, I didn't know. But what I knew was one thing: it ruled this part of the woods, and it wanted me to face it.

It roared, shaking everything around it, including my own brain. It felt like my head was going to explode. I closed my ears and screamed from pain as my legs failed me, and I fell to the ground. I felt like my entire body was getting pulled by gravity. When I opened my eyes, the beast grabbed the poor man with its massive trunk and hung him on a branch and began moving faster towards me. If I won't do something, I will die. I felt weak, defenseless, but I felt adrenaline pumping. With tears in my eyes and my ears feeling like they are about to start bleeding, I grabbed my poles and began to quickly slide on my skis away. I didn't want to turn around, I did not want to face the horror, and I did not want to let it get to me. I pushed myself forward as hard as I could. And then in my field of vision, I saw the slide! I can use it to get some speed and leave! But I felt the gravity pulling me again, and I fell.

The rhythm of the beast's foosteps was getting closer. I had a hard time getting up, and I started to crawl. My head was aching, and I tried my best to get away before it can crush me underneath it's feet. But then I remembered. The flare gun! I grabbed it from my pocket, loaded it, and pointed it at the towering mass of wool. I could see its red eyes staring into my soul. I pulled the trigger with all my might and shot at the thing. The flare hit it right in the face. The beast stood up on its hind legs and roared in agony. The roaring was loud; I wanted to cover my ears, but I had to run. I climbed the slide desperately. Trying not to lose valuable time before it notices me again. I got to the top, and without looking back, pushed myself forward. I began sliding down, pushing myself as many times as I can to increase the speed. I saw thousands of bodies hanging and lying in the snow and heard the beast's roars echoing and shaking the trees. I did not look back, only forward. But then, a miracle happened. I saw the sun peeking through the trees; the trees began to look normal again, and I was finally free. I started to laugh hysterically, realizing that I survived. I finally managed to get out. I laughed so hard I probably sounded like a maniac, and my throat hurt, but I couldn't stop. But before I could even get outside the woods, I couldn't make a turn, and I tumbled down the hill and hit my head, knocking me out.

I woke up in a room. Some medical cabinet. Apparently, despite me going skiing for about a year now, I never realized that there was a medical cabinet at the resort. The nurse came to me, relieved I woke up. I tilted my head in confusion, curious how I even got here. She explained that my limp body rolled down a hill and nearly hit an elderly couple on a stroll on the walking path. As funny as the situation may sound, they were concerned for me and called for help. So they brought me here. She asked me to try and walk. As I got up, I got into a skiing position and began to move like I was trying to walk with skis on. The nurse looked at me funny before I realized what I was doing. I shook my head, realizing that I no longer had the skis on my feet and no sticks in my wrists. I walked forward; my legs hurt a bit, but I was fine overall. The nurse said that I got a bunch of bruises, but they patched them up; nothing was serious, and nothing was punctured. I thanked her and went back to the lobby, ready to walk back home.

That's when the counter man came to check on me.

"Jesus Christ, Kid, the fuck happened??"

"I am fine. But I did get a little lost."

"A little? You were gone for hours! What happened!?"

"Well, I-"

Almost like something clicked in my brain, I couldn't remember what happened in the woods at the time. The only thing I could remember was the start of this whole incident. The fallen trees on the correct path are blocking my way, and I am going the other way at the crossroads. And the last thing I remember is me leaving the woods and losing control. So I came up with a half truth.

"I found a bunch of trees on the road I was supposed to take, so I decided to find another path and got lost. And when I returned, I was so excited to get out I tripped and fell."

"For several hours?"

"Yes"

"It would've been easier to find you if you shot the flare."

I looked through my pockets. And... found nothing. 

"Oh... I lost it... I guess it fell out"

"It's okay. At least you are safe."

"Aren't you upset that you lost such a valuable thing?"

"I am happy that you did not lose your life. The flare gun is just a flare gun. What's important is that you are back safe and sound. Of course, you might need to pay for the broken skis."

"What?"

"Just kidding, just kidding. We will buy another pair. But we will probably have to check on the path and clear it up. We have to go through each path and also check if any arrows have been stolen."

"Stolen? Someone steals arrows?"

"Some unknown vandals like to steal arrows we set up around the woods. I have no clue why they would do that. Just mess with people like that. Maybe we should cover it with one of those special paints that leave stains whenever someone touches it."

"Good idea.."

After that, I just left and took a taxi home. Ever since that day, I haven't tried to go down that odd path. The path was closed for a month, and the only paths available were 5, 7, and 1 km paths. I had no idea where I even came from and how I managed to end up at the walking path. I started to enjoy my regular skiing path a lot more, and I tried not to risk going back to the 10 km route, but I heard that the path was cleared and the other paths at the crossroads were taped off so people won't get lost by accident. Don't know how long this will last.

I also noticed a poster on one of the walls of the resort looking for a lost person. At that moment... I recognized that face. The same face as that I saw in the woods, who is probably still hanging on that branch. Dmitry Kemph, his name was. I guess that was the moment when I finally remembered the events, but at the same time, I did not want people to go deeper into the woods just to find one missing person and end up causing more disappearances. So I stayed quiet. I did not think about whether or not that beast was still out there. Or what if it will hunt people going down shorter paths? And what will it do during spring, summer, or fall? I tried to think that I did all I could to prevent people from getting trapped.

I went to a museum recently. A small exhibit about the Ice Age and its animals. My mom bought tickets, so we can spend time together. I came along because I just can't say no to my mom. There were statues of various animals and moving animatronics. These were usually made for kids and not for adults, but I liked them. But that day, as I looked around the museum. Something caught my eye. A mammoth. A big moving wooly mammoth. And, this may sound silly to you. But at that moment. As I stared at its lifeless eyes. I felt a primal sense of fear.
