It felt like forever ago when the first poster appeared. When I walked into work and saw a crowd of people hovering around the service desk, I knew something was wrong. Pushing past the crowd I laid my eyes upon what had transfixed them. On the news board which lay on the wall opposite of the counter, was a missing person poster. We were a small community, but we were close. The thought of one of us mysteriously going missing in the middle of the night was perplexing. Yes, we had the occasional death but nobody had ever simply vanished before. Strangely, nobody admitted to putting the poster on the board and even stranger was the fact that kid, Desmond W only went missing the previous night. Strangest of all, his parents were still asleep and had no knowledge that their son was missing. 

This began a town wide search party. Folks combed the woods, the corn fields, the abandoned houses, the school, the church; every single building that Desmond could have been hiding in was searched. This unfortunately showed no results. The local police searched the families home in and out and found no evidence of foul play. It was like he truly vanished, quicker than the snap of a finger he was gone with no trace of his whereabouts. Months passed with still no sign of Desmond, his family became desperate and offered a hefty prize for his return. Unfortunately nothing ever came of it. There were false reports of people seeing a small boy wandering around the woods or the cornfields but unsurprisingly nothing ever turned up. 

It was no surprise when the school children came up with legends and tall tales of a local serial killer that stalked children and prayed upon those that were unfortunate enough to have unlocked windows. These stories were all fake and made up to scare the younger children. To tell the truth, when my little brother, James, came home one day and told me the stories, I felt a little unnerved myself. After a while the flyers that were strewn about the town were eventually torn down. There was no hope for Desmond or his family, and it was a sore reminder to us all on how we failed to protect our own. Then the second poster appeared. 

People were outraged, how could we let this happen again? This time it was Harold P, an older man who lived alone at the edge of town. Harold's wife, Marire, died years before of cancer and his children had moved out months prior. Just like the first poster however, it was posted the morning of him going missing. I looked at the poster and thought about how if Harold truly went missing, it would take us several days to come to that conclusion. The same response happened again, the whole town was torn apart looking for him, and just like Desmond, there was no sign of him ever leaving. I thought back on what James had told me, about a killer stalking our small town and picking us off one by one. I quickly shook off the idea, after all there was no sign of foul play whatsoever. 

Working at the store while all this was happening created a lot of unnecessary attraction. Teenagers would come in and take photos of the posters. One day as I was wiping off the service counter I felt a tap on my shoulder. As I turned around I locked eyes with two teenagers, one with a camera and the other with a small clip-on microphone.   
“Can I help you with anything?” I asked, annoyed.   
“Can you tell us anything about the posters and how they showed up? Word is that nobody knows who's putting them up,” The kid with the microphone asked excitedly. I chuckled and looked back at the poster. We were told to give unsatisfying responses to any questions we may get. We were told to say something about how upper management was investigating the matter and to relay any questions to the store's email account. I looked back at the kid and shook my head with a frown.  
“Sorry guys, I really don’t know a thing. I know just as much as everybody else,” I said, placing my cleaning supplies down.   
“Well, what do you have to say about the theory about a killer putting up these posters?” He giggled pointing the microphone way too close to my mouth.   
“Thats the first I’m hearing about that one, I can assure you that nobody here is a killer,” I chuckled pushing the mic away gently. The kid grunted and waved at the cameraman to stop filming.   
“Cmon man, you can't even play it up for the camera? We need some good content and this is the only thing happening in this boring ass town,” The kid sighed rolling his eyes.   
“Nope, sorry bud, I really can't. I don't wanna lose this job and I'm not in the expertise of pushing bullshit information. Especially not bullshitting about something like this,” I replied pointing my thumb back at the poster.   
“Whatever dude, let’s get the fuck out of here Zach,” The kid groaned walking away.

A part of me wanted to bullshit the kid and tell a whole story about conspiracies and other horseshit. If only the kid waited a month, then he would have had the story he desperately wanted. God, only if any of us knew what was going to happen. If we did we might have taken more precautions but fuck who knows. I’ll try and write more later I promise but this whole situation is fucked and I need to try and wrap my head around some things, please stay safe.
