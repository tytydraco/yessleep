**This is a story about traditions and youth—and about why some traditions may be a bad idea.**

**I’m twenty-five now. This happened when I was nineteen.**

**Every Halloween, my boyfriend Brian, his best friend James, James’s girlfriend Stacy, and I drove into the woods to tell scary stories. It was our tradition. We thought the stories were better when there were no houses, no streetlights, and nobody nearby to hear us scream.**

**We were wrong about that last part.**

**Brian packed the truck while James brought a cooler and Stacy gathered blankets. We drove until the paved road narrowed to gravel, then followed an old forestry track to a clearing deep among the trees. It wasn’t an official campsite: there was no sign, picnic table, or fire ring—just a patch of open ground surrounded by trees, with the old track disappearing into the woods on the far side.**

**That was how we liked it. Maximum creepiness.**

**We built a fire and settled in with drinks. We had brought more alcohol than we needed, which seemed like a brilliant idea at the time. We were nineteen, and none of us had much experience recognizing when we’d had enough.**

**The drinks went down easily. We played music from Brian’s truck, argued about which horror movies were actually scary, and traded stories about people we knew—or people we claimed to know.**

**Somewhere along the way, the night got away from us.**

**I remember laughing so hard that I spilled a drink down my shirt. I remember James trying to stand up and nearly stepping into the fire. I remember Stacy telling him he was an idiot, then laughing so hard she had to sit down.**

**We should have stopped drinking then.**

**Instead, after the bottles were mostly empty, Brian brought out the weed.**

**We smoked, telling ourselves it would help us relax. By then, we were drunker than we intended to be, and the combination hit us harder than we expected. The fire blurred at the edges, and conversations trailed off mid-sentence. Every little sound in the woods seemed funny—until we stopped laughing long enough to notice how dark it was.**

**We were nineteen, sure we were invincible, and far too pleased with ourselves to think about how isolated we were.**

**Eventually, we started telling scary stories.**

**My story was about being lost in the woods while someone—or something—chased me. I described footsteps closing in behind me whenever I looked back. The worst part, I said, would be never knowing what was following me.**

**Brian’s story was about being tied up, helpless and alone, in a place where nobody could hear him.**

**Stacy told a story about being abandoned somewhere without a phone, without money, and without any way to get home. She hated being stranded. She hated not knowing where she was even more.**

**James’s story was different.**

**He didn’t believe in ghosts or demons. He thought most frightening things had ordinary explanations and that people often ended up in trouble because they’d made a series of bad choices—not because they deserved it, he was quick to add, but because actions had consequences.**

**“Everything has an explanation,” he said, poking at the fire. “Even if you don’t know what it is yet.”**

**His story was about a man walking alone through the woods at night. He heard footsteps behind him, but every time he turned, no one was there. He heard his name whispered from the trees. He saw lights between the trunks. Each time, the man came up with a sensible reason: an animal, a branch, a reflection, someone playing a trick.**

**“Then what happened?” Stacy asked.**

**“Nothing,” James said. “He went home.”**

**“That’s not an ending,” I said.**

**“It’s the most likely ending.”**

**We laughed.**

**I remember thinking that James had missed the entire point of a scary story. If nothing happened, what was the point of telling it?**

**We thought we were alone out there. We thought nobody would want to scare four teenagers in the middle of the woods.**

**We were wrong about that, too.**

**By then, the fire had burned low, and the cold was beginning to creep through our clothes. Stacy complained that she was freezing and headed into her tent. James followed a few minutes later, after making sure the fire wasn’t going to spread.**

**Brian and I stayed up longer.**

**We had our own tent, and we’d been looking forward to having some time alone. We started kissing almost as soon as we zipped ourselves inside. He pulled me close, his hands warm against my back, and I ran my fingers through his hair. We were laughing between kisses, whispering things we didn’t want James to hear through the thin fabric of the tent.**

**Clothes started coming off. My shirt ended up somewhere near the entrance, and Brian’s jeans were kicked into a corner. We were both drunk, high, and exhausted, but at the time we thought we were perfectly capable of making our own decisions.**

**I remember Brian kissing my neck. I remember reaching for him and telling him to stop laughing because he was tickling me. I remember him saying something, then pulling me against his chest.**

**The next thing I remember clearly, I was cold.**

**I was standing in the woods in my underwear, my arms wrapped around myself.**

**For a few seconds, I couldn’t understand why I was outside. The ground was damp beneath my bare feet. My knees were muddy, and a twig had caught in my hair. The firelight was gone.**

**I turned in a slow circle.**

**No tent. No Brian. No campsite.**

**The trees looked taller than I remembered, their branches tangled together overhead. The darkness between them seemed unusually thick, and I couldn’t see the moon.**

**I tried to remember getting out of the tent.**

**Nothing.**

**“Brian?”**

**My voice sounded too small.**

**I took a few steps toward what I thought was the clearing, rubbing my arms to warm them. I told myself I must have gotten up to use the bathroom and wandered farther than I realized.**

**Then I heard something behind me.**

**“Megan...”**

**The voice was high and thin, as if someone were speaking through a cheap toy voice changer.**

**I froze.**

**“Megan,” it called again. “Oh, Megan...”**

**I knew it had to be Brian. It had to be. I was angry before I was scared.**

**“Brian, you asshole! This isn’t funny!”**

**The voice answered from somewhere among the trees.**

**“Megan.”**

**Then it came again, closer.**

**I turned toward it. I couldn’t see anything, but I was certain the sound had come from the left.**

**Then the voice spoke from directly behind me, so close that I felt its breath against the back of my neck.**

**“Megan.”**

**I ran.**

**I didn’t know which way I was going. I only knew I wanted distance between myself and that voice. Branches scraped my arms, and my bare feet slipped on wet leaves and caught on roots. I tried to keep the clearing in sight, but the trees closed around me. I fell hard, scraping my knees, and for one terrible second I couldn’t get my feet beneath me. Then I heard the voice again, not calling this time, only making a thin, pleased sound, and I scrambled up and ran blindly onward.**

**I didn’t know that the campsite was only about a hundred yards behind me.**

**I was running away from safety.**

**Brian later told me he woke on a wooden floor.**

**He had no idea how long he’d been unconscious. His mouth was dry, his head throbbed, and for several seconds he couldn’t remember where he was.**

**Then he tried to move.**

**His wrists and ankles were tied.**

**He was wearing only his underwear, and the rope around his wrists was tight enough to hurt. He pulled instinctively, then stopped when he felt the fibers biting into his skin.**

**The room looked like a cabin. A wood stove stood against one wall, with a pot simmering on top of it. A bare bulb hung over a wooden table. A stained shirt lay folded beside a knife, and dark marks discolored the floorboards near the door.**

**A second chair had been pulled away from the table.**

**Someone had arranged the room carefully. Brian was certain of that. Nothing looked accidentally misplaced.**

**He yelled my name.**

**No one answered.**

**The door stood open. Beyond it, the woods looked much like the ones where we’d made camp, but the trees seemed unfamiliar. There was no moonlight, no distant glow from the fire, nothing to help him judge the distance to anything.**

**He started working at the knots.**

**The person who had tied him up hadn’t done a very good job. The knots were tight enough to frighten him but loose enough that he eventually managed to free his ankles. He worked his hands loose afterward, rubbing his wrists and trying to steady his breathing.**

**He looked around for a phone, a flashlight, anything that might explain where he was.**

**There was nothing.**

**Then he heard something outside.**

**Footsteps.**

**Slow, deliberate footsteps on dead leaves.**

**Brian backed away from the door.**

**A voice came from the woods.**

**“Brian?”**

**It sounded like me.**

**He took one step toward it.**

**“Brian, help me!”**

**He stopped.**

**The voice had come from the left, but the open doorway showed nothing but trees. He looked back at the room: the stained shirt, the knife, the pot on the stove, and the second chair.**

**He remembered James’s words.**

**Everything has an explanation.**

**“There’s an explanation,” Brian said aloud. His voice shook. “There’s always an explanation.”**

**From somewhere behind him, a voice answered in his own voice.**

**“There’s always an explanation.”**

**Brian spun around.**

**No one was there.**

**He told me later that this was the moment he nearly lost control of himself. Not when he saw the knife, or when he realized he was tied up, but when he heard his own voice coming from an empty room.**

**He stood perfectly still, listening.**

**The stove crackled.**

**The pot bubbled.**

**Then, from somewhere outside, I screamed.**

**Brian ran through the open doorway.**

**He didn’t stop to look for a road or a path. He ran toward the sound, stumbling through brush and over fallen branches, calling my name until his throat hurt.**

**He couldn’t tell me how long he ran. He remembered passing a leaning tree with a split trunk, then seeing a light between the trees. He thought it was a lantern, but when he reached the spot, it was gone.**

**He kept running.**

**Eventually, he heard my voice again.**

**This time, it sounded real.**

**Stacy told me afterward that she woke because she was cold.**

**At first, she thought James had pulled all the blankets toward himself, as he sometimes did. She reached across the sleeping bag, expecting to find him beside her.**

**Instead, her hand landed on a pile of clothes and blankets tucked beneath the sleeping bag.**

**She sat up.**

**The tent was empty.**

**She checked her phone. The screen lit her face with a weak blue glow, and she blinked at the time, trying to make sense of it. She felt groggy, dehydrated, and a little sick from the alcohol. Her mouth tasted awful, and her head hurt.**

**She crawled out of the tent.**

**The campsite looked almost as it had when she’d fallen asleep: the blackened fire, the empty bottles, the truck parked near the track. The air smelled faintly of smoke and weed.**

**“James?”**

**No answer.**

**She went to our tent and hesitated before opening it, not wanting to walk in on Brian and me. We were known to be affectionate, especially after a few drinks.**

**“Brian? Megan?”**

**No one answered.**

**She unzipped the tent.**

**It was empty. Our things were scattered around the floor, and our clothes were still there, but Brian and I were gone.**

**Stacy stood very still.**

**Being alone was her worst fear. She didn’t have a good sense of direction, and she’d always relied on James to figure out where they were going, where they’d parked, and how to get home.**

**Now she couldn’t even see the end of the track.**

**She called our names again, louder this time.**

**Nothing answered.**

**Then she noticed the blood.**

**A small, dark stain marked the inside of her underwear. Her period had started while she slept, just as she’d expected it might. Ordinarily, it would have been an inconvenience and nothing more. She had supplies in her toiletry bag.**

**But the bag was inside her tent, and suddenly the tent felt like another place where someone could be waiting for her.**

**She stood in the open for several seconds, cold air prickling her bare legs, trying to decide whether she could bear to go back inside. Then she felt a warm, damp tackiness against her skin and knew she couldn’t simply ignore it. If she kept moving, she’d stain the clothes she had on, and she had no idea how long she’d be out there.**

**She switched on her phone’s flashlight and hurried back to the tent, keeping the beam on the ground and the entrance. Inside, every shape looked wrong for a moment: the sleeping bags, the heap of clothes, the empty space where James should have been. She found a pad in her toiletry bag and changed as quickly as she could, one hand holding the phone while the other fumbled with the packaging.**

**A noise sounded outside the tent.**

**She froze, listening.**

**Nothing followed. No footsteps. No voice. Only the faint hiss of the phone’s speaker and her own breathing.**

**She finished, shoved the used wrapper into her bag, and crawled out without looking back.**

**She was embarrassed afterward by how much time she’d spent frightened by something so ordinary. But it had forced her into the tent alone, with no clear view of the clearing, and she’d hated every second of it.**

**She wanted James.**

**She wanted the truck keys.**

**She wanted to be somewhere with lights and walls and a locked door.**

**Instead, she was alone in the woods, cold and uncomfortable, with a dead fire and no idea where her friends had gone.**

**She called James again.**

**A little bell rang in the woods.**

**Stacy stopped.**

**The sound was delicate and almost cheerful, completely out of place in the darkness.**

**She waited.**

**The bell rang again, farther away.**

**“James?”**

**Silence.**

**Then it rang a third time, close enough that she turned toward the trees before she could stop herself.**

**Stacy backed toward the truck. She could see the driver’s door, but she couldn’t remember where Brian had put the keys. She checked her pockets, then the ground beside the fire, then the pockets again. The cold made her fingers clumsy; the fear made them worse.**

**The bell rang once more.**

**“Stacy?”**

**She nearly dropped her phone.**

**It was James’s voice, coming from beyond the trees.**

**For one desperate second, relief came before suspicion. She took a step toward it, then another, until she remembered the story he’d told by the fire: the man who heard voices and found an explanation for every sound.**

**“James, if that’s you, come into the clearing!” she shouted.**

**No answer.**

**The bell gave a single bright chime.**

**Then James’s voice came from the opposite side of the clearing.**

**“Stacy, come here.”**

**She turned so quickly the phone beam swept wildly across the truck and the trees. The voice had not merely sounded farther away. It had moved. Or something had made it sound as if it had.**

**A soft scrape came from the truck’s far side.**

**Stacy stared at the vehicle. She couldn’t see anyone beside it, but for a moment she thought a darker shape was sitting behind the windshield, in the driver’s seat.**

**She raised the phone’s light.**

**The cab was empty.**

**The scrape came again, low against the metal, just out of sight.**

**She ran to the fire and grabbed the thickest blanket she could find. She wrapped it around herself and sat with her back against the truck’s front tire, where she could see the clearing and the track at the same time.**

**She didn’t follow the bell.**

**She didn’t go into the woods.**

**She called for help until her voice grew hoarse, then sat in the darkness listening for the sound of someone approaching.**

**At one point, she heard footsteps on the track.**

**She stood up so quickly the blanket fell off her shoulders.**

**“Hello?”**

**The footsteps stopped.**

**“James?”**

**Nothing.**

**Then, from somewhere just beyond the edge of the clearing, a voice whispered:**

**“Nobody’s coming.”**

**Stacy screamed.**

**The sound echoed through the trees.**

**She never saw who had spoken.**

**James told us his story later, in fragments. Some details changed between retellings. He could never agree with Brian on how long we’d been missing, and he insisted that certain things happened in a different order than Brian remembered.**

**He woke up on a narrow path beneath the trees.**

**He was fully dressed. His phone was gone, and he had no idea how he’d gotten there. He remembered falling asleep in the tent beside Stacy, then nothing until he opened his eyes to the darkness.**

**For a moment, he sat still and listened.**

**Then he stood and looked carefully at the ground.**

**The path was covered in leaves, but he could see faint marks where someone had walked ahead of him. The ground sloped gently downward, and he could feel a light breeze against his face.**

**“Okay,” he said to himself. “Someone moved us. That’s all.”**

**He followed the path.**

**A few minutes later, he heard footsteps behind him.**

**He stopped.**

**The footsteps stopped, too.**

**James turned around.**

**Nothing.**

**“Very funny,” he called.**

**The trees gave no answer.**

**He walked on. The footsteps started again.**

**James turned quickly this time.**

**A pale shape slipped between two trunks.**

**He stared at it, breathing hard.**

**“Someone’s out there,” he said. “That’s what’s happening.”**

**He kept moving, studying the slope of the ground, the direction of the breeze, and the gaps between the trees. He chose a direction and fixed landmarks in his mind, determined not to chase every sound. The path dipped, curved around a stand of firs, then rose again. He thought he could smell smoke, which meant the campsite might be close—or that someone else had a fire.**

**A branch snapped behind him.**

**James turned at once.**

**Nothing.**

**He waited, counting slowly to ten. When no one appeared, he crouched and examined the ground. There were footprints in the mud, though the edges were soft and the pattern unclear. He couldn’t tell whether they belonged to him, to one of us, or to a stranger. That uncertainty annoyed him. He wanted evidence, not impressions.**

**He continued along the path.**

**Then Stacy called his name.**

**“James!”**

**The voice came from ahead, so clearly that he could hear the breath between the syllables.**

**He didn’t move.**

**“James, over here!”**

**He looked for a person between the trunks, a light, or any sign of movement. Nothing. He called back, “If that’s you, tell me where the truck is!”**

**The woods stayed silent.**

**A moment later, the same voice spoke from behind him.**

**“James.”**

**He turned slowly this time. The sound had shifted without the rustle of someone crossing the leaves, without a footstep, without anything he could use to explain it.**

**“There’s an explanation,” he said, but now it sounded less like a conclusion than a request.**

**The path opened into a small clearing. A lantern hung from a low branch, swaying gently despite the still air. Beneath it, muddy footprints led to a tree and stopped. Someone had carved a word into the bark.**

**WHY?**

**James approached carefully, looking for wire, a speaker, a hidden track—anything that might turn the scene into a trick. The cut in the bark was dark with moisture, and the edges had weathered smoothly. It looked old.**

**That was worse than if it had been fresh. If someone had made it tonight, he could imagine a person standing there with a knife. If it was old, then he had no idea what he was looking at or why it had been left for him to find.**

**He heard a click behind him.**

**The lantern went out.**

**Something brushed his shoulder.**

**James recoiled, striking the tree with his elbow. A laugh came from the darkness—not a distant echo, but a short, breathy laugh close enough that he felt it more than heard it.**

**He ran.**

**He ran until the path opened into another stretch of woods, where he finally stopped, bent over and gasping for breath. He told us later that he had never been so frightened in his life.**

**Not because he believed in ghosts.**

**Because he no longer trusted his own ability to distinguish an ordinary explanation from a convenient one.**

**By then, I had fallen hard enough to scrape my knees, and the voice had stopped following me.**

**That somehow made things worse.**

**I stood among the trees, shivering and trying to get my bearings. My arms were covered in scratches, and my feet hurt. I had no idea which direction I’d run or how far I was from the campsite.**

**I tried to retrace my steps.**

**Every tree looked the same.**

**Then I saw a light.**

**It was small and yellow, bobbing between the trunks.**

**I ran toward it, crying now, too tired to care how foolish I looked.**

**“Brian!”**

**The light vanished.**

**I stopped.**

**For a few seconds, I could hear only my own breathing.**

**Then a voice came from ahead.**

**“Megan?”**

**This time, it sounded real.**

**“Brian!”**

**I ran toward it. A few seconds later, he emerged from the trees, pale and shaking, his wrists red where the ropes had been.**

**We grabbed each other.**

**“What happened?” I asked.**

**“I don’t know. There was a cabin—or something that looked like one. I was tied up. There was blood everywhere.”**

**I looked at his wrists.**

**“Oh my God. Brian, what happened to you?”**

**“I don’t know!”**

**He sounded angry, but it was fear. I could hear it in his voice.**

**“Where are we?” I asked.**

**“I don’t know.”**

**A crashing sound came from the woods behind us.**

**We ran.**

**We didn’t stop until we reached the campsite.**

**Stacy was sitting beside the truck with a blanket around her shoulders. When she saw us, she stood so quickly that the blanket fell off.**

**“Megan! Brian!”**

**We ran to her. She clung to us, crying and laughing at the same time.**

**“Where’s James?” Brian asked.**

**A voice called from the trees.**

**“Over here!”**

**James stumbled into the clearing a moment later. He was pale, muddy, and breathing hard. He looked at each of us as if he needed to make sure we were real.**

**Stacy grabbed his arm.**

**“Where have you been?”**

**James opened his mouth, then closed it again.**

**“I don’t know,” he said finally.**

**He patted his pockets automatically and found the truck keys still there.**

**For a while, nobody spoke.**

**We stood together in the middle of the clearing, listening to the woods. Every small sound made us turn. A branch snapped somewhere in the darkness, and all four of us flinched at once.**

**Brian wouldn’t let go of my hand. Stacy kept looking over her shoulder. James stared at the tree line, his jaw clenched.**

**I was suddenly aware of how cold I was. My skin prickled in the night air, and my bare feet were filthy. Brian was standing beside me in his underwear, shaking so badly that I could feel it through the hand he was gripping.**

**Normally, I would have been mortified. I would have wanted to find my clothes, cover myself, and demand to know what had happened.**

**But I couldn’t make myself care.**

**I didn’t care that I was half-naked in front of my friends. I didn’t care that my clothes were back in the tent. I didn’t even care about the cold anymore.**

**All I could think about was getting away from the trees.**

**“Let’s get in the truck,” I said.**

**Nobody argued.**

**Brian moved toward the passenger side, still looking over his shoulder at the trees. James unlocked the doors, and we piled into the cab, all four of us crowding together, leaving the blankets and everything else behind.**

**James slid behind the wheel. Brian sat beside him in his underwear, his arms folded tightly across his chest, while Stacy and I squeezed into the back.**

**Only after the doors were locked did I realize that Brian and I were still in our underwear. I pulled my knees toward my chest and tried to stop shivering. Brian kept staring out the windshield, his hands clenched together.**

**Neither of us said anything about our clothes.**

**We were too frightened to care.**

**For the next several hours, we sat there with the engine off, too afraid to go back into the woods and too afraid to fall asleep.**

**We didn’t talk much. When we did, we spoke in low voices, as if someone might hear us.**

**At one point, Stacy whispered that she’d heard someone say nobody was coming.**

**Brian turned toward her.**

**“What?”**

**She told us about the bell, the voice, and the shape she’d thought she’d seen behind the windshield.**

**James stared at her.**

**“I heard you calling me,” he said.**

**“I never left the campsite.”**

**“I know.”**

**“Then who was it?”**

**James looked out the window.**

**“I don’t know.”**

**Nobody tried to answer that question.**

**When the sun came up, the woods looked ordinary again. Our tents were still standing. The bottles and blankets were scattered around the campsite. The fire had burned down to a layer of ash.**

**We searched the immediate area together, staying close enough that we could see one another. We found no building, no lantern, no obvious path leading away from the clearing. The old forestry track continued in one direction, but it was the same track we’d driven in on.**

**Brian had rope marks on his wrists. I had scratches on my arms and knees. James’s clothes were muddy, and Stacy’s voice was hoarse from calling for us. We all changed and were eager to get home and shower.**

**We drove to town and reported what had happened.**

**The police took us seriously enough to ask detailed questions and check the area. They photographed Brian’s wrists and my injuries. They asked about the alcohol, the marijuana, and how much we remembered. They asked whether we’d argued with anyone, whether we’d seen other vehicles, and whether we’d noticed anyone following us.**

**We answered as honestly as we could.**

**The problem was that we couldn’t agree on everything.**

**Brian remembered a cabin with a stove and a table. James remembered a lantern and a carved word. Stacy remembered a bell and a voice. I remembered walking alone in the woods and hearing someone call my name.**

**None of us could provide a reliable location for the things we’d seen.**

**The officers followed the forestry track and checked the accessible ground around the clearing. They found old tire marks among the tracks left by our truck and other visitors, but nothing they could confidently connect to what had happened. They found no cabin in the area we described, although none of us could point to the exact place where Brian had been held.**

**The police didn’t tell us that nothing had happened. They simply couldn’t establish what had happened, or who had done it.**

**We never learned who had been in the woods that night.**
