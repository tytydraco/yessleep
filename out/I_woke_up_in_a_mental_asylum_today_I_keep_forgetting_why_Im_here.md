*Hopefully writing will help it stick this time.*

Last night, I fell asleep in my apartment. When I awoke, I could still feel my fiancées hair on my face, hear the rumble of traffic outside and see the warm light of the lamp behind my eyes. But as I opened them this morning that mirage gave way to white LED lights, coarse bed sheets and blank walls. Raising my head, I took in the space. The room was bare, there were two beds, one being mine and another across from it, stripped of its sheets. And between the beds was a wooden bedside drawer atop which was a King James bible. That was the breadth of my new space, a far cry from the homely Manchester apartment I had been just before. The curtains were drawn but from behind them white light bled out, it was the middle of the day. There was a throbbing behind my eyes, like the shock of my new surroundings was causing my mind physical pain as it struggled to make sense of it. I was holding my head in my hands as someone opened the door opposite me and entered. A young woman, dressed in a green nurse’s uniform, her hair tied back lazily, she seemed tired and stressed. She paid no notice of me as she silently placed a Styrofoam container on my bedside. “Where am I?” I finally spoke as she turned to leave. She turned back and gave me a distressed look “What do you mean Nick?” She knew my name and spoke gently, as though we knew each other, but no matter how much I strained my eyes on her, nothing was coming to me. Seemingly taking in my confusion and panic, she once more turned to leave.

“I’ll go get Dr Arnold!”

 

 

A few moments later a bald man in a white coat entered. In one hand he carried with him a plastic chair which he placed opposite me and sat. In the other hand was a clipboard and pen in which he immediately began taking notes, glancing me over as though examining a specimen. Eventually he spoke, his voice had none of the empathy of the nurse from before, “So what seems to be the issue, Nick?” He didn’t raise his gaze from the notepad. This was a formality, one he was begrudged to fill out, this man wasn’t going to help me. “Where the fuck am I?” I yelled, his indifference had brought my confusion to rage, I now felt as though I was the victim of some trick.

“You are at Broadwalk Hospital. Your name is Nicholas Harrison, and you are here to receive treatment for a mental disorder. Another memory spill is it?” he finally raised his gaze slightly from the clipboard, only to shoot me a sarcastic glance. All of this was too much to take at once. Broadwalk Hospital? Mental disorder? For now, I clung to just one question.

“What do you mean another memory spill?”

“I mean one of your patented memory spills. Just when we are beginning to make any progress in our sessions, you *coincidentally* seem to forget everything, setting us back to square one.”

“So, we’ve met before?”

This question seemed to only further infuriate him.

“Yes, Nicholas. We’ve met before,” he grumbled rising from his chair. “Perhaps some fresh air will do you some good.” He strode over to the curtains and tore them open. Slowly, the outside world came into view, it was snowing outside. The sight brought me horror; it had been summer when I had fallen asleep. My anger at once wilted away and my confusion rose up once more like a sickening bile.

“What day is it?”

“December 7th.” Six months since I fell asleep.

 

 

Dr Arnold took a few more dispassionate notes and then was gone, leaving me with my madness. Six months of my life had slipped through my fingers without my noticing, my mind stretched out desperately, but still it couldn’t cross the cavernous gap in my memory. Seeking distraction, I flipped open the Styrofoam container, inside were some bacon and eggs which were now cold, but I doubt they would have been much more appetizing before. A plastic knife and fork were packaged with it; a reminder I am not trusted here. I put the container back to the side. The bible placed on the same drawer drew my attention, the spine was creased and pages well worn, a napkin was slipped inside as a sort of makeshift bookmark. I opened the book to the marked page; the text was almost illegible behind a mass of scribbled notations. What more could be expected of a communal item in here? As I read, the nurse from earlier timidly re-entered, perhaps fearing my agitated state, but she seemed to soften when she saw me with the bible.

“Well at least some things have stuck this time,” she said gesturing toward the book.

“What do you mean?”

“Your bible, you’re always reading that thing.”

Mine? I’m an atheist, always have been. The frenzied notes took on a new frightening form now, they were a physical manifestation of what had become of me, an unrecognizable mess. I needed to speak with someone, someone from before all this.

“Can I use a phone?”

 

 

She took me down the hallway to the common area. The place was a whirlwind, a woman screamed as a nurse forcesd meds down her throat, an elderly man cried as he rocked himself in the corner, a chessboard flew across the room and almost cracked me in the head, here were my compatriots, this is where I now belonged. The nurse ushered me to the back of a queue for the phone behind three others. At the front of a queue a man of about forty screamed down the receiver.

“Yellow mug, Mum! You sent me the blue one you stupid bitch!”

Behind me, a slender man slips into the queue, his pinkie plunged deep into his nostril, the other already bleeding from his past “expeditions”.

“No, no, no!” the man continued down the phone.

“You need to come right nowwwwuhh!” such unnatural squealing from a man of his age sent a shiver through me. The gaunt man behind me finally retracted his finger from his nose, on the end of it now stood about a marble sized globule of snot, blood and filth. I couldn’t hold back my disgust and gagged. Seeing this the man behind me shivered with glee and in one smooth motion lurched forward, smearing the snot on the back of my neck.

I exploded, the shock seemed to expel the fog I had found myself in and for the first time since I woke, I saw clearly. This is not where I belong! I am not mad! I need to get out of here! I surged forward to the front of the line and ripped the phone from the whining man’s hands. To this the man lets out a piercing scream like that of a schoolgirl and began beating on the back of my skull as I tried desperately to tap in my home number.

“Nick!” the nurse called to me; the disappointed tone of her yelling reminds me of my mother.

“Hand the phone back now!”

The older man grabbed me by the hair and smashed my skull into the wall. I felt blood run down my eyebrow line and my vision blurred. I turned and swung back at him with the phone forcing him to fall back. Security began to close in on the commotion and just in time pulled the man back as he went back at me. “Please! I just need to use the phone!” I mustered through my haze and began again tapping in the number. As the phone began to dial, I slumped back against the wall exhausted.

“I just need to call her!” I whimpered, I had begun to cry now. Security sedated the older man and began towards me. The nurse pushed through the crowd and gestured for them to stop, desperately trying to deescalate.

“Call who?” her voice remained calm and measured but her fear showed in her eyes. I acted only in self-defence; why couldn’t she see that.

“My fiancée, Sarah. Please just let me call her.” I could barely stand, confusion and anger had completely drained me, the phone felt a thousand pounds in my hand. I looked to the nurse, hoping to find understanding, but all I found was profound pity. “Nick…” her voice was all but a solemn whisper now. It may sound strange seeing as where I was, but it felt for the first time, she was looking at me like I was crazy. As I heard the line go dead, a cruel understanding ran through me, *I finally remembered why I was here*.
