My son and I have a ritual we do every afternoon.

He gets home from school, and I ask him one thing he learned about that day.

It’s something he and his mom used to do, before she sadly passed a year ago. We moved back to my hometown, back in with my parents about six months ago and I’ve kept it going to retain some normalcy.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

My son told me something rather interesting when he came home one afternoon about a week ago.

“We learned about the Marsden Maiden today. I guess it’s a local urban legend?”

I was vaguely familiar with it. But I’d left town after graduation and hadn’t really been back in probably 20 years, so I asked him for a recap, just to refresh my memory.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

“So there was this teacher. She got engaged on Easter. The next day, a cold and rainy morning, she got called to the principal’s office in the middle of class and was seen running crying out of the school after. Rumor had it that her fiance, a cop, had been mortally wounded during a traffic stop.

While driving on the windy Marsden Road in the pouring rain, she swerved to avoid a deer and crashed in a ditch. She knew someone who lived a little further down the road so she decided to walk there for help.

I guess she was wearing a long white sundress to celebrate getting engaged, and with the rain she got soaked and the dress stretched so the end was on the ground. She saw headlights coming in the opposite direction, and tried to flag them down.

But she tripped on her dress. Then the car tried to swerve and hydroplaned in the process, hitting her head-on and killing her instantly.”

He stopped, as if waiting for a reaction. I simply nodded at him to keep going.

“Maybe a year or two later, the first sightings came up. A woman in a long dress, walking down the side of the road in the rain. Blurry at first, but once you saw her up close… her face was smooth. Like she had no face.”

“Have you ever seen her?” I asked.

“No…but no one really has. To be honest, I don’t think it’s real. Just one of those stories they tell you to like, drive safe in the rain or whatever.”

“Right.”

With that, he took his backpack up to his room to get some homework done.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I wasn’t entirely honest with my son.

Because I didn’t just know about the legend.

I created it.

Allow me to explain.  
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

There was a teacher at the high school I went to and where my son goes now. Ms. White.

One day, right before spring break, she had caught me altering my grades in her gradebook before school. My scholarship had some sort of GPA threshold, I was dealing with some stuff at home that caused my grades to slip… and I panicked.

I guess she had some tropical vacation planned for the week and didn’t want to worry about school stuff during it, because she told me I had a week to think of a reason she shouldn’t report me.

I stressed the entire week over it. My parents weren’t exactly made of money, so my hope of going to college almost entirely depended on the scholarships I’d been given. If those were pulled because I got reported? No more college for me.

That Monday morning, I had overslept my alarm.  
I was driving as fast as was possible given the downpour. My wipers could barely keep up. In all honesty, I should have pulled over, but I was panicking too much to even consider it.

It happened before I could even process what was going on.

*Wipe*. Movement off to the side.

*Wipe*. Ms. Whi-?

I tried to swerve, to get out of the way.

The awful, sickening crunch that ensued told me I hadn’t.  
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I don’t remember much of what happened afterwards.

She had indeed left due to a work incident with her fiancé, but it was nothing more than a car crash due to the wet roads. He came out with nothing worse than a broken ankle. Nobody died that morning except her. But no one remembers that part.

Everything else my son had told me was true, except for the white dress, which I’m almost entirely sure was to make it more dramatic since she had just gotten engaged. She was actually wearing a blue and orange striped dress. If it matters.

I was not charged with anything. The conditions were bad enough that it was simply deemed a tragic case of “wrong place, wrong time.”

Which was more than fine with me. Because, if I’m totally honest, I’m not sure how much of it was an accident.

And I think she knows that.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

Because I saw her for the first time a while back. Early on a dark, crystal clear morning.

And she had a face.

The same sharp green eyes. Eyes that made eye contact with me as I drove by.

Have you ever had a ghost recognize you? If you haven’t, it makes you feel cold.

It felt like an ice pick drilling into my soul, and then holding on as her dead eyes watched my car drive by.

And she’s gotten closer, and closer every time I see her.  
\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

This morning, I was in the kitchen making breakfast for my son and me.

It was a gorgeous Saturday morning. Clear and chilly. I had started seeing an old fling from high school, a woman named Kate. We were planning to spend the day at a nearby orchard. For the first time since my wife passed, I could honestly say I was happy.

I was flipping pancakes when my son’s voice called out from the hall, slightly confused.

“Hey… dad?”

“Yeah?”

“There’s like… a lady standing in our driveway?”

“Is it Kate?”

“No. To be honest, she kinda looks like the Maiden.”

My heart skipped a beat.

“Why… why would you say that?”

“Because she’s wearing a long dress and a cardigan.”

“… and? She could just be going somewhere nice?”

“Well, like, that’s the thing… she’s like, soaking wet.”
