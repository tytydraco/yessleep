My mother died in March. She died in her sleep, which was almost funny, because Ruth Calloway was the worst sleeper anybody ever knew. Thirty years of insomnia. She used to call at two in the morning to say what was wrong with the yard, or what the preacher's wife wore (which was somehow always too "slutty" or the wrong thing), or which of the cousins had put on weight. She was sixty-six and otherwise healthy, and nobody could ever say why she didn't wake up. The coroner said it looked peaceful, like that was the part we'd want to know.

She called the night before. I didn't pick up. We'd been fighting about Thanksgiving, in March, about whose house, and I watched the phone ring on the counter and figured I'd call her back in the morning. She'd be mad I hadn't, and then we'd fight about that instead, which would at least have been something new.

She wasn't a monster. She was a hard woman who loved her daughter in a way that mostly felt like being graded, and I loved her back in a way that mostly felt like waiting for the grade to change. I understand that part much better now.

Starting in April, I'd wake up and couldn't move. My eyes would be open, the ceiling fan going around, and I couldn't lift one finger. And there was always somebody in the room.
