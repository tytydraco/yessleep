Entertainment is what makes a life worth living. I don't care if that means watching movies at 2:00A.M or listening to heavy metal, but I'm sure most of you will agree with that.

For me, that meant helping the Earth through creating wall art. And no, that wasn't a recent obsession. I had been showing off my bottle cap portraits ever since I was a little girl in the 90s.

I became great at art, trust me. Often, when I would run out of recycling myself, I would resort to asking my neighbors for some more, and they were always glad to.

I even got my own exhibit in the museum for a couple of weeks. Beautiful work. Beautiful work that inspired many.

As for my full-time job, I currently work at a recycling center myself.

I genuinely thought my obsession was a healthy one. I helped the planet, I kept myself happy, and I delighted others.

That is, before this 'upcycled art' took down my apartment.

It started yesterday, when I was in the living room. I heard a knock on the door. When I opened it, however, I saw no one. Bear in mind I live in the middle of a long corridor, so even if someone leaves a package at my door and runs away, I'll still see them.

But you know what I did see?

A bin load of metal bottle caps! Thousands of them, in every shade of color that you can imagine, all stacked on top of each other. I no longer cared where the bottle caps came from or who dropped them. All I could see were the endless possibilities in front of me! I could create art for days on end, portraits of celebrities and still life galore!

I pulled it through my apartment door, my name stained in a graffiti-like substance on the top of the bin. Even though it just barely fit, that was alright.

Unfortunately, I had more work to do than to create art. It was a very tragic situation to see your love standing by the doorway, waiting to be used, and not being able to do it!

Anyway, I made dinner for myself (I live alone. I'm a bit too engaged in art to start a family) in the kitchen, and once I returned to the living room where the bin sat, the bottle caps no longer filled the bin to the brim. They filled it 3/4s of the way.

I must've hallucinated that though, right? Plus, a wrong memory feels just as right as a right one, so who knows.

Plus, if no one came to steal the bottle caps, and they're not spilled anywhere, the bin couldn't have mysteriously lost them on its own.

Or, so I thought.

I stared at the bin as I ate dinner (I don't remember what I ate that night. Probably mac n' cheese), completely ignoring the flavor of the food.

Instead, thoughts like these swarmed my mind:

"Whoever donated this is an extremely kind person!"

"The possibilities here are endless!"

"What should I create first: a wildlife scenery or a famous singer?"

"Imagine how proud folks will be of me!"

I imagined the color schemes I could create out of the bin, but, of course, I had more work to do, so I couldn't do any art for the rest of the day.

When I fell asleep at night, the urge to go to the bathroom awoke me, so I switched on the lights again. Although the sudden light blinded me, I tore my eyes wide open and stared at the bedroom wall in front of me.

A portrait! No, not a portrait. A soft drink! Bottle caps covered my bedroom wall, even though I never keep my art in the bedroom.

No, it couldn't be. It just wasn't possible. I've just seen so much recycled art over the years that my eyes default to seeing upcycled art when they're confused.

But even once my eyes adjusted to the light, I still saw the soft drink. Is this where all the missing bottle caps went from the bin? But, I didn't make it. Then who did? And I didn't see anyone make it either.

Hoping it'd disappear in the morning, I went to the bathroom.

When I came back from the bathroom, the soda can bottle was still there.

I turned off the lights but kept the lamp on so the art stayed visible. I lay in bed, like a rat in a trap.

Where did it come from? Why was it there? Didn't the bottle caps come from the soft drinks themselves? Are the bottle caps trying to pay homage to their original bottle? No, no, bottle caps aren't sentient. It couldn't be there.

I don't know when it happened, but I fell asleep.

When I woke up, my entire bedroom was covered in bottle caps. It wasn't a soda bottle anymore, though. Birds covered my entire room. A pelican, a sparrow, a penguin, a flamingo....you get the idea.

I blinked.

And now the birds weren't birds. They were a collection of male and female actors.

I blinked. Palm trees. I blinked. Modern architecture. I blinked. Portraits of famous artists.

I blinked again.

It seemed to be from the perspective of looking out a car window, but I don't quite remember. I blinked. The perspective of looking out the car window didn't change, but words flashed.

"Thank you," it said in bright red. What was once a red bottle last night was now a message. I blinked my eyes again. It reminded me of those old-fashioned animations where they would draw everything frame-by-frame. I blinked. "We could've been many things."

Images of bottles flashed. Images of bells. Images of robots. Images of metal doorknobs and boxes.

"But instead we're this: "

My art showed. The portrait I made for my aunt appeared. I spent many weeks on that thing.

"Why can you let us free?"

I blinked again and again, but that was the last frame.

Why can't you let us be free?

What does that even mean? How different is it to make a doorknob or bell out of some random metal than to make art out of it? What difference does it make?

I've been pondering this question the entire day, and now it's evening time, and I've spent all my free time typing this out.

Please don't try to upcycle recycling yourself. Recycling is better left in the trash bin.

I don't think I'll ever touch a bottle cap again.
