Let me preface this by saying that I am a security agent of the Echo Supernatural Institute. Before all of that, I made a living delving through abandoned military infrastructure searching for old Soviet doo-dads and secrets that had been left behind after the Third World War.

I was an able-bodied but poor and uneducated young man in Eastern Europe during the ‘90s, and as such with the military crippled from the War with the West I, like many others, found scavenging to be perhaps the most accessible job for me considering my circumstances.

Eventually after the Echo Institute started to crack down on the black markets I sold most of my loot to, they found me. Lydia, a woman I'd met on the job who'd taught me English and who would later on become my wife, had recommended me to them for a job as a security agent at their headquarters in Hawaii, a place which is essentially a small city's worth of laboratories, containment facilities, offices and all sorts of other amenities. They wanted to take me on for my experience with the supernatural. 

The choice for me was easy - I’d learned from the woman who would become my wife before about how good it was to work for the Echo Institute. I didn't necessarily want to leave her and besides all that, exploring old Soviet military sites wasn’t exactly the safest choice of profession, nor the most stable. You could find dangerous things down there.

On good days it was a fellow scavenger (scavs, as we were so affectionately nicknamed by the local populace), and on the better of these days the two of you simply wanted nothing to do with each other, nodded as you passed with your gear clinking here and there. Perhaps you sometimes even traded supplies.

On worse days your fellow scav wouldn’t be nearly as agreeable or the both of you would be looking for the same very valuable thing somewhere in the immediate area, and more often than not that would result in a firefight. People died, died at my hands, but I just always tried to rationalize it within my mind by telling myself they knew the risks when they volunteered to go concrete-diving. 

Then on the worst of days you would come across supernatural obstacles. Splotches of destabilized reality that would skin you or turn you into flowers if you got too close. Man-eating monsters and beasts that stalked dark Soviet hallways, abandoned bio-weapons that had seen the war’s end before their deployment; Tammers, as these supernatural things are called in more professional settings.

In those situations, most of the time the only thing you could really do was run - but still, to this day I have a scar down my right arm from running into a particularly vicious tammer, a little homunculus goblin-looking thing that took hold of my forearm and bit down hard before I was able to draw my pistol and shoot it through the head.

In any case, security at the Echo Institute suits me. Some days are better than others, of course, but by and large it's a good fit. Lots of structure, schedules organized to a T. Sometimes you would get put on surface duty, too, and as it happened that was where I was when all of this happened.

The day I am writing about I was at first just driving along some of the more unpaved roads that weaved throughout the Hawaiian island the facility had been built on, heading towards a traffic incident that had been reported. I'd taken this particular path many times before, and by now I'd memorized it to a degree that many of my coworkers called impressive.

It was a fine day, with the sky particularly clear and carrying only a small gathering of clouds on the horizon, wispy and light. A welcomed contrast from the day before, which had been wreaked with tropical thunderstorms rolling across the island, clusters drifting in from the mainland. 

On days like this I'd often take to reflecting on how much the facility's surface could look like an advertisement from a travel brochure, like some kind of tropical paradise. Green foliage everywhere, palm trees vibrant and full of life, exotic birds calling from the branches of trees, and picturesque ponds and small lakes filled with tropical water. Then, if you go close enough to the coastline you can see the Pacific stretching out to the horizon, coloured this really beautiful shade of cyan when the clouds clear up. All of this considered, the Echo Institute really could've chosen a worse place to set up. But I digress. 

I eventually came to where usually there was a fork in the road, where the path split off into three distinct routes. I had gone down all of them before, and as such I knew exactly which one I had to go down to get to where I needed. At this intersection specifically there was a meagre little waterfall pouring from an elevated position just to the left of the fork in the road, coming from a little stream and collecting in a small pool before running off further down the island and reaching the Pacific. Sometimes people would come here to relax, to rest, and in the past I'd counted myself among them. Today, however, as I approached a sense of confusion replaced the usual serenity this spot gave me. 

The fork in the road was gone - simply vanished, almost as if it had never existed. Instead, the road continued moving forwards, not splitting and instead turning left some ways up the road into a more heavily-forested section of the jungles. The first conclusion that came to my mind, of course, is that it must’ve been a tammer, must’ve been some kind of supernatural occurrence. I knew of tammers who could do things like this, after all, could alter terrain like this. Perhaps it was a trap, and as such I reached for my radio transceiver. 

“Hey, Campbell?” I asked, looking ahead at the now straight pathway and reflecting on how such a simple change could be so unnerving to me as the sense of confusion began to turn to a strange kind of dread within my mind. 

“Yeah?” a voice asked over the radio. 

“I’m at the fork with the waterfall, right?” I said, beginning to tap the dashboard of my SUV absentmindedly. “Except it’s the weirdest fucking thing - the fork in the road is gone. Now  the path just… goes straight. Veers left off into the jungle a bit up the way.” There was silence on the radio for a few seconds before my coworker responded. 

“And you’re sure you just didn’t take the wrong path again?” she asked over the radio in a tired voice which had been dragged through the dirt by an overtime shift. I rolled my eyes almost instinctively. “I know you’ve been a bit unfocused, ever since that tammer killed your wife -”

“Fuck off. I’m being serious. I’m at the waterfall and all, with that bench that has the memorial to Doctor Williams. This is the same intersection as always, no other way around it.” I heard Campbell sigh over the radio.

“I was being serious too, you know,” she grumbled as I heard pages begin to flip, with Campbell almost surely beginning to look through sets of documents looking for procedures. 

“That fucking kid,” I mumbled to myself. I had the sneaking suspicion that Campbell wasn’t taking this nearly as seriously as I was, a situation we found ourselves in a bit too often. Don’t get me wrong, I like Campbell, she’s a good coworker and all, but I can tell she doesn’t treat tammers with the amount of caution I have. Hasn’t been around them nearly as long as I have - after all, by the time I was sneaking through old Soviet military sites, dodging monsters here and there, she was still in kindergarten. Hasn't lost the people I've had at their hands. 

“Alright,” she eventually said after a lengthy pause. “I’m gonna send someone else to deal with that traffic incident. Meanwhile, uh, protocol says that you’re supposed to move forwards and check this out. Confirm if possible if the area’s really been affected by reality-altering tammers, and then we send a QRF over and have them quarantine the area until they can find the source of the reality destabilization.” I had no choice but to sigh. 

“Fine,” I muttered to myself. “Fuck this…” 

“It’s just a path,” Campbell retorted. “Just a path going through the jungle on a nice, sunny day. You’ll be fine, Vasily.” 

*Mhm,* I thought to myself before getting out of my SUV. I travelled to the back of the vehicle, popping the trunk open and extracting from it my rifle before travelling back to the driver’s seat, resting the rifle in the passenger’s seat. With a somewhat shaky breath, I grabbed my steering wheel with hands that were perhaps too stiff to be safe and began to drive forwards down the path.

It wasn’t long before I had reached the point that it veered into the rest of the jungle, and stopping only momentarily to check my rifle’s ammunition I began to drive forwards. At first the path seemed to be well-paved to the extent that my ride was actually pretty smooth, all things considered. 

Soon enough, however, my route had begun to seem more unpaved, more wild. Tree branches hanging low began to brush against my SUV's windshield, scratching against the sides of my vehicle as I jolted here and there periodically, driving over pivots and bumps in the increasingly untamed road.

As a matter of fact, it got to the point that the path had begun to narrow so much, had begun to seem so overgrown and undermaintained, that I was forced to stop my SUV. It took some mustering of my courage after that, but eventually I retrieved my rifle from the passenger seat, checking its chambering before exiting the vehicle, having been forced to leave it behind in danger of having it stuck in the suffocating greenery.

As I set out I recounted the five most pressing thoughts in my head., one of the common tactics to keep one anchored to himself in areas of unstable reality. *I am Vasily*, I thought as I pressed forwards through the foliage in front of me. *The sun is warm - not uncomfortably so, though. I am in the jungles of Hawaii. I wish reality had picked a different day to destabilize. I want my wife back.*

This I kept repeating in the back of my mind as I continued down the beaten path, shoving aside branches and foliage as I went, until suddenly just as it seemed I was almost swimming through greenery the vegetation suddenly stopped, opening into a clearing. And what an odd clearing it was.

Sunlight streamed down through the open jungle canopy, illuminating a ground covered with thick, tall grass. I saw an old vehicle - a very old vehicle, with rust adorning it like a rich woman's coat with the glass of its windows having been destroyed. And then, beside it there was what seemed to be a bunker leading down into the Earth. It was of concrete, old and cracked with mould and thick vines covering its surface, and at its front was a thick, reinforced door which had turned to rust long ago. 

I wandered forwards, but as I reached the car, I noticed something. There was something reaching out of one of the shattered windows, something white and chalky, and as I approached my heart sank as I saw that it was a human skeleton, laid across the front seats of its car and clad in the tattered remains of the signature navy-blue uniform of the Echo Institute’s security agents, much like the one I was wearing right then.

I noticed something else, though. Fallen to the floor of his vehicle was an old M4 carbine, dusty and forgotten. We had stopped using the M4 a while ago, however, back in 2022 when after almost two decades of service we replaced them with more modernized rifles. Quickly, I let my rifle hang limp against my chest on its sling and reached for my radio transmitter.

“Campbell?” I asked over the radio. “I've found…” I glanced back over at the rusted door of the bunker before turning back to my radio. “A bunker of some kind, I think. Going down into the Earth. An old skeleton outside, too, in this old, broken-down car. I had to venture a guess, I'd say this place has been abandoned since… sometime in the early 2000s to the late 2010s.” I waited, but it seemed only static came through the radio. I couldn't help but sigh with annoyance as I pressed the transmitter button once more. 

“Campbell?” I asked again. “Can you hear me?” As I was met with only more static, I swore. *The reality distortion must be fucking with the signal,* I thought. *I didn't sleep a wink last night. I'm gonna kill Campbell the next time I see that kid. I want to see what's in that bunker. God-damn me and my sense of curiosity. I want my wife back.* 

Looking back on it now, I'm not sure it was purely my sense of curiosity that made me go over to the bunker, made me open that rusty door and wince at the sound of its decayed hinges screeching almost like they were in pain as the rusty door swung open. Curiosity had certainly got me into shit like this in the past, both when I was a boy and older, but now, I was far from sure it was the only culprit.

Regardless, the first thing that hit me once I opened the rusted door was the smell. It was an uncomfortable kind of stench, filled with the scent of stale air, dust particles irritating my nose and the distinct smell of rubber not unlike masks in a Halloween store, the distinct aroma of tammeryn which in this line of work had become all too familiar to me. A smell which meant the area ahead was more likely than not highly unstable.

The stench made me cough, forced my eyes to water, and as I activated the flashlight on my rifle and aimed it into the open door I saw that this indeed did seem to be a bunker of some kind. A very narrow staircase descended down, concrete steps leading down into the Earth, and although cage lights were attached to a dark concrete ceiling they had all seemingly lost power long ago, meaning that the staircase down, and more likely than not what followed, would be shrouded in pitch blackness. Cold air emanated from the darkness, carried with the smell and contrasting the summer atmosphere outside quite dreadfully. 

I remember that there were booted footprints leading down into the bunker, however. They seemed newer than the rest of the concrete the structure was made of, outlined by mud which had not yet completely dried and had more likely than not had come from the thunderstorm which had rolled through the island a day ago. After I took my respirator from its bag upon my hip, fitting it upon my face, I began to descend.

It seemed like I must’ve traversed hundreds of steps, leading farther and farther into the ground and away from the open door I had left near the top of the staircase. Sometimes I would turn and look back at it, back at that slit of light at the end of the tunnel with the blue of the sky and greenery of the tropical foliage peeking back at me, just reminding myself that it was still there should I decide to turn back. Near the end the thought certainly crossed my mind, and I cannot deny that I almost entertained it. 

Eventually, however, just as I was about to turn around the light of my flashlight finally fell upon the sight of the floor flattering out, and as I approached the end of the staircase I saw that there was another door which had seemingly been blown off of its hinges. Another skeleton laid in its frame, but I saw that his bones had seemingly been stepped on, skull fractured and coated slightly in the same mud which outlined the footprints I had been following into the darkness.

I looked closer and saw that he was clad in a formal shirt and pants, tattered to worthlessness, and close to his right breast was an ID badge showcasing that in life he had been an office worker within the Echo Institute. a member of our Archival Department. 

I peeked out from behind the door frame then, taking care to not step on the skeleton which laid in it. The staircase down opened into a large hallway, moving both to my left and right and was bathed in pitch blackness which I was able to dispel with the flashlight of my rifle. More skeletons laid here and there among the hallway, lying on dirty tiled floors which had accumulated a fine layer of dust and grime over their years of abandonment.

Funny, I thought, that one day they had put on those clothes and never known they would never come off again. Banishing the thought, however, I saw that the footsteps continued still into the darkened hallways, heading left before taking a sharp turn and moving to my right, almost breaking out into a sprint with their placement ever-increasing in distance. 

In the end, I ended up following these footsteps into the darkness. The walls I passed were old, covered here and there with long-dried splotches of brown blood. Not too far ahead I found a puddle of more recent blood, deep red, surrounded by empty shell casings - 9-mil, I deduced. Evidently, however, they did not belong to the bearer of these footprints which continued on sporting a new light coating of blood.

As I followed the newly reddened prints, however, a sneaking sense of deja-vu began to fill my mind. It felt almost as if I had walked these halls before with my wife before she was killed, had opened these torn-open doors surrounded by rusted bullet casings.

It did not take too long for me to realize the source of this feeling. As I continued to walk through the facility, following the footsteps which had begun to fade back to muddiness, the blood drying, I realized that this seemed almost to be a copy of the facility above-ground - more specifically, the expansive building where we tended to seal away the various dangerous tammers in our possession. I found myself bewildered as I continued to move.

How could the Echo Institute have ignored this, all of this space under the island? *Have they ignored it?* I thought to myself. *What if they know it’s down here…?* A stupid thought, I deduced, and quickly banished the idea to the back of my mind. The Echo Institute wouldn’t do that, I thought. Wouldn’t hide something like this from its own employees. Now? I’m still not sure. It seems like a stupid thing, to know something like this existed underneath the island and not do anything with it, but with what I learned before the end of that day I am beginning to question many things about my employers. 

*I wonder whose footsteps these came from?* I asked myself, moving cautiously through the pitch-black abandoned containment complex. With each step of my feet it was like I disturbed some rubble, accidentally crushed some bones (causing me to cringe with a spine-tingling sort of vehemence). *Am I alone down here? It feels so odd to see all of this - to see the containment complex, abandoned. I want to know what happened to my wife.*

With that, however, as I entered a hallway lined with destroyed doors leading into empty containment chambers which had once held any assortment of beasts, there was a noise from behind me. Quickly I pivoted with my rifle, and as my flashlight illuminated the hallway which I had come from, I saw something at the end of it. A figure was visible there with pale skin and clad in dark robes, which looked almost like a burqa as some women wear in the Middle East, and its feet were like the hooves of a goat. 

It shuffled aside as I aimed towards it, eyes consumed with blackness staring at me before it disappeared around a corner. It had been watching me. *I need to move quickly,* I thought, and with that I turned and continued down the hallway at a pace which had increased to a light jog.

Soon this increased pace had led me to an intersection of three hallways, one to my left and another to my right, and in the centre of this room I remember there was a large blast door with little signs of damage compared to some of the others I had seen around these abandoned ruins. There were signs beside it, as well, but they had faded over the years to the point that they no longer appeared at all eligible. Just then, however, as I stepped towards the hallway to my right there came a voice from the darkness.

“Hello?” It called, causing me to jump out of my skin with fear. 

“Who the hell is there?” I shouted. “Show yourself!” 

“Relax,” continued the voice. It was in a soft, feminine tone, perhaps even somewhat sultry, somewhat seductive. “I'm not here to hurt you.” 

“Where are you?” I asked, calming down somewhat. “...what are you? Are you human?” 

“I'm human enough, darling, and I'm behind the door,” the woman said to me, “the one just behind you.” I turned, and although the door stayed locked tighter than a miser’s purse it seemed as if the voice was, indeed, coming from that direction. The footsteps I had been following, I noticed, stopped here for a while, seeming to pace this way and that before they turned and began to walk another way. 

“Are you a tammer?” I asked, walking up to the blast door and aiming my rifle as I moved. 

“I won't lie to you: I am, yes. That's how you're able to actually hear me - without these powers, my voice would just be muffled to hell and back behind all this steel.” 

“How… long have you been down here?” I asked. “What is this place, anyways?” 

“Oh, I don't quite know how long it's been.” There came a slight tapping sound from behind the door, almost as if something was absentmindedly drumming its fingers across the metal. “As for what this place is? It's the Echo Institute’s headquarters, of course - was, at least. They brought me here a long time ago, back in the early 2000s - I came willingly, I'll stress to you.

"The Echo Institute was here for a long time. Testing me, trying to find the extent of my abilities. Then, there was a day of chaos. I got out of my chamber, ended up running. Eventually I found this open containment chamber and decided to hide in here, but the door locked behind me and I haven't been able to get out. Ever since that day of chaos, it's been quiet down here. So very quiet.” 

“This isn't your original chamber?” I asked at the door, finally lowering my rifle.

“Oh, no. I was in a much lower-security one before. The greatest extent of my abilities is that I know everything. It's more of a curse than a blessing, believe you me.” 

“You know everything?” I asked, and the woman behind the door responded with grim affirmation. *I don't trust this thing,* I thought, re-anchoring myself in reality. *I shouldn’t be down here, anyways. I should just turn around now and…*

“You don’t trust me?” the voice said from the door, and I was stuck in surprise at first. Right before I had almost found it within myself to respond, however, the tammer continued. “All that I’ve told you, and I still haven’t earned your trust?” 

“I don’t easily trust tammers,” I responded somewhat coldly. 

“Because of what happened to your wife, correct?” the voice cooed irritatingly. “Dead at fifty-one, just three months ago. Dead from a tammer attack - a home intruder, forced her door open with his abilities - an act of corporate espionage, perhaps? Is that right?” With that I shook my head.

“If all you’re going to do is invade my mind like this, then I think we’re done here,” I said, and began to walk away. I hadn’t gotten far, however, before the voice said something else.

“You want answers, don’t you?” I paused in my tracks. “You want to know how it happened. What she’d been whisked away to work on. What she was never able to tell you about. It had twisted you to no end - once you had been such honest lovers, such star-crossed partners, but then the Echo Institute hired her to that top-secret project and you began to grow distant. I would say the people who took your Lydia from you are very close. So close, in fact, that you see them every day -

“Tell me what you know,” I said with a voice that bordered on an aggression-fuelled shakiness as I turned back towards the door once more. “Tell me what you know about Lydia, now, and don’t you dare talk about us like you did before again.” 

“Frankly, young man,” the voice said as I stepped forwards, “you are in no position to be making demands at the moment. I will tell you all you want to know about how your wife died, all you want to know about… everything else you’ve ever wondered, really, but in return, I will need something from you.” 

“Quite the sales pitch. I’m listening,” I responded almost instantly. 

“You don’t need much convincing, do you? I like that.” 

“Don’t be so fucking smug about it and I might take you up on your offer. What do you want me to do?” As I finally approached the front of the door, the incessant tapping which had radiated from it since I'd asked how long it had been trapped underground suddenly stopped.

“Right, then. What I’ll need you to do is open this door. After that, I’ll need you to escort me to the surface of the facility.” I couldn’t help but scoff at the request. “You don’t trust me - I know. I understand. In any other case than this you would be a wise man, not to listen to my request, but in this case I truly must assure you that my intentions are correct. When I get to the surface, I’ll turn myself in to the Echo Institute’s security forces. Willingly, without resistance, just like when I was first contained within these walls.

"Because, you see, at the end of the day all I want is to see the sun again. To be in the brightness of the world. I’ve been trapped down here for so long, for so very long. Can you even imagine what it must’ve been like for me? I’ve forgotten the sun. Forgotten the wind on my face and the feeling of water on my hands, forgotten hot showers and warm bed sheets. I liked this world, but I've forgotten it, and I would very much like to see it again.” With that I could only hesitate, could only stop in front of the door. 

“Could…” I stammered. “Could I just have some time to think?” 

“Of course,” the door said. “The code is 5-1-6-0-5. Got that? 5-1-6-0-5. Though, I must warn you: Something is coming. A monster.” 

“What?” I asked, raising my rifle and looking around wildly.

“Oh, yes,” the voice responded. “Not by my own will, I must remind you, but there is something approaching. It will be hostile to you - you must hide before it reaches us. There is an old storage cabinet not far from my door - you should be able to hide in there.”

With the voice’s recommendation I suddenly heard a noise approaching, coming from the dark hallway to my left. It sounded almost like a rattling to me, like some kind of skeleton being shaken. Quickly I leapt into action, throwing open the rusted door of the cabinet before more carefully entering, being vigilant to avoid the more sharp shards of rust lining its walls. 

As I hid in there the voice did not speak again, did not draw any attention to us while the sound of rattling bones drew closer and closer. Eventually it made itself known, and out of the corner of my vision in the cabinet something which looked like a human skeleton with extended arms which had been deprived of legs walked into view.

It looked this way and that around the empty room, gaze eventually landing on the large blast door as it approached. Its skull had been split in two, I remember, and as it walked this way and that upon its bony hands, clicking on the floor, these two halves of its skull clacked together not unlike the jaws of a Venus flytrap. 

Eventually, however, after looking around here and there the skeleton shuddered, dust falling lightly from its bones, and began to amble down the opposite hallway from which it had come. I only stepped out of the locker a while after the thing had moved away, and with that I heard a voice ring out from the door once more. 

“Such things have been my only company down here,” it said. “These monsters are all that remain. Everything else has rotted away or left. Now - have you had enough time to think about if you shall open this door for me?” When I told the door that I still needed more time, it gave me a sort of tsk-tsk sound. “Fine, then.”

With that I sat on a part of the ceiling which had fallen, rifle sitting beside me as I moved through my thoughts like wading through knee-deep water. Eventually, however, my mind began to drift. I looked towards the muddy footsteps which had stopped in front of the door, paced here and there before moving down the hallway to my right, almost where the skeleton thing had wandered not too long ago. 

“The footsteps came from somebody who was down here not too long ago,” the door said, almost reading my thoughts once again. I hated when it did that, hated the lack of privacy I was afforded around this thing, but regardless my intrigue won out. 

“Who was it?” I asked. 

“A young woman,” the door responded. “A field agent of the Echo Institute, or so she told me, but a remarkably well-mannered one, considering her work. Her name was Joy.” 

“Joy?” I asked, my curiosity piqued as I turned back towards the door. With both her name and her line of work considered, there was no mistaking who had come through here before. The woman the door spoke of was one of the Echo Institute's most elite field agents. But what had she been doing down here?

"Yes. She stumbled upon me much as you did,” the door responded. “And what interesting questions she had to ask. She wanted to know one thing: if the Echo Supernatural Institute would ever sacrifice her life for the sake of the mission. I tell you, that poor girl must've been going through it.

"To her credit, however, she was about to open the door. Joy was… interrupted, however. Another tammer came along and attacked her.” I could only nod to myself, glancing towards the footsteps once again and noticing the increasing distance between each as Joy had seemingly ran away from whatever had entered in pursuit of her.

“She seemed like such a troubled girl, too. I’m glad she made it out alive. If you follow her footsteps, you probably can, too. Now, what shall it be? Will you let me out of here, afford me the freedom I so very desire?” I remember sitting there, shaking my head and scoffing in frustration. 

“The tammers aren't getting any farther away,” the door continued. “Who knows when another could round the corner again, could come searching for you? I would certainly advise you to move quickly.” At this point, I was certainly beginning to err on the side of the door.

Looking back on it now, there was so much I could've asked the tammer. I could've questioned it more indepthly about the abandoned version of the facility. Could've asked it anything in the world. But at that moment the only thing I could reserve space for within my mind is that I wanted answers. Above all else, I think, I wanted to put a name to the crime, to give me a target. I wanted to find whoever had killed my wife, had murdered my Lydia, and I wanted them to pay. 

“Alright,” I surrendered. “Fuck it. I’m going to open this door, but then you’re going to tell me what you know about my wife. About who killed her. Understood?” 

“Absolutely,” the voice said. “Without a shadow of a doubt - I am a woman of my word.” With that I took a shaky breath, walked towards the keypad on the door and punched in the numbers, one after another. 5, 1, 6, 0, 5. 

“Alright,” I said. “Fine. The door’s open. Now just come out and tell me what happened to -” before I could finish my sentence, there was a tremendous blow against the blast door like a tree dislodged in a hurricane smashing against a home. I saw the blast door explode outward, saw something dash forwards in a burst of colour and wind with a sound not unlike the horn of a train bellowing before I was knocked to the ground.

Quickly I scrambled to my feet, reaching for my rifle before whirling around, trying to see what had run from the blast door. There was nothing, however, with the thing having fled before my gaze could find it, but as I stood there I still heard something scurrying away in the distance. I couldn’t help but curse my own stupidity. I’d trusted this tammer, trusted it to fulfill its end of the bargain only for it to leave me out to dry, and now I’d released God only knows what upon the world. 

I turned back to the now-empty containment chamber, dread beginning to fill my mind and quickly being replaced by panic over what I was going to do next when I suddenly noticed something inside the chamber. It was a folder, one which was branded as heavily classified and appeared to have been the subject of an attempted burn.

I remember just taking the folder and following Joy’s footsteps back to the surface. They eventually led to the stairs of another bunker leading up and out of the abandoned facility, towards the surface, and as I ascended them I felt the temperature of the summer day outside gradually grow, the open door beckoning me closer with light from the island. 

When I finally reached that door, I emerged onto one of the tropical beaches of Hawaii. The Pacific ocean stretched out in front of me, and from here I could see the port of the research facility, the ships which seemed to sail almost leisurely around its calm tropical waters. In addition to all this, I saw that the sun had begun to set. I don’t quite recall when I entered the abandoned facility, but regardless, I must’ve stayed in there a lot longer than I thought.

I simply sat there on the sand of the beach, I remember, watching the sun descend slowly into the horizon. The waves were gentle and washed carefully around me while the skies were lit in colours of orange, pink and purple.

Emotions and thoughts swirled through my head. Stress over what I'd just released into the world, over what it can possibly do. A sense of accomplishment for finally having information on who murdered my wife. In the end, all of these emotions culminated in me opening the folder that the tammer had given me. 

Lydia had been assigned to a project that was called Krash-416 within the Echo Institute. Details on it were scarce in the document that I'd been supplied but regardless, it told me all that I needed to know. Lydia had uncovered something in this project. Something that disturbed her so deeply that she felt she needed to warn me about it.

So, in the name of OPSEC, the Echo Institute killed her. Sent one of their field agents. They had pictures. A photograph of her dead with a gunshot wound between her eyes, a photograph I haven't been able to get out of my mind since. I caught only glimpses of a report of her autopsy, but I chose not to peruse it any further.

I suppose I’m still just reeling from what I’ve discovered. The abandoned version of the facility most of all. I still don't know what it was all about, how it had gotten there, and when I travelled back to the exit which I had taken to the surface it still remained, although now when I peered into that darkness the beam of my flashlight met only a pile of fallen rock burying the entrance to the abandoned structure. 

I don’t want to think the Echo Institute could’ve killed my wife. I don’t want to think that they could do that, but everything seems to connect. The agent, they say, used a tammer to enter her home, to bypass the door. Besides that, with the abandoned facility I found I'm beginning to question just how much else the Echo Institute has hidden from me. I just don’t know.

I’ve given twenty years to these people - wasted twenty fucking years, damn near a half-life, and now there’s a non-zero chance that they were the ones who killed my wife, who took her from me solely because she was concerned for her husband.

I've only just gotten back home now and I really don’t know what to do. I don’t know what to believe anymore, and I feel tired. I can't stop thinking, however: What was Lydia trying to warn me about?
