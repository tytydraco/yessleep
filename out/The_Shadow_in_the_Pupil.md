I still don’t know if what happened that day was just a coincidence, or something I will never be able to explain.

A few years ago, I was involved in a home invasion murder case with several of my colleagues. The victim was a woman who lived alone in an apartment complex. The suspect had apparently planned to break in and steal something. He tricked her into opening the door, but when she realized what was happening and started shouting, he panicked and strangled her. The apartment was left in a mess. Drawers had been opened, but the suspect seemed to have been in such a hurry that he barely took anything before leaving.

My colleague Lao Xu was responsible for photographing the crime scene. The victim had bruising around her neck and signs that she had fought back. Her eyes were still open, and the expression on her face was something I still remember. Later, investigators found evidence under her fingernails that suggested she had scratched the attacker during the struggle. When we returned to the station, the case became a major priority. The police chief personally organized meetings to discuss possible leads.

But the investigation quickly reached a dead end. A week passed, and we still had no clear suspect. Then one afternoon, Lao Xu pulled me aside. “Take a look at this photo,” he whispered. “Don’t you think something looks strange?” At the time, we used Nikon cameras for crime scene photography. Lao Xu had been reviewing the images on a projector when he noticed something unusual. He had enlarged the victim’s pupil. There was a blurry shape reflected inside it. I stared at the image for a long time. My first thought was that it looked like a person wearing a baseball cap. I asked him: “Does that look like a cap to you?” He looked at it carefully. “I think so too,” he said. “There’s something strange about this reflection.”

Neither of us told anyone else. Maybe because we knew how ridiculous it sounded. A reflection in a dead person’s eye? It could have been anything. But the more we looked at it, the more it looked like a baseball cap.

Later, when I returned to the neighborhood to conduct follow-up interviews, I casually asked the security guard: “Have you noticed anyone unusual around here recently? Someone wearing a baseball cap?” The guard thought for a moment. Then he said: “Actually, yes. A few days ago, a maintenance worker came here to check the heating system. People around here rarely wear baseball caps, so I remember him.” I felt something drop in my stomach.

I immediately went back and asked my colleagues to investigate the man. After further investigation, we eventually focused on him. In the end, he confessed. He was the person who had committed the murder.

To this day, I still don’t know what happened with that photograph. Was it just coincidence?

Was it simply our brains trying to find a pattern in a random reflection? Or was there something about that moment that helped us notice a clue we would have otherwise missed?

Years later, my colleagues and I looked at that photograph again. Some people saw a baseball cap. Others saw something completely different.

Maybe it was nothing more than a lucky coincidence. Or maybe it was one of those rare moments when intuition finds something before logic can explain it.

I still don’t know.
