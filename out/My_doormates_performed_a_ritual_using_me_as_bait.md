I apologize in advance for any grammatical or spelling errors; English is not my native tongue. I'm also uncertain where else I can securely record this experience. My hands are trembling, and the tears won't stop.

I need to write this somewhere and post it, because I am sure that once I get on that train, I will somehow forget this hellish night, even if it doesn’t make sense.

It is normal nowadays for kids, adolescents, name them whatever you want, to get out of their parents' house for collage/university. My time came a little late.

My mother started drinking heavily once I turned 12, and it not only messed up the family dynamic; it also changed our roles in that apartment. Somehow I started taking care of cooking, cleaning, groceries, and keeping an eye on my little sister. Dad brought home the money, and mom… well, for 5 years she didn’t get up from bed, for a good reason, at least.

I don’t want to indulge in my not-so-happy past, but I want you to understand why, at 21, I decided it was time to get out.

I started working weekends in a cafe to save money for the internship, because I knew my parents couldn’t pay for a place up north in the country, and I really wanted to get out.

When it was time to apply for a hospital internship, I wasted no time choosing the hospital farthest from my city, and they accepted me.

Since I would be there for 4 months and wouldn’t be able to work there, I chose one of the cheapest rooms I could afford. It was 30 minutes away from the hospital on foot, and it felt like a great place.

To cut to the chase, I lived in the most horrendous conditions for a month. Mold was all over the place, including the mattress they gave me and even the pillow. They denied my complaints, so I had to find another place.

The search was quick, and I moved the same day. The douchebag from before didn’t give me my down payment back, saying the conditions were okay.

Nonetheless, I was happy with the new place; it was a two-story house, glued to the other by the sides, like almost all the houses in this country. There were three rooms on the lower floor and five on the second, two bathrooms, a big kitchen that was connected to the living room, and a terrace. The owner… I thought he was a sweet old man.

On my first day there, I didn't see anyone, and let me be clear: the room I got was the last available; all the other ones were occupied. Still, the house remained silent for two whole days. Granted, it was a weekend, so I thought everybody got out to see their families, something I didn't want to do. My parents still called me from time to time, tormenting me about leaving them, and I still cried over it.  
On the third night, I heard the first noise ever made in this new house: the clatter of dishes. Since I was trying to be an extrovert and make new friends, I got out of my room with my dirty mug, preparing to introduce myself.

A short girl stood near the sink washing something.

“Hey.” She jumped at my words, and I cringed.

“Hi.” She was a petite, dark-skinned girl with glasses that weren’t hiding her red face.

“I’m new here; my name is Eve.” I got closer, not knowing what to do with the rising anxiety.

“Yes, Oscar told us that we had a new neighbor.” Good to know everyone already heard about me, while I stayed in utter silence for two whole days. “I am Luisa. We are doing a dinner all together tomorrow; you should join us.”

Her words didn’t sound like an invitation, more like a demand. I nodded, and once she was finished with the dishes, I quickly cleaned my mug, said goodnight, and excused myself.

The next day, I remember buying some groceries to prepare something for the said dinner. I think I got home earlier than anyone, since the place was deserted and quiet. I recall how uneasy it made me; the house was always silent, even if the street behind the door was alive.

Putting the groceries out, I noticed I had to share my shelf with someone, because there was a bag with some kind of meat that definitely wasn't mine. It smelled unpleasant, but I decided not to bring it up since I didn't know anyone yet.

It took me a while to take a shower and organize the notes I had taken earlier, and before I knew it, the kitchen, not far from my room, buzzed with sound.

Carefully, I left the room, took a deep breath, and quickly walked down the hallway to the kitchen, saying hello to everyone as I entered.

Luisa was setting up the table; she looked up and nodded. “This is Eve, guys; she will do dinner with us.” The earlier buzzing sound of life stopped its existence once more. All eyes were on me, and for some reason no one dared to speak.

“Rick will explain to you what we are doing.” She blabbered out before I had a chance to object.  
A tall guy with dirty blond hair stepped closer and, grabbing me by the elbow, pushed me towards the corner of the kitchen near the refrigerator.

“Listen, Eve, we will have chicken; the table is set up, but we do not sit there. For eight of us, there will be eight guests; we sit on the floor behind the candles, one plate for each of us, no cutlery. Make sure you leave your cellphone in the room; you don’t speak, only sing, repeat the words, don’t look, don’t lean forward…”

He rambled, whispering; it was clear that his raspy voice couldn’t be any lower, so sometimes the rougher words sounded like a distorted vinyl record.

I couldn’t listen to him anymore; the information kept piling up, and it made me feel uneasy. “I didn’t know you had guests.” I made sure to say it loud enough so Luisa could hear. “I will join you another day; don’t worry, I will just be at my friend's place.”

I tried to step away, but Rick grabbed the same elbow and started to squeeze it, making me gasp at the pain.

“You have to stay; the guests know you are here.” Said a girl with black hair at the counter, cutting what I assumed was chicken from the bag earlier. “I need help.” She didn’t look up. Rick walked me towards her, and I stood there, looking at her trembling hand, holding the dull knife that didn't cut but tore the rancid yellow meat apart. I stifled a gag as the acidic smell passed through my olfactory epithelial cells.

Rick made me help her. As I cried, I put the pieces of meat on plates, then on the table. They didn’t let me wash my hands, and all I could do was stand there, looking down at the table full of rotten vegetables and meat.

Another girl, whose name no one said, lined up the hallway with eight white candles, all covered in some sort of herbs; some were tied to the candle, some just glued.

Once the sun settled, Rick lit the candles, and all eight of us sat behind one; Luisa was first, then sat the girl with black hair, Rick, two guys who were paler than the white painted walls, then the chubby girl who put out the candles, who was squeezing her shorts, me, and a guy with glasses.

“Just say what we say,” he whispered, looking straight ahead. I shook like an autumn leaf in the harsh winds, so all I could respond with was an uncontrolled ‘huh’. He shushed me.

“We sing, and you repeat the words, don’t look at anyone, don’t lean forward, don’t stand up, don’t talk, don’t use your phone, no cutlery, one plate for each, eight of us, eight of the guests, we sit on the floor behind the candles…”

His rumbling was stopped by the silence that consumed the air around us; even the flies that roamed the table that appeared by abiogenesis stopped emitting any kind of sound.

A single flicker, and the lights were gone. The only light left was the fire of the candle that danced carefully. I focused on it, feeling the tears sting my eyes once more.

“Don’t let the fire go out.” I heard it from my left, before the creak of the front door echoed in the hallway. As the first dragged step followed, my doormates started chanting, a stanza over and over again. For the life of me, I can’t remember the chanting; all I know is it poured out of me as if I knew them my whole life. Latin and our language bled together as I spoke, staring ahead into the dimly lit wall; my mind remained blank until the first guest stepped into my peripheral vision.

Its long, thick white toes seemed to reach for the tiles, yearning to curl around them, but they couldn't. The shin was far too slender to bear any weight. Then came the hands, dragging along the ground, as pale as the rest of it, I imagined. The snores that came out of it were dull and reminded me more of a death rattle than a sleep one.

It dragged and positioned the foot one after another, without stopping. I sensed my calves starting to fall asleep under my weight, praying in the back of my mind that I wouldn't need to run.

The chant thundered through the room, ricocheting off every object in sight. I could have sworn I heard someone sobbing, yet the singing never stopped, that unnatural tempo pressing on. 

The next guest's lower body was hidden beneath a grimy, pale magenta robe, folded and draped in disheveled layers. The only contrasting texture I could distinguish was a white lace veil. It came to a stop before me, rotated to meet my gaze, and hunched its back low, bringing its face to hover just in front of my own. 

The veil covered its face, but I could still see the pulsating grey flesh and protruding eyes; the nose hung heavy and low, massive beside that thin, withered mouth. We didn’t stop the chant, yet with each word, my body heated up, as I felt my belly get full.

I wanted to hug that bizarre thing; for a moment it felt so dear and welcoming.

This comfortable sensation dissipated quickly once the guest took another step. So as not to hold up the parade, it didn’t turn back; it just shuffled off, keeping its eyes on me.

I kept mine on it, and I think it’s my fault everything went downhill after that.

Something scuttled to me, a blur of emotion that I only acknowledged when it stopped in front of me, showing its dark, long face to mine. Praising myself, I can say that I didn't scream or even get up. I sat there, sharing the same oxygen with the guest whose skin was soggy and full of warts and wrinkles.

Only when it withdrew, letting out a pleased sigh, did I finally see it. It was on all fours, its arms and legs twisted at grotesque angles. The joints were where they should be, but they bent backward, inverted.

The empty sockets fixated my gaze, and then I noticed how the hallway got quiet; they stopped chanting, as did I. But once it dawned on me, I resumed it, the words spilling out of my mouth once more. No one helped me; I felt how empty my body was. The only thing looking at me was the guest in front of me.

It opened its mouth, showing rotten teeth and a long, black tongue that missed a piece. “Join us at the table.” Each word dragged itself in my mind, feeling longer than it actually was. The hollow sound of its voice was only in my mind; my body trembled in response.

I was scared, but at the same time, I didn't understand why I felt the urgent need to rip my clothes apart and lie down at that table. I think I passed out when I noticed my hands undoing the buttons on my shirt. I don’t know how long I was out; all I know is I woke up on the table. Thankfully, only my shirt was unbuttoned, and all the candles were out. The only light entering the house was the moonlight, and it wasn’t much.

From somewhere, I heard a wet chomping sound; muffled moaning followed. I felt my insides clench as the realization tried to cling to my still-foggy mind. Carefully, I got off the table and tiptoed to the kitchen, wanting to get to my room, stupidly thinking it was a good place to hide or close myself in.

Rick's silhouette was in the corner of the kitchen, where he had dragged me earlier; he stood there facing me, his eyes locked on the ceiling, muttering the rules he told me earlier, as something moved behind him, making those nasty wet chewing sounds. But the moaning didn’t come from him; it was from down the hallway, emerging from the room with the open door I would have to pass to get to my room.

I stifled another gag reflex as I walked past him, apologising for not helping him in my mind. All I knew was that I had to save myself, not the people who forced me to participate in this horror.  
Clinging to the wall of the hallway, I looked down so I wouldn’t step on anything and alert the guests who were having a feast. Abruptly, loud slaps of skin on skin began to spread from the open room, from where I had earlier heard the moaning.

In the dull light provided for me, I could make out the hunchback shape of the second guest; its veil covered Luisa's face, which fortunately was turned to the wall of her bedroom. It sat on top of her, producing sounds of pleasure; the bed stood still and did not move, as if there was no one jumping on top of it, producing one of the sins known to mankind.

Another body lay at the feet of the cheap bed, and the glasses near it suggested it was the chubby girl or the guys who sat by my side; around it was a pool of dark liquid. The house now smelled of rot and iron.

I didn't know where the first pale guest had gone, and honestly, I didn't want to. My only thought was to get to safety.

As I crept past the closed doors, muffled cries and screams bled through from the other side, enough to confirm just how deeply wrong this place was. It took agonizing care to ease my own door open without a sound. The room came into view, and there it was, a writhing black tangle of limbs darting beneath the bed. I only needed my phone. Pressing my spine flat against the wall, I inched toward the table, grabbed it, and began retreating.

The guest beneath my bed seemed to take my hesitation as an answer. It emerged from the darkness, its contorted form unfolding: six limbs, all arms, no legs, a grotesque semblance of a spider. Instinct took over and my foot connected with its head. Its many hands seized me, but released almost instantly, as a second black mass collided with it. A piercing shriek echoed through the house as the two thrashed violently across the floor. Giving me enough time to run out.

I'd always thought the door to my room was too close to the front entrance. At that moment, I thanked every god I didn't believe in for it. Nearly tearing it off, I ran out into the street and saw the sun breaking in the distance. I made it to the train station. I'm sitting here now, waiting for the train to my parents' house. From one nightmare to another, but I'll take servitude over slaughter.   
Oscar's called twice. I'll have to answer eventually.

If I come back with no memory, I'll know they followed. I just hope the other seven will keep them full for long enough.
