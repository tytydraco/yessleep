Seeking reasoning or an answer to something is a basic human instinct, I like to think. In times of tragedy or strife, people always ask how or why things got this way. Those inquiries, more often than not, fall on deaf ears. That knowledge simply isn't readily available. That knowledge, might not be for us to know.

My shift at Costco just wrapped for the day. I grabbed my backpack that had my crappy old laptop in it from my locker and headed towards the front exit. It was late enough into the evening that the sun had already set, the faintest last moments of sunlight still brushing the horizon. However, a dark gray blanket of overcast clouds had settled in. On my way out, I grabbed myself dinner from the food court and met up with some coworkers out front. The store was still open, maybe about half an hour until they were ready to shut the large metal doors for the night.

"What'cha get this time, Wayne?" one of my coworkers asked me, even though the tin foil wrapped foods clearly gave away my order.

"The ol' standby. Lil' pep pizza, hot dog, and hey why not some vanilla ice cream?" I said as I flicked away some of the melted ice cream that was beginning to drip onto my hands. "Ah, napkins. Damnit." I didn't want to walk all the way back inside to grab them.

We all took a seat on the built-in concrete benches on the sides of the warehouse building, eating our food and just chatting about our work day. I've been trying to be more sociable recently, just for the sake of being sociable, and I felt like this was a good step. More often than not, once my shift ended, I would clock out and head straight out the door without so much as a "later!" to anyone. Can't say why I acted like that.

While my coworkers were talking and laughing about a certain experience they shared with a customer, I was busy devouring my food. I could eat Costco food court stuff for the rest of my life, I swear. As I was eating, I turned my attention toward the road that runs adjacent to the one that the warehouse sits on. A half mile away, or maybe less if my estimates are any good, I could hear loud bass coming from what I assume was someones car audio system. Someone was showing off, I thought to myself, though I'll admit it was slightly impressive that I was hearing it from this distance, and from around the corner. However, a few moments later, it got louder, and closer. Then I realized it wasn't just one cars audio system making that noise. It was a rather large group of souped up cars, all blaring different rap and hip hop songs that made it nearly impossible to identify which songs they were actually playing.

One of my coworkers looked over at the group of cars, now traveling down the road adjacent to the parking lot, and joked "Gas station attendant is about to go deaf." He would've been on the mark with that quip, if it wasn't for the fact that, instead of continuing down that road towards the gas station entrance, they instead turned into the parking lot, each car following tightly behind the other. "Orrrrrr we are," he corrected himself.

We kept watching them, just seeing where they would end up parking. It's not like we haven't seen people with obnoxiously loud sound systems pull up before, but a group of, what was that, like 8 of them? Were we about to witness some sort of large scale robbery? Nah, no way, I thought to myself. Why telegraph your approach like this? But as I was trying to use my rational thinking about the situation, they started to pull their cars up. Right up to the entrance doors. People scattered to get out of the way, swearing and flipping off the cars. The cars in the front had essentially blocked most of the width of the entrance and exit doors, though people could still walk around the cars and leave if they needed. The rest of the cars piled in behind the front cars, pulling all the way underneath the overhang above.

At this point, my coworkers and I had already stood up, ready to book it if we needed to. No way in hell we were putting our lives on the line for a corporate entity, especially off the clock. The car doors flung open, substantially increasing the volume of the already blaring music. Like clowns out of a clown car, all sorts of young punks spilled out of the cars and began grouping up near the front doors. A couple of them hung back and climbed onto the parked cars and pulled out what looked like rather expensive video cameras.

"Sideshow!!!" somebody in the cluster of people yelled out. Is that even the right word for whatever this is, I thought? Whatever, I'm out.

"I'm gonna head outta here," I told my coworkers as I started to turn and head towards the parking lot to my car.

"Yeah us too," they said. It was weird though, we all agreed we weren't comfortable with the situation. But I guess in a weird, rubber-necking way, we just kept watching.

As I was scanning the parking lot, looking for a safe way to navigate around the sideshow to get to my car, I heard more and more loud bass coming from the adjacent road. "Jeezus, there's more?" I yelled, pointing at the road so my coworkers could see. At least double the amount of cars that were already here we're speeding into the parking lot and filling in every last square inch of road they could take up. I hopped back onto the concrete bench and stood on the tips of my toes to see over the mass of cars now piled into the lot. "Where did you guys park?" I asked my coworkers.

"We're all in the back," they pointed over the sideshow cars. Great, I thought. Our cars are all trapped, and there's no way we're getting these people to move until they're done with, whatever the hell it is they're doing. What we're they doing? I looked back over at the people holding the cameras, and they were filming the rest of the people, all shoulder to shoulder, dancing and singing. My best guess was that one of them was an aspiring rapper who wanted to make a music video for their latest track. Lot of those around here. Well, that'll be interesting to see on Youtube later, if it gets uploaded, I thought.

"Well shit, we're not getting out of here anytime soon." I told my coworkers. "Let's head to the back near the receiving dock and just chill there for a little, yeah?" I figured for our safety we should put some distance between us and this commotion, in case some random bullshit starts to happen. I don't know, call me cynical or paranoid, but those were the vibes I was getting.

"Aight, sounds good. Gives me time to finish my food anyway." one of my coworkers replied.

I hoisted my backpack on one arm while still awkwardly carrying the rest of my food in the other as we headed down the side of the building and around its corner. There were still a few more straggler cars coming down the road and into the parking lot, also blasting their own loud music. This was definitely a planned meetup of some sort. I imagined the conversation leading up to this was something like "Yo, lets pull up at Costco and shoot the music video there," and then no plan at all was made. Well whatever, I'm sure a manager already called the police to come and disperse this group of people.

About 15 minutes had passed while we were sitting outside of the receiving dock doors. A faint drizzle started to come down as the overcast clouds rolled in. Very dark grey, I noted to myself. It's probably gonna pour tonight. Luckily there was some small overhangs we could stand under, just in case it did start coming down harder. We could still hear the music and the yelling from all the way around the backside of the warehouse, but luckily nobody came back here to bother us. We also took solace in the presence of a security camera that was placed back here.

My coworkers and I were all chatting about the current situation, mostly just noting how crazy, but also annoying it was that we were essentially stuck here and not able to get ourselves home. I figured, once police get here and begin dispersing the disturbance, that would be our cue to head back up to the front and get to our cars and leave. Just had to wait.

I turned my attention back to the security camera, nodding and waving at it, on the off chance someone reviewed the footage later and would see me. I could see the red light on the corner of the lens, indicating it was recording. However, I noticed it wasn't a steady red light. It was ever so faintly pulsing, like it was just barely drawing enough power to keep running. And it wasn't random intervals, it was a consistent tempo.

Fade-fade...fade...fade...repeating.

"Weird," I thought to myself. Probably just a wiring issue. I've been in the room where they keep the cameras all hooked up before, maybe I'll bring it up to someone tomorrow when I clock in, if I remember or give enough of a damn. I checked my phone for the time. A couple of raindrops touched down onto the screen, causing me to quickly wipe my phone screen on my shirt. I looked back at it. 8:25P.M. Store should be closing pretty much now, assuming the front cars aren't too far into the building to close the metal gates. But then right as I was about to turn my phone screen back off, I noticed it. The little notification symbols on the top corner of my phone that display things like location, battery, cell connection, all that stuff? They were flickering too. I kept watching them for a few moments.

Blink-blink...blink...blink...repeating.

The rain suddenly began to come down much harder. A torrential downpour that you would typically only see once, maybe twice during the summer. It was enough to temporarily distract me from the weird tech issues I noticed earlier. The rain coming down was hitting the concrete with such force, it was nearly enough to match the loud music coming from the sideshow. From underneath the overhang, I looked off into the distance where the clouds came from, and I saw a flash of lightning. Great, big storm tonight, I thought to myself. Don't remember seeing that on the forecast. Whatever.

From the same direction of the storm clouds rolling in, I could see the red and blue flashing lights approaching from down the opposite road. Finally, we would be able to get out of here fairly soon. I kept my sight towards the lights that were flashing against various buildings and foliage, as the police cars were too far out to visually see them. The lights held their position for a few moments, not continuing up the road towards us. Must be at a stoplight, I thought to myself. Wait no, they're police, they can run through those while they- my train of thought was interrupted. The red and blue lights all cut off simultaneously. The sirens stopped. Then, it seemed like everything else was stopping.

Street lights from that direction began to shutoff. The lights on the bus stop nearby flickered off. Next, the lights hanging high up on the warehouse walls shut off. I turned and looked at the camera. No red light whatsoever. My coworkers let out an exhausted sigh together. "Ugh, I hope the power isn't out at my house too," one of them said. I thought the same as well, but something else caught my attention.

The noise. There was much less. The only sound being made was from the heavy rainfall. All of the loud music and bass coming from it had stopped completely. I could hear people yelling and screaming in confusion about their cars, trying to figure out what was happening. I set my backpack down underneath the overhang and ventured out into the rain to see what I could down the side of the building towards the front of the warehouse. I was surprised at what I could see. Or rather, what I couldn't see. With the power outage in the area, combined with the heavy rain, overcast clouds, the only things I could make out towards that side of the building were the faint outlines of people moving around, still yelling loudly about whatever was going on.

I walked back to where my coworkers were, now moderately drenched by the rain. Rain doesn't bother me, I used to have to push carts all day in all sorts of weather. As I got back to them, one of them said "Hey, can I use someones phone? Mine just died on me. Damn Apple..." We all pulled our phones out ready to hand them over, but we all came to the same conclusion. All of our phones were dead.

At this point, anxiety started to kick in for all of us. Things were happening that shouldn't have been. None of us had any clue what was going on, and we were rightfully starting to freak out a bit. We all jumped when lightning flashed directly overhead of us. We did our best to huddle up against the side of the building as far underneath the overhang as possible. I tried knocking on the door that leads into the receiving dock area, in the hopes that someone would be there to let us in. No answer. Well I guess store policy does say to not let people in that way.

More lightning flashes. The accompanying thunder, louder than the sideshow ever was, boomed overhead almost instantly. This storm was directly on top of us. I swear I could feel the earth shake simply from the thunder alone. I could hear the group of people from the sideshow also yelling and screaming from the thunder. Really? It's just a thunderstorm, it's not a big deal, it's not the first one this area has ever had. People are overreacting for no reason, I thought to myself. It's like when you're in school, and the power goes out, every single girl in the classroom screams.

More lightning. Louder thunder roared overhead like a beast of myth. Man, was there an earthquake happening too? No, no way. It's just the thunder and lightning. More yelling from the front. More screaming. More chaos. This went on for a few minutes. A few minutes that felt like forever. After a few minutes, the torrential downpour finally started to ease up. It was still raining, sure, but a normal amount now. And once again, my ears picked up on something strange.

Nothing. Other than the rainfall, there was nothing. Still no music from the sideshow. Still no police sirens. But also, no more yelling and screaming. I once again took a few steps out and around the corner of the building to take a look, and I was utterly confused by what I saw. Before I began taking note of the finer details of the scene, I turned back towards where my coworkers were at to tell them to come take a look. I froze. They weren't there anymore. All that was left slumped against the wall next to the receiving door was my backpack. I quickly jogged over to the door, looking around the other end of the building to see if maybe they were checking the other side without me. Nobody. I called out a few times for them, but no answer.

"What the actual fuck is going on" I said out loud, hoping somebody would reply. Only the rain replied. I walked back over to my backpack and picked it up, slinging it over both of my shoulders. I probably should have waited for the rain to pass, in case the water seeped through and leaked onto my laptop, but at this point, more pressing issues needed to be addressed.

I walked back towards the other side of the building I initially went to, and slowly walked down the open portion of the parking lot up towards the front. I stumbled slightly when my foot kicked a piece of metal on the ground. I leaned down to look at it, and it appeared to be a piece of scrap metal. I couldn't tell what it was from immediately, so I kept walking forward, now being sure to be careful with my steps. Visibility was still very low, all things considered. There were more and more pieces of scrap metal, plastic, various debris scattered all across the parking lot as I approached the front. I eventually noticed an entire car bumper separated from whatever car it was attached to lying broken and mangled on the ground. Then I got to the front of the warehouse. And what I saw honestly reminded me of a warzone.

All of the cars from the sideshow were utterly destroyed. They looked like they were pulverized from above by a giant hydraulic press. They weren't completely flattened out, but they had definitely been crushed in some rapid manner, enough to send chunks of the cars outwards and into the parking lot. Guess that explains the debris I found on the way. But, what crushed these cars? If it was something like, I don't know, meteors or something, where are the meteors? Falling satellites? Something? Like something came down, smashed up these cars, and then disappeared? And to add even more confusion to the situation, the cars that were parked up close to the big entrance doors into the warehouse were even smashed up. But how? They were parked well underneath the overhang of the building, something would have had to punch through that overhang first in order to reach the cars. So how did these cars get wrecked?

But then I made a realization. The people. Where is everyone? There was a whole motley crew of people here just now making a ruckus. Did they take shelter inside of the warehouse during the storm? I didn't see any blood or guts, thankfully, while I was inspecting the damage outside. Guess I should see if the people inside are alright. The large metal gates had collapsed during...whatever mess happened earlier, and I couldn't find a safe way to navigate around them to get inside. I could see inside the warehouse though, but eerily, heard no noise coming from within.

"Hey!" I yelled into the void that was the interior of the warehouse. All I heard was my own echo. I tried again. "Hey! You guys alright in here?" I assumed that there were people in here. Surely, there had to be. Silence, again. I could not believe what was happening. I stepped back away from the doors, closed my eyes, took a couple of slow deep breaths I was taught to do by my therapist, and composed myself. I gave myself a couple of soft slaps on my face with my hands, just to make sure I was present in this situation, before deciding what to do next. Home, I guess would be the best bet.

Home. Maybe there I'll find answers.
