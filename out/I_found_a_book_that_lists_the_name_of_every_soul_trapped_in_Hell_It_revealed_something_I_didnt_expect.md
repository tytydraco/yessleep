Forgive me, friends, I’m not used to writing or “posting” online, but I feel I must in this instance to provide a warning in case you ever come across a certain bizarre and evil book.

My name is Edvard Larsen. I’m a Dane and an antiquarian bookseller.

On the night in question I locked my shop’s door with an ornate iron key before turning to stroll down the dark-rain cobblestone alley, within which my beloved business sits, a mausoleum to enigmatic tomes, dust, and dead ideas.

“Edvard … I need to see it.”

Startled, I turned toward the disembodied voice in the black fog.

“Viggo, is that you, my friend?” I said, as an old man—like me—emerged from the shadows.

“It’s me, Edvard. The book—no—the *ledger* you told me about. I need to see it.”

I scrunched my face in discomfort and pushed the round Windsor glasses to the bridge of my nose. *Ah, yes,* I thought, *I remember …*

Too much wine. Loose lips. Tantalus and the gods. An old man’s indiscretion.

*Fool—damned fool!*

I had told my longtime friend Viggo about the weird—and potentially dangerous—artifact that had come into my possession just last week, an ancient book I had taken to calling the *Damnation Ledger* as it bore no other apparent title or identifying description, just … *names* within.

Viggo continued: “You believe this ledger lists the name of every person—every soul—that resides in Hell. My daughter, Astrid, you know she led a … *challenging* life. I have to know if she—I have to see if her name is in that book, Edvard.”

My friend had lost his daughter years ago to an accident, one I will not divulge the details of here as I do not believe it pertains to this account. As well, the chaotic eras of that girl’s life are not mine to share. For you, dear reader, I will simply tell you that Viggo loved his child deeply despite her faults—as all parents should—and her death left an indelible signature upon his heart.

“I trust you know, my friend,” I said softly, “that some knowledge is not meant for us apes, that we should satisfy ourselves with the Mystery and meddle no further.”

Viggo spoke no more, just watched me, distraught and defeated. Had his devout religious belief led him to this—this need to know the fate of his daughter’s soul?

I paused. I wavered. I caved.

Against my better judgement and the gnawing pinch in my gut, I—the old bookman—led Viggo through the front door and beyond the smoky hardwood shelves and cabinets that almost audibly groaned with the weight of antique volumes, papers, maps, and ephemera.

We passed through a dense cellar door adorned with sturdy vintage locks and down a winding stair to the immaculate stone-walled basement where I kept the finest gems of my bookshop’s collection. Only the most valuable, most curious pieces graced the subterranean labyrinth of shelves and bookbinding workbenches.

“It’s here, Viggo,” I said, pointing out a black leatherbound tome, ornamented with a red gilding that wrapped the volume in strange and nonsensical lines, a demonic vine constricting the cover and spine as if binding the wicked names within.

Viggo approached cautiously and opened the heavy top cover of the book.

“Finally, Astrid—my sweet child—I’ll know. I’ll know where your tumultuous soul resides.”

It creaked and whined like a primeval ghost as he flipped the warped paper, his eyes growing wide as he contemplated the crimson ink therein.

“Strange the various languages and symbols here,” Viggo said. “I didn’t know what to expect, but I should be able to pick out her name if it’s here. Interesting, as well, how the names are not alphabetized or ordered in any meaningful way—perhaps in the order or year of death? Oh well, it doesn’t take long to flip through and, after all, we can’t expect the Gatekeepers of Hell to adhere to our ways of bookkeeping. Ha! I’m very relieved at how short this work is, Edvard.”

“There’s something I need to tell—” I began after a few minutes. Viggo had finished his review of the book and loudly closed the back cover. A smile of relief spread across his face.

“She’s not here, Edvard. She’s not in the book—your so-called *Damnation Ledger*, ha! Thank, God!”

“I said … there’s something I need to tell you.”

Viggo paused, his face forming a mask of concern. “What—what is it?”

I again pushed the glasses higher onto my nose and placed a trembling palm to my chest. Then, lifting it, I pointed into a shrouded corner deep in the cellar. In a supernatural cloud of translucent black there stood a dripping, oily wooden door set deep into the stone.

We both looked with horror at the sinister portal beyond the reach of light.

I said: “Viggo, I acquired this book unknowingly, as part of the purchase of a collection of books from a centuries-old estate in Germany. Out of that entire collection, this ledger was the only book that could not be identified, by me or my trusted consultants.”

“Go on …” said Viggo.

“I brought the ledger here to my basement workshop to investigate further. There are names inside, yes, but also symbols that cannot be found in any reference book or online. On occasion, foul odors emanate from the pages. And Viggo … I cannot say for certain that this book is not made or marked in part by human skin, blood, or even bone. It wasn’t until I had continued working through the pages that I realized a door had appeared in my cellar … *that* door.” Again, I pointed a shuddering digit at the dripping ebony wood.

Viggo’s eyes widened. “What’s behind it, Edvard? Did you open it?”

“Yes.”

“And?”

“My friend, this book before us—this *Damnation Ledger*—appears to grant its owner access to *that* door, and beyond it, to its siblings.”

“Its siblings?” said Viggo.

I sighed and continued: “This isn’t the only *Ledger*. There are more—so many more. I—I’m so sorry, my friend.”

In a burst of panic, Viggo raced to the dusky wet door, pulled upon the heavy ring handle, and threw open the portal. He ran into the inky blackness of the space beyond and disappeared from view.

A horrid scream exhaled back out through the gaping maw.

I felt a single tear cascade down the deep wrinkles of my face.

I walked to the door and entered, placing a hand on the tremoring shoulder of Viggo, who had collapsed into a shattered kneel only a few meters within.

“*Astrid … oh my dear God … Astrid …*”

Within the black and cavernous space was a near-infinite library, as if ten thousand cathedrals had been merged at grotesque and illogical angles that only the vicious geometry of Hell could accommodate. The air stank of sulfur and rot and the red light that did come through the murky windows assaulted our eyes with prejudice. Across the vast expanse towered bookshelves of basalt and obsidian which writhed and twisted deep into labyrinthine corridors beyond our vision, and stacked neatly upon all of these shelves, side-by-side, were the brothers and sisters of the *Damnation Ledger*, binding and archiving every name lost to perdition, infinite and expanding like the grim universe above.

“Listen to me, Viggo,” I said calmly, “this could all be an illusion meant to torment us, it could be—"

Viggo made to run and thrashed as I caught him, raging against me as I tried to pull him back through the doorway and into the bookshop’s cellar.

“Let go of me, Edvard! Get the fuck off me! I can find it—if I start *now* I can find it. If her name is here in this cursed place, I can find it. Astrid! *Astrid!*”

“We have to get out, Viggo!” I screamed, pulling my friend harder to the doorway.

“*Astrid!*”

With a heartbreaking shriek, he ceased fighting and crumbled into a sobbing mess against my frail chest and whispered, as if for the last time: “*Astrid …*”

At last, I was able to pull him over the threshold and out of the Morningstar’s black archive, but not before I heard the dismal whisper of his daughter’s name echo louder and louder through that indifferent vault, across the hopeless expanse we never should have entered.

Everything returned to silence once I slammed the door shut behind us.

My friends, I don’t know if other copies—*other siblings*—of the *Damnation Ledger* have made their way into our earthly realm. I can only tell you that I secreted away my copy where no one is likely to find it—at least, not in this century—and when I did, the door to the black archive vanished from my cellar wall.

So, if you find the book, do not open it. Do not wander into that dark place beyond our world, to a place that holds secret knowledge not meant for our mortal eyes.

Embrace the Mystery and look no further.
