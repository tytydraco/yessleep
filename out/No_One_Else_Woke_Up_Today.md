*The world is quiet today and I am alone.*

 

 

My mother was supposed to wake me today, I had a job interview at nine and I was terribly anxious about missing it. However, when I finally roused myself this morning it was already eleven, I had missed it. Such terrible anger filled me, this job was meant to be the start of my career, the rest of my life - though that all seems childish now. I stormed into her room and found her passed out in bed. Sleeping so soundly, completely oblivious to the extent of her betrayal. I moved over to her side and began to shake her, but still, she didn’t stir. I shook more violently now… then I yelled… and then I was screaming. Nothing could wake her, no matter how much I shook or yelled, she seemed completely oblivious to it, remaining soundly asleep. Panicked, I reached down to her neck and tried to take a pulse. But my own heart was beating so hard and fast now, I couldn’t be sure if I felt anything. Desperately, I dialled 999, all the while continuing to shake her. It rung once, it rung twice, it kept ringing and my sorrow gave way to an implacable dread. No one answered.

 

 

I fled down the stairs and outside. The sun was high in the sky, the smell of damp earth and freshly cut grass told me it was any other Sunday in spring. But there were no children playing in the streets, no cars on the road, no birds flying overhead. It’s an impossible thing to describe, the sensation of total silence, it hit me immediately as I stepped from my front porch out on to the street. Clinging to rationalisation, I ran to my neighbours and began furiously banging on the door. When no one answered, I tried the next house, and then the next, working my way down the street. After the fifth house, I don’t know why, but I just ran, ran as far as I could, the sound of my feet against asphalt echoing undisturbed into the cavernous silence. Only when the burning in my legs and pain in my chest became too much to bear did I finally stop and fall to my knees. For the first time since I began running, I properly took in where I was, I had made it to the newsagents. For some reason, the front window had been smashed like someone had broken in-

 

 

Suddenly, someone crept out from the hole in the window. The man was dishevelled and unwashed, possibly homeless. With difficulty he made it through the small hole and began walking away, taking no notice of me. He stumbled away clumsily, in each hand he carried a six pack of beer which swung wildly at his sides as he moved, he was pissed as a fart. I set after him, despite his appearance here was my saviour, my north star in the foreign waters I had found myself in. “Sir, are you alright? Do you know what’s going on?” I pleaded. He shot me a glance over his shoulder and continued walking. “Leave me alone, would ya!” he grumbled back at me. I grasped him by the shoulder but immediately he spun away from me and in his stupor he fell backwards. As soon as he crashed against the ground, the man passed out. I couldn’t wake him. Solemnly, I collected the now discard beers and walked back home.

 

 

The sun has now set and with it the window for a reasonable explanation has been shuttered. I write now, not in the hopes that anyone will ever read it, but merely to stay awake, a terrible hypochondria has now gripped me, a fear that when rest takes me I to will never wake. Sure, I have avoided the endless slumber once before, but I feel whatever business has caused this is not done yet.  About an hour ago, I had sworn I heard a knock at the door, a faint one, almost shy, but in the silence I had heard it. However, when I bounded down the stairs and opened the door, no one was there, perhaps I had simply imagined it, a desperate trick of the mind but I’m not sure. When I stood there on my porch, the nights chill on my face, staring out at the silent dark, it felt like something was watching me. *Something is waiting for us to be asleep*.
