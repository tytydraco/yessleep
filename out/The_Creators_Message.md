TW: suicide, blood, depression and self harm.



I was reborn.



I was told that it wasn't possible, that there was nothing after death. There was no soul, no energy, no afterlife, no heaven or purgatory. Absolutely nothing.

Why did I believe that? Because the Creator himself told me, and science proved it.

It's not like I was special or something, he always sends at least one message to each of us during our lifetimes. For me, one day while I was lying in bed, the knowledge that this was my only chance at living dawned on me. It was as if someone had implanted a false memory of my mother in my mind, the one dearest to me. I knew at the moment that it wasn't her, she was way too gentle and had an otherworldly aura surrounding her. Don't get me wrong, she was a kind woman, but really loud and religious. It was out of character for her to deny the existence of heaven.

I must admit, it was quite funny since I had terminal cancer at the time and didn't have more than three months left. It felt more like torture than anything. Some people cling to religion or other beliefs for comfort before dying, but I had nothing.

You don't need someone to tell you this to live your life to the fullest, but I would have done more exciting and reckless things if I had known.

I died at 32 years old, leaving a wife and a five-year-old boy behind.

— Are you okay? — A male voice asked. —I'm sorry if I overshared, do you... want me to ask for the check?

He stuttered a lot, I could tell that he was nervous. I stared at his face without blinking.

That was MY voice.

MY face.

— Oh, no, no, I'm sorry! I was processing it, I shouldn't have asked — A female voice escaped my lips.

We continued chatting. The conversation, the place, and the voice all came back to me. It was one of our first dates, when I told my wife how my parents had died. Dad killed himself and mom died in a car accident. Back then I was insecure because that was something I never shared with anyone, until I met her.

My body was moving as if it had a mind of its own, but I felt it, I could fight against it.

— I... I need... — I choked on the words. — I need to go to the bathroom.

I practically bolted out of my seat and ran, desperate to see my face.

— Uh, excuse me, ma'am? This is the men's restroom.

I ignored him and touched my face while looking at the mirror. The reflection staring back at me was my wife. Her 24-year-old face looked back from the mirror.

A cute brunette with brown eyes wearing subtle makeup except for her dark red lips. She was wearing a short skirt, a fitted blouse and knee-high brown boots. She was so stunning that I wanted to eat her back then.

I still did. It was a bit weird because now I was her.

I resisted the urge to retouch my lipstick and went back. It felt strange, foreign. I was a man for God's sake.

I pushed my male instincts aside and finished the date. He walked me back home. We both wanted a kiss. It felt disgusting, but I let her body take over.

Thinking about it later in my bed, the possibility of becoming a lesbian crossed my mind because, of course, men did nothing for me. The thought was hard to accept because despite being handsome, I felt no attraction toward myself, but I wanted my son to be born. I loved that little rascal; he was mine, perfect.

 There was a problem, though. Being reborn as the opposite sex sucks. Not because I think less of women, but being stripped of your magic wand felt ominous. I had trouble figuring out the bathroom, and refused thongs, feminine clothes and makeup. Also, there was some sort of phantom limb pain with my poor missing third leg.

Of course, intimacy was out of the question.

Needless to say, practically becoming a tomboy overnight took a toll on our relationship. At first it was tolerable, I let her body take control, but the more days passed, the more it felt mine. And I couldn't, for the life of me, act purposely like a young girl.

I really wanted to ask myself to be a sperm donor at that point.

That night we had a huge fight after I flinched when he touched me. He said that I changed, that he felt undesired, that I stopped taking care of myself and he wanted to break up. I refused. I told him I was going through something and needed time. 



I ended up storming out of his apartment, annoyed at how much of a prick I had been. He didn't even offer to walk me home.

In my defense, I was young and immature, but we were all like that once. Sue me.

As I walked, I heard footsteps behind me. I turned around, but found nothing. I didn't have enough cash for an Uber. At that time my wife was still a student working as a waitress, living paycheck to paycheck. It wasn't so late yet, almost 9 pm. There were still cars passing and some people walking nearby.

That was when something pricked my back.

— Don't scream.

It was a knife. 

Then I remembered something. Her little sister on the playground. She stopped swinging and approached her.

— You're going to be murdered — she giggled, her smile had a missing tooth.

Admitting what he had done to me, to her, in that alley was impossible.

I was furious at myself, at the man, and the world. I felt absolutely traumatized. I could still feel the knife slicing through my limbs while blood started pouring out, but he didn't stop. Not even when my body was falling apart.

I wish she hadn't felt any of that.

I started to cry and shake violently.

— Sweetie, what's wrong?

A woman asked. I recognized her, she was my kindergarten teacher. Was I back in my body?

She picked me up and soothed me the best she could, but I couldn't stop. I wailed until I passed out.

When I woke up at the infirmary there was a woman sitting beside me, talking on her phone. When we locked eyes, she winced in pain and stroked my hair.

— Honey, what happened? — her voice was kind. — You can tell mommy.

But she wasn't my mother. It dawned on me that I was someone else. I didn't respond, despair filled me and I stared blankly at the ceiling. She got up, talked with the nurse and took me to her home.

— I had a nightmare — I said with a childish voice.

She nodded, but I could tell she didn't believe me. Soon, I was seeing a therapist twice a week. If it had only been that one episode it would have gone unnoticed, but since I was trapped in a kid's body with a traumatized adult mind, I had a lot more. I feel ashamed to admit it, but I began to wet the bed, waking up screaming and sleeping with the lights on. I became quiet, didn't speak or play. His mother didn't know what to do, she was desperate and cried every night when she thought I couldn't hear.

They diagnosed me with depression and prescribed me antidepressants. The woman was reluctant since I was only 3 years old, but after a rough week where I barely slept, she relented.

The will of a child's body is weak, not fully formed, so his consciousness finally took control when I started taking the pills and stopped thinking. To everyone I got better, acting like a kid again, but deep down, my adult mind remained hidden and broken.

After a while I realized I had been friends with my original self in kindergarten. The magic wand was back at least. We got separated when we started middle school and we weren't the best of friends to begin with, that was why I had forgotten about him.

After a couple of years in that body, I started to heal and accept that life like my own. I began to love that woman like a mother. She was alone, but she was hardworking, loving and smart. I even made a few friends by her insistence. It felt weird because I was an adult and they were real children, but I knew that I had to pretend a bit or she would get worried. I couldn't make her suffer like I did back then.

Everything changed when one of my classmates took his own life. He was eleven. A baby in my eyes. Alan screamed in the middle of our math class and jumped out the window head first. We were on the third floor.

The whole class attended the funeral. The teacher who was in charge at the time cried the loudest besides the family. Nobody blamed him. Alan had been a happy child before that and it happened so fast that no one could've predicted it. The parents were understanding and let him attend the service.

While I stayed there in silence, holding hands with my mom and hearing the priest talk, she suddenly stared at me with a blank face.

— Kill — she said.

It was like my mind went haywire from then on. That was the only thing I could think of, dream about, and wish for. It was as if I had been given a command I had no choice but to follow. My existence was solely for the purpose of killing.

The messages were changing.

And I had to obey.

I became a serial killer in my teens. One who cried after every murder while reliving my past death each time. It was an order that my body eagerly followed, and my mind became more and more twisted, falling into it like an addiction I couldn't stop.

My mother never suspected a thing. My adult mind helped me cover my traces seamlessly and, besides that, I was a good child. I didn't overdo it and made it quick, only a few victims per year and didn't leave a pattern. However, the deaths became more and more bloody.

First, a stolen car. The second time, I threw a brick from the rooftop.

Then, they began to smile.

When I used my fists, the girl was crying for more, smiling.

I stabbed a man, and he twisted the knife.

Once, a policeman found me with my hands around a man's throat.

They both grinned.

— Thank you — the man said.

He handed me his club, not his gun, and I used it.

I didn't even want to stop anymore.

Then one day, when Mom was out of town, after my last killing, I saw my face in the mirror while washing the blood away. I was almost a grown-up now. My baby face was fading and adult features were emerging on my face.

The little stubble gave it away.

I started seeing it: the face of the man who killed me in my past life.

My mind returned as I realized everything I had done. I looked at my crying face, but then I smiled.

No, it wasn't me. It was his fault. Now I could get my revenge.

The next day, my mom found me in the apartment, smiling. I had killed myself. Maybe that was the message he really meant to send.

I thought it was over, but I was reborn again.

This time, I was my mom. The original one. Holding newborn me in her arms while my father rested on the sofa. I was wearing a hospital gown and was in so much pain despite having an IV drip with painkillers. I pressed a button and a nurse helped me to the bathroom. I confirmed it was her when I looked in the mirror. Peeing hurt and I had stitches down there. The woman told me she got a third-degree tear while giving birth, and it hurt like hell.

— Want me to help you with the pain? — she smiled without blinking.

She pressed on the stitches. I winced in pain and the injury began to bleed.

— I could help you with the baby too.

I refused, struggling to break free from her grasp. She looked sad.

— They told me to help you. It's my duty.

I took the needle out of my arm and stabbed her. She loosened her grip, and I pushed her and bolted out of the room. I woke up my father, gave him the baby and dragged him out. We didn't even sign the release papers, but nobody noticed because the hospital was collapsing. Ambulance sirens were blasting at full force, doctors were running everywhere and patients were screaming. The baby started crying while we ran to the car.

When I sat down, I felt a sharp pain and looked down. There was a pool of blood between my legs.

— Are you okay? — he asked. — What's going on?

I began to feel dizzy. My thoughts were getting blurry.

— Drive — I blurted out.

He complied, but insisted on getting medical attention. We were heading to another hospital, but it was the same. We couldn't get past the entrance. We went to the next one. Nothing. My consciousness was slipping; I could feel my life fading again. My father was desperate while baby me cried in the backseat.

My mother's memories flowed into me. Her as a child, teenager, young adult, marrying and giving birth.

During labor, I saw that wrinkled face coming out of the womb. I started crying, my face red.

— Let me hold him — my mother pleaded.

A nurse smiled, congratulated her and gave her the baby. He stopped and locked eyes with his mother. Then he grinned, but it was impossible. I was a newborn then; I shouldn't have been able to do that yet.

— Kill me.

It came out of the infant's mouth, but it was my voice, the adult me.

My eyes rolled back and I died.

I was reborn.

Big hands on the steering wheel, knuckles white. I looked next to me and saw my mother lying in a pool of her own blood in the passenger seat. There was a giggle and another memory made its way into my brain. I tried to fight it and continued to drive, but my body folded, refusing to abide by my orders. It wanted me to listen and I didn't have any strength left.

My mind was falling apart. I agreed.

On the right side, where a woman should be, was an old man. It was my grandpa, my dad's father. He patted my shoulder.

— The Creator is dead, son.

 His eyes were hollow, his lips curled into a smile.

Dread filled me, but it wasn't mine. I had known this for a long time. However, I understood now why my father killed himself. Why all those people did it too.

They were scared of the things that wanted to toy with us now that we no longer had protection, but I refused to give in like they did. My wife was one of them, she didn't understand. Our little boy did, I killed him before I died so they couldn't reach him.

Maybe it's my punishment.

If you read this, ignore their messages. Those memories that seem out of place and look like advice are not good. Don't listen. They like to see you in despair.

It's not the creator anymore.
