The radio in my '09 Civic had been dead for six months, so I enjoyed my midnight commutes down Route 9 in peace. It was full of pine trees, blacktop, the occasional flickering streetlight. I knew the rhythm of the dark because I drove it every night.



until the deer hit my passenger side



There was a sickening crunch of metal and bones. I slammed on the brakes, skidded to a halt on the gravelly shoulder of the road. My heart hammered against my ribs as I dug my phone from my pocket, switched on the flashlight, and climbed out into the humid July night. The front right bumper was crumpled, but there was no animal on the road. There was only a dark smear of something viscous that snaked away into the underbrush of the pine trees.



I had no idea what compelled me to follow the trail of black ichor, but I pushed through the damp ferns until my phone's beam picked up the shape of something on the ground. A man, facedown, his arms pinned awkwardly beneath him. He was wearing faded jeans and a dark hoodie, breathing heavily.



"Hey," I said, clearing my throat. "Hey, don't move. I'm going to call an ambulance."



The man didn't groan. Didn't even twitch. Slowly, with an unpleasant fluidity, he rotated his neck until he was looking straight up at me. His face towards me while his stomach still pressed in the dirt. His eyes were completely black and he was smiling. Showing rows of extremely fine teeth.



"Thank you," he said. His voice sounded like dry leaves rubbing together. "I couldn't get to the road by myself."

My hands were shaking so badly I fumbled for my phone. The beam wavered and fell across his arms, which were lengthening, twisting like pale spider legs, fingers pricking out of the leaves.



I didn't pick up the phone. I turned and ran.

Branches slapped against my cheeks as I shot back up towards Route 9. I threw myself into the driver's seat of the Honda, slammed the door, and leaned against it, heart pounding. The engine roared to life and I shoved the car into drive, tires screaming as I raced down the empty highway. I didn't stop until I pulled into my driveway, wheezing, shaking from head to toe. I forced myself to get out and look at the damage under the porch light. The left passenger door was pushed in, dark smears coating it. But right in the middle of the dent, embedded in the dried blood, was the unmistakable imprint of a bare hand. Human. But with six long fingers.

I locked the doors as I walked inside, pulling the blinds shut behind me. 

The living room lights were all on, casting long shadows across the floor. I sat on the couch, waiting. It's been three hours since I got home. My phone is still out in the woods.

 But five minutes ago, my dead car radio in the driveway suddenly turned on, the sound of static hissing out through the open windows, carrying a faint scraped leaf whisper.



"You forgot me."
