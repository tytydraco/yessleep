This happened about a year ago. My parents sent me to a summer camp somewhere in British Columbia, Canada — if I'm not mistaken, it was called "Camp Birchwood." The first thing that put me on edge, though I didn't pay much attention to it at the time, was that they immediately confiscated and took away all our phones and means of communication. At that moment I just shrugged: "Well, they took them, so what? What could happen?"

​The next day at morning roll call, a guy about fourteen years old started loudly complaining and demanding his things back. One of the counselors, with a completely empty, lifeless stare, quietly told him: "After lights out, come to my room." I noticed this scene, felt a strange, clammy unease, but quickly got distracted and forgot about it.

​At night I woke up thirsty and went outside the building. In the pitch darkness of the yard, I noticed three figures: two adult men silently dragging that same guy by the arms. He was trying to break free, but his movements were accompanied only by a quiet, panicked whisper — as if his mouth had been clamped shut for good.

​I don't know what devil possessed me to follow them. Trying not to make any noise, I moved from shadow to shadow until they approached some old, half-abandoned storage sheds at the very edge of the grounds.

​A heavy metal bolt slid open with a screech. From the darkness of the opening came a heavy, rotten stench — an acrid mixture of dry dust and raw, stagnant meat.

​Deep inside the shed, right under the roof, there was something. It didn't come down to the ground — it slowly moved its broken, thin, jointed limbs, like those of a giant spider. Black, dried-out skin stretched over the bones. On its disfigured face, only a sunken, senseless maw stood out.

​The guy went into hysterics, begging for mercy. But the men, with a complete absence of any emotion, as if on autopilot, simply threw him inside.

​There was a dry, wet crack — the sound of joints breaking. In the darkness, a torn-off head flashed by, stuck in the creature's deep throat, and then came a nauseating crunch. The being lunged at the flesh with ravenous hunger. On the spit-covered concrete, all that remained was a single dried-out, bloodless kidney...

​I nearly threw up right there. On stiff, cotton-like legs, I started backing away. Only one thought pounded in my head: we're just food to them. I have to run.

​In the morning, the camp went about its usual life. The counselors smiled falsely, the kids laughed. No one even mentioned the missing guy, as if he had never existed. I understood: there was no time. I could be next.

​By four in the afternoon, my bag was packed. For the rest of the day I played the role of an ordinary teenager — chatted with the guys, joked around, even though everything inside me was clenching with icy fear.

​At 11:00 PM, when the building sank into sleep, the hour of "X" arrived. Holding my breath, I slipped outside the fence.

​The night forest greeted me with deafening silence. After walking about three kilometers, I froze: ahead of me flickered the silhouette of a man with a weapon. A guard. I instantly dropped into the wet grass.

​Suddenly, from above, right from the branches, a dark shadow fell with inhuman silence. Sharp claws sank into the sentry's throat in a fraction of a second. Blood sprayed. Something only remotely resembling a human opened its maw to an unimaginable width and literally disfigured the remains of the body in just a couple of moments, tearing out flesh with a dry crack.

​I lay in the grass, afraid to even breathe. My heart pounded in my ears so loudly that it seemed the creature would hear it at any moment. When the being vanished into the thicket, I waited another hour or so before forcing myself to get up. Approaching the guard's remains, I noticed a grenade on his belt. Doubts burned my soul: what if it malfunctions, what if it's defective? But I still clenched it in my fist and moved on.

​By dawn I could no longer feel my legs. Running out onto the highway, I dropped the grenade into a ditch — I didn't need any extra questions.

​Soon an old, rusty pickup truck stopped next to me. A hunched-over old man was sitting behind the wheel.

​"Where are you headed, kid?" he asked in a dry, emotionless voice.

​"Well... I got lost, was hiking," I lied, though I probably looked like an escapee from an asylum.

​The old man gave me a heavy look:

​"Did you run away from 'Camp Birchwood'?"

​My heart sank into my heels.

​"How do you know? Are you... are you going to take me back?!"

​"Don't be afraid, I used to work there," the old man cut me off quietly, staring strictly at the road. "I won't hand you over."

​"What are those creatures?!" I blurted out.

​The old man was silent for a long time, then said something that made chills run down my spine:

​"Those are the ones who weren't lucky. They weren't just tortured... they were broken. Forced to eat living flesh, turned into cannibals and monsters. Those who couldn't endure it — went to feed the others. I didn't last a month there. And if I tell anyone — they'll destroy my whole family. They make you sign a non-disclosure agreement for life. And who would believe it anyway?.."

​He glanced at me in the rearview mirror:

​"Leave. Forget it. Don't tell anyone — not friends, not the police. Everything there is covered up."

​He dropped me off at the nearest small town. I changed into clean clothes from my bag and started making my way to my aunt's house taking local buses. I lied to her that there had been a fire at the camp and we had been sent home. Barely, but she believed it.

​A year has passed. Right now I'm sitting in my family's remote cabin, the clock reads 2:34 AM. All this time I've had the feeling that someone is relentlessly watching me from the darkness. I reassured myself that it was just paranoia, but...

​Damn. Just now the lights went out in the entire house.

​I'll go check.
