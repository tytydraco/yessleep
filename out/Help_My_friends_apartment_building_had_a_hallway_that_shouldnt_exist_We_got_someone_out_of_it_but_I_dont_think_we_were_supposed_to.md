I'm still trying to come up with a normal explanation for what happened last night. Apologies ahead of time that this isn't the most narratively compelling action story ever written. I just need to get it out there my friends and I need help in figuring out what happened. 

My name is Max, I’m a college student studying psychology, which means I’m usually the person asking for evidence while everyone else is telling ghost stories. I love reading about ghosts, demons, or most of the things people post on here, but I don't really believe them. 

After last night, I’m not sure anymore.

The party started normally.

A friend of mine was celebrating her birthday at her apartment, and by ten o’clock the place was pretty crowded. Twenty or thirty people, music rattling the walls, cheap alcohol, laughter spilling into the hallway. The usual college-party chaos.

Nothing felt wrong.

I noticed the time was approaching 11:00, so I convinced one of my friends to try one of those ridiculous paranormal rituals involving a closet and the time 11:11 - we both got in, and spun around 11 times at exactly 11:11. We weren’t expecting anything. We were drunk enough to be curious and sober enough to laugh at ourselves.

After nothing appeared, we came out of the closet and I jokingly said it was a little colder in there than outside… My friend put his hand back in and it was definitively colder. Not drafty. Cold. The kind of cold that seemed to cling to the inside of my wrist after I pulled my hand away.

We placed a tennis ball in the closet and closed the door, then when we reopened it, the ball was several inches from where we’d put it. Neither of us saw it move, but obviously an uneven floor - We laughed, blamed the floor, and forgot about it.

I mention that because of what happened afterward, although I still don’t know whether the two things were connected.

To keep the spooky vibes going, I mentioned a story I had seen on reddit about a murder that had supposedly happened in the building decades ago. The story changed depending on who told it, but the version I heard involved someone being dismembered and stuffed into a washing machine so that any identifying evidence would be washed away.

I have no idea if any of that was true, but of course we had to go down to the basement to check it out.

The laundry room was exactly what you’d expect: concrete walls, rusted pipes, old machines, and the damp, sour smell of a place that never fully dries. One of the washers was making a repetitive thumping sound.

Thump.

Scrape.

Thump.

Scrape.

We unplugged it.

It kept going.

That was the first moment I stopped laughing.

We opened the machine, but the only thing inside, caught beneath the drum, was a strip of dark blue fabric, soaked through and twisted into a rope. When we looked behind the machine, we found another piece had snagged near the back support. Then we dragged the washer farther out, we found a small black plastic bag wedged behind it.

There was more dark blue fabric inside.

We didn’t find anything that proved the murder story was real. No bones. No blood. Nothing we could point to and say, This is what happened.

Then suddenly THUMP, SCRAAAAPE, from behind the concrete block wall. We went back out into the hall to see if there was another room next to the laundry room. We found an unlocked storage room and went in to check the wall behind the washer. 

The wall was lined with metal shelving. We moved the shelves aside and found a small access plate, maybe six inches square. The screws had been painted over so many times they looked fused into the metal.

I found a hammer and pry-bar on one of the shelves, which we used to pry it open.

Behind it was a corridor.

Not a crawl space. Not a maintenance shaft.

A corridor.

It stretched away into darkness, far beyond the wall, far beyond the building’s footprint. The walls and floor were concrete. Pipes ran along the ceiling. It was impossible. 

And lying, just visible in the space, was a shoe.

I called into the opening.

“Hello?”

For a few seconds, there was only the hum of the basement lights.

Then someone answered.

“Max?”

My name. 

I called out, “who are you?” she said her name was Sarah, then, “Please help me get out. I'm not alone.”

We couldn’t see what she meant. Our flashlights reached down the corridor, but the beam seemed to weaken after a certain point. Sarah stood just beyond it, pale and shaking.

I used the hammer and pry-bar to widen the opening.

She crawled toward us, but when she reached the edge, she stopped.

Her hands struck something.

There was nothing there.

No door. No glass. No wall. But when Sarah tried to push through, her palms flattened against an invisible surface. Her face twisted with panic.

“What's going on?!,” she said. “Why can’t I get through?”

I slammed the invisible barrier with the hammer and - The air bent.

That’s the only way I can describe it. The space between us rippled like heat rising from pavement. The corridor stretched and snapped back. For one second, I saw the walls bend inward around Sarah’s body.

We could hear the footsteps getting closer, but still couldn't see anything behind her. 

She screamed.

I hit the barrier harder.

The distortion spread in trembling waves. Sarah shoved her hands through, then her shoulders. We grabbed her wrists and pulled.

Something grabbed her ankle.

She kicked wildly. I saw fingers close around her leg, pale fingers with nails blackened at the tips.

I slashed at the hand with the pry-bar and it let go.

Sarah came through all at once, collapsing onto the basement floor. She was bleeding from one knee, her clothes were filthy, and she was so exhausted she could barely speak.

She said she’d woken up on the concrete floor of the corridor with no idea how she’d gotten there. She’d been walking in that space for what felt like hours. 

Every time she stopped, she heard footsteps behind her.

When she started walking again, they followed.

She’d seen shoes scattered along the corridor. Sneakers. Boots. Dress shoes. Some looked old enough to belong in a museum. Some were still wet.

Some had blood on them.

She hadn’t seen any bodies.

We got her out, and for a moment I thought the worst was over.

We left the basement and started back up the stairs to my friends apartment, but as we reached the landing just below, the party upstairs didn’t sound right anymore. None of us could quite put it into words, but there were too many voices.

People were calling my name from above, but the voices overlapped strangely, as if dozens of people were speaking through the same mouth. 

My friend stared toward the stairwell.

“That isn’t the party I remember,” she whispered.

We exchanged looks and decided to head back downstairs and get out of the building.

There should have been two flights to the first floor - we went down at least four. 

The landings didn’t line up with the building. A window that should have faced the courtyard showed another interior hallway instead. Behind that hallway was another stairwell. Behind that one was another window.

I could see the details clearly: peeling paint, a dead fluorescent bulb, a red fire extinguisher mounted crookedly on the wall. My friend's closed apartment door. 

It wasn’t a reflection.

Going up or down was impossible, so I slammed the hammer into the window. 

The first strike cracked the glass. Through the fractures, I saw two places at once—the hallway and the courtyard outside, layered over each other like badly aligned photographs.

The second strike shattered it.

Cold air rushed in.

The courtyard was there.

My friends climbed through. Sarah’s leg buckled, so we helped her down. I stayed behind for one second, still holding the hammer, watching the stairwell.

That’s when something appeared on the stairs behind us. It had a human shape, but it was too tall. Its arms hung almost to its knees. Its skin was pale and stretched tight, and every time I tried to focus on its face, the details seemed to slide away from me.

I couldn’t tell if it had eyes.

But I knew it was looking at me.

Then it spoke.

It used my voice.

“Wait.”

I swung the hammer.

The head struck its hand. I don’t know if I hurt it, but I know the thing recoiled and the sound it made was my own voice screaming.

I jumped through the window.

My friends caught me. We dragged Sarah away from the building.

The figure appeared at the window.

It didn’t climb out.

It simply leaned down and looked at me. 

We ran.

Outside, the building looked completely normal. The windows were where they should be. The stairwell was where it belonged. There was no sign of the corridor, the impossible landings, or the thing watching us from above.

We took Sarah to a friend’s apartment. She didn’t want to go home alone, and none of us wanted to return to the building that night.

For the next several hours, we wrote down what we remembered separately before comparing our accounts. We did that because we didn’t want our memories blending together. We didn’t want anyone’s explanation contaminating someone else’s.

We agree on the major events.

We don’t agree on every detail.

Sarah’s memories of the corridor are incomplete. She remembers the footsteps. She remembers the shoes. She remembers something following her.

She says she never saw its face.

She says she heard it breathing behind her.

We still don’t know what the shoes were doing there. We don’t know who made the footsteps. We don’t know why the barrier kept Sarah inside, or why hitting it allowed us to pull her through.

And we don’t know what was standing on the stairs.

I keep coming back to the fact that it knew my name.

The voice in the corridor said it before I had any idea who was inside. The thing on the stairs used my voice twice.

I don’t know if it was copying me, or if it had been listening to us from the beginning.

I also don’t know whether the closet, the washing machine, and the corridor were connected. We have no evidence that they were. All I have is a series of impossible things that happened in the same building on the same night.

We’re planning to look into the building’s history. We also want to find out where Sarah was before she disappeared. She remembers studying in the library, turning into a back hallway, and then nothing until she woke up on that concrete floor.

I keep telling myself there has to be a physical explanation.

A hidden maintenance space. A gas leak. A trick of the light. A shared panic response.

I know how that sounds, but I need there to be an explanation.

I don’t think we were the first people to find that corridor.

I don’t think the shoes were all there by accident.

And I’m not sure the thing we saw was trapped inside it.

I think it was waiting for someone to open the door.
