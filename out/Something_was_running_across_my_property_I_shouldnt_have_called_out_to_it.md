I've been having trouble sleeping lately and a gaming buddy of mine said this had a place here so I figure why the hell not? 

It's late, I'm only mildly drunk and getting it out of my system might help me settle down.

My nightly strolls are part of the last little freedoms I have in life.

I’m not unlucky, not really. My mother was a good woman, even if I never got to see her much. Dad always let me know she cared, in her way. He’d always tell me how hard she worked for the family.

When I wasn’t in school, I’d be helping around the house. Dad thought of himself as something of a gardener and landscaper, so we were always busy with little tasks. A new berm here to plant some flowers, a shed for chickens, laying rock out for a walkway or just bringing Dad water while he spread gravel out for a driveway for his riding mower to reach the fields easier.

The house really was beautiful by the time he passed. Mom died on some business trip to Colorado, and Dad…

Well, his health declined pretty fast after that. Mom had plenty of money in the bank to retire on, but she always insisted, “The organization needs me.” 

Still, sixty-eight and sixty-nine felt too young. 

Of course they left everything to me, and for a while things were great. I was smart with the money, didn’t buy a new house.  Took the savings I’d built from work along with some of the insurance money and went out on a couple of trips to try and get over things. Nothing huge, just to see some sights.

It wasn’t the travel that got me, just good ol’-fashioned drunken stupidity. Went out to a party with a friend of mine, and he insisted he was good to drive.

He was not. 

Still, bum leg and messed up face aside, I don’t think I’m unlucky.

I got a nice two bedroom with a good porch, decent view of a field of wild flowers, grasses and fruit trees. A nice stone walkway around the place for my exercise and just a thirty-minute drive from the nearest grocery store.

I don’t get out much anymore except to tend to Dad’s plants and refill the bird feeders.  I’m pretty content to just play some games with a few buddies and relax with a glass of whiskey.

But one thing I never did give up was the nightly walk-about. Dad always called it “security duty” when really it was just his way of making sure I was nice and tired before bed.

We’d walk around the house, check on the pond and fences towards the edge of the property to make sure there weren’t any deer or wildlife tangled up or bored locals fishing out Dad’s prized catfish.

We didn’t walk the field at night. Too many gopher holes, ticks, snakes and all that. Instead, we’d just walk the steps to the very edge of the yard, right as it started to slope down towards the overgrown remnants of a farmer’s field.

Somewhere in the distance, breaking up the gentle whistle of wind was the obnoxious squeak of a half broken-down windmill on the neighboring lot.

It used to bother me, but now it just reminded me why I liked being out here.

The quiet. 

Even that stupid windmill has just become part of the background. Though I didn’t own the land over the fence, nobody had lived there in ages; the old house that was there had fallen over years ago. My only real neighbor for miles was an old man and his dog who lived a good few miles up the road near that burned out trailer park.

Standing there with the cool wind blowing in my face, the smell of wild flowers mixing with just a whiff of pond water. I took a deep breath and just soaked it in.

It’s almost enough to make somebody forget their troubles for a while.

But somewhere in between the creak of metal and the rustling of grass and tree branches there was something that stood out.

A rapid shifting of weeds and a rhythmic tamp of something hitting damp earth.

My eyes aren’t that sharp at night, but I could swear a good few yards off I could just barely make out a shapeless blob hopping the fence between my property and the abandoned lot.

Whatever- whoever it was, they didn’t seem like they were slowing down much. The moment they cleared the fence, the sound continued and grew louder. They were really booking it, running just outside the edge of the yellow light of the old light post towards the crowded growth of trees that bordered the property.

They’d already made it past the pond by the time I’d thought to do anything.

It wasn’t really my brightest moment but at the time I’d just thought it must’ve been somebody in trouble or some local idiot looking for trouble. So, I clicked on my flashlight, pointed it off in their direction and called out.

It came to a sudden stumbling halt just before reaching the woods.

And just as suddenly as they stopped, they’d whirled around and started running- sprinting- towards me. 

I didn’t own some fancy fifty-thousand-lumen flashlight but it had always done the trick before…

But now, even in that stark white light, the thing's figure never really shook off the shadow- or maybe they were just wearing some all-black getup?

I didn’t stop to find out. 

My leg burned, but there wasn’t any choice. I hobbled myself towards home, eyes locking on the front door and struggling to remember if I’d left the shotgun loaded.

Behind me, not too far off, I could hear the rapid crunch of feet hitting gravel.

I wasn’t going to make it. Every lurching movement towards the porch just hammered that thought home.

When I got a hold of the stairs railing, I started hauling myself desperately towards the door.

The scream I let out when something raked its way across my back isn’t one I’ll soon forget. 

There was a loud “thump”; I could feel something hit the deck, followed by another ripping sound as whatever it was made some kind of grab for my legs.

I spared only a second to look back and kick at the thing- now swiping at my legs from the ground- before throwing open the thankfully unlocked door and slamming it shut behind me.

It was too much. One leg already ruined from the accident and the other now sporting a nice gash in the back that made movement painful.

Not to mention the wet feeling of my shirt clinging to my back around fresh wounds.

My legs just… gave out. I managed to lean up against a nearby wall and allow myself to slide down rather than full on collapse.

Hands still shaking, I pulled my phone from my pockets and started to call emergency services, half convinced I’d mistaken a bear or something for a man.

Then the pounding started. An initial slam that sent the framed picture of Mom falling to the floor, followed by a brief pause.

 Then three rapid, door-shaking knocks…

A low, throaty rumble just before whatever it was started really going at it. Rapid thumps from smaller hits mixed in with larger doorframe-shaking impacts where the thing must’ve been throwing its whole body into it.

And the screaming…

I don’t know how else to describe it. 

Raw, throat-shredding cries that pitched up and down in intensity, broken only when the thing sucked in air or slammed its body against the sturdy slab of wood that separated it from me.

The worst part?

Just before it all ended, I could swear I heard something in that screech. 

“Help me.”

The first gunshot didn’t stop the thing’s efforts. The second slowed it down- and tore through my door, striking the wall somewhere behind me.

And everything went quiet after the third.

I lay there, curled into myself with my arms covering my head. 

I expected more pounding, a gunshot or maybe some kind of scream. But all I got was the steady crunch of feet on gravel and the creak of wooden steps as somebody approached my door.

Then a soft scrape followed by a series of small dull thuds and the sound of something being dragged across gravel.

As quiet as I could, I pulled myself up off the ground, using the wall to steady myself. 

Somewhere off to my right- towards the driveway that leads off the property- I heard a loud thunk, like somebody slamming a car door…

Then the hum of an engine coming to life.

I didn’t really think about it. I just opened the door and stepped out, not yet noticing the blood at my feet or the scratches across my door.

All I could pay attention to was the red glow of taillights as a white pickup slowly rolled its way off my land and onto the road.

A muffled “Hello?” came from my hand. I’d forgotten the phone. Apparently the call had gone through at some point during the whole exchange and I’d just never let go of the thing.

It took the cops a while to get there. One of the downsides of country living, I guess. They’d only heard some of the tail end, and apparently I’d covered the phone since all they could make out was some “growling and gunshots.” They were already taking photos and scrounging around for shell casings when the ambulance loaded me up.

Thankfully the wounds weren’t too deep. A few more ugly scars and a little extra trouble walking.

I tell local people it was an animal. Some desperate bear or maybe just a large aggressive mutt and an overzealous poacher.

I don’t tell them about the screaming I still hear sometimes. In the background, when the wind blows but the squeak of the windmill

hasn’t quite kicked in yet.

And I never walk outside in the dark.
