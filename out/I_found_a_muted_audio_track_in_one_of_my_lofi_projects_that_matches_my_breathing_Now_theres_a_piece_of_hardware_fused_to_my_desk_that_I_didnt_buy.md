I’m posting this from my phone in the hallway outside my apartment because I can’t get back inside, and my fiancé thinks I’m having a psychotic break. If anyone here knows anything about audio engineering, custom MIDI/USB hardware, or how a Windows PC can execute commands without 3rd party software or a virus, please tell me I’m missing something obvious.

For context, my life is painfully predictable. I’m 22, I'm a manager and work the 12 hour shift at a Goodwill store, and I live alone. Every single day looks the exact same: wake up around 7, brush my teeth, shower, breakfast, clock in, clock out by 9:00pm, and pass out by 9:40pm. Because my fiancé lives a few states away right now, our routine is that I wake up around 3:00am, call her for ten minutes while she’s getting ready for her early shift, and go back to sleep.

On my days off, I make lo-fi beats. Nothing serious—just dusty drum breaks, detuned Rhodes chords, and vinyl crackle in FL Studio to help me decompress.

This started three nights ago, on Tuesday.

I got home at nine, dropped my keys on the kitchen counter, and collapsed on top of my comforter without even taking my jeans off. When my eyes snapped open at 3:00am, the room felt heavy, almost pressurized, like the air right before a summer thunderstorm. I reached over to my nightstand to call my fiancé, thumb hovering over her contact, when my screen lit up on its own. She was already calling me.

"Hey," I rasped, rubbing the sleep out of my eyes. "You beat me to it."

Silence hung on the line for three full seconds, save for a low, rhythmic hum. Then, her voice crackled through the speaker, thin and stretched out like a cassette tape caught in the rollers.

"I'm still watching you sleep," she whispered.

I sat straight up. "What? Baby, are you okay? You sound--"

The line went dead. When I checked my call log, the duration read 00:00. According to my carrier history, no call had ever connected.

Shaken, I walked over to my desk and woke up my PC, half convinced my studio monitors were picking up local radio interference or I was in a stray Discord call. My DAW was still open from Sunday’s session. My six track beat sat frozen on the playlist, except there was a seventh mixer channel routed at the very bottom that I hadn't created. It had no input source, no VST plugins loaded, and the volume fader was pulled all the way down to -INF dB.

The channel was labeled The Mute Track.

I dragged the fader up to 0.0 dB and hit spacebar. There were no drums, no keys, no vinyl hiss. Just the wet, close-mic’d sound of someone breathing heavily right next to a condenser. I zoomed in on the waveform. The peaks and valleys didn't match a stock sample—they matched the exact cadence of my own chest rising and falling as I sat frozen in my chair.

I didn't wait to hear the rest of it. My hand was shaking so bad I could barely click, but I highlighted the audio clip, located the source .wav in my project data folder, and dragged The Mute Track straight into the Recycle Bin. I hit empty.

The Windows deletion progress bar popped up, froze at 99%, and sat there humming. My PC fans spun up to a deafening whine, vibrating through the cheap particleboard of my desk. Then, the window vanished.

Before I could even exhale, the folder refreshed on its own. The .wav file was back. Not only had it restored itself, but the "Date Modified" timestamp had shifted forward by three minutes...to 3:06am, a full minute into the future. The file size had doubled.

That was when I noticed the faint, rhythmic green pulse reflecting off the bottom bezel of my monitor.

I looked down past my audio interface. Sitting flush against the left edge of my desk was a matte-black metal enclosure, roughly the size of a guitar pedal. The finish didn't reflect the light from my screen at all, like it looked covered in Vantablack, just a flat void sitting on the wood. A thick, braided cable ran from the back of the housing directly into a rear USB 3.0 port on my motherboard... a port I know for a fact was empty when I left for work. There was no branding, no screws, no power switch. Just a single recessed LED blinking in exact time with the breathing waveform on my screen.

My phone buzzed on the desk beside it. Incoming call: my fiancé.

I reached for it, my fingers trembling, and stopped short. Through the earcups of my studio headphones resting on the desk, I could hear a faint, tinny voice. I put them on.

Somehow, the call had bridged directly into my PCs audio routing. I heard her voice, warm and sleepy, saying, "Hey, baby. Did you just get off work?"

Then, I heard myself answer.

It wasn't a choppy soundboard stitched together from old voicemails. It was smooth, relaxed, and entirely present. "Yeah, long shift at Goodwill," my voice said through the headphones, matching my exact cadence, my slight rasp, even the way I sigh through my nose when I'm tired. "I'm just getting into bed. Go back to sleep, I'll call you at three."

I yanked the headphones off and grabbed the matte-black box with both hands, pulling upward with everything I had. It didn't budge. There was no adhesive residue, no brackets. The metal felt freezing cold and completely fused into the grain of the desk, as if it had grown up through the wood. I reached behind the tower to rip the braided cable out of the IO shield, and the moment my knuckles brushed the insulation, a sharp, white arc of static threw me backward out of my chair.

I hit the floor hard, gasping for air, staring up at the underside of the desk.

The green LED wasn't blinking anymore. It was solid.

Slowly, I pulled myself up by the armrest and looked at my monitor. FL Studio had closed itself. In its place was Notepad, open and actively typing out lines of text at a dead even rhythm:

03:12 AM - Vocal calibration complete.

03:13 AM - Routine mapped.

03:14 AM - Hardware sync: 100%.

My phone screen lit up one last time, showing a sent text to my fiancé that I hadn't typed. "Heading to bedding. Love you."

Suddenly, the exhaustion from my shift hit me all at once,e xcept it didn't feel like normal fatigue. My limbs felt like wet concrete, pinned down by an invisible weight, and my eyelids dropped against my will. As I slumped forward onto the desk, my cheek pressing against the freezing metal of the box, I heard the deadbolt on my front door turn from the outside. The door opened, and softly clicked shut.

In the dark behind me, heavy footsteps matching my exact stride walked calmly toward the bedroom.

I don’t know how long I was out, or how I managed to drag myself out the front door when I woke up, because the bedroom door was locked from the inside and I could hear someone snoring behind it. I called my fiancé from the stairwell, frantic, begging her to believe me. She just sighed, sounded exhausted, and told me we had already talked at three, that I sounded fine, and that I needed to stop staying up late messing with my music gear.

It’s 8:45pm right now. 

Whoever is inside my apartment is going to clock out of my routine soon, and I don't know what happens when he realizes I'm not in bed.
