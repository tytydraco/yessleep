I wasn’t sure where else to turn, and I’m hoping someone here can help me. I’ve never believed in boogeymen or ghosts. When I was little and my friends whispered at sleepovers about monsters hiding under their mattresses, I nearly laughed in their faces (pretentious, even for a child, I know). The world has always been straight forward to me. Black and white. Crystal clear. But now I don’t know what to think. 

It started when my Uncle Henry died. He was kind of a loony tune, and always had been since I was a child. He used to terrify me, and I would cringe anytime he pressed his wiry moustache against my cheek in greeting. His voice was too loud and booming for my little ears. I liked it better when he didn’t show up to family functions. 

As I got a little older, I was scared of him for a different reason. He lived out in the middle of nowhere in Wyoming, about five hours from our home in the city. Incidentally, his property was pretty close to a ski resort and my dad and I would drive up and stay with him during the peak seasons. I was 16 when something first happened. 

I woke up in the middle of the night, thirsty and headed for the kitchen. All the blinds in the house were open, and the moonlight bounced off the snow, washing the windows in a somber night light. I didn’t expect anyone to be up, so I nearly jumped out of my skin when I noticed Henry, standing at the living room window. 

“Jesus!” I remember yelling. Then I laughed a little, embarrassed by myself. “I didn’t see you there.” 

He didn’t answer. Didn’t turn around. Didn’t move at all. He stood as still as the snow on the ground, the dim light casting him in shadows. 

Henry had a history of episodes. I think they’re called absent seizures, but I don’t know. To be honest, I didn’t care enough as a teenager to ask. But seeing him standing there made me worried that something was really wrong with him. I didn’t want to be responsible for my uncle keeling over. I took a couple steps towards him. 

“Uncle Henry?” 

Still, he didn’t move. Didn’t speak. I started feeling really nervous then, like bugs were crawling all over me. The fact that he wouldn’t answer was egging me on, pushing me forward, like maybe he was whispering and I just needed to be close enough to hear it. So I moved closer. 

I was only a few feet from him when I stopped dead in my tracks. With his body cast in shadows, I had only assumed he was facing the window, looking out. But as my eyes adjusted to the low light, and I came close enough to feel his breath in the space between us, I realized that Henry was facing me. And he was staring right at me. His lips were turned up in a hideous grin, his teeth parted and his tongue moving around in his mouth like a worm. I remember that so vividly. It was the only part of him I had seen move. 

I backed up. I was startled. I didn’t really know this man, even if he was my Uncle, and in that moment he felt evil. 

Then he spoke.   
“Go back to bed,” he told me. “You won't like what you see out there.” 

I did what he said, quicker than I had ever listened to an adult before in my life. In the guest room, I pulled the blanket over my head and shut my eyes tight, wishing for morning to come. And when it did, I told my dad I wanted to go home and explained what happened. He talked to Uncle Henry alone for a while, and I could hear dad yelling, and then we left that same day. Uncle Henry watched us pull off of his property from that same window. 

We kept visiting, and a few other weird things happened, but that night stuck out to me. When the snow begins to fall each year, I get a chill down my spine, like my body remembers that moment better than I do. 

But that was over a decade ago. I grew up, and instead of being terrified of Uncle Henry I just treated him like the outcast he wanted so badly to be. Our entire family lived in the city, and he lived isolated on his property, not checking in with anyone, making my grandmother sick with sadness. 

But two weeks ago, Uncle Henry died. My dad called me while I was at work, and told me to come over as soon as I got off. When I got there, him and mom were sitting on the couch, looking at me like their dog had run away. They told me my Uncle had fallen off his roof. The garbage collectors saw him that morning. He had been there for a while. Days, probably. 

My dad seemed sad, and my mom just seemed uncomfortable. But nobody was heartbroken. I feel guilty saying that, but he was nearly a stranger to us, even if he was my dad’s brother. 

The funeral was closed-casket. I guess his injuries had been substantial enough that the funeral home thought it best we didn’t see him like that. 

A day after the funeral, I got a call. I thought it was spam, and declined it. But the number kept calling me. I finally answered.   
It was Henry’s lawyer. Apparently in his will, he had left his property to me.   
I was shocked to say the least. I wasn’t close with him, but then again, neither was anyone else. But I thought his brother would be the obvious choice. There was no rational explanation I could come up with in my mind for why he had chosen me. Maybe he felt guilty for being a creep. Maybe he liked me more than he let on. I honestly have no idea. But the property was mine. 

I had no idea what to do with it, but it was my responsibility. So I packed up my dog, Ollie, a handle of jack, and enough clothes for the weekend and drove to his place.   
The drive was nice. I had never made it by myself, and I had never driven up there in the summertime, either. The typical quilts of powder I had grown so familiar with were nowhere to be seen, replaced by lush rolling hills and jagged prickly pine trees. 

I got there in the afternoon, and let Ollie jump out of the car. Being a city dog, I knew he would enjoy the free reign, the open space. Bounding across the lawn, he looked all too similar to his wolf ancestors, so wild and happy. I unloaded the car while he played. 

Stepping into that house felt like opening a tomb. It was obvious Henry hadn’t had any visitors in years. He let the place go to hell. Trash littered the floor, and piles of books towered above the pit of garbage that was the living room. I couldn’t help but be a little resentful at the sight. Part of me wondered if he left the house to me knowing I would have to clean his mess up. 

I set my stuff down in the guest room, which was also crowded with junk. Then I made my way back out to call Ollie in so I could get started on cleaning, and get the hell out of there as soon as possible.

Stepping out onto the lawn, I couldn’t see him. The way the property is set up, there’s a huge lawn that encircles the house on all sides, I guess like 2 acres in each direction, and then there's forest beyond that on every side but the road. Ollie must've run pretty far, because he wasn’t on the lawn. 

That scared the hell out of me. In the city I walk him on a leash, so I thought maybe he got big and bold and decided to run off. I started running across the lawn, shouting his name. 

Out of the corner of my eye, I saw a flash of color contrasting the green. I pivoted and ran straight for it.   
I ran into the trees, out of breath and heart pounding, still screaming for that damn dog. I saw him again, and I kept running. This time he was staying still, his head bowed down to the ground.   
Before he could bolt again, I lunged for him and grabbed his collar, thanking my lucky stars he found something good enough to stop and sniff.   
“Don’t scare me like that, dude,” I told him.   
I tried to drag him away, but he wouldn’t come. His nose was pressed to the ground, still sniffing hard. 

And that’s when I saw it. 

I could hardly understand what I was seeing. It had the consistency of mushrooms, and I thought of those fairy circles you see growing. But the mushrooms weren’t in a circle. They were perfectly in the shape of a human skeleton, halfway in the ground like a person was being born right there in the dirt, being shaped from the earth. 

I could see the ribcage. I could see the skull and the hollow holes where the eyes might be. And I could see its teeth. The bones were white and puffy like clouds. There was a stench too, like something rotten. 

Part of me wanted to reach out and touch it. The other part of me wanted to vomit. I wondered for a moment if it was a real skeleton, if someone had died here on my uncle’s land, and if he was responsible for it. But I knew that it wasn’t truly bone. It couldn’t be a person. 

As I stared at it, something happened with the teeth. I squinted hard, got a little closer, trying to understand what I was seeing. And to my horror, I processed what was happening. 

The teeth were shifting. The ones on the ends, the molars, were moving upwards, stretching  and twisting out in the shape of a crescent. The thing was smiling at me.   
And as I watched it, a dark earthy thing that looked all too much like a tongue slithered out between the open teeth, flicking and wiggling in my direction. 

Before I knew it I was running. I was running hard and screaming at Ollie to follow me, and thank god he did, because I don’t know if I could’ve gone back for him. We made it to the house and I slammed the door behind us, locking it tight. 

I tried to come up with an explanation in my mind for what I had seen. Like I said, I don’t believe in the supernatural. I don’t believe in ghosts. But what I saw in the trees was not human. It wasn’t natural. I saw it move. I know I did. 

I’m throwing this out here in the hopes someone will know what I saw, or have some kind of explanation. I feel like an idiot typing all this out, but I can’t lie, I’m also terrified just thinking about it. It’s gonna take 5 or 6 drinks tonight for me to calm down enough to sleep. I keep thinking about what my Uncle said, about how I wouldn’t like what I saw outside. I wonder if he was talking about something to do with this. What I know for sure is, I’ll be sleeping with the blinds closed as long as I’m here. And I’ll be staying inside, clearing this junk as quick as I can.

I’m hopeful someone here can help me, and in the meantime, I’ll post any updates if something else weird happens.
