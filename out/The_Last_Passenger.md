I drive a taxi at night.

I've been doing it for almost six years, so I've learned not to be surprised by strange passengers.

Drunk people.

People fighting on the phone.

People who refuse to pay.

People who give you an address that doesn't exist.

But one passenger still bothers me.

I picked her up at 2:14 AM on a Tuesday.

She was standing alone beside an empty road.

No streetlights.

No houses nearby.

Just an old bus stop.

She looked to be in her twenties, wearing a black coat and holding a small handbag.

When she opened the back door, she didn't say hello.

She simply gave me an address.

"Take me to 17 Willow Street."

I started driving.

After about five minutes, I noticed something strange.

She hadn't made a single sound.

No phone.

No movement.

Nothing.

I looked at her through the rear-view mirror.

She was staring out of the window.

"Long night?" I asked.

She slowly looked at me.

"Very."

Then she smiled.

I don't know why, but that smile made me uncomfortable.

A few minutes later, she asked:

"Do you ever drive past places you shouldn't?"

I laughed.

"What do you mean?"

She looked at the road ahead.

"Sometimes drivers pick up people who aren't supposed to be there."

I didn't know what to say.

So I changed the subject.

"Almost there."

She nodded.

Then she asked:

"Do you know what happened at Willow Street?"

"No."

She looked at me again.

"Good."

That was the last thing she said.

We arrived at 17 Willow Street at 2:31 AM.

The house looked abandoned.

Broken windows.

Overgrown garden.

No lights.

She opened the door and got out.

I expected her to walk toward the house.

Instead, she stood beside the car.

"How much?"

I told her the fare.

She handed me a banknote.

I looked at it.

It was an old note.

Very old.

I told her I couldn't accept it.

She stared at me.

"Keep it."

Then she walked toward the house.

I watched her disappear through the front gate.

Something felt wrong.

I checked the banknote again.

There was a date printed on it.

1998.

I thought it was some kind of joke.

I threw it into the glove compartment and drove away.

The next morning, I showed it to my friend.

He looked at the note and laughed.

"Where did you get this?"

I told him about the passenger.

He stopped laughing.

"You picked her up where?"

I gave him the location.

He searched something on his phone.

Then he showed me a news article.

The headline read:

"Young Woman Killed in Hit-and-Run Accident Near Willow Street."

The date was October 17, 1998.

I stared at the photograph.

It was her.

Same face.

Same black coat.

Same handbag.

I didn't sleep that night.

I told myself there had to be another explanation.

Maybe someone looked exactly like her.

Maybe the article was wrong.

Maybe I was imagining things.

But three nights later, at exactly 2:14 AM, my taxi app showed a new passenger request.

Pickup location:

The same empty road.

I almost cancelled it.

Instead, I accepted.

I don't know why.

When I arrived, she was already standing there.

Same black coat.

Same handbag.

She opened the back door.

"17 Willow Street," she said.

My hands started shaking.

I looked at her through the mirror.

"You're the woman I drove last Tuesday."

She smiled.

"I know."

I couldn't move.

Then she said something that made my blood run cold.

"You didn't take me home last time."

I swallowed.

"Yes, I did."

She shook her head.

"No."

She pointed toward the glove compartment.

"Check."

I opened it.

The old banknote was still there.

But there was something underneath it.

A photograph.

I picked it up.

It was a photograph of my taxi.

Taken from outside.

In the photograph, I was sitting in the driver's seat.

The woman was sitting behind me.

And standing beside the taxi…

was another person.

Me.

Except the second me was wearing the same black coat as the woman.

I dropped the photograph.

When I looked into the rear-view mirror again, the back seat was empty.

I immediately locked the doors.

Then my phone rang.

It was my brother.

I answered.

His voice was shaking.

"Where are you?"

I told him.

There was silence.

Then he said:

"Come home immediately."

"Why?"

He took a breath.

"Because the police just called."

My heart started pounding.

"What happened?"

"They found your taxi."

I looked at the dashboard.

My hands were still on the steering wheel.

"What do you mean? I'm sitting in it."

My brother whispered:

"No."

Then he said:

"Your taxi was found abandoned at 17 Willow Street."

I couldn't breathe.

He continued.

"They found you inside."

I stared at the rear-view mirror.

The woman was sitting behind me again.

She leaned forward until her face was beside mine.

And whispered:

"See?"

A pause.

"You did come home."

Then everything went black.

I woke up in a hospital three days later.

The police told me I had been found unconscious inside my taxi.

They asked me what happened.

I told them everything.

They didn't believe me.

Before leaving, one officer placed something on the table.

An old banknote.

Dated 1998.

He said:

"We found this in your hand."

I asked him where my taxi was.

He looked confused.

"Your taxi?"

"Yes."

He shook his head.

"You don't own a taxi."

I laughed.

"Of course I do."

He looked at the other officer.

Then back at me.

"You've been in this hospital for three weeks."

I stopped breathing.

He continued:

"You were hit by a taxi three weeks ago."

"And the driver?"

The officer didn't answer.

He just placed a photograph on the table.

It showed the accident scene.

A woman in a black coat standing beside the road.

And behind her…

a taxi driver staring directly at the camera.

It was me.
