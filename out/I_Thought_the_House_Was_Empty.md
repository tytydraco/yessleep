I inherited a house from my grandmother. On the very first day, I threw a party for all my friends. We drank, listened to music, and enjoyed each other’s company.

When the last guest left, I took a bath and went straight to bed. I had the feeling I could hear footsteps, as if someone was walking up and down the stairs. I assumed I was just imagining it.

I couldn’t fall asleep at all, and when I finally did, I was woken by a terrible crash and the sound of breaking glass. I didn’t dare leave my room. There must be a burglar, I thought.

I lay completely still for several hours, and when the first rays of morning light reached my room, I went downstairs to see what had happened. I grabbed the candlestick my grandmother had kept in her bedroom for her entire life.

I walked down the stairs, but there was no sign that anyone had been inside. I entered the living room and saw that the door was broken.

Damn. I had forgotten to close the window. It must have been windy outside, which caused the door to slam shut and the glass to break. Although I didn’t remember there being any strong wind that night, I had been pretty drunk, so I might not have remembered it correctly.

The next day, more and more strange things started happening. I was watching TV in the living room when I heard a creaking sound. I turned around and saw my grandmother’s armchair slowly rocking back and forth on its own. I looked toward the window.

It was closed.

I turned on all the lights in the house and quickly went back to the bedroom. There were those footsteps on the stairs again. The chandelier above me started flickering and eventually went out completely. My heart was pounding, and I gripped the blanket so tightly that my knuckles turned white. I squeezed my eyes shut and tried to fall asleep.

Eventually, I did.

In the morning, when I left the bedroom, there was no sign that anyone had been there.

I went to work that morning. I was glad I had at least managed to get some sleep, and I tried to look as normal as possible. I work at a preschool, and I didn’t want the children to notice anything was wrong.

While the children were playing, my phone rang. It was my friend Nela. I told her what had been happening and that I felt like I was going crazy. She tried to calm me down, saying that old houses sometimes made strange noises, and she tried to come up with a logical explanation for everything.

She actually managed to convince me.

What she was saying made sense.

I ended the call by saying, “You’re right. There’s nothing in my house.”

I noticed Clara standing in front of me.

She was the youngest girl in our class. She was holding a doll and staring directly at me with her big blue eyes.

“Do you need something, sweetheart?”

She looked at me and said, “It’s not true that there’s nothing in your house, Abigail.”

She shook her head as she spoke, making her blonde ponytail sway from side to side.

I blinked in surprise.

“What did you say, Clara?”

She smiled and said, “There’s a man at your house. He doesn’t like noise. When you make noise, he gets angry.”

Everything went dark for a moment, and I had to grab onto the table. My heart started pounding again, and the fear came rushing back.

I wanted to ask her how she knew that, but her mother came to pick her up and took her home.

I immediately started looking for somewhere else to live. That same day, I arranged a viewing of an apartment and scheduled my move for two days later. I also took a few days off work.

The house was quiet that evening, but I wasn’t going to risk staying there anymore.

Moving day finally came.

As I stood there holding my suitcase, a sudden wave of anger came over me. Anger that I had to leave after being so excited about finally having this place.

I started rummaging through my tool bag, grabbed a hammer, and smashed that damn armchair into pieces.

Then I turned up the music and started hitting anything I could hit.

When I finally calmed down, I went upstairs one last time to make sure all the windows were closed.

Everything was fine.

I was walking back down the stairs when I suddenly felt a hard impact against my back.

There was no way I could keep my balance, and I fell.

I lay still on the floor for a moment, slowly checking myself to make sure nothing was broken. It seemed like I was fine, but when I touched my forehead, I felt a bump that was already starting to hurt more and more.

I got back up, grabbed my suitcase, and got the hell out of there.

I didn’t calm down until I opened the door to my new apartment.

No more house. Just an apartment.

I’d be fine here.

I slept through the entire day and went to work the next morning. I was getting some crayons ready for the children when Clara came over to me.

“Is your new apartment nice?”

The crayons fell from my hands.

“Clara, how do you know about my new apartment?”

She came closer and told me it was a secret, but that she would tell me if I promised to keep it to myself.

“Of course. I promise,” I said far too quickly.

She motioned for me to come closer with her little hand.

I bent down until our faces were almost touching. She leaned toward my ear and whispered,

“He told me about it. He’s sitting in the corner right now, and he’s very angry.”

I swallowed.

“Why is he angry, Clara?”

She whispered,

“Because he doesn’t have his favorite chair.”
