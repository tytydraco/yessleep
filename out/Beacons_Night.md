The old, abandoned clock factory had sat dead across the dense patch of woods for thirty years, its shattered glass windows staring like empty eye sockets at my quiet suburban street. It was 3:11 AM when I first noticed the glow. I was sitting at my high kitchen table, trying to clear my mind, when a stark beam of crimson light cut through the dark woods, slicing directly through my window and painting the opposite wall a blood-red hue. I froze. The factory had no electricity, and the thick treeline usually blocked any distant headlights. This light was intense, completely stationary, and originating from the top floor of the decaying brick structure.  
I walked to the window, pulling the blinds back. The red beam was focused entirely on my house, sharp and unblinking.  
I tried to dismiss it as teenagers messing around with a high-powered laser, but a cold knot of paranoia began to twist in my stomach. I grabbed a pair of old hunting binoculars from the drawer and focused them on the factory's top window. Through the dusty lenses, the red light was blinding, but as I adjusted the focus, my breath hitched. The light wasn't coming from a flashlight or a laser pointer. It was radiating from a massive, antique glass bulb mounted on a heavy metal tripod. And standing right next to it, completely still, was the silhouette of a tall figure. The figure wasn't looking around; its head was turned directly toward my house.  
I quickly dropped the binoculars and stepped back into the shadows of my kitchen. My heart hammered against my ribs.  
Ten minutes passed in absolute silence. I crept back to the edge of the window, my eyes wide. The red beam hadn't wavered an inch. It was a perfect line of light connecting the empty factory to my exact location. Suddenly, the red beam flickered. It went dark for a millisecond, then snapped back on. *Dash. Dot. Dash.* It was blinking in a rhythmic, deliberate pattern. Someone was signaling. I didn't know Morse code, but the repetitive nature of the flashes felt deeply threatening. I felt entirely exposed, a sitting duck in a brightly targeted house. I decided I couldn't just sit there in fear. I needed to document it, to get proof for the police.  
I hurried upstairs to my bedroom to grab my camera.  
The bedroom window also faced the woods. As I stepped into the room, I noticed the red beam was gone from my wall. I walked up to the glass and looked out across the street. The factory was completely dark. The crimson light had been shut off. I let out a long, shuddering breath, my muscles instantly untangling from the tension. I laughed weakly at myself, realizing how worked up my imagination had gotten over some local kids pulling a prank. They had probably seen me looking and decided the fun was over. I tossed my camera onto the bed and turned to head downstairs to lock up for the night.  
I reached for my bedroom doorknob, my mind already drifting toward getting some actual sleep.  
Then, a sudden, blinding flash of crimson light exploded through my bedroom window, instantly illuminating the entire room in a horrific red glow. I gasped, spinning around to face the glass. The factory across the woods was still completely dark. There was no light coming from the brick building at all. I stared, paralyzed, as my eyes traced the brilliant red beam backwards. The light wasn't shining *into* my house from across the street. The beam was blasting *out* of my own open closet door, reflecting perfectly off my window pane, and shooting straight across the woods to illuminate the empty factory.  
From the pitch blackness inside my closet, a heavy, mechanical *click* echoed, and the tripod shifted forward into the red light.
