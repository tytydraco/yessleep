The back of my neck tingled. My shoulders tensed. I looked around slowly. My bed, my shelves, everything was as it should be. Still, I grabbed the mini sword that served as my parcel opener. 

A cold putrid breath on my cheek. I screamed, slashed at the air. 

Nothing there. Nothing I could see.

I held my breath. My brother called out, asking if I was okay. 

I stayed frozen, every nerve in my body ready to pounce, to run, to fight. 

A wet, heavy rattling sound snaked from under the bed. 

I screamed again. I leapt up, eyes darting from the bed to the door.

My brother came bursting into the room. 

“What’s going on?” he stared at me, eyes wide with alarm. 

“There’s…there’s something here…” I whispered, tears filling my eyes. I pointed underneath my bed. 

“Whoa. Put that down, I’ll handle it,” he said, gingerly prying the letter opener from my grasp. 

“You don’t understand, it’s…I don’t know what it is.”

He laughed. “Come on, it’s just a rat. Or a cockroach. I’ll get it.” 


He looked under the bed, as I leapt backward. 

“Nothing here,” he said, turning, grinning. 

“It’s here, I can feel it,” I grabbed his arm, pulled him away from the bed. 

“Relax, there’s nothing. See?”

He gestured to the empty space beneath the bed. 

Behind him, one of my plush toys, my favourite one, a fluffy dog, turned its head. I screamed again, ducked behind my brother. 

“It moved!”

“What? What moved?”
“Fuffles!”

My brother turned, looked at Fuffles and laughed uneasily. “Really? Even Fuffles is freaking you out now?” 

There was a tightness in his tone that belied his words. 

“You sense it too, right?” I asked urgently. 

“No…I…” his eyes darted around the room. 

There it was, the wet, rattling sound again. “Did you hear that?” I shrilled. 

“Go get dad.” His tone was quiet, steely. 

I ran. As I thudded up the stairs, I heard my brother yell. A loud slam. I cried out, screamed for my father. 

I flung his door open, not bothering to knock. 

“Dad help!” 

My father was at his desk. He turned in his chair, looked at me calmly. 

“Dad, something’s in my room! Leo is in trouble!”

“Is this another prank?” My father asked in an even tone. 

“No! I swear! Something is there! I don’t know…Leo is…dad help!”

My father remained seated. He looked at me with a tinge of amusement. 

“Dad? Please. I’m not joking. Something’s wrong. Something’s here.”

“It’s all fine,” was all he said. 

I ran up to him, grabbed him by the shoulders. “Dad, listen to me! I’m not joking, I’m…”

He blinked sideways. 

Beneath his lids, another pair of lids had swept shut and open, in a horizontal movement. 

I let go of his shoulders. My father’s lips parted, and pulled wide open. I could see all his teeth. “Calm down, it’s all fine.”

I screamed, felt my throat tear from the strain. I sprinted from the room. I ran back down, saw that my room was empty. My brother was gone. I froze for a split second, then ran out the front door. 

I kept running for a street or two. I slowed, looking back to make sure no one had followed. I had stupidly left my phone behind. 

I half ran, half jogged towards the 24/7 convenience store at the end of the street. 

A man stood before the entrance, holding the leash of his poodle. I slowed, got closer. The poodle turned to look at me. It tilted its head, bared its teeth. The light of the convenience store lit his furry face. 

The poodle’s eyes glowed in the light. He opened his mouth, and let out wet, phlegm-filled rasps. The same rancid stench I had inhaled in my bedroom reached my nose. I looked at the owner, terror warping my features. He frowned at me, irritated, confused.  

I backed away, legs shaking. I turned, ran again. 

I was running out of breath when I tripped over something. I fell, cut my knees and hands. I looked around, trembling, nausea flooding my stomach and throat. I was in an alley with a single street lamp. 

I leaned against the wall, gulping in air. My chest was tight, heart thudding. I clasped my shaking hands together and made myself take a deep breath. Another. 

A chuckle echoed around me. The same stench enveloped me.

“No!” I screamed in frustration and terror. “Stop it! Stop!”

The juddering sound of laboured breathing slithered around my ankles. I backed away, climbed up the dumpster at the end of the alley. “Fuck off!” I shrieked, my voice hoarse and torn. 

I was shaking, tears falling free. My heart thundered, blending into the roaring in my ears. 

“Are you okay? Miss?” Someone was waving in my face. 

My vision came back into focus. It was a young man. He looked concerned, almost fearful. 

“Help me,” I pleaded in a small, broken voice. 

“Okay, I’ve got you, calm down. I’m going to get help.” He pulled out his phone. 

“Everything okay here?” Another voice chimed in. Another woman had come running down the alley towards us. 

“She’s…she needs help. I’m calling the police,” the young man called out to the woman. “Help her,” he gestured to me. “I think she’s having a panic attack.”

The woman ran up to me. 

“Something’s here,” I said.

She placed a cold hand on my shoulder. “It’s all fine,” she said, and her face split apart in a wide grin. Her yellow teeth glinted in the soft glow of the lamppost. 

“No!” I cried out, flattening myself against the wall. “Watch out, she’s-” The woman reached out, grasped my neck. 

“What’re you doing?” The young man shouted. The woman kept grinning at me. The man grabbed her shoulder. She held out a hand, and the young man stopped. He fell still, and his face blanked. Then he smiled. He turned slowly and walked away. The woman turned back to me. I screamed, flailed with all I had. I kicked at her, and toppled off the dumpster. The rough ground slammed against my head. 

I came to in pitch darkness. Someone was wrapped tightly around me. My shoulders and chest were encircled in a firm embrace. 

A flicker of hope stirred my heart. Was I safe? Was I somewhere safe, with someone safe? Was it my father, was it my brother? 

Then cold fear pierced my chest. 

The neck was too long. The chin, sharp, pointed, was tucked beneath my right shoulder blade. The neck snaked across mine. The shoulders and arms were wrapped tight around my chest. Every part of this thing was pointed, sharp, angular. And it was damp and cold.  

Panic and terror flooded in, ballooning up in my pounding head. Then subsided. I was too tired. Too drained. I couldn’t tolerate any more fear. I had this unshakeable sense that my body would fall apart if I let the fear in. 

I gave up. I stopped struggling, stopped fighting. I lay in that thing’s suffocating embrace, and waited, limp. I would accept whatever came. I didn’t care anymore. 

The thing wrapped around me seemed to melt right into my skin. Into me. But instead of feeling it fill me within, I felt an empty, sucking sensation, like my whole body was being hollowed out. I shut my eyes.

I woke up again. I was in bed. I sat up slowly, sluggishly. I felt empty, numb. 

I went to Leo’s room. It was empty. 

I went to my father’s room. I wasn’t afraid anymore, not of what I would find. 

He seemed to be my dad again. 

He greeted me. He had no memory of the previous night. 

Leo was at a friend’s place, he said. 

I walked back to my room. I felt untethered, floating in my own body. 

I stared in the mirror. There were the scrapes on my knees from the night before. I looked at my hands. The cuts had clotted over. 

I looked back at my face in the mirror and blinked. I thought I saw something beneath my eyelid. With each blink, I caught a flash of it. I couldn’t be sure of what I was seeing. But somehow, I didn’t really care. 

I thought about Leo. If I should check in on him, make sure he’s okay. 

I thought about going to the police. But what would I say?

I felt nothing. I still feel nothing. No fear. No feelings. Hollow. A gnawing emptiness.

What should I do? Do any of you know what that thing could have been? Or was it all in my head?  

I still smell the stench. But I think it’s coming from me.
