The open box in the middle of the yard in broad daylight wasn't an invitation. It was a summons.

​The cold that invaded the house froze the water in the pipes within minutes. I heard the slow, heavy footsteps crossing the porch. The front door wasn't broken down; it simply swung open, as if the wood itself refused to act as a barrier against them.

​The three men in gray entered my living room. For the first time, they were inside my house.

​The leader, with his shifting head of gray smoke, stepped forward. The other two held cast-iron staves, engraved with the same runes I had seen around the keyhole. I stood frozen beside the kitchen table, my freshly cut arm still bleeding beneath the cloth, the knife trembling in my hand.

​"You used the Bleeding before the appointed time," the smoke's voice echoed directly inside my mind. It wasn't an audible sound, but a forced thought that made my teeth vibrate. "You burned the prisoner with the weapon of the bloodline."

​"It was trying to use my voice!" I screamed, clinging to whatever little courage I had left. "It was projecting my family's voices into my yard! What do you want from me? I paid the tithe last month!"

​The leader tilted his amorphous head. The gray smoke stirred, briefly revealing the faint contour of an ancient, withered human face before dissolving once more.

​"The prisoner is not an external entity, João," the mental voice echoed, cold as ice. "The Mouth of the Earth is not a prison for a demon. It is a spine. And what lies beneath is the residual shell of your own bloodline."

​My legs almost gave out. "What?"

​"Every generation of your family that shed blood on this land left a piece of their soul behind to contain the fissure. The thing downstairs speaks with your grandfather's voice, your mother's, and your own because it IS made of you. The 'monster' is the accumulation of pain and vitality your family offered over a century. If you destroy it with pure blood, you destroy the barrier itself. You almost ruptured the seal forever."

​Everything clicked into place in the most terrifying way. The blood tithe wasn't an offering to feed a monster... it was the fuel needed to maintain the barrier my own family had become. The gray patches on my neck weren't a disease; they were the smoke of the Order spreading through my body, preparing me for my final fate.

​"Your grandfather understood when he grew too old," the leader continued. "There is no escape. There is no cure.

The generational pact only ends when the bloodline dies out, or when the fissure is sealed entirely."

​"And how do you seal that fissure permanently?" I asked, tears of despair finally streaming down my face.

​"Not with monthly tithes. But with the full harvest. A final sacrifice of pure blood all at once. The entire life of the 

last heir to close the Mouth forever."

​They didn't come to kill me today. They came to give me a choice.

​I can continue the cycle. I can use my grandfather's equipment, gradually drain my own blood into IV bags every month, mutilate myself, lose my mind as the voices of my ancestors call to me from the basement, and pass this curse on to a future child.

​Or I can end it.

​The men in gray left the house and walked back toward the road, leaving the wooden box open in the yard. They gave me until next month to decide.

​This is my last post here. If I keep writing, I know the voices will start using your words against me. I can already hear the whispers coming from the hatch even during the day, calling me by name, telling me that the darkness down there is just the rest of my family waiting for me.

​I've made my decision. I won't let this curse pass to anyone else. If my blood built this prison, it will be my blood that locks it forever.

​I am going down to the basement now. This time, without the knife to defend myself. I'm bringing only the transfusion bags and my grandfather's journal, which I will burn down there alongside the last drop of life I have left.

​If you ever happen to drive down a dirt road in the countryside and see a farm with a completely dead, gray yard surrounded by thick fog... don't stop. Don't help anyone crying at the gate. And above all, if you hear your own voice calling out from underground, just run.

​The Order of the Pure Harvest thanks you for the cleanup.
