I can’t believe I’m making this post. When I made my [last post](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1xmqy39/update_my_daughter_was_missing_for_9_days_well_be/), I thought things were looking up; I thought she had come back so we could work things out.

After the police came, we spent the night together watching old cartoon movies and she slept in my bed. It felt surreal to have my daughter still in my arms when I woke up. I truly didn’t think it would happen ever again after she went missing.

“Can we go for a walk?” she asked me after we ate breakfast. And so, we went.

It was the first time since that fateful night that I could go outside and actually take in how beautiful this town could be. The reds, oranges and surviving greens of autumn painted an idyllic picture of this boring neighbourhood. When I was younger, these endless rows of chipped white picket fences felt like the rusty jaws of a bear trap. Now, my nostalgia and her existence had softened its edges into a home.

One day, maybe my daughter would see it the same way.

Most likely she wouldn’t, but it didn’t really matter. As long as she found her happiness.

“It’s natural, right?” she uttered, unprompted.

“What is, sweetie?”

“Some things have to die so others may live. Humans… they just get angry at the cycle of life.”

“I guess…” I replied cautiously. “Why are you thinking about this?”

“Oh, you know…” she replied absent-mindedly, “the trees.”

From then on, I tried to make conversation, but she would only give me one-word answers. Her attention seemed to be completely pulled in by the trees. I quickly gave up and surrendered to silence, but not before I offered her my hand. The warmth of her palm in mine shrouded me from the cold wind.

 

That night, as I was sitting in the living room, trying to read but mostly listening to the sounds of normal life coming from her room, she came up to me and planted herself right in front of me.

“Mom,” she declared formally.

“Yes?” I replied, putting down my book after I had pretended to finish my passage.

“Would you mind if I went on a date tomorrow?”

I knew this would be coming up one day, of course. But I never would have expected it to happen so quickly after the incident. Every cell in my brain screamed at me that it would be a bad idea to let her go so soon. She was obviously still confused and hurting. I needed to tell her to stay home.

“A date? With whom?” I asked instead.

She blushed. “Jake.”

Saying I’ve known Jake for most of his life wouldn’t make him very special, because this town is so small I know just about every kid my daughter’s age. But what I could say is that I’ve known Jake had a crush on my daughter for most of his life. They’ve been good friends since their first play date, but it’s obvious he’s always pined for her. So obvious I always thought my daughter simply wasn’t interested, but it seems she really hadn’t noticed until now.

“I guess it’s OK if it’s with Jake. But you’re keeping your phone on and you’re home before midnight.”

“Wouldn’t want the car to turn into a pumpkin,” she replied. The smirk that spread itself across her lips brought me back to a better time. I thought maybe I had made the right choice. “Thank you so much, Mom. I love you.”

 

The next day, after I couldn’t convince her that Jake would probably prefer that she keep things simple, we went shopping together. She stressed she needed a new dress, fake nails and fancier makeup. Nothing but the best for a boy who had probably been lost in her vibrant green eyes since the first time their gazes had met.

She convinced me to buy her a beautiful copper floral dress. It seemed a bit old school to me, but I’ve long since learned that being a mom means that what's hip is always the opposite of what you think it is.

The sight of her all dressed up for her first date made me tear up. It wasn’t helping at all that I finally noticed how well her dad’s old leather jacket now fit her. She used to lose herself in that thing, determined to wear it anyway. Maybe he had always been there to protect her, even when I couldn’t.

“I think it’s going to be OK, mom,” she said as she closed her arms around me.

I spent the whole night trying to read, but really checking the tracking app I had her install on her phone every five minutes. It seemed they went for a classic dinner and a movie combo. The dot stayed fixed at the movie theatre on the edge of town for an hour, then two, then three. It stayed there for five hours until it finally moved again, quickly making its way back to me.

At half past midnight, the front door slammed shut.

“I don’t love him,” it murmured.

I turned around to see it dead still in the entrance, bathed in shadows my reading lamp was too dim and too distant to dispel.

It stood cloaked in the reaper’s mantle, with only the claws protruding from its tense fingers distinguishable through the darkness.

“I can’t love him I won’t love him I don’t love him I can’t love him I won’t love him I don’t love him,” it repeated like a mantra.

“I JUST CAN’T!” it roared.

“What happened?” I asked tentatively as I got up.

“He kissed me and then I knew I couldn’t do it because if I did it you would be disappointed with me and if you're disappointed with me I can't live with myself and if I can’t live with myself what’s the point of doing it?”

“You shouldn’t do anything you’re not ready for, but it would also be OK if you did. I promise I wouldn’t judge you,” I explained, thinking I had gotten the gist of what she meant.

“You wouldn’t judge me?” it echoed as the white of its eyes emerged from its silhouette, their intensity locking me in place. “Why do you always have to lie to me why can’t you understand that it makes it harder to trust you when you spend every waking moment telling me that you would love me no matter what I was or what I did but I know you’re lying because you’ve taught me that there are some things you can’t do because they make you a bad person if to become a person I have to do bad things does that mean I was born a bad thing?”

“That does it,” I exclaimed, putting my foot down figuratively and literally as I took another step forward. “You need help—real help. I know you don’t want to talk to me about your problems and I get it. But you clearly need to talk to someone. I thought maybe Jake could be that someone, but I was wrong. What do I have to do for you to be willing to talk to a professional? I know I can make you go, but what do I have to do to make sure you’ll actually talk?”

“You don’t understand…” it moaned, my daughter’s features drawing themselves on the shape as I got closer and closer. The mascara streaking down her cheeks did little to spoil her natural beauty.

“I can’t understand if you don’t let me in, sweetie,” I explained as I opened my arms.

“You can’t understand,” she emphasized before sliding past me and running off to lock herself in her room once more.

I knew we couldn’t keep doing this song and dance anymore. And I know I should have called the police then and there. Call me stupid, but I thought maybe we could figure something out the next morning, after she had calmed down a bit.

 

When I woke up this morning, she was gone. All that was left of her were the words she had painted with lipstick on her full-length mirror:

*I was born a bit*  
*You made me true*  
*I'm sorry, I quit*  
*She won't get her due*

About ten days ago was the first time I'd ever called Jake directly instead of his mother and it felt just as weird the second time.

“Miss B., what’s up?” he answered with audible tension in his throat.

“You were with my daughter last night, weren’t you?” I asked.

“I don’t know what she told you,” he babbled. “I swear I wasn’t trying anything weird!”

“She’s gone. Again.”

“No, no, no. WHAT!? I swear I thought she was going home. I swear!” he prattled on.

“She came home,” I replied, trying to strike a balance between compassion and authority in my voice, “but I need to know exactly what happened yesterday. From the first moment you saw her.”

“Miss B., whatever’s up I know nothing about it. I swear.”

“I believe you, Jake,” I lied, “but humor me.”

“Look, it got a bit weird at the end, but it was a pretty normal date. We went to dinner on Main Street and then to the movie theater for a movie. After that, we went for a stroll outside of town. Look, I kissed her and I shouldn’t have but…”

“What do you mean a stroll? Where did you take her?”

“I didn’t take her anywhere, I swear, Miss B.,” he pleaded, “After the movie she grabbed me by the hand and just dragged me off. I just went along with it. We made it all the way to the old house and then she kinda just left me in front of it.”

“She left you?” I repeated.

“Yeah, look, she was fiddling with the rusty gate in front of it, saying she needed to show me something and just really rambling a lot. She looked really nervous and, you know, the latch is all janky so she couldn’t get it open. I might have put my hands on hers to try to calm her, and I might have kissed her. BUT THAT’S ALL! She got really weird and kinda told me off as if I wasn’t there and just ran off. She was my ride and all, but I walked home. I texted her to say sorry and she ignored me, which is fair, ‘cause I really shouldn’t have. That’s all I know, Miss B., I swear.”

“Thank you,” my mouth emitted.

“If you need he…”

My finger ended the call.
