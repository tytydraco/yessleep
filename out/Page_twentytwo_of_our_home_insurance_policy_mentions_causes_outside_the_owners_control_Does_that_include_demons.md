Dale,   
  
I’m sorry, yes I did lie to you. I know I shouldn’t have but I have gotten into the habit of lying about the “incident”, as you would call it, because no one believed me when I first told the truth. But you’re right, my insurance claim doesn’t add up. And you’ve been far kinder and more understanding in our conversations than I would have ever expected from a Claims Auditor.  
  
So what follows is the true story. Hard as it might be to believe, please keep all of this in mind when you revisit my claim. I'll also post this somewhere online to keep a public record of it.  
  
As you know, after years of renting we finally bought our own dream house this past October. Four bedrooms, oak floors, a kitchen island with room for seating, everything I was looking for, but it was eight hundred a month over what we’d talked about. Emma stood in the spacious basement, looked me dead in the eyes and said excitedly “I'll be able to film so many more videos here!”

As a content creator, her usual contribution to our finances was between $0 and $100 a month so I was skeptical but she promised to work hard and then asked, "Ben, don't you trust me?" So, that was that.

The realtor held the door and congratulated us a few too many times. While Lorraine was immensely helpful in our house buying journey, I found her to be a bit creepy. She was always smiling a little too widely, bordering on inhuman. But I was told that you meet all kinds when house hunting.

We signed at the kitchen island. There was a stack of paper about two inches thick, and Emma flipped through hers by the tabs. I started on page one, intending to read every word, and she put her hand on my arm, right over a large scar, and pressed until I felt it in my elbow. “Ben, you said you trusted me.” So I signed.

Our Golden Retriever, Buster, seemed to like the place too. He ran upstairs, picked a random room to run circles in, then ran back to me and wagged his tail so fast and hard I could imagine him achieving flight. Then he was off to another room to repeat the cycle. Emma complained about his energy but I didn't mind. Buster is a good boy.

The basement was practically empty with just a water heater in the far right corner and a trash compactor on the right hand side. About a month in I took the paneling off the opposite wall down there because I wanted a workbench. But behind it was a circle of red paint, five feet across, with what looked like runes all around it. I was creeped out and almost put the paneling back up but I made the mistake of showing it to Emma first.

She practically squealed with delight. To her, it was obviously some kind of prank the previous owners left behind but it gave her a new idea. She wanted to use it for the backdrop to her newest series where she would recite true crime stories while doing yoga.

So I left it alone. Ultimately, it wasn't doing any harm and Buster, who came down to the basement often, just wagged his tail when he saw it. I figured if Buster was OK with it, it couldn't be anything bad.

A month later, I came down to the basement to let Emma know that dinner was ready and I found her scraping the circle off with a knife. Apparently, her yoga videos hadn't been doing well so she was pivoting to doing make up tutorials while dishing out the latest Hollywood gossip. She gave me the task of painting the wall vermilion by the end of the week.  
  
That night I got up to get a glass of water. I went downstairs towards the kitchen but I found myself in the basement instead. Not sure of how I got there, I walked up the basement stairs but found myself in an unfamiliar hallway, full of doors. I opened one and found another hallway, also filled with doors.  
  
Suddenly, I was struck with the powerful feeling that something very large and malevolent was right behind me. I ran down the hallway, occasionally running through a random door and down the hallway I found behind it. I ran faster than I ever had because I was convinced that to do so otherwise would result in a very painful death.  
  
And then my alarm woke me. It going off meant I must have had a full eight hours of sleep but I felt exhausted. I went downstairs and made breakfast for Emma and I. While I was stirring the eggs, she told me about a horrible nightmare she had. She described hers and it was the same as mine.

I noticed then that the basement door was open. I felt silly but I felt like I had to check it out. I called for Buster to join me but he wouldn't go down the steps. I listened for a moment for any sounds coming from the basement but it was silent, I decided that it wasn't really all that important and I closed the basement door.

Immediately, there was a massive crash from down the stairs. Emma pushed past me, flung the door open, and flew down towards the noise, worried about her filming equipment. I followed and Buster did too, though I noticed that his tail was stuck between his legs.  
  
We found Emma standing in the center of the basement with her back turned to us. She told us that everything was fine but didn't turn around. I was unnerved but looking around the basement, everything did look fine. I couldn't see what had made that terrible crashing noise.  
  
Still facing away, Emma told me to leave. “I have a new idea for a video and I want to film it right now.” I asked about the noise and she told me that I must have imagined it.   
I wanted to ask her why she came running down to the basement.   
I wanted to reach out and spin her around.   
I wanted to check if she was still my Emma. I slowly reached my hand out to her.  
  
But I chickened out. Of course it was Emma. Who else would it be? I pulled back and started to turn away when Buster suddenly leapt forward. Still with his tail tucked, Buster jumped on Emma’s back and pushed her down to her knees. She immediately pushed back, stood up and turned around, with the same annoyed look on her face that was so familiar to me.  
  
She asked me what was wrong with Buster. I said nothing and made the mistake of asking what was wrong with her. That completely shut down the conversation and she stormed off to check on her equipment again. I took another look around and couldn't see anything out of place, so Buster and I happily exited the basement.  
  
Unfortunately, that was just the start. At noon, all the faucets in the house suddenly ran. After I went around and turned them all off, I noticed the basement door was open again. I assumed it was a loose door frame and left it open this time. Then I opened our fridge to make lunch but all of our food was rotten. I couldn't understand how. The fridge was working fine and despite getting groceries yesterday, the food looked like it had been there for months.  
  
I decided we should leave but Emma refused. She thought I was overreacting, but neither of us slept much that night. The next day wasn't much better. I work remotely and I think I had a busy day but I kept zoning out and suddenly it was time to clock out while I felt like I had barely gotten anything done.  
  
Emma spent the whole day filming videos in the basement. I went down to check on her and she had red eyes, like she had been crying, but she refused to talk about what happened. I tried to talk about it again when we went to bed but she insisted that things were fine. She revealed a kitchen knife and put it on her nightstand. When I flinched she rolled her eyes and asked if I was ever going to let it go. I asked why she felt like she needed it. She said she just wanted it to feel extra safe tonight.  
  
I rubbed the scar she had given me two years ago and said it was fine. Whatever she needed to feel safe.

We went to bed but I had that same nightmare of being chased through endless hallways. This time, I woke up in a sweat and then realized that despite that, our room was freezing cold. Emma wasn't in bed so I got up to look for her and found the basement door open again.  
  
I walked down and turned on the light to see her by the trash compactor, which was currently running. Metal walls were slowly crushing whatever was inside. Emma turned to me with white eyes, like a statue’s.  
  
She said my name, but the whole thing, Benjamin, which she never does. She said, "Have I ever told you how much I loathe you?" in a voice that became increasingly deeper. Instead of answering, I asked what was in the trash compactor.

"Our cell phones, smart watches, iPads, everything that you could use to call for help."  
  
Out of good ideas, I walked over and tried to hug her close but she put her hand around my throat and lifted me off the floor. I kicked my legs and pulled on her fingers but she was too strong, black spots started to crowd my vision.

Buster slammed into us at a full sprint and she dropped me. He latched onto her forearm and didn’t let go. Her blood dripped onto the floor and black smoke poured out of every orifice and up the basement stairs.

Emma seemed to wake up and couldn't remember anything. Buster had let go of her when the smoke left but Emma soon realized that he had bitten her. She tried to kick Buster but I jumped in the way instead. While she screamed about wanting the dog put down, I tried to explain what happened.  
  
Emma had a hard time believing what I was trying to tell her but even she realized something was seriously wrong. She didn't resist this time when I said we should leave. We packed up just the essentials and headed to a nearby motel where we argued over who we should or even could call.

In the end, we just went to bed. Things were a lot better when I awoke the next morning. The sun was shining, Emma was cooking breakfast, and Buster was in bed cuddling with me. I went out and spoke to the motel manager about our situation. I didn't think he could help, I just wanted to spread awareness.

I walked back to our room and opened the door to find stairs.

I took a step back and slammed the door. I was back in our house, in our kitchen. Emma was standing in front of the cold stove and Buster was laying down with his head on his paws. I wasn’t sure if we had even left. 

I tried the front door but it wouldn't open. I took a few steps back and ran at it, intending to drive my shoulder through the wood. All that happened was a partial dislocation between my humerus and scapula. 

We all heard footsteps upstairs, directly above us. Emma told Buster to, "Get it!" and pointed to the upstairs. Buster complied obediently and flew up the stairs.  
  
I immediately blacked out and then woke with my hands tight around Emma's neck. Buster had again come to the rescue and had my ankle in his jaws. He was attempting to drag me off of Emma and as soon as I let go of her neck, he also let go of me.  
  
Emma immediately ran to the kitchen. I tried calling for her to come back but she just yelled back a string of curses. I pulled Buster in close and we walked to the kitchen together. My ankle was sore but he hadn't done much damage.

I pushed open the kitchen door and Emma was pounding on the counter with a meat tenderizer. She turned to me and revealed she had just smashed our landline receiver. I also got a good look at her milky white eyes. She swept away the debris and laid her left hand down on the counter.  
  
I let Buster loose and we both leapt across the kitchen as she brought the tenderizer onto the back of her hand. Buster jumped on her back and I grabbed the tenderizer out of her hand as she brought it up for another whack.  
  
Emma was already back to herself and screaming in pain. Buster hid behind me while I tried to tend to Emma. I explained that whatever this thing is that is messing with us, Buster seems to repel it. In fact, I felt like it might be afraid of him.  
  
“Why?”  
  
“Well,” I answered hesitantly while I tried to think of anything that made sense. “Well, he is a very good boy.”

Emma’s sobbing calmed down to the occasional hiccup and she nodded. I did a rudimentary check of her hands, and, luckily, it wasn't broken. She hugged Buster and started apologizing to him. She told me to follow her, that she had a plan. I tried to ask questions while I followed her and Buster to the front door. She made him rub up against it.

I didn't think it would work but past experiences had taught me to keep my doubts to myself. And I did think it would be good to try anything. I certainly didn't have any ideas myself. I tried not to think about it, but this situation seemed hopeless to survive in the long run.  
  
Emma told me to try the door. The handle didn't budge. She told me to keep pulling and I pulled with all my might. I kept pulling until my sore shoulder was on fire but nothing happened. I turned to apologize to Emma but she and Buster weren't there.

I called out for them and Buster barked from the basement. I don't think I have ever moved as fast as I did just then. I was at the basement door in a flash. It was wide open and I could see Emma in the center of the basement. She had Buster by the collar and a kitchen knife in the other hand. Buster was crouched and shaking, just looking at her.

“I'll make you a deal,” she was saying, to whatever dark force was in there with them. “Open the front door and I'll get rid of it. It won't bother you anymore!”

She saw me, staring at her in disbelief.

“He’s afraid of the dog,” she said. “If the dog’s gone, he’ll leave us alone.”

“No, the only reason we're still alive is because of Buster,” I said, walking down the stairs and getting closer to her.

“No,” she said. “It’s just a dog, Ben.”

Something laughed from the basement. Low, like it was enjoying itself.

I grabbed at her hand that was holding Buster, she swung the knife and opened my arm from elbow to wrist, along the old scar. I managed to wrestle it away from her and she screamed at me. Then, while I stood there with my own blood dripping from the knife in my hand, she started sobbing.

"I'm sorry, Ben, I don't know what happened. I must have been possessed again."

I was shocked. I looked at her like it was the first time, seeing a completely different person than the one I had married.

"Emma. Your eyes were brown."

She slapped me hard and, as if on cue, the lone light bulb shattered and plunged us into darkness. Emma pushed past me, and ran up the basement stairs. Buster came to lick my wounds but I picked him up and ran after Emma. I yelled out to her that we needed to stick together. But she was gone. I put Buster down and we searched each room and found no trace of her.  
  
Nearing despair, I opened the last door upstairs, one that led to a comically shallow closet and unlikely to fit my wife, thin as she was. Instead, it opened to the kitchen. There was an old book on the island, open to a page with a green circle drawn on it.

I picked up the book. It looked like it was describing a ritual. There was a depiction of a woman laying down in the middle of the circle.  A drop of blood fell on the page.  
  
I looked up to see Emma, writhing on the ceiling and bleeding from numerous deep scratches that appeared to be self-inflicted. Her eyes were white.

“Read it,” she said. “Read it, Benjamin. You know what I plan to do to your beautiful wife. You can still save her, you know."  
  
I shook my head. "No, she's already dead, isn't she?"

"Fool. I don't throw away my toys so easily. I will kill her if you don't intervene but I will give you a fair chance."

"If you don’t, I swear to God-"

But I suddenly was back upstairs, looking at an empty closet. The book was still in my hands though. The page described a process that delivers a soul straight to the underworld and empties the vessel to allow for a permanent possession.

Emma screamed in pain from the basement. My first instinct was to run towards her but everything about this felt wrong. I read the next page too, unsure if this Demon was actually trying to play a game or was rigging a trap.

Emma screamed again. "BEN! WHERE ARE YOU!?"

I realized I didn't have any good options. I put Buster on a leash and we walked down the basement stairs. It was filled with fog but there was a glowing green circle on the floor that lit up the dark room.  
  
Somebody called “Buster!” in my voice, far more cheerfully than I could even fake right now. Buster looked off into the fog, confused. I pulled him close and ran to the center of the circle.  
  
Emma wasn't there.  
  
I was lifted in the air and thrown before I realized she had been standing behind me. Buster's leash slid off my bloody wrist and I slammed against the wall hard. The green circle glowed brighter and the fog dissipated, the Demon wanted me to see what would happen next.  
  
Emma still had white eyes but had carved runes into her skin. They glowed red as she picked up Buster. The smoke started to leave her, slower than last time. She had just enough time to grab Buster and throw him into the trash compactor.  
  
I ran over to stop her but she felled me with one punch breaking my nose in the process. She grabbed my leg and dragged me over to the circle. I thought this was their plan, to make me the sacrifice but no, she laid down on the circle and leather straps emerged from the floor and wrapped around her wrists and ankles.  
  
A dagger with a wicked looking curve to it appeared above her, floating directly over her heart. The dagger started lowering slowly while Emma's eyes turned brown. Black smoke surrounded the compactor and I instinctively knew there was no way of getting through it to save Buster.

I could hear Buster yelping and the scraping of his claws as he tried to climb out of the top.

“FOCUS,” Emma said. “Please, baby, you have to save me.”

I grabbed the dagger and was surprised to find it moved easily.  
  
"Please Ben. I was possessed, I'm not responsible for what happened. Any of it. You know this Thing, it messes with your mind. I haven't done anything to you. It's always been the Demon. Remember baby, you love me. We can get another dog."

“I’m sorry, Emma,” I said. “But we’re through.” Then I continued to push down on the dagger, hastening its journey into her heart. If I was going to have a chance to save Buster, I needed the ritual completed as soon as possible.

The green circle turned purple. Her mouth opened and the black smoke was sucked into her. I immediately ran to the compactor and stopped it. Emma thrashed at her restraints, two voices coming out of her and both cursing me.  
  
The purple circle then turned white and started glowing as bright as the Sun. I had to shield my eyes but it only lasted a second before the light faded away. I could see the circle was completely empty before the whole basement went dark again.  
  
I opened the compactor and felt inside. Buster was covered in trash and shaking but, as soon as he recognized me, his tail started wagging.

I carried him out of the house and put him in my truck. The second page of the ritual had described how the vessel filling part, done in the underworld, would take six vespers to complete. I had no idea how long that would take so we drove to the nearest gas station and filled up several gas cans.

I stood on the lawn and watched the house burn down to piles of ashes and debris on the foundation. Nobody came out of the other houses. I heard no sirens. Every window on the street was lit. I looked around and for the first time noticed that every lawn in sight had the same For Sale sign. Contact Lorraine for more details.  
  
Buster and I are now living in a small studio apartment. I wish I could do more for him but the mortgage company still wants its payment too. So, coming back to the insurance policy, on page twenty-two, it does say something about “causes outside of the Owner’s control.” I’m not sure if that covers demons but I assure you the situation was very much out of my control.  
  
Oh, and I’m sure you’re curious how I’m handling the disappearance of Emma. When people ask what happened, I truthfully say that I broke up with her and she left to go somewhere far away. All of my friends and family are happy for me.  
  
And I honestly don’t think there was any way I could have saved her too. I would have preferred to but when it boiled down to a choice between Emma and Buster, well, it wasn’t much of a choice at all.

After all, Buster is such a very good boy.
