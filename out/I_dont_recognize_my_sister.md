About a week ago I was sitting the couch watching tv with my mom when I heard the front door creak open. A young girl casually walked through the door, neatly placing her bright pink shoes on the rack and casually throwing her bag in the hallway with the ease of someone who’d done it a thousand times before.

I looked around baffled but my mom showed no reaction, her eyes still remaining fixed on the tv. The girl walked in, smiled and said she was going to her room. Finally my mom looked up, yet the words which came out her mouth next did nothing but increase my confusion; “ok honey, I’ll bring you some lunch before soccer practice”. Mouth agape I watched her swinging ponytail disappear up the stairs.

Turning to my mom i asked who the hell that was. Genuine confusion spread across her face so I repeated the question, louder this time. She warned me that I should lower my voice and stop joking around. “It’s not funny” she said sternly. “Don’t ever talk about your sister like that”. I analyzed her face but I couldn’t tell what she was thinking, was she angry, sad, worried or confused? Did she know something I didn’t? I didn’t want to find out, so I kept quiet and turned my head back to the glowing screen. 

I didn’t mention it for the rest of the day, but my eyes caught on a photograph on the fridge. A place I didn’t know, unfamiliar palm trees rising in the background yet i saw my own teenage face looking back at me, eyes locked on the camera and a wide smile spread across my face. My mom has her arm around my shoulder and is looking down at something. Or maybe someone.  
I look at the edge at the photo. At the very bottom is a little girl, no more than 5 with curly blonde hair and a tiny hand reaching up at my mother. 

We haven’t been abroad since I was 9. 

When they finally left I searched the house for any clue of this apparent “sister” and found dozens of pictures, childish drawings as well as multiple papers listing her name. Her full name is Anna Smith, she’s 8 years old, she is my sister and has been for half my life now. 

Letters were strewn across the kitchen table. Names in childish clumsy writing decorated with hearts and shiny stickers, she must be popular. Everything suggested an ordinary sweet girl with a perfectly mundane life.  
And to my horror a pink bed stood beside mine.

I ate dinner with “Anna” and mom in the afternoon while they made small talk and I sat quietly picking at my plate. Afterwards my mom asked if something was wrong, but I mumbled that i was just tired after school and went for a walk. She didn’t seem to notice my absence.

I sat on a nearby bench and chain smoked until my newly bought pack was almost empty. Not able to control my shaking hands, I dropped lit cigarettes and watched them extinguish in the wet grass, then lit another. I coughed through the smoke, my throat burning but I didn’t mind the distraction. I wiped my hands in the grass and popped a piece of synthetic strawberry gum in my dry mouth, then walked home. 

By the time I got home I’d almost convinced myself that she wasn’t really there or that it was been some sort of sick joke.I was still in disbelief, people dont just appear like that, it’s impossible. Maybe I’d gone crazy? 

Yet when i walked in i was greeted with Anna’s grinning face at the door. “Hi sis!” She said and I freezed as she hugged my waist tightly. “I’ve been waiting for you” she whispered and smiled up at me.

The worst part was sleeping with her. It wasn’t any weird noises that scared me; it was the complete opposite. The silence made me feel uneasy, no sound of breathing or creaking from turning around in the bed. I laid awake, tried to absorb any noises from her side of the room but all I heard was my own breathing. Eventually I put in my headphones and slowly drifted off to sleep.

Mornings were weird. She sat eating her cereal while reading books as my mom shuffled around looking for her keys which always ended up being in her pocket or occasionally in the kitchen cabinet. We walked to school together and when someone asked how she was I would freeze up. Often they seemed confused by my reaction, so eventually i learned just to say “fine”. People seemed relieved which contributed to my confusion, did they also know what she was? That she was an intruder, a ghost of the past or a whole other species of unnatural being. I couldn’t tell and I didn’t dare to ask. 

Sometimes I babysat her since my mom was working late due to a deadline at her job. I could tell she was stressed, so I tried my hardest to push down the unnerving feelings and helped out around the house when I could. Anna seemed oblivious to my reluctant behavior around her. She babbled on about her friends, school and a movie she saw at the cinema with mom last week. I responded with vague nods or mumbled ahh’s or mmm’s. I asked questions about her life but kept it surface level, careful not alert her of the discomfort I felt about her.

A night a few days after her appearance she approached me with something hidden behind her back. I asked what she was hiding, and she excitedly showed me a drawing she had made of us and exclaimed that it was for me. Two skin colored blobs, mine with glasses and hers with a pink bow both standing in tall bright grass. Behind us was what I guessed were tall snowy mountains, with the sun rising just over the peak. She had drawn pink hearts above our wonky smiling faces. 

I must’ve looked confused, so she quickly handed it to me and asked if I liked it. I quickly replied with a smile and told her that I liked it very much. She cuddled up to me on the couch, and to my own surprise I laid my arm around her warm shoulders as we both fell asleep.

She has been here for a week now.   
We walk to school, watch tv while eating dinner and sleep less than 6 feet apart. It feels weird, but strangely she is starting to appear in memories, her face gradually getting clearer and clearer. 

However, few days in I noticed something else that confirmed my suspicion. Sometimes late at night when she thought I was sleeping I saw her staring at me or an invisible spot on the wall for hours. I waited for her chest to rise and then sink, but I couldn’t make out the details in the darkness. The silence felt suffocating. 

The day after I’d discovered her strange nighttime activities, my mom came to our room and told Anna that she should get ready for the drive to the hospital. When she left the room  to use the bathroom, I approached my mom and asked her why she’s going there. My mom glanced at me and sighed. “You really don’t remember?” She said with a look of concern flashing across her face. I was about to ask another question, but she quickly turned around and left as Anna called to her from the bathroom.

I felt bewildered. Of course I’d noticed Anna’s tiny frame, much smaller than an average 8 year old, and the eyes that even when smiling still looked tired, maybe even sad. But didn’t think she looked sick, a bit frail maybe but really nothing out of the ordinary. Again, I pushed it away.

We’re going on a weekend trip to the mountains. Anna seems excited, running around her room to find her favorite teddy bear and filling her suitcase to the brim with unnecessary items. We listen to the radio in the car and Anna keeps interrupting the music, going on and on about all the exciting things she wants to do. 

We set up the tent at the edge of the forest, close enough to other people to feel safe, but also far enough from them to have our own quiet space. Anna wants to go up the mountain trail. Mom says she’s too tired and Anna starts throwing a fit. At last mom sighs reluctantly. “Ok you can go, but your sister is coming with you” Im tired as well, but Anna cheers and grabs my hand “Let’s go!” and I obey.

We follow a steep trail up the mountains. She runs in front of me while i keep an eye on the trail making sure she doesnt leave my sight at any moment. When we get to a viewpoint we sit down to eat our packed sandwiches and talk for a bit. She talks eagerly about her friends, but when she gets to her best friend she looks down and tears start filling her eyes. I’m quiet for a second, then a weird feeling of protectiveness washes over me and I hug her. “It’s ok” I say, trying to sound as motherly and comforting as I can. Somehow It feels weirdly.. natural?

Later I watch her excitedly running around, stopping to look at shiny bugs and bending down to pick up flowers to make a bouquet for me and mom. I’ve not seen her so happy and energetic since she came, her face curled up in an almost euphoric smile and eyes gleaming at me. 

I discretely puff on my vape while she’s turned around and look down to check my phone for a second. I get caught up in a news article about a girl going missing nearby but it’s suddenly eerily quiet and i look up from the screen. 

Anna is standing at the edge of a cliff waving at me. “Look!” She shrieks, lifting her right leg like a flamingo and swaying in the autumn breeze for few seconds before she stumbles forwards and plants her foot solidly on the ground again. Below her is a drop of at least fifty feet leading directly to certain death. I yell for her to come back and run to pull her away from the edge. She has no reaction, I try to pull her back but her feet are liked nailed to the ground. Suddenly she hugs me, im caught of gaurd as her arms squeeze me tighter. For a moment everything stops and i can only hear my own  loud heartbeat, then nothing. 

I wake up at the bottom of the cliff. I see my body lying next to me, lifeless. Suddenly it jolts awake and clumsily stands upright. I can’t move my limbs, I’m frozen in horror and a wave of pain flashes through my body. I stare at it moving closer. It stumbles on a branch but its feet keep dragging through the thick piles of leaves. It stops and picks something off the ground before making its way the last few feet from where it am laying, breathless and wheezing in pain and fear. I try to move, but my body is refusing to cooperate, not even able to manage a single twitch of a finger as it towers over me.

The moment I recognize the object true terror rushes through my veins. 

I can do nothing but watch as my arms lift the rock and my own face smiles down at me.
