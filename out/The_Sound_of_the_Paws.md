*The wooden front door burst open, slamming against the plaster wall with a deafening bang.*  
*My heart shot into my throat. I squeezed deeper into the cramped crawlspace beneath the floorboards, wrapping my arms tight around my knees. It was inside.*  
*Through the narrow gap in the wooden slats, I watched the heavy, dust-caked entrance. Then came the sound—a low, wet thud of massive paws dragging across the floorboards. Heavy. Deliberate. The shadow it cast across the hallway wall was monstrous, broad-shouldered with two glowing eyes that seemed to burn through the pitch black.*  
*From the front room came a blood-curdling scream, instantly cut short by the sickening tear of flesh and a wet crack.*  
*My aunt.*  
*Tears burned my eyes, but I forced my hand over my mouth to smother my own gasps. We were completely trapped in this isolated countryside home. No cell service. No neighbors for miles. One by one, over the last two hours, it had hunted my family down. It didn't care where anyone hid. It found my uncle in the cellar. It found my cousins in the attic. It moved through the house like an inescapable spirit, tracking every breath, every heartbeat.*  
*The heavy, rhythmic padding of its paws moved toward the kitchen. Toward me.*  
*Thud. Thud. Thud.*  
*A guttural, chest-rattling growl vibrated through the floorboards, shaking dust down into my hair. The smell of copper and rot filled the air. Through the gap, I saw the sleek, powerful shoulder of the beast turn the corner. It stopped right above my head.*  
*Panic seized my chest. It knew I was here.*  
*I didn't wait for it to rip the floorboards open. I shoved the trapdoor upward with all my strength, throwing the creature off balance, and sprinted blindly toward the back door.*  
*"Get away!" I screamed, bursting out into the freezing night air.*  
*The storm raged outside. Fog hung thick over the treeline, and the narrow dirt path led straight toward the sheer drop of the Blackwood Cliff. I couldn't run toward the village—the beast was right behind me. I could hear its ragged, furious breathing heavy on my heels, the grass snapping beneath its weight.*  
*I ran until the ground suddenly vanished.*  
*I skidded to a halt, rocks tumbling over the edge into the endless, foggy abyss below. A hundred-foot drop to the jagged river rocks. A dead end.*  
*I turned around, hyperventilating, backing up until my heels hung over the void.*  
*Out of the thick fog, the spirit tiger emerged. It was towering, ferocious, its bare fangs dripping with fresh blood. It stalked toward me, blocking the only path to safety. Its cold, glowing eyes locked onto mine. It let out a deafening roar that shook the trees, stepping closer and closer, cornering me against the sky.*  
*There was no escaping it. It was going to tear me apart just like the rest of them.*  
*"You won't get me," I sobbed, staring directly into the beast's eyes. "You won't break me."*  
*I took a deep breath, looked to my right, and stepped off the edge.*  
*In that final, suspended second of freefall, the tiger leaped with me. Side-by-side, we plummeted through the dense fog together, two shadows falling toward the jagged rocks below.*  
*The morning sun finally broke through the storm clouds, casting pale light over the base of Blackwood Cliff.*  
*A search team crept along the riverbank, their boots crunching against the wet gravel.*  
*"Over here!" a deputy shouted, waving his flashlight toward the rocks. "I found her!"*  
*The team rushed over, shining their lights on the broken body lying motionless at the base of the gorge.*  
*The lead investigator knelt down, checking for a pulse, then sighed heavily and shook his head. He looked around the immediate landing area, sweeping his light across the sharp stones, the muddy banks, and the surrounding trees.*  
*"Check the rest of the perimeter," the officer ordered. "Her entire family was slaughtered up in that house. Blood everywhere. If the killer chased her out here, he might have fallen off the cliff with her."*  
*The deputy spent ten minutes meticulously searching every inch of the rocks, scanning for claw marks, animal tracks, or another body. He walked back, looking completely baffled.*  
*"Nothing, Chief," the deputy said, shaking his head. "No tracks. No animal prints. No second body. It’s just her."*  
*The investigator looked down at the woman’s hands—her knuckles raw, skin scraped, and thick, crimson blood caked deep beneath every single one of her fingernails.*  
*"There was no beast," the investigator whispered, staring at her blood-stained hands.*

 *"It was only ever her.”*
