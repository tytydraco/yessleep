I don't dream, and I never have. For as long as I can remember, sleep has always come and gone in the blink of an eye. Whenever someone talks to me about a dream they had the night before -- crazy, scary, sad, happy, or otherwise -- I just nod and tell them it sounds similar to one I've had in the past. It's faster and easier than telling the truth. They're the ones who sound crazy to me, though I try to remind myself that I am the outlier. Frankly, all the stories make me glad I don't have to deal with dreams myself. It's a blessing to me, really.

My girlfriend Mandy and I have been together going five years now this October , and we've lived together for about half that long. She didn't know about my "condition" until then. She finds it fascinating, but also not a little concerning at times despite my affirmations that not going to wonderland every night has no effect on me. She, however, believes this can't be the case.

Mandy believes dreams are just one of the many facets of the human condition, that they exist to inspire or otherwise force our subconscious minds to see the familiar in brand new ways. So, naturally, having no dreams must be a symptom of something wrong. I'd be willing to eat my words if there was tangible proof of this actually being detrimental; spiritual answers to things have never sat right with me -- unfortunately enough, since Mandy was a spiritual sort of person. We made things work, though. My focus on practical things bring her down to earth, and her flights of whimsy helped me remember to look up sometimes and see the world with wonder.

People who don't know me well assume that, because of my no-nonsense personality and the fact I don't dream, I must not be a creative person; *maybe you're just more focused on the practical things in life?*

But that's not true. I'm a painter, and I have been for, well, as long as I can remember. My usual work consists of surrealist landscapes, architecture, animals, and people. It's funny -- whenever I've submitted my work for critique or show off to friends, I'm told it's very dreamlike. Maybe I just get my fill of dreams in the waking world. I don't know.

Mandy brought the subject up once again a couple of weeks ago. She told me about how she had the most vivid dreams of her life for several nights after taking some magic mushrooms.

"Wasn't that the trip with --"

"Yes. Him," she said, cutting me off.

"Him" being some asshole who broke her heart three years before we met. She'd never told me all of the details, but I knew enough to surmise that he was an abusive piece of trash.

She continued: "You don't even need a big dose if it's your first time. I took a microdose because I was scared from all of the stories about bad trips. I was thinking, if it was okay with you, we could try it and see if it finally happens?"

After some back-and-forth, I agreed on the condition that she finally put the matter to rest if it didn't work. And, secretly, I hoped it didn't. Faced with the possibility of dreaming for the first time in my life, I found myself afraid of what I might see. Was it possible that dreams could "build up" over time, so that when you finally did dream, it was more vivid and intense than usual

And if so, what would that be like for someone like me who's never had one in their entire life?

I seriously considered backing out the moment I said yes, but Mandy was so excited I couldn't bring myself to tell her I'd changed my mind.

"Okay, okay," I said. "But only a microdose."

Two days later, she came home from work an hour later than usual after visiting one of the old friends who'd been there the night she'd taken shrooms for the first time (not *him*). As soon as she came inside, she retrieved a little baggie from her work bag and waved it at me, smiling. She asked if I was ready. I told her I was ready to dream.

I patted a spot on the couch next to me, and she hurriedly kicked off her shoes and plopped down. She took one of the little mushrooms out of the baggie, tore off a little piece, and handed it to me. She held on to the rest.

"Are you really taking that much?" I asked.

"Yeah! Why not? I don't work tomorrow. Besides, I didn't get the full experience before, and I really had a good time. I wanna feel what more is like."

Fair enough. We "clinked" our shrooms together, cheers'd, and down our hatches they went.

All in all, we had an amazing time. Mandy got so giddy that I had to calm her down and remind her of our noise-averse nextdoor neighbors several times, but they never ended up bothering us. We spent the rest of the night watching movies, playing video games, eating junk food, and talking about the most random things. It felt good. But I couldn't tell if it was because of the mushrooms or if it was just spending time with her.

She started falling asleep sitting up at around 4:00am, so I carried her to bed and got in with her. We whispered to each other things so sweet that they still make me smile now. We slept fast and hard.

But I did not dream. I got up early, relieved but also a little disappointed, and made us breakfast. By the time I carried it into our room, she was awake and sitting up, waiting on me with a wide, sleepy grin on her face.

"Soooo?" she said.

I considered telling one of my white lies and relaying some grand tale about how dream-me was a dream-knight, about how he slayed some dream-dragons and rescued a dream-princess who, strangely enough, looked just like her, only twelve-and-a-half feet tall and with bird feet.

But, knowing I couldn't have handled being that dishonest about something she was so invested in, I just shook my head and told her the truth.

She frowned. "It really doesn't make sense to me, Rob. I seriously think you should consider getting a sleep study. I looked it up before, and dreams happen during REM sleep. If you've never, ever dreamed in your life, then maybe there's something wrong with your sleep cycle?"

"You promised you'd drop it if this didn't work. Why is this such a big deal to you?" I said, louder and angrier than I'd intended. But I had just about had it with the dream talk.

"I'm sorry. I'm scared of just letting this go in case there is really something wrong. I know you think the whole spiritual thing is silly --"

"I don't think it's silly, it's just not for me."

"It'd both. We both know that. And I'm secure enough in my beliefs that I'm really not offended by that. But to put my concerns in a realistic sort of way that you can understand, I've noticed over the years that you've always needed a little more sleep than most people. You can go to bed at nine-o-clock and wake up past noon if I let you. I know some people are just like that, but combined with the fact you don't dream might mean you don't have good sleep quality."

"I got up early today," I rebutted.

"It's past four, Rob."

"Oh," I said. "I guess that does all make sense."

"Would you be willing to do a sleep study? If they don't see anything wrong, then I'll accept I'm just being ridiculous and I'll let it go forever. I promise. Please?"

"Alright, hon. I'll shop around right now and make an appointment today. I've got a few days off coming up early anyway, so it's perfect timing. I love you. And I'm sorry for yelling."

"Oh, you barely raised your voice. I love you, too."

I moved to hug and kiss her, realized I was still holding our plates loaded with eggs, bacon, biscuits, and gravy, and we both laughed. The tension was banished like a curtain had been thrust open in the dark, and before long we were cuddled up in bed eating our evening breakfast and watching some old childhood cartoons together.

A few days later, I went to my appointment. An attendant took me to a room that looked a lot more like a mid-scale hotel room than the sterile medical environment I'd anticipated. I slept well.

The few days after that, I went back to their office for my follow-up appointment and sat with a professional.

"How are you doing today, sir?"

"Good! I'm excited to see the results."

"And I'm excited to go over them with you. They were . . . a little different from what we've seen before."

"In a good way, I hope?"

"We're not quite sure yet. Now, I want to reassure you, by all accounts, you're sleeping just fine. One could call you a textbook sleeper, if ever there was one. You were still, your breathing was normal, and you didn't snore once. You did mention being concerned at the amount of sleep you tend to need, but ten hours isn't all that strange as long as you can function as normal throughout the day. All-in-all, pretty good."

"So what was the catch?"

"Your brain wave activity. The whole time, from when you'd just laid down to when you woke up, the results were indicative of someone who was awake. Brain wave activity during REM sleep normally tends to read similarly to an awake brain."

"What does that mean for me?"

"We're not sure right now. Typically, REM is the last stage of a good night's sleep. There are three stages before that, and you skipped them all. By sleep science standards, you're in REM sleep as we speak."

I could tell I must have been making a funny face, because the doctor's expression softened, and she seemed about to reassure me before I spoke.

"But that's the problem. I've never dreamed in my life. How can I be in REM right now?"

"I only said your brain waves were indicative of someone who was in REM, not that you literally are. That'd be ridiculous. You'd be dreaminggggggggggggggggggggg . . ."

My jaw fell, and a hot flash of panic wracked my body. The doctor's body had gone completely still as if she'd been frozen in time, but her voice kept drawing out the last tone of the word like an audio glitch. I thought I was having a stroke.

I shot up and ran out of the office. Everyone I passed looked at me like I'd gone crazy, and perhaps I had -- I had no other way to explain what had just happened.

I didn't know where I was going, only that I wanted out of that room. What the fuck had happened?

I ran into the parking lot, felt a rush of vertigo, suddenly off-balance. I was on the driveway of my home. And my car wasn't on it. I hadn't simply experienced skipped time -- I had *moved*.

It's taken a long time, but I'm able to control it now. I'm still unsure what "it" is, but I don't care anymore. I'm happier now. So long as I hold my grasp on this reality, it's better than the real thing.

Mandy's asshole ex? He never existed, so far as this world is concerned. I've had the chance to get acquainted with a Mandy who'd never gone through that trauma. I never knew how much it had affected her -- she's a completely different person, now. So different that she didn't really like me much anymore. But I fixed that, too.

The weather is beautiful when I want to be outside, and gloomy when I want to stay in and read a book. And, when I don't like that book, I can make it better on a whim. If I don't like an author, I can make their books terrible. Obviously I could make it to where they didn't become a writer or weren't ever born in the first place, but I get the most satisfaction out of the little, impactful changes.

I don't dream. I am the dream.

Tell me what you want, and maybe I'll make that a reality, too.
