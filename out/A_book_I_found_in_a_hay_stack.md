I work on a family run farm. Pretty normal, nothing crazy, a couple horses and cows.

But while feeding my horse “Gregory” I found a book called “Animism” 

It was worn down, cover barely holding on. It looked like a weird bunny mask.

I didn’t know what to expect so I just put it on a shelf until recently. 

While cleaning I found the book and gave it a read.

This is what I read:

A boy sat on a crooked wooden chair, his eyes fixed upon the plate before him, underneath a mess of crudely butchered meat and large bones, both covered in a thick coating.  
​  
The food was prepared with little recognizable methodology; however, it still showed clear signs of impropriety. The boy gazed only at the meat, observing the occasional bubble and thin mealworm, both hardly visible under the table’s dim lighting.  
​  
“There’s no reason for this now. You know as well as I do why we HAVE to do this.”  
​  
The boy gazed at the expanse of the table, before reluctantly raising his view to the man’s chest, then his neck, unblemished and smooth. He squinted, as an unease fell deep within his heart, then he met eyes with the man opposite him.   
​  
He wore a mask on his face, its details hard to discern, but the face unmistakable to the boy. With a toothy smile, one that warped around its protruding nose, rubbery fat folded on itself to cover his neck, ears around a hand's length, and eyes hollowed out to convey a sense of inscrutability, together formed the visage of an off-white bunny.  
​  
“That MUTT had it coming, you know it as well as I do. It wasn’t pure. NOT PURE AT ALL. Nothing PURE about it.”

The boy, struggling to keep his eyes open, continued to stare straight into the blackened eyes of the mask.  
​  
The man took a long pause, staring intently at the mess of meats before him. With strained breaths, he announced his dissatisfaction before looking to his side.  
​  
He gradually raised his hand above the table.   
​  
“THIS IS WHAT IT DID TO ME. TO YOUR OWN FATHER. I have no qualms with righteousness; I have no hatred for the purity of intention. But there was no such thing. I WAS ITS MASTER, yet it INJURED ME. There was no purity in that.”  
​  
The arm he raised presented a monument of disfigurement. His hand was mangled so that it barely remained connected to a sleeve of skin that had been sliced within an inch of complete detachment.

​  
The boy wanted to close his eyes for a brief moment of rest, but couldn’t force himself to curtain the sight. The red, although darkened in the light, was visibly festered.

Just then, the man stopped. He lowered his arm, again looking intently at the unidentifiable gristle before him.  
​  
His ragged breathing came to a halt as he raised his face to the ceiling, almost like he was pursuing an answer to a question never asked. He raised his other hand in front of his mask, stretching it as he rubbed his black-gloved hand across his unchanging smile. He continued for a couple of seconds before sharply throwing his arm back down.  
​  
“Close your eyes, child.”  
​  
So the boy did just that. He finally laid his eyes to rest, while still adamantly keeping his face affixed towards his father.  
​  
With only sound to guide him, the boy passively listened to the sounds of meat squishing in itself, followed by sounds of smacking and saliva squelching. The man swallowed gutturally, as pained wheezing followed each series of slurps.

​  
“That’s enough now. I will carry him to his salvation. And so will you.”  
​  
The boy once more struggled to muster the strength to unstick the surrounding flesh of his eyes. The boy's father, however, remained direct. Still adjusting his mask, he once more peered into the flesh of his son’s face.  
​  
“Eat now, boy. For what his life was worth, he was worth carrying. I’ll give you the privilege of indulging just this once.”  
​  
The boy’s face quickly fell back down to the plate in relaxation. The strain of raising his face for that long left his neck sore.  
​  
A liquid, bloody and viscous, dripped slowly from his agape mouth. Laboriously, the boy moved his hands onto the table before penetrating the mush.  
​  
Grabbing the largest handfuls, the boy raised his hands halfway to his face, meeting them with his fallen face.  
​  
With his tongue, the boy lapped up as much meat as he could before licking the rest up into his mouth. The mush slid into his neck, as more blood continued to seep from where he lacked lips.

He continued, each time becoming less strained and more fluid. By the time the plate was nothing more than bloody saliva, the boy finally closed his jaw in a rough chewing pattern.​

“There we go. How’d you like the taste? I made sure to add some pepper, just the way we like it.”  
​  
The boy continued, each chew audibly squelching.  
​  
The man looked on expressionlessly, but briefly glanced at a luxury watch on his wrist.  
​  
“After you finish eating, I want you to go fix your bandages. I did say you could indulge yourself just this once, but that’s no excuse to remain slothful.”  
​  
While the man pushed his chair out from the table, the boy raised his hand to his cheek, feeling the blood that oozed out from his bandages.  
​  
“The day is still young, I must pray once more.”  
​  
The man stood up, placing his hand onto the shaking table for support, before starting towards the leftmost door.  
​  
The boy kept his eyes and thoughts trained on the blood, his bandages quivering with each effort. He too stood up, using all the might found within his brittle bones to push his body up.

He kept his hand firmly on the table, as his legs strained to keep steady. This shook both his arm and the table, which creaked under the now trembling meat.  
​  
Once at a more certain position, he began moving each foot. He removed his hand from the table, which flopped to his side.  
​  
His destination was the nearby door, not more than a couple of steps away from the table. The boy made steady, but slow progress, each step easier than the previous.  
​  
After over a minute of walking, the boy reached the handle of the door. It was a dull yellow on a wooden door, weathered and cracked. Gently, the boy pushed it open, its handle remaining in place and the lock far too worn to catch.  
​  
A brighter light beamed into the boy's dreary eyes. Instinctively, the boy closed them as he continued ahead for a few paces, then turning to his left.  
​  
He raised his arm to feel the wooden surface, then opened his eyes to find a mirror. He lingered on the face before him, blood gushing out from heavily stained bandages that blended into his pulpy tissue.

This only remained for a moment before his eyes darted downwards at his hand, which held onto the wooden sink. He couldn’t bring his gaze higher than his chest. He never intended to witness the mess.  
​  
There was no faucet, but there was a small platform to the right of the bowl that held a large roll of browned bandages and a small bottle of syrupy purple liquid.  
​  
The boy carefully grasped the bottle before trying to unscrew the drenched lid. The tips of his fingers pushed restlessly against the lid, digging into the skin that lacked protection from any nails, until finally twisting the lid free.   
​  
He raised his face to the ceiling before pouring the contents on his still bandage-covered face. Unevenly, the fluids spread across each crevice, until running dry.  
​  
Vacantly, he lifted a small slip of bandage and stuck it to his left cheek, before emptying the rest of the roll haphazardly across the rest of his face and head.  
​  
Once fully covered, he felt around his face, searching for pulp, and once certain of his face, he looked back down towards the mirror.

The face previously indescribable was now fully sheltered in a copper veil.  
​  
His gaze fixated on the person in the mirror. He yearned to look away, but sought after each blemish.

He raised his hand to the boy before him, then to the side of the mirror itself. Bending his fingers around its edge, he pulled it open, first with a burst of pressure, then with tense persistence.  
​  
The gap revealed a series of sprawling mildew that lined both the rim of the mirror and the gap between the base and now pulled surface. The yellowish-green substance partially broke upon the complete opening of the mirror.  
​  
Within the hidden cabinet was an ecosystem of various mold and waste. The boy’s hand searched within the mass. He scanned the surrounding area, until feeling the edge of something thin digging into his palm. Grabbing the object, he pulled out a small slip of paper, smaller than a hand's length.  
​  
On the slip was an image that held the likeness of a boy of healthier build and a larger adult, similarly sized to the boy’s own father. The depiction presented the pair in a standard photography room, one where a family would visit to take their pictures. The man held one hand over the sitting boy’s shoulder, while both held large smiles.  
​  
The boy emptied the rest of the bottle onto the backside of the image,  and after closing the mirror, strained his arm outwards to place the image level with his face.  
​  
His eyes darted between his own face and the picture of the boy’s face. 

After minutes of doing so, his eyes closed again and again, only to open just enough to continue. Each time, his face felt more and more alien, his eyes and nose more sunken, while his ears contracted further into smaller holes beside his face. The little openings of flesh beneath his bandages grew larger, oozing more blood. 

His hand slowly moved closer to the remaining bandages. It retracted.  
​  
After minutes of stillness, he finally looked downwards, only to see darkness, so he raised his eyes back onto the mirror.  
​  
‘Senselessly ignorant’

‘He lacked so much.’

‘I am true to God.’

A discomfort followed as thoughts rose, imposing themselves.  
​  
‘Father knows I won’t hide in my skin.’  
​  
He peered back at the man in the photo for some time, into his face, his perfect smile, his golden eyes. 

He knew that his father was the same, that he’d too leave behind his doubts and fears for a life devoted to holiness.  
​  
But a slight twinge spread across his heart, the same feeling every time he saw the man’s face. One nothing like his own. A face of the distant past, before the radiance that illuminated the truth. Truth alien to the past, yet so visible in the now, and like a beacon to the future.  
​  
The boy wasn't certain what that ray would lead him to, but if for his father, then he would follow.  
​  
But there remained an unease, the thought that man’s face was nothing like his own. 

‘Is that still…’

‘Is that still what he sees in the mirror?’  
​  
He brought his thumb to the man’s face, staining it with little bits of his skin.  
​  
‘That must be the truth…I want to see what he’s given.’

Different ligaments across his face began twitching, his face exercising into something without intention.  
​  
‘Wouldn’t he let me? Purity entails listening to the depths of one’s heart, so why can’t I see it just once?’  
​  
He finally let his gaze leave the image, as he heavily twisted his neck back to the beam of light. There, positioned near the top of the wall, was a barred window. 

‘Wouldn’t it look good on you too?’  
​  
A loud bang followed by multiple yells came from behind him.  
​  
Upon hearing the noise, the boy turned for the door, but with greater haste. Finding a source of vitality within himself, the boy shot the door open in one motion, revealing a frenzied sight: his father whose arm was locked around the neck of a black goat, which lashed around fruitlessly.  
​  
There were deep cuts across his chest freeing large trails of blood, but as quickly as he entered, he carried the goat into his sacred prayer room, kicking it closed from within. However, upon doing so, the door slammed against its casing, leaving it open enough to pour a slight glimmer of light.  
​  
While the boy remained on the prayer room’s door, he failed to notice the front door, which remained open.  
​  
With legs that found themselves newly robust, he slowly but surely closed the gap between him and the door. Once there, he heard a frantic kind of noise, not dissimilar to an awkward laugh.  
​  
He slowly pushed the door open, just enough to peer one eye into. It squinted at first, not seeing past the golden rays of light.

The man had prostrated before the goat, which hung cut in half atop of a cross. Its insides were cleanly removed, leaving a red gap where flesh would connect both sides. To the left was the other goat, somehow completely headless.  
​  
But above all else, the only person in the room was the man from the picture. 

His father was nowhere to be seen; the mask had been removed. Instead, what remained was the other man, making a series of increasingly frenzied cries.

Lifting his body upwards, face turned to the ceiling, a large rose-tinted window beamed blinding light onto his figure. Nonetheless, he demanded an answer to his wails and howled to the heavens.  
​  
“MY BOY!”  
​  
“SAVE MY BOY, HE KNEW NOT OF WHAT HE DID!”  
​  
“WHY SHALL YOU PASS SUCH MISFORTUNE UPON HIM!”

“I HAD TO ACT, YOU KNEW THAT!”

The heavens remained silent.

Lowering his head, he let out a faint whimper.  
​  
“That boy wasn’t supposed to die like that…I was just teaching it grace…was it weakness or was it a fault of my own?”  
​  
The boy’s eye widened far past the remains of his eyelids, stuck in observation, unable to hide the sight. 

He looked upon the goat, the blood that drenched its skin, its chest that opened, revealing a mess of detached organs, and already cut ligaments. He looked upon its face, covered with the blood that should have flowed within its skin, and rigid eyes staring back at him. 

There was a moment of faint familiarity, before his vigor vanished. With a loud thud, the boy’s knees smashed into the wooden floor.  
​  
Without a second to spare, the man turned to face the boy once more, the holy light illuminating his golden eyes and radiant skin.

His brows furrowed in uncertainty, then sharpened into rage, with his teeth closing, grinding together as the boy’s bandages loosened from his daze.   
​  
The man tore through the room, breaking the door open and sending the boy sprawling to the floor.   
​  
A feeling long lost resurfaced within the boy, a large pit opening in him.  
​  
Somehow, with a new urgency flowing in his blood, the boy sprang onto the soles of his blood-covered feet. Before he could fall once more, the man grabbed his arm, holding it high.  
​  
The man drove his fist into the boy’s stomach, spraying its contents across the floor, as a river of blood immediately escaped from the boy's chapped mouth, spraying across the man's chest.  
​  
The man threw his chest into the air before arm-first launching the boy towards the wall, shattering a series of bones all across his body.  
​  
The boy remained conscious, however, now unable to blink away the blood flowing out of his eyes, obscuring much of the man who faced him, tensed.

“IS THIS YOUR ANSWER! MY OWN SON!”  
​  
“A BETRAYAL OF THE HIGHEST CALIBER!”

He continued to yell to a silent audience, while the boy could only hear the flowing of his own blood. 

But within himself was another voice.  
​  
“Boy. I gave you EVERYTHING! EVERYTHING! I GAVE YOU WHAT YOU NEEDED AND RIPPED AWAY THE EXCESS! I WAS PERFECT, I WAS-”  
​  
‘What did he rip away? What of himself was given to the light?’  
​  
‘Who is he really? No-I left my face behind, but he…’  
​  
‘He hasn’t ripped away the excess.’  
​  
The man knelt to one knee. While holding the mess left on the boy's face, he stared directly into where his eye should be.  
​  
“There is no mercy for those who cannot give themselves.”  
​  
There, shredding into the boy’s hand was a mess of wooden splinters, some small, but some larger than the hand itself. In fact, the impact had shredded much of the wall and the boy’s body. 

‘There is no mercy for those who cannot give themselves.’  
​  
The boy, with a motion as fluid as the pus covering his hand, tore the wooden splinters into the side of the man’s face.  
​  
The man forced the boy’s head into the wall, as he pulled his own back, while the boy’s hand ripped away even more of his skin.   
​  
He rushed to place his hand over the wound, which failed to cover the vast area of lacerations. After the initial shock, the man lowered his hand, studying the pool of blood, before shifting his gaze onto the boy.  
​  
Slowly, the man’s mouth shivered; it crept; slowly it inched across the man’s lacerated face, blood trickling down from each crevice.   
​  
“Thats truth…for the first time you’ve reanimated my fervor…”

Tears welled in the man’s eyes, which fell onto streaks of blood.  
​  
“I’m so happy I had you.”  
​  
Grabbing the boy’s shoulder, he raised one arm to the sky, then released it into the boy’s face. Over and over again his knuckles split open, bleeding, fingers twisting and cutting into his hand; over and over again the boy’s face spread across the room and over, as his bones pierced the man’s flesh.  
​  
He stopped when all that remained was the smashed bones of the boy’s skull and the darkened mass of fat that outlined them.  
​  
He stood back up, staring at his boy, then at the face left on his tattered hand.  
​  
He took one finger and licked a single clump of flesh, leaving it for a short length before his shoulders fell.  
​  
“I’ll carry you to salvation now. We’ll all make it.”  
​  
Something other than blood fell onto the hand he extended. A few droplets escaped the sides of his face and mixed with the boy’s pulp.  
​  
He remained standing, his body swaying ever so slightly.

 From around the corner of his eye, shone the door previously left open.

A weaker smile quivered across his face before he started towards the light, his legs straining to keep steady. 

Reaching outside the door, he was blinded. The ground below greeted him with each step, sinking his boots, as a wind blew across his face and the scent of blood thinning.

The world was so bright until it slowly gave way, the sun itself expanding to cover more and more expanse.

His heart draining, the man felt the warmth of a mother.  
​  
“O’ lord, won’t you gaze upon us all?”
