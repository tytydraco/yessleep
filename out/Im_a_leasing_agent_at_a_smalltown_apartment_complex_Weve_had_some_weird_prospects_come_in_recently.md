3 Months ago, I started as a leasing agent at a small apartment complex near my place. It's a small team here, composed of just me, our community manager, Ava. She's pretty nice to meet at times, but she is *very* pushy about community policies. Then, there's our Assistant Manager, Demi, she kinda just hides in her office all day, occasionally sending a Microsoft Teams message asking, “How many leases can we get today, team?!” Followed by some cheesy gif. And finally our maintenance techs, Matt and Paulo. I barely see them throughout the day, but in return, we rarely get any maintenance complaints. 

We all started at the same time, since our parent company took over from the previous owners. The community itself isn't half bad for Middle-of-nowhere Texas: Garden style, recently constructed (if you consider 2010 recent), Nice views of the city or the backside of the local bar. Plus, we have some of the best rates in the area, so you would think that people would be beating down the door to get in. Well, that's what corporate would like to think at least . In reality, it’s pretty much been a ghost-town for almost my entire time working here.

Then yesterday, at 5:30 on the dot, the Carter family came in.

They looked like your average Texan family: Dad was tall, covering a balding head with a “Don’t tread on me” trucker hat, a greying mustache sat above his dip filled mouth. Mom’s poorly dyed blonde hair dangled almost in line with her turquoise earrings. Then there was Junior, who moped around behind his parents while they talked.

“We’re looking for a 3 bedroom.” Dad muttered. His voice was hoarse, almost like a wheeze or a whisper.

I put on a cute little smile, hoping it covered up my displeasure in such a late tour, especially right before the holidays, “Sure thing! We have a couple different options to choose from: Are y’all looking for a--”

“We would like to tour an available 3x3” Mom rasped, cutting me off, her voice was worse off than Dad’s.

‘Rude.’ I thought as I continued my professional little spiel.

“Gotcha! Do y’all mind if i take down your names and contact info so we can get in touch later?”

They stared at me, not saying anything.

Up until now, I hadn't really made eye contact with them outside of a few glances as I fumbled around my desk. But when I finally met Dad’s gaze, I jumped a little.

He stared through me with glassy, bloodshot blue eyes, Mouth hanging open, making the clump of tobacco in his lip all the more obvious. Mom looked the same.

They kept eye contact with me as they both reached into their back pockets, nearly in perfect unison. Each of them pulled out a Louisiana ID, and slapped it on my desk.

I read over them, trying to wipe the grainy film that coated them off of my fingers, Both expired over 5 years ago. 

“Sorry guys, but these seem to be expired. We’d need an up-to-date Photo Identification in order to conduct a tour!”

They continued to stare at me, silently.

Now, this community isn't my first, I've had plenty-a-prospect or resident give me the ‘stare of a thousand deaths’ as a former CM called it, but this was the first time that it looked like my words didn't register to someone, like, at all… it was kinda offputting. 

“Is everything alright?” I asked, growing concerned.

“Sorry about them,” A grainy, weak voice spoke up from the lobby, it was their kid. “They drove all night to get here.”

He shuffled over to the desk, flipping his black bangs out of his brown eyes,  stuffing something that I assumed was his phone in the back pocket of his black skinny jeans.

The young man pulled out his own ID, and handed it to me: 22 years old as of last week. Kinda cute.

Typically, everyone interested in touring needs to have an ID on file, but it was 5:30 on the last day before thanksgiving, and I couldn't have cared less. At least I tried.

“Alright, my name’s Ellowyn and i'll be giving you your tour today…!” 

We started with the amenities. As I pointed out all 3 of the— mostly broken—weight machines, the parents stared straight forward, while the son asked all the questions.

“So is this part open 24/7?” He asked.

I nodded, “Yep, all of our amenities are open all day every day--with the exception of the pool  
Which is open 10-10 every day.”

He nodded along, “Nice, mom likes her late night walks so that’ll be perfect.’

Trying to build a rapport like any good leasing agent, I looked over to his mom, “Oh, nice! Well look no further, mom! Our treadmills are the perfect place to get those steps in!” I gestured over to the machine. She continued to stare off into space.

The son’s pale face had turned red. He stepped up close to whisper to me.

“She’s uh…sensitive about her weight.” He hissed.

I glanced at him, then to his twig of a mom. ‘Really?’ I thought. 

Walking the model unit wasn't much better. Being courteous I opened the door and let them in first, which I immediately regretted.

When they walked by, my nose was assaulted by the smell of ammonia, shit, and spoiled chicken. I don't know how, but it didn't register to me just how dirty and gross-smelling these people were. Well, the parents, The son, smelled like expensive cologne, kinda like a nice Dior or Chanel (I'm a perfume enthusiast, sue me). 

Walking in behind them, I noticed that the son’s hand started to twitch, 

‘Poor kid probably has some form of Parkinson’s or something’ i thought to myself as the parents each shuffled into separate rooms of the unit. 

The son continued grilling me.

“How much is the rent?”

“For this unit, $1580 a month before fees and utilities”

“Is water included?”

“While some places in the market may offer that, our residents are responsible for their utility billing”

“Okay, do yall have security cameras?” 

That one stopped me in my tracks, but I gave the best answer I could:

“Unfortunately, we only have cameras by the common areas, but you are more than welcome to install a doorbell camera.”

He smirked, yellow, jagged teeth poked out from his lip, “Nice, good to know.”

A gentle thumping started in the farthest back bedspace. Worried that one of the parents might be tampering with something, I speed-walked over to find the dad tapping mindlessly at the back wall of the walk-in closet.

“Do you…think…a safe would fit…in here?” I could barely make it out through the gurgling in his throat.

Instinctively, I cleared mine, “Absolutely! Residents are more than welcome to have a safe in their unit!” 

The man nodded, his head jerked back and forth, kinda violently, a toothy grin crawled along his dirty, pale face. 

The glob of dip packed into his cracked lips fell onto the carpet below. He stayed frozen, still smiling. 

I did my best to keep it professional as I slowly looked down to see the clump of spit-soaked tobacco as it oozed it's juices into the carpet. Right as I was about to say something along the lines of “oh don’t worry, accidents happen,” I hear a loud

“UGH, DAD!” The son stormed into the closet, shoving his father, whose mouth stayed stretched, and eyes remained locked on the wall behind me, out of the way. 

He pulled out a stained red satin handkerchief from his jacket, and picked up the tobacco.

“So sorry about that,” he nervously chuckled. “He always forgets that he has a lip packed, isn’t that right, dad?” 

He nudged his father right in the beer-gut…hard. The dad let out a wheeze, still smiling. 

My stomach sank, something felt really off about the dynamic here. That, along with the flurry of odors coming from this guys Dad were beginning to get to be too much for me.

Then, the mom shuffled into the room, “Ugh…” it sounded like wet sandpaper being run over gravel, “Bill…told you…no dipping…” my nose began to sting.

Her head turned to face me. I could’ve sworn I heard her bones cracking as she did. 

“Sorry…we should…be leaving now…” She grunted.

‘Thank god,’ I thought, but before I could even let a single word out, the son chimed back in.

His hands were in his pockets as he damn near body checked me on his way out of the closet, “great idea mom, let’s go.” 

I could still see his hands twitching through the purple and black striped fabric as he walked by, and ushered them out, apologizing profusely about the trouble. 

As his parents jittered into the front seats of their rust bucket of a car, the son called out to me,

“We’ll be in touch!” He then pinched his fingers together, and  twisted his hand. His dad started the car. 

I called Ava as soon as the vehicle jerked out of the parking lot, and told her everything.

“Hey, if they wanna sign, let ‘em! That’s more commission for you!” Her voice barely intelligible over what I guessed was the hoots and hollers of the local bar’s patrons. 

I sighed, “yeah, I guess you’re right. See you after the holidays…”

I closed up the office for the day, and made my way home. 

I flopped down on my couch, turned on the news for background noise, and began my nightly doomscrolling. 

I was around halfway through reading a twitter post about the latest hollywood drama when I decided to glance at the TV.

The reporter was talking about someone’s kid being alive but missing, while a picture of a very familiar looking family floated over his shoulder. Big, bold letters at the bottom of the screen read:

 “In the morgue one day, gone the next: The mysterious case of Two loving Louisiana Parents gone missing”
