**My wife, Melonie, and I struggled with infertility for five years. We’re both in our early thirties, have been married for six years, and were finally ready to make our family a little bigger. We had recently moved into a new home. It’s a four-bedroom house with a big basement and a wraparound porch, right in the middle of the country. Pretty much everything we’d ever wanted. Mel and I own a business making bird calls. Sounds strange, I know, but it’s actually how we met.**

**Ten years ago, I was working in the city and needed a break. I’ve always loved birdwatching, so one afternoon I decided to get away from work for a while. It was practically a rom-com meet-cute waiting to happen. I spotted her with her goofy-looking binoculars and decided to strike up a conversation. I asked her what her favorite bird was. “The yellow-billed cuckoo,” she answered. Mine was the blue jay. She smiled hard and her bright eyes caught the sunlight.**

**I asked if she’d meet me there again the next day, same time. She said yes, and it’s been me and her ever since. She was my rock when my mom passed away. Mom and I used to sit around for hours at my childhood home, making bird calls and seeing what we could get to answer us. I miss those days. I miss her. Losing my parents was devastating, but their passing also meant they left me enough money to turn my hobby into a career. It gave me the opportunity to do something I loved instead of spending my life working a job I hated.**

**Well, with this big house and nobody in it, we decided it was finally time to try for a baby. Three months. I know that’s not long, but Mel was already worried about being “too old.” She didn’t want to be changing diapers when she was forty. We had considered just about every option and were almost ready to give up when we decided to take one more test.**

**Two blue lines.**

**Mel was over the moon. Before I knew it, she had me working through a whole list of honey-dos for the nursery. Bird-themed, of course. I paint all of our bird calls by hand, so she asked me to paint some of our favorites on the nursery walls. Her favorite, the yellow-billed cuckoo, and mine, the blue jay. We added a few woodpeckers and purple martins, too. Everything was perfect.**

**Mel was tired throughout the pregnancy, but healthy. Her doctor assured us the baby was measuring properly, and a couple of months ago, we finally brought our bouncing baby boy home. He’s got his mother’s eyes.**

**We settled on the name Finch.**

**Our days have been simple and lovely. We got pretty far behind on orders during the pregnancy, so I’ve had plenty of work waiting for me. I didn’t want Mel to worry about work. While Mel and Finch spend time together, I’ve been trying to get bird calls made and orders shipped as quickly as possible. I finally finished a big batch and decided to take a week off. I wanted to spend some more time enjoying my family. I have been working late and haven’t had much time to spend with them I eat my meals and sleep. She says not to worry but I’m being unfair to Mel and our baby.**

**My workshop is in a barn about a couple hundred yards from the house. From there, I can see the nursery window. Every now and then, I’ll catch a glimpse of Mel walking past it, rocking our sweet little Finch. She prefers to keep the windows open. “Fresh air is good for the baby,” she always says. I don’t argue as it’s spring it’s nice and warm. I wrap up my work and head inside. It’s still fairly early, just about lunchtime. Mel comes down from the nursery with a big smile on her face. I tell her about my decision to take a break for the week, and she’s clearly excited.**

**Finch spends some time resting while Mel and I enjoy an hour together. I noticed something today, though. Finch doesn’t cry very often, but when he does, it’s been getting awfully loud lately. Louder than I’ve ever noticed most babies cry. Mel thinks it’s normal. I’m a little skeptical, but who am I to question a baby’s cry?**

**Eventually, Mel heads upstairs to get him while I straighten up around the house. She comes back into the living room holding Finch, and when I turn around, something seems a little off. Don’t get me wrong. A big baby is a blessing, but he doesn’t look like a baby who’s only a couple of months old. He looks closer to a year old. He still looks like himself, just… bigger. I don’t want to give Mel anything else to worry about, and the last thing I want is for her to think I’m judging how she feeds him. So I keep my mouth shut. I’m going for now I need to make sure Mel and finch have my full attention. I’ll update soon.**
