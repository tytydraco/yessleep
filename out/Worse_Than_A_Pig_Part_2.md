When Kevin was born, I didn’t think I could ever be so happy. John was ecstatic, of course. A brilliant hands-on father. I thought the arrival of our baby would change him for the better. It did, at the start. A perfect father. He did all the night feeds without complaint. Kevin was the apple of his eye. That was ten years ago. Kevin was growing fast, tall, strong, just like his father. Too much like his father.

The other day, Kevin was sent home for hitting his classmate. I was furious, we both were, but it was something John said that got under my skin,

“How dare you lay your hands on someone?” John scolded, hands on his hips,

“That’s not how I raised you.”

And Kevin, my once sweet boy who couldn’t say boo to a goose, raised his head, and without a hint of remorse in his eyes, asked,

“What about mum?” His voice high pitched with youth,

“Why do you hit her?”

My mouth went dry like it was filled with dust. I thought I kept that side of myself hidden, but children are perceptive I suppose.

And do you know what John said? He barely glanced at me, waving his hand dismissively,

“That’s different. Your mother asks for it.”

The world around me stopped. Bile rose within me, burning hot and poisonous. Is that how he justified it to himself? I didn’t say anything. What could I say when I saw my son gazing at his father with awestruck eyes? The way a worshipper gazes at their god, absorbing the bitter words like honey.

That was the beginning of the end.

My husband continued his onslaught, no longer hiding it. My son watched my face swell, and bruise, and never questioned it. He trusted his father, why would he lead him astray?

The witch hummed, “Alright, I will help you.”

Though her words filled me with hope, it turned bitter.

“What will you do to him?”

The witch was out of her chair, moving with a speed that belied her age,

“Never you mind,” she muttered, peering at strange, discoloured jars.

As I watched the old woman gather ingredients, just for me, a wave of gratitude washed over me,

“Thank you for doing this, for listening to me,” I bowed my head, throat tightening,

“No one seems to listen, so, thank you, again,” I couldn’t find the words to express my gratitude,

“I-I know I don’t matter, it-it really means a lot-,”

“Who said that?!”

I looked up, the witch’s eyes were flashing red. I cowered under her harsh glare, worried I had somehow offended her again.

I nervously shrugged at her words,

“It-It’s alright. I made peace with it.” My sigh was a little too heavy.

The witch wouldn’t let it drop though, “Who says you don’t matter?”

I shrugged again, my fingers playing with a loose thread,

“I’ve no friends, no family, I don’t have any money to fall back on. I’m-,”

I swallowed the lump in my throat, “I’m nothing.”

“I don’t care what he does to me, as long as my boy doesn’t end up like him.”

The witch scoffed, “And, what gave him the right to treat you that way?”

“Just because he chose you, it does not give him the right to treat you horribly. You are not his possession. You are a person. A person with your own thoughts and feelings, and he’s nothing more than a pig.”

The urge to speak up for him grew weaker,

“He’s not…all the time.”

The witch turned back to her pot, “I don’t care,” she tossed in her ingredients, “I’ll soon see to him.”

“Finish your tea,” she told me in a softer voice.

My mind went blank, taking tiny sips. Whether it was the heat of the fire, or whatever was in the cup, my eyes soon grew heavy. The cold earthen floor felt comfier than I first thought. The world became horizontal and I embraced the darkness.

It was a dreamless sleep, but my body still felt heavy when I woke. I blinked my bleary eyes, my vision filled with a glass bottle, a bottle that stank something fierce. The witch crouched in front of me, waving it in my face.

“It’s ready.”

I sat up, wincing as I did. The glass was painfully cold against my fingers. I went to drink it,

“No, no,” her clawed hand stopped me, “Give this to your man.”

I eyed the pink liquid, it was viscous, it looked like the bloody juice from raw meat. “What will it do?”

With a flick of her wrist, she pulled the shadows back around her. The darkness swaddled her like a baby.

“It will bring out the side he keeps hidden, the beast he tries to hide.”

I nodded, still unsure what that meant, but slid the vial into my pocket. I went to pull out cash, but her bony fingers stopped me.

“No need, I don’t do this for money.”

Before I could say anything else, the witch settled back into her chair with a creak, and waved her hand.

“Leave.” Her eyes flashed red again, “He’s waiting for you.”

I didn’t bother asking how she knew. What was the point?

I gathered my stuff and left. Back through the sharp roots, pulling away their clinging fingers, to reach the cool night air. The wind was like a slap to the face after the witch’s warm domain. I threw one last glance behind me. The entrance was just a dry clump of tree roots, tangled like a knotted ball of hair. There was nothing underneath. The witch didn’t want any more company.

I breathed in, head still swimming. The forest still felt dark, uneasy, but the heavy feeling was gone. Like a breath was finally released. I hurried home, the pain dogging my steps reminding me what had to be done.

I traipsed through the woods, the crackling footsteps a distant echo. I don’t remember the walk back to the house, lost in my thoughts. My heart jumped into my throat when I noticed the lights were on. Morning was fast approaching, the sky turning a dusky pink, and John was waiting for me in the doorway.

Despite my blood turning ice cold, the vial acted like an anchor in my pocket.

He stood there, a dark figure blocking the light. He watched me quietly, a smile on his face. When I met his eyes; they flashed like the side of a knife. I stopped inches before him, still blocking the doorway.

“Where’d you get off to?” He asked, showing his teeth,

I stilled my face, “Just on a walk,”

“In the middle of the night?”

“Yes. I-I c-couldn’t sleep.”

His strained smile somehow grew wider,

“And, did you not think to tell me you were going?”

He was losing patience. it had me stammering, something he hated,

“I-uh-you were asleep,”

“You could’ve left a note!” He snapped, “How hard is that? You know how to write.”

I flinched; the vial burning a hole in my pocket. John took a deep breath, eyes darting back and forth. Even now, he was still checking for witnesses.

He moved just enough to let me enter, clutching the door with white knuckles.

He didn’t mention my scratches, or my hair in disarray. He just wanted me back.

He took his place at the head of the table, shooting me a hard look. I knew what he wanted without speaking.

“What would you like for breakfast?” I glanced over my shoulder, feeling his cold eyes on me. In the span of a heartbeat, something in his eyes changed. It was the hungry look of a predator, and I was his prey.

“Eggs,” he answered coldly, I had to ask how he wanted them prepared. I had to know; it would be worse if I didn’t.

The moment the question left my mouth, I felt him behind me. I didn’t even hear him move. His hand was in my hair before I could react. Twisting my neck painfully so he could hiss in my ear.

“Scrambled.” He held me there for another beat, before finally letting me go. With my stinging scalp, I pulled out the eggs. John sat back down in his chair, back straight, hands clasped, never taking his eyes off me.

The pain fading into the background.

As the kitchen slowly filled with the sounds and smell of breakfast, I searched for an opportunity to use the vial, but I could feel John’s eyes on me, following my every move.

Luckily, a familiar pair of footsteps came scampering down the stairs. Our son rushed into the kitchen, still in his pyjamas, and clutching his bear. John turned, face breaking into a real smile. I took my chance and poured the vial into the coffee pot. The liquid poured like blood, but it carried the scent of wet earth, like everything the witch touched. I slid the empty vial back into my pocket, just as his eyes turned back to me,

“When are the eggs ready?” He all but barked, “You got two hungry boys here,”

Kevin giggled, “Yeah, mommy, hungry.”

“How can we get her to hurry?” John asked him, a devious look in his eyes, Kevin hummed thoughtfully,

“Say please?”

John snorted, “You can if you want, I’m going to do this.”

He then grabbed his knife and fork and thumped them against the table in an unsteady rhythm,

“Whhhy are weee waiting?!” He chanted, wild grin on his face, “Whhy are weee waiting?”  

Kevin giggled at his father’s antics, copying him in his own childish way. Even when I asked him to stop, John chanted over me.

Finally, breakfast was served. I patted Kevin on his curly head, and set down John’s plate, with more force than intended. He noticed, he always did. He grabbed my wrist,

“Thank you, dear,” he smiled, showing too many teeth again, “This looks delicious.”

I looked away, skin already crawling with the promise of more pain. I took my own seat, pouring me and Kevin a glass of juice. The coffee was all for John. I kept my attention on Kevin, wiping his mouth when he copied his father’s open-mouthed chewing. Out of the corner of my eye, I saw John reach for his coffee.

He took a sip, and stopped.

I waited, holding my breath.

John licked his lips, and gulped it down.

I breathed again. No going back now.

“What are you sighing about?” He asked, forkful of eggs halfway to his lips.

“Nothing,” I said, keeping my eyes on Kevin, “Just tired.”

“Course you’re tired,” he chuckled, “Going out gallivanting at night,”

Kevin laughed with him. He didn’t understand the joke, he was just copying his father.

“So, why did you go out walking?” He asked, scooping up more eggs. I watched him, searching for any miniscule change.

“Told you, I couldn’t sleep.” Were his movements getting sluggish?

“Bull,” John shot back, stopping himself from swearing in front of Kevin.

Were his eyes drooping? No. This is ridiculous. I don’t even know what I was supposed to be looking for.

“I want you,” John paused for another mouthful of eggs, “To tell me the truth.”

He shovelled more food into his mouth, barely taking in breaths. I shook my head,

“I am. I just went for a walk.” When was the potion going to work?

John just watched me, shovelling fork after fork into his mouth. He chewed loudly, waiting for me to say something.

I could only shrug, “Sometimes I can’t sleep. I don’t know-,”

Suddenly, a fist crashed down onto the table, shaking the plates.

“You’re lying,” he spat, spitting something wet onto my face. A fleck of egg stuck to my bruise. Kevin whimpered at the noise, feebly slapping his little fist to copy him. I gently shushed him, taking his small hand in mine. He didn’t pull away.

I had been avoiding it but the heat I felt earlier returned. I went to look John in his eyes, they gleamed back at her, rimmed with red.

“Why would I be lying?” I asked, confused.

Was this aggression a side effect of the potion?

Yellow spit dribbled over John’s lips, “Oh, I know why,” he made no move to wipe it away, “You’re cheating on me.”

I couldn’t help it; I barked out a surprised laugh.

“What? Don’t be stupid.”

I shouldn’t have done that. He slammed his fist again, knocking his plate to the ground.

He swore, “Now, look what you made do!”

My fault, of course.

Kevin finally burst into tears.

“Shut up!” John shouted at him. Kevin looked at his father with wide eyes. He had never spoken to him like that, that kind of talk was for mommy. John reached for him, his hand big enough to smother him.

“Don’t you dare.”

Once again, that strange fire blossomed within me, burning through my veins, fuelling my ire.

“You can do whatever you want to me,” I spoke each word strong and clear, “But you will not lay a finger on my son.”

I didn’t raise my voice; I didn’t need to.

The words cut through the atmosphere like a knife through butter. John blinked, looking at me as if I was a stranger. His mouth hung open in shock, half chewed food spilling down his chin. Slowly, he got up from his chair. The screech set my teeth on edge. He loomed over me, over us. I felt Kevin shiver behind me. It must have been like David and Goliath to him.

When John spoke next, it was like a bomb going off.

“How. Dare. You?! How dare you talk to me like that?” His nostrils flared with every angry breath.

“After everything I’ve done for you! This is how you treat me?!”

The urge to cower reared its head, the urge to placate him, to quell the fire, but I refused. Not anymore.

“John, I love you,” I began, shielding Kevin with my body, “But this isn’t working.”

“This isn’t how you treat your loved ones,” tears stung my eyes,

“I wish you could’ve talked to me, maybe we could’ve got through this, but not now. You’ve pushed me too far.” I took a deep breath, all the words I longed to say for years finally pouring out of me.

“I want to thank you for marrying me, and for Kevin, but that’s it,”

I then said the words I never fully believed until now.

“I don’t deserve to be treated this way.”

“We’re done.”

I stopped, breathing hard like I just ran a marathon, but I felt lighter. Kevin still clutched my arm; he was my new anchor.

John stared at me, his face a deep red. Drenched in sweat, his bulging eyes flickered from me to my feet for some reason. He opened his mouth; I braced myself for the outburst.

Instead, John fell to his knees. I thought it was a heart attack, and stepped back. I saw my husband on all fours, face down. Was he prostrating himself? Was this some extreme show of remorse?

No. With his face planted to the floor, he was sifting through the remains of the breakfast, scattered on the floor. He hoovered up the eggs, along with the broken plate pieces. The wet crunching emanating from him made my eyes water. His heavy breathing of anger devolved into wheezing. John looked up, eyes darker than before. In shock, I could only watch as he spewed up red, frothy spit.

“Daddy?” Kevin asked, fearfully, peering around my legs, “What’s wrong?”

At the sound of his voice, John tried stumbling back to his feet. He was unsteady, legs trembling like he was walking on a tightrope. He clutched his face, wheezing through his fingers. I caught one last glimpse of John’s eyes through his spread fingers, wide, unblinking, filled with a kind of fear that was contagious.

John suddenly charged forward. Kevin and I moved just in time as he slammed into the cabinet.

The sound he made, the only way I could describe it, it was like a dense thud, followed by a sickening, wet crunch. Blood exploded across the cabinet door. He made a garbled sound, like he was yelling through a mouthful of syrup.

With a snort, he wiped and rubbed at his ruined face, a red, fleshy mess where his nose once was. He snorted out a thick glob of dark blood, revealing two black holes. John turned to me, his once cruel eyes brimming over with tears.

“What’s happening?” He rasped, clawing uselessly at his ruined face. He had no idea; all he knew was the extreme bone breaking pain. With another bloody groan, he hunched over from a violent coughing fit. Red splattered against my white linen floor. Kevin burst into tears behind me. I longed to turn and comfort him, sweep him into my arms, but I couldn’t take my eyes off my husband, or what used to be my husband.

With another groan of pain, he bent forward until I thought he’d snap in two, still making that horrible, wheezing sound. His skin grew redder, and redder, like a beetroot. I thought it was simply more blood, bursting from the pores of his skin.

The mass on his face stretched and flattened itself into a snout. His fingers melded together like clay, tapering into fleshy nubs. He was nothing more than a hulking mass of pink flesh. My son and I watched in horror as John threw back his red, shiny head, and released an ungodly squeal.

It was worse than nails on a chalkboard, worse than glass crunching under a wheel. It was the sound of a demon trapped on earth.

I covered Kevin’s ears but it still sent him into hysterics. Without another glance at us, the transformed John barrelled out of the kitchen, knocking over the table. Plates crashed to the floor; a chair splintered like matchsticks. Like a tool of destruction, he was gone, leaving only a trail of chaos in his wake. Only when the squealing grew fainter, did I finally turn to Kevin. His little face was a mess of tears and bubbling snot.

“Wuh-What-,” he sniffled, “W-What was that?”

“Was-Was that daddy?” He dissolved into sobs, the emotions too big for his small body.

All I could do was nod, my mouth wouldn’t work. How could I even explain what happened?

Then, a commotion came from outside, people screaming. I told Kevin to go hide in the bathroom, the only room with a lock, and hide until I came for him. He nodded, tripping over his feet in his haste. I ran out the front door, following the cacophony. More horrified screams, followed by blaring horns. A crowd was gathering in the middle of the street. I knew why before I even saw him. There was John, flailing and bleeding like a stuck pig. His thick folds quaking with every lumbering step. John squealed again, dragging his useless hind legs behind him as he rooted through trash. Snuffling and snorting, snapping at anyone that got close to him.

Through the gap in the crowd, I watched John rear up on his knees, rising like a whale breeching the sea, and brought down his clubbed fists on a car. He did it again, and again, even when the driver escaped, mouth open in a silent scream, John continued to pummel the car, squealing in a mixture of pain and anger.

My bruises ached just watching.

The windows shattered, glittery shards of glass rained down on John. Each miniscule cut threw him into another wave of squealing.

People came out their houses to witness the chaos, some covered their mouths in shock, others hid their children’s faces from the sight.

“John, what the hell are you doing?!” Someone shouted, I recognised her, one of John’s oldest patients, Mary.

John twitched, turning his monstrous head to stare her down. His eyes were fully black. Steam wafted from his body with every heavy breath. Before anyone could react, John charged at the old woman, knocking her to the ground. The old woman’s screams joined the cacophony of John’s squeals. Someone rushed to Mary’s aid, but John’s sharp teeth kept everyone back.

I watched it all happen, numb; it’s like I was watching outside of my body. Finally, John released Mary who scurried from his reach like a rat. He returned to his chaos, uncaring of the witnesses, finally showing everyone who he really was.

I didn’t see the car coming, maybe I did. It was too late for me to do anything.

John let out his loudest squeal yet, high pitched, the death throes of a dying animal. His black eyes swivelled in his head one last time, lips stretched back in a sneer, his swollen tongue lolled from his slack mouth. The car barrelled right into him. He died on the spot. Felled like a massive tree. His body shuddered; he let out one final pathetic squeal before falling still. Stuck in this beastly form, naked for all to see.

Instead of first aid, or calling for an ambulance, the townspeople gathered around me. Their exclamations a different sort of cacophony.

“I can’t believe it. I just can’t believe it.

“John seemed like such a nice man.”

“How could he do that?”

“Why didn’t you say anything?”

“Are you alright?”

The words washed over me, their pitiful faces a blurry wave of shapes. I couldn’t take my eyes off John. He deserved this fate, and yet, despite everything, I couldn’t stop the spark of sadness welling up within me. He was still my husband. I loved him, and I’m glad he’s dead.

Something dark flickered at the tree line, I looked up to see a pale floating face, watching the scene with thinly veiled interest. When she caught my eye, she gave a single nod, and sank back into the darkness.

I never saw her again after that.

The town got rid of John’s body for me. Mary told me they left him at the mouth of the forest, an offering to keep the witch at bay. She accepted it. I don’t know if that was meant to reassure me, it didn’t.

Kevin took longer to get over it, but he’s back to being my sweet boy. He never raises his voice, or his fists. He asked about his father only once, and I told him the truth, he deserved it. His father was a bad man, he loved us, in his own warped way, and he’s gone forever.

He seemed satisfied, he still suffers the odd nightmare, oh, and he won’t eat bacon anymore, but that’s alright, more for me.
