My Great Aunt Mildred wasn't exactly a social butterfly. She lived alone for decades in a sprawling old Victorian house on the outskirts of town. When she passed away last month, I was the only family member left who could inherit it. I'd only visited her a handful of times as a kid and always remembered the house being strangely dark and full of heavy furniture. The biggest oddity though, was the distinct lack of mirrors.

  
Now, as the new owner, I've had more time to explore. And it's true. In a house with probably twenty different rooms, there is precisely one mirror. A small, oval-shaped thing in the downstairs powder room. It's old, with a tarnished frame, and even then, it's angled slightly downwards, making it hard to see your whole reflection unless you really stoop. I found this peculiar and mentioned it to my friend, Sarah, when she helped me move in a few weeks ago.

  
"Maybe she was just vain in a very specific way?" Sarah joked. "Only wanted to check her lipstick or something."

  
I laughed it off, but the thought stuck with me. Why only one mirror? And why such an awkward one at that? It wasn't like the house was devoid of other reflective surfaces. There were plenty of polished wood tables and glass cabinets, but no actual looking glasses.

  
I started spending more time in the powder room, studying the mirror. It had a strange ripple effect to the glass, making my reflection seem a little distorted, a little stretched. My eyes looked a bit too wide, my smile a bit too thin. It was unsettling, but I figured it was just the age of the thing.

  
Then things started to happen. Small things at first. I'd be brushing my teeth in the main bathroom upstairs and swear I'd caught a glimpse of something moving in the periphery of my vision, just behind me. When I'd turn, there'd be nothing. Or I'd hear a faint whisper from down the hall, like someone calling my name, only to find the house completely empty.

  
One evening, I was feeling particularly on edge. I had just finished dinner and was tidying up the kitchen when I heard it again. A distinct, whispered "Hello?" from the living room. My heart hammered against my ribs. I knew for a fact I was alone. I crept towards the living room, a heavy fireplace poker gripped in my hand. Nothing. The room was empty, the shadows dancing in the dim light.

  
As I turned to leave, my gaze fell upon the small mirror in the powder room, just visible from the living room doorway. And there, reflected in its distorted surface, was a figure standing behind me. Not my own reflection. Someone else. Taller. Gaunt. Their head tilted at an impossible angle, a wide, unsettling grin stretching across their face.

  
I screamed. Dropped the poker with a clatter that echoed through the silent house. I spun around, expecting to see something there, some ghastly intruder. But there was nothing. Only the empty space behind me.

  
I fled the house that night, spent it at Sarah's. She tried to convince me it was stress, my imagination playing tricks. But I knew what I saw. And I knew where I saw it.

  
I went back to the house the next day, in broad daylight, armed with a hammer. I walked straight to the powder room. The mirror seemed to shimmer, almost mockingly. I raised the hammer and brought it down. The glass shattered into a thousand jagged pieces, scattering across the sink and floor. The frame cracked, splintering the old wood.

  
A sickening, high-pitched wail echoed from somewhere deep within the walls, a sound that made my teeth ache. It lasted only a moment before dying into an unnerving silence.

  
I haven't put another mirror in that house. I won't. And I won't ever again trust what I see in reflections, especially when I'm alone.
