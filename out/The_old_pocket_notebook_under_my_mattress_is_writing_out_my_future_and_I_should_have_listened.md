I’m a writer, and I usually write poems because writing has become a necessity for me lately rather than just a passion. I’ve written over a hundred poems and have a hobby of maintaining pocket notebooks. I started this particular thin notebook, which has less than fifty pages, on the 1st of January. I didn’t keep it on me at all times; instead, I used to slide it right under my mattress. By April, I completely forgot about its existence. Its condition deteriorated a bit over the months until I accidentally came across it again on the 15th of September.

I gave it some thought and decided I should complete it. I started writing some usual, mundane stuff about life. But when I leafed through the pages, I saw that a page I had written on February 13th was now completely blank. That left me utterly in shock. I remembered exactly what I had written. The only thing I forgot back then was to timestamp the entry in my notebook on that particular date. Confused and unsettled, I closed the notebook. I tried not to stress about it too much and shoved it back under my mattress.

After I dozed off watching a movie, something changed. A freezing, disembodied voice whispered directly into my ear. I woke up terrified, my heart pounding against my ribs, and stared out the window for what felt like hours. Paralyzed with fear, I finally forced myself to go to the kitchen, drank a glass of water, and came back to my room to lie down.

The exact words that were whispered kept running through my mind. I couldn't get them out. Driven by a sudden, desperate urge, I pulled the notebook out from under my mattress. As I flipped the pages, I realized new words were actively appearing on the paper.

The ink bled into the page, forming a terrifying command: “At 2:15 PM, do not answer the door.”

Because I was confused and rationalizing my fear, I ignored the weight of the warning. At exactly 2:15 PM, someone knocked. I didn’t open the door fully. Instead, I peeped outside through the small peephole in the middle of my front door. We have a double-door setup, and both of them are heavy, metallic welded iron doors. I saw absolutely no one through the hole. I decided not to open it anyway, knowing the iron doors make loud, echoing squeaks that would wake the others up and cause unnecessary panic in the house.

I went back inside my room, looked out the window, and froze. Moving through the darkness, I saw the stark silhouette of a human figure—a shadow of someone hiding right inside the bushes. Knowing something was terribly wrong, I frantically drew the curtains and shut the blinds.

In a sheer panic, I checked the notebook one last time before trying to force myself to sleep. There was a fresh entry on the exact same page where my original handwritten thoughts from February had been erased. The old graphite was completely gone, replaced by these new entries that I had never written or even conceived. I stared at the blank space where my memory used to live, my mind racing.

But the true horror began just an hour ago. As I watched the paper, new words began to form from underneath the fibers in shaky, erratic handwritten letters. It wasn’t my handwriting. It was a terrifying warning for today, September 16th.

The ink is still drying, and it tells me exactly what will happen to me if I leave my bed at midnight. I am trapped under my sheets, watching the clock tick closer to 12:00, and I don't know what's waiting for me if I move.
