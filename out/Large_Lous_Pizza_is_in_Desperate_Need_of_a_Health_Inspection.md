Let me make one thing abundantly clear. Do not, I repeat, DO NOT. Come to this restaurant. You will probably get sick, and then explode, with your entrails decorating the walls. Walls which will never be cleaned, and then I will have to stare at your exploded entrails, and I will throw up, and it will be your fault that I threw up, and I won’t even be able to be mad at you. Because you exploded.

Okay, you won’t actually explode. In fact, I haven’t even seen a hint of nausea in any of the patrons here. There’s just so much gross shit here that I want to make this post in hopes that somebody, somewhere, sees this post and can contact the right people to get my boss to implement better health regulations. 

I’ve been working here at Large Lou’s Pizza for about two years now, and I have yet to see a health inspector walk through the front door. I let it slide at first, and then when the weird shit started to pile up, I wrote it down on a notepad. That way, when a health inspector did finally arrive, I could whip it out in front of them like, “You don’t even need to look, I’ve got the worst shit right here.”

But I’ve never seen a health inspector. Not even an upturned nose at how our churros dispense from the back of the soda machine. Or a sidelong glance from a wandering customer as they catch me scraping rust off the dumpster into a cup.

So I’m resorting to Reddit, since getting the word out in any way possible will do more than nothing at all. Here’s some of the stuff I’ve found so far.

**One: The “Vegetables”**

Our vegetables have demonic sigils on them. At least I think they do. They’re not your usual upside down pentagrams. But they’ve got, like, a pattern to them. I haven’t been able to decode them, but Brent, who works with me in the back, has agreed with me that they look weird.

“It’s like some kind of alphabet or something.” Brent muttered. Rolling a medium carrot between his hands and taking a sip of YumYum Delicious. It was still early in the shift, so I gave him a slight breadth of space, as I could still smell the weed on him.

I could tell he had taken a little too much, and was zoning out as he pondered the carrot, so I chimed in, “I know, right? Should we try to decipher it, or something?”

Brent snapped back and threw the carrot into a third pan, “Help yourself, but I’m not doing that. Worst case scenario, you summon a demon. Best case scenario, you look like a dumbass. Also, it’s almost 10:00. We’re going to be opening soon, and there’s twenty more pounds of produce outside.”

I shrugged. At the time, it wasn’t really worth it to check. I’m not exactly the brightest bulb in the pencil box, anyway, and I valued my soul more than asymmetric symbols on cucumbers.   
Now that I think about it, and this is a little disconnected from the whole vegetable ordeal, there was something weird with the meat delivery too. This happened right after the shipment of vegetables had shown up, so it kind of counts.

Brent was late for work, and I asked Francine to help me bring the vegetables in. 

“Have you ever noticed weird stuff on the produce?” I asked Francine, as she slung a 30 pound box of cherry tomatoes onto a shelf in the walk-in fridge.

“Don’t know,” she grunted as she put back the boxes of old produce, “I usually spend my time working, instead of staring at vegetables. It explains why you two are so slow with the salads, though.” She made her way back to the front of the kitchen.

“Right, thanks for helping with those boxes, Franz!” I called, right as she disappeared from view.

Francine leaned back in, holding her cup of Woof’s, “Yep.”

I went outside to have a cigarette but had my smoke time cut short as the meat delivery arrived. We must get our meat from a local deli or something, because they have a very unique logo. It looks like the cross-section of an apple, but there are no seeds in the middle, and there are lots of line zig zagging about inside the mutilated apple. It’s also a gross shade of pinkish grey. But I guess that’s how a lot of raw meat looks, so it could be on purpose.

I still don’t like it.

I forlornly mushed my half smoked cigarette into the asphalt as the truck pulled in. The delivery man hopped out and handed me the invoice. I turned to place the sheet on the door, to have a flat place to sign, and by the time I turned back around, the delivery man already had boxes in hand for me to carry inside.

“You have a good day, pal.”

I took the boxes while handing him the signed invoice, “Thanks…”

He tore off the customer’s copy and handed it back, and that’s when I got the sudden feeling that something was off. Like not everything was where it was supposed to be.

I looked back up at the delivery driver, his back turned to me, as he approached the truck door, “Wait!”

The delivery driver quickly turned back around, “What,” huffing as he said it.

I pointed to the boxes of meat, which I had placed on the ground, then back at the invoice, “I’m missing three pounds of salami.”

The delivery man looked at his copy of the invoice, then down at the boxes. Then grumbled something in Hebrew as he marched back to the truck. I assumed I had done something to upset him, since as soon as he climbed into the back again, he began shouting unintelligible words at a rapid pace that most certainly were some of the most deplorable slurs that any one individual could muster. 

Loud bangs soon followed. But here’s the weird thing. Something was responding to the man and his cries. Deep, guttural moans, and wet slaps. Like a soggy towel being flopped on the walls of the box truck. After each slew of shouts, there’d be labored groans and meaty slaps in response. I honestly thought the delivery guy was having a stroke or something until he stepped back out of the truck with three more pounds of salami in a plastic baggy with the words “Thank you” printed about a hundred times in a gradient on the front.

He wiped some sweat off his brow and smiled towards me, “Sorry about that, that should be all.”

I quietly took the bag, and watched him slam the back of the truck closed, then hop in the driver’s seat, and drive off.

I picked up the meat and brought it inside. We were also short about a pound of pepperoni, but I wasn’t going to say anything after whatever kind of blow up I had just witnessed. It’s just disrespectful to treat your customers like that. Not to mention our meat is always a dark shade of purple. Which I think means the meat has gone bad, but we serve it up anyway, and like I said before, nobody’s gotten sick, at least to my knowledge.

**Two: The “Red Pepper Flakes”**

I used to love having red pepper flakes on my pizza. My usual lunch before working here was two cheese slices and a smattering of red pepper flakes on top. Especially since I had about 16 dollars to my name at any given moment, and cheese pizza was cheap and easy. 

Now, I’d rather gargle a glass of mustard than see a red pepper flake anywhere near my pizza ever again.

It was early on a Monday morning, me and Brent had gotten almost everything finished, and were sitting around on our phones when Linda came into the back of the kitchen, neither me nor Brent were able to put our phones away in time.

“Charlie, Brent, if you two need something to do, I’ve got a long list of tasks to keep you guys occupied.” Linda remarked, staring us down like a cat waiting for a mouse to stop playing dead.

Linda is our manager. For those who will need to describe her to the CDC, I will provide a brief description: She has straw colored hair that hangs down to her shoulders, and one of those people who looks like they could be anywhere between 35 and 50 years old. She has a scar on her right arm from a time Francine tried to carry far too many pizza screens from the dishwasher to the line, and narrowly missed burying Linda under them as a grease spot on the floor sent Francine and the pizza screens flying. Normal height, and usually a nice person to those who carry their weight, and ruthless to slackers. 

“Linda.” Brent remarked, as he shoved his phone back into his pocket.

I shot Brent a quizzical look. Brent looked at nothing, because he was high.

Linda grabbed two drink cups near the sink, ignoring the comment entirely and said, “We need a restock on pepper flakes, grab some bench scrapers and get to it.”

Brent did as requested, I followed suit, but still wasn’t entirely sure on what we were supposed to be doing. When we started walking outside and towards the dumpster, that’s when I caved and shot Brent a quizzical look.

Brent crouched down while saying, “What’s wrong with your eyebrow?”

“What are we doing outside?” I asked, as Brent started chipping away at some rust on the side of the dumpster.

“We’re collecting red pepper flakes.”

I laughed, “Dude, Linda’s gonna flip if she finds out you’re just giving her dumpster rust.”

It was Brent’s turn to give me a quizzical look.

I heard the door to the back of the kitchen open, and whipped around. Praying beyond all prayers it wouldn’t be who I-

“Brent, don’t let Charlie just stand and watch! Show him how to collect the seasoning. You two need to quit screwing around when it’s slow here.” Linda barked. Taking a long sip from a paper cup most likely filled with Blubblub (I swear I’ve never seen her drink anything else) and then going back inside.

That’s when it dawned on me, and I proceeded to get a wet lump in the back of my throat.

Brent showed me the ropes, and then headed inside, as he had already filled his cup, and told me he would show me the next steps after I filled mine. 

I had mostly gotten over the idea of how gross it was, and was left with putting up with the stench of rotting fruit, and other such trash related elements. It became almost therapeutic to chip the red crust off of the can, and into the cup.

I don’t think people realize how much rust can look like a well made croissant, and peeling back each layer of the metallic pastry felt like I was peeling back layers of stress off of myself.

I took a deep breath, no longer aware of the stench, but fully immersed in the sounds of oxidized dessert crunching, tonally blending with the mad mutterings of the homeless man behind the dumpster, staring at me while I worked, whom I had not been aware of for quite a bit.

“He chips, he chips so fast, I ran, I ran so fast. So fast. So fast. So clever. I’m so clever. I’m so fast, and I’m so clever.”

“What the hell!” I staggered back in surprise, dropping the cup, and spilling its contents across the asphalt.

The man went silent. I couldn’t see him, but I knew he didn’t run away, because I also didn’t hear any footsteps.

For a few moments, it was dead silent, save for the occasional cold gust of wind from the August air. Then, like a startled raccoon, the man crawled away. Quietly at first, but still muttering to himself. It was unintelligible at this distance, all I heard were the wet slaps of breathy lips against his tongue, but I’m sure it wasn’t much different than what I already heard. Then, he picked up speed, until I saw a dark blur disappear behind the bushes a few feet beyond the dumpster.

I bent down, and picked back up the cup of pepper flakes, and finished filling it up. This time, I went completely unbothered, but far from falling into the same trance as before.

Once inside, I learned that we don’t just use the rust as “red pepper flakes”. We actually use it as our sole seasoning in just about every single dish we produce. I thought this place just really liked paprika, but I guess that’s just what blended rust looks like. 

If the stuff I’ve talked about hasn’t grossed you out enough. Then I’ve got one more story. It takes time to get these compiled, so I’ll make another post as soon as I can, but this last one is easily the most disturbing of the three.

**Three: The “Churros”**

I mentioned them earlier, how the churros come out of the back of the soda machine. But I want to talk more about what these cinnamon turds look like, and how I discovered this production.

If you’ve ever had a churro before, I want you to create an image of a churro in your mind. The crispy ridges, browned and crunchy. The light, fluffy, and doughy texture on the inside of that scrumptious crust, all bubbly and airy. The cinnamon sugar dusting that brings the whole dessert together. The ideal pastry for any Costco Wholesale or carnival patron. 

Now imagine the inside was no longer bubbly. It’s completely filled in. Then remove the cinnamon sugar, and then unbrown the whole pastry. The only optical signifier that these beige bricks of sadness are churros is the ridges that still pinstripe the length of them. They still taste like churros, the texture is still mostly the same, a little grainier, like sand was added at some point, but not enough to notice unless you’re really thinking about it. That’s our churros. Smooth cinnamon turds of sadness. They kill with the customers.

Usually Brent and I work in the back: making salads, prepping ingredients, and cleaning dishes. Francine and Cezar work in the front: Making the actual pizzas, and taking orders from customers. It’s usually a decent system, as long as people actually show up to work on time, or at all. (*Marley, if you’re reading this, you piece of shit, stop missing shifts. Shirley’s pure soul does not deserve to be tainted by your laziness.*)

We have a soda machine near the POS system, which customers could access to get their own drinks, and it wasn’t your usual Coke or Pepsi dispenser. It was one of those weird secondary companies. The company had no name to be found on the machine, simply a logo that looked like a salmon skin colored kidney bean had been almost cut in half, with the two sides spread open like a book. 

The soda names are also weird, but having tasted all of them, these are my best guesses as to what soda flavor each one is.

Lemmy Limmy: A lemon lime type soda, kinda like Sprite or 7-Up.

Blubblub: Plain old flavorless soda water. (There is a syrup used for this one, but I’m not sure why, since it’s definitely *just* soda water.)

Woof’s: Root beer. Not a good one though, I’d even take Barq’s over that shit.

Hill Sprinkle: Very similar to Mountain Dew, but better. There’s like a spicy kick reminiscent of Mountain Dew’s “Flaming Hot” flavor that just brings a tear to my eye every time I take a sip. Everything else is a shoddy ripoff, but Hill Sprinkle makes up for the others for the most part. 

St. Andrew’s: Cola, because every soda brand and their mother is legally required to have their take on cola I think.

St. Andrew’s Zero: Diet cola, because every soda brand and their- you get what I mean.

YumYum Delicious: Cream soda, maybe? It kinda tastes like watery yogurt that someone farted in, but the thought was there, and I can’t think of anything else it might taste like.

On one particular day, Francine got a flat tire. So Cezar called me up front to help with the customers until Francine got there. Cezar spoke some English, but wasn’t the most fluent, so it was my job to talk to any of the customers ordering takeout, and then occasionally stretching dough.

I hadn’t figured out how to spin pizza dough in the air or anything cool like that yet, but I didn’t really want Cezar to show me, since it seemed like he was in his own little world while doing it. He would even say “wee” while the pizza was in the air. Like a toddler getting pushed on a swing. Especially odd when it was coming out the mouth of a Mexican man pushing fifty.

After the lunch rush had ended, there was a lull in the flow of customers, and suddenly the soda machine began lurching, and making a heavy groaning sound. Like a blender with constipation. A small hatch flipped open in the back from the shaking, and I backed away, afraid the thing might explode. Cezar flipped a switch on the bottom of the soda fountain, gave it  a hearty thump, and turned back to me, who was still confused, and a little scared.

“I need to empty the machine.” Cezar informed me, “Ayúda me, Señor!” Pointing to a halfpan on top of the pizza ovens.

I grabbed the pan, and handed it to Cezar, who held it under the hole where the hatch had flipped open.

The machine continued to groan, lurching more aggressively. If I didn’t know any better, I could have sworn I saw beads of sweat form on the sides of the labored machine. Finally, something off-white poked like a turtle’s head out of the hole. It was slow, and mostly solid. As it continued to push out, I saw it had ridges around the outside.

I fought back a familiar wet lump in the back of my throat, and watched in horror as smooth pieces of churro slid out from the soda machine, and into the pan. It felt like it was taking forever as more and more continued to emerge. The whole time I stood with my mouth agape.

At first they were dull plops against the empty pan, then muted, soggy, thumps as more layered upon each other. Finally, with a shaky groan, the churros finally stopped. Francine walked in, and grabbed a churro as Cezar handed the pan to me.

“Thanks for covering for me, Charlie.” Said Francine, through crunchy mouthfuls of soda fountain poop.

I said absolutely nothing, and placed the churros in the walk-in.
