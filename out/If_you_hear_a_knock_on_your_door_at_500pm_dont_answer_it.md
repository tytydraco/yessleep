I didn’t know where else to put this, but the group [r/justsmalltownthings](r/justsmalltownthings), seems the most fitting of my options.

I live in sweet. Literally, I live in the town of sweet, \[redacted\]. You could describe it as a small drive through town of about 1000 people, best known for our local honey. Ironic.

Save for one gas station, a surprisingly well stocked grocery store, and a radio station, that’s about all there is to see. I say radio station, it’s a dinghy shack that’s been in need of a new roof for 20 years that only broadcasts one station, the local news. Which in sweet, you can imagine is pretty dry. “Mrs.sanders lost her cat again, please be on the lookout,” and the like.

Except everyday at 4:00pm, when it broadcasts the same message like clockwork. “Please remember sweet family, if you hear a knock at 5:00pm, don’t answer the door.”

Not 4:59, not 5:01pm, not 5:05pm. Precisely 5:00pm. If you heard a knock at your door at 4:59pm and you decided to answer it, you’re in the clear, if you heard a knock at 5:01pm go right ahead. 5:00pm.

Growing up here, myself and my brother heard that message 1,000 times. Never paid any mind too it. Maybe we should’ve.

It was a Friday, the itch of the weekend was upon us. I had just gotten back from school after picking up my brother from his life skills class. He was older than me by quite a bit, but I always felt like the older brother, he was non verbal and my mom would always tell me “he’s gifted in other ways.”

As we walked in the door my mind was already on how we were going to spend the evening until my parents got back. Crossing the room I noticed a note on the fridge. “Your father and I have too work an extra shift at the grocery store, defrost the lasagna in the fridge for dinner, make sure you cut it into small bites for you brother, and don’t open the door at 5:00pm. -love mom.”

Standard note, I hated lasagna. I checked the clock 3:30. I could hear my brother in the background as I was taking the lasagna out of the fridge. Already preparing for his after school routine, a bowl of red m&m’s and turning on the local radio station. I never understood why he always liked to listen to that drivel.

I realize it’s 4:00pm, when I hear the announcement “Please remember sweet family, if you hear a knock at 5:00pm, don’t answer the door.”

Anyone in sweet will tell you, people start getting a little anxious around this time. Shutters close, people lock their doors, the streets get pretty quiet. I’d never personally known anyone who opened their door at 5:00pm. But I’ve heard the stories.

Nothing grisly, no murders, but enough to make people obey day after day.

Someone after opening the door starts walking and is never seen again.

A former school teachers husband was drunk one day and opened the door at 5:00pm not realizing the time, and had a heart attack on the spot.

I was at home when that happened, you could hear his wife pleading with him, or at least I thought so. “Please don’t, please, we can fix this, I’m sorry.”

Other than a drinking problem, healthy guy, early 20s. He lived, but was never the same after. Now he just sits on the front porch from morning till 4:50pm. He has a look in his eyes, one that you don’t ever want to have.

I stop reminiscing as I hear the oven beep, it’s done preheating. I check the clock again as I slide the oven in 4:50pm. Almost time.

I take a couple quick strides across the room and deadbolt the front door, and close the curtains. I debate if I should close the blinds that sit behind curtain….it should be fine.

Each minute that ticks by brings an increased level of anxiety, I feel each hair on my stand in edge. We just have to make it till 5:01pm. I steal a glance at my brother. His bowl of m&m’s is almost gone. I bring him the bag to sort through so he can pick out the red ones. He loves to sort them out. He flashed me a smile as I walk back to the kitchen. A man of few words, no words actually.

4:55pm, I need something to keep myself busy. I start pulling out plates, cups and silverware. Lasagna isn’t even close to being done, but I have to do something. It actually works and I get in a rhythm well all of a sudden I hear the m&m’s fall to the floor, they sounded like rain on the cheap linoleum.

“Miles, are you okay bud-?” I don’t get to finish that sentence. I turn around just as I hear the knock and see my brothers hand on the door. The knock itself sounded just like an old friend was knocking on your door, so inviting. Of course you’d want to open it.

I don’t know why but I still turn to the clock on the stove even though I knew exactly what time it is. My body will always know when it’s 5:00pm.

It’s too late. The door creaks open, and the room fills with static. That’s the best way I can describe it anyway. I didn’t even realize but I had dropped to my hands and knees, looking down I could see red spots of blood on the floor. I instinctively bring my hand to my ears and it comes back red.

I have to use the counter to help me stand up as it feel like gravity has increased by a metric ton. My brother is at the door his hand still on the door knob. He was gripping it so hard it started to dent. I tried to cry out but no sound would come out of my mouth and I couldn’t take a step even if I tried.

Something wanted me to look away, I could feel it. But I couldn’t, if I couldn’t help my brother I at least needed to see. I followed his gaze out the door just as a spindly white hand was reaching through the doorway. A sickly sweet odor appeared as more of this thing was making its way in. Rotting flesh.

The whole time it was whispering something to my brother, something it only wanted him to hear. Its gaunt figure was started to tower over my brother. I was covered head to toe in sweat, and about to pass out when my brother suddenly snaps his head towards me and says “run”.

Just like that the spell was broken and I tore out the back door as fast I could. I didn’t stop running until I got to the grocery store.

My parents took one look at me and knew. We slowly walked back towards the house. The front door was still open, and my brother was gone.

The evidence anything had actually happened was the door knob, the smell of burnt lasagna, and crushed red m&m’s.

We’re moving today.No more sweet for us. I took the door knob with me. As a reminder of my brother I guess. Typing this I’m still trying to process everything that happened. My parents don’t want to talk about it.

All I can say is, if you ever find yourself in a small town with a strange rule or set of rules. Follow them.
