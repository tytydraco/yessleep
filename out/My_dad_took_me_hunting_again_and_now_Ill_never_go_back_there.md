I had once thought that I had known pretty much all there was to know about the section of the Rocky Mountains where I had grown up hunting. I had thought there wasn’t anything up there that me and my dad weren’t prepared for. I was wrong.

 

My name is Eric, I was raised with my sister Alisa by my dad Daniel on the front range of the Rockys. I was back to living with my parents to save money while I worked and attended community college. My Dad and I used to hunt every year when I was younger, but after our first truck broke down we haven’t been back since. He recently got a new and bigger truck and wanted to go hunting again with me.

 

“Are you sure you want to go?” I asked my dad, my voice unsure as he packed extra clothing.

 

He didn’t look up and continued packing for a moment before he spoke. “Why not? I’ve already bought us tags. We’ve got the truck almost packed; your sister even made us some food for the trip.” He responded. My sister had never cared for hunting and preferred to look after the house while we were gone.

 

I sighed and looked over our guns one last time. Thankfully even a year or so away from them I still knew how to get them ready for use. “It’s just that…” I say, pausing a moment. “Things are a bit different now.” I finish quietly. Back then we had much more disposable income, it was going to hurt taking this amount of time off of work was going to hurt us both.

 

“We’ll be fine Eric.” My dad said with a smile, zipping up the large sack full of our extra clothes and standing up. I felt his hand on my shoulder as he passed by. “I’ll toss it in the truck and head to bed. Don’t stay up too late.”

 

With that he left me in the down stair living room. I finished prepping our guns and stored them in their cases, then placed them on the back seats of our truck.

 

I changed and hopped into bed, but I couldn’t sleep. I just lay there looking up at the ceiling. Before I had stayed up with excitement, now it was thanks to the worries of adulthood. After a few hours alone with my thoughts, I heard my dad rummaging around downstairs and got ready. Thick under clothing and a light layer of camo until we got up to our usual spot as always.

 

My dad and I did one last check to make sure we had everything, then we were off. Driving on the highway at 2 30 in the morning so we’d get to our spot just before dawn.

 

“Hey, do you still remember how to get there?” I asked, my eyes were feeling heavy since I didn’t sleep.

 

“Of course, I’m not that old.” My dad chuckled, seemingly not at all tired like I was. He started to reminisce about how this didn’t change much. I would always doze off as he drove us up there when I was younger. I guess that part didn’t change in 20 years. I turned away to look out the passenger side window, the sparse lights we passed by made my eyes feel heavier, and I dozed off.

 

I don’t know how long had passed when I woke up. I rubbed my neck to soothe the ache and yawned. It seemed we had made it, as snow and trees were all I could see when I glanced outside. I turned to my dad and expected to see him dozed off as well, but he wasn’t. Instead, he was wide awake, looking into the trees ahead of us and squinting.

 

“You see something dad?” I asked, looking to where he was and finding nothing.

 

My dad startled for a moment, not expecting me to be awake. “Jesus, kid.” He said with a shaky breath. “No, I didn’t see anything. You didn’t hear it?” He asked seriously.

 

I raised my brow at that. “Hear what? I just woke up.” I ask slowly. He and I had been up here so many times and ran into damn near every kind of wild animal too. What had he heard that got him to act like this?

 

He grunted and looked ahead for a few more moments and shook his head. “I’ve never heard anything like that before.” He said quietly, and the two of us sat in silence for a full minute.

 

“Well, whatever it was, let’s get our outer gear on and get the rifles out. I’ll put the chains on too, didn’t expect it to snow this much…” He said, getting out of the truck. I opened the door on my side and shivered as soon as the wind hit me.

 

It was cold, but I was used to it, though I could see my dad outright shivering in the cold on the other side of the truck. “Are you alright?” I ask, concerned.

 

My dad took a moment to himself, rubbing his arms before nodding. “Y-yeah. Just colder than I thought, must be getting old.” He spoke. My dad was on the wrong side of 50 as he called it, but still he was used to being up here in the cold. I guess old age could do that to you.

 

My hiking boots crunched the snow around the truck as I hurried to the back to get more layers on me. Getting out thick jackets and hunting overalls on didn’t take long, and as my dad began putting chains on the wheels of our truck I got our rifles out.

 

“Hey, maybe you heard bigfoot.” I joked with a smile, handing him his .30 ot 6. “Or a yeti, maybe we’ll bag ourselves a cryptid.”

 

I relaxed a bit when he smiled and grabbed his rifle. “Maybe, whatever it was I doubt it can take a few shots from these.” He chuckled, and I felt better that he wasn’t nearly as unsettled as before. I had never really seen him like that before.

 

By the time we were back in the truck the area was well lit by the sun. My dad seemed to be doing better, though he did have the heater on high. I was starting to regret putting all these layers on, but I could deal with it.

 

I took over the wheel as we headed up the mountain, the snow was quite deep in spots, but we eventually made it. Seeing a clearing with a nice view of the surrounding mountains reminded me why I enjoyed being up here. I believed nothing on earth could match the beauty of mountains after snow, and so far nothing had been enough to change that for me.

 

“Never gets old.” My dad muttered beside me, and I nodded. We drove forward slowly, our eyes scanning the fields and trees to try and spot any sign of elk. If could just get one, the meat would let us skip out on store bought meat for a while if we were careful with it.

 

We eventually found some tracks leading through a clearing into a tree line. They looked fresh and it was a place we had walked into before and knew the land fairly well. I parked the truck off the road a bit and we both got out, taking some extra bullets with us just in case.

 

“Want to split up? I could follow them into the trees, and you could take the path?” My dad suggested, and I pursed my lips. We had split up before and had success with it, but I could tell something was still bothering my dad the moment we turned off the truck and stepped out.

 

“Nah, we can both follow the path, then take a look through the trees on the way back.” I said instead. This way I could keep an eye on him and avoid any incidents.

 

My dad must’ve known what I was thinking and rolled his eyes. “Alright, we’ll do it your way. I swear you take after your mother a lot.” He grumbled, but I knew he didn’t mean anything by it. He still had carried a picture of our mother with him wherever he went.

 

We set off down the path, walking quietly down it as it twisted in between patches of trees and clearings. We stopped every now and then to just listen and look out.

 

“Wait.” My dad said suddenly. I stopped and readied my rifle as he did, crouching down into the snow.

 

“Elk?” I whispered, but he didn’t answer me. Instead he just looked around, his eyes snapping from one tree line to another.

 

“Dad?” I whispered again, looking along with him but seeing nothing.

 

“I…I could have sworn I seen something looking at us through the trees…” He whispered eventually. “Eyes blue like ice.”

 

I raised my brow and him and looked around again. “What? Like a wolf or something?” I ask worriedly. There might have been some wolves in the area, but I had thought they were still too far south for them.

 

My dad shook his head. “No, bigger than a wolf. Like a bear but…” He said, and then sighed. “Never mind, must have imagined it.” He said, standing up and putting his rifle back on his shoulder with its strap.

 

“Like bigfoot?” I say with a chuckle.

 

I couldn’t see my dad’s face but I could tell he was rolling his eyes again. “Whatever smart ass, come on.”

 

We trudged on, reaching the end of the path and with no sign of any elk. We decided to take a small break and rest on a log to the side of the path.

 

“Could still be some in the trees.” I think aloud, passing my dad a bottle of water I had brought.

 

“Maybe.” He said absent mindedly.

 

Once we were ready to go we stepped into the tree line, heading back to the truck. Thankfully there was a section that was relatively flat and easier for us to walk through. We were right about one thing, a herd of elk was here at one point today. We even found a broken off antler.

 

I picked it up and showed it to my dad. “Think Alisa would appreciate this?” I ask with a smile. My younger sister didn’t really care for this kind of thing and I knew it.

 

My dad shrugged. “if you want to carry it back I won’t stop you.”  

 

As soon as I had pocketed the antler I heard a sound I will never forget. It started out high pitched, then dropped down as it dragged on for several moments. Immediately I felt a chill run through my body, like the wind had cut through all the layers I had put on this morning.

 

“T-the noise!” My dad gasped, falling to his knees and shaking, his rifle falling of his shoulder to the ground. “That’s what I heard this morning. What the hell is that?” He asked looking around frantically.

 

“Dad.” I say through a shuddering breath. I put my hands on his shoulders to try and steady him. “Let’s just get back to the truck alright, come on get up.”

 

I helped him up, though his shivering remained constant as we hurried through the woods, uncaring if our noise drove away any elk. I could feel the cold settle in on my exposed skin, the hair on my face went stiff.

 

“S-so cold.” My dad muttered, and I huddled together close to him as we walked, trying to keep him warm.

 

“Just stay with me, the truck isn’t too far.” I try to encourage him. Thanks to the straight path we were following it wouldn’t be too long before we reached it, but every minute felt like hours. With each noise we made from the snow or cracking of a fallen branch felt too loud.

 

Both of us heard a snap in the woods somewhere to our left, and I yelped when my dad quickly readied his rifle and shot in the direction of the noise.

 

“What the fuck!” I yelled, until I saw it. A pair of icy blue eyes looking at us through an area of thick trees some twenty feet from us. They were much too large and were too high off the ground to be a wolf.

 

I was pulling my rifle off my shoulder to fire at it too when they vanished back into the trees as if it was never there without a sound.

 

“That’s what you saw earlier? Is that thing stalking us or something?” I pant, now jogging with my dad through the woods away from that thing.

 

My dad was panting hard, but he was still keeping pace with me, his rifle in his hands even though he was still shaking. “The truck, we get to the truck and leave.” He gasped out. I was damn near carrying him by the time we reached the clearing and could see the truck.

 

We hobbled as fast as we could and finally reached our sanctuary. We jumped in and I fumbled with the key, getting it in and turning it. Nothing happened. I turned it again, and again, but still nothing. My heart dropped as I got back out and looked under the vehicle. There was damage all over the bottom, with the last drops of oil and something else spilling onto the ground. We weren’t going anywhere with this truck. I got back up and looked at my dad, an unfamiliar expression of pure fear was on his face, as I’m sure there was on mine.

 

We were trapped.

 

Before I could get back in and talk about a plan with my dad, I heard that terrifying call once again. I felt as if the cold had seeped right into my bones, my limbs growing weak. I had to steady myself on the hood of the truck to keep myself from falling. In the distance I could just make out those icy blue eyes staring at us from the tree line.

 

I took a breath and raised my rifle, laying on across the hood and looking through the scope. I could make out a little more of whatever it was now, its body was mostly obscured by the trees, but I could tell it was massive and covered in a dark fur. Its neck was incredibly long, poking out through the trees. Its face seemed almost blank except for its eyes.

 

I fired, the shot going wide and striking a tree to the right of it. I fired again, this time breaking a branch next to it. I saw it rear its neck back and move back into the trees when I fired again, not seeing where this one hit. I kept looking into the trees for a moment just in case it came back. When it didn’t I got back in the truck and nearly cried. My dad was curled up in a ball, shivering violently and teeth chattering together loudly.

 

“H-Hold on dad.” I said desperately. I leaned the seat all the way down and dug into our supply pack. I got out our gas powered portable stove and got it set up in between the front and back seats. I knew it wasn’t a good idea to light it inside, but it was like we were freezing to death even though we were covered head to toe in winter gear.

 

I cracked open my window and lit the stove with a match. The fire lit up and I felt its warmth, though it felt muted somehow. It was as if the fire itself was trembling in the cold. Still, it was better than nothing.

 

“T-turn it on all the way, what are you waiting for?” My dad stuttered, getting as close as he could to the stove without getting burned.

 

“It is dad, this is as high as it goes.” I answered after checking it again. “We can’t have it much higher even if it could anyway. If the truck catches fire…” I say trailing off.

 

We were both silent for a while, just trying to get warmer. “We’ll have to wait for someone to come around, you fired three shots. Hopefully someone else is around here to help.” My dad muttered hopefully.

 

I nodded and looked outside, leaving was out of the question. We were much too weak to make it anywhere and even if we weren’t we’d never make it to one of the few houses we had passed miles away from here. Here in the truck, we at least had our supplies and shelter.

 

“We’ll stay put then.” I agree, though I still felt uneasy. A bear, mountain lion or pack of wolves wouldn’t have done that to our truck. It had to be that thing that was stalking us, but if it could wreck our truck then it could easily break through the windows too. At least our guns could drive it off.

 

I broke out our food as we waited, some grain bars and snacks. They were cold of course, but my dad had complained that it was almost like eating ice. The stove had kept us warm enough, but we had moved on to our last can of gas for the thing, it wouldn’t last much longer going at full strength. The sun was getting low in the sky, and still no sign of help. We hadn’t seen the creature since then either, but the unease was still there. Hours of sitting, watching and the cold was starting to get to us both.

 

“You should get some sleep dad.” I mutter, glancing at him. He had stopped shivering so much awhile ago thankfully, though I could tell he was still cold.

 

He shook his head tiredly. “Can’t. Might not wake up.” He said quietly.

 

“Don’t say that. We’re both going home.” I said firmly, more for myself than him.

 

Before he could answer, we heard the call once again.

 

This time I swear the cold was creeping into my soul itself, my body becoming numb and wracked with shivers. I felt as if my body could never again know warmth of any kind.

 

“N-No. Not again.” I pleaded, closing my eyes. Beside me I heard my dad make a fragile sound that broke my heart.

 

“W-why isn’t it working?” I heard him say through tears. I smelled something foul, and glanced his way, only to gasp. He had put his gloved hands directly into the stove top fire.

 

I quickly reached out and smacked his hands away. “K-keep it together dad.” I said as sternly as I could, but he wasn’t listening. His gloves were burning; I could smell the fibers and some of his skin which made me gag.

 

“S-still c-cold.” He stuttered, looking down at his hands as they burned. His head snapped up to the trees, where I saw those eyes again. “T-there.” He said shakily.

 

I grabbed for my gun, but couldn’t muster the strength to pick it up, or open the truck door. We were helpless. I stared back at it, hoping it would just be content to stand there in the trees when I heard rustling.

 

My dad was taking off his clothes, layer by layer. “W-what the hell are you doing?” I ask, gritting my teeth.

 

My dad looked at me as if I was stupid, the kind of look he gave me when I didn’t know what was wrong with a car. “C-can’t you feel it?” He asked and pointed towards the trees. “T-that’s the only warmth left, I have too.” He explained.

 

I grunted with effort, raising my hand to his shoulder, only for him to shake it off and remove the last of his clothing. “S-stop, dad please.” I begged him as he stepped out of the truck.

 

“Stop!” I yelled as loud as I could, honking the truck horn with my hand desperately. But my dad didn’t listen, he just walked slowly over to the woods, his shiver completely gone. “Please.” I sobbed, tears falling from my face as the last sight of him disappeared into the wood with that creature.

 

I sat there, helpless. Wondering if it would call for me again, make me follow it into the woods as my dad did. I barely even registered another vehicle pulling up, and a park ranger opening my door. She tried to talk to me, but I didn’t say anything, only choking out sobs and shaking. Eventually she took me out of the truck and into hers, then covered me with a blanket that did nothing to keep the cold away.

 

She brought me to the nearest town where they asked me lots of questions, many of which I couldn’t answer. They looked for my dad when I mentioned him, but even after several days all they found was blood and bones.

 

I returned to my sister, bringing the bad news. The cold seemed to follow me off the mountains. I still felt freezing even in the summer and in air conditioned buildings. I drove up our heating bill so much, and I couldn’t put my sister through it anymore. I moved as far away as I could to southern Arizona, but even in the summer I still feel a chill outside.

 

My sister calls often, but I can’t go back. I’ve had a long time to think about what happened on that mountain, and I still don’t know much. I feel the urge to go back every single day, either to avenge my father or join him. I miss him so much. I just wish he would have turned the truck around when he heard the call and never looked back.
