“I do not like the look of that guy over there.” Reggie said, his green form walked beside me, a large piece of metal sticking out of his middle. A memento from the car crash he had died in, he had assured me that it didn’t hurt. I looked past him to see the ghost he was talking about, the old man had been rather shy. That was odd, most spirits loved it when I saw them, they loved to tell me everything about themselves. I always understood though, some of these ghosts had been wandering the country for over fifty years.

“He’s probably just shy.” I said. “See those robes? They look old, he probably talks a little different from us too.” I squinted at the ghost again, offering him a wave. The bald man only stared back his dark black robes sitting still despite the cool fall breeze. Shrugging I kept walking, none of the ghosts had ever felt even remotely threatening before. This one would come out of his shell in due time.

Reggie adjusted his jean jacket, his denim tuxedo marred by the piece of steel that impaled him, dark blood soaked around the wound. “I don’t know he’s got a shit disturber feel to him.” Reggie said the h so light it sounded like he said ‘im. The Canadian accent used to be more severe back in the day I guess.

“You said that about Angie, and now you guys are best friends.” I replied, I didn’t really know what ghosts got up to in their spare time, but they seemed to make the most of it. Reggie shrugged,

“Yeah well she can appreciate a sixer of Kokanee like no one else can.”

“She’s from the forties, what can you possibly be talking about?” I asked, trying to not laugh at the ghost. Reggie shrugged again, flipping out a cigarette and lighter, somehow they worked and he seemed to have an infinite amount. “The joys of Kokanee, and tobacco.” he replied promptly. We kept the banter up as he accompanied me to school, Reggie always insisted on it. He loved following me just about anywhere, until he got bored or distracted.

“Morning Ellie!” a plump old lady jogged in unnecessarily tight pink running clothes. A heart attack had taken Betty in 95, and she was still trying to lose that weight. We waved back, Reggie cheerfully calling out to the living and the dead. People always averted their eyes whenever I waved, I was just the weird girl to everybody in town. That was fine by me, I had plenty of friends as far as anybody was concerned.

“Ready for another week?” a voice called out, I turned to see a large old man sitting on a bench. He wore a plaid shirt, and a torn up piece of jeans. Gerald’s most noticeable quality was the piece of wood that had impaled his left eye and drove up into his brain. “Got a smoke on you kid?” he asked. Reggie tossed him a dart and offered his lighter to the man.

“I hope so Gerald, I’m not looking forward to dodgeball though, I’m pretty sure those girls are picking on me.” I said, eyeing the doors of the school nervously, kids were shuffling in talking to one another. I caught a few glances my way. 

“Give em hell kid.” Gerald said, puffing smoke. “If you have to nail one of them in the face, hit that mean little ringleader if you have to.” He stood up and stretched as if he still had any purpose for it before giving me a wave. “Lets go Reg, we should see what Angie’s up to and our friend in the forest, he might like some company today.” The two spirits waved and then took off into the sky, puffing away on their cigarettes as they floated away into the woods.

I found myself wishing I could go with them, but Kihew didn’t always seem to appreciate the living in the same way the others did. He would come out of his shell in his own time. Taking a deep breath I hunched my back and walked into school. Despite being used to the stares from other people in town I always crumpled under those of my peers, if I could even call them that.

The stares were manageable to an extent, I just had to keep my head down and ignore them. The whispers were the worst, and I could always hear them. Small sharp words packed with hate, everyone passing judgement down on me, like they had the divine right. Throwing some books into my locker, I kept my head down and ignored them, until my feet hit something and I faceplanted to the ground.

My arms stung. I had managed to throw them up as I fell, but the laughs were worse. My face burned with shame as I picked myself up, I felt like I was in some stupid movie. The snickering ringleader had shown herself, and had sent me flying to the ground.

“Those parachute pants can really trip a girl up.” Sam Rithow smiled at me the way a cat does when it;s about to pounce. My face was red, and I felt like crying, people were snickering, pointing, I caught the words freak and loser amidst the snickers. “You really got to watch out ghost girl, those spirits really have it out for you.” Her cronies laughed as if Sam had just told the funniest joke ever.  
Feeling utterly humiliated I kept my head down and walked into my homeroom. This only gave me a slight reprieve as soon everybody who saw me fall gathered into the room, including Sam and her goons. The sound of her talking to her boyfriend in a cutesy voice made me sick to my stomach, the girl was such a wretch.

“Guess what Ellie?” a slightly nasal voice said next to me. I rubbed the bridge of my nose and sighed.

“Do you actually have a picture of a ghost this time Cedric?” I asked. This was the last thing I needed to start my day. Last week he had brought in a cursed goblet that had come from Goodwill of all places. Then he brought a camera that he swore could catch ghosts.

“Yep check these out, do any of these ghosts look familiar?” he asked. Cedric was tall and geeky, with round spectacles and frizzy blond hair that stuck out at crazy angles sometimes. He pulled five photos, the camera flash lighting the scenery in them, though they were mostly blurry. I squinted at each one trying to make out any humanoid figures, but all I saw were specks of light, and an old house. The last picture was the worst.

“This is just a dog.” I said, showing him the photo.

“An undead dog,” he replied quickly. “It ran into the woods and I couldn’t find it at all!” He was clearly very impressed with his findings. 

“I’ll phone the animal shelter, did it have a collar on or anything?” I asked. Cedric could have lost some fingers. 

“Yeah from its deceased owner, I’m going back to that old house to take some more pictures tonight.” he said. His big blocky camera sat on his desk next to him. I found myself wondering where he had got it from.

“No, don't do that, if that dogs there it might bite you, don’t be dumb.” I said as the bell rang, grateful for the reprieve from the conversation. My eyes caught a flickering shape outside. Despite myself I jerked my head up and squinted outside, to see a black robed figure duck behind a tree outside. I peered at the spot watching it intently, until I could hear Sam sniggering at me with her ape-like boyfriend joining in. Cedric joined me in peering over my shoulder, eyes failing to see anything. I jerked my head back down and tried to pay attention for the rest of the class.

Gym was always before lunch, and I wanted nothing more than to skip it. However I had already made a habit of skipping the class, and was being watched like a hawk. Sighing I locked myself in a bathroom stall to change into my gym strip, I wasn’t giving Sam any more ego today. That oath went out the window as soon as my foot stepped into the gym.

Our teacher who pulled double duty as our social teacher was putting us on opposite teams. I ended up standing next to Cedric, while Sam eyed me like a hungry shark from the other side of the gym. The red dodgeballs all sat on the line, looking mean and angry. I could already feel the sting of rubber against my face.

“Don’t worry I got my throwing arm on today.” Cedric said, winding his shoulder in a circle.

“You're going to get pelted, don’t you ever worry that you're making a target out of yourself?” I asked. Mrs. Guile blew into her whistle and suddenly everybody was running forward, I hid behind Cedric very bravely.

“I never am Ellie!” he called. “It’s how I strut my stuff.” he scooped up two of the red balls and tossed one to me. I only just managed to deflect an incoming ball that would have otherwise hit me in the face. I scrambled back to the far side of the gym, ducking as another ball whizzed over my head. Cedric was in his gangly glory, flinging his awkward limbs around as he ducked under balls and chucked his own back. I wished that I had even half of his weird prowess.

Something cracked into the wall behind me and I flinched and scurried away as Sam grabbed another and whipped it at me. The volleyball team had turned her into a prime athlete, and she used that skill to try and hunt me down. One ball whooshed past my shirt and I jumped away.

“Do what Gerald said Ellie!” a green figure was suddenly flying over my shoulder ducking the balls like he was still corporeal. “She’s right there, beam her in the face, in the face!” I flung my ball so pathetically at her that all she had to do was knock it out of the air. She was going to drill me with a ball, there was no easy way out to be gained.

“Pathetic!” she called out from her side, she had another ball in her hand already. I dived and slid across the ground securing a loose ball. “Do you even know how to throw?” she called again. “Did your freak parents ever teach you anything? Or did they just enjoy making you some sort of freak.”   
My face burned, nobody had noticed in all the chaos, but even Reggie had gone silent. I knew Cedric was nearby as well, and that made it worse. How many people had to see me be taken down another peg?

“Don’t let her talk to you like that.” Reggie said. “Don’t let her do this to you.” My hand clenched the ball in my hand, and for once in my life I let my anger take over. I ran in a blind fury somehow not getting hit by anything. Sam stood right on the center line jeering at me like she always did, not expecting me to do anything.

I roared and hurled the ball when I was within a few feet. The red ball spun through the air, the white logo becoming a blur as it rolled through the air, and directly into Sam’s face drilling her in the nose. She fell to the ground and began to cry as blood leaked out from it. I immediately felt terrible, even as Reggie pumped his fist.

I was suspended for the rest of the day, and had to tell my parents what had happened. I felt sick leavng the school my backpack felt like it weighed a hundred pounds. Reggie floated next to me watching me with concern on his face.

“Don’t worry about it, she’ll think twice before doing that again,” he said. “I did the same thing in 82. Guy kept harassing me until we scuffled in the parking lot.”

“Did you win? I asked not in any mood for Reggie’s old glory. 

“No, but I socked him once real good, and things were fine after that.” He bounced from one foot to the other letting him drift into the air like he was swimming in zero gravity.

“Ellie, wait!” a voice called out. I groaned to see Cedric with his camera running towards me. 

“Oh it’s the ghost hunter kid, I don’t know why you guys aren’t friends.” Reggie said, waving at Cedric. Cedric caught his breath and then suddenly brought up his camera taking a picture. I yelled out as the camera flash blinded me, while Reggie laughed at Cedric.

“What the hell Cedric? Leave me alone. I'm having a bad day already, god.” Cedric suddenly started cheering behind me, whooping as he held a developed picture in his hands. Without saying anything the breathless boy shoved the picture in my face. I frowned as I looked at the picture. My hand was raised to try and stop the flash. My wild black hair blended in with the shadows behind me. My eyes widened when I saw the glowing green figure above my shoulder, a piece of metal sticking out of his torso.

“I caught a ghost!” he shouted.

He did not stop badgering me with questions as we marched back to my house. My head reeled from the events of the day, so much had happened in such a short time. Reggie was delighted and kept trying to answer Cedric’s many questions, the result was an annoying cacophony of two teenagers cackling away.

“He looks young, is he young? How did he die? What’s the afterlife lie?” Cedric barely stopped for a breath. “He looks like Angus Ents, oh my god is that him?”

I turned sharply, stopping just outside the gate to my house. “His name is Reggie first of all.” I said. “And if you shut up for twenty minutes while I get you inside, I’ll tell you about the others.”

“Ohmygodthere’sothers.” Cedric wheezed out, I was surprised he hadn’t passed out from talking so much. I clamped a hand on his mouth and grabbed him by the sleeve guiding him up the old stairs with their chipping paint. The house was warm, and the scent of mixed spices hit my nose as I walked. Mom was making something, probably a big pot of soup. 

She bustled around a corner, her curly black hair done up in a ponytail. “There you are, I just got a call from the..” she trailed off when she saw Cedric with her. She raised an eyebrow and her eyes peered deep into mine, but I wasn’t letting her mom powers win today.

“Bad timing, we’re paired for a school project, we’re going up to my room.” I said flatly, praying she wouldn’t press the issue any further. 

“We’ll talk about it later, your mom tells me you like soup Cedric would you like to stay over?” she asked. I rolled my eyes, leaving it up to my mom to be such a mom.

“Yes please Mrs.Brent!” I practically pulled Cedric up the stairs, Reggie was enjoying this far too much. Ushering Cedric into my room I shut the door, and turned to talk when I stopped dead. Somebody was in my room. Reggie noticed it too, darting back suddenly through the air, what could scare a ghost? The tall dark form extended until it reached the roof, my mouth was frozen and dry, as the tall ghost of the priest turned, a bald pale skull had its own unearthly light, a wide skeleton smile was visible through transparent skin. The mouth opened and a horrible dry sound started to emerge from its throat. I wanted to shout to warn Cedric, when a flash of light lit up the whole room.
