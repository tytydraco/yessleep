To start, I apologize for how long this will be. My memories of my grandma's old house in the mountains are extensive, as my parents frequently dumped my sister and me there before their divorce later in my childhood. And I really want you all to get a feel for how is was up there.

It seems crazy; maybe I am crazy, but everything I say here is true. I’ve never told anyone this story, because I know how it looks. If this ever gets back to my family, it will tear us all apart. But please, please believe me.

There is something that lurks behind my grandma's house.

When I was a young girl, my grandma used to live in a big house on the side of the mountain. The house was uphill on a dead end, surrounded by a densely wooded forest, with maybe one or two neighbors sharing the road. The houses in the neighborhood were large, multi-floor houses probably worth millions more than what my grandma bought it for as a recently divorced mother of 4. The journey to Grandma's during the Utah winters was the most brutal, as the roads up to it were steep and they weren’t visited by the snow plows as often. I went there often with my cousins and sisters, the house being a large portion of my childhood memories, as during the winters when our parents would drop us off there to have some time alone, one night would become three as the snow would trap us on the mountain. We would watch movies in the movie theatre room, sleep on air mattresses in the living room, and fight over stupid things like toys or who gets the dolphin pillow pet that night. The only thing that separated my childhood memories from my sisters' and cousins' was the woods.

The woods surrounded the woods from all angles and at night, deer and raccoons would find their way into the backyard at night. I loved to stay up and watch the yard from the upstairs window, seeing what made its way from the forest at night. During the day, I would explore the woods as far as I could. Everyone else was always too scared to join me, so I was alone more often than not. My grandma never really minded my wandering, not really noticing for a majority of the time. I would just say I’m going outside and disappear for hours. As long as I came back in one piece, I was allowed to explore the forest as I pleased. I never went too far, as even to the most adventurous 7-year-old, at some point the forest becomes too scary to explore further. I found a creek, and that ended up being my stopping point. I would go there and play with whatever toys nature provided. Pine sap, mud, and odd berries became the perfect potion-making tools, or some pine needles and two leaves became a sailboat to float down the creek.

One non-wild animal I encountered frequently was my grandmas chickens. She had gotten a dozen eggs one spring and hatched them as a fun activity for when we visited. Each kid was allowed to name two, which I found to be unfair because two of my cousins never visited the house like me, my sister, and my aunts two kids. I picked two little yellow chicks with black markings as mine. Dinosaur and Princess. Very original I’m aware. But, my grandma wasn’t a verry good chicken owner and as soon as they were big enough, she just allowed them to free roam the backyard with no coop in sight. This quickly became problematic when, within the first month, they were picked off by raccoons. My grandmother eventually had the bright idea of using the old dog house next to the back door as a shelter for them and placed a sliding door to cover the entrance. Every night before bed, she closed the door, and any chicken not in the dog house was left to fend for themselves. It took 4 more chickens before the rest finally got the idea. So any scraping in the woods more often than not ended up being a chicken looking for bugs on the forest floor.

The woods were never frightening to me like they were for my sisters and cousins. They would only get close to the end of the yard before shrieking and claiming they saw something duck behind a tree or that they heard someone scream for help. I always thought it was to scare me and my younger cousin, Becca, who was only a year younger than me. My sister and hers were both the same age, nearly a decade older than us, and always were ganging up to tease or scare Becca and me. Just being mean teenagers. They grew out of it the older we got, but when they were 15 was when they were the most brutal. One would ask me or Becca to go to the basement to go find something for them, then the other would jump out with a mask from the Halloween bin in storage and send us crying to our grandma.

The basement is what truly scared me about my grandma's house, more than woods ever had. It had a large, barely used living room with just a couch and a shelf full of my grandpas hunting trophies that he left behind when he ran off for another woman. The door to the garage was there as well as a door out to the backyard facing the woods. There was also a long, dark hallway that lead to one of the many spare rooms that my grandma refused to let us use as she didn’t want to bother cleaning them each time we came over as well as my grandfather's old office and a storage room. My grandfather's old office was the same as the hunting shelf, only a hundred times worse. Stuffed rabbits, pheasants, ducks, and the mounts of long dead bucks decorated the walls. Taxidermy wasn’t an art I truly learned to appreciate until I was older; looking into their cold, dead eyes scared me nearly to death. So, the basement tricks worked on me more than Becca.

It was one of those days when their sights were set on me that changed how I viewed those woods.

Becca hadn’t made it up to Grandma's house that weekend due to a sleepover with a friend, leaving it just me, my older sister, and my older cousin. Our parents were supposed to come get us that day, but lost track of time and didn’t beat the snow. They promised they’d get us once the plows got to the road the next morning. So, grandma set back up the three air mattresses in the living room and went to bed, leaving us three to watch a movie on the TV.

“I don’t want to watch a baby movie, Maya.” My sister, Christi, had whisper-yelled at me as to not to wake up our grandma.

“But Grandma says we have to watch something we can all watch,” I whined back, clutching the DVD case of my chosen movie. She scoffed and kept flipping through TV channels. I tried to emphasize my point by putting the DVD case in front of her vision, but she just yanked it from my hands and threw it across the living room. “Hey!”

They both angrily shushed me as I crawled over to it, picking it up.

“I’m going to tell Grandma!” I stood up, but Leah just grabbed my arm from her air mattress.

“Well, you’re going to get in trouble; you're still awake past your bedtime.” She pulled me back down, making me whine. “We already watched your movie, it’s our turn.”

“Yeah, Maya, I’m being a really good sister by letting you stay up until 9:30. So stop being a brat.”

“I am not being a-“ My voice rose a bit, causing the other two to shush me. I stuck my bottom lip out, making an emphasis to stomp over to my bed in the corner and lay down angrily, grabbing my stuffed tiger, and pulling the blanket over my head. The other two girls giggled at my tantrum, continuing to scroll through the channels to find a good show to watch.

Down the hall, I heard the door open to my grandma's room. The light of the TV quickly went off as there was stomping coming from down the hall, but it was too late.

“Girls!” My grandmas anger ridden voice whispered from the other side of the living room. “It is nearly midnight, and you are being too loud. Give me the remote and go to bed.”

The two older girls groaned in defiance.

“It wasn’t us, though! It was Maya! We keep telling her to go to bed, but she won’t!” My sister complained, throwing me under the bus immediately. I felt footsteps approach, and I quickly shut my eyes, feigning sleep. A trick that I was all too good at, using it often so my parents would lay me in bed after car trips instead of making me walk. The blanket lifted away from my face, and I felt the rush of cool air meet my face, but I didn’t crack.

“She’s asleep.” My grandma whispered, placing the blanket back on my small body. “Stop trying to blame your sister. Now give me the remote.”

After a few defiant groans, my grandma's footsteps made their way down the hallway, punctuated by the sound of the door closing. The room was silent except for the sounds of the other girls shifting in their beds. It wasn’t until my little sleeping trick was starting to catch up to me and I was on the verge of actually falling asleep that I heard my sister leave her bed, crawling towards me.

“You're such a brat. I hate you.”

Her voice was cold and angry, filled with utter hatred that could only be justified when you’re a 15-year-old girl not getting what you want. My bottom lip quivered, and as she settled back into her air mattress, soft cries left me.

Christi always claimed when we were older that the last part never happened, but I knew that sinking feeling in my chest as a child was real. I know now that my sister's anger never stemmed from an actual hatred towards me; rather, she, as the oldest sibling, was the only one aware of my parents' failing marriage. But she misplaced this anger towards me instead, a clueless victim. I wasn’t as broken as she was, so she felt the need to make my life just as bad as the one she was living.

So, when I woke up in the dead of night that same night, I found myself in a different location than I had fallen asleep in. My little eyes fluttered open as the sound of scratching tore through my consciousness. I looked around the pitch-black room, dread immediately setting in as I was profoundly afraid of the dark. The living room had a night light to help with my fear, but at that moment, no light came in. As my eyes adjusted, I realized there was a bit of pale blue light coming from the other side of the room, reflecting off the beady, plastic eyes of taxidermied rabbits and ducks. I froze in fear, finally pinpointing where I was. I was no longer on my air mattress, now snuggled deeply in my blankets on the downstairs living room couch. I was scared and confused; how had I gotten there?

The scratching returned, making me shoot up and turn towards the sound, heart beating heavily in my chest. My throat tightened, seeing where the light was coming from. The door to the backyard, which had a large glass panel covered by blinds. It was dark, but the snow outside reflected the moonlight into the room. And there, I could hear the scratching. My body shook, my eyes now fully adjusting and recognizing movement outside, the shadow shifting behind the horizontal slits. The sound of nails on wood sent a shiver up my spine. Something was trying to get in.

Though I was young, scared, and shivering, a little bit of rationality still lived in my tiny mind. I had seen countless times on nights like this, the tiny hands of raccoons scratching at the back door, only from the large windows of the upstairs living room. Though I was scared, my love for wildlife pushed past the fear, and I became more curious. I had never seen a raccoon up close before. And in my childlike mind, it made me feel a bit better, like the little creature was my friend. It knew I was scared and came to tell me I wasn’t alone. So, against my better judgment, I slowly got off the couch. The door felt like it was so far away as I took soft, slow steps, trying not to scare off the raccoon. The scratching got more aggressive and louder as I got closer.

When I reached the door, I bent down slowly, my face now a few inches from the blinds. Carefully, I brought up a hand and used two fingers to separate the blinds. But, instead of finding the face of a friendly cartoony raccoon that wanted to be my friend, I was instead face to face with-

A chicken.

Clawing at the snow-covered wooden stair step, head jerking and looking for bugs under the white powder. A smile slowly spread across my face as I recognized it as my chicken, Dinosaur. Though she had lost the black markings she had as a chick, I could still recognize him from the other chickens in the yard. She was the only survivor of my chickens, Princess had become one of the first two that got snatched up that first month, so Dinosaur had quickly became my favorite. I regularly picked her up and carried her around the yard during my adventures in the back yard. I giggled seeing her turn her attention to the sudden movement and running to the closed door of the doggy door, frightened by my presence.

“It’s okay.” I whispered, thinking she could hear me. Or understand what I was saying. “Its just me.”

Dinosaur returned to the yard, now scratching at the snow on the grass, forgetting I was even there. I watched her, happily before finally pulling away. As I turned away from the door, fear started to seep back into me as I remembered the situation I was currently facing. I needed to grab my blankets, my stuffed tiger, and go back to the safety of the night light. I went back to the couch, grabbing my Dora the Explorer blanket from where it lay crumbled up on the floor. I look at the cushions where I expected to see my stuffed tiger, I see, in dismay, it was not there. The tiger, I lovingly dubbed Tigey, was my prized possession, it went with me everywhere. My dad had given it to me for my birthday and it only left my side when I explored the woods as to not get it dirty. But, he was no where to be seen. My lip quivered and my eyes started to fill with tear. Not only was I alone, in the dark, but my one comfort was suddenly gone. I got on my hands and knees, thinking maybe he had fallen under the couch when I had gotten up, but I couldn’t see into the inky blackness under the couch. My breathing began to pick up as, suddenly, the room went pitch black. I sat up quickly, turning towards the door, where moments before was my only source of light.

No light shone through the window, now suddenly blocked out. I could only see the soft blue outline of whatever stood in front of it. I froze, still on my hands and knees on the carpeted floor. I held my breath, too scared to even react to what was happening. Tears slowly started to escape my eyes, but they were locked, unblinking at the door. It felt as if I'd stared at that door for hours before a high-pitched, shrill, repetitive sound suddenly rang out just outside the window. The shadow moved quickly, now no longer blocking the light from the door. Shadows moved frantically behind the blinds; the screeching suddenly cut off with a thud. I recognized the sound, the distress call of a scared chicken. I shot up, knowing something got Dinosaur. I ran to the door, scrambling to turn on the lights outside. The light outside the door flashed on, yellow light now lighting up some of the living room. I reached for a small lever beside the blinds and raised them as high as they would go. As they rose, I was met with a sight that stayed so clear, I can still see it in my mind as if it was happening right in front of me.

Scarlet seeped into the torn-up snow, soft flakes now floating down and landing onto a pure white canvas now painted with splashes of crimson and dark feathers littering the massacre. Large chunks of snow were now thrown away, showing the dark, muddy earth beneath it. The trail of blood led from the doorstep towards the forest, and there, my eyes met another. There, covered by the shadows of the dark wood, two green eyes shone back at me. Like a cat. Our eyes were locked onto each other's. I don’t know how long we stared at one another, but I couldn’t look away.

A motion finally broke the contact; something dark hit the rock in front of it, rolling off it limply and landing on the snow. It twitched and shook; a streak of red left behind on the rock. Dinosaur’s head was snapped, her body covered in her own blood and chunks of feathers and flesh torn away, exposing bone. A droplet of blood dripped down onto the snow, the light reflecting off of it as I realized the eyes were starting to raise. I stood, shaking and crying as I watched them grow taller and taller, until it stopped. This thing was not an animal. It was just hunched over.

I watched as a white appendage slowly reached out. As soon as I saw the elongated claws touch the snow beneath it, it suddenly lunged forward.

My body finally decided to cooperate as I scrambled backwards to get away before I could even see the rest of it. I fell onto the carpet, my knees and elbows burning as I slid against the surface, trying to find my footing. A loud bang sounded behind me, the door's hinges creaking as a mass was flung against it. I was finally able to get back onto my feet, and I shot my body towards the stairs. I ran as fast as my little legs could get me, my body slamming the door that led into the basement. I fumbled for the door handle, twisting it. It barely turned, clinking. It was locked. I heard something heavy banging against the glass door downstairs. The light now entirely blocked out, leaving me in the blackness of the stairway.

As if something in my throat just suddenly unlocked, I let out the loudest, highest scream I could muster, my tiny fists banging on the door.

“GRANDMA! GRANDMA, HELP ME!”  I screamed, banging on the heavy wooden doorframe so hard it felt like I was going to break. “GRANDMAAAA!”

The banging downstairs was drowned out by my screams, tears hitting my hot pink and purple pajamas. Light suddenly returned to the room behind me, convincing me that whatever this thing was, it got in.

There was commotion at the door, and as soon as I brought my fist against the door, it gave way from underneath. Bright lights lit up the stairway, and two arms scooped me up instantly. I nearly fought it before I recognized the smell of my grandma's shampoo. I wrapped my arms around her neck, my loud sobs deafening the older woman.

“Maya! Maya! Please calm down!” My grandma tried to calm me, but I was inconsolable, my body shaking and arms locked around her neck as if I were to let go, she couldn’t protect me any longer.

“There’s- T-there’s- mon-mon…” I choked out between sobs, “I-It- Dinosaur! D-Dinosaur!”

In my distressed state, I opened my teary eyes, seeing Christi and Leah pressed against the living room wall, pale as they watched my grandma continue to fail to get me to calm down. Christi clutching Tigey in her hand.

I didn’t sleep that night. No one did. I was insistent that there was a monster in the basement and it was coming for me. My grandma tried to get me to go back to sleep, letting me sleep in her room, but I only became more distressed that it would get Christi and Leah. So they dragged their air mattresses into the room, and my grandma made a point to show that the door was locked. Even then, I cried until morning. As soon as daylight deemed the downstairs safe, my grandma brought me down to the basement to show that the door was locked and intact. I wouldn’t get anywhere near it, pressing myself up against the wall by the staircase in fear. My grandma went to close the blinds, only for her eyes to widen. Her hand shot away from the lever as if it burned her. She turned and grabbed my little wrist, dragging me upstairs.

The truth came when my parents came to get Christi and me. To get back at me for getting them in trouble, Christi and Leah had carried me gently downstairs, laid me on the couch, and locked the door leading down to the basement, knowing I was going to be afraid and cry. They planned to get me as soon as they heard me wake up but had fallen asleep, only being awoken when they heard my screaming and banging. They insisted they had nothing to do with the “monster” outside, promising they were asleep. Christi was grounded for 2 weeks, having to write me a letter of apology. My parents explained softly to me that what I had seen may have been a coyote or a cougar, chalking up my experience as an exaggeration that my little mind had come up with in my fear-stricken state. They let me make a fake grave in our own backyard for Dinosaur.

It wasn’t until years later that I learned what my grandma had seen outside and why there was suddenly a large white spot on the doorframe for the rest of her time in that house.

Dinosaurs decapitated head, laid out on the untouched snow. No footprints to or from the door; the blood and carnage now covered in a flesh blanket of white. The head lay there, without a speck of white on it. As if something had returned hours later and had placed it there.

Hours after I had left is when they found it, carved impossibly deep into the wooden door frame, uneven and jagged, as if written by a toddler.

M

A

Y

A

My grandma had it covered, but took a single photo, one that stayed hidden in the downstairs storage until I found it the day after she passed. I wonder if she ever showed my mother, but it’s too late now. It lives in a book on my bookshelf.

I still think of Dinosaur, though I had only known her for a year of my life. I want to think that it was just a hungry coyote that had gotten her, but I know now that it wasn’t. It was something hungrier, something impossibly tall that looked back at me in the forest that night.

And I am sure that, as I was looking at the shadow that covered the light of the moon from my hands and knees on the floor, if I had looked up only a touch above the doorframe, I would’ve noticed that there was a small window right above the door.

Maybe I would’ve seen those green reflective eyes staring down at me.
