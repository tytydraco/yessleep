My mom has always been a very weird person, the kind of person who makes a child grow up very fast.

She loved horror movies, and sometimes she had these weird ramblings. My mom is sometimes the crazy person in a horror story. But she is nice and caring, and I love her, even if I’m too old now to tell her that without feeling some kind of embarrassment.

You see, we believe in things.

In an old, traditional way. We aren’t living like witches or anything. We just believe in things. We believe you shouldn’t open an umbrella inside the house, that breaking a mirror will bring seven years of bad luck. We believe there is some kind of afterlife. We take care of our dead and their graves because of that.

What I don’t exactly believe in—but don’t completely dismiss either—are UFOs.

I mean, the universe is big.

But I believe what I see. Things that feel at least somewhat tangible.

At the same time, a few things have happened to me. All of them exist in this weird space between memories, dreams, and something else. You know when you remember a scene from a movie you watched once as a child, except you can’t find the movie anywhere? Maybe the movie doesn’t even exist.

So why do you remember the scene?

My mom saw something.

Whatever happened, it left a huge impression on her. She is absolutely convinced that something contacted her.

When I was a baby, almost twenty years ago now, something followed her.

She says it happened at night when she was leaving work. There was this open space behind a huge mall, but behind it the area became almost like a little residential forest.

That’s where she saw the orbs.

Colorful orbs.

They followed her.

She became so afraid that she started begging them not to take her. She told them she had a baby. She had me. What would happen to me if she disappeared?

According to her, the orbs continued following her for a while.

Then they disappeared.

I never knew what to think about that story, so mostly, I didn’t think about it at all.

Years passed.

I had a weird memory of my own, but again, I never thought too much about it.

Recently, my mom started talking about things in the sky again. But lately she has also become a little more... creative than she used to be, so once again, I didn’t pay much attention.

There are weird stars in the sky sometimes.

Stars that don’t really look like stars. They’re brighter, somehow more artificial-looking. I assume they’re satellites.

Maybe that’s what she sees.

Still, sometimes I feel observed.

One of those “stars” is often directly in front of my bedroom window, after all.

Sometimes there are several.

One night, I was coming home at around 10 PM.

I look at the sky sometimes. I don’t know why. I’m curious, I guess.

That was when I saw what looked like a military jet.

I live near an airport. I know planes. More importantly, I know how loud planes are.

But that night was calm.

Completely silent.

The thing looked like a normal jet to me, but it made no noise at all.

I watched it and started wondering what exactly I was looking at.

Eventually I came up with the most reasonable explanation I could think of: maybe my mom had been seeing some kind of prototype aircraft all these years.

So I told her.

She was happy that I had finally seen something too.

But she was also afraid.

She remembered the orbs.

“Mom, I don’t know if they would hurt you anyway,” I told her. “Even if the things you saw really did exist.”

She didn’t understand what I meant.

So I told her something I had never really known what to do with myself.

“You know, I have this weird memory. I really don’t know if it was a dream, something from a movie, or something that actually happened.”

She looked at me.

“I just know that I was a child. Younger than ten. And I was alone in some kind of metallic facility.”

Even describing it made the memory clearer.

“I was afraid. And I think I had a reason to be.”

There was a gigantic wall in front of me.

Except it wasn’t really a wall.

It was a shelf.

A huge shelf stretching upward, filled with what looked like aquariums.

And inside those aquariums were people.

Human beings.

They were asleep, floating in some kind of greenish liquid.

I remember being awake.

I remember being terrified that whatever had put those people in there would realize that I was awake.

“But you know,” I told my mom, “I’m here.”

She stared at me.

“So if that memory is real, whatever happened, I survived it. I’m alive. I’m here. Maybe you don’t have to be afraid.”

My mom looked terrified.

And confused.

I think she reacted the same way I had reacted to her stories all these years. She simply didn’t know what to do with the information.

So we didn’t talk much more about it.

It’s not like I was traumatized by the memory anyway.

After that, though, I started looking at the night sky more often.

Whenever I had the chance.

Maybe I looked weird to other people, walking home with my head tilted toward the sky.

But I only ever saw those strange stars.

Nothing else appeared to me after the silent jet.

My mom wasn’t so lucky.

One day, she came home and said:

“You’re never going to believe what happened to me.”

She sounded out of breath.

“Hm?”

She started putting away the groceries while I listened only half-attentively. I was hungry, and there was delicious food right in front of me.

“I saw something,” she said. “Something big. So big that I couldn’t see all of it.”

I need to explain where we live.

The apartment buildings are arranged around a small square park, maybe twenty meters across. There are two main buildings, both shaped roughly like an L, facing opposite directions.

According to my mom, whatever she saw was longer than the buildings.

She couldn’t see the whole thing.

“And then...”

She stopped putting the groceries away.

“I was looking at it. I was terrified. And suddenly it disappeared.”

“Disappeared?”

“It became small.”

I remember looking at her.

“Very small,” she said. “And then it just disappeared.”

So either my mom had completely lost it...

or she had just seen a damn flying saucer.

She described it as something almost half-round, if I remember correctly.

She looked exhausted while telling me. I couldn’t tell whether it was from fear or excitement.

And that was it.

We never learned anything more.

I don’t have many reasons to come home late, so maybe I simply missed my chance to see something else.

Although the strangest part is that the flying saucer didn’t even appear at night.

My mom said she saw it at dawn.

Sometimes, as the world gets crazier and crazier, I think about the aquariums.

And I wonder if they got me.

I wonder if I’m sleeping in one of them.

Because some things about me don’t really add up.

Sometimes I feel like I’m an imitation of a human being.

I don’t seem to age the same way other people do. My hair grows strangely fast. I’ve noticed physical changes in my body that don’t seem like they should be possible.

Small things.

Things that probably have explanations.

At least, I assume they do.

But sometimes I think about that metallic room.

About the people sleeping behind the glass.

About that greenish water.

And I wonder:

If I was one of them...

how did I wake up?
