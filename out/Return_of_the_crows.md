RETURN OF THE CROWS: 



I couldn't sleep. It's hard to sleep. The vodka wears off and I wake, and then it's just darkness and thoughts.

Most people like sleeping in the warmth. Some like the cold. I like to be warm and cold at once, and so, on this chilly October night, I opened my window and pulled the duvet around me.

Thinking is antithetical to sleeping, which is funny, because I believe you think most deeply when you're asleep. I've probably had my deepest thoughts dreaming. I just can't hold onto them long enough to tell you.

A flutter. Old but familiar. The sound of a clutching grip, then silence.

If there hadn't been a moon, the crow would have been as black as the night itself. But there it was in the silver light, the sheen of its feathers and a glint in one dark eye.

I used to sing to them. Then I abandoned them. Then I slowly abandoned everything else.

There was a time when I wondered whether they knew what they'd done, or whether they'd simply thought they were being kind. Or even if they cared about any such distinction.

I didn't know what I'd started when I started, but I knew what I'd finished.

Another drink to Martina.

Months, years, consumed with something worse than anger, hate, or fear. Consumed with nothing. An insidious fucking nothing.

But nothing is something, isn't it?

"Oh, I have company," I said after several minutes.

It wasn't regarding me. It wasn't sizing me up. If one could ascribe a human quality to a bird, especially a corvid, I'd have said it was watching over me.

The room was dark. I couldn't sleep.

And for the first time in thirty years, I picked up my out-of-tune guitar and played.

The crow seemed to listen. Or maybe it just thought me amusing. An old, sad man playing some shit tune on a shit guitar. To a crow.

It departed with a flap of heavy wings and a caw so ugly I laughed.

I left the window open.

I poured myself a drink and sat through the infomercials. Five thirty in the morning, in and out of some sort of dream state, and some effeminate male voice on the TV rouses me... offering something about a dignified burial, payment plans available, no one left to worry about the cost.

"Does this appeal to the amnesiacs or early risers... Jesus, did I get old?"

I laughed again. Louder this time.

Then I slept, and I dreamt of crows, and I dreamt of my last listener.

An alarm went off. I hate mornings. I hate them almost as much as nights. I hate alarms more.

And I'm trying my best to hate the phrase hate.

I dragged a hand across the nightstand to kill it, and found there was nothing there to kill.

It came again. The most hideous, disgusting caw in the world.

I didn't hate it.

"Fucking crow, isn't it?"

Postscript

The insidious alarm clock woke me again. I named him Stephen.

A caw. Well, I can hardly call it a caw, can I? Some jarring screech from the depths of a hellscape, or something to annoy me. I like to think God laughs. Perhaps he does.

It was twelve-thirty in the afternoon, and I was on my fourth mimosa. Refreshing, though I wished it could be eighty proof. I stood on the balcony of my parents' old house. My house now, though I still thought of it as theirs.

And there they sat. Two beautiful black crows, perched on the balustrade.

They even let me pet them. Well, they always did.

I rested my arms against the railing to steady myself. My legs weren't particularly interested in the business of standing.

I looked at the two, then three...

"You killed for me."

There were coos. Still the flutter of wings.

Then another crow arrived.

And another.

One settled on my shoulder. Another on my head, clutching at what little hair I had left. I laughed and brushed it away, but it returned almost immediately.

Then came the others.

They descended in droves, wings beating against my face, their little claws gripping my clothes, my shoulders, my arms. None of them pecked. None of them seemed angry.

Nothing to hurt. Just to be there.

All the crows had come back. A murder of crows. Don't forget.

I felt a peculiar tugging at my clothes. Their wings beat furiously, and for one absurd moment I thought they were trying to lift me.

Perhaps they were.

Probably not, but it felt that way.

I laughed, and tried to steady myself. The world tilted. The balustrade slipped beneath my hands. For a moment there was nothing but feathers, the October sky, and the peculiar sensation of being very light.

Then the ground came up.

I remember a crack. Whether it came from my head or the paving stones, I couldn't say.

And then I was lying on my back, looking up at the balcony, two or three stories above me. It seemed an awfully long way to have travelled without going anywhere.

Something moved beside my face.

A beautiful black crow stood upon my chest, its feathers shining in the afternoon light.

It regarded me with those ancient, almost tyrannosaur eyes. There was something prehistoric about them, something that had survived millions of years without ever finding a good reason to become more agreeable.

It cocked its head.

I thought it might be waiting for me to say something.

Perhaps it was.

Perhaps it had been waiting thirty years.

"Fucking crow," I whispered.

It opened its beak and let out the most hideous noise I'd ever heard.

I laughed.

Then something occurred to me. Something that, for thirty years, I'd managed not to understand.

My smile faded.

"Just desserts," I said.

The last thing I saw was that beautiful black crow, its head tilted to one side.

Then I flew.

And so did a murder of crows.
