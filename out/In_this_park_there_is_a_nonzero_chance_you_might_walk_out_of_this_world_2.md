[Part One.](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/comments/1w2fmkr/in_this_park_there_is_a_nonzero_chance_you_might/)

I’ve come to call the small brick house that I live and work in “the Shack”.

The Shack was originally created to accommodate the needs of the rangers taking night shifts, so it was built with a bathroom complete with a shower, a toilet and a sink (no bidet, which is a harsh everyday reality for me). Also, in its bigger and only other room, the Shack has a cozy little gas stove. The only other pieces of furniture I found when I moved up here were a half busted chair and the desk where an old computer hooked up to the camera system sits. Basically a two-room apartment, minus the rest of the apartment.

When I took over, this place needed to become livable for longer stretches of time. By scouting the web and asking around, I managed to get my hands on a little sofa that can turn into a bed by pulling out a hidden mattress under it, a coffee table and a television to put on top of it. I don’t have cable or a steady internet connection here, so my main entertainment source is an old DVD player that one of my fellow rangers found for me. I also bought a small wardrobe, but the main room was already super cramped, so I stuffed it in the bathroom. That has been my whole setup for the last couple of years.

I usually wake up a little after 4 pm, which gives me a few hours before my watch officially begins. Time that would be well spent to keep the place tidy. During my first week, I promised myself to keep everything clean at all times. I was thinking about that while observing the ever increasing pile of clothes sitting next to me on the sofa. 

Without a washing machine, doing the laundry was a real drag. Sighing, I went to the bathroom to fill up a basin. While the sink was running, I heard something. After turning off the water, I listened carefully. This time, I could recognize what it was. Scratching coming from the other side of the wall. 

“You bastard.”

I muttered, taking slow steps back to the main room. On the other side of the bathroom wall, there is a small wooden shed. Inside, other than various tools, I keep some non-perishable food, bottled water and a minifridge. It was the third time this week I thought I’d heard sounds coming from there. The first time it happened I was suddenly woken up by a couple of cans falling on the ground. But I couldn’t find the culprit.

I got out of the Shack and started to pace around it, ready to face the intruder, when another anomalous sound distracted me. Car wheels. I snapped my head towards the noise and saw a familiar car parking at the end of the road. Shit.

I quickly ran back inside, grabbed the pile of clothes with both arms and tossed it in the bathroom. Looking out the window, I saw a man walking down the dirt road that connected the end of the main road to my place, two grocery bags in his hands.

The fellow ranger approaching was Luca, one of my superiors. After a couple of months of living here, Luca kindly offered his help with this chore, saving me the trouble of finding a new lift downtown every week. He had been punctually coming every couple of weeks with few exceptions and he was always nice about it. Despite that I still manage to forget when he is coming a bit too often.

I opened the door and clumsily offered my help.

“Thank you Luca.”

He nodded as I took one of the bags. I would have taken the other one too but he said it was fine. I’m ashamed to say I wanted to take it just to avoid having to let him in.

“How have you been, Alex?”

“Oh, you know. The usual.”

I looked at him. He nodded again, offering a slightly tight smile. He probably noticed the few socks I lost while grabbing the dirty clothes and the general energy emanating from the room.

“Need anything else?”

“I don’t think so. Thanks again.”

I let out a sigh of relief when he drove away.

I started unpacking what he brought. Rice, canned tomato sauce, bottled water, chips, shampoo, toilet paper… the usual. I kept what I needed to make some dinner and the bathroom goodies, leaving the rest in the bags, and I got out of the Shack. The sun was still visible. A delicate light passed through the branches of the sparse trees surrounding my post. I took a deep breath. It vaguely smelled of ozone. Rain was probably coming tonight.

Walking to the shed, I suddenly remembered. The scratching. Slowly, I put the bags down and pressed an ear against the wooden door. I doubted there would be anything. To my surprise, I was proven wrong almost immediately. It was a softer noise this time. There was a rustling coming from the other side. I took a deep breath to steady myself, taking in the evening air once more. I quickly unlocked the door and opened it. Light filled the room and I saw a mess of torn up potato chip bags left on the ground. There was nothing else in the room. 

I checked behind every box and even behind the minifridge. It had escaped me again.

The way this mysterious intruder had been getting in and out of the shed was something plaguing me from the first time I’d heard the noise. An animal stealing food was normal, but the thought of it just appearing and disappearing made me a bit anxious. I wasn’t sure I really wanted to know how that happened.

You see, the way the abnormality of this place interacts with the wildlife is something I still can’t put my finger on. But surely, something is up with them. If this place conjures things based on the mind of whoever is inside it, it isn’t outside the realm of possibility that some of the weirder, unfamiliar things I sometimes see are from the minds of the actual inhabitants of this place.

Once, while walking out here, I found a bird’s nest on the ground. Upon closer inspection, it looked like the whole thing was built from only one, impossibly long twig. The eggs inside of it twitched slightly. Their yellowish surface seemed soft and it looked like they were breathing.

It gets weirder than that. Sometimes the animals themselves act… unnaturally.

We have lots of bats out here at night. Little grey things flying in every direction, only visible against the yellow street lights or the night sky. When a camera detects some movement, a red light flickers in the corner of the screen and a beeping noise starts playing. There was a night where the computer was signaling to me that some movement had been detected from the camera pointed at the end of the road. But looking at the monitor, I couldn’t see anything, not even some branches moving from a blow of wind. I didn’t want to risk anyone passing by without me noticing, so I quickly pulled out a flashlight I kept under the desk and went there to check. 

The tarmac road doesn’t just end abruptly, but it expands into a crude, vaguely rectangular shaped parking lot. As I shed light over the black pavement, I was met with at least ten little pairs of eyes looking back at me. Bats. They stood on the ground in a little group, unmoving, all of their little noses now pointed up at me. Some of them would occasionally open their mouths, just to close them back after a second. As I looked, a couple more landed on the ground and joined the others.

After being sure that they were not going to do anything else, I took a couple of steps back and then pointed the light back on the dirt road. As I reached the Shack’s door, a realization hit me. They were all facing this direction even before I pointed the light at them.

“Do bats open their mouths to echolocate…?” I thought.

I quickly fumbled with the lock and slammed the door behind me, eager to put something solid between me and them.

After cleaning up the mess in the shed, I went back inside, taking all of the chips with me. The sky was draining of color. My watch was beginning. I booted up the computer and put the cameras on. Then… nothing. I sighed and let myself fall on the sofa, opening a bag of onion-flavored rings. I put on some silly superhero movie and started eating, without really following it.

On most nights, I can bear the boredom of my job. I watch movies, read something every once in a while and, lately, I’ve started writing notes about my experiences to post.

Other times, it feels like I’m rotting here. The rest of the world hidden behind an endless sea of roots and branches. That was the kind of night I was having.

I found myself reminiscing about my time as a regular ranger. I have liked the outdoors since I was a kid. 

“Maybe I should get out of here sometimes. I do have some free time after all.” I told myself. 

I say that often. Thinking about how much time has passed since I actually acted on that thought makes my stomach feel funny.

I realized I missed walking around the woods. There were times when I would surprise myself by forgetting where I was and what this place is. Despite all the bad things that had happened, all the times I put myself and others in danger out of stupidity… 

“God, what am I thinking…?” I thought, rubbing my face.

Fact is, doing something so boring for so long makes you feel nostalgia even for parts of your life that at the time you only wanted to bury.

“I mean, it’s just a car, right?”

My head snapped back up. My eyes were fixed on the TV screen. But that was stupid of me. It could not have come from the TV. I knew that, because it was my voice.

“It’s a cool car too…”

Now I was standing. I wanted to lower the volume of the television, but I too was scared to move. I didn’t need to.

*THUD!*

The loud noise came from the bathroom. I snapped out of my trance and ran outside. Thin, steady rain hit my face. My slippers got muddier with each step. I didn’t know what was going on, but I felt compelled to find something, anything to defend myself. I stepped into the shed and searched for something passable for a weapon. I knew that if it was anything like the things appearing out here, that was a pointless act. Despite that, the thought of facing anything vaguely resembling an animal empty-handed terrified me.

Suddenly, I stopped. Something was scratching on the wall. This time on the bathroom’s side. My hands found a broom and tightened around its handle.

The scratching stopped. After what felt like an eternity, I heard the intruder take small steps. Only it wasn’t in the bathroom. It was above me.

“Alex, stop it! STOP!” This time, it was a female voice.

I could feel goosebumps rising all the way to my neck.

The steps moved to my left, then down the wall. I was totally disoriented. On what was it walking?

The walls felt tighter. Everything was cramped and dark in the shed. I looked towards the door and realized the feeling wasn’t just some side effect of fear. The door was getting smaller and the walls closer.

My body moved on its own and I was out of there. I had to crouch down at the last second to get under the shrinking exit and that made me fall on my knees in the mud. Under the moonlight, I saw small, tightly packed pawprints going towards the treeline, following two parallel lines until they disappeared in the dark. My gaze followed them until they stopped. Just then, I saw that right there, in the shadows, a glimmering orb was floating in the air. Something I couldn’t see was lying low in the undergrowth, staring me down with one singular eye.

“Stop it. STOP!” It screamed. For a moment, I felt mocked.

My legs were jelly. I couldn’t get up. Quickly, my hand reached for the phone in my pocket. I turned on the flashlight and pointed it towards the thing staring deep inside my memory.

I could barely make out small white teeth and a rotting jaw before the animal pulled itself deeper into the woods like a ragdoll. 

I stood there, on my knees, in the rain. When I felt strong enough, I walked back to the Shack. I needed a shower. The bathroom was filled with muddy pawprints. They were on the ground and on the walls. Even on the ceiling. A torn and bent metal can stood abandoned on the floor. The tomato sauce inside spilled all around it.

I couldn’t sleep for the life of me after that, even after the sun rose. Eventually, I must have dozed off, because the next thing I remember is being woken up by my phone buzzing. 

“Uhm, Hello…?” My voice was still thick with sleep.

An older, uptight man that I immediately recognized answered.

“Did you just wake up?”

I knew there was no point in lying.

“Yeah… hi Mark. What is it?”

On the other side, officer Mark let out a groan.

“Look, Luca tells me you don’t look too hot lately.”

Luca was snitching to Mark? I felt betrayed and, at the same time, embarrassed. I could only imagine what old Mark had to say about how Luca probably described the state of the Shack. I rubbed my eyes.

“Yeah…? That’s nice of him to care.”

“Well, cheer up. You’re getting some days off.”

If he was trying to make it sound appealing, his flat, matter-of-fact tone didn’t help. Then again, even if he put all of the energy his body could gather to deliver that news, the silence that followed would have stayed the same.

“Alex, are you there?”

“What do you mean by that?”

I was pacing around the room now.

“You’re getting a colleague. She’ll stay there a couple of days a week so you can get out every once in a while.”

I stopped in my tracks. I knew it wasn’t a good idea to raise my voice with Mark, but I could not help it.

“Are you being real?! You’re putting someone else up here?!”

“Settle down, kid. She’s a forest guard, fresh out of training.” He tried to sound reassuring, but I wasn’t having it.

“A forest guard. Wow! You mean the guys that write tickets for throwing trash on the ground?”

I was walking a dangerous path. Mark opted for a calm tone once more. He probably expected a reaction like this out of me.

“I’ll have you know that forest guards receive formal training and are licensed to carry a firearm. Honestly, she is more prepared than you are on that matter.”

I scoffed.

“Like that’s going to help her out here.”

“Look kid, I appreciate your dedication to this, you know that, but-” I cut him off.

“Oh, don’t give me that bullshit!”

“ENOUGH!”

I bit my tongue. I had stepped out of line and now Mark had switched from friend to boss. He continued, a hint of anger and disappointment lingering in his tone.

“This is happening, I’ve already talked with your higher-ups at the park. It’s not up to you. Understood?”

I fell on the chair at the desk, all the fight drained from my body.

“Yes.”

“Good. She is starting next week. Get that place cleaned up.”

He hung up on me and I remained still, looking at the ceiling. A hundred thoughts and even more nice words for Mark were swirling in my mind. I finally decided what problem I had to tackle first. Phone still in hand, I made another call.

“Hey mom… yeah, it's me, hi. Listen… I need a place to sleep.”
