My name is Talitha, though everyone knows me as Lita. I was born in Kentucky, but we live in a small town in Texas now. Our life is quiet. 

Peaceful, even. 

At least, that’s what it looks like from the outside. My mother Sidney has only one rule. I am never to leave my bedroom at night.



Only when there is clear daylight.. real daylight may I come out. She had a bathroom and toilet installed in my room. A small fridge she restocks every day. Shelves of books. 

The latest Video games. She says we’re lucky the house belongs to family, that we only have to worry about a few bills. She makes it sound normal. 

Thoughtful. Loving. Whenever I ask why, her face tightens, like she’s holding herself together with string. “I’m not myself at night,” she tells me, with a trembling voice. 

“Nothing is.” Then she kneels in front of me and takes my hands like she’s afraid I’ll disappear. “Promise me, Lita. Come back before nightfall. Lock your door. Stay there until morning.” I always nod. 

On my nightstand sits a bundle of rosemary. Mother forbade me from moving it. I never have. I don’t want to rebel, not when she lets me do almost anything else. It’s such a small rule, really. Things go smoothly. Until tonight.



I decide to stay up late, curled on my bed with a show playing softly. I tell myself I’ll turn it off soon. Then I hear something downstairs.

 A wet sound. 

I pause the screen, listening. My unopened soda is cold in my hand. Mother’s warning whispers at the back of my mind, but the window is pale blue now. It’s dawn, I think. It’s fine.

 I step into the hallway. When I lean over the railing, I see her. My mother is crouched in the dark, shoulders hunched, muttering in a language I don’t recognize. 

She’s hissing. Hissing at something on the floor. Her face is smudged with red as she tears into raw meat.

The smell hits me next. 

Iron...Rot…

 I gasp. 

Her head snaps up with a sharp pop. “Who is there?” My mother croaks. I run.



The soda slips from my hand and clatters across the floor, rolling straight into my doorway. I fumble with the door as heavy footsteps thunder behind me.

 I kick the can aside and slam it shut, locking it just as something crashes against the other side. 

I dive under my bed, choking on sobs. She stalks the hallway and stood in front of my door without a sound first. 

The doorknob rattles violently. “Lita?” she croons. “I know you’re awake.” I shove my earbuds in and squeeze my eyes shut, forcing myself not to breathe. Not to move. Eventually, the house goes still. 

Sunlight finally cuts through my blinds golden and warm. 

The feeling lifts, like a storm passing. When I come downstairs, I clutch my tiny cross and the rosemary bundle so tightly my hands ache.

 “Mother?” I whisper. She turns. Her eyes are normal again. Soft. Full of love.



“Lita?” she rushes to me. “Are you okay, sweetie?” “You frightened me,” I say, as my voice was breaking. Her face crumples. 

“Did I hurt you?” 

“No,” I whisper. “But you were… frightening.” 

“Forgive me,” she sobs. “Help me protect you from the night. Don’t leave your room until clear daylight. 

Promise me, my Lita.” I nod “Yes,” I say. And I mean it. We embraced..



I wanted to ask my mother more.. but I feared I might learn about things that will haunt me forever.
