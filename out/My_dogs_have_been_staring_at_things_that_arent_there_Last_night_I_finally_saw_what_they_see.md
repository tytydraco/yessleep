I don’t believe in ghosts. I don’t *not* believe in them either.  
I guess I’ve always thought of ghosts the same way I think about aliens. Maybe they’re real. Maybe they’re not. I’ve never seen one, so I’ve never had much reason to have an opinion.  
At least I didn’t before we moved.  
My partner and I relocated to the Pacific Northwest a few months ago. We wanted more space, more trees, somewhere quieter. We found a house north of Seattle that checked pretty much every box.  
It’s not particularly old, which feels worth mentioning. This isn’t some Victorian farmhouse with a sealed basement and a hundred years of dead owners. It’s a normal house in a normal neighborhood. The kitchen was renovated sometime in the last decade. The previous owners had kids. There’s a Costco fifteen minutes away.  
The only thing I wasn’t prepared for was how dark it gets here.  
We have enormous trees around the property, and when it’s cloudy, which it usually is, nighttime seems to arrive before sunset. You can be standing in the kitchen at five in the afternoon and look through the windows into what feels like midnight. When it rains, the windows basically turn into black mirrors. You see the room behind you more clearly than you see anything outside.  
My partner is a nurse and works nights, so I’m alone in the house more than I’d like to admit.  
Technically, I guess I’m never alone.  
We have three dogs.  
Our oldest just turned thirteen. I’ve had her most of my adult life. She’s incredibly gentle and, at this point, mostly interested in finding the softest surface in any given room and sleeping on it.  
Our youngest is still a puppy.  
And then there’s our two-year-old border collie.  
I need you to understand something about her because otherwise none of this sounds particularly strange. She isn’t afraid of anything.  
We’ve spent a frankly embarrassing amount of money training this dog. She’s been through professional programs more than once. Her recall is nearly perfect. She doesn’t bark at delivery drivers. She doesn’t react to other dogs. I can drop food next to her, tell her to leave it, walk out of the room and come back to find her sitting there staring at me.  
More importantly, she isn’t skittish. Fireworks annoy her. Thunder doesn’t bother her. If something falls over in another room, she doesn’t run away from it. She runs toward it.  
I work from home, so I know these dogs extremely well. They’re basically my coworkers. They follow me from my office to the kitchen, sleep under my desk during calls, and somehow know I’m finished working before I’ve even closed my laptop.  
Most nights my partner is working, the four of us have the same routine. I finish whatever I’m doing, make something to eat, then lie on the couch watching TV with three dogs competing for increasingly ridiculous amounts of space.  
That’s what I was doing the first time it happened.  
It was maybe three weeks after we moved in. Around 11:30, our border collie suddenly lifted her head.  
Not unusual.  
Then the puppy did it.  
The old dog was last.  
Within maybe two seconds, all three were awake. They were looking toward the back windows.  
I muted the television.  
Nothing.  
The border collie got off the couch. Her ears were forward, but she wasn’t barking. She walked slowly toward the glass door leading into the yard and stopped about six feet away. The puppy followed her halfway, then stopped. Our old dog stayed beside me.  
“What’s up?”  
The border collie didn’t react to my voice. She was staring through the glass.  
Our backyard isn’t huge, but it backs into a line of trees. During the day you can see between them into the neighboring properties. At night, you can’t see anything beyond the edge of the patio.  
I turned on the outside light.  
Empty patio. Empty lawn. Trees.  
I stood up and walked toward the door.  
The puppy immediately started whining.  
I stopped.  
It wasn’t his normal attention-seeking whine. It was low and continuous, almost under his breath.  
The border collie’s posture changed too. Her head lowered. The fur along her shoulders stood up.  
Then she made a sound I had never heard from her before.  
It was somewhere between a growl and a hiss.  
I remember laughing because it made me uncomfortable.  
“Who’s there?”  
I said it loudly enough that if some teenager or neighbor was screwing around outside, they’d know I’d seen them.  
Nothing answered.  
I got closer to the glass and cupped my hands around my eyes. All I could see was my own reflection. Behind it, barely visible, were the trees.  
I unlocked the door.  
All three dogs backed away.  
That bothered me. Not enough to think *ghost*, obviously. But enough that I didn’t open it.  
Instead, I checked the security cameras. Nothing. No person. No animal. No movement at all.  
I texted my partner something like, *Your children are being weird tonight,* and sent a picture of all three dogs staring at the back door.  
Then I went upstairs.  
By morning it was funny. My partner came home, looked at the camera footage too, and decided there had probably been a raccoon or deer outside the camera’s field of view. That explanation made sense. We live in the Pacific Northwest. Animals are everywhere.  
I stopped thinking about it.  
For a while, nothing else happened.  
Or at least nothing I recognized as the same thing.  
A few days later, I was working when I noticed the puppy standing in the hallway outside my office. He was staring into the guest bedroom.  
I called him.  
He didn’t move.  
I called again and he came running over like nothing had happened.  
The next afternoon, the border collie did something similar. I went downstairs for coffee and found her sitting at the bottom of the stairs, facing the living room.  
“What are you doing?”  
Her ears flicked toward me, but she kept looking into the room.  
I walked past her, opened the refrigerator, made coffee, and when I turned around she was beside me.  
Neither incident bothered me. Dogs stare at things. Dogs hear things we don’t. We had just moved. The house made different sounds than our old place. There were squirrels on the roof, birds in the gutters, neighbors we’d never met.  
There were a hundred explanations.  
Then it started happening often enough that I noticed.  
Never dramatically. That’s probably why it took me so long.  
I’d be on a work call and all three dogs would suddenly look toward my office door. I’d be cooking and the border collie would stop eating to stare into the hallway. Once I came downstairs after showering and found the puppy sitting alone in the living room, looking up the staircase I’d just walked down.  
I started joking about it.  
“What do you guys see?”  
“Is your friend back?”  
One afternoon, I was on a call when the border collie stood up from underneath my desk. She was looking behind me.  
I was sharing my screen, half listening to someone talk, so I didn’t pay much attention.  
Then she walked around my chair.  
Very slowly.  
She stopped beside me and stared at the empty corner behind my desk.  
I snapped my fingers and pointed down.  
She ignored me.  
That was unusual.  
I muted myself.  
“Hey.”  
Nothing.  
I turned around. There was nothing there.  
When I looked back at her, she wasn’t staring at the corner anymore.  
She was staring above it.  
Her eyes were moving slowly upward.  
Like she was watching someone stand up.  
I don’t know how else to describe it.  
I got out of my chair. She immediately came to me.  
I finished the call from the kitchen.  
I still didn’t connect it to the backyard. Not consciously, anyway.  
The first time I did was about a week later.  
My partner was working.  
It was raining hard enough that I could hear it hitting the bedroom windows. I woke up sometime after two because our old dog was growling.  
That scared me more than anything else had.  
I’d had her for thirteen years. I could probably count on one hand the number of times I’d heard her seriously growl.  
She was standing beside the bed, looking through the bedroom doorway.  
Our hallway light was on. I leave it on when my partner works nights because otherwise the upstairs hallway is completely black.  
I sat up and listened.  
Nothing.  
Then I noticed something strange.  
She wasn’t looking down the hallway.  
She was looking at the doorframe.  
Specifically, the top of it.  
I followed her eyes.  
Nothing was there.  
“Hey, come here.”  
She didn’t.  
I got out of bed.  
The moment my feet touched the floor, she stopped growling and climbed onto the mattress.  
I checked every room upstairs. Nothing. I even checked the closets, which felt ridiculous while I was doing it.  
When I came back, all three dogs were pressed together on my partner’s side of the bed.  
I remember standing in the doorway looking at them.  
And for the first time, I thought about that first night.  
The backyard.  
The whining.  
That strange hiss from the border collie.  
All three dogs staring at something I couldn’t see.  
I slept with the bedroom door closed.  
The next morning, I told my partner. He laughed at first, then stopped when I told him about our oldest dog. He knows her as well as I do.  
We agreed there was probably an animal in the walls, so we had pest control come out.  
No mice. No rats. No droppings. No scratching. No signs of anything living in the attic or crawlspace.  
The guy actually complimented us on how clean everything was.  
That should have reassured me.  
It didn’t.  
After that, I started paying attention.  
That’s when I noticed something I wish I hadn’t.  
The dogs were never looking at the same place twice.  
But whatever they were looking at seemed to be getting closer.  
First the backyard.  
Then rooms downstairs.  
Then the staircase.  
My office.  
The upstairs hallway.  
Our bedroom doorway.  
I didn’t tell my partner that part.  
There was something embarrassing about saying it out loud.  
*I think something is moving through our house.*  
I’m a grown adult. I have a job. I pay taxes. I wasn’t going to start burning sage because my dogs stared at corners.  
So I did what people do when something doesn’t fit into their understanding of the world.  
I ignored it.  
That worked for about two weeks.  
One night I was brushing my teeth before bed. My partner was at work. The puppy and our old dog were already in the bedroom.  
The border collie was standing in the bathroom doorway behind me.  
I could see her in the mirror.  
She wasn’t looking at me.  
She was looking over my shoulder.  
I kept brushing.  
Then I noticed her ears flatten.  
I turned around so fast I hit my hip against the sink.  
Nothing.  
“Jesus.”  
She kept staring.  
I actually got angry with her. I know that sounds stupid.  
“What?”  
She looked at me.  
“What the hell are you looking at?”  
Her tail went between her legs.  
I felt terrible immediately.  
I knelt down and hugged her.  
“Sorry. Come on.”  
While I was holding her, she began growling again.  
Very quietly.  
I could feel it vibrating through her chest.  
Right beside my ear.  
She wasn’t looking over my shoulder anymore.  
She was looking between my legs.  
I stood up.  
There was nothing there.  
I didn’t brush my teeth in that bathroom again for three days.  
After that, things went quiet.  
Completely quiet.  
A week passed. Then another.  
The dogs stopped staring into rooms. The border collie slept under my desk again. Our old dog went back to being interested exclusively in blankets and food.  
Eventually, I convinced myself we’d had mice after all. Or squirrels. Or some combination of unfamiliar house noises and me freaking myself out.  
I was relieved.  
Last night, my partner was working.  
I finished work late, ordered food and put something on TV. All three dogs were with me. Our old dog was asleep against my leg. The puppy was stretched out on the rug. The border collie was on the other end of the couch.  
Sometime after midnight, I realized the house had gone completely silent.  
Not quiet.  
Silent.  
Normally you hear something here. Rain against the windows. Wind moving through the trees. The refrigerator cycling on. One of the dogs breathing.  
I remember realizing I couldn’t hear any of it.  
Then the border collie stood up.  
She was looking behind me.  
I didn’t turn around.  
I don’t know why.  
Maybe because I’d spent weeks training myself not to react.  
The puppy woke next. He looked at the same spot.  
Then our old dog raised her head.  
All three of them were staring directly behind me.  
I muted the television.  
“Stop.”  
None of them moved.  
I turned around.  
There was nothing behind me except the other sofa.  
I stared at it for a few seconds.  
Nothing.  
The border collie jumped off the couch and approached it slowly. The puppy followed.  
Our old dog didn’t.  
Instead, she climbed into my lap.  
I actually laughed. Not because anything was funny. I think my brain just needed to do something.  
“What is it?”  
The border collie stopped in front of the sofa.  
Then she barked.  
Once.  
The puppy barked too.  
Both of them were staring at the bottom of the couch.  
I assumed a toy had rolled underneath.  
I got up.  
Our old dog jumped down and immediately walked out of the living room.  
That should have been enough.  
I should have followed her.  
Instead, I walked over.  
The border collie backed away as I approached.  
There’s maybe a four-inch gap between the bottom of our sofa and the floor. I could see darkness underneath it.  
I took out my phone and turned on the flashlight.  
“Did you lose something?”  
I crouched.  
The border collie made that sound again.  
That horrible hissing growl from the first night.  
I put one hand on the sofa.  
And looked underneath.  
There was a woman under it.  
For a second my brain couldn’t understand what I was seeing.  
She was lying completely flat on her back. Her body disappeared into the darkness beneath the couch.  
Her face was directly below where my feet had been.  
Maybe six inches from my toes.  
She was staring straight up at me.  
I fell backward so hard I dropped my phone.  
The flashlight landed sideways, but the beam stayed underneath the couch.  
I could still see her.  
I couldn’t scream.  
I couldn’t breathe.  
She didn’t look dead.  
That’s the part I can’t explain.  
There was nothing obviously wrong with her face. She looked like a woman in her fifties or sixties. Pale skin. Dark hair. No blood. No wounds. Nothing supernatural.  
Normal.  
Except she was smiling.  
Not a huge smile.  
Just slightly.  
Like we’d made eye contact across a room and she was waiting for me to recognize her.  
The dogs were losing their minds.  
I scrambled backward.  
She didn’t move.  
I remember saying:  
“Who are you?”  
Her eyes moved.  
Not toward me.  
Toward the dogs.  
Then back to me.  
And she whispered something.  
I couldn’t hear it over the barking.  
I grabbed my phone and ran.  
I didn’t check upstairs. I didn’t get shoes.  
I opened the front door, got all three dogs outside, put them in the car and drove away.  
I called my partner first. I could barely explain what had happened. He thought someone had broken into the house and told me to call the police immediately.  
So I did.  
I called them from a gas station.  
Two officers searched the house.  
Nobody was inside.  
They moved the sofa.  
Nothing.  
They checked every door and window.  
Locked.  
No signs of forced entry.  
One officer asked whether I’d been drinking.  
I hadn’t.  
Eventually they left.  
I didn’t go back inside.  
My partner left work early and met me at a hotel about twenty minutes from the house.  
None of us slept.  
But that’s not why I’m posting this.  
About an hour ago, I remembered our security cameras.  
We have one covering most of the living room.  
I didn’t want to look.  
Eventually I did.  
I watched the footage from last night.  
I watched myself sitting on the couch. I watched the dogs wake up. I watched them stare behind me. I watched myself turn around.  
I watched the border collie approach the other sofa.  
I watched myself walk over and crouch down.  
Then I watched myself fall backward.  
There is no woman in the video.  
I expected that.  
I don’t know why, but I did.  
What I didn’t expect was what happened before.  
I started going backward through the recordings.  
Last night.  
The night before.  
The week before.  
I found the first time the dogs stared at the sofa.  
It was eleven days ago.  
Then another.  
Fourteen days.  
Then another.  
Seventeen.  
Every few nights they would wake up and look toward it.  
So I kept going.  
Eventually I reached the first week after we moved in.  
That’s when I found something I can’t explain.  
It was 3:14 in the morning.  
The living room was empty.  
Then our old dog walked into frame.  
She stood there for a while.  
Then she looked toward the sofa.  
Her tail tucked underneath her.  
She backed away.  
A few seconds later, she left the room.  
Nothing happened for almost a minute.  
Then the camera glitched.  
Just once.  
When the image came back, nothing had changed.  
Except there was a hand sticking out from underneath the sofa.  
Just the fingers.  
Pale.  
Resting on the floor.  
I watched it for maybe thirty seconds before I realized something.  
The fingers weren’t coming out from underneath the sofa.  
They were curling around the bottom edge from behind it.  
Like someone was lying on the other side.  
Holding on.  
I skipped forward.  
At 3:19, the camera glitched again.  
The hand was gone.  
I went through everything after that.  
Hours of footage.  
I found dozens of moments I’d forgotten about. The guest bedroom. My office. The staircase. The bathroom.  
The dogs weren’t staring at random places.  
I thought they were.  
They weren’t.  
The first night they saw her outside.  
Then the guest room.  
Then the hallway.  
Then my office.  
Then upstairs.  
Then our bedroom.  
Then behind me in the bathroom.  
Then under the sofa.  
She wasn’t appearing in different places.  
She was moving through the house.  
And every time the dogs noticed her, she was closer to me.  
I still couldn’t understand why she’d been under the sofa last night.  
Why there.  
Why underneath my feet.  
So I kept replaying what happened.  
Especially the moment she whispered.  
I remembered the shape her mouth made.  
I think I know what she said.  
I think she said:  
“They keep telling you.”  
The border collie just started growling.  
She’s standing at the foot of the hotel bed.  
The puppy is awake too.  
They’re both staring at the space underneath it.  
I’m on the bed.  
I haven’t looked.  
My partner is asleep next to me.  
I’ve tried waking him twice.  
He won’t wake up.  
Our old dog isn’t looking underneath the bed.  
She’s looking at me.  
And for the first time in thirteen years, she won’t come when I call her.
