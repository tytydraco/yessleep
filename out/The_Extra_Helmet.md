Sparta and Athens were at war. Sparta amassed an army of 500 battle ready men, willing to bleed for the sand they were raised on. They stood in the courtyard under the sun, their shields braced and swords sharpened, their bronze helmets and armour glimmering beneath the relentless sun.

King Archidamus started off towards the front of the gates where his men faced.

"You are all aware why you stand in this courtyard today. Today, you will march alongside your brethren and bleed beside them in battle. You will starve. You will thirst. You will curse. Many of you standing here today will die in the arms of your brothers you grew next to. I am not a cruel king. No. I love you all deeply. You fight for Sparta, so you fight for me, for our families, for your wives, your children, and all those you hold dear to you. Now march. March out of these gates. And still your eyes to not bat even a glance back here, lest your heart breaks apart your valor."

The king stood just outside the gates where the sand met the walls. There, he shook the hands and kissed the heads of every face that left the kingdom. And while the count was correctly established, he kept his own record silently in his head as he waved every man off. From the first soldier to the last, he counted 500 exactly.

And King Achidamus, as pure as he was in heart, knew that not many would return. If at all. A terrible price to pay for war.

&#x200B;

The king, generals, families, and soldiers that stayed back to defend the kingdom watched as that army of 500 slowly shrank over the cloudy horizon.

&#x200B;

From 500.

To 400.

Then 200.

Then 50.

And then 1.

Then none...

&#x200B;

Many months pass, the kingdom grows anxious with every passing full moon. Then one night, a scout surveying the area from the walls sees an army growing over the horizon. At first 10, then 100, then more, until eventually a mass is revealed. It's the army they had sent.

&#x200B;

The king and all the generals line every man up back at where they were counted all those months ago the next day to cross out the names that made it back and leave the ones that didn't.

&#x200B;

The generals are divided to effectively speed up the count. Hours pass as the king plans his compensations and condolences to the families of the men deceased.

&#x200B;

But to the shock of the king and his generals,  each and every single name was crossed off. Every Spartan that was sent is here and alive. Not a single man was slain.

&#x200B;

But that rapidly growing joy was extinguished and replaced with confusion when one of the generals states the impossible. Much to everybody else's dismay.

&#x200B;

\*There are 501 men standing in the courtyard right now\*

&#x200B;

The king is struck. The generals are dumbfounded. Even the men themselves are confused, turning to themselves in attempts to recognize every face beside and behind them.

&#x200B;

Minutes pass as the king and the generals discuss amongst themselves. The men grow restless and loud.

&#x200B;

"Count again"

The king orders.

"Count again and triple check every name on that list"

The generals follow.

&#x200B;

Hours pass again

The king's puzzled look never left his face.

The generals return, the confusion still displayed on their faces.

&#x200B;

\*501\*

"Again, and this time write the new lists from scratch. Name every man on that paper and then compare both. Single the outlier out,"

The king was sure this would work. How could it not? This was logic. Unbreakable, reality set, logic.

&#x200B;

Once the records were written, the generals began comparing both lists meticulously. Their eyes squinted with caution so as to make sure no mistakes were possible.

&#x200B;

And none were.

On the old list, there were \*500\* men. Not one man was miscounted.

&#x200B;

&#x200B;

On the new list

&#x200B;

The Spartans grew restless immediately. The generals beckoned them all to remain silent.

Even the king himself shared the same sentiment all the men expressed, not out of anger, not out of frustration, but out of confusion...

&#x200B;

The generals scanned the lists again, counting every single name written with ink on parchment and comparing each. But it felt like their minds were bending around an invisible pole. Their brains tried to single the name out, yet everyone belonged. Everybody there was meant to be there.

&#x200B;

How? There were 500 men sent out into the battlefield, the king himself shook the hands and kissed the heads of every man that walked out the walls that day.

&#x200B;

"Enough! Walk down the isles of every soldier and confirm the names from both lists in the order they were written."

One single general was sent to count, and while this took much longer, the margin for error was greatly reduced. It was impossible to count a name wrong.

&#x200B;

Hopeless.

&#x200B;

The general returned, his face displaying what his heart felt as he walked back.

&#x200B;

"I counted. Every single name there, on both lists, I counted. But everyone is meant to be here, there were no errors, my lord!" Exclaimed the general.

&#x200B;

Then they tried sending a different general, then another, then all of them at once to confirm, they counted with the old list and the new list, they counted with just the old, then only the new, then they told every man to step out if his name was called and then return into formation from both lists, they asked every soldier if he knew the man behind him, everything worked. Everything was alright. It was true.

The sun gently hugged the sands over the horizon now. It was some time before midday when the count started.

&#x200B;

It was now dusk. And not a single satisfying conclusion was made.

&#x200B;

Eventually, the king waved everyone off back to the city. And in a last desperate but lazy attempt to count again, he committed himself into the same gesture he'd done when he first sent them out.

&#x200B;

Yet every hand was the same, every head sent off was there that day.

&#x200B;

The Athenians lost the war. Sparta rejoiced in all their riches, in all their glory. All the men there that day grew old with their families and spoiled their kin with their wealth.

&#x200B;

The military arsenal men recorded the numbers of every spear, every shield, every sword, every pair of sandals...

&#x200B;

and every helmet.

&#x200B;

499 were returned over time as every soldier passed away peacefully. 2 remained, a trace of the inexplicable horror that happened on the day of their return.

&#x200B;

The 500th man sat by his fire. Embracing the comforts of his home. He, like every other man, let the memory of that day fade away. It was of no use. If human certainty couldn't confirm the impossible, then what was the point in living your life around that one day?

&#x200B;

The night was silent. Cold. It reminded him of the camps they would sleep in under the moonlight.

&#x200B;

His wife slept behind him, as did his three warrior sons he'd raised.

&#x200B;

The flames twisted and danced in ways that made him reminisce about his life.

&#x200B;

He remembers being behind Nikolas and in front of Andus.

&#x200B;

&#x200B;

He remembers the way his heart broke as he thought back to his family while they marched

He remembers himself gargling on his blood as his tears mixed with the hot crimson blood on his cheeks as he prayed for his family's safety.

He remembers the fade to black.

The way his chest stopped rising.

&#x200B;

He \*died\* staring into the corpse of his dead friend beside him.

He felt the warmth of his skin leaving his body, so why is he sitting here embracing the fire's warmth?

&#x200B;

Like the rapid darkening of a mountain as the sun sets behind it, his heart raced as he kept going back over these details.

&#x200B;

There was blackness... Thick, foggy, suffocating darkness that swallowed his soul into his final resting place.

&#x200B;

And then there was a helmet in front of him as he and his men marched under the moonlight back into the city walls.

&#x200B;

His eyes never blinked once despite the foot gap between him and the fire. Tears had started to well up in his eyes as he unraveled his life before and after the war.

&#x200B;

Under the dead of night, he ran to Byron's house, his old bones working as if remembering the way he charged into his sandy casket. The casket that was never there until tonight.

Marcus and Byron sat down, the cold of the night encroaching the light that the old oil lamp cast. Every shadow stretched longer than they should have. Was the world itself breaking apart? As if it too realized alongside Marcus the inconsistency in reality that transpired that day.
