I dont use reddit a lot, and its my first time actually writing a story like this. English isnt my first language so sorry for any mistakes. I hope I could get your thoughts on this.

  


I've been to mental clinics more than once by now, for PTSD, Insomnia, Depression, etc. But the first one I ever went to really stuck with me. For context: I was actually doing quite alright at the time I got in. We'd been on their wait list for three months and my mother insisted I should go. "Its not gonna hurt to go"- that kind of logic.



I had a feeling that the place was bad from the beginning, i didnt even want to go there in the first place.
But since it wasnt a private clinic, but one that was managed by our state, i tried to give it a chance.

I'd start by saying the clinic was located inside of a rather big city and in a nasty part of it too. Homeless people, drug deals, crime and that stuff were normal around this area. The building itself was ugly. Utterly. It was just a tall gray block with grills infront of the windows.

The personnel was unprofessional and simply un-trained. No medical, pedagogical or psychological training. They treated patients like animals. Like we were unable to make our own rational decisions and completely stupid. Now I know this is a clinic and some patients arent able to be rational. But the personell disrespected us, yelled, and disregarded our concerns. There were even cases of absuse that were simply ignored. Therapists triggered their own patients and kids got high/c\*t themselves without anyone taking their drugs/blades away from them. 

It was like they didnt care about us at all, as long as we stayed quiet and obedient. 

My roommate was quite literally schizophrenic and in the middle of a psychosis, yet they still forced me to sleep in a TINY room with her.
We were only allowed to leave our station for 30 minutes and that only three times a week.
If you misbehaved you weren't allowed to leave your room. If you left your room or the station outside those specific times you were allowed to, the personnel acted like you just tried to infiltrate the Pentagon. They yelled at you, send you back to your room and a girl even got slapped once.
The ceilings were low and the floors narrow, im not even claustrophobic but it made me feel uneasy and anxious.
The food was a crime too. Cheap, re-heated, classic hospital meals. Even for the kids with EDs.

My mental health declined in there rapidly. I had panic attacks daily again (it had been over 8 months since my last one) and even hallucinated wich I've never done before.

The 'doctors' explained to me that the hallucinations were simply because of my ADHD medication but come on! I had been taking these meds for weeks and the hallucinations only started when I had begun to take (allegedly the same) pills in the clinic. But the pills they gave me were different from the ones I knew from my home. The capsule wasnt purple and gray and it wasnt filled with tiny round pearls. It was yellowish and filled with a white powder. When I told them these were the wrong meds, I was waved off with a "it's the exact medication from a different brand, quit asking about it".

In the clinic I met a boy, let's call him Tyler.
Tyler was pretty quiet but we got along together quite well for some reason. I was 15, he was 14. While I quickly made a lot of new contacts he was either alone or with me. He rarely spoke, almost always wore headphones and was really introverted.
I wasnt sure why he behaved that way but Hey- we were in a clinic, everyone is atleast a little odd there. Nothing unusual.
The only thing i knew about him, was that they had kept Tyler in the clinic for a while now (11 months or so) and that he'd been diagnosed with a lot of stuff. 

When everyone went to take their medication in the mornings, I saw that Tyler got handed 4-5 pills. 
When I asked about them, he explained that one was for his ADHD and two were simply vitamins/omega 3. The other two pills were never explained to him. He just had to take them since they forced him to. Said they were to help with his panic attacks.

Fast forward a few weeks later,
I finally had persuaded my parents to let me leave the place. It only took that long because everytime i asked my parents to leave, the therapists and doctors in the clinic told my parents i was in a terrible state and they had to keep me for my own safety. But FINALLY i convinced them that i was doing bad \*because\* of the clinic. When the head of the facility heard about my parents plans he got wierd. He'd been wierd before but he got even wierder, almost panicked. All of a sudden he claimed I was in an unstable state, a danger to myself and others and that I needed to get diagnosed immediately. (For clarification, I wasnt in a good state because the clinic treated their patients horribly but i wasnt harming myself or others. He never had wanted to diagnose me with anything new before that either.)

While my mother signed the papers that I could leave, the head-doctor (or whatever the dude was) explained to my father that I was psychopathic, autistic, a danger to society and most likely in psychosis. Mind you- he only said that AFTER we planned to take me out of the clinic.

But after some trouble and paperwork I got out of the clinic with a mild trauma (My therapist outside the clinic later confirmed i developed an actual new trauma in there) but Tyler was still in there.
He got out a few weeks later, after spending almost a whole year there.
During that time he'd struggled with SH, ED, panic attacks, depression and 'felt like a zombie in there' (his quote).
As soon as he got out, he got much much better. We met a few times and he looked healthier, happier and less zombie-like.
Turns out, he stopped taking the meds the doctors prescribed him.

Him and his parents talked to Tyler's doctor and she told them, that the clinic-doctors had been giving him lithium and strong antidepressants to 'stabilize' him. Tyler was 15 at the time and neither him nor his parents were consulted before the prescription or even informed that he'd been taking that stuff.

I talked to some of the other patients to make sure I and Tyler weren't overreacting. And ALL OF THEM told me similar stories.
Wierd medication, sudden diagnoses, symptoms magically disappearing when they got out and how they were mistreated in there.

Its been a little over a year now, since I've left. I'm doing good now. Tyler is doing good too. But we both have a strange feeling about our time at the clinic. My family, therapist and doctor tried to get my patient file from the clinic several times. Every single time, they refused. Came up with bullshit excuses or only send parts of the papers. 

Me and some other kids from the clinic researched a little and found the head-doctors private facebook account. And I cant even put this nicely, he was a WILD conspiracy theorist and extreme Trump supporter (we arent in the USA, neither is he from there). When we looked up more about him we barely found anything, even though you'd expect to find more about the head of a mental facility. This is probably not even relevant i just wanted to add it.

I dont know what was going on there but something was utterly wrong. To this day I still cant remember most parts from my time in there, Tyler cant either. Blackouts, blurred and distorted memories plague us both. I am pretty sure I'm not paranoid and I dont believe I was part of some big, crazy thing, I just felt it was creepy and weirdly unsettling. Like they tried to keep us sick instead of helping us. I still shudder when I think back to then.
