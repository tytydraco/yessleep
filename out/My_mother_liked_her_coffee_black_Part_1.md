The best way I can describe this room is bleak.

Featureless concrete walls, a dirty tile floor, and a small circular table. 

Fluorescent lighting overhead.

Feels like I’m in the backrooms. What I wouldn’t give to be there right now.

Instead, I’m in the interrogation room at the county jail. Likely about to join the list of people who’ve come in here knowing their lives outside the bars and the barbed wire have come to an end.

I’ve lost most everything over the last few months. No one vouching for me outside now. 

But the police said I might be able to get off easier if I write everything down, so here goes.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

My mom liked her coffee black. 

Nothing else in it. Just add the paper filter, throw in the grinds, pour in the water, and *voila*. 

Hot, black coffee.

My mom was rigid like that. Grew up an army brat. Strict rules and routines. She didn’t join the military herself, but she did inherit the very black-and-white views of her parents.

Nothing was grey with her. It was either you did, or you didn’t. It was right, or it was wrong.

Don’t get me wrong. She was a great mom. 

Never missed a single game, school event, or a day without a note tucked into my lunchbox.

Then came the milestones.

I went to college, got a job, and moved in with and got engaged to my high school sweetheart. 

In my mom’s eyes, that was all right.

Then I got into sports betting.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

It started when I turned a grand into 75 on my buddy’s bachelor trip at a casino in Vegas. Had me convinced I’d found some sort of cheat code to give Krista the dream wedding she’d always wanted.

Instead, it turned our lives into a nightmare.

Betting apps turned into me connecting with this guy Rick, who was a bookie in his spare time.

Things spiraled from there, until I accidentally used my work card to try and drop a parlay on a work trip and was unceremoniously shown the door by my boss, who had been watching the entire time.

Then, I arrived home to find out I had been shown the door by Krista as well, 

Two suitcases and a garbage bag of my stuff, placed outside our apartment door.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

My parents had differing views on me moving back home to rebuild. My dad was all for it, just as long as I helped out around the house.

My mom…less so.

She accepted it. But by no means was she pleased about it. And she made it abundantly clear. 

I don’t know if it was tough love or some weird attempt at trying to motivate me, but it had the complete opposite effect.

I remember going to a Kentucky Derby party, maybe a few months after I moved back home. 

They were doing one of those Derby hat draws, where you pay $5 to take the name of a horse out of a hat and whoever’s horse wins gets all the money.

Super low stakes. Could hardly even be considered gambling.

And yet my mom felt the need to pipe up in the middle of a crowded room with probably 30 people in it.

“I wouldn’t do that if I were you, Eddie. You’ve already lost your job and your fiancé. What’s next?”

Total silence. Someone coughed. The sliding back door was open while my neighbor grilled, and it was one of those unseasonably cold and rainy late spring days, so the room was actually a little chilly.

But my face *burned* with humiliation. And I didn’t want to call her out right then and there. 

So I just smiled, and laughed it off, like it had been a little jab and not the worst moment of the year for me.

Which was saying something.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

This whole thing got started late one night. I had just gotten home from my job at the time - waiting tables at a local sports bar.

I’d walked in around midnight and been greeted by the smell of freshly brewed coffee, a sign that my mom was up.

You see, my mom had insomnia. It wasn’t uncommon for her to wake up in the middle of the night, make a pot of coffee, and just…stay up, puttering around the house and doing this or that. More than once, I’d woken up to find the house had been deep cleaned overnight.

On this particular night, I entered the house and walked into the kitchen to find her sitting at the table, poring over some papers with a fresh cup next to her.

She’d read a page, then sip. Read a page, then sip.

It went on like clockwork for probably 10 pages before she looked up, gasped, and shrank back.

“Eddie! … you scared me.” She took a deep breath, hand on her chest. “How long have you been standing there?”

“Uhh… maybe 10 minutes? I just got home from work.”

“….oh. You had work tonight?” 

…

“…yeah. What’re you looking at there?”

“Oh, nothing. Just some insurance papers. In case I die.”

“Oh - how much?”

The look she gave me told me exactly what she was thinking, and it took considerable effort not to roll my eyes in response.

“That’s something you’ll find out down the road.” she said, with a wink. “This isn’t for you to use on your betting.”

I nodded, clenching my teeth to avoid saying something I’d regret. Something along the lines of telling her to go fuck herself.

Instead, I simply sighed. Feigned sudden exhaustion and said I was going to bed.

But I decided to take the “long” way upstairs, walking right past my mom at the table. 

As I passed her, I turned my head just enough to glance at the papers in front of her. She had returned to her reading and was so engrossed she didn’t even notice.

There was a dollar value printed. Three digits, then a comma, followed by three zeroes.

Maybe it was the jab she had made towards me a second before, and everything preceding that. All the guilt trips.

But dammit if I didn’t see that number that I knew was at least six figures, and think to myself.

*This would solve all my problems. Instantly.*

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I waited three weeks before doing it. 

Two to make sure this was something I wanted to go through with, then a week to actually plan it out.

Obviously, I’m here now. I’ve confessed. So I’m not going to bore you with the details of it. 

A magician never reveals his secrets, you know?

All I’ll say is, it involved something in her coffee. That’s it.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I lay awake for hours, waiting for her to go downstairs. 

Then, after she did, I waited even longer, unsure as to whether I would need to go down myself and put on the performance of a lifetime.

The creak of my parents’ door answered my question. I heard the heavy footsteps as my dad walked down the stairs. 

And I knew it worked minutes later when I heard the sound of my father’s agonized screaming.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

He had gotten up to use the bathroom. Smelled coffee downstairs and decided to see what she was up to. 

Found her slumped on the couch in the family room, the cup next to her.   

I never found out what the toxicology reports said. Didn’t matter much to me. 

She was dead. No suspicion of foul play. That was enough for me.

But standing there, trying to comfort him as he wept over her, I’d be lying if I said I didn’t feel something.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I cried about it for the first time the day of the funeral, but not for the reasons you might think.

The funeral, through some cruel stroke of luck, took place on what was supposed to be my wedding day.

My dad and I were standing there, next to the open casket, when she walked in.

There was something about seeing her walking toward me - in a simple black dress and a cardigan instead of a beautiful white gown, with her hair in a half ponytail instead of flowing underneath a veil, and with a somber expression of sympathy on her face instead of a radiant smile - that finally brought forth tears that I didn’t have to force as she took me into her arms.

“Eddie…I’m so sorry.”

Her voice cracked a little at the end, and I could feel a hint of dampness on my shoulder as she shed a tear of her own.

“It’s…it’s okay. It was just really, really sudden.”

She pulled back, wiping her face with her hand before lightly placing a hand on my arm. 

The look on her face was so tender, so…kind, that it made me wonder briefly if there was still something there between us. 

Or if she was also thinking about what should have been happening that day.

“Have you been…doing okay?”

Whether she meant my mom, my recovery (if you could even call it that), or both, I wasn’t sure. I forced a rather weak nod.

“Yeah…I guess. Kind of just…hanging in there, you know?”

She smiled, and I felt a new wave of regret washing forward. Over us, and how I’d ruined it.

“I’m…going to find a place to sit. Please don’t hesitate to come talk if you need anything…okay?”

“Right.”

She smiled. Walked away.

I realized I felt rather numb.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

The service went…fine, as far as funerals tended to go. My dad had to ask multiple people to repeat themselves due to his hearing issues, but that was about it.

Everyone went to a local Italian place after, one of my mom’s favorites. My dad had catered a small buffet.

Krista and I made eye contact, several times. She would smile. I would smile back, and then kick myself on the inside for being unable to just go up and talk to her. 

About what, I don’t know. I obviously couldn’t go up and be like “hey, so I actually murdered my mom. Please don’t tell anyone. Okay? Thanks!”

I mostly sat with my dad and kept him company. Could tell he was still as absolutely wrecked as he was the moment he had found her on the couch. I think his eyes had been perpetually red and swollen since that moment a week ago.

I still couldn’t bring myself to feel bad about what I had done to her. But seeing how my actions had affected him?

That shit damn near tore me open.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

Krista stayed after for a bit to help clean up. She had always been fit, but she lost her cardigan at some point due to the exertion of folding tables and whatnot, and I could see she had been working out since the breakup.

Her dress, in the absence of the sweater, had revealed itself to be sleeveless, and her arms to be, well… very toned.

I hadn’t let myself go by any means, but she was clearly working on herself in a way that I was not.

It didn’t do me much good in the events that followed.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I was holding a large tray of lasagna, trying to open my trunk, when there was a voice behind me.

“Hey! You Eddie?”

A man was leaning against the hood of a beat-up black sedan across from me. He wore jeans, a wife beater, and boots.

He clearly hadn’t been at either the funeral or the reception. Lord knows how long he had been sitting in that car, waiting for me to come out. 

It wasn’t until later that I began to wonder just how he’d known I would be there.

“Yeah. You?”

“Doesn’t matter who I am. You got Rick’s money?”

*Fuck.*

“Hey, man. Now’s not really a great time. I just buried my mom."

“Sorry to hear that. Doesn’t matter, though. Rick wants his money.”

“I hear you. Just… tell him I’ll get it to him in a few days. I have some cash coming in. Just need to get a-“

*Crunch.*

Blinding pain coursed through my skull as the thick, meaty fist connected with the side of my face.

My ass hit the ground, and the tray did a somersault then landed facedown on my chest, lasagna flying everywhere.

I squinted up at him through a half-closed, swollen eye as blood poured out of my nose and mouth, the taste of copper assaulting my taste buds.

“What the fuck, man?”

He looked down at me unsympathetically. I couldn’t see his eyes through his tinted sunglasses, but I assumed his expression was rather vacant.

“Rick wants his money. Consider that-"

*Click-clack.* The sound of heels on pavement.

*Shit.*

We both looked over as Krista stopped, then almost dropped her bag, clearly in shock at what she was witnessing.

And then we made eye contact.

And it was immediately clear from the hurt evident on her face, and the hot, angry tears that she had to blink back, what she was thinking.

That I was not, in fact, better. 

And that I was back to all the old habits that had gotten me in trouble and driven us apart in the first place.

Wife Beater looked at her and waved her off like she was nothing more than a curious puppy.

“Get lost. Scram. You didn’t see anything.”

She looked at him. Looked at me, as the first of probably many tears cracked out and ran down her face.

Turned, and ran before I could say anything myself. 

I sensed that that was the last I’d be seeing or hearing of her.

Three months after she’d left, and on the day we should have been exchanging vows before 120 people, we were almost certainly done this time.

For good.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

My dad came out a few minutes later to me still sitting on the ground.

I told him I’d tripped over a shoelace I hadn’t been able to see was undone due to the large to-go box. 

I knew he knew I was full of shit, but he didn’t say anything.

He took me to the ER, where I was released after about an hour with nothing worse than a bruised tailbone and a nasty shiner.

Oh, and some lost dignity. Not to mention a broken heart.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I was lying in bed late that night. 

My dad had gone to bed almost immediately after getting home. I’d showered, then grabbed an old baseball bat out of storage and stashed it in the corner. 

Just in case.

I had a baseball game on. Had been enjoying something resembling a mild high due to the painkillers.

I was beginning to drift off, when I heard a noise from downstairs.

The slow, steady *drip-drip-drip* as fresh coffee began to drip into the pot.

I smiled as sleep began to overtake me.

My mom, making some coffee late at night, as she did many late nights.

Something I had fallen asleep to my entire life.

And then I remembered my mother was dead.
