I’m not sure what I did next. It is a memory of a few moments in time, no more. I ran home, I guess, but afterwards, later that night, dinner, the next days at school – I can’t say. I can give you no exact time or date. All I know is that I saw him lying there just as the *News Mail* wrote it up weeks later:

***Local man found chained to tree, dead***

Or perhaps it took months before the authorities found him. It was not me who reported his presence to the Police; until I saw the headline, I had not wanted to believe he was real. 

I stumbled upon his body one afternoon after school. Most days, I went for long bushwalks out into the scrub, slipping away alone while the other kids went to rugby training or piano lessons.

The memory begins with the silence. I stepped into a sudden clearing, and it was a new place, somewhere I had never been before. Its suddenness made me stop short, because any open area is strange in such dense bushland. And the world was silent. Not a single bird sang, there was no rustle from the undergrowth, no flicker of a lizard’s tail or the buzz of a drowsing fly. 

I saw the man immediately. He lay a few metres away, half-seated and half-lying against the trunk of an ironbark tree. He slumped against that tree like the couples at school, the ones that say they are ‘going out’, letting us all know that they have found someone who likes them best of all. His face was at peace, content, a man relaxing in the sun with his girl.

Then I stepped closer and saw that he was dead. The only living creatures who remained in that clearing were the ones who had come to feed. Corpse fauna. I could see maggots creeping noiselessly through the small wounds in his bare arms and legs. A cluster of carrion beetles had met to feed and their orange shells glowed bright in the afternoon light, winking at me. Then the smell, like rotting metal, hit me, and I began to vomit where I stood, half bent, and the bile ran in thin rivers past my sneakers and puddled into tiny yellow lakes in the dirt. The sound of my retching was the only sound in the world. I stumbled away, still gagging, falling onto my knees and then scrabbling to my feet. I did not look back.

I’m not sure if I could have found that place again, and I never tried. I made no conscious decision not to tell someone, but I think some part of me always knew he was real, and that part of me wanted to keep him to myself. Perhaps it made me feel special for a while. No one else I knew had seen a dead body, outside, in the world, rotting and squirming with small, silent life. It gave me a secret edge. You don’t know what *I* know.
