I used to think getting anonymous photographs was harmless. I don't anymore. The first one arrived three months ago, at 11:43 PM. I was sitting on my couch eating dinner when my phone buzzed. No notification from an app, no message preview, just a picture sent from an unknown number.

It was a photograph of an empty laundromat. Nothing unusual about it. A row of washing machines, fluorescent lights, a vending machine in the corner. The photograph looked like it had been taken through the front window from across the street.

I ignored it. Five minutes later, another photograph arrived. This one showed a supermarket aisle. Then another. A bus stop. Then a parking garage.

I blocked the number. That should have been the end of it. The next morning, I received a photograph from another number. It was the laundromat again. Except this time, I was in the picture.

I was standing outside the building, looking through the window. I recognized the jacket I was wearing. The problem was, I'd never been to that laundromat. I reverse-searched the image and couldn't find anything. I searched the address, called the number on the laundromat's sign, and even drove around the neighborhood trying to find it.

Nothing. There wasn't a laundromat there. I figured somebody was messing with me. I forgot about it for almost a week. Then I received another photograph.

It showed a small convenience store. I was standing inside, facing the refrigerator section. Again, I recognized my clothes. Again, I'd never been there. This time, I noticed something in the reflection of the refrigerator door. Someone was standing behind me.

Their face wasn't visible. I zoomed in until the image became a mess of pixels. They were wearing a dark jacket. I didn't sleep much that night. The photographs continued.

A train station. A hospital corridor. A restaurant. An underground parking lot. Different locations, different days. But I was always somewhere in the frame. Sometimes I was obvious. Sometimes I was just a reflection in a window.

Once, I was barely visible at the end of a hallway. And every single time, the same person was somewhere nearby. Always behind me. Always just far enough away that I couldn't see their face. I started keeping the photographs in a folder on my phone. There were twenty-three of them.

I began noticing another pattern. The photographs weren't showing me where I had been. They were showing me places I would visit later. I tested it. One photograph showed me standing outside a bookstore I'd never heard of. The next day, I deliberately avoided that area.

I took a completely different route home from work. At 6:18 PM, I stopped at a traffic light. Across the street was the bookstore. I had never seen it before. I didn't cross the road. I went home.

Another photograph arrived that night. It showed me standing across the street from the bookstore. Whoever was taking these pictures knew exactly where I was going to be. Or they were making sure I went there. The thought made me sick.

I stopped going out unless I absolutely had to. I changed my routes. I stopped using public transportation. I kept my curtains closed. For a while, nothing happened. Then, one night, my phone buzzed at 2:06 AM.

There was a new photograph. I opened it. It was my bedroom. I was asleep in my bed. The photograph had been taken from inside the room.

I stared at it for several minutes before I could move. My bedroom window was locked. The door was locked. I checked both anyway. Nothing. I called the police.

They searched my apartment and found nothing. The officer told me it was probably some kind of prank. I didn't tell him about the other photographs. I didn't know how to explain them without sounding insane. After he left, I checked my phone again.

Another photograph had arrived. This one was taken from the hallway outside my bedroom. The door was open. I was standing beside my bed. Looking directly at the camera. I wasn't awake.

At least, I didn't remember being awake. I stopped sleeping in my bedroom after that. I spent the next three nights on my couch with every light on. Nothing happened. On the fourth night, I finally fell asleep.

I woke up at 4:31 AM because my phone vibrated against the coffee table. There was another photograph. I opened it. My stomach dropped. It showed me sleeping on the couch.

The photograph had been taken from only a few feet away. I looked around the living room. Nobody was there. Then I noticed something. The photograph showed a person standing behind the couch. I was looking directly at their legs.

Dark pants. Bare feet. I turned around. Nothing. I called the police again. This time, I told them everything.

They checked the building's cameras. The hallway footage showed nobody entering my apartment. Nobody leaving. Nothing. The officer asked me if I knew anyone who might be doing this. I said no. He asked whether I'd recently upset someone.

Again, no. Then he asked me something that I still think about. "Have you ever seen the person taking these photographs?" I told him I hadn't. He looked at me for a long time.

Then he said, "You might want to look more closely." He showed me the photographs I'd given him. We went through them one by one. At first, I couldn't see what he meant. Then he pointed at the first photograph.

The laundromat. There was a reflection in the glass. A person standing across the street. Then he pointed at the supermarket. Same person. The bus stop. Same person. The parking garage. Same person.

Every photograph. Always there. We zoomed in on the clearest image. For the first time, I could almost see the face. It looked like mine. Not similar. Mine.

Same nose. Same eyes. Same scar above my eyebrow. I told the officer I needed to go home. He didn't argue. That night, I deleted every photograph from my phone. I changed my number. I bought a new phone.

I didn't sleep. For three days, nothing happened. I started believing it was over. Then, yesterday, I received one final photograph. It came from my own number. There was no unknown sender. No strange account.

Just my number. The photograph showed me sitting on my couch. Exactly where I'm sitting now. I could see myself holding my phone. Looking at the photograph. Behind me was the hallway. And someone was standing there.

This time, I could see their face clearly. It was mine. I turned around. The hallway was empty. Then my phone buzzed again. Another photograph. Same room. Same couch.

Same hallway. Except the person behind me was closer. Another buzz. Closer. Another. Closer. I stopped opening them. They're still arriving. One every few minutes.

I can see the previews without opening them. The person in the photographs is getting closer to me. The latest preview showed them standing directly behind my couch. I don't want to turn around. I don't know what happens if I do.

So I'm sitting here, staring at my phone, waiting for the next vibration. And I think I finally understand something. The photographs were never showing me where I was going. They were showing me where someone else was going. And I've been following them the entire time.
