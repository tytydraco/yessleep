The Congregation Hall

The rain had been falling since morning.

Not ordinary rain.

It was the kind of rain that seemed to have no intention of stopping. Heavy, continuous, almost violent.

By late July, the monsoon had settled over the Kudremukh range with complete authority. The sky had turned a deep, dirty grey, and the jungle had swallowed whatever remained of daylight.

It was around two in the afternoon.

Yet it looked like evening.

Anirudha didn't care.

He was riding his green Dominar from the Sakleshpur side toward Kudremukh and Sringeri, through one of the most remote stretches of road he had ever taken.

The road cut through deep jungle, with enormous trees standing on both sides, their branches and leaves forming a thick ceiling above him.

There was hardly any traffic.

In fact, there was hardly anyone.

That was exactly why he liked it.

Anirudha wasn't one of those riders who rode motorcycles simply to show how fast they could go.

He was technical about riding to almost an obsessive degree.

He knew what the motorcycle was doing beneath him.

He could feel when the front tyre crossed from good asphalt onto a slightly greasy patch. He noticed subtle changes in the suspension when the road dipped. He understood weight transfer, braking forces, engine braking and available traction almost instinctively.

His speed was around sixty to eighty kilometres per hour.

The rain was absolutely hammering him.

Yet inside his riding gear, he was warm, dry and completely comfortable.

His hands were steady.

His body was relaxed.

The Dominar's engine was humming beneath him.

Water was running across his visor in streams.

He didn't feel rushed.

He didn't feel nervous.

He was simply riding.

This was one of the few places where Anirudha's mind became quiet.

He loved riding for precisely that reason.

He was a deeply analytical person. Even when he wasn't consciously thinking, his brain was constantly processing things.

People.

Machines.

Roads.

Systems.

Patterns.

Why something behaved the way it did.

What caused something else.

What explanation best fitted the evidence.

He had an atheistic tendency that came naturally from this way of thinking.

He didn't hate religion.

He didn't particularly care enough to fight people over it.

He simply had a difficult time believing things because someone said they were true.

If there was a God, fine.

If there wasn't, fine.

But where was the evidence?

He had always been more comfortable saying I don't know than inventing an answer.

That habit would become important later.

For now, he was simply riding through the rain.

Then the road began descending.

A hill descent.

Anirudha eased off slightly.

He had a guest house booked near Kudremukh, farther up the hills.

He looked at the road ahead.

Let's take a small break.

There was no reason to hurry.

Then he saw it.

Something through the rain.

At first he thought it was simply an old house.

But as he approached, the structure became larger.

Much larger.

An old mansion.

Or perhaps a bungalow.

There was a boundary around it, but the boundary had deteriorated badly.

The main gate was made of wood.

It was broken.

The mansion itself stood about twenty metres inside the property.

Anirudha noticed something else.

The property wasn't actually beside the highway.

The main road was roughly two hundred metres away.

A small tar road branched away from it, badly broken and partially swallowed by vegetation. It led toward the old property and then continued in another direction, disappearing into the rain.

Anirudha looked at the mansion.

It was enormous.

Old.

Completely isolated.

He wondered:

Why would someone abandon a property like this?

He didn't think about ghosts.

He thought about property.

Ownership.

Access.

History.

Maintenance.

There had to be a reason.

He decided to stop.

He rode through the broken wooden gate and parked the green Dominar beneath the veranda.

The motorcycle was loaded with everything he needed for the journey.

Touring luggage.

Accessories.

Everything arranged exactly the way he liked it.

He switched off the engine.

The engine's disappearance made the jungle suddenly feel enormous.

Rain.

Only rain.

Anirudha got off the motorcycle, put the stand down and walked toward the veranda.

There was a stone structure there.

He sat down.

The rain continued.

The smell of the place was distinctive.

Old wood.

Wet leaves.

Damp earth.

Something stale underneath all of it.

He opened a small pouch attached outside his luggage.

He always kept snacks there.

Easy access.

There was no point opening the main luggage every time he wanted something to eat.

He ate a little.

Then he looked around again.

The old wooden gate.

The broken road.

The abandoned property.

The jungle.

Nobody.

Not a single person.

He thought again about the mansion.

Why would someone leave something like this?

He drank some water from the bottle mounted on the motorcycle.

Then he noticed his helmet.

The visor was dirty.

Anirudha had a small ritual whenever he stopped during long rides.

Clean the visor.

He had a dedicated cleaning pouch.

Cleaning liquid.

A small microfiber cloth for the visor.

A larger microfiber cloth for the helmet itself.

He took out the small cloth and began cleaning the visor.

It was almost done.

He was relaxed.

The rain was pounding against the old structure.

Then—

Clap.

Anirudha stopped.

It wasn't loud.

But it was distinct.

The kind of sound that immediately attracts attention.

He looked toward the mansion.

Nothing.

He finished cleaning the visor.

Put the cloth and liquid back.

Secured the pouch.

Then he thought:

Enough. Let's go.

He was about to put on his balaclava when—

Clap.

Clap.

Clap.

Three.

A little louder this time.

From inside the mansion.

Anirudha stopped.

He looked toward the wooden door.

His helmet was still on the motorcycle.

He left it there.

And walked toward the door.

The wooden door was old and damaged.

He pushed through it.

Inside, the building was gloomy.

Not completely dark.

The roof had collapsed on one side.

Grey daylight came through the broken structure.

One side of the interior was therefore dimly illuminated.

The other side was dark.

Not pitch black.

Still visible.

Just gloomy.

Anirudha stepped inside.

Immediately he noticed something.

The temperature.

It was colder inside.

Noticeably colder than outside.

And he was still wearing his riding gear.

He could feel the cold against him.

He paused.

His brain registered it.

Nothing more.

Then he walked a few steps toward the broken roof.

The smell changed.

Old wood.

Wet leaves.

Dampness.

Something else.

He looked around carefully.

This wasn't a normal house.

The scale was wrong.

The interior was too large.

The open space was too extensive.

There were signs of a structure built for people.

A place where people gathered.

For a moment he wondered if it had been a factory.

Then perhaps a church.

Or some kind of congregation hall.

Whatever it had been, it didn't feel like someone's residence.

He moved from the entrance toward his right.

About twenty metres.

Toward the side where the broken roof allowed light to enter.

He stopped there.

From this position, he looked toward the darker side of the building.

On the opposite side of the entrance, around twenty to thirty metres away, he could make out a staircase.

It led upward.

Second floor.

Possibly third.

Anirudha didn't move toward it.

He simply looked.

Then—

Clap. Clap. Clap.

Three claps.

Clearly from the direction of the staircase.

Then came a chuckle.

A small one.

Almost casual.

Anirudha stared.

He didn't move.

His brain was already working.

Was there somebody inside?

Some local?

A prank?

An animal?

An echo?

The structure could produce strange acoustics.

He looked upward.

Only then did he realize how large the building actually was.

At least three storeys.

The right side was badly damaged.

The roof had collapsed there.

The left side was still standing.

He mentally reconstructed the structure.

The entrance.

The open central space.

The broken roof.

The staircase.

The upper floors.

The direction of the sound.

He was still trying to understand it when—

Clap. Clap. Clap.

Then a shout.

Anirudha looked toward the sound.

His first thought was simple.

Could be some animal.

He had no reason to stay.

He was already getting late.

He turned toward the entrance.

He started walking back.

He was almost outside.

Then—

A shout.

Right beside his ear.

And a clap.

Extremely close.

Anirudha didn't freeze.

He didn't panic.

He became angry.

Very angry.

There was something about being deliberately startled that hit a particular nerve in him.

He looked around.

There were stones and random pieces of debris nearby.

He grabbed them.

And threw them into the darkness.

"Come out, you motherf—!"

His voice echoed through the building.

More colourful Hindi abuse followed.

He stood there.

Nothing happened.

No response.

No movement.

Nothing.

Anirudha shook his head.

Fine.

He turned again.

Then he heard footsteps.

Step.

Silence.

Step.

Silence.

Step.

Someone was walking toward him.

The footsteps were unmistakable.

Then the clapping started again.

And beneath it came a deep, rumbling voice.

Someone—or something—seemed to be saying something.

But the words were impossible to understand.

Anirudha stared directly toward the sound.

His expression wasn't frightened.

It was a strange combination of anger and amazement.

What the hell is this?

The footsteps stopped.

The clapping continued.

The rumbling voice continued.

It sounded almost as if someone was talking while clapping.

The whole thing was becoming deliberately provocative.

Anirudha's anger rose further.

"Enough, you...!"

More abuse.

"Come show yourself!"

His voice bounced off the old walls.

"I don't give a flying f—!"

The sound continued.

That was it.

He noticed a metal rod lying nearby.

He picked it up.

And threw it hard toward the direction of the voice.

Then he ran.

Straight toward the sound.

And something happened.

He heard movement ahead of him.

Something was running.

Away from him.

Anirudha chased it.

He shouted abuse while running.

For some reason, the thing seemed to be moving away from him.

Almost as if his anger had frightened it.

It moved toward the staircase.

Upstairs.

The clapping continued.

The strange noises continued.

Anirudha picked up a few objects and threw them toward the upper floor.

But he stopped himself from going upstairs.

The building was ancient.

Partially collapsed.

He didn't know the condition of the stairs.

Whatever his personality, he wasn't stupid.

He wasn't going to risk falling through a rotten floor simply because something was making noises upstairs.

He turned back.

The sound continued.

Second floor.

Maybe third.

Somewhere above him.

And his analytical mind finally produced a thought that he couldn't dismiss.

Humans can't run that fast.

He didn't know what it was.

But he knew what it wasn't.

Or at least what it shouldn't have been.

And still—

he wasn't scared.

He was furious.

Partly because the thing was provoking him.

Partly because he couldn't explain it.

And partly because he was getting late.

He walked back toward the entrance.

Then out through the main gate.

Even outside, the sound continued.

Clapping.

Strange noises.

Apparently coming from the upper-floor area.

Anirudha put his gear back on.

He put on his helmet.

Got onto the Dominar.

Started the engine.

He was about to ride away.

Then—

A shrill shout.

Anirudha stopped.

He looked up.

Second floor.

A window.

There was something standing there.

A dark figure.

He couldn't see its face.

But he could see its eyes.

They appeared to glow.

The figure was looking directly at him.

Anirudha stared back.

His helmet was still on the motorcycle.

Rain was pouring over his head.

His clothes were getting wet.

His face was wet.

He didn't care.

His eyes remained fixed on the figure.

Furious.

The figure stared down.

Anirudha bent down.

Picked up a stone.

He aimed.

And threw it directly toward the figure.

He never broke eye contact.

For a few seconds, nothing happened.

Just rain.

The dark figure.

The green Dominar.

The old mansion.

The jungle.

Then the figure moved.

Without making a sound.

It disappeared from the window.

Anirudha continued looking.

He waited.

A minute.

Then another.

Nothing.

Eventually, he went back to the veranda.

Sat down.

And stayed there for a while.

He deliberately dried his head.

Almost angrily.

Almost defiantly.

As if he were telling the mansion:

That's all you've got?

Finally, he got back on the motorcycle.

Started the engine.

Rode through the broken wooden gate.

Followed the damaged tar road back toward the main road.

Reached the highway.

Then began the climb toward Kudremukh.

The guest house was almost empty.

It was a weekday.

There weren't many riders around.

In fact, there were only two other people there.

A middle-aged caretaker.

And a younger worker who helped him.

Anirudha changed out of his riding gear and put on warm clothes.

He had dinner.

The normality of the place felt almost strange after what had happened.

At some point, he casually asked the caretaker about the old mansion.

The man's expression changed.

"You went there?"

Anirudha didn't answer immediately.

The caretaker told him the old building had once been a congregation hall.

It had been built a very long time ago, during the British period, supposedly by British officers.

Then came the other story.

One of the officers' wives had apparently died by suicide.

Afterward, stories began circulating among the locals.

People claimed they sometimes heard her inside the old building.

Voices.

Sounds.

Something moving.

The caretaker spoke about it as an old local legend.

Not as proven fact.

Anirudha listened quietly.

He didn't tell him everything.

He didn't describe the clapping.

Didn't describe the footsteps.

Didn't mention the voice.

Didn't mention the figure.

He simply sat there.

And thought:

Strange experience.

That was it.

He wasn't suddenly converted into a believer.

He wasn't sitting there thinking:

Ghost.

His mind didn't work that way.

He had experienced something he couldn't explain.

That was all.

For now.

He went to his room.

The rain continued outside.

Eventually, he fell asleep.

He dreamed about the mansion.

Anirudha was standing inside it again.

But this time there was no rain.

No jungle.

No Dominar.

No sound except the slow dripping of water somewhere inside the building.

The place looked different.

Older.

Darker.

He could see the staircase clearly.

There was someone standing at the top.

He couldn't see her face.

She didn't move.

Anirudha started walking toward the staircase.

Then he heard it.

Clap.

He stopped.

Clap.

Clap.

Clap.

The figure raised her head.

And looked directly at him.

Anirudha couldn't see her face.

But somehow he knew she was looking at him.

Then he heard a voice.

Not loud.

Not angry.

Almost a whisper.

"Come back alone."

Anirudha woke up.

It was still dark.

For a few seconds he didn't know where he was.

The guest house.

His luggage.

His helmet.

Everything was normal.

He checked the time.

Three in the morning.

He sat there for a while.

Then laughed quietly at himself.

"Just a fucking dream."

He lay back down.

But he didn't sleep immediately.

Because the mansion was still on his return route.

Five days.

Maybe ten.

And if he took another route, he wouldn't pass that road again until the following winter or monsoon.

He closed his eyes.

He told himself it was just a dream.

There was no reason to go back.

No reason at all.

And yet, somewhere in the back of his mind, one thought remained.

Not fear.

Not belief.

Just an unanswered question.

What the hell was that place?
