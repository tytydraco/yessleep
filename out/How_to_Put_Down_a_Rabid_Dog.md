Growing up I read "How To Kill a Mockingbird" many times. Not so much read as I heard it read to me a lot.

 

My father and mother were both lawyers. Dad especially liked that book, he took Atticus as a role model, an example to be followed not just by a juris doctor but by anyone living in our society.

 

I was so fascinated by a specific passage of the book. The one where Atticus kills a rabid dog that shows up. He takes up his rifle and shoots it down right then and there, did what had to be done.

 

A rabid animal is too dangerous to be allowed to roam freely. They are already gone the moment you can see the foam out of their mouths, dead and buried. I was in awe of the efficiency of the shot. BAM, the beast drops dead and everyone watches the deed being done in awe.

 

I had to put down many dogs over the years. Good for nothing mangy mutts that mostly got in the way. No one would miss them.

 

The first one I was a preteen. I was excited at the opportunity to see to my dad's example of a man. There was this little whelp that kept getting on my nerves - played too rough, barked too loud, bit and complained. 



Took it to the backyard and bashed it's head in with a rock almost too heavy for me to lift over his head. Done and done in about three hits.

 

I buried it deep in the backyard behind some bushes before mom and dad got home from court. We three had done our duty and dealt with justice that day, more or less. Their boy was exasperated from playing with his friend all day in the creek, they figured - seeing all the dirt and sweat covering my body, plus my uneasiness that got mistook by my usual hyper activeness.

 

I dealt with the many dogs that life threw at me. They bit more than they could chew, bit the wrong hands and got done in. I would get so used to it sometimes my mind would wander, I'd find myself tiredly kicking their guts in an alleyway - I was cleaning up the streets and could get carried away.

 

Not always at my own whims I’d have to do it, too. Wouldn't be too sparsely people would call me to deal with their own problem dogs. No mind, it would pay well because no one else would do it.

 

Mom and dad weren’t around much, you know? They had too much work, many long hours; I pretty much raised myself. Every family has their black sheep, and I was an only child, so I had to bear that cross, I guess. Into my late teens I was out and about on the streets, one day I went away and never came back, got caught up with a bunch of butchers that took me in – pig slaughterers, sheep killers and dog dispatchers.

 

I only did what I thought had to be done. I did enjoy it though, you can tell. It felt good, I did well by my father's example, I served justice, I kept people safe from plague, feel me?

 

I would show on the news from time to time. Well, I wouldn't, but my feats did. Many animals die in the city so very few people care, but every now and then I would end up hurting a person, or maybe killing some rich pricks prize pooch.

 

I can't be faulted, really. In the mess and confusion you get caught up in so much fuzz, the tools for the job get exponentially more destructive as you have to kill sometimes a pack of strays. Guns, sub-machineguns. Run the Block and shoot the fucking things dead. You hurt some people in the scuffle. Some of us couldn’t give much of a fuck about other people.

 

You get seem kicking a pup in the Alley by old Ms. Johnson and she goes screaming about it, what the fuck do you do then? Had to be done, didn't it? Sometimes shit is rough. 

 

The problem with being good at something is that you start to get more and more careless. You slip; you get sloppy. It all started to get to me as well, you know? The methods get sloppier and the mind slips more and more.

 

I let myself go. What did me in was an accident, trust me? I ended up killing a man. Really unnecessary, not any worth in doing what I did that night. I was always on edge back then; shit started to really get to me. Drugs, alcohol, heavy stuff, man. All paranoid, straight schizo.

 

Got hammered in a bar and couldn't get back home on my own, my buddies let me out way outside of town and I guess they forgot the way back after they told me they wouldn't be long. Hitched a ride with a dude that was going the same way.

 

The guy seemed cool at first. We were talking, shooting the shit. But every now and then he would make a little offhanded remark. "You wouldn't know it" this, "i don't believe you" that. He'd give me them side eyes too, those sketchy, skeptical looks. I didn't like it.

 

I started seething when the fucker told me to "tone it down" when i was talking. Then, the straw the broke the camel's back. Asshole snapped his fingers at me, like i was some god damn fucking dog.

 

Who the hell did he think he was? The mongrel was barking orders at me now? Snapping his fingers to get my attention and shit; I saw red then. Grabbed the fucker’s throat and started punching his nose in. He wasn’t even putting up a fight anymore, whimpering like a crying pup, gargling on his blood like a kitten drowning in a sack.

 

I bashed his head into the dashboard. Grabbed the mongrel by the throat, bit his shit deep in, gnashed his arteries. Grabbed his scalp and bashed his dome into the driver’s window, many, many times. Broke the window and kept at it until I heard his head crack and it felt like hitting a wet sponge against a wall. The dry thuds that bounced his skull back to me slowly had turned into wet sloshes that started to offer more and more resistance every time i pulled his scalp back up for another hit. Soon I was holding a bunch of hair and meat and bone.  

 

I came to my senses when I was almost fully exhausted. My entire body painted red alongside the whole of the front seats, my hands cut up and mine and his blood mixing in a shaking bowl of muscle and trembling, adrenaline fueled rage. By that time the car had come to a standstill at the side of the road, miraculously not crashing. I took a few breaths and got out.

 

Soon as I took a few steps on the road, I saw a family of deer. A doe and her fauns. I could keep walking, you know? Go the other direction. They couldn’t see me all that well anyways, not under that facemask of blood I wore. They backed away and started to run away, and in that moment, I felt like a predator foaming at the mouth wanting to give chase, blood rushing and a one-way mind to catch them – that wide-eyed expression of fear and helplessness an invitation to savagery.

 

Catching them was surprisingly hard. Does run quick and the fauns are fleetfooted. The adrenaline makes everyone much faster, but I was exhausted and they were running for their lives. They shrieked as they hurriedly pranced away.

 

Not too far along the way I got caught by the pigs. The family had caught the attention of everyone around, hollering and screaming, pointing at me. I tried to run away and got a pretty good distance on them for a good while, but they released the K9s on me.

 

At that moment I just kinda gave up, you know? I mean, I wouldn’t hurt a pup, I got a soft spot for the furry things. They chomped and gnawed at me, and I left it at that. Guess I kind of deserved it. I had a puppy when I was a kid and loved the little rascal. He died of old age and I never had another canine after him, shit devastated me. I could never bring myself to hurt an innocent creature.

 

On the way to the station, they all screamed at me, the animals. Sheep and dog alike in a choir of indignation. I saw their faces, all of them. In the crowd I saw it, many had those red eyes and foamy mouths, rabid fucking things, hypocrites.

 

Now I’m here in the pigpen. The fucking slaughterhouse I call it. All these mangy mutts foaming at the mouth, veiny eyes looking you up and down, ready to bite out your neck. And the pigs look down at us up top, just the same, slobbering over their garments and squealing when the chance comes to beat up a stray. Violence for violence.

 

It's all fucked. I look at myself in the mirror every now and then, I can see my bloodshot eyes and dripping mouth. You look scared too, you know? Like a hare staring down a predator. You wish you could fuck me up, don't you? I see it in you, you're all rabid.

 

I’m all the same, too. I do get it, you know? I understand this is the place that people like me come to, but maybe this is a bit too much, isn’t it?

 

After all, I did hurt some people, but I only did ever put down rabid dogs. 

 

\*Excerpt transcribed from the interview of death row inmate Richard F. Haygrave, condemned on accounts of 1^(st) and 2^(nd) degree murder, armed robbery, drug possession and aggravated battery.
