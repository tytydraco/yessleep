"The Depths are calling to me. They have told me Hell is opening up tonight. They have said Beelzebub is stirring. They speak of the Harpies and the Heretics, the Sinners and the Selkies, the Netherbound and the Nephilim; the congregation of the Church of the Inferno. He rises tonight." 

 I stared at Charles for a while. Such a truly unique man, he was. Well educated, charming, handsome, innocent; all of the qualities that matter in this life. And yet here he was, confined to the brig.

 "Charley, I want to help you, but you have to give up all these fanciful notions. Do you understand the sickness in your mind? Or do you believe in these delusions?"

 Charles only held my gaze. He rarely blinked, and constantly picked at the ragged uniform he wore.

 The frigate creaked and groaned in protest against the ever restless sea as we continued our patrol of the trade route. A wicked storm had been brewing for the whole day. A storm which was otherworldly in its power. 

 I should have been up there doing my duty. But yet, against my will, I was confined here. Stuck in the moldy, rank cell next to this man whom I had once called a brother, but now considered a complete stranger. 

 "I am damned. Cursed with this knowledge. And you treat me like some asylum patient. You are a true bastard, William. Pretending to not know the fate that is written upon my forehead. And upon yours." He spat this at me like a cobra spitting venom. 

 Such lucidity was sobering. It made me see the man he used to be. Three days ago, we had been playing checkers and laughing about our college days. Of the girls we had loved and lost, and of the families we left behind for the call of the great waters. Those last moments of normality were precious. 

 I didn't even consider his last statement. I should have.

 The normality was shattered just a few hours after our game of checkers. Night had fallen, and I was below reading some book or other when I heard a chorus of raised voices from above. I sprinted up just in time to see Charles being restrained by three men. They could barely hold him.

 He was foaming at the mouth, and his eyes were wide and red. He was screaming; deep, gutteral, gasping screams from the deepest pit of his heart. 

 I ran to him and tried to calm him, but he was beyond consolation or help. He tried to bite me, and succeeded. A small chunk of my shoulder tore off into his snarling mouth. And recoiling in pain, I reacted as I would in any situation like this: I struck him in the face, and broke his nose with one strike. 

 He yowled in pain, and fell to his knees. This undoubtedly was what woke him from this fit. He began to sob as he found his voice again. And he spoke thus: 

 "HELP ME, OH LORD JEHOVAH HELP ME!" 

 He took a raspy breath, then continued:

 "I was looking over the port side when my eyes caught a glimpse of the end of times. The eye of Poseidon opened and gazed slowly upon me. His face was gargantuan, and His eye could have swallowed this ship with one blink. 

 He told me of the coming Age. Of the days of Darkness and Blight that begin tonight. They begin TONIGHT! We must find a way to warn the world. We must. We must. Must must must mustmustmust...." 

 He began to mumble then, and his incoherent babble was all the Captain needed to order him to the brig. The moment he made the order, I ran up to him. For the first time in all the years I had served under him, he looked afraid. 

 "Captain please, I beg you, don't lock him away. I know him well, and he would never do or say these things without proper provocation." 

 "Did a shark happen to jump up on deck and take that bite out of you?" The Captain asked dryly. The uneven placement of his words betrayed his fear. He was a truly superstitious man.

 "With all due respect, Captain, I believe he suffered from a momentary mania caused by stress or poor diet. He-" 

 "Do not make excuses for him unless you are willing to defend him from the brig as well."

 Such an ultimatum was strange and out of place for the Captain. He was usually a stoic, good-tempered man. He must have caught the mania. 

 "Sir, what is the matter? Why do you say such things?" 

 "Embry, clap him in irons."

 "Sir! I have done nothing to deserve this!" 

 "Do as I say, Embry! Take him down as well." 

 Embry reluctantly restrained me. I dared not fight back, because Embry was a large man who could easily kill me if he chose. Slowly, Embry lead me down into the dankest, darkest corner of the ship. Into the brig.

 For two days I had rotted there. And my anger had grown to a fever pitch this night. Which was only made worse by the storm that raged above us. I had tried to reason with him. To plead with him. To pray for him. And yet, all he did was fall into fits of delirium and terror. By this night, I had had enough. 

 "You are the reason I am here. I didn't deserve this." I was booring holes into Charles with my eyes. My anger radiating and making me feel sick. 

 I was so busy with my emotions, I had neglected to notice the hulking frame of Embry lurch into the room. By the time I had registered his presence, he fell to his knees in front of Charles' cell. 

 I gasped at the sight of him. He looked like he had been in the fight of his life, and had lost. His arm was missing. A stump was the only thing left; blood gushed freely from the freshly cleaved faucet where his arm once was. 

 "Embry..." I started, but could get no further.

 Without a word, he removed a set of keys from his pants, and unlocked Charles' door. 

 "You were right. Hell is already here. Without a sound, we were set upon. My arm was gone before I knew what was happening. My arm is gone. My arm... The Captain! Oh, dear Captain. He was the first of us. He somehow knew this would happen. Before the sky, he slit his own throat... Oh, gentlemen. Pray.... " 

 Embry fell then. Never to rise again. 

 Charles wordlessly grabbed the keys from Embry's fresh corpse, and unlocked my door. 

 "Thank you, friend." I said cooly. I still didn't trust him fully, but he had freed me, and I was genuinely thankful. 

 "Follow me, and do not say a word." He said with not a hint of emotion.  

 Slowly, he exited the room with me in tow. Something I had just noticed was that the world was quiet. Not just that the storm had stopped its howling, but that I couldn't hear the sea or the ship. All I heard was our collective breath and our footsteps as we ascended into hell. 

 When we reached the trapdoor to the deck, Charles stopped and turned to face me. 

 "You will see many things this night, William. The Lord did not intend for mortal men to view His other creations. But we must look upon them now. And bear witness to His forgotten children." 

 I couldn't comprehend what I had just witnessed with Embry, and the tragedy of the Captain's fate was hard to swallow. And combined with this mad prophet's ramblings of Revelation, my mind was torn assunder. With no other option, without another word, I nodded to him and he opened the trapdoor... 

 The sight that greeted my eyes was hard to describe. But I will attempt. 

 The sky had opened. Not a hole in the sky, but the sky itself. The veil had been parted, and visitors were making their way through the gap between the worlds. 

 It was completely silent. Not a sound could be heard except for our collectively shallow breaths. We stood in this unholy quietude and bore witness as Charles had said. We two alone would receive this blessing. This curse. 

 I walked out onto the deck in a awestruck stupor; like a babe taking its first steps. And I struggled to grasp what I was watching. 

 At the farthest reaches of the cosmic void that had replaced our once beautiful blue sky, a figure sat on a throne. He was in shadow. But I knew this was Beelzebub. For from his mouth, galaxies were being spun into existence. Wicked, black masses of rock and dark waters were being vulcanized and shaped into kingdoms and continents. 

 And from his loins came legions of innumerable terrors. All of our darkest dreams were not mere fantasies, but realities. 

 Leviathan, the great whale, glided among the inky particles of dark matter as it sliced its way towards our virgin seas. 

 On chariots of fire rode four horsemen, all armed for battle, and all dripping flesh as if they flew too close to the sun. 

 Minotaurs and centaurs bounded from where their constellations once stood and tumbled their way down, down, down into the kingdoms of blackness that were making their way to rest on the awaiting ocean.

 Ra, Sun God of Egypt, flew on the wings of a hawk; serene and terrible to behold. It would be king of this realm, if its Father allowed. 

 Lilith, the sin-stained goddess of night and chaos, glided gracefully through the Milky Way. I was drawn to her immediately. As are all men, before they lose their souls to the Tempter. 

 Beast after beast, evil after evil, sickness after sickness came hurtling down to us. And we could only watch. 

 Charles reached out a hand to me, and I took it. 

 "Why?" I whispered to him.

 "There must always be witnesses." He said as he looked into my eyes. The weight of the world had fallen upon him. He was no longer Charley. He was just a witness. And so was I.

 These evils had not been seen for millennia. They had been banished by our Lord, Jesus Christ, at Golgotha. 

 But Christ had already come for His own. And He would not be returning. 

 Never again would He return to this forsaken place. 

 To these foul and filthy depths.
