My phone vibrated against the nightstand at 3:33 AM. The screen illuminated the dark bedroom, casting a pale glare across the ceiling.



“She’s in the basement again,” the text read. It was from Sarah, my wife, sent from downstairs.



We had bought the old Victorian home three months ago, but the basement remained a damp, mold-scented void we rarely touched. Lately, Sarah had started sleepwalking. I’d find her standing near the cellar door, staring intently at the rusted latch, whisper-counting backward from one hundred.



I threw off the duvet, grabbed a flashlight, and stepped into the hallway. The floorboards groaned beneath my bare feet. When I reached the kitchen, the basement door was standing wide open, revealing a black square of absolute nothingness.



"Sarah?" I called into the dark.



Only a wet sloshing answered me, followed by a sound like dry branches snapping.



I flicked on the flashlight and descended the wooden steps. The beam cut through the thick dust. Halfway down, the metallic stench of copper hit my throat so hard I gagged.



At the bottom of the stairs, Sarah was kneeling on the cold concrete.



Her back was turned to me, her shoulders trembling. The pale skin of her spine was smeared with deep, glistening crimson. In front of her lay our golden retriever, Barnaby. The dog was horribly, brutally torn open—his ribcage cracked outward like a shattered birdcage, glistening organs spilled across the stained concrete.



"Sarah... oh my fucking God, Sarah, what did you do?" my voice cracked.



She didn't turn around. Instead, her hands kept moving, plunged deep into the dog's cavernous torso, pulling out slick strands of meat and stuffing them …oh lord she was stuffing them violently into her own mouth. The wet crunch of chewing echoed through the room.”im dreaming im dreaming”I mumbled to myself,as if they were any consolation against the absolute terror that was unfolding in front of me.



Panic clawed at my chest. I rushed forward to grab her shoulder, pulling her back. "Stop it! Fucking Stop!"



She spun around instantly, throwing her head back with an unnatural screech.



Her jaw had fucking unhinged, tears of flesh hanging from the corners of her mouth where the skin had ripped apart to accommodate the unnatural stretch. Her eyes were gone—just …oh my god they were just empty, bleeding sockets leaking dark fluid down her pale cheeks. She thrust a hand into her own open stomach, where a wide, jagged tear revealed her own glistening entrails pulsing in the flashlight beam. She wasn't just eating the dog; she…she was frantically stuffing its organs into her own torn abdomen, trying to patch herself together I realized.I lost my dinner and probably everything I’d ever eaten then and there.



I shrieked, dropping the flashlight. It rolled across the floor, illuminating the far corner of the cellar.



My breath caught in my throat.



Lying slumped against the wall, bound in heavy duct tape and covered in flies and maggots, was Sarah. Her face was pale, cold, and her eyes were frozen open in terror. She had been dead for days.



I slowly backed away,bile rising to my throat anf my heart hammering against my ribs, and fumbled in my pocket for my phone. The screen lit up with another notification.



A text from Sarah: “She’s in the basement again.”



I looked back down at my hands. They were covered in dried blood. My fingernails were torn off, packed with dirt and raw meat. I didn't have a phone in my pocket. I was staring at a blank piece of cracked plastic.



I reached up to touch my face, but my fingers sank straight through empty sockets, hitting slick, wet bone.



The wet sound of my own hand exploring the cavern inside my skull echoed through the darkness. There were no eyes left to cry, but dark, viscous grease seeped down my cheeks, hot and thick.



I didn't control my feet as they shuffled forward. My body moved with a violent, jerking twitch, operating on some horrific autopilot. I dropped to my knees beside the rotting corpse of my wife, the real Sarah, bound in tape against the cold foundation wall.



Her neck was broken at a sharp, unnatural angle. Massive, brutal bite marks were torn into her throat and shoulders—human teeth patterns, wide and jagged.



My teeth patterns.



Memory flooded back not as thoughts, but as sensory spikes: the metallic tang of her blood on my tongue three nights ago; the sickening crunch of her throat yielding under my jaws while I sobbed inside my own locked mind; the frantic urge to fix what I had broken, to sew her back together with whatever meat I could find in the dark.



I looked down at the pale, hollow entity sitting beside me. It wasn't a demon. It wasn't a double.



It was a mirror.



It was a decaying shell I had carved out of my own flesh during my lost hours, an armless, mindless puppet built from torn skin and stolen organs, operating on the final, distorted memory of my wife. It opened its torn, hinged jaw, emitting a wheezing sound like a leaking pipe.



Above us, the ceiling creaked.



Heavy, slow footsteps began to walk across the kitchen floor directly over our heads. Step. Drag. Step. Drag.



Then, the floorboards right above the cellar door groaned. The door at the top of the stairs clicked open. Light spilled down the wooden steps, framing a tall, dark silhouette standing in the doorway.



The figure held up a phone. The blue glare illuminated a face I knew intimately—unblemished skin, warm eyes, and a frantic, terrified expression.



It was me.



The me standing at the top of the stairs looked down into the pit, flicked on a flashlight, and called out into the dark:



"Sarah? Is that you?"



Down in the black, I felt my unhinged jaw force itself open. My chest tore itself wider to make room. I didn't want to move, but my hands reached out into the cold dust, ready to pull the next version of myself down into the wet, waiting dark.
