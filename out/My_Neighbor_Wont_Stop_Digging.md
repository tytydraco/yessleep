Greg was a totally normal person by all accounts. Everyone I knew in the neighborhood thought well of him as far as I could tell. I even helped him build an extension onto his house so he’d have a guest room for his daughter to sleep in whenever she visited, which occurred irregularly but still frequently. He was on the fresher side of sixty, retired, and liked to play golf. I’d known him about five years before, one day, he just started digging. From dawn to dusk he never stopped. It only took a day or two before everyone in the neighborhood got nervous and asked me to talk to him. I apprehensively accepted the job. Being closest to him I guess I thought I had more skin in the game.   


Our houses faced away from each other so I could talk to him from behind my backyard fence, which was only about waist high but still made me more comfortable. He dug with a machine-like rhythm. The exact same amount of time between each strike of the shovel. I rested one hand on my fence in an attempt to appear relaxed.  


“Hey Greg, say uhh… watcha doin there?” With one more strike he left his implement in the earth.   


“Sod farming,” he spoke monotone, then he smiled. “Family business!” He went straight back to digging. I was about to press further before I realised he hadn’t stopped looking at me. The digging was just as precise as before. His face wore the mask of hard work as his eyes, completely relaxed, looked straight at me.   


“Uh yeah okay. Well, good luck with that!” I walked as calmly as I could back to my house. The beat of his shovel I unconsciously followed with each step.  


As each day passed the hole got deeper and deeper. I kept some kind of track of his progress by the amount of dirt he was unloading onto his yard, which he pushed to the edges of the property creating a mountain that wrapped around his entire backyard.  


As odd as it might sound, the neighborhood became sort of desensitized to it. He wasn’t hurting anyone and no one had his daughter’s number to check in with her so we just kind of waited and watched. Or, I waited and watched. From my window the hole was still visible. I set up a makeshift lookout post with some binoculars on a tripod and a tent made from end tables and my dark red comforter.   


The second thing of note was that he stopped coming out. Prior to this he had a bunch of coolers delivered to his home which he took down into the hole. Once those were down there I never saw him again. At least, not with my binoculars. The mounds of dirt and eventually rock continued to grow in size despite this. The time soon came that his daughter would usually visit but I did not see her come or go. I couldn’t see the driveway from my vantage point though and at that point I was too freaked out to go anywhere near that house. I smoked some weed, tried to calm down, and watched.   


He had a concrete mixer delivered to his house, which he took down the hole. After that, it was various household appliances like fridges, ovens, and shit. Enough to start a fine dining establishment. Then, I heard it for the first time. That divine flute, playing a light, airy melody. At that moment I remembered. His daughter plays the flute.  


Later that week my friend Sally came over for dinner. This was a semi-regular thing where I’d make dinner for her and she’d come straight over from work. She was a beat cop and worked crazy hours. This was how she liked to start her weekends. That particular evening I made us homemade BBQ chicken pizza, packed six joints, and had some Arizona Iced tea in the fridge. Ginseng and honey to be specific.   


“How’s the neighbor thing going?” She asked after exchanging pleasantries.  
	

“Not since I told you about the flute.” I lit her joint. “Honestly I’ve been trying not to think about it too much.”  
	

“Oh my bad.”  


“No, it’s okay, it’s not like that.”  She still had her uniform on but it was in what she called casual mode. The button up was untucked and unbuttoned revealing a white undershirt, she sighed with relief as she sat and took her belt off. I went to retrieve the pizza from the oven. As was customary, not to brag too much, a long period of silent eating followed. We wordlessly passed the joint back and forth. Two joints later there was no pizza left. Sally slouched in her chair and let her entire body relax.  


“Oh man… thank you.”  


“Anytime,” I stood and started cleaning up the table.  


“Oh shit let me help hold on,”  


“Dog, hell no.” She raised her hands in mock surrender then collapsed back into the chair.  
	

I put some music on with my record player. I brought water and we kicked up our feet with another joint and just listened. I put Miles Davis’ Kind Of Blue on. Our brains swirled through the music and for about twenty minutes our minds were worry free.   


It was after that I noticed an errant discord in the music. I heard a flute. Not even remotely the same time signature it was way slower. Like a lullaby. I remembered.  


“The fuck is that?”  


“It’s the flute I told you about.” I turned the jazz off, kind of pissed that shit got interrupted. “This guy is driving me fucking crazy.” Sally looked out the back window.  


“Uhh, there’s whole families just walking calmly into the hole.”  


“What?” I went all the way up to my lookout position and saw it for myself. All the families were hand in hand. The hole was wide enough that four or five people could easily go in at a time. “What the fuck!?”  


Sally went out into the street to my left and tried to talk to the people with her uniform back in professional mode, she even had her vest on. I joined her and watched as everyone calmly ignored us. When we stood in front of them they simply said.  


“Excuse me,” with a fake smile and then they would just go around. Everyone said those same two words the exact same way. They also all walked barefoot dressed in nightgowns, underwear, and pajamas.    


“I’m gonna call Merle. I don’t think I can explain this to dispatch.”  


“Sally, please try.”  


“Yeah I’ll try.” She pressed.   


“Are you fuckin’ high?” I could hear the dispatcher on the other end laughing. Eventually it became a chorus of laughing voices.   


“No guys, I'm being completely serious. Hold on, let me call one of you on facetime.”  


“Ain’t no shot, I can’t wai-” The lights that I had left on, which turned my house into a beacon, suddenly went dark. The laughter, cut off, left the sound of shuffling feet and the kicking of loose rocks.  


“Shit, do you have any idea what is happening?”  


“Fuck if I know. All he ever told me was that he was sod farming.”  


“Jesus, do you think Merle can hear the flute from his place.” Almost in answer, we heard the honking of a car horn and slowly but surely headlights rounded the corner onto my street.  


“Get out of the way damn it!” Merle’s yell echoed to us. “Can’t you fuckin idiots hear me!”  


“I don’t think they can, Merle!” The response was delayed.  


“I know, I’m just venting some of my anxiety!” He decided to put the car in park and walk the rest of the way. He had his uniform on but no vest.  


“Merle, why don’t yo-”  


“Don’t argue with me, woman. Now, should we commence investigating or are we gonna just keep fussin’ around.” We decided to table the discussion before making our way to the front of the house.   


“That’s his daughter’s car.” I said.  


“How long has she been here?” Sally asked.  


“I don’t know, the neighborhood was supposed to keep their eyes peeled.”  


Merle ran up and kicked the front door open in one grand motion. Sally just stood a few feet from the door.  


“Are you drunk?”  


“Ya’ll are both high so I don’t want to hear it.” He drew his gun and rushed into the building, dramatically clearing corners.  


“Merle can you just chill the fuck out?”  


We looked through the whole house and found no sign of anyone. It was like the house was ready to get sold. Rooms were all clean, beds made, no signs of human inhabitance, not even a toothbrush. The oven, the fridge, and the washing machine had been removed.   
	

“Your neighbor dug this by himself?” Merle asked.  
	

“Yep,” I answered.  
	

“He brought a concrete mixer down there too, so there’s no telling what he’s got down there.” Sally said.  
	

“Shit, don’t you think we should wait for backup?” Merle was sweating.  
	

“What happened to your jingoist spirit?” She asked.  
	

“Oh fuck it.” He started walking down the hole. We followed. I can’t remember how long we were walking. Wooden beams held the ground above. He made some concrete pillars as well.  
	

“He took great care to make this safe,” Sally said.   
	

“Long lasting.” I said. Eventually we came across a room carved into the side of the tunnel. Up some wooden steps and we were suddenly in an idyllic suburban kitchen. A door led to an empty bedroom.  
	

“How long has he been down here?” Merle asked. He was breathing short.  
	

“Half a year, give or take.” I responded. He gave a dry chuckle that was more of a scoff. The air seemed to get heavier as we went further down. Eventually we rounded a corner and entered a corridor that led to a set blood red double doors.  
	

“What the fuck?” Merle began hyperventilating.  
	

“Merle?” Sally stepped closer and put her hand on his shoulder. “It’s okay, it’s just a door.”  


He seemed almost in pain then, whimpering. “No it’s not!” he yelled. “It’s not just a door!” he reached for his gun. Sally stepped back doing the same.  
	

“Merle, don't do that.” His eyes locked onto mine. The flute shrilled.  
	

“It’s not just a door,” spoken calmly. He unholstered his gun and began raising it, level with those eyes. Sally was faster, she quickly drew and shot one time center mass. Merle still squeezed the trigger. The bullet hit my left calf and hurt like hell. I screamed and fell to the ground, clutching my leg.   
	

“Damn it Merle!” she reappraised and drew her attention to me. “Are you okay?”  
	

“Yeah I’m great,” I winced. She wrapped my leg with a piece of Merle’s shirt.   
	

“Why didn’t he wear his vest?” She was crying. I pulled her into a tight hug.  
	

“You saved my life.” I said.  
	

“Can you walk on the other leg?”  
	

“Yeah.”  
	

“Okay, let’s get you out of here.”  
	

“No wait.”  
	

“What?”  
	

“Try the door.”  
	

“What!?”  
	

“They might need help. I’ll be fine.”  
	

“Stay right there.”  
	

“Of course,” I lied. She opened the red doors. I crawled in behind her.  


We were welcomed with silence as we entered. The room was bathed in golden light from four light fixtures at each corner and an impressive chandelier hanging from the high abyssal ceiling. Looking into the darkness, I felt a terrible unease so I quickly leveled my eyes with the scene before us.  
	

My neighbors were all neatly arranged around a huge banquet table. I’d say there were about fifty people down there. I didn’t see any of the kids. What I did see, at the head of the table opposite me, was a woman. She sat on a bare stone pedestal with no back. Her skin was dried out and pulled taut against her bones. Hanging from strings that led up into the void, a flute rested. Hands clutched the instrument but she sat frozen with her eyes closed and a frown of deep sorrow.  
	

Greg entered from a door behind her with a tray filled with plates of fresh food. There was meat, potatoes, and carrots on every single plate. He kissed his daughter on the head before serving everyone at the table.  
	

“Ah, you made it.” He said as he neared Sally and I. “And you brought a plus one how nice. Please take a seat, dinner just finished. There aren’t plates for you but I’ll hop back there lickety split as soon as I serve everyone else.” He passed us and continued his way down the opposite side of the table. Sally had her hand on the holstered pistol and I could see the thoughts in her head as she debated drawing on Greg.   
	

“Wh- what the fuck is going on Greg!?”  
	

“Ah I wouldn’t do that miss.” Sally had her pistol drawn and pointed at him.   
	

“Why?” she asked. Her voice only shook a little, demeanor remaining steadfast. He didn’t say anything, he just pointed up. We both slowly turned our heads upwards. All we saw was the same darkness. With this brief moment of distraction, Greg silently grabbed a huge kitchen knife and drove it into Sally’s neck vertically. She quickly brought her head back down her chin pushing the knife horizontal and deeper. Eyes wide, she looked at me. Blood shot from her neck covering the meals of some of my neighbors but they kept eating. I maintained eye contact for the brief moment Sally was still upright. All she mustered was a gurgle before falling flat forward. The knife hit the floor and drove through to the other side of her head. Her body twitched and squirmed like she was having a seizure. More blood gushed and the sounds of her choking became the most prominent as she attempted to cough up what was blocking her breathing. Slowly, the agitation subsided and she lay motionless, blood still spreading across the hardwood floor.   


“S-Sally?” I was in shock and frozen. “Sally please.” I crawled to her. I felt helpless. “Why did you kill her?” I asked Greg after some time.     
	

“Forever must be forever, Clarke” He served the last plate on the tray before kissing his daughter again. “Do you want to be Clarke forever?” he asked as he walked backwards pushing the door out with his back. It closed, and a new fear took me. I was alone then. Even Greg gave me some sense of familiarity and comfort. I started hyperventilating, unable to take my eyes off of the darkness above me. Was there something up there? My mind began making shapes that weren’t there.   
	

“Look.” I heard Greg speak right next to me. He was holding a plate full of food out to me. “Don’t look up there now. Ain’t nothin’ up there.”  
	

“Are you sure?”  
	

“Oh yes, absolutely.” I looked again at the meat and suddenly became horribly hungry. I felt the saliva building up in my mind.  
	

“What is this?” I asked. He let out one loud laugh as he set the plate next to me on the floor. He continued laughing, approaching his daughter who he kissed again.   
	

“Sweet Cynthia.” He looked back at me. “What are children if not extensions of ourselves, Clarke? Continuations so to speak.” Looking at his daughter pensively. “Yeah I would prefer permanence personally.” He smiled as I realized what the meat was. “Ain’t it something. You don’t even have a kid, damn. This costs nothing for you!” He began yelling. “You little piece of shit freeloader! Why!? Why don’t you have kids? Your dick doesn't work or something like that.” He shook his head. “Jesus Christ, you youngsters are pathetic I swear.”  
	

I took Sally’s gun from her dead hands without thinking and fired once. The sound was trapped in the room so the crack was deafening.  


Greg looked at the new bullet wound in his hand. “You shot me.” He looked bewildered. I pulled the trigger two more times aiming for center mass. One hit him in the gut and he fell back against the wall. “What? No! But… but.” He looked up, almost accusing the darkness. “Forever, forever, forever,” he repeated as I walked over to him and painlessly put a bullet in his head.  


The flute music resumed and next thing I knew I was devouring my uneaten plate.  
	

“Forever must be forever,” I said. The meat was delicious.  


If you’re reading this I turned out more than alright.
