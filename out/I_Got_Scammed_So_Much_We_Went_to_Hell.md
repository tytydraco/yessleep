I've been scammed so many times that I don't even know why I keep falling for it anymore.

I'm an idiot, I know. It's ridiculously easy to scam me, especially with the fact that I have crippling muscle pain every day and excruciating back pain that never seems to go away. I'm also depressed and lonely, so any attention feels like good attention at the time.

I would try buying stuff from people on Telegram or Snapchat, believe whatever lies they told me, send the payment, and end up getting blocked everywhere. The cops couldn't do much besides investigate, which I understand, but I still couldn't figure out why I kept falling for the same things again and again.

It finally hit a nerve when a supposedly good witch I found on Fiverr moved our conversation to Telegram. She said all the right things about me.

"You're lonely and in a lot of pain. You need help."

Man, even the easiest sweet talk got me.

I sent her $200, only to be blocked everywhere.

I used to laugh at those catfish videos where people got romance scammed, but of course, I was also a victim. I guess I could even say I was an enabler of a romance scam too. Nearly $1,000 of money I'll never see again. At least I met that one in real life, only to be told two months later that he was getting married to someone who had barely turned nineteen.

Ironic, isn't it?

Here I was, a janitor with a master's degree in business, struggling to find a decent job in this miserable job market. My body hurt every day, I was lonely, and I kept making stupid decisions because I was desperate for something, anything, to make life feel a little less miserable.

I guess the deeper I sank into hopelessness, the easier it became to believe people who promised they could help.

"You need help."

Those words kept echoing in my head.

I tried sleeping them off, but they only seemed to get louder. Eventually, somewhere between depression, exhaustion, and disappointment, I thought maybe it would be a good idea to research ways to get some sort of revenge.

Not physical revenge or anything. I just wanted them to feel something.

So I Googled rituals.

Curses, hexes, spells, whatever I could find.

Google's AI overview suggested random things like candles, salt, and other stuff. I rolled my eyes because tadaa, six years ago me had already tried those things too.

I spent hours clicking random links, reading occult forums, religious websites, and badly translated pages that looked like they hadn't been updated since 2003.

I'd started around 6 AM, and by the time I looked at the clock again, it was 3 AM.

Twenty-one hours.

I was exhausted, my back was killing me, and I was getting increasingly annoyed at how useless everything was.

"Oh, just send me to Hell already!" I yelled at my screen.

Then, out of nowhere, I felt this burning, freezing cold slap every part of my body.

I could barely move. I wanted to jump around just to shake the cold off, but my entire body felt paralyzed. It was so cold that it burned, and for a moment, I thought I was having some kind of medical emergency.

Then I looked around.

I wasn't in my bedroom anymore.

I was standing in a room that felt ancient yet futuristic. Everything looked out of place, yet somehow perfectly in place. There were strange objects and furniture from different periods, and some things I couldn't even identify.

The room had this weird mixture of Frutiger Aero and natural wooden cabin aesthetics going on. There were warm wooden panels and furniture, mixed with glossy, almost transparent surfaces, soft blue and green colours, and these strange curved structures that looked like something from an early 2000s vision of the future.

The wallpapers reminded me of the bedroom at the end of *2001: A Space Odyssey*, except instead of the bright white, almost clinical look, everything had this warm wooden cabin colour scheme mixed with that early 2000s futuristic aesthetic. It felt like someone had tried to imagine what a luxurious room in the future would look like, but using designs from several completely different centuries.

It was a little weird because nothing really matched, yet somehow it all worked together. Almost like a room I'd seen in a dream years ago but couldn't quite remember.

It was actually quite nice.

I'm not sure whether the room adjusted its temperature or my body just gave up, but eventually the cold stopped bothering me.

Then an androgynous person slowly glided towards me with a comforting smile.

"Your wish is done. Do you have more?" they asked softly.

I blinked.

"Where am I? And what wish?"

"Well, you're in Hell. But I think you could be more useful sharing your wishes than staying here. Your intentions alerted us that you might know a handful of others who would appreciate being here."

I looked around again.

"Not to sound mean, but this place looks more decent than where I'm living now. Besides the cold."

They smiled.

"Dear, the cold is simply the journey here. Everyone experiences it. This place, however, is especially for you. Your intentions and how you've lived your life are reflected in your surroundings. The people you had in mind might enjoy rather different scenery."

I was confused.

"I mean, I don't know. Do I have to do anything? Sign something? Faust stuff?"

"Dear, no. Don't get confused. As I said, you wished to come to Hell, and now you're here. We want to do you a favour. The type of pain you've experienced and the people who knowingly caused it are in high demand here. You just have to tell me when you're ready to bring them."

"Uhh. I don't know. I guess so. Whatever."

I said it nonchalantly because I was convinced this was a really elaborate lucid dream.

"Alright."

Then they let out this really weird guttural scream, followed by a deep horn that rumbled throughout the room.

Suddenly, ten people appeared in front of me.

Three I recognized immediately. The others took me a moment.

"We have your ex who scammed you, your friend who stole your ideas and ridiculed you, and the man who said he'd fix your phone but ran off with it."

They continued introducing the others.

"The people behind the fake KitchenAid sale, the romance scam, the man who created a GoFundMe for his fake illness, the witch who told you that you needed help, the man who claimed your brother was in trouble, the one who threatened you with inappropriate pictures, the people creating AI adult material of you, the grocery delivery scam, and the fake animal shelter asking for donations."

Some of them had been involved in more than one scam. Others were people I'd never actually seen before because I'd only dealt with anonymous accounts.

I hadn't realized how many there were.

They all looked frozen, but their faces were twisted in pain. They wanted to scream, but their muscles wouldn't let them make a sound.

I chuckled because the whole thing was ridiculous.

"Now, would you like me to send them to their spaces?" the person asked.

"Nah, don't kill them. Lol. It's fine."

"Oh, no, dear. They won't die."

The figure tilted their head.

"They'll spend a thousand years in their assigned rooms before returning to the living world."

I laughed again.

"Wouldn't they be dead by then? Lmao."

"Nope. Only three hours will pass in your world, but they'll experience a thousand years here. Their bodies won't age, and they cannot die during their stay."

"Well, seeing as my room isn't too bad and apparently there's some cosmic calling going on here, sure, I guess."

They smiled.

"You have nothing to feel bad about. As your room reflects you, their rooms will reflect them."

"Uhh. I don't know how this room reflects me. Looks pretty decent. Nice stuff from different times, pretty calm. A little melancholic, I guess, but comforting. Are you sure this is Hell? Haha."

"Quite sure, dear. I'll put them in their rooms now."

They made another low guttural throat noise, followed by a high-pitched horn that pierced through the room.

Suddenly, individual doors materialized out of nowhere, and all ten bodies were violently snatched into them.

The doors stayed open.

And I could see inside every room.

They were all equally terrifying, yet terrifying in different ways.

Eight of them had their hearts torn out repeatedly. Five were standing on blue embers that burned through their skin and muscles as everything kept growing back. Nine had their arms slowly flayed, and all ten had their eyes plucked out.

Their tongues were pulled out and rolled around these enormous spiked wheel contraptions that kept turning.

There were also these gruesome animalistic beings in some of the rooms. I couldn't even tell what kind of creatures they were supposed to be. Some crawled on all fours while others stood awkwardly on their hind legs. Their bodies looked almost human in certain places, but their movements were completely wrong. They kept pouncing on the people, biting and tearing chunks out of them while making these horrible wet growling noises. One of them was pulling a man's body apart while another creature was already chewing on his arm. The flesh just kept growing back, so the creatures never really ran out of anything to tear apart. Some people had several of these things attacking them at once, on top of everything else happening in their rooms.

The man behind the fake GoFundMe had his hair and scalp pulled away, only for it to grow back and happen again.

Some had the air sucked out of them. They kept trying to breathe, but no matter how hard they struggled, they couldn't get any air.

A few had these horrible, sad laughs echoing throughout their rooms.

And the worst part was that these things weren't happening one after another.

They were happening at the same time.

Someone standing on the blue embers could also be having their heart torn out, their eyes plucked out, and their tongue caught in the wheel, while one of those creatures was tearing into their body. Their bodies kept regenerating while everything continued.

I couldn't even imagine how anyone could experience so many things at once.

The rooms themselves were strangely ordinary.

They weren't black voids or industrial torture chambers. They were bare, almost like empty rooms in a house from the 1990s.

The walls had this weird greenish-yellow colour that was hard to describe because the brightness of the blue embers seemed to overpower everything.

There was so much happening that I didn't know where to look.

Then I noticed the whispers.

Every room had its own.

At first I couldn't make out what they were saying, but then I realized they were the scammers' own voices.

They were repeating the things they'd said to their victims.

The lies, the promises, the excuses, and all the things they'd said so casually to convince people to trust them.

But there were other voices too.

People crying, begging, asking for their money back, and asking why they'd been lied to.

There were hundreds of voices.

Maybe thousands.

I turned towards the person beside me.

"Why are there so many things happening to them?"

"Because there are so many things they've done."

"But some of them only scammed me out of a little money."

"Dear, you're not the only person they've harmed."

They looked towards the rooms.

"Some of them have deceived hundreds of people. To them, it was easy. Just another message, another payment, another person to block. They knew what they were doing and continued anyway."

I looked back at the rooms.

"But the people they deceived continued living with what happened to them. Some went hungry, some lost their homes, some lost their trust in others, and some suffered in ways their deceivers never bothered to think about."

They smiled gently.

"The rooms reflect their intentions and the suffering they knowingly brought into the world."

"All at once?"

"Much of that suffering happened at once, dear."

I looked through the doors again.

I suddenly felt uncomfortable.

"Isn't this a bit much?"

The person tilted their head slightly.

"Would you prefer to choose something else for them?"

"No. I mean, I don't know."

I wasn't exactly scared of the person or the place. I just felt weird realizing that something I'd casually agreed to was actually happening in front of me.

"I guess we can call it a day," they said.

"As much as it pleases us that you've brought so many people to our attention, please try to take care of yourself and don't make the same mistakes again. We'll have it from here."

They walked gently towards me, placed their right hand on my shoulder, and kissed my forehead.

And just like that, I was back in my bedroom.

Still sitting on my broken gaming chair.

My computer screen was still glowing in front of me.

Everything was so quiet that the silence almost hurt my ears.

Microsoft Word was open.

In the middle of a blank document, written in a huge cursive font, were two words.

*Take care.*

I didn't remember typing them.

Then again, I'd spent twenty-one hours clicking random links while half asleep, so maybe I'd typed it without realizing.

I sighed.

"What a weird effing dream."

I was actually quite amused by how ridiculous the whole thing had been.

I closed the document and went to bed.

The next morning, my phone wouldn't stop pinging.

Ping.

Ping.

Ping.

I groaned and reached for it.

PayPal.

Another notification.

Then another.

And another.

I panicked.

"What the hell did I buy now?"

I opened the app.

Ten money gifts.

Not payment requests or pending transactions.

Actual money received.

The amounts ranged from $500 to $50,000.

I didn't even know PayPal could accept that much money.

Every single payment had the same caption.

*Eternally in debt.*

I stared at my phone.

I refreshed the app.

The money was still there.

Different accounts, different names, and none of them looked familiar.

Still half asleep and confused, I groggily walked to the bathroom and splashed some water on my face, thinking I might still be in that weird dream.

Nope.

I checked my phone again.

The money was still there.

Then my phone rang.

It was my internet friend.

I answered.

"Yowh! Sup, girly?"

"Hey."

"GIRL. Sammy got into an accident and he's paraplegic now. And apparently his wife said she's too young to deal with it and asked for a divorce. Girllll!"

"What?"

I sat down.

Sammy.

My ex.

The one who scammed me.

The one who used to make fun of my crippling pain while constantly showing off his athleticism.

He'd been in several accidents before and survived them all. He used to brag about how strong and lucky he was.

"That's his ultimate fear," I said. "He always showed off his athleticism. And he used to make fun of my pain all the time. He's survived so many accidents, but now he's paralyzed? That's wild."

"And he doesn't have insurance! Remember how he kept saying insurance was a scam?"

I remembered.

"But he's the scammer now in trouble because he didn't want to get scammed!" she continued.

She started howling with laughter.

"Man, karma really is something. Lmao!"

I tried laughing too, but something felt stuck in my throat.

She lived in the same city as him, almost ten thousand miles away from me. I'd originally met her through him, but over the years she'd become my best friend.

She absolutely hated him, so I wasn't surprised she found the whole thing funny.

But I couldn't stop thinking about the rooms.

The doors.

The horn.

The person smiling at me.

After the call, I started doomscrolling on my phone.

And then I saw the headlines.

A scammer had been found psychologically disturbed after returning money to several victims. Witnesses said he kept screaming, "My feet! My feet!" over and over again.

Another article described several men and women found wandering along a road, gasping for air and screaming, "Please let me breathe!"

Apparently, there was nothing medically wrong with their lungs.

Then there was the man behind the fake GoFundMe.

He had suddenly developed an aggressive form of immune disorder that caused excruciating pain. Doctors were baffled by how quickly it had progressed.

When offered treatment, he started throwing things at hospital staff and screaming at them to stay away.

Another article was about a man in Southeast Asia who had burned down the call centre where he worked.

Witnesses said he kept screaming, "Not mine! Not mine!" while the building burned.

There was also a video of a man confessing incoherently to things nobody had accused him of.

He was crying, apologizing, and begging someone to stop.

He later tried to end his own life, but his neighbours heard the commotion and called an ambulance. He survived and had to be restrained to prevent him from hurting himself again.

Lastly, there was a man who refused to speak anymore.

He communicated through handwritten notes, claiming that using his voice was unbearably painful.

Whenever he tried to speak, his throat started bleeding, as though there were pieces of glass stuck inside it.

I kept scrolling.

Different people.

Different countries.

Different news stories.

Yet somehow, they all seemed to be experiencing the same thing.

Fear.

Not just the fear of being caught or losing money.

Something else.

Something that didn't make sense.

The kind of fear someone might experience after living through a thousand years of something they couldn't escape.

I put my phone down.

My hands were shaking.

I thought about the money.

The rooms.

The doors.

I thought about Sammy, who used to laugh at my pain, and the witch who told me I needed help.

Then I thought about all the voices I'd heard in those rooms.

The hundreds of people begging, crying, and asking why they'd been deceived.

And I remembered how casually I'd agreed to everything.

"Sure, I guess."

Like someone had just asked me whether I wanted another cup of tea.

I'd wanted revenge, sure.

I'd wanted those people to understand what they'd done.

But I hadn't imagined anything like this.

Whatever I was feeling wasn't exactly satisfaction.

I wasn't happy.

I wasn't relieved either.

I just felt sad.

Sad that people could be so cruel. Sad that I'd let myself get hurt so many times. Sad that things had somehow come to this.

And guilty.

Because I was starting to realize that maybe it hadn't been a dream after all.

I sat there for a while, staring at nothing.

The money was still in my PayPal.

My back still hurt.

My apartment was still the same.

Nothing about my life had magically become better.

Except that ten people had apparently spent a thousand years in Hell, and I might have been the one who sent them there.

Then I heard a soft, calming, familiar voice close to my left ear.

"It's okay, dear."

A brief pause.

"Take care."
