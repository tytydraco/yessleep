I don't remember entering the forest.

I remember the road.

I remember the rain hitting the windshield, the headlights cutting through the fog, and the old man standing in the middle of the road.

I remember stopping.

I remember him walking toward my truck.

After that, I remember waking up in my own bed.

The truck was still parked outside.

The driver's door was open.

There was mud across the floorboards.

Something was growing under my fingernails.

At first, I thought it was dirt.

I tried scraping it out with a knife.

The first piece came loose easily.

The second didn't.

It pulled.

Not like dirt.

Like a root.

I stopped pulling.

For a few seconds, I just stared at my hand.

Then something knocked against the bedroom window.

Three times.

I looked up.

There was nobody outside.

Only the trees.

They seemed closer than I remembered.

I went downstairs.

The front door was covered in scratches.

Not on the outside.

On the inside.

Someone—or something—had been trying to get out.

I didn't sleep that night.

At 3:17 in the morning, I heard footsteps walking across the roof.

Slow.

Heavy.

Dragging.

They stopped directly above my bedroom.

Then came the sound of wood splitting.

I looked down at my hand.

Another crack had appeared across my palm.

This time, it was bleeding sap.

The bedroom door creaked open.

A tall, pale woman leaned through the doorway.

She was barely taller than the door frame.

She stepped into the room and slowly walked toward me.

I backed away.

“What are you doing?”

She didn't answer.

She grabbed my hand with a vice grip and turned it over, examining my palm.

She looked at the crack.

Then she looked up at me.

She smiled.

Not like an adult.

Like a child seeing their science experiment finally work.

Suddenly, a long proboscis shot from her mouth and shoved itself into the cut on my palm.

Pain exploded through my arm.

I jerked backward.

“Get the fuck off of me!”

Her grip tightened around my wrist.

I mustered what little strength I had left and grabbed her face with my other hand.

I pushed my thumb against her eye and squeezed as hard as I could.

She didn't even flinch.

She still wouldn't let go.

Then the light started to fade.

Not outside.

Inside me.

My arm went numb first.

Then my fingers.

I could still feel her grip around my wrist, but it felt farther away now, like my hand belonged to someone standing on the other side of the room.

She tilted her head.

The smile disappeared.

For the first time, she looked concerned.

She pulled the proboscis from my palm.

A thin strand of something clear stretched between us before snapping back into her mouth.

I fell against the wall.

“What did you do to me?”

She didn't answer.

Instead, she leaned closer.

I could hear her breathing.

Slow.

Wet.

She sniffed the wound on my palm.

Then she whispered something.

I couldn't understand the words.

It sounded like a language being spoken through a mouth full of leaves.

I looked down at my hand.

The crack in my palm had gotten wider.

Dark lines were spreading away from it.

They crawled across my skin in branching patterns.

Veins.

No.

Not veins.

They looked like the cracks in the bark of an old tree.

The woman stood up.

She walked out of the room.

I heard a loud clunk from the other side of the door.

A board had been placed against it.

She had locked me in.

I threw myself against the door.

Nothing.

I tried the handle again.

Locked.

I slammed my shoulder into it.

Pain shot through me.

The door didn't move.

“LET ME OUT!”

Nothing answered.

I hit it again.

Still nothing.

I backed away, breathing heavily.

The room was small.

Too small.

A bed.

A table.

One window.

One door.

That was it.

There was nowhere else to go.

I ran toward the window.

My fingers wrapped around the frame.

I pulled.

It didn't open.

I pulled harder.

The skin on my palms cracked.

Golden sap seeped through my fingers.

I stopped.

I stared at my hands.

“No…”

The itching returned.

Only this time, it was everywhere.

My chest.

My neck.

My face.

My legs.

I scratched at my stomach.

Something hard caught underneath my fingernails.

I pulled.

A piece of bark came away from my skin.

I screamed.

I dug my fingers underneath another piece and pulled.

The bark peeled away with a wet tearing sound.

Sap poured from the wound.

I expected blood.

There wasn't any.

I looked at the piece of bark in my hand.

It was warm.

It twitched.

I dropped it.

The sap kept pouring.

It seemed to never end, as if my body had an infinite supply.

Soon, I was covered in it.

My hands were soaked.

I wiped my face without thinking.

Some of the sap got onto my lips.

I tasted it.

It was incredible.

Sweet.

Warm.

Like rich honey mixed with maple.

For a moment, the pain disappeared.

The itching stopped.

My entire body relaxed.

I wanted more.

I hated myself for wanting it.

But I wanted more.

A loud thud came from upstairs.

I froze.

Then came a groan of pain.

A woman's voice followed.

It sounded like someone in a hospital with an incurable disease, begging God—or anyone—to save them.

“Why?”

A loud crack of splitting wood followed.

I flinched.

Silence.

I waited.

Nothing.

Then I heard footsteps.

They weren't coming down the stairs.

They were walking across the ceiling.

Slowly.

Dragging.

I looked up.

A dark stain was spreading across the ceiling above me.

Sap.

It dripped through the cracks.

One drop landed on my forehead.

I wiped it away.

It was warm.

I stared at it on my finger.

The smell was incredible.

Sweet.

Warm.

Maple.

I put my finger in my mouth before I could stop myself.

The taste hit me immediately.

My entire body relaxed again.

I wanted more.

Another drop fell.

Then another.

I opened my mouth.

I hated myself for doing it.

But I wanted it.

I wanted all of it.

A shadow moved across the ceiling.

I stopped.

Something scraped against the floor above me.

Then the woman's voice came again.

Only this time, she wasn't crying.

She was laughing.

Quietly.

Almost peacefully.

“You taste it too.”

I backed away from the ceiling.

“How do you know I'm here?”

There was a long pause.

Then she answered.

“I can hear your roots.”

My blood ran cold.

I looked down at my feet.

Something had pushed through the skin between my toes.

Thin.

Pale.

Wet.

A root.

I screamed.

I grabbed it and pulled.

Pain shot through my entire leg.

The root stretched.

It didn't break.

It was attached to me.

“No.”

I pulled harder.

The skin around my ankle split open.

Sap poured onto the floor.

I screamed again.

Then something answered from upstairs.

A deep groan.

Not a person.

Wood.

The entire house creaked.

The walls bent inward.

The floorboards beneath me began to crack.

And from somewhere outside came a sound I'd heard before.

Three knocks.

I slowly turned toward the front door.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

The old man was standing on the other side.

I knew it.

I didn't know how I knew.

But I knew.

Then he spoke.

“You're almost ready.”

I didn't open the door.

I couldn't.

Something had begun growing through the cracks in the floor.

Roots.

Dozens of them.

They wrapped around my ankles.

Then they tightened.

The roots dug deeper into my legs.

A pain I never imagined feeling shot through my entire body.

I tried to move.

I couldn't.

I was stuck.

The bark began crawling up my legs.

It spread across my stomach.

My chest.

My arms.

Everywhere it touched, I could feel something changing underneath.

My organs.

My blood.

Everything inside me seemed to dissolve.

I could feel my insides becoming liquid.

Becoming sap.

My entire body felt submerged in water.

I couldn't breathe.

I couldn't scream.

My arms began to stretch.

My bones cracked and split.

Branches pushed through my shoulders and crawled toward the ceiling.

My fingers became thin.

Then thinner.

Until they weren't fingers anymore.

They were twigs.

I felt something inside my head begin to change.

My skull stretched.

My thoughts became slower.

Wider.

I could feel my mind splitting apart, spreading through the branches growing from my neck.

I was still aware.

That was the worst part.

I could still think.

I could still feel.

And somehow...

I could still see.

My face hadn't changed.

Not yet.

The front door opened.

The man stepped inside.

Eight feet tall.

Pale.

Thin.

The same man I had seen standing in the road.

He carried an enormous axe.

The blade was dark with dried sap.

He walked toward me.

I tried to move.

Nothing happened.

He reached out and snapped one of the smaller branches growing from my arm.

It broke easily.

Golden sap leaked from the wound.

He examined it.

Then smiled.

I wanted to scream.

Instead, I managed to speak.

“Why me?”

The man looked at me.

For a moment, he seemed genuinely confused by the question.

“Because we were hungry.”

He walked toward the tree growing from my body.

He ran his hand along the bark covering my chest.

“We take people.”

He looked at me.

“We make them into special trees.”

My mind screamed.

My mouth barely moved.

“Please.”

The man lifted the axe.

He smiled.

“Don't worry.”

The blade rose above his head.

“We use every part of the tree.”

The axe came down.

And then everything went black.
