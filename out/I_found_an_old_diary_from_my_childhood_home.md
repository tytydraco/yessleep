I’ve always considered myself to be a sentimental person. I tend to keep invaluable things from any and all outings I go to. I’ve probably collected enough lucky coins to buy a nice pair of pants for myself. If you were to compile all of the movie tickets I’ve gathered throughout the years, I could bind them all to form a complete novel or two. I have way too many things and I have such a problem in letting things go. The thing is, most people who hoard things just keep these things and forget about them after a week tops, but I like to sift through my belongings once in a while and reminisce about each memory attached to a particular item. It brings me comfort holding that perfectly shaped acorn I came across that one morning walk I took, I feel the cool breeze and hear the light rustling of the leaves that day. That's just the feeling I chase being so sentimental, I crave re-experiencing my memories; no matter how mundane.



Nostalgia plays a large role in that feeling, I think everything holds a certain value just by being a part of my life somehow. So whenever I get the chance to retrieve something that I once thought was lost, I am overwhelmed with excitement. Yet, I don’t think anything could have prepared me for what came into my possession.



For the first 10 years of my life, I lived in this apartment complex. I remember playing in the backyard with all the stray cats and, on slow days, even running around in the office next door. This house was a huge part of my life growing up and I guess my parents found it just as difficult to let go so we kept the property within the family. We moved out around 2010 and my uncle moved in with his family. A part of me was sad that we were letting go of my “playground” but I remember thinking that just meant I’d have to visit my uncle more frequently.



Fast forward to a few weeks ago, my uncle finally decided to move out since his family is now outgrowing the apartment just like ours did. Although I didn’t like the idea of never being able to set foot in the house again, my uncle did call to let us know that he found a box in the attic that didn’t belong to him. I was ecstatic. I get to rummage through old stuff and rediscover memories I didn’t even know I forgot about? Count me in.



My parents were out of town for a medical check-up and they’d be back in a couple of days. So it was just me when my uncle showed up to hand over the box he had found. It was Wednesday and I remember waiting by the window a couple of hours before he had even arrived to open the door as soon as I saw his car pull up. He called me over as he popped open the trunk.



“*Give me a hand over here, this is a two-man job*”



I quickly went over to help him out and realized just how heavy this box was. I maybe had to shift my grip two or three times to keep the box from slipping from my hands. All I could think was how could a cardboard box hold up from the weight? When we finally got the box in the living room (we were not going to carry that box any further) my uncle remarked:



“*All that time in the gym and you can’t keep up with an old man?*” He laughed



Not to sound defensive but I could’ve sworn that all the weight was bearing down on my side. I just laughed it off. I love my uncle but I wanted him to leave as soon as possible so I could begin my walk down memory lane. He soon left but not without helping himself to a large glass of water; that lift wasn’t so easy huh old man? As soon as he started his car, I cut through some old tape on the top of the box and opened it. When I looked inside I was feeling a little tense, there were lots of antique looking decorations like porcelain figurines, an old clock, some broken wine glasses, a fountain pen, and an old looking book. I remember taking each item out and examining them trying to associate each one with some distant memory of mine but nothing. These things didn’t belong to my family; I think I would have remembered creepy looking porcelain figures around the house growing up. I was so disappointed, I just blankly stared at the empty box with all these random items scattered across the table. One by one I packed each item back into the box and closed it. 



“*Fuck, how am I going to carry this into the garage without any help?*” I thought.



I wrapped my arms around the box anyway to give a half-hearted attempt at lifting it and to my complete surprise, I nearly tossed it over my shoulder. It was far lighter than it was earlier that day. I looked around to see if I had forgotten to put something back like an old typewriter that I somehow missed in my inspection. After a couple of glances around the living room I finally noticed that the old book was still out of the box; it had blended in with the other books we keep as decoration.



“*Ah, here we go*” 



But as I went down to get it, I realized that it didn’t make any sense at all that a book just barely bigger than my hand could contribute that much weight to the box. I picked it up and sure enough, it was as light as a feather. I was befuddled to say the least, and even more so when I opened it to the first page.



“*May 16 1841*” was written on the upper left corner of the page in a fancy-looking script. There was no question that this was someone’s diary. I began to read to find some clue as to who this belonged to.



“*When will this end?* 

*What did we do to deserve this?* 

*I yearn to see the sun set on the horizon. Mother said we must keep low and stay silent or they take our tongues…*



*Unintelligible Script*



*…crites, they preach of a God yet the devil smiles upon them. They lashed brother today, I saw his spine. I held his mouth shut, he must not scream or else. His tears felt cold on my hand, I was thirsty…* ”



I slammed the book shut. A shiver ran down my back, my ears, my arms, everywhere. I didn’t think it would stop, and yet, I couldn’t seem to put the book down. It was as if my hands were itching to turn to the next page. What was this person going through? What God? Who’s “they”? 

Who’s diary am I reading?



I snapped out of the trance-like state I was in and managed to put the book down. I don’t know what came to me but I decided to stash the box in the garage but left the diary on the table. I felt some sort of a connection to the book, it held so much meaning, perhaps I started to feel sentimental about it. Regardless of how I felt, I was uneasy and the only other thing I could think about was how I was going to be able to go to bed that night alone in a house with the memories of a stranger calling me to the other room.



I woke up the next morning feeling awful, I wasn’t able to sleep. I just kept thinking about the morbid things this diary was hiding in its pages. I had read much worse things on the internet at that point but seeing as this was only the first page, it could only get worse from there. I made my morning coffee, whipped up some scrambled eggs, and began to read the second page.



“*May 20 1841*

*Brother won’t make a sound again, he never got to see the sun set. If there is a God, I hope he sees brother’s smile. I forgot what it looked like.* 

*It’s just me and mother now, they don’t do nicely to girls.*

*Our tongues hold their secrets. I don’t know if tomorrow is going to be any different. Is the sunset worth seeing?*”



I didn’t even realize it but at that point I had been sipping from an empty coffee mug. I felt how scared she was. I felt the heaviness of living a life knowing that tomorrow wasn’t going to get any better. As I was processing the entry I had just read, my phone started ringing, it startled me just enough to drop the diary; my hands weren’t really gripping on to the diary that well to be fair. It was my dad just checking in to see if I was able to get everything my uncle left for us. After I confirmed everything, I reached for the diary that was just laying open on the floor. Before I actually grabbed the book, I made out the first line at the top of the page.

My heart sunk.



“*July 24 2007*”



I was frozen in that position for a bit, my sweat started falling on to the pages of the diary. My eyes were transfixed on that line, I wasn’t sure if I wanted to read on what that page had in store for me. This book had been in that house the whole time we lived there. The dates suggest this is a relic, yet, I am reading a passage that existed at the same time I could have read a TIME magazine? Something was not adding up, I had never felt this kind of creeping fear. As if the walls were full of eyes just watching me constantly. I don’t know if there was someone across from me anymore, I was not aware of what was around me, in that moment, all I could see was the diary. I cautiously grabbed it, and started reading.



“*Another acorn, this one more intricate than the others. Surely a more meaningful object. The boy has a knack for finding value in things. I can feel the tenderness of his hands already gripping these pages. The time will come.*”



I felt as though just touching the diary was a sin. But I pressed on.



“*July 24 2010*

*I cannot breathe, I need him. He will come back. I will find him. His laugh was identical to brother’s. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows. He knows.*”



Amidst the immense fear and tension I was feeling in that moment, a little sliver of confusion sneaked up on me. I lacked so much context in the previous entries of the diary and yet I could understand what was written, this was the first time where I could not find a reason as to why the phrase “*He knows*” was repeated so much. I was scared, even though I tried to ignore the connection, my mind couldn’t help but think that the boy that was mentioned in the entry was me. I felt waves of chills every time I thought that. How can I be referenced in this diary? None of this made sense. I was trying to convince myself that this was just a prank that my uncle was pulling to rid myself of feeling so tense, so I decided to skip to the very last page of the diary.



“*Today*”



I fell out of my seat, kicking myself away like a pitiful dog, I remember wailing, not screaming nor crying, wailing. Like I couldn’t physically scream but my body was desperately trying to call for help. I kept on kicking until my back hit the wall. I was breathing so heavily that I thought I was going to pass out. I didn’t dare move a muscle, I had my back fully against the wall and my eyes fully fixated on the table. And so I was until the sun went down, and when the sun went back up. I was fighting to keep my eyes open at that point, the will to find out what was on that page got me to get back up and, well, find out. I kept my distance, staying away enough that I could make out words from the scribbles that I could see.



“*Today*

*How can you start a day when you haven’t had an ounce of slept yet, my dear?*”



I slowly turned to the previous page.



“*Yesterday*

*Sleep was awful. Am I too alluring? So easily entranced, your cup is already empty. My secrets are yours. Ours. My memories are forever, my pain is forever. Our memories, our pain. Our. Our. Our. Our. Our. Our. Our. Our. Our. Our. Mine.*”



I turned the page back to find more had been written.



“*Today*

*How can you start a day when you haven’t had an ounce of slept yet, my dear? Go make your coffee, be prepared. They come today. Dad is innocent but how would he feel seeing mom’s tongue in your hand. You know what to do.*”



There was a knife in my hand, I was scared. I am sorry.
