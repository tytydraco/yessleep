The skeleton animatronic in the office entry laughed evilly and waved as I opened the door. “Morning, Mike,” said Chelsea with a sly smile. “How was camping?”

“It was great. No better way to spend a weekend,” I said, puzzled at her smirk. I took a piece of candy from the jack-o-lantern bucket on her reception desk and headed to the office in the back corner of the building.

Fake spiderwebs with plastic spiders splayed across the wall and ceiling. A wolfman mask hung on the wall, and a gargoyle grimaced at me from atop the printer.

I turned into the office and admired the rubber machete and hockey mask adorning the surveyor’s prism on the corner of my desk. It strategically blocked the trash can full of energy drink cans. I slung off my backpack at the foot of the surveying tripod in the corner and ate the piece of candy I had grabbed. 

“For breakfast, Mike?” said Reece, walking up and leaning on my doorframe. 

“There's not a better meal to start a Monday with,” I said. He stood and smiled at me while I booted up at my computer.

“A question at eight a.m. on a Monday after vacation? Give me a break,” I said.

He stepped into the room. “Did you get it on video?” he said, looking around at the filing cabinets and bookshelves of engineering codes.

“What?”

“You had to get it on video. It was too good not to. Where’d you hide the camera?”

“What are you talking about?”

“You’re playing it straight? That’s Jason’s bit,” he said, looking over at the other desk in the office. It bore no Halloween decorations but only the bone white drawing sets and engineering paper neatly organized around a black laptop. The name plate held several sets of letters behind the golden title.

Chelsea’s brown hair streaked around the edge of the door. “Oh-my-gosh! You should have heard it! I heard him yelp all the way at my desk!”

“I seriously can’t believe you had the balls to pull something like that on Jason,” said Reece.

“I just about choked on a piece of candy, watching him storm out of the office,” Chelsea said.

Reece looked giddy. “He was mumbling over and over ‘what the f-.’”

“Dan saw it happen!” said Chelsea as Dan walked past the room with his coffee mug.

Dan stopped. “What? Oh, the prank on Jason? Yeah that was funny. I was getting a refill when I turned the corner and saw him just sitting there, eyes wide. I didn’t know he could be scared, let alone smile. I asked him about it. He just got up and left.” He chuckled.

“He didn’t even take his computer home,” said Reece. “That’s insane. You must have broken a 10 year streak of him bringing his computer home.”

“That’s pretty daring of you to prank your senior engineer and then leave for the day,” said Dan.

“I’m confused,” I said. “What prank is this?”

Reece cocked his head at me. “The Halloween decoration? In the laser scan?”

“What Halloween decoration?”

“The black robed figure. Hanging from the tree. Where’d you get it? How’d you get it there for the laser scan?”

I had been out surveying a new job site the previous Friday morning. It was a half-developed site. Someone had wanted to build a slaughterhouse years ago, but ran out of money after pouring the concrete slab and the six by six grid of columns. The new property owner wanted to see what could be used. I had taken a laser scan, got photos, and shot points for the topographical surface we would use in designing the new building. I had uploaded everything before noon on Friday, sent it to Jason, and left on my camping trip. What I had not done was put a black hooded Halloween decoration below the tree at the property’s edge, like Reece said.

The four of us huddled around my computer as I waited for my 3D modeling software to boot up, my knuckles white as they gripped the mouse.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about, guys,” I said as I took a big gulp from my energy drink. “You sure this wasn’t on another property? There’s a new housing development right across the road.

“No,” said Reece. “It was near the back of the property, by the old cemetery, the big oak tree.”

I zoomed in over the field of grass into the structure of evenly spaced concrete columns about ten feet high, rebar sticking out of the top, red with rust like blood. I flew over it like a bat flitting over the landscape. The laser scan reloaded in waves with each scroll of the mouse, the individual points of color blinking in and out of existence. 

“There!” pointed Reece.

At the edges of the scan, a one sided tree stood solitarily on a hill. A thinning canopy extended upwards, and the wide oak leaves turned dark red brown with the season.

A dark shape materialized beneath, a group of black dots in the form of a dark hooded figure about six feet tall. It stood under the shade of the dead leaves. The silhouette’s feet were blocked by the foot high grass in front of it. It gave the illusion of the figure floating off the ground.

“Wait…” I said. “I took a picture of that direction while the scan was running.” I opened the folder and clicked the photo. The concrete pad sat with several columns scattered like tombstones across it. In the background, the red tree jutted into the grey sky. Only dying grass stood beneath its branches.

“See, it must be a glitch or something. There’s nothing there,” I said, emphatically circling the empty space with the mouse.

We all held the silence. Dan looked over at the empty desk. “Anyone seen Jason yet this morning?”  
Jason didn’t come in before lunch. It was strange to be in the office and not have the accompanying clicking of a keyboard across the room. I half expected him to come walking in at any point with his normal grimace and giant mug of steaming black coffee, cursing about his truck breaking down or a leaking pipe or family emergency.

I did my best to refocus on modeling the existing structure to send to the client. That dark figure watched over the shapes as I drew the next column based on the scan. I selected the collection of laser points that made up the figure and hid it from the view. Even as I tried to keep working, my eyes kept drifting to below the tree, my mind filling in the blank space.

It was about four in the afternoon, and even Dan, who knew Jason best, looked worried as he walked by my door. “I’m going to head over to his place. He would have said something by now,” he said, shaking his head.

“Ok, give me a call and let me know how he is.” I said.

“Will do.” He chuckled. “Can’t lose track of him. The company would go under without him.”

I worked in a stupor until my phone rang.

“Somebody’s home.” Dan said, as his face popped up on video call. The house sat sleepily with dark windows. “I’ll go see what’s up.”

“Must be doing ok. He’s up and walking around.” Dan walked on the driveway, then flipped the camera around. “There, he’s waving at me.”

“Where is he?” I said. All I saw were dark windows.

“There, in the window.” He pointed at nothing.

“I don’t see him.”

“He already opened the door.” Dan flipped the camera back around as I heard the door swing open. “Jeez, you look like hell,” Dan said to whoever was in the house.

 “You sick?” Dan said, closing the door after him. Jason did not reply. “Jason? You good?”

I watched Dan follow him into the kitchen. “What’s wrong with him?” I said. My hands were sweating.

“I don’t know, he hasn’t-“ Dan flinched, gasped, grunted, and cried out. The phone fell from his hand, and I could hear him struggling. Then it hung up.

I called Dan back. It went straight to voice mail. I ran out the front door to my truck and peeled out of the parking lot.

I pulled up in front of the economical house: a one story ranch, a two car garage, a clean lawn that was still green, free of leaves. The windows were dark eyes above teeth of white rock landscaping. I hurried up the smooth driveway and the uniform steps. I reached to knock on the door, bypassing the video doorbell.

The door was open. I pulled my hand back like it was a live wire. After a moment, I pushed it. The door glided on silent hinges. I stepped onto the grey rug and passed the mirror in the entry way. The house was completely silent, deafening the noise of leaves blowing on the trees outside.

I tread softly through the never-used dining room and into the spotless living room. Another mirror on the mantle sent my view into the kitchen. The white countertop was polished to a shine, though I suspected Jason did not cook. Dan’s phone was laying there, broken. A few scuffs on the white tile led to a trail on the carpet in the hallway and back into the bedroom. I nearly jumped at my reflection at the end of the hall. I made my way to the mirror and turned into the bedroom.

The spotless record of the house ended at a tipped-over dresser. Rain grey shirts and socks were strewn about the room with the collage of jeans in identical colors. A mirror lay smashed upon the bed. Its shards were flaked across the bedspread; the outline looked like a person’s form in shattered glass. It was as if the person had gone up in smoke.

The sound of the trees returned as I stepped into the room. The window was open. Cool air bit at my ears. The hair on my neck stood at the chill. I looked out the window. A lone tree stood across the large backyard. My eyes looked below its canopy, expecting to see a dark figure there.

There was movement somewhere that reflected off the mirror shards. I spun around to catch more movement in the mirror in the hall. “Dan!” I called, leaving the bedroom and following the glint. I thought I saw a reflection in the stainless steel of the kitchen. “Jason?” I said.  A figure moved past the mirror in the living room. I sprang forward only to see the front door swing wide open. Running to the doorway, I saw a trail of red spots leading across the carpet out onto the driveway. I followed them to their end, out into the street. Where Dan must have parked.

My mind raced as I wondered where Dan and Jason were. I called Chelsea, hoping she had heard from them.

“Have you heard from Dan? He was at Jason’s house and randomly hung up on me. I’m at Jason’s house and there’s no one here.”

“I haven’t heard from them. Do you think they went to the hospital?”

“Not sure. Are you still at the office? I need to come back and grab my stuff.”

“Yeah. Payroll isn’t going to do itself. Hold on a second.”

I heard the office front door unlatch and open in the background. The skeleton laughed.

“Hey Dan, did you hear from Jason?

“Dan’s there?” I said.

“Yeah, just a sec.” Chelsea said.

“Wait, something’s-” I was cut off when Chelsea shrieked. The phone hung up. I felt my stomach turn. My fingers fumbled to open my truck door and turn the key. My legs shook against the gas pedal I flew off down the street.

I screeched into the office lot, dashed from my truck, and threw the door open. The skeleton animatronic laughed. There was no sign of Chelsea. I made my way quickly down the hall. My voice bit in the darkness as I called for Chelsea. I crept forward, silent spiders watching from above, then made it to my office. Stopping in the doorway, the chill of wind greeted me. The window was open.

My computer and its monitors lay strewn across the floor. They were smashed to pieces, gouges in the plastic slightly smoked where the battery was burning. Another small plume of smoke came from Jason’s computer, cast into the far corner. Shards of his monitor mixed with shreds of his paper, like a collage of black and white teeth on his desk.

My phone rang. It was from Chelsea. 

I looked at the dark screen in the flickering office light. Someone was breathing behind the camera.

Several dark rectangles rose from the ground. A glimmer of daylight showed the outline of a large tree. Its silhouette of black arms webbed to block as much of the sun’s dying light as possible. My eyes instinctively examined the horizon below the tree. I ran to my truck.

The slaughterhouse foundation next to the cemetery, with its looming concrete columns, lay quiet as my headlights swept across it. Sluggish clouds limped across the sky to unveil a bright moon.

I grabbed a flashlight from my center console, then stepped out of the truck. My boots crunched on the gravel. I opened my back door and moved the prism pole and lidar scan tripod in the back seat. Their fiberglass rods and metal tips rattled against each other, along with the wooden lath stakes I used to mark boundaries. I reached under the seat and pulled out my pistol and its holster. Too many run-ins with squatters and angry landowners had taught me to have some kind of plan when things went sideways. I racked one into the chamber of the gun and flicked the safety.

Dan’s truck was parked here, a blue scarab in the dark night. I stepped forward to the property boundary, where the grass grew long and wild. There, at the entrance to the property, were two piles of wooden survey stakes I had placed the previous Friday. They were snapped in half. I stepped over the threshold they marked.

My boots swished through the grass. The flashlight beam touched the grey golems of concrete, the makings of a planned slaughter that never took place. With each step, my muscles tightened. My finger hovered over the trigger.

Something was laying in the middle of the concrete slab. My flashlight caught the shine of brown hair. Chelsea lay on her back, not moving. I lowered my gun and stepped onto the concrete, moving to check on her. 

I breathed deep when I saw her chest rise and fall. When I reached her feet, I swept around looking for any sign of someone who might have brought her here.

A dark shape receded behind a pillar as I moved past it. When I jerked back to examine it, I could have sworn a hand retreated after the shape.

Footsteps came from behind me, running parallel to the columns. I turned and saw a leg slide behind a column.

Someone moved on the other side of me. Closer. I turned and shined my light directly at a column. An arm was protruding behind it.

A cold hand grabbed my ankle. I jumped back to see Chelsea looking up at me, a rabid look in her eyes. 

“Chelsea, what the-” She froze when my light and the gun sights fell upon her.

Footfalls sounded behind me. I turned. Dan froze in the light. The same hunger showed in his eyes. There were scratches and dried blood on his arms.

I looked up at the sound of another pair of shuffling feet. Jason ran at me, his stern face more hateful than any office annoyance had ever twisted it. His eyes glowed as the light crossed them, but he did not stop.

I exclaimed in surprise, before jumping out of the way.

Jason skidded to a stop and stood still on the other side of me, opposite Chelsea. My flashlight bounded between them and Dan as they stood silently, hands open like claws. Their eyes stared into my soul. My tongue was too dry to speak, my heart beating like it would burst.

As the light shook across Jason’s face, I saw scratches on his neck and face. Three sharp marks from claws ran down his cheek. I looked at Dan and Chelsea. They shared the marks as well. My light went back to Jason, but he was no longer looking at me. His gaze went above and behind me. As did Dan’s. As did Chelsea’s. 

I turned as something jumped onto my back. Claws dug into my skin, scratched at my face. I dropped my flashlight as I flailed. Two shots flew off into the night, let go in a panic. The dark thing raked my sides. I tumbled onto the concrete, landing on top of it and feeling the thud as its head hit the ground. It gutturally hissed as it released me, and I scrambled away, putting three shots in the direction I thought it might be. It hissed louder before falling quiet, save the scratching of claws on concrete after the second shot. 

Heavy hands grabbed me from behind. Dan and Jason each had one of my arms. I held onto the gun for dear life as they tried to pry it from me. Dan’s large hand grabbed my hair and pulled my head back. 

I saw Chelsea’s hair glistening in the moonlight and the dark figure standing over her. She raised her chin to the sky, and the figure’s hands went around her head and arm. It looked directly at me, now bearing glowing yellow eyes and teeth that gleamed in the moonlight. It buried its teeth in her neck. Her body jolted at the impact. I cried out, then smacked Dan in the face with the gun barrel. Something cracked, and he recoiled, holding his face. Jason threw me to the ground, but I kicked out his legs before he could get on top of me. 

I leveled the gun at the figure. It turned to me, dropping Chelsea’s limp body to the ground. I fired two shots that had no effect, for it took heavy steps toward me. The hood of its cloak was thrown back, and its bald head glowed white as its skin stretched around the gaping mouth of fangs. What had seemed so small earlier now towered over me as I scrambled backward, emptying the gun in its direction. It seemed to look down and smile at the wounds hidden by the black cloak. I got to my feet and ran for my truck.

The grass swished in panic out of the way of my feet. I heard a “whooom” behind me. I gasped as a dark shape blocked moonlight guiding me, then darted in my peripheral vision. I passed the piles of broken stakes. A great, leathery sound flapped to my right, and just as I reached for the door handle of my truck, I was hit by a force like a charging rhino. I slammed into the side of my truck, denting the door, and crumpled to the ground. Something snapped in my forearm.

As I laid there trying to breath, I watched the shadow circle in the sky. I felt it hit the ground as it landed on the other side of my truck.

Adrenaline forcing me awake, I used my one good arm to prop myself up to my feet. The clawed feet scratched the ground, relishing in the click of gravel on talon. I thudded against the rear door, grabbing the handle with my good hand and throwing it open. The talons drug across the hood of the car, peeling the paint. I clutched at my ears. The creature’s hissing filled the air like a hundred snakes. As I stepped to get in the backseat, claws dug into my ankle, dragging me away from the door. I cried out and clutched at anything I could, grabbing the prism pole, its metal point reflecting the moonlight.

I flopped on my back, my head hitting the running board of my truck. My vision swam as I slid across the grass. I gazed up to see two glowing orbs, materializing into the moon and the head of the creature. It released my ankle, baring its teeth. It spread its black wings and blocked the moonlight.

I thrust the point of the prism pole forward with everything I had. It caught the creature in the chest. Surprise flooded the yellow eyes as I sat up and stabbed at it again. A gurgling hiss filled its throat as it swiped at me. Blood flowed down my cheek at a newly opened cut. I stabbed at it again, and felt the point sink deeper into its flesh. It cried out in pain.  The long arms grabbed the pole, and as it turned away, the fiberglass snapped. The creature stumbled backwards.

It turned and leapt, as if to fly, but after its wings beat, it plummeted into the tall grass.

I lay looking up at the moon. The night was silent.

Someone shuffled from the property to my left. I turned myself to see it, wincing as I put weight on my broken arm.

Jason stumbled out of the long grass. He limped toward the truck, his face full of confusion.

“Mike?” he said, his voice hoarse. “Why am I here?”

“I don’t know,” I said.

“You saw it too?”

“Yes.”

His eyes were wide in fear. “I let it in,” he said.

The wind picked up. Leathery wings beat in the distance. Jason crumpled to the ground. I stumbled to him. Two gashes on the back of his neck bled rivers down his back.

A shadow passed over the moon.
