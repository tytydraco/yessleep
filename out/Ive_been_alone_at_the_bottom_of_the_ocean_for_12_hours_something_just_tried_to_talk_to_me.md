I need something, a record of this. An account for me to look back on and laugh at when I get back to the surface. Which I will do, there’s no doubt in my mind I will return to the land of the living. I will get my payout tenfold my usual rate. 

I’m usually with a crew of two or three other people. Good people, hard workers who spend most of their time down here. Down in the nothing. 

This isn’t really a summer job that you can just pick up and drop as you please. You train, you study, you get comfortable with the idea of being surrounded by darkness as water thick enough to kill you and you train. Demetri had seemingly made the mistake of forgetting the former and the latter.

He was so fucking skinny, if saturation diving had a weight minimum he’d be well under it. But when you need divers you need divers. And with the usual crew being a man down on the account of Max’s appendix’s bursting the higher-ups decided to fast track Deimtri straight down to the ocean floor. It was meant to be one of our simpler dives. Get to the habitat and over the course of 20 odd days check a series of valves and gages on a couple of defunct wellheads. Then bob’s your uncle we’re back right in time for payday. Of course that’s not what happened.

We’d gotten to deeper than deep.

“Plenty of company” Mick chuckled as we unpacked. He, A deck of cards, a bottle of whiskey and a dead rockstars biography. Myself, a few comedies for the cd player, a knife for whittling and enough nicotine gum to kill a horse.

“How so?” I asked.

“Three of us, at least a million billion bits of sand. Like I said, plenty of company.”

“Heh.” I half laughed as Demetri threw up his breakfast in the habitat’s sole bathroom.

“Y’alright in there lad?” Mick called out. He was met with the sound of more vomiting.

Mick gave me a nod to imply I should check on the kid.

“You do it.” I responded.

“Good time to introduce yourself.” Mick said.

I poked my head round the bathroom door.   
  
“Hey buddy. The first time made my stomach turn a bit too. Promise this stays between us.” I smiled, not that he could see me, his head was deep in the toilet bowl.

“Fuck you.” he choked out between retches. “Stop speaking so slowly.” Another retch. “I’m going back up”

“Sorry man. You want some water.” I took a step back.

“You’re speaking twice. At the same time. Stop speaking twice.” He sputtered out.

“What.” I asked.

“You’re fucking with my head. You slipped me something. Fuck you. Fuck you man” He started shouting. I heard Mick step into the doorway behind me.

“Woah okay, It’s weird and it’s stressful the first time. You’ll get used to it, we all do.” I reassured him as he slowly staggered to his feet. 

“No.” He spat.

He swung for me, a sloppy punch on sea legs. He fell to his knees.

“Woah!” Me and mitch both took a step back. It silent, completely silent in the habitat. 

“Stop talking. Stop saying it in that voice. Stop it. Stop it. Stop it.” He slammed his fists against his head. Mick ducked under my arm and kneeled to his level.

He looked back up to me and mouthed. “Radio in.”

As Mick started murmuring and reassuring Dimitri I ducked away to the intercom in the storage/rec area.

I pressed the little green button on the intercom and spoke into the Mic.

“Check Check Check. Surface crew you read me?”

The speaker buzzed for a moment.

“Loud n’ clear.” One of the managers replied.

“Listen that kid, he’s lost his marbles. I think he needs to go back up.”  
  
Silence.

Then another buzz.

Silence again.  
“...You hear any of that.” The manager said.

“No sir.” I replied.

“Well shit, comm’s fucked too. If you get up now. The depressurisation habitat hasn’t started so he won’t get the worst of it going back….”

Silence.

“Hello?”  
  
Silence.

Then a long awaited buzz.

“Jesus Christ these comms are a nightmare. Listen, one of you goes up with him. We’ll send a crew down to help finish the job asap. But whoever’s staying is gonna have to sit pretty in the meantime.”

“Alright, which one of us should it be?”  
  
Silence, this time one that felt like it wouldn’t be interrupted. I prayed that I’d be the one going up but as I looked into the bathroom and saw Mick slowly helping Demetri to his feet I’d soon realise out that couldn’t be the case.

The young man stuck an accusing point in my direction. “You. You’re the one.. You. You. Stop it. Stop.”

I sighed. In what felt like I was signing away my sanity, I said. “Mick surface crew said you guys can go up in the bell. You gotta go now though.”

And they left, thankfully they all left their shit. 

I have a ritual when I’m down here, we’ve got this big window. Me and Mick call it the reverse aquarium. It’s a glass wall that looks out into complete darkness most of the time. Every now and then a fish will swim by. When it does you have to take a shot. Drinking games are more pathetic than fun when you're alone.

I gave up trying the comms about three hours in. figured if they needed to talk they’d respond or call by now. But it’s probably more likely the things busted. *Great*.

I was looking out into the darkness. It’s a rare kinda darkness. I’ve always thought that on the surface, it’s almost dangerous to stare unblinking into pitch black. In the woods, something could be looking back, in space well who knows.   
But down here.

Another thought cut through that one, in my late wife’s voice. “Hello?”

*Fuck I’ve lost it.*

I took a deep breath and swig from the bottle.

I stood up, time for an early bedtime.

“Can you hear me?” Again her voice.

I began walking away. 

“I *know* you can hear me. It’s okay. I’m not a predator to you.” She said again.

I scooped up the cards I was previously playing solitaire with.

“You can talk to me. It’s okay.” She said, with the calmness of a day care teacher.

“Stop it.” I unthinkingly blurted out.

“I heard that. “Stop” like your friend was saying. I’m sorry they had to go away.”

I shrunk into silence and finished collecting the cards.

“Those things. Show me one of them without you seeing it. You don’t think I'm alive. Part of reality. If i’m not. Prove it. Put one up against the separation between water and air. I’ll tell you what’s on it.”

I sighed. I don’t want to be another diver like Dimitri who didn’t have the stuff to handle the solitude, to handle the water. If it was wrong, I was insane. But at least I knew that, at least I’d have a chance at fighting it.

“Fine.” I shot out.

I picked one from the deck and pinned it face down against the window.

A moment of silence save for a distance noise I couldn’t quite make out.

“It’s mostly the colour of bone… Of sand on warm shores. But it has a large mark of red in the centre. And two smaller dots of red.” She said.

“What number is it.” 

Silence.

I looked at the previously unseen card, my eyes unmoving from the piece of paper. It was the ace of hearts. Whatever was talking to me was right.

“Do you believe me now? I promise I’m of no harm. I’ve eaten today and even if I hadn’t I couldn’t eat you. We can just talk for now.”

“Why.” I asked, not taking my eyes off the card, not out of fascination but fear of whatever was outside my field of view.

“Would it help if you could see me. I think it’s only fair. I can see you.” She said with a hint of playfulness or charm or something not meant to threaten.

“O-okay.” I slowly looked up from the card. “Will you be at the windo-” Before I could finish my sentence, she emerged from the darkness slowly gliding into the light that bled from the window.

It was a blue whale.

“Can you see me now?”
