Do you know what a fairy ring is?  

It’s a phenomenon that happens when a group of mushrooms grows in a circle. In my country they’re very common, especially during the rainy season.  

Grandparents often tell little kids superstitions about never crossing one of these circles, because you might upset the fairies or end up in their realm. It sounds like a simple story to keep children away from poisonous mushrooms or from trampling a neighbour’s lawn, but I know that, at least in one case that happened, it’s true.

My name is Liam. I own a pub in the village. I have a wife and two beautiful daughters. My life is monotonous, almost tedious.  

I’ve had a habit for many years now. I like to walk before or after work near Mrs. O’Reilly’s garden (the current owner of the name; when I was a boy it belonged to her grandmother). Fairy rings sometimes grow there.  

When I see one I walk carefully, making sure no one is watching, take a stick, and pull up enough of the mushrooms to break the circle. On one of these occasions I came across something completely unexpected.  

A book.  

In the centre of the circle lay a small book, about the size of a notebook. It was bound in black leather, had straps, and its pages were yellowed and irregular. It looked ancient, like a pirate’s logbook from a story.  

I touched it with the stick I was going to use on the mushrooms and carefully moved it outside the circle. Then I picked it up. Before I could read anything, I felt drops of water on my head. It had started to rain. I slipped the diary inside my coat and ran home.  

My family greeted me as always. My older daughter handed me a towel, the younger one hugged me, and my wife asked about work. Everything perfectly normal.  

The rest of the day passed without incident. We ate dinner, I showered, and I went to bed. I wanted to look at the diary I’d found, so I got up with the excuse of going for a short walk.  

I went to the workshop, lit a lamp, sat down at the table, and placed the diary on it. Carefully I opened the book. It did look old, not deteriorated, but as if someone had bound it recently using archaic methods.  

To my surprise the writing wasn’t some strange dialect from another country or an old Celtic variant of English. It was modern English. I flipped through a few pages and could understand it without trouble. The handwriting wasn’t elegant at all; it looked like the writing of a child who had never taken a calligraphy class or had to write a proper letter.  

I decided to start from the beginning. On the third page there was a neat paragraph that began with a sentence that froze me solid when I read it. It was the name of someone I hadn’t seen in many years.  

“Hello, my name is Cillian O’Sullivan.”

\*\*Year 1986\*\*

Cillian was an energetic boy, like a radiant sun in human form. With his blond hair, green eyes, and a smile that could charm the devil himself. He was my best friend.  

I was a shy, introverted kid, but Cillian always pulled me out of my comfort zone. We’d known each other since nursery school. Our families were friends, and we always played together, sometimes at my house, sometimes at his.  

He loved football. He used to come home covered in mud after spending the whole afternoon on the pitch. I’m certain that if he had reached adulthood he would have been very popular with the girls and would have achieved anything he set his mind to.  

Unfortunately that never happened.

One day when we were ten, walking along the pavement after a long day at school, we noticed Mrs. O’Reilly’s garden. The grass was damp from the seasonal rains, and near the path was a circle of white mushrooms, a fairy ring.  

We stopped. Cillian, in a mocking tone, talked about how his grandmother had warned him never to step into one because the fairies would curse him. I told him my grandfather said the fairies would turn anyone who entered the circle into a toad.  

Cillian said, “Watch this,” raised his arms like an aeroplane, and jumped hard onto the grass inside the ring of mushrooms.  

I watched his feet touch the wet grass, and in the space of a single blink he was gone.  

He vanished into thin air. No lights, no glittering dust, no disturbance in the air. He was simply gone.  

I blinked several times in confusion, looked around, behind me, but he wasn’t there. Outside the circle lay a pencil sharpener that had fallen from Cillian’s pocket when he jumped. I picked it up and walked toward the ring, carelessly stepping on some of the mushrooms, and stared at the centre.  

There was nothing.  

I touched the earth to see if he had sunk into some kind of bog in the garden, but there was nothing. I stood there for several minutes not knowing what to do. I shouted for Cillian, telling him to stop joking, that it wasn’t funny, to show himself, but he never appeared.  

I ran home and told my parents. They tried to calm me down and asked me to explain. I did the best I could. I showed them the fairy ring in Mrs. O’Reilly’s garden and repeated the jump Cillian had made, but my parents thought it was just a game that had gotten out of hand and that Cillian must be somewhere.  

Hours passed. Cillian’s parents, the O’Sullivans, reported him missing to the police. He never came home from school. They questioned me about what happened after we left school. I told them the same story I had told my parents.  

A search was started. They asked Mrs. O’Reilly, but she said many children played in her garden and nothing strange had ever happened. Days became weeks, weeks became months.  

The O’Sullivan family mourned the disappearance of their son. The case was recorded as a missing person, as if he had gotten lost in the woods and died without his remains ever being found.  

I remained firm in my testimony: Cillian disappeared when he entered that fairy ring. Almost no one believed me.  

At school they mocked me or called me a murderer; they thought I had killed him and hidden the body. My parents took me to therapy. The psychologist helped me cope and calm my emotions, but I never stopped telling the truth. I knew Cillian hadn’t died in the woods. He had vanished inside that damned circle of mushrooms.

I said almost no one believed me. That’s because one person was more open to believing my story: Cillian’s grandmother.  

Shortly before the O’Sullivans moved away to try to forget the memory of Cillian, she comforted me and told me it wasn’t my fault. She said the fae are capricious and amuse themselves with the suffering of mortals.  

She told me that when she was a girl her friends used to play in a field on the outskirts of their village. One boy saw a fairy ring and jumped into it, vanishing. Another boy saw it and told the others. In their innocence they all followed him, jumping into the circle and, seeing him disappear, imitating him, hoping to travel to the fairy world.  

A friend of young Mrs. O’Sullivan ran and jumped onto the circle. Young Mrs. O’Sullivan tried to stop her, but as she ran to the circle and watched her friend vanish, she skidded to a halt, stepping on the mushrooms of the fairy ring without jumping onto it.  

A boy pulled her aside and tried to jump into the circle, but he didn’t disappear. Another tried, and another. It no longer worked.  

The children went home and told their parents. The adults searched the field without success. They dug under the fairy ring and found the remains of a body, not a child’s, but an adult’s. The story went that some criminals had killed a man and buried him there, and from his grave the mushrooms had grown, forming the fairy ring.  

When Mrs. O’Sullivan told me this, she said she had insisted to her grandson that he must never cross one of those circles, but unfortunately he hadn’t listened. Shortly afterwards the O’Sullivans moved away, and I never heard from them again.

At sixteen I decided to investigate on my own. One early morning I took a shovel and dug in the spot where the fairy ring had grown six years earlier. It didn’t take long to find a body buried there.  

I alerted the police and an investigation was carried out. Mrs. O’Reilly had bought the house only a few months before Cillian’s disappearance. The body belonged to the previous owner’s husband. He had been reported missing, and a few days later she had killed herself. Apparently he used to beat her, and one day she had had enough, poisoned him, and buried him in the ground in front of the house.  

They asked me how I had known he was there, but I just invented an excuse about still looking for Cillian. My reputation in the village was already well known. They took me for a madman, and life went on.  

I graduated, got a job in a pub, got married, had children, inherited the pub, and forty years passed without me knowing anything more about Cillian, until now.

\*\*Year 2026\*\*

When I read his name in the diary my mind filled with doubts. I went into denial. I thought perhaps it was someone with the same name and surname, though I remembered that “O’Sullivan” isn’t a common surname around here. Cillian’s family are the only ones with that name I know.  

There were other details too, like the little lightning bolts instead of dots over the i’s, a characteristic I remembered from Cillian’s writing because, according to him, “it looked Cool.”  

I steeled myself and kept reading. It seemed to have been written with fountain-pen ink and with the prose of a boy who had never had to read the classics of world literature in secondary school.

“Hello, my name is Cillian O’Sullivan.  

I write this while my mind still not go crazy. I fear I have little time before the madness take me.  

I came to this place many, many years ago. I used to be a normal boy, I had family and friends, I miss them a lot. Now a giant sadness invade me because I want to see them again.  

I remember the day I appeared in this place, I was walking with a friend, I think his name was Lance, or Liam, I don’t remember very well. I jumped on a fairy ring, one of those circles of mushrooms on the grass and next thing I knew I was in the forest.  

I got scared and walked along a path until I reached a village, it looked like one of those from the stories of medieval knights, old and full of old houses, but what surprised me most were the people, they had pointy ears and eyes without pupils, most of them green, like circular jewels.  

These people saw my clothes and my ears and came close to me, I was afraid, but they were friendly. I didn’t understand their language at first, but they gave me a potion to drink, when I drank it I could understand what they said, it was magic.  

They explained that people from my world sometimes arrive here by accident, I asked if they could help me go back but none of them knew how. I got sad, but they gave me some mushrooms to try. When I ate them I felt happy, I forgot everything bad.  

The days passed and I got used to living with these beings I called fairies. They ate different mushrooms that made them see beautiful colours, made them feel like they were flying, they didn’t get sad, they didn’t feel pain, it was wonderful.  

Their life was simple. They ate what the earth gave them, food never ran out, they spent their days celebrating, drinking, eating and loving each other. I don’t know how much time passed, it seemed like decades.  

My body grew slower than I expected. I looked like a young man just graduated from secondary school even though I had lived about 35 years according to the fairies’ calendar. I lived in a kind of dream, I ate, drank alcohol, made love with beautiful fairies and slept and the next day I repeated the cycle.  

Unfortunately one day we noticed the mushrooms started to become scarce, they didn’t appear as many as before when they were cultivated in abundance. Apparently a plague made them wither. Some tried to stop it, but it was useless.  

There were no scientists or people expert in plants because except for growing mushrooms, everyone dedicated themselves to celebrating, eating and getting drunk. The fairies didn’t take long to go mad, the world little by little became grey, sad and violent.  

There were no more mushrooms to take away the sadness or the pain. The fairies cried all day, they fought, they killed each other. What before looked like a colourful and free village now looked like a place full of rubbish, mould, uncut grass and excrement. Without the mushrooms reality was horrible.  

While I was walking in a kind of trance I noticed a group of people, they were different from the fairies, with round ears and eyes with pupils, they were humans. In the past I couldn’t notice them, now, without being under the effect of the mushrooms I could see them clearly.  

I told them about me, they told me they were a group of children who played jumping on a fairy ring and ended up the same as me, they travelled here, the fairies received them and gave them mushrooms to eat that made them forget their home so they could celebrate and eat without end.  

They looked a bit older than me, they seemed about thirty years old. They offered me a mushroom they had saved as if it were a jar of beer for a thirsty traveller, they told me it was one of the last ones left and that if I wanted it to last I should eat it in small bites.  

I knew I didn’t have much time, most of the fairies had lost to the sadness and killed themselves. I decided to take this book and write my story in case someone finds it.  

It’s been years since I wrote anything, I’ve been a long time without going to school and I didn’t finish primary. I’m consuming the mushroom they gave me little by little, I feel I want to continue a bit every time I do it, I only have half left.  

I will keep writing as far as I can, I want to talk about my happy days in this place before my end.”

I kept reading Cillian’s diary. The following pages were hard to understand. The handwriting was crude, with many crossings-out and blank spaces.  

The last written page had a blot of ink and bloodstains. I can only assume that Cillian took his own life because of the sadness once the effect of the mushrooms wore off after he finished the last one he had. Similar to what happens to drug addicts when they stop using. It’s called withdrawal syndrome.  

I meditated on what I had read. After decades I finally knew what had happened to my friend. I knew I hadn’t been crazy. I had the proof right in front of me.

I began to wonder how it was possible that the diary had reached me. It could have been a miracle, an act of God, fairy magic I didn’t understand, but the most likely explanation was something simpler.  

Cillian died in the same forest where he had arrived in that world, and from his grave a fairy ring grew. His diary remained inside it and travelled here, where I found it because I had become obsessed with destroying the mushroom circles on Mrs. O’Reilly’s lawn. Anyone else could have found it.

You might ask, “What did I do after that?”  

Not much. I transcribed Cillian’s diary into a document I saved in the cloud and then I burned it. No one would believe me if I told them. They had already called me crazy once, and I didn’t want to go through that again, especially now that I have a family.  

That said, I acquired a new habit. Every time I went out with my family, instead of ignoring the fairy rings and hoping my daughters wouldn’t take an interest, I would pull up some of the mushrooms from the circle and jump onto it. I told my family it was a good-luck ritual and encouraged them to copy me. They refused because they thought it was embarrassing, exactly as I expected. That way they would never want to approach one of those rings, let alone step into them.

The story Mrs. O’Sullivan’s grandmother told me made me understand something. There are certain key conditions that must be met for the same thing that happened to Cillian and to the children who played with young Mrs. O’Sullivan to occur:  

A fairy ring must form from the grave of a human being.  

A person must jump onto it without the mushrooms being stepped on or pulled up first.  

If that happens, the circle loses its magic and will not make anyone disappear. That’s why neither I nor Mrs. O’Sullivan disappeared. We both stepped on the mushrooms of the circle without jumping first, preventing other people from vanishing.

Which made me think:  

What would have happened if I had imitated Cillian?  

What if I had jumped without stepping on the mushrooms first?  

Would I have travelled with Cillian and eaten the mushrooms, living in eternal revelry until the plague made them disappear and the withdrawal drove us to suicide?  

I will never know.

I took one extra precaution. I modified my will, requiring that I be cremated when I die. My family is Catholic, but I do not wish to risk my grave becoming a portal for another curious child to disappear, not the way it happened to Cillian O’Sullivan.
