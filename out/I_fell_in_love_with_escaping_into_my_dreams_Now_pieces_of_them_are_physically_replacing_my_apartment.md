My waking life is exhausting. Just a relentless cycle of long shifts, physical burnout and constant stress. But one night, everything changed. I didn't just drift off into chaotic, blurry dreams. I woke up in a place I quickly came to call SleepLand.

The first time I arrived in SleepLand, it took my breath away. If you're familiar with The Lord of the Rings, you would think that SleepLand was one of those elvish cities. It was this impossible, sprawling sanctuary of soaring white stone spires that seemed to weave seamlessly into the trunks of massive ancient trees. The air tasted crisp, like autumn after a fresh rain. There was no sun in the sky but a permanent, breathtaking twilight. The stars seemed endless and the flora itself glowed and seemed to replicate the night sky. 

In SleepLand, I felt entirely weightless and I was completely lucid. This was no dream and I, myself was physically teleported to another world. I could walk through the halls and run my hand over the cool, flawless stone walls and think with a clarity I never possessed during the day. It was a beautiful world and I was the only soul there.

The first day I woke up to my alarm felt like punishment to me. My bedroom felt grey and heavy. It felt so suffocating. So I started chasing SleepLand. I started taking melatonin, I went to bed earlier and earlier, sometimes I skipped dinner just so I can escape and get back to those white towers. I became addicted to the peace. 

Exactly one month into my visits is when I first saw it. 

I climbed my way to the top of one of these great white stoned spires. I went out onto the high arched balcony overlooking a forest of silver glowing trees, just breathing in the quiet, when I noticed a movement on the marble walkway below. It didn't belong. In a world of perfect, elegant symmetry, this thing was a heavy, lumbering mass. It was a massive, dark entity. It was cloaked in pitch black fur. It was bear like but very distorted. It had no mouth, no nose, no claws. It was a hulking silhouette that seemed to swallow the twilight around it. The most jarring aspect was its two giant unblinking white eyes that burned out of its dark fur, the only feature visible on its body. 

My curiosity overrode my fear. I leaned over the balcony to get a better look but my foot scraped against the stone. The creature froze, its massive head snapped up and its giant white eyes locked directly onto mine. And in an instant I awoke in my bed. It felt as I was pushed off the balcony and warped into my bedroom. My heart was hammering against my ribs. I rolled over, rubbing my face, trying to shake off the intense adrenaline rush. When I reached for my phone to check the time I froze. 

The drywall next to my bed wasn't drywall anymore. The paint had vanished and the sunlight beaming through my bedroom window glistened off a perfect square of white stone wall identical to the smooth, cold and blindingly white stone of the balcony I was just standing on in SleepLand. I should have been terrified. But a consuming addiction took hold of me. SleepLand was my paradise and this thing was trespassing. I needed answer.

The next night I went to sleep early and flipped into SleepLand. I wasn't going to explore the spires anymore. Instead I went hunting. I followed the marble walkway in which I first encountered it on into the opposite direction leading to the edge of the forests of silver trees. It didn't take long until I found it. I kept to the shadows, tracking it from behind massive, ancient trees. I was desperate to figure out what this thing was. I watched as it left the forest and moved to the nearest giant white spire. It peered into the massive entrance scanning the empty white halls and its giant white columns. I wanted to get a closer look, wanted to see if it was looking for something. As I stepped forward it spun around. The massive shadow loomed over me, giant white eyes piercing into my soul. And in an instant, once again it felt as I was pushed and suddenly I flipped back into my bedroom. 

This time the smell of autumn rain was so thick in my bedroom it masked the smell of my dusty carpet. When I stood up my feet sank and inch. The cheap grey carpeted flooring was gone, completely replaced by a thick, silver glowing moss that rippled when I stepped on it. 

I am losing control of my apartment. If I were to wake up from SleepLand without being spotted there are no changes. But once those  giant white eyes catch me stalking it, I wake up with a piece of its world hitching a ride and anchoring itself back in mine. 

Tonight I went too far. I fell asleep determined to confront it. I found it sitting at the base of a massive white tower, it's bear like shadow casting a dark void across the glowing silver grass. I didn't hide this time. I walked right out into the open and demanded to know what it was. Those giant white unblinking eyes locked onto mine instantly.

 This time I didn't wake up. There was no flip.

The creature slowly stood up, it's thick black fur bristling as it towered over me. Its giant white eyes widened as if mirroring my own terrified expression. And then a sound tore out of its featureless face. A deep grinding vibration that formed words into my own head.

"You carved the door. Now let us through."

FLIP

I tore out of sleep screaming, throwing myself backwards. I didn't hit my headboard but instead back into cold and hard flawless white stone. I scrambled to my feet and looked around at what was once my room. My apartment was gone. Drywall and ceiling all completely replaced by the towering marble architecture of SleepLand. The endless breathtaking twilight sky stretched out right above where my ceiling fan used to be. The silver glowing moss had completely engulfed my bed. My apartment is no longer in the real world. It's an open courtyard within the elvish spire. 

And the worst part? I'm completely awake. Waking up didn't save me this time. There is nothing left of my reality to wake up to. It has  used my obsession to anchor its world to mine. I didn't think. I just ran. I bolted out the room and began descending what used to be stairs of my apartment building, but they were wrong. They were steep, winding and made of white marble. I was running down inside of a massive, towering spire. 

 

I made it to the front doors and practically threw myself through them. The cold pavement of the real world hit my feet and for a second, an overwhelming wave of relief washed over me. It was still the middle of the night. The streets were dead quiet, the dark asphalt illuminated only by the yellow glow of the streetlamps. I just needed to get away. I sprinted down the sidewalk, desperately trying to put as much distance between myself and that spire as humanly possible.

A block away, I spotted a sign of life. A man was walking his dog completely oblivious to me. But as I stepped toward him, it happened. My vision jerked violently.

FLIP

But I didn't flip back to SleepLand. I was still standing on the asphalt, but everything felt... heavy. I tried to call out to the man, but no sound came out of my throat. Just a deep, grinding vibration that rattled around inside my own chest. I looked down to check out my hands but my hands weren't mine anymore. My arms were massive, elongated limbs covered in a thick, suffocating coat of pitch black fur. My body felt lumbering, massive, and entirely wrong.

The dog froze first. It let out a terrified whine, tucking its tail tightly between its legs and pulling frantically on the leash. The man stopped and turned around, looking for whatever had scared his dog. His eyes scanned the darkness until they landed directly on me.

I watched the color completely drain from his face. He stumbled backward, his mouth falling open in absolute, paralyzing horror. And as I stared at him, I realized what he was looking at. I could feel the cold night air hitting the only two features left on my entire face.

He wasn't looking at a man. He was looking at two giant, unblinking white eyes staring back at him from the dark. Then suddenly the man had vanished. He didn't run away. It was as though he had flipped.
