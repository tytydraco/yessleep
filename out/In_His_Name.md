I never was interested in celebrating Easter, sure I went with my family to go see other family, ate the food that the others brought, make small conversations with them but it was more connected to a horrific event that made me stop celebrating.

My grandkids were often disappointed when I didn’t want to partake in finding Easter eggs but I told them that they were always welcomed to find my egg.

 Which they gladly did. 

My own kids had asked me; “Why don’t you celebrate Easter, mom? You allow us to celebrate but you don’t.” 

I had to lie about why I didn’t. 

I didn’t like to lie to them since they were my kids but I don’t think they would understand why I had stopped or understood the horror that’s been burned into my mind like someone taking a Livestock branding iron to my brain. 

My dislike of celebrating Easter began when I was 12 years old.

I grew up living in a small town in Louisiana, where everyone basically knew everyone. I rather not say what the towns name is, since writing or saying the name would give me chills. 

Or that ***THEY*** are watching. 

Waiting to drag me back to town to be their next meal to be devoured. 

Every Easter celebration, we go to church to celebrate the resurrection of Jesus Christ. 

Father Deacon was old, around 70 or 80, but spoke with passion of the sermon on this day. But one particular thing he does is having someone make a bred Jesus that was on a cross.

Normally churches would have people in lines to have a thin, tasteless wafer that was supposed to be the body of Christ, and stale wine that was supposed to be his blood. 

When it came to eat the body and drink the blood of Jesus, everyone lined up then took a slight chunk of the bread to eat, almost as if they were taking Jesus’s skin off of him to consume. People in the town loved this tradition during Easter and have talked about how they hope this would continue.

A little morbid once you think about it. 

Taking a chunk of bread from the bread Jesus almost like we were digging our fingers into the fleshy body of Jesus out of him and then eat.

Sadly, this tradition was on a halt due to Father Deacon passing and the woman that was the one responsible to make the fresh Jesus bread had to move back to Texas due to a family emergency.

Everyone was devastated by this news, they were hopeful for the tradition to continue and maybe ask Father Deacon if they could incorporate fresh bread to be like the body of Christ’s but they were to late to ask. 

Father Maxim took charge of church every day and did the funeral for Father Deacon, though it didn’t feel the same when Father Deacon was doing the sermons.

In 1942, it was announced by Father Maxim that the town will have a new priest in February. 

Everyone was excited to meet the new priest of our church, everyone was buzzing about what he was like and if he was in away like Father Deacon was when he was alive.

But this priest that we got… Wasn’t like Father Deacon or Father Maxim.

Father Eliakim, was his name. He was around his 40’s, tall, lanky and had a sort of strange gleam in his eyes whenever he does his sermons. His sermons were not like any priest that we had.

He spoke in a way that made him seem like he was egocentric and he spoke constantly with his hands about the subject that was in church.

This made everyone chalking it up that he was just new to the community and trying to make a good impression but somehow the other kids and I knew something was not right about Father Eliakim.

Some said that they have seen him with people that they don’t know or never seen before.

Some made a rumor that he was kicked out of his previous church for a behavior that was deemed too extreme.

I always took doubt in those rumors, something else was telling me that Father Eliakim was dangerous or deranged of being a priest. Maybe it was just me being paranoid at that age, but I don’t know.

Easter arrived. 

No one was prepared for the horror that they were going to witness on that day in 1942.

My gut was screaming at me to not go to church this day since something was wrong but I ignored it because if I told my parents that I didn’t want to go to church, I would received a whipping then lectured about the importance of going to church.

So here I was, wearing my Sunday dress following obediently with my parents following other families that were piling into church. 

Once inside, the atmosphere was calm as church would be. But there was something else lingering in the air that was unsettling. 

Something… Terrifying.

I glanced at each townsfolk going to the pews to sit with their families but something or someone caught my eye.

It was a woman.

A woman that I never saw before, standing next to the candles where you light one in memory of a loved one that departed. 

She was around in her 30’s, fair skin, and wearing what looked like robes that priests wore. But her face….. 

Her face gave me chills; it was almost blank without any expression and she looked at everyone that was around then towards another that had the same expression on her face.

I clutched my mom’s hand in fright of this, she tensed like I did, knowing something was off.

I heard murmurs of others whispering around, some ignoring the strange people that were placed on each corner in the church.

“Who are they?”

“Never saw them folk around here before.”

“Friends of Father Eliakim?” 

The church bell ranged from the belfry, everyone hushed as church started. Someone spoke asking to go to the page that was on the board to sing. 

Organ music began and everyone started to sing in their books, there was no joy in their voices only uncertainty.

Two men appeared in the aisle, walking down towards the alter, holding trays in their hands with covers as if they were trying to keep what was underneath from being exposed. 

Father Eliakim followed behind, singing loudly, a chilling tone in his voice. Singing as if he was excited for this day that arrived, he made his way to the altar. Gave a bow to the statue of Jesus before turning to face us with a grin on his face.

The organ music ended and the echoes of our voices silenced.

“Peace be upon you!”

We replied. “And to you as well, Father.”

“Today is the most precious day! The day where Jesus Christ rises again from His tomb.”

Father Eliakim spread his arms wide.

Usually, that would have been the part where everyone smiled. Instead, I remember looking around the church. 

Nobody smiled.

My mother squeezed my hand, almost a little too tightly that would break my hand.

Father Eliakim continued. “We gather here to remember His sacrifice. His suffering. His death.”

He paused.

His eyes traveled across the congregation.

“And yet, so many of us forget what it truly means to partake in His body.”

I remember thinking that was a strange thing to say. Why would he say of how many of us forget to “partake in His body”? Why would he say that?

“Now,” Father Eliakim walked away from the podium to the middle where the two men that held the trays stood. “I had heard about a particular tradition that this town partakes in with a bread Jesus. Such a delightful tradition to consume His body.”

The air tensed up, almost in a suffocating way like you were drowning in the ocean with something weighing you down.

“So I have a pleasure of continuing this tradition,” His smile widened, a crescent moon smile wide as if he was a Cheshire Cat that smiled at Alice when she appeared in wonderland. He gestured towards the back, the doors banged open, making everyone jump from the sound. 

A man and woman pushed a gurney, the table was covered but there was an outline of a person’s body underneath it. 

They stopped in front of the alter then retreated to two different corners in the church.

Father Eliakim stepped down from the altar with the two men with the trays. “We have been taught that the bread is merely a symbol.”

He reached down to the cover then unfurled it to reveal what was underneath. “But what if it wasn’t?”

Everyone gasped, I leaned forward a bit to see who it was on the table and nearly felt sick.

It was Miles Lukey’s older brother, Abel, laying there. He was half naked, with a cloth that covered his dignity almost like Christ and his arms outstretched like Jesus. There was blood on his hands. 

Father Eliakim turned and lifted the cover on the silver tray then lifted what looks like a knife. A knife that I have seen butchers use to skin a cow or pig.

He faced us, wearing that same Cheshire smile. “What if our Lord intended for us to truly consume Him?”

The other man that was standing on the left that’s carrying a tray already had the cover off to reveal a rusty goblet that looked like it had black stains. 

Father Eliakim turned his attention back to Abel Lukey, who was unconscious still, he lowered the knife to Abel’s leg then pierced it.

The sound Abel made… 

The sound of someone in pure pain and agony echoed in the church, everyone gasped, a woman started to cry, some children cried in terror.

But nothing was more gut-retching was hearing Abel’s skin sounding like a zipper.

Abel screamed in agony as Eliakim continued till he stopped.

He turned to us, holding between his bloody fingers was Abel’s flesh. Smile still wide.

“This! This is what we should consume! His flesh.” He declared, turning to the man on his right he lowered it to his mouth and the man accepted it.

I felt sick. 

The smell of metallic blood was in the air. The screams of Abel burned in my skull.

“Come, let us feast. Let us feast on His flesh.” Eliakim said, everyone didn’t stand after what they saw Father Eliakim did to Abel, no one stood instantly for the communion. 

Father Eliakim noticed then scowled, darkly with anger. “Stand! Stand and feast upon His flesh! Do you not want to consume your dear lord and savior?!”

The woman in the loft started to sing uneasily. 

I winced at his shout, I looked around and saw everyone slowly starting to stand on their feet to start a line for communion. Two lines. Abel started to scream again, the same zipper sound, repeat.

When it was my turn, the state of Abel…. The state he was in made me sick. Chunks of his flesh and muscle were taken out of him, you could see the pinkish-red muscle and I swore on his chest I saw a rib bone.

The metallic smell was overbearing but in addition of the fresh smell of urine.

I was shaking as I held the knife in my hand, I didn’t want to hurt Abel, he was already in such agony with his flesh gone from his body. I blinked allowing hot tears down my cheeks.

He opened his mouth to scream again, but it came out hoarse, I pierced his flesh, trying hard not to puke from the grotesque sounds.  

Having a chunk of his flesh in my fingers, blood trailing down my fingers, I stared at it. 

“Hurry, child. Eat His flesh.” Eliakim encouraged.

I swallowed a lump in my throat and popped Abel’s flesh in my mouth then walked away to head back to the pew. The metallic taste was so strong that I forced myself not to vomit, the flesh and muscle was tough. Once I had it in a mushy consistency, I forced myself to swallow.

The taste lingered in my mouth. I did not want to know what the drink was made of but I had already know what it was made of.

Abel’s screams had died out, the only sound that came out of him was a whimper of pain. The woman stopped singing, the organ continued to play until it died.

Father Eliakim smiled at everyone. “Now we have all consumed His flesh. Do we all feel connected to Him?” 

“Yes, Father.” Everyone replied.

“Good! Good. Isn’t that amazing to feel His presence in your own body, to feel His sacrifice, to feel His pain.” 

He continued the sermon, ignoring what he had put people through after watching him take a slice of flesh and muscle from Abel, then forcing everyone to follow his lead of cutting him to pieces for them to have his flesh.

My parents were so distraught of what happened on that Easter in 1942, we moved out of that place to live in another town. My parents told me to never say where I was from if anyone asks.

I still could hear the screams and flesh in my dreams and my head, but my parents didn’t take me to therapy for it because they didn’t know how therapists would react to my trauma of what I saw.

After I graduated from college, I started to think back about the small town in Louisiana, wondering what ever happened to everyone and who was Father Eliakim. 

Doing my own research, I searched everything for about the town I used to live in and Father Eliakim.

Running on caffeine and determination on figuring out Eliakim, I was running into dead ends of my research about him.

It wasn’t until I received a letter from my friend, Vera Michael, she was still living in that small town. When I received the letter, it had my address but no return address. I thought it was a mistake that someone made until I saw the wording above my name.

“*Please Open and Read*”

Curiosity got to me, which I wished I hadn’t allowed it to happen, for what Vera had said in the letter chilled my bones.

“Holly, 

I hope this letter made it through to you in your new life and county you are in that’s far away from {small town}. 

And I hope they haven’t seen me trying to get this letter to you by sneaking out to the mail carrier to get it to you. 

Things here have gotten more isolating than it was years ago, it hardly feels like home anymore but somewhere that’s different than what it was before. 

It doesn’t feel like home, Holly. 

We continued to do that grotesque tradition of eating human flesh and muscle, but it’s with people that we have known for our entire lives.

Mr. Bowen, god help us that he wasn’t killed by them, he asked during one Easter of why we were doing this. Why are we eating human flesh.

Eliakim… 

He smiled that Cheshire Cat smile at everyone and revealed that he all wanted us to feast upon actual flesh from people in town like we are eating the flesh of Jesus Christ.

But we know it wasn’t the truth, Marcus did his own investigation into Eliakim and it isn’t good at all.

Holly, thank the Lord that you and your family made it out. 

It turns out that Eliakim has been doing this since 1930! Taking people to be the flesh and blood of Jesus, he chooses people to become a sacrifice to be consumed by everyone.

And the people who held a blank expression at church? They’re his followers, his actual followers. 

He’s been starting to isolate the town from the outside, people have asked the mayor to stop him from doing this grotesque tradition and to kick him out to get a new one.

But… The next day at church, the mayor was in front of the church, his body contorted into a scarecrow. 

More bodies that spoke out against Eliakim or just trying to escape, stood in front of the church’s building. 

Those who continue to go to church, they have become his new followers and obey his every word.

I don’t know if my act of sending you this letter is going to be an act of treason or whatever but I just wanted to let you know of what’s truly going on here in {small town}.

They have eyes everywhere.

If this is my last letter you will received from me, Holly. I want to let you know that I will always be grateful to have a friend like you in my life and slightly jealous that you left before it gotten this bad.

\- Vera Michael”
