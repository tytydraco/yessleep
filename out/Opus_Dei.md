I opened my eyes after completing my blessing, and surveyed the scene before me. My mental checklist was ready, the instructions condensed into bullet points I could check off as I went so I could ensure perfect compliance. This was a masterwork; it was more than simply: "enter the house, secure the children, kill the adults, stage the tableau, clear the evidence and leave." The instructions covered every aspect, down to where individual blood droplets needed to fall. Nothing could be less than absolute perfection.

The scene was sickening, of course; it had to be. I had done my work with exceptional brutality. Bones and viscera lay exposed to the viewer, ensuring even a veteran homicide detective would be affected. Satisfied that my work was complete, I removed the last of the evidence and cleared the scene.

The work used to bother me. I was corrupted by doubt when I started, but have grown to understand it - even admire it. It truly is amazing. 

This case, for instance: they're all Innocents. The adults are Good People; devoted followers, lovely parents and neighbors, true pillars of the community. They will be missed, and the horror of what happened to them will affect more than just the local population. The children will go to a... challenging couple, but will be strengthened by their experiences. We won't lose them, not in the long run. 

The effects of this were explained to the adults. Sometimes sacrifices must be made to shock people into Faith, and these kinds of mysteries stick in the mind like the thorns of a nettle - one may forget it's there, until the whisper of a thought brushes against it. A sharp intake of breath, a grisly memory brought to the fore, a drawing of focus until the ripples of pain die off... It seems barbaric until you realize its scope. Hundreds of people will seek to understand. Criminologists and armchair detectives alike will discuss this family, telling their story to an audience of hundreds more. The children will tell their part of the story; and it will be revisited when the sins of their adopters are brought to light. Mercy will be poured over them. A community of protectors will be born, and the children will grow up surrounded by blessings for which they will be grateful until their dying breaths.

The adults were made aware of all this. Whether or not they consented, I would have killed them, but it's a test all Innocents must take. Will they give of themselves while they're still ours, or will an act of selfishness blemish their being?

Of course, the instructions covered dissent. With Free Will in the mix, we can't be completely sure of the outcome, which is why the instructions are so detailed. Innocents are usually more constrained, but Free Will has always been a wild card. 

As archangels, we can do the work without being corrupted by it - it's an act of Love, after all. We take what is ours while we can. The more horrific the tableau, the more turn heavenward to find answers. We can leave the corrupted with enough time to seek salvation.

The hope is that those who know will understand that Hell offers a worse fate for a longer period, should you turn away for too long.
