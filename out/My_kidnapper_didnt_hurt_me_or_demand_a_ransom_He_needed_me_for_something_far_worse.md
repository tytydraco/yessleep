“Yeah, I think so.” 

“Perfect.” He gestured towards the cylinder. “Take a look and tell me what you see.”

A few feet away, David was rifling through the pocket of his suit jacket. He shook his head. 

“Not yet.”

My stomach grumbled. I nodded.

A cathode ray television sat alone at the center of the massive room. The rest of the furniture was stacked carelessly against the walls. Half-filled book shelves, ornate armories, luxury sofas, red satin chairs, and fully stocked bar carts, all moved out of the way to create space for a single, bargain-bin TV. 

David grabbed a comforter from a nearby closet and laid it in front of the TV. 

“Take a seat.” 

Hesistantly, I began walking. A strange smell lingered in the air, harsh and metallic, like burnt gunpowder or welding fumes. I plopped down on the comforter and set the timer next to me. A nervous smile spread across his face.

“The next two days are going to fly by. We’re just gonna relax and have ourselves a movie marathon - my personal recuts of the classics.” A sputtering chuckle poured from his lips. The pained laughter reverberated through the seemingly empty estate, and all the while, he stared at me with unblinking eyes, clenching and unclenching his sweat-caked palms. 

His mouth gradually closed.

“Don’t ever leave this room without my permission. Got it?”

“Great. Be back with the mac N’ cheese in a jiff.”

Whispers behind the walls. Airy, breathless noises. Was it his family? Did he have other kids trapped here, too? I considered squeezing through the heaps of furniture and pressing my ear to the wall, but before any bravery could crystallize, David burst into the room, carrying two things: a bowl of macaroni and a stack of VHS tapes tucked in his armpit. 

The meal wasn’t gourmet, but it was palatable, and trauma tends to work up an appetite. As I inhaled hot spoonfuls of store-bought pasta, David carefully placed the tapes onto the comforter. They had no identifying labels. Instead, each bore a crudely scribbled number between the reels. One through eight. A sequence. 

He inserted tape 1 into the machine and pressed play. 

The Walt Disney logo twinkled across the screen, a cheery melody galloping over random pops of static, followed by previews, and eventually, *Mulan*. 

As we watched, a sudden wave of feverish euphoria surged through my veins. My heart raced. I became enraptured with the movie, talking David’s ear off about every little detail. He grinned, nodding along. I shouldn’t have been smiling, but I was; I couldn’t control it. 

Then, during the tail end of *A Girl Worth Fighting For*, something strange happened. 

The tape seemed to skip. The movie froze on the image of a snowy mountaintop. It was a blip - couldn’t have lasted more than a tenth of a second - but during that time, my body went haywire. Pressure exploded behind my eyes. My limbs were on fire, but my chest was seized by an aching chill. A thrumming noise swirled around me, like the beating of helicopter blades pitched down ten octaves, so deafening that I couldn’t hear anything else. I sucked in a sharp gulp of air, but just as soon as I did - 

it all stopped. 

The movie continued. 

My body settled. 

The thrumming vanished. 

“You OK, Sofia?” David asked, laying a gentle hand on my shoulder. I turned to him and thought for a moment. The experience was so fleeting, so bizarre and inexplicable, I had no idea what to say other than: 

“Yeah, I’m OK…could I have some more mac N’ cheese?” 

He tussled my hair. 

“Coming right up.” 

As he paced away, David snickered and whispered something under his breath: a double entendre that I wouldn’t understand until much later. 

*“A spoonful of sugar makes the medicine go down…”*

**2026:**

I always knew this day would come.

Days? Weeks? Months?

So, unlike before, I wouldn’t have the luxury of a countdown.

**1999:**

Tape 2 was *Hercules.* 

Tape 3 was *The Sandlot.*
