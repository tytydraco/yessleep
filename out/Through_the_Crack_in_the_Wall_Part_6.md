When I was 8 years old, I was full of boundless joy and wonder of the world. My brother was the complete opposite, and for the longest time I never understood why. He often spent his days in his room, and always carried a dark disposition with him that made me think of him as a grouch. One day on summer vacation, my parents left me under his care while they were at work, something they often did until I found friends to hang out with. I crept into his room hoping he didn’t hear the obnoxiously loud creak from the door behind me. Hopefully the whirring of his ceiling fan masked the noise.

“What do you want?” He grumbled, facing the wall away from me as he laid motionless on his bed.

“I wanna go outside!” I said, my surprise visit now sullied.

“Then go outside, I’m not stopping you.” He responded. He then proceeded to slide his phone from his pillow, before staring blankly at the home screen. 

“Mom said I can’t unless you’re out there to watch me.” I whined back at him. 

“Tough shit then.” He mumbled.

This response was unsatisfactory, and it spontaneously put me into a pout. “Why are you so mean! All you do is lay around.” I cried. Mom and dad always attributed his behavior to teenage angst, a word I still didn’t know the meaning of at the time. He sighed, laid in silence for a moment, then finally let out an exasperated, “Fine.”

My face instantly turned into a bright smile, and I ran downstairs and out the door before he even had the time to get ready. I immediately hopped on my bike and stood on the pavement patiently for him to finally emerge from the doorway. 

“You can only go around the block. I’ll be here when you’re ready to go inside.” He said. He then plopped a seat on the rickety wooden rocking chair we kept on the front porch.

“Okay!” I said excitedly. Our neighborhood wasn’t the best, which is why I was confined to riding my bike around our small block. I didn't listen of course, and immediately started exploring the nearby streets once I got out of view. I had barely made it three streets down when I confronted it. 

Emerging from an alleyway behind someone’s house, a mutt with foam drooling from its mouth snarling at me. My two options were flight or fight, and being a small child, I foolishly ditched my bike and chose flight. The dirty thing chased me all the way back home, barking at my heels. Ricky jumped into action as soon as he saw the sobbing mess with a rabid animal hot on its trail running up the driveway. With the aluminum baseball laying against the garage, he had managed to chase it off but not before it grabbed a hold of my leg and drew blood.

“Shit, are you okay John?”

I couldn’t answer him, I was too busy scream crying between rattled gasps for air.

“Alright, shhh. It’ll be fine, get in the car, I gotta take you to the hospital.”

The drive was long, and my cries drowned the music playing from the withered speakers in his beat up Honda. My cries only stifled when we pulled into the parking lot. “I’m not going to die am I?” I asked wiping snot off my upper lip. 

“What? No. I’m pretty sure that dog had rabies though, so you’re going to have to get a shot.” Ricky said bluntly. The notion of a needle coming anywhere near me threw me in an anxious fit, and tears immediately started welling in my eyes again. Not wanting to listen to another fit of crying, Ricky resorted to coercion.

“Tell you what, if you take it like a champ, I’ll take you to get the biggest ice cream sundae I can afford.”

This worked. “Really?” I asked, wiping my face off on the sleeves of my shirt.

“Yeah, I got a little money. No crying though.”

“Will it hurt?”

“You’ve been bitten by mosquitoes before. They’re basically living needles and do they hurt?” He used his pointer finger to imitate a mosquito with buzzing sounds, then poked me on my side in a playful manner.

I giggled at his gesture, he knew that I was ticklish. “No, but they get all itchy.”

“Well there’s your answer. It even has the added benefit of not getting itchy.” 

With that we went inside to get my Imovax shot. He lied. It did hurt, but I took it like a champ and he held up his end of the bargain. This is my happiest memory of my brother. It was a simple one, but I hold it close to my heart.

It was when I was 12 when he took his own life. I had just come home from an all-nighter with my friend playing Mortal Kombat Armageddon. I don’t even know why I went to his room that day, maybe the devil was drawing me in to show off the work he had done. I never saw the signs, and I curse myself every day for not doing anything to help with his depression, even if there was nothing I could do at such a young age. I curse my parents everyday for not seeing it. Even if they were busy working all the damned time, it only makes me curse them more because I was the one to find him. Two of dad’s belts and his ceiling fan forming the gallows that held his purple face and limp body. I curse Ricky every day, because his act of weakness is what destroyed my family and forced me to make the 911 call that a 12 year old boy should never have to make.

The dread of seeing Ricky like that will never leave me, a stain on my psyche that cannot be scrubbed away.

The events that unfolded in the factory that night will forever be the same kind of stain, one I can talk even less freely about.

Time seemed to warp, I sat staring up towards the stairwell for what felt like eternity. My only true marker of time was the thumping in my chest, and in terms of flight or fight, I chose freeze. A stretched out silence produced a ringing in my ears and I desperately tried to make myself move. Before long, I realized that I was moving, just not the way I wanted. My knees were buckling underneath me and the strength was fleeing my limbs. 

Crackling like the embers of fire, two large thuds, followed by two smaller ones, then the roar of a thousand crickets. Those sounds surged me with adrenaline, and before I had time to collect myself, I could hear it moving through the halls. Each step that thing made sent tremors throughout the factory. A lone locker just to the left of the stairs was my only salvation, and I scrambled inside as quietly as I could muster. When I got in, I recoiled at how much I could see through the grills on the door.

The stairs, Jane’s sedated body at their base, my backpack off in the middle of the room. I secretly prayed that this visibility was one way, otherwise I found myself a metal coffin rather than a hiding spot. The Gatekeeper was perusing the halls, each loud step a testament to its might. Loud crashing, frenzied movement, with those sounds I knew that it was checking each room in its pursuit of unwelcomed guests. It was only a small matter of time before it knew to check the open area, and before long, the noises grew louder and louder, until I was certain it was atop the stairs. 

Suddenly, silence. 

*Click, click, click, click.* The hollow noises were faint but recognizable. 

*Click. Click.* 

My heart beat so wildly it hurt, and I began another silent prayer for it not to alert my position. The loud crackling sound ushered in a new painful silence and I was left alone with my heart and mind racing to unimaginable lengths.

Silence, then more silence. Was it gone? Was it really that easy? Could I just slip out and carry Jane with me to the exit? 

Those questions were soon answered, and I quickly regretted asking myself them. The crackling sound came directly from my right, it was so close the sound was deafening. Thinking about it now, I would attribute it more closely to the sparks coming off of a taser next to my ear. 

Out of the corner of my eye I saw it. A behemoth sized forelimb, stretching out before landing its 3-clawed paw on the ground in front of me with a “*Thump!”* that shook the metal sarcophagus I enclosed myself in. It was coming out of the wall! 

God the thing was so horrible to look at, the etching of its presence simply didn’t do it justice. 

The Gatekeeper was massive, at least 10-15 feet tall even in its crawl. Its skin was a faded swirl of rainbow coloring, like it was made of water based paint that stretched tightly against its muscular physique. Sporadic bursts of the chirping of locusts would periodically echo in the room, and as it came into full view, I saw the sound was made by the long black bristles on its back vibrating at blinding speed. A black haze seemed to emanate from them too, but the longer I looked I realized it was seeping into them instead. Somehow, from this realization I knew, the very presence of this beast is what was pulling the color from the world. The stench of dandelions filled the room like perfume, and it slunk towards Jane with its head bobbing from its long neck in a weaving motion. 

First it circled her like a wolf, before closing in to nudge at her with its massive paw. Hot tears ran down my cheek as I watched it roll her onto her back with one swiping motion. It stretched its long neck over her and gnawed at the air above her face. *Click, click, click.* Its eyes undulated in a grotesque fashion, most of them trained on her. Then, it lifted her by the arm with an iron grip, and I heard her arm snap under the weight of its claws. That’s it, she’s dead and I’m next. The thing would eat or brutalize her and it wouldn’t stop its search until it found all intruders. These thoughts pounded against my skull in a mix of fear, guilt, shame, and utter despair.

To my horror though, her fate was much worse.

It sat down with her still in its grasp, as if she was just a doll for it to play with. The four arms tucked against its chest stretched out in a slithering motion, human like hands attached with fingers wriggling in a fevered frenzy. First they poked and prodded her, testing the waters before getting to work. Each time they made contact with her skin I saw her eyes widen and she choked out pained gasps each time it happened. Once satisfied, they viciously began to set themselves to work on her. 

They slowly began transforming her, molding her like a clay sculpture. They grabbed and pulled, twisted and contorted features, injected new colors and ripped others away. Her clothes melted into her skin as they too were becoming a part of the amalgamation of disfigurement. They didn’t stop until she was completely unrecognizable from a human. Mid-transformation, her belongings fell from her tattered body one by one. Her gun, her keys, her phone, and finally her wallet. I am deeply ashamed to admit that when I saw the gun, I grieved over the possibility of her not being comfortable enough to follow me to Paradise without protection. I’m such a horrid person, I know.

Once the Gatekeeper was satisfied it walked her disfigured body over to the wall ahead of it. Pinning her against the wall with one forelimb. It took the other and pressed with all of its weight against her. She squeezed like a balloon, letting out soft groans of gurgled pain until…

*Splat!*

She disappeared into a splatter of crimson that mirrored a crime scene chalk line on the wall. I was certain she was dead, but upon closer inspection I saw what the beast had done. She had become another animated painting on the wall, in a style very reminiscent of the works of Picasso. Trapped within the confines of her concrete canvas, I saw her in a perpetual loop of crying big, bright blue tears that turned into a puddle beneath her.

The thing cocked its head to the side, admiring its work. Bursts of excited chirping whirred from its back, signaling its satisfaction with its work. It turned to face the room before craning its neck back.

*Click. Click. Click.* It gnawed at the air, like it was trying to catch a taste of a scent. I held my breath as if it would help. Its head jerked towards the center of the room, twisting its elongated neck in the process. The Gatekeeper shambled over to my bag, and gnawed at the air around it before nudging it with one of its paws. 

This was my chance! I shot out of the locker like a missile and bolted for the stairs, stooping to grab the gun in the process. I turned only to see that its attention was entirely on me now, its head tilted to the side like a confused dog. I wasted no time in my ascent, the crackling of sparks behind me only pushing me harder. I ran up, took a right, then another, and found myself at the intersection. 

An intersection. The wrong one. The hallway I was staring down was the Gatekeeper’s cage.

Whether through fear or panic clouding my mind, I had foolishly made the mistake of tracing my path to get into Paradise, not escape it. Dammit! God fucking dammit! All I had to do was make a left up the stairs and freedom would be right in front of me, another stupid mistake!

To my left I saw that the timer was a blinking red “0:00” and the smiley face was now caught in a looping scream. A crackling sound, far too close behind me alerted me that I was caught, and I turned to see it emerging head first from a charcoal etched drawing of its form on the wall. 

“STAY AWAY!” I screamed, drawing the handgun on it. It didn’t pay any mind to the weapon in my hands. Instead, it crouched over to me, all of its eyes drawn on me with predatory intent.

“I said stay away!” I screamed again, this time with less intensity and more sepair. When it was about 10 feet away from me, I pointed directly at its head with the gun cocked. I started squeezing the trigger, and in unison its jaw unhinged. I expected a screech or a roar, something horrible and in alignment to a monster of such tremendous caliber.

Instead what came out was more akin to a dog whistle, and as the sound pierced my ears, it completely paralyzed me. My finger was just a few millimeters from firing the gun and I was stuck looking at its grotesque face within firing range. Its eyes began to wildly pulse with colors in a hypnotic fashion, before visions flooded me in flashes of glimpses of my fate. The Gatekeeper wanted to show me my sentencing before my conviction.

My body twisted in agony, every nerve screaming in wretched pain. My vision limited by a razor thin edge of blinding light. My organs squashed unbearably tight against my body as I lose all forms of depth, forcibly turned from a 3-dimensional being to a 2-dimensional nightmare. A fate of crying out in agony with no sounds to carry out my pleas of help. Eternal suffering, with no form of release other than the eventual deterioration into madness. Is this what I condemned Jane to? Was this the fate that befell Randy and his brother Manny? Oh god, what have I done?

Between visions, I could see the Gatekeeper slink towards me wearily. It never let up the visions or its paralyzing scream when it crawled over to me, even when it lifted me up by the torso, the gun now inches from its face. I felt a few of my ribs crack in its grasp, and the pain tensed my body ever so slightly. Its hands stretched out towards me, ready to deliver me my penance. They felt like cold acid when they hit my skin. 

*BANG!*

By some miracle, the pain tensed my body further squeezing the trigger and the bullet ripped through one of its eyes. Black ink drooled from its face and it dropped me flat on the ground. I groaned when white hot knives stabbed at my abdomen. I scrambled to my feet as fast as I could, before drawing the pistol on it and unloading most of the clip in its head. A few more of its eyes ruptured from the bullets. It wiped the black ink smearing its face, and I half- ran, half- hobbled away from it. I could hear it quickly following my pace, gaining distance on me in my struggling lap of the building. I cried out in both pain and despair, “ Oh god, oh god please. Please just let me go, let me go I’m sorry!” I soon could tell that it was right behind me towards the end of my pitiful escape.

I could see it though! The Gate to Paradise right in front of me. As I dove through, I felt the flesh on my back get ripped away as it swiped its claw against me. It didn’t matter, I had made it freedom. I hit the ground with a thud, feeling fluid slowly leak into my lungs from my broken ribs. I let out as loud of a groan as possible, and I limped all the way to the stairs before collapsing on the hard stone and passing out. 

Physically, I’m fine. I somehow was able to get myself to the hospital the following morning, even with all the blood I lost and fluid build up in my lungs. I didn’t really know what to tell the emergency room doctors. I just said I was attacked by a bear, despite us having none in our state, let alone area. They believed me though. I guess that’s the only thing they could point to for the extent of my injuries. 

Mentally, I’m not okay. I ended up putting myself into rehab, anything to keep me away from that place, that thing. At first I tried cold turkey, but that was so horrible I nearly gave up. I got myself a psychiatrist, with what little money I had left. She helps with the things I can tell her about, but everything else still lingers. She even helped me get a prescription to help me quit my addiction, I didn’t know that was a thing. It doesn’t help with the headaches though, or the voices. I no longer work at that burger joint, too many painful reminders there. That, and the missing posters I see of Jane that her mom put up. Sadly enough, Randy’s father never seemed to even file a report.

I could say I’m doing better than when I first started this story. I got a new job at a DSP for Amazon, and even if the pay isn’t the greatest, it’s still better than what I was making. It’s good for me, driving alone helps clear my head and gets me away from the house. I try sending whatever money I have left after paying for my essentials over to Jane’s mother. A small form of payment for the mistakes I’ve made, I don’t keep tabs on her but I hope it helps with her chemotherapy.

Paradise still tries to hold its grip on me. Every now and then, Ricky’s choked out voice will slip through the cracks of the bathroom door, pleading with me to go back. I use nearby truck stops now to take a shower or relieve myself, and every now and then when I’m on route, I find myself going off course towards that dirt trail. 

As a kid, I could never comprehend the darkness that lived in my brother’s head. That thing that ate up all his joy and left only sadness and apathy. Ricky killed himself, and as much as it hurts that I couldn’t stop it, I can’t keep blaming myself for it. What happened to those 3 people I abandoned IS my fault though. They were promised heaven, but were thrusted into hell.

I still have Jane’s gun, and a mountain of guilt. Nowadays, I understand the monster Ricky had to live with just a little more. Although sharing this story helps get it off my chest, it still hurts. But I’ll keep trying to live to the best of my ability, even if I don’t deserve to. If I can’t handle it, I still have a few bullets left.

So now I’ll just leave you with this warning. If you’re struggling with substance abuse, get help. No matter how, just do it. If you ever find yourself high somewhere and see a crack in the wall with graffiti inviting you in, whether it's in a tunnel, an old building, or even your home, ignore it. 

Paradise is real, but it’s not to be trusted, and you’re not to be trusted with the risks.

Each scar on my back is a testament to those three facts.
