I had the dream again.

It always starts with the red. Splattered all across the room, on the curtains, all over her bed. And then I see it. As I stand outside my sister's room, I see the slender frame, the leather wings sprouting out it's back, the blood dripping down it's chin and onto my sisters corpse. It looks at me, eyes yellow and glowing, and it laughs.

I woke up with a dry mouth and a drier throat. Another bed I didn't feel right in, another nameless motel I'd forget I ever slept in by the time I was back on the road. It was barely four in the morning. "God damnit," I muttered. I reached for my phone and looked at the Google Maps app I left open. Just another five hours in the morning, and I'd be back home. For the first time since that night.

When slumber returned to me, I dreamed the rest of that night. That thing put a finger to its lips, gave a quiet s*hhhh*, and then it was gone. My heart hammered in my chest. As I took careful steps to Elena's bed, I saw the horror again. Her throat was ripped open, her eyes wide with fear and having lost all life. For some reason, I couldn't bring myself to scream. I simply stood there, mouth agape, eyes locked on hers. I stood there for five minutes before my mother screamed behind me.

When I woke again at nine, I punched my pillow. Again and again. I never wanted to see that night again. Yet, it intruded every corner of my mind. Invasive as the thing with wings, the thing I told the police was there that night. No amount of therapy could convince me it wasn't.

The time it should have taken to get from Madelyn, Texas, to Lancaster, Pennsylvania, was twenty-four hours, almost exactly. But, fate had other plans. There was a snow storm sweeping through the Northeast, and it was fucking up my trip severely. I'd been on the road for nearly three days, taking detour after detour, until finally, I was told I could go no further.

In the middle of West Virginia, there was a small town named Bellview (that was affectionately called *Hellview*, according to the graffiti on the welcome sign) where I was pulled over by a local officer. "Afternoon, sir," He said with an easy-to-spot Boston accent, then widened his eyes when his met mine. That always happened. "You aware yous got different colored eyes?"

I gave a curious smile, acting as though I hadn't had this conversation for decades. "Since childhood, officer." He tilted me a nod and then cleared his throat, holding his hat against the growing winds.

"You're aware of the storm, yes?"

"Yes, sir."

"Well, we've had to close all roads for the night. We don't know for how long, but this bitch of a storm is too risky to let anyone drive in." I sighed in response.

"I have to get home officer, it's only another hour. My mother, she's—"

"I am sorry for the inconvenience sir, but this is well above me. I'd let you if I could."

"I'm sure," I breathed out, leaning my head against the headrest. There was a flurry of snowflakes whipping against my windshield, and I was sure it would get worse as the sun sank. The officer cleared his throat and nodded again.

"Well, about two miles up the road here, there's a rest stop. You can hold up there for the night, see if we have better luck tomorrow morning."

"They have beds there?"

"Emergency cots," He gave a humorless smile as he said that. "I already sent a few cars down that way, so you'll have company."

"Lovely," I rolled my eyes, tapping my fingers along the steering wheel. "Alright, well, if that's that—"

"Sure is, sir. Be safe now, friend."

I bid him farewell and began the slow crawl towards the rest stop. As predicted, the lower the sun got, the worse the snow came down. By the time I had reached the parking lot, I could barely see and my car could barely move. When I opened the door, I had to struggle to open it wide enough to step into the deepening snow. A shiver went down my spine as I bundled into myself, trudging through the snow and to the door of the rest stop.

"Well, another traveler!" A voice called as soon as I stepped through. I looked up and saw the scene; a fair skinned man in a suit was the one who greeted me, his hair straight and cut just before his shoulders. Sitting at the table beside him, a young couple. She, with red frazzled hair and pale skin, and he with a buzz cut and unkempt beard. An old man paid me no mind as he stood by the window on the far end of the building, sipping on a metal flask. I couldn't wait to ask him for a sip myself.

"Seems you'll be the last one here, that storm gets any worse." The suit said with a slick smile, walking over to me and extending a hand. "Frank Murphy," He introduced himself, his smile still on as he looked at my eyes. I knew what was coming. "Are your eyes two different colors?"

Something about the way he asked gave me pause. He asked it not like a question of curiosity, but a question of reassurance. I paid it no mind and shook his hand. "That they are," I replied before introducing myself. "Ash." I looked around the building as I took my hand back. "You seem to be the only friendly one here." I remarked.

"Well," He began with a chuckle, "seems necessary to have at least one optimistic person here! I understand why everyone is a little bummed. I'm sure it's not where you wanted to end your day."

"Not in the slightest," I sighed and found a coffee machine sitting on a small bar on the far wall to my left. I started towards it, Frank following behind.

"Where were you headed, friend?"

"Lancaster."

"Pennsylvania? What's got you heading to Amish town?"

I laughed a little at the remark as I grabbed a paper cup. "My mother. She's dying." I could hear the frown on his face when he spoke again.

"I'm sorry to hear." I shrugged.

"And you?"

"Right here, actually." He leaned against the bar and crossed his arms. "About twenty minutes from getting home before that cop had me come down here."

Steam rose from my cup as I brought it to my lips, already defrosting me from the inside out. I took a sip and felt my shoulders stoop a little. "What's with the graffiti on the welcome sign? Hellview?"

"Murders," The old man suddenly interjected, his voice gruff. We both looked over to him, still staring out that window, flask in hand. "When I was a teenager. Largest string of murders this side of America."

"I was going to explain it nicer than that, but yes." Frank gave a nod to the old man. "Thank you, John."

"They never found him, you know?" John disregarded Frank's words, finally turning his head to look at the both of us. "Bastard kills fourteen people and just disappears like smoke. Fuckin' unbelievable."

"You two know each other?" I asked with a small chuckle, taking another sip of my coffee.

Frank nodded. "Been my neighbor the past six years. Lots of fun, I can tell you."

"And those two?" I motioned to the young couple sitting at the table, to which Frank shrugged.

"No clue. Much like you, I imagine. Wrong place, wrong time." He pushed off the bar and turned towards me. "There's one more here. Hannah, I think her name is. Went to the back for a smoke."

"God, I could use a smoke," I said with more desperation than I meant. Frank clapped my shoulder with a smile, then pointed down a hallway in the center of the wall opposite us.

"Right down there, my friend. I'm sure she has one to spare." I turned to where he pointed, regarded him with a nod, and began to walk. I took in my surroundings; John had gone back to staring out of the window, the young couple still had yet to acknowledge my existence, and looking out the window...

I counted again. Me, John, Frank, this Hannah girl and the young couple. Six of us accounted for, There were six cars outside. It was safe to assume the couple came in one car, so; who was missing? I kept this in the back of my mind as I walked down the hallway. It was short, and led to two doorways on either side. One was marked for employees only, the other was marked *Family Restroom*. I did try the employee door only to find it locked. When I opened the door to the restroom, I found a pretty young woman with pink hair (similar in length to mine) and covered in tattoos, blowing smoke out of the side of her mouth. "Sorry," I said with a polite smile. "I heard there were was someone with some smokes here."

"Found her," She gave me the same polite smile, cigarette hanging between her index and middle fingers. The smoldering ash fell into the sink beside her as she reached with her free hand into her small clutch purse, pulling out a beaten pack of Camel Crush's. "Hope you don't mind Camels."

"Haven't smoked 'em since high school," I remarked as I took an extended stick in between my fingers, sticking it in my lips and letting her light it for me. "Thanks."

"Sharing is caring," She tossed her burnt out butt into the sink and offered a hand. "Hannah."

"Ash."

Her head cocked slightly as we shook hands. "Your eyes—"

"If you don't mind," I interrupted, "I've had enough talk about my eyes today. For the rest of my life, actually." She let out a small laugh and nodded.

"Understood." She leaned against the sink and looked over the rest of me. I followed her eyes, trailing up and down my arms. Seemed she was trying to figure out who had more ink than who. "How long that sleeve take ya?"

I sucked in a lung full of smoke, blowing it out the side of my mouth before answering. "Two years."

She let out a bellow of a laugh. "Jesus, either you're rich or-"

"Artist." I finished for her with a small smirk. "Best part of the job is getting as many as I can without paying a dime."

"Well, when this shit blows over, maybe I'll get something from you. To commemorate." I let out a small laugh of my own and nodded.

"Where were you headed before you ended up here?"

"Maine. Castle Rock. You?"

"Lancaster."

"Amish town?"

"Says the girl from Maine."

A small snort escaped her and she pushed off the sink. "I'm going to head back out there, get some coffee."

"Before you do," I stopped her, stepping a tiny bit closer. "How many people are here?"

She thought for a moment. "With you? Six, now."

"There's an extra car outside." I stated plainly. One of her eyebrows raised slightly and she clicked her tongue.

"There were four cars when I got here," She said quietly with a shrug.

"But two of the people here are a couple, right?" I asked before taking another drag. "They probably came in one car."

She stared at me for a moment. "And?"

"So, we're missing someone, no? If they came in one car, there's an extra car out there with no owner."

"Could be broken down. Left here by somebody before the storm hit."

I shook my head. "Just weird, is all."

"You're gonna be fun here, aren't you?" She gave me a small, mischievous smile before walking past me and out of the room.

That extra car stayed with me as I finished my smoke. I couldn't place it, but something felt wrong about it. It was out of place, it was somewhere it shouldn't be and that was bugging the shit out of me. I sank the butt of my cigarette and walked back out into the hallway.

I pulled out my phone only to find there was no signal to be found. It was going to be a long, boring night.

When I arrived back to the main space, the sun had completely vanished and there was nothing but snow crashing into the glass of the windows. Everyone except John had convened to the table the couple sat at, a deck of cards sat in the middle. "Ash!" Frank called, cheery and jolly as Santa Clause. "Come, we're playing poker."

Hannah's eyes met mine, and I felt myself pulled towards the table. I took an empty seat next to the nameless young man and, finally, he recognized that I was another human being in this building, and gave me a tip of his baseball cap. "'Sup?"

"Hey."

"You're Ash?"

"I'm popular around here."

He gave a small smile. "First person here in a couple hours, so yeah. I'm Oliver, call me Ollie. This is Diane," He gestured towards the young redhead across from him, She barely regarded me with a quick flash of the eyes, then returned to shuffling cards.

"Where were you guys headed?" I asked, and he took a strangely long moment to answer.

"Nowhere in particular. Just travelling." It was a very unconvincing answer. But, I took it for now and put my head down as Diane began to pass out cards.

There was only small, polite conversation throughout the game of poker (a game that Frank was amazingly good at) and it felt as though we were all prisoners here. And, it felt like we all had something to hide. Something was in the air that told me everyone in this building was running from something. Or to something. I couldn't quite figure out which.

Eventually, when topics of conversation became sparse, I let that festering thought in my mind emerge. "Who got here first?"

All eyes on me. A moment of silence, then Ollie spoke up. "Me and Di got here just after Frank, I think. He was here when we got here." I leaned over the table and looked down at Frank.

"Were you here first?"

"That I was," He answered with a smile.

"Were there any cars here when you got here?"

He gave me a quizzical look, then shrugged. "I suppose there was. I didn't think much of it."

"Why do you ask?" Ollie chimed in.

"Did you and Diane come in one car?"

"Yeah?"

"Then who's car is out there?" I pointed to the window in front of us, and everyone looked. It was hard to see, as almost all cars except mine were buried in snow, but there were six cars in that lot alright. And it seemed to finally dawn on Diane as she turned back around.

"Maybe someone left it here? Broken down?"

"That's what I said," Hannah added, giving her eyebrows a small *see?* gesture. I shook my head.

"Well, there was no one here when I got here," Frank said with a light chuckle. "Unless you're suggesting someone has disappeared."

"Just doesn't feel right," I mumbled, looking back down at my cards. I heard a sigh and footsteps begin to walk towards us.

"It's not right," John called, causing all of us to look back at him. He was clearly beginning to show signs of drunkenness, with a little spittle on his chin. "Something is fucked up around here."

"John, please," Frank tried to plea, but John waved his hands in dismissal.

"No no. What day is today?" We all stared at him, a little dumbfounded at the question, until Hannah finally answered:

"It's Sunday."

John got a small smile and pointed at her. "Correct. You notice the open hours on that door?" Before we could look and see for ourselves, he answered himself. "CLOSED ON SUNDAY!" He nearly yelled with a hoarse laugh.

"What's your point, John?" Frank asked, an annoyance in his voice that told me he's lived through this multiple times.

"Why lock down the employee room if the place was gonna be closed?"

Frank laughed. "But the front door was unlocked, John—"

"By the cops!" John quickly interjected. "They had to open it because of the storm, for us! So, I ask again, if the whole place was locked up, what point would there be in locking the employee room?"

I decided to play Devil's advocate for a moment. "Extra security? In case someone broke in for whatever reason?"

Again, John laughed. "I don't blame you for not knowing, son, but I worked here when I was about your age. Not once did we ever feel the need to lock that damn door. Not once did anyone ever break in here. I mean, what would they steal? The coffee machine?"

"So," Hannah stood, still looking at John. "You think someone's hiding something in there?"

"I know so," There was a little smirk on his face. "Wanna know why?"

"Spit it out, John," Frank snapped.

"Someone always, *always*, worked here on Sundays. Security. They'd be here till nine o'clock. What time is it?"

"Seven," Frank said with disbelieving sigh.

"So...where are they?" John asked, and the question hung over all of us for a moment. I finally stood and looked over at John.

"We have to get in that room."

"It's locked," Frank reminded us, also standing. "We couldn't—"

"Who locked it?" Diane spoke up. "If none of us have a key, and if security isn't here, who locked the door?"

"Curiouser and curiouser," Hannah said with a small laugh. I felt a pit in my stomach. This was more wrong than I thought.

"Is there any way to get in there?" I asked John, who nodded.

"Know how to pick a lock?"

"I do," Ollie answered before I could.

"Easiest lock to pick I've ever seen," John added. Ollie, Hannah, Diane and I all started towards the hall.

"Are we serious?" Frank asked in a slightly nervous laugh.

"You got something to hide, Frank?" John asked, suddenly deadly serious. Frank frowned and sat back down.

Ollie was already knelt, working away at that lock. John was right, it was not a hard lock to pick. The door was open in a matter of a couple of minutes. When it opened, the stench of death leaked out.

Ollie pushed inside, and I was right behind him. It was a small break room, with a single table, chair and a small microwave sitting upon a mini fridge in the corner. And, on the floor, was a corpse. My eyes widened as Diane screamed.

Blood everywhere. A man's eyes wide open, face frozen in fear. His throat was ripped open. *God*,  I thought, *this can't be happening.*

"My Lord," John gasped behind me. "He's back."

"What?" I whipped my head around to look at him, the surprise in my voice more apparent than I wished it to be.

"Those murders? They all looked like this. The bastard is back."
