It would follow my hand a little way, enough to lift the front of it off Luke. But there was a strip at the bottom that went under the skin, and when I moved my hand past that point Luke made a sound I'd never heard out of him. I looked up at Sarah. I could pull him loose or let it settle and try to take him with it still on him. Luke squeezed her arm and put his face down on the sill.

I told Sarah to pull. I wrapped the end of the shirt around my elbow and dropped my weight backward as she dragged him out. I felt him come apart from it before he made any noise. A piece of him came with it, flat and pale on one side. The adult took its hand off my wrist and snapped it up around the young. For a second it couldn't get low enough to catch the one that had fallen too. It made the same movement with its fingers that I'd seen it make with the basket. I got up on the bed on my knees and left it both of them.

Getting out was uglier than I'd imagined. I pulled the cedar stop out when my bad hand caught against it. The sash dropped across my shoulder and I crawled through a space barely high enough for one side of my ribs at a time. Sarah had her knee planted against Luke's hip to keep him from sliding down the bank and both her hands against his back. When I could see inside again, the adult was bent over the deer. I watched it lower the young it had just taken off Luke onto the deer's ribs. The deer had kept quiet until then. It brought its head up and bawled, so abruptly that I lost my grip and came down on my brother's legs.

We dragged him as far as a cedar on the bank. Sarah kept the dressing against his back while I had my good arm around him below the hips. There was room for him by the roots where he didn't have to keep his feet under himself. He asked me where Mike was. I started to tell him what had happened again and he said he knew. He meant where we'd left him. I told him by the truck. He shut his eyes and put his head down on Sarah's shoulder. She let him stay like that until I could hear something coming up through the leaves.

It was Jess. She'd got Walter past the culvert, and from there he'd been able to walk the last stretch to the gate while she came back for us. She'd watched him reach the spot where we'd all got out for the picture. Before they'd split up she'd given him Sarah's phone and her hat, in case he needed to flag someone down. I remember that she'd got a scrape on her scalp where the brim would have been. She crouched down and ran the back of her hand over Sarah's upper arm, once each way. Then she got her shoulder under Luke and said she had him.

We reached the road above the cut. The other side was soft enough to take prints, and for a long time after we stopped I kept looking down at that shoulder. I didn't hear anything below us except water going through the culvert. When a deputy finally came through the gate on foot with Walter behind him, Jess walked toward the lights with her hands up and told him where the ambulance needed to stop. I lay down in the gravel next to Luke where I could look at his face. The last thing he said to me before they took him was that he'd been looking forward to seeing what Nolan thought of the lake.

They brought Mike out the next afternoon. I gave his watch to his wife in the hospital parking lot. When I took it out of my pocket there was blood under the glass where it had cracked. She put it on right there. The band was big enough to turn around twice on her wrist. I still see it sliding over that little bump of bone while she was asking me if he knew I'd come outside to help him.

The wrist it broke will turn about halfway now. I can change a light bulb left-handed, and I'm learning to shave that way. My brother spent most of that winter learning to walk up stairs again. He'd lost muscle along one side of his back and couldn't stand very long without having to sit. Walter drove him to appointments when I was working. The deputy showed me a picture of the box under the bed in Six. Even the gravel was gone. The county blocked the access road, and Luke put the folder with all his numbers in a grocery bag with the stuff they'd emptied out of his pockets at the hospital. I found it when I helped him move. The sketch of the light for Nolan was still in it. I asked if he wanted me to throw it away, and he took it out of the bag and stuck it to his fridge with a magnet.

It was there in his kitchen that I finally asked Luke about the first time he'd said my name in the craft building. He'd sounded like I'd just walked over to see what he was doing. He asked if I'd seen the adult's hand under his ribs. I said I thought it was propping him up. Luke put his fingers just below his ribs on his good side so I could see the place. There had been two of the young on the bench when it carried him in. One stretched itself toward him. He watched the adult lift it off the bench and put it in that basket, right beside the door.

It gave him air when he called for me and held him shut when he tried to shout for us to stay away. By the time I came in, he'd found that he got more air if he kept his voice low. It gave him the longest breath after I answered him from the paint room. He said that was what nearly broke him. All he'd wanted in the world was for me to hear him, and the moment I did, he felt the animal get down off the bench and make room next to him.
