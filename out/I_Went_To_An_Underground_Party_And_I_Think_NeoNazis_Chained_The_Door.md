I don’t know what to do.

Please help.

I’m hiding in a dirty toilet stall at an underground party and I think if I leave I’ll be killed.

There are no windows in this cramped room, the walls are concrete and what I can hear through the vent isn’t music. My friends are scattered around the stage and just about anywhere that isn’t closed off.

I can’t call anyone with my phone in this state and even if I could my friends wouldn’t hear a damn thing over the music. In the state we’re all in I’m not sure they’d even be able to read the endless barrage of messages none of them have seen.

It’s hard to escape something that’s trying to kill you when you’re already in its stomach. 

Okay, how do I explain this without sounding insane? 

I guess I can’t. 

Let me at least give you a disclaimer for everyone thinking I’m just having a bad trip— yes, I am high. Nothing fancy, the cheap as piss cocaine cut with anything you can think of that clings to the back of your throat like globs of spit. A few pingers for the energy, half my weight in alcohol and enough caffeine to keep my heart thumping so  hard I’m one breath away from a heart attack. 

You know, the usual.

Spending your mid teens and twenties in a drug fueled haze has a way of making you this unreliable junkie that cried monster. 

After a few bad trips left me running from my unfortunately very untalented bands “ Tour” van like a pig with his ass on fire or deciding the safest place to sleep would be a blow up mattress floating in the middle of a pool makes stories of sinister creatures prowling in the shadows or a million eyes following you… A bit farfetched.

Especially when it happens on one of the many nights my mates. I’ve spent a thousand nights blind drunk and hopped up on stimulants under seizure inducing strobe lights and the deafening beat punching so hard it threatens to collapse the half finished construction, warehouse or tonight— slaughterhouse down on the glittering blur of blown pupils swaying under half lidded eyes and thrashing and flailing mess of limbs dancing around the zombies like bodies possessed by the ear bleeding screech of live music.

That’s sort of what you did at the impromptu parties and alternative concerts filled with hundreds of people all claiming the niche “ You gotta know a guy.”  music scene was punk. Of course it was punk but you tend to feel like a bit of a pretentious dickhead when no one can agree on what real punk is and who is worthy of the title, that and the fact that everyone seems to “ Know a guy”.

I’m a skinhead, at least I was before it went from alternative to racist. 

Those psychos really had a way of ruining shaved heads and suspenders.

I already sound like a dickhead.

Honestly, my friends and I all had our own reasons for getting trashed at these illegal parties beyond the obvious cool factor of rebelling against the norm. 

I’d like to say the musician in me went for the music but it was mostly drugs, I liked being the life of the party and I didn’t have the talent to achieve that with a guitar— liquid confidence and uppers certainly gave me the energy needed to give into my poor impulse control and stupid bravery though.

Out of my friend group I was the scrappy one, the one who’d broken more bones in my body and always seemed to lack that little squishy bit in your brain that tells you to stop punching something after you’ve broken your hand. Stereotypical traveler, right? Shelly would say I was the crew’s jailbird but I haven’t stolen anything in at least a few months. 

Shelly is my best friend, a guitarist with real talent— unfortunately he’s also dumb as dog shit. We always figured that was because of a fight a couple years ago where he’d been bricked.

“ Fink.” Finkle is a greasy tub of butter cursed with the perpetual look of a scared rabbit always looking out for a fox, clumsy and wearing thick glasses like he’d been born to be a high school punching bag. 

I can’t say we never pushed him into a few humiliating stunts or told a few jokes at his expense but we’d always looked out for him in high school and despite the pungent stink of loser he radiated, he’s damn strong. He can carry a lanky thing like a drunken Shelly home with the mental fortitude to do it even with Shelly’s lunch dripping down his shoulder.

Next is French. I don’t really know the origins of the nickname but he wasn’t French, mixed-irish kid just didn’t have the same ring to it. He’s my cousin. He’s also a prick. He’s that friend you don’t entirely get along with but they had their own TV— something very valuable in a friend when we were all kids. 

French was one of those golden boy types, deceptive considering he’s just as much of a fuckup as the rest of us.

Finally and most importantly there’s Jupiter Mable Rose— clearly a child born to a pair of hippies with a first name like that— We call her Mable, she had a real mean right hook so calling her Jupiter wasn’t an option if you liked your face. Dark skin, prettier than anything and mean. I’d never loved a girl like I love Mable but my underdeveloped brain and general stupidity made doing anything but bickering with her a tall order. 

That and Mable always came with a young hippie tag along, her sister Moonbeam— Again, Mable could punch your teeth in so Moonbeam Lily was dubbed “Minnie”.

I don’t want tonight to be the last time I see them.

The party kicked off as they always do, we drank ourselves stupid in preparation for going to the impromptu concert and drinking ourselves flat into a night passed out and drooling on a cement floor with nothing but splashed drinks, wrappers and smashed glass as bedding.

Shelly’s mate Donnie heard about it from a friend who’d be playing but a few of us were apprehensive.

The party really was in the middle of nowhere.

A big industrial warehouse out in fuck nowhere nearly an hour out of town. A slaughterhouse that went out of business for one reason or another years earlier, now left to rot in the middle of wide stretches of abandoned farmland as far as the eye can see— toxic waste, the lingering miasma of death clouding the air or something about bad ground killing the livestock. 

Either way, it was exactly the type of location you’d expect a large crowd wanting to listen to deafening music and drink themselves stupid without a thousand noise complaints would congregate.

Cars parked along the unused roads and any surface flat enough or strong enough to keep the wheels from sinking deep into the mud. 

The “tour” van quickly found itself arse deep in it.

The sound of music radiated from the slaughterhouse with an electric thrum that vibrated through the dirt like a heartbeat, creating ripples in stagnant puddles of rainwater and the wet boot prints in the mud.

It stuck on the rusted entrance like a bottleneck of gates leading livestock into pig pens.

The cold air smelling of grass and exhaust gave way to  humid air thick with sweat. A haze of smoke hung like fog and we were struck with the uncomfortable feeling of our bones baking under bright lights, all attached to webs of cords and generators clogged with plugs. 

The gritty concrete felt cold to the touch, the metal warm and beneath the pungent stink of tobacco, alcohol and piss there was the lingering taste of something rancid like old blood. 

The music came from the depths with none of the beckoning calls of excitement heard from across the countryside but a twisted sound that rushed from the halls in the disorienting, distorted squeals of a million pigs, wailing and waiting for slaughter.

Concrete stairs wet with mud and stains make Fink slip, grabbing and trying to rip my arm off in the process— I don’t really know if my phone getting thrown into the mud or losing my arm would’ve been better, “ Told you not to wear sneakers, Finkle.” French says and from the moment they reach the packed main floor he’s off to whoever he’s decided is infinitely more interesting than his own band. 

“ Disloyal bastard,” Shelly mumbles, patting my shoulder and cocking his head toward the rusty old conveyor belts serving as a bar off in the next area, “ I’ll grab us some lagers, vodka reds for Mable and… Water for Fink”.

I spent a long time trying to clean the mess out of the speakers and ignoring Fink’s frantic apologies. “ Christ, look at those.” Mable said, half disturbed and half mesmerised by the meat hooks hung across the metal beams above. Most had been moved as a macabre decoration but the grit still caked on each sharp point was real, once used to drain cut pigs and hold their bodies up as just one in a hundred slabs of meat swaying in a frozen room. 

Between the chains was a maze of iron pipes and metal sprinklers fanned out like shower heads ready to wash away pools of blood on what was now left smashed tiles. Years of fluid seeping into each line and grate had left these dark stains across the exposed concrete. 

The stains, the rumbling music and the odd feeling of the floors vibrating only tripled as we reached the heart of the slaughterhouse.

A man thick as a linebacker and built like a brick bumped into my shoulder so hard I nearly added a new layer of blood to the hard ground but my phone took the brunt of it— straight into the damn mud, “ Oi! Watch it—“ I shut myself up quick as anything at the figure, it wasn’t the linebacker but another, gaunt with the type of lines a face only gets from scowling and jagged gashes that carved up what’s left with scars. It’s hard to say it’s a face at all, it didn’t look like a face, it looked like a blind sculptors approximation of what a human face wrapped up in ribbons would be.

“ Sorry, mate. My friend don’t have manners.” The man extended a burn palm stained with old ink and pulled me to my feet, keeping me in a bone cracking shake he didn’t break until he’d given me a once over with veiny orbs I wouldn’t call eyes. They’re as much eyes as his face is a face. “ No harm. I’m—“

“ O'Doherty.” He said with an unfitting grin and a slap on my shoulder, “ Enjoy the show, kiddo.”

The linebacker and O’Doherty left for what we assume was a smoke break in the unclean country air and the music returned to my ears, unsettled by its jarring return. It had never left but when that man spoke it felt like the noise, the squeals, the people, Mable’s grip on my arm had all run away to escape his presence alone. 

“ Fuck, Shell said Donnie’s wasn’t wrapped up with guys like that!”. 

“ What was so strange about him?” Minnie shouted over the noise but anything I said was swallowed by its power. Minnie might be too naive to know but Mable and I have been wearing yellow laces since we were kids playing shitty music in French’s basement. 

Ladders on boots have always been a way of knowing the dangers in a crowd where bodies have formed a mass of limbs with a thousand heads. You know which laces could string you up on a hook. If you see red then you’re already seeing someone who has probably done it and if you’re seeing white— well, you know who doesn’t accept any other colour.

“ White laces.”

“ Yeah, but what the fuck does magenta mean?”

O’Doherty had them. Ladder laced for a cause, based on the clear affiliation with Neo-Nazi’s inked on his knuckles he’s just as bad as the Linebacker but those damn boots. Even in the haze of smoke I could see they weren’t white, they weren’t red, they were something else.

Magenta.

This is when that unsettling feeling twisting in my gut began bubbling up like bile in my throat.

Shelly put drinks in our hands and we forced our way through the pit of a thousand heads, getting caught up in the music. 

The drums thumped, the amps made the air vibrate with a thick tension and the thrashing bodies made the walls shake and the meat hooks sway. 

But I couldn’t focus. 

I was several drinks deep at this point, Shelly, Mable and I had snuck into a back corner for a little something extra to take the uncomfortable edge off and by this point I’d normally be so buzzed and lost in the music that the only thing keeping me from spiralling off into space was the blindingly bright lights of the stage pulling me in like a moth ready to bash its brains in against them.

That’s the feeling a place like this gives, a rush of adrenaline and excitement. Except I couldn’t shake that one thought— Magenta.

“ I’m gonna grab more drinks!”

“ What?”

“ Drinks!” I shouted so loud my throat is still hoarse now and weaved my way back through the pit.

No red, no white, no magenta.

Nothing until I reached the crossroads between the countryside above, the bar to the right and the closed off area to the left. O’Doherty was there talking to another man by the open doors seemingly guarded by the second man with magenta laces. They’re the first signs of any of those bastards being at the slaughterhouse since I’d first bumped into the Linebacker.

Have you ever had that strange feeling where you hear a  word and it suddenly seems to be everywhere? Frequency illusion. It’s just one of those things where your brain is so focused on that one word that you think the world just learned it too. That they can’t shut up about it.

That’s magenta.

Except it wasn’t an illusion, it really was just appearing— it’s just that none of them were in the pit. 

They talked and hung around by doorways and stairs like guard dogs waiting to attack anyone that tried to leave, caging us in like they planned on locking those precious few ways to freedom.

I don’t know who they are or why this party but I know those skinheads are Neo-Nazi’s and magenta is nothing good.

That sickening feeling finally had a name to it when the bile shot up my throat. I didn’t want to run toward O’Doherty but the safety of the bathrooms were right beside that big metal door echoing with that unexplainable wailing like the hallway itself was a throat, staying open even as the others were being closed.

I threw my guts up to the sounds of rattling chains I knew weren’t those meat hooks and now I’m sitting in a bathroom stall trying to work out if I’m losing my mind or if my friends and I are truly in danger. 

If I’m not losing my mind, why can I hear the pigs squealing through the vents?
