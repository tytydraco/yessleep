“Turn my petals into newspapers, you will weep at what you read.” 

I was born when she was fifty, a miracle birth some might say.   
All the old pictures of her hung around the shitty little house didn’t portray she was happy before me. 

The only difference from the photos and now, is she looks like she ate the woman from them. 

The household I had lived in wasn’t your typical pre 2008 crash nuclear family with a picket fence and sprinkler in the garden, our fence was chain link with barbed wire. 

Walls weren’t fine cut wood blessed by the father of modern suburbia, they were puke yellow chipped paint walls with mold crawling up it, moss growing from the roof tiles. 

Our garden was immaculate in comparison, like a blooming rose next to a rotting deer carcass. 

Living in this piss stained shack was me, my mother, Bill and my brother Chester. 

The rotting corpse on the outside was cold and desolate but on the inside it collapsed on itself, all that function of the organs turned to deteriorating decoration. 

I am posting this here for two reasons, one on request of my dad. “Write all that fucked shit down, turn it into a reality you can read so it loses its grip on you”.   
  
I am certainly positive he meant as in a note or journal, not to post it online but I’m sure he would understand. Which leads to reason two.

The house was a labyrinth dedicated to my family's laziness which I traversed in Theseus esc daring even until I was old enough to be taller than the piles of magazines, newspapers and shelves scattered around the house. 

Except I was no Theseus, none of us were, we were all the minotaur under the gaze of a horrid and depraved Athena.    
   
The first Theseus was introduced to us at the family kitchen table. Chester proudly showed off his creation, blonde perfectly brushed hair, pink and white polka dot dress, bright blue eyes and ceramic white skin. 

Chester's first doll, the second reason I am posting this.

“Everyone, this is Jenny! I worked very hard on her so please only say nice things!” Chester awkwardly but proudly pointed his arms at her. 

Jenny was slanted, her feet being replaced with rubber peach feet as well as her hands, contrasting terribly with the pale white. 

My mother sat with her mouth slightly open while Bill didn’t even look up from his paper.  

She sat up, walked over the counter and started using one of the many espresso machines to make another coffee. 

“What about that Anne girl, from the internet?” she didn’t look up from her cup. 

“Well that was a wild goose chase, a ploy to embarrass me since apparently that name belonged to another gal that they said I should’ve known about.” Chester rubbed the back of his neck.

“What was her full name?” my mother said, still looking from her cup. 

“Anne Frank.” Chester said with his lips straight. 

Bill placed the paper down and stared off before quickly rubbing his hands across his face.

“Well, what about that girl from the video game store?” my mother lumbered back to the table. 

“I caint go in there no more..” Chester looked down in defeat. 

“Why not?” Genuine upset in her voice as she asked. 

“Some baloney about not respecting boundaries when I had full intent to respect all her boundaries!” Chester shrugged before taking a bag of chips out from one of the cupboards. 

“I do not know why women in public do not find me attractive! To heck with em, I can find love somewhere else!” Chester rubbed the shoulders of the mannequin. 

“Well, if my little lamb finds love like this as long as he is happy.” My mother put her hand on her chest before moving up from her chair to hug Chester then kiss him on the cheek. 

“Ma, not in front of the lady!” Chester chuckled. 

Bill eventually lumbered out of the kitchen to his study as my Mother and Chester left to their own devices. 

I sat at that table staring at the pieces of paper, clothing and fast food wrappers as my stomach growled. I was ten years old at the time, I looked like them but I was always so hungry. 

Constantly stuck in my pink and baby blue pajamas that stopped fitting me years ago, a dried out slug desperate for food that wasn’t salted chips and burgers, liquid that wasn’t fizzy cola and attention that wasn’t chores or spiteful acknowledgement. 

Besides Jenny’s existence as a love doll for my sex depraved brother, there was nothing strange about it. 

Everyone in the house continued with their miserable existence while Chester would show her around the house, garden and other little buildings in our compound. Excluding the garden shed, that was the only thing Bill had a backbone about. 

The horror started when he arrived, Ed. 

Ed was a "family friend”. 

Back in the day he and Bill rode in the same motorcycle crew around Louisiana which is how Bill met my mother who was one of their groupies. 

In comparison to us, he was scrawny and tall with long messy black hair and a clean shaven aged face. 

He wore a long brown duster trench coat with cut up cargos. 

He had an atmosphere to him, the surrounding loblolly trees with their birds silenced whenever he approached the gate. 

I assumed mother called him directly after Chester showed off his new girlfriend. She always did when something in the house wasn' t going her way. 

If that didn’t work she would then fall back to her classic tactic. 

“I guess I would be better off dead than. Don’t even change my clothes, bury me with what I got on now.” 

She probably hoped Ed would talk some sense into Chester or even scold him. Instead he was intrigued by Chester's new hobby. 

The three of them were gathered in the front garden, Ed hated the inside of the house so he insisted on speaking outside. 

I was listening from my upstairs bedroom window which overlooked the front of the house. 

Chester had brought Jenny out with him, standing alongside my Mother and Ed like she was breathing and speaking amongst the group. 

“Uncle Ed, Jenny likes all sorts of things! She is integral to the plot lines in my comics, fending off evil avatars like smoking, ganondorf and homosexuals!” Chester played with her hair. 

Ed took out a tin of chewing tobacco rubbing it between his blacked gums and ivory teeth. 

“An innominate object can fend nothing off without the proper preparations dear boy.” he spoke with a deep yet calm put on slur. 

Chester looked offended on Jenny's behavior before processing what Ed had just said.      

“Preparations?” my mother chimed in only speaking in a soft tone towards Ed. 

 “Objects need a purpose inscribed onto them, power of Christ hanging from the cross ripples throughout history. To the point a dollar store plastic set of rosary has even the slightest hint of his presence.” Ed spoke casually. 

Ed squatted, exhausted from standing there or even by their presence. Even at that age how my mother treated him confused me. 

Bill wasn’t around, I didn’t know if he was at a motel in the town close by or in his study since he couldn’t stand Ed. 

“I’m starved, Martha, can you make us a sandwich or something?” Ed rolled onto his back and my mother nodded. 

She was making him food? I think in all my years alive up to that point I had only seen her make coffee for herself. 

Chester lingered over Ed, slouched and dead stare like a shaved bigfoot. 

“Chester boy, my lucidity is compromised. When your mummy makes that sandwich, bring it to that willow”. Ed pointed to the willow tree close to the garden gate. 

“Tamara can do that.” Chester huffed picking up Jenny and heading back inside.

“When I wake, I'll show you what I mean.” Ed added before shambling over to the willow. 

I hadn’t directly interacted with Ed in my whole life, so when Chester called me down to bring him that sandwich I was mortified. Everyone in this house screams and yells but Ed never so much as raises his voice. 

Traversing the stares of all its obstacles it took me almost a full two minutes just to get to the kitchen. 

My feet were always damp from cat piss or split unknown liquids clung to the carpet. 

My mother was putting together Ed’s sandwich, the bread was moldy, she only put butter and waffle chips onto it before passing the plate to me. 

She glanced over me, like she had placed the plate on the counter. 

She lumbered back to her living room hoard, burying herself in the armchair surrounded by coffee cups, the landline phone to call places she sees in magazines or ads on the tv and more fast food packages. 

Ed sat under the willow with his eyes closed, I stared at him for a while before stepping onto the grass. 

Moving slowly towards him I placed the plate down beside him but before I could bolt off, he spoke. 

“He’ll grow hungry, sooner rather than later.” Ed smirked, not even opening his eyes. 

I stood frozen before slowly backing away, he continued chewing his tobacco before only eating the bread of the sandwich mold and all. 

Ed stayed for a couple of days, picking one of the abandoned houses on the property to sleep in. 

He had Chester bring Jenny to that place over the next few days, I was too petrified to go anywhere near where Ed was staying.  

I wish I did at the time, on one night the two of them brought in a black bag into the corner building. A dilapidated garage Bill used to have his harley collection in before he sold them. 

During a heavy rainstorm one night, I heard a bell being rung from the garage. Pelleting against my window I saw in the droplets, the colors changed.   
From clear to a tinted red like tears of wine. 

After that night, Bill had decided enough was enough and for the first time in a long time he stood his ground. 

He kicked Ed off the property, who had been sleeping in the garage as my mother made him meals. 

Kicked out  is a strong phrase, Bill asked sheepishly   
“H-how long are you gonna be here?” 

Ed just smiled at him, saying nothing as he walked towards the gate where he vanished into the woods. 

Once the rain had turned red and the bell chimed five times, Jenny the doll started to move around the house. 

At first she just appeared around the house without Chester, my mother grew annoyed at him just leaving her around but he said she was just “Lounging around her house!” 

Bill had gotten up to do the only thing he cared about, his garden, when he saw Jenny under his willow tree where Ed liked to nap. 

Multiple twigs surrounded her in a circle, leaves plucked and sprinkled about. 

Pulling his garden gloves off and straightening his coveralls, Bill marched inside shaking the house with each step. I heard him stomp up the stairs straight to Chester's door before pounding on it. 

“Chester, come out here now!” 

I moved close to my door, the room was right next to mine so I creaked the door open. Chester then opened his door, white faced like a deer in headlights caught off by Bill's knocking. 

”Wh-what?” 

“My willow, my beautiful sallows. I march through cat shit and yer dirty undies without a peep but that tree out there has properties I hold in great importance!” A fury I had never seen until this point was redded across Bill's beady face. 

“Wha- why are you complaining about your tree?” Chester whined.

”Your store bought bride to be is currently out there underneath my tree, I told you not to fashion yer magic wands or gay little stick blades from it years ago. I shouldn’t have to repeat myself!” 

“Jenny? I wasn’t out there pa! She likes it though! Maybe she got a green thumb!” Chester chuckled and rubbed the back of his neck. 

Bill stared at him, he turned away staring off into nothing still red in the face. He narrowed his eyes before marching back towards the stairs. 

“You-she caint be touching my trees, plants, fountains nothing. Tell- let it- just pass the message on!” Bill waved his hand before moving back downstairs. 

“Erm, okay pa.” Chester shrugged before handing back to his room to play games. 

I quickly rushed back to my bed to look out over the tree, Jenny was gone and the branches were scattered from the circle they were in. 

Bill walked back to the willow seeing her absence, he stood frozen shooting a look around every direction in a panic before he looked up at me. 

His eyes lingered on me, it was probably the longest he paid me any attention. He stared back at the willow before lumbering over to the gate, opening it up then leaving in his red truck without even closing it. 

I looked around quickly to see if Jenny was around anywhere but I couldn’t find her. 

I went back to how I spent most days when I was young, lying in bed tucked into the covers before my mother woke up to demand I do my chores around the house. 

Bill left the house in a silent huff constantly, usually to stay with women in motels for a couple of nights. My mother knew and so did Chester, he referred to them as “Maidens of the night.” 

My mother once said to me, unprovoked, that the idea of sex with Bill appalled her. 

It was in her den watching the shopping channel as I was bringing her a packet of chips and a litre of cola. The only noise for a moment was the shopping channel, cat themed nick-nacks and a clattering ball if I remember correctly.

She broke the silence, told me to change the newspapers on the floor. 

Changing the papers amongst the dust clatters and crumbs flecked into the carpet was a recurring chore. 

Putting the cat houses back, filling up their food and milk. I minded them more than she did but I would watch the multitude of cats curl up onto her lap, the way she tickled their tummy and held them cracked at me everyday. 

They would meow and purr for her, saving only hisses and scratches for me. 

I used to think she whispered stuff about me to them, ordering them to scrape my cheek or bite at my fingers when I moved their bowls around. 

A couple days after the garden incident there I was again, crawling on the floor. 

Moving through the boxes that towered over me and stepping in all sorts of fluids and foods left from ages ago. 

I made it to the living room window where the cat bowls were, a ginger tabby my mother called “Sir Rowelly” hissed at me before fleeing behind the dormant Christmas tree that had been there for a decade. 

I clinched myself, expecting one of them to claw me again or bite but for the first time.

I heard a meow. 

It was quiet, weak almost but the first meow I had gotten was outside the window. 

I was excited, maybe a friend I thought finally, I didn’t even have the concept of a friend in my mind besides what Chester would mention about his online friends. 

I shot up, looking around the windowsill for the cat that called out to me from the night, I couldn’t see one. 

Looking deeper into the fogged glass I heard the weak and deep meow again.

It wasn’t a cat. 

Jenny was standing out in the driveway, looking into the living room. 

At that age you have a vivid imagination, but I couldn’t have imagined its mouth actually moving. 

A line had been cut in the mouth and around forming a little opening that moved with each deep meow it made. 

I was stuck in place, the more I listened to it meow the more it sounded like a grown man muffled, pretending to be a cat. 

Its form was obscured in darkness but its jaw unhinged all the same. 

Eventually I crawled away, slowly, not taking my eyes off it. Jenny continued to meow, her puff plastic dead eyes glaring into the living room. 

When I travelled back through the tunnels of tupperware and clothing piles I was back to my mothers side. 

She was staring out the window, at Jenny, in silence, eyes wide and lips opened slightly. Jenny continued to meow, I wasn’t even looking at it, tucked in behind a pile of laundry. 

My mother slowly turned to look at her tv, the meowing stopped, she looked back at the window. Jenny was gone, my mother turned back to the TV, hands trembling.   
I didn’t leave her side for the night, usually she would tell me to leave but this night I could tell she wanted some company too. 

I hid in that little laundry pocket for the night, covering myself in it like a shield watching my mother slowly succumb to exhaustion and fall asleep. 

I didn’t move until the sun came up, I didn’t want to look out the window and see her standing out there. 

When the morning came I slowly left through the living room door without glancing back at the window. 

I was pulling myself up the stairs, moving around the junk scattered around it before I had finally reached back up to my room.

Chester's door was closed, I could hear him loudly snoring from inside, completely unaware of how me and my mother had spent the night. 

I slept for a long time once I reached my bed, my stomach was growling and every muscle in my body refused to activate. 

At that age I didn’t want to exist anymore, there was no cat waiting to be my friend. This house was my crypt, I was going to roam these halls until a bookshelf eventually collapsed onto me. 

So I decided to wait in my room, waiting for time to pass. That's all my life was back then, waiting for nothing. I didn’t eat that day. 

Night came again, sluggishly quick. The darkness felt bright, the garden outside felt suffocating, the labyrinth felt compressed and the dust felt omnipresent. I was living an oxymoronic life, fat but hungry, ignored but trapped. 

A slug trapped in its shell. 

I couldn’t even escape to my dreams, I had no reference to a world outside this maze. Every dream was this house, folding and pressing against my chest like little dust nats nuzzling in every nook of my lungs. 

This night I dreamt of something new, Jenny, not in a statue esc form but a mobile human-like frame with the same appearance and a cat on her lap. 

Sitting in my mothers chair, tv with a static background but audio of the shopping channel advertising fruit supplements. 

Jenny spoke to me in a language I didn’t understand at the time, in an old deep voice of a man muffled by layers of either dust or dirt. That dream has lingered in my mind ever since, so I remember the speech it gave me. I spent a long time in a library when I was sixteen, asked some locals when I had finally mustered up the courage to socialize. I didn’t know then however, still a ten year wait to rot in a house of dust. 

 From everyone I had asked, Jenny was speaking in the chitimacha language. Her-its voice was like a gargling corpse, the tv static accenting the chant. 

It repeated the same sentence over and over for what felt like an eternity in that croaking on dirt growl. 

“The soil was of an arid and thick sand.”

I could feel that thickness, catching across my eyelashes and seeping into my pores.

 I felt a heaviness in my throat, and I began to retch. I retched myself awake, I couldn’t see around my room dirt was in my eyes and face. 

I heard something moving around my room fast, then I quickly felt scratches at my legs from under the covers. 

I started squirming, still choking, unable to scream. I pulled the covers over myself and desperately rubbed my eyes.  

Something hard hit against me then, the moisture from my tears was absorbed by the dirt but I was successfully clearing my throat.

 A whimpered scream came out, gasping and retching. 

My vision was slowly returning, I heard my bedroom door quickly slam as I started to cry out. I was crying for my mother, I knew deep in myself she wouldn’t come but I still did. 

Eventually Bill came in, the one time he was home. 

He looked around my room frantically as I was still whipping the dirt off my face. It was all over my bed, my pillow and pajamas.

I felt around my head where some muck was mixed with crimson blood, a cut across my head from a coffee cup that was flung at me during my squirming. 

For the first time, Bill came to my level and started whipping the dirt and blood away from me. 

I was crying at him like I was his daughter, for once he felt like my father. 

He picked me up, I clung to him tight praying he would never let me go. 

He brought me slowly downstairs to the bathroom, a mess of towels and the espresso machine connected to faulty extension cords. 

He washed me off in the bath, it was the first time he or any one had washed me. 

I watched the streaky brown line mix in with the last drops of blood from head twirling into a spiral going down the drain. 

It took Bill a while to find me new clothes around the house, I was sitting in the bath staring into the drain for a while. Eventually he returned with a clean towel and some other pajamas for me to wear. 

He sat on the toilet while he dried me, clearly exhausted by any form of movement he had to do. Bill was staring off into nothing again while I put the pajamas on, his eyes looked like he was witnessing wet tissue paper being squashed up and stuck to walls. 

He took a deep breath, looking up at me. His frown broke through, one that had been sitting behind his face his entire life. Bill puffed his chest and composed himself. 

“It’s okay kiddo, I'm gonna sort this all out.” he patted my shoulder. 

“Yer messier than the boys marching through them jungles, listening fer whispers.” Bill smiled.

Finally he stood up, pulling his shirt over his belly and proudly restated.   
“I’m gonna work this all out kiddo, don’t you worry.” 

It was our longest conversation at this point in my life. I held my arms out to him, his facial expression changed between a smile and dread before he just nodded and walked out the toilet. 

My arms hung in the air for a little too long before I brought them down. 

I made my way back upstairs, scratches across my legs and a cut on my forehead. 

I cleaned the remaining dirt off my bed and picked up the cup it threw at me. It was my mothers dual handled cup, now a single handled cup. 

My mother would kill me if she found this cup broken, even if it wasn’t me. I stored it under my bed for the time being. 

Outside in the garden none of the sticks were pulled off the willow and Jenny was nowhere to be seen. 

The loblolly trees stood watch as always and the sound of tv shows or games from Chester's room never subsided away in his little world. 

After that night Bill's attitude changed. 

He began staying around more, purchasing a bunch of digital cameras and setting them up around the garden and house. 

Chester never spoke to Bill unless he wanted something but when Bill decided to limit the spending on his bank account Chester had no choice but to argue with him. 

I was in the living room with my mother again who was asleep in her pile when they started arguing in the kitchen. 

“Yer online friends are bleeding us dry! What choice did you give me?” Bill roared. 

“Bu- their helping me with supplies! Ed says these guys are my pals, they want more of my art!” Chester whined, stomping his feet in tantrum.  
   
“Ed ain’t your father I am! Who even are these people! You gotta quit sending them photos of the house! Do you realize, do YOU realize if the city council or our church saw the state of the inside they’d come clean the place out! Now if you can go out for yer conventions or to video game stores and the like, then perhaps I can change my mind about you not working!” 



“A job?! I get enough from the disability checks and ma can always sell sumtin online! Please Pa, don’t cut me off I need this!” Chester began to cry. 

    
Sitting in the living room next to my mother flipping through one of her magazines she had circled all she wanted in it while hearing Chester cry about not getting his way was incredibly satisfying. 

My mother just scowled at the tv, eventually Chester marched up stairs still crying as Bill went back to his study. 

A couple of days passed, with Bill having set cameras up around the place and me locking my bedroom door. 

I was hoping Jenny wouldn’t be able to find her way inside. I refused to look outside the window near my bed. 

Bill was taking more and more initiative I guess is the right word, putting Chester in his place by barring him from using the other little house that he and Ed used for the bell chimes. 

He even tried to tidy the house up a bit, but stopped immediately after my mother said “Check the bathtub, that's where I’ll be if you throw out my stuff. Don’t even have a funeral for me, I don’t want Father Desmond giving my ceremony. He’ll turn it into a comedy club.” 

Jenny’s activity had almost stopped completely however, to Bill’s annoyance. He desperately wanted to catch it moving but ever since its attempt to bury me she has stayed put in Chester's room.  

One day, Bill returned from what I thought was another late night binge but instead had gotten a bunch of kid gardening tools. 

He opened my door and huffed.  
   
“Cmon now, caint have you doing nothing round the house no more, get changed and meet me in the garden.” 

I felt a strange rush go through me, it was a new feeling, excitement. I quickly threw my crusty pajamas off and changed into them almost rolling down the stairs. 

Me and Bill spent the day watering his roses and marigolds, however a lot of water and fertilizer went to his willow tree. 

“Need them nice and damp, this big feller takes the most as we hadn’t got much rain lately.” Bill rubbed the side of it. 

“Everybody tells me the tree is a metaphor fer depression or some shit, I ain’t never associated it with that. My pa grew these all the time, never liked my harleys and I never cared for his coffins.” Bill turned to me. 

“Sept we both liked this tree, ma would always read under it.” As Bill looked at me, he struggled to fight off sadness or regret. 

“Don’t know if he would’ve cared for the fountain features. I caint get enough of em, make me feel like an emperor buying and setting em up, specially the obsidian one I got a couple years back.” Bill pointed at one near the gate. 

It was a pure black disc where water ran off the top to the bottom in a hypnotic stream, Bill took some stray leaves out from the water to keep it clear. 

“This used to be such a nice place, I got native blood in me so I feel connected.” Bill rubbed his hand into the grass. 

“Wh-what was your dad like?” I asked him. 

Bill turned and looked shocked, he probably never even heard me speak at least for a couple of years, I had almost forgotten I could.

 His face melted away, a mixture of rage that was then drowned by that sadness he was hiding once again. 

We spent the rest of the day gardening in silence, he even struggled to make eye contact with me.

 At the end of the day, he finally approached me, put his hand on my shoulder and just said “Thanks kiddo.” 

At the time I wished the day didn’t end, I hoped tomorrow he would garden with me again, I wanted to garden everyday. 

I also wanted a dad, this was the closest he ever got to being that. He got me food from a pizza place too but he ate it in his study. 

When the night came, walking past Chester's room he hadn't left it. 

I heard movement from inside, at first I thought he was moving something but it was the sound of his mattress springs creaking over and over again. 

I closed my bedroom door and locked it, still refusing to look out the window. 

Despite everything with Jenny and my family for the first time in my life everything started moving in an upward direction. 

I spoke to Bill for the first time and he was actually starting to take notice of me. 

Jenny also seemingly stopped moving, even if it was because of the cameras. I thought maybe Bill could move out with me, move into somewhere in town and forget about Chester and his love doll. 

Or maybe Bill was going to finally claim his house back, tell Chester to move out and help my mother. Maybe Bill was ready to actually be my dad. 

Hindsight's a bitch. 

Bill must’ve watched one too many of his John Wayne movies from his study because this new bravado was just that, bravado.   
He was still very much included in the labyrinth with us, while his confidence with Chester was something he flat out ignored my mother. 

There was someone he couldn’t ignore any longer, and all his bravado would filter right out of him and he would return to the lying coward he was. 

Ed had come back to the house.
