“There are gods on earth.” I didn't want to believe it… until that night.

It all began before summer break, or at least that's how I remember it.

The trees were turning from brown to a brilliant green. It was my favorite time of year because of how quiet the streets were, especially in the evenings, when pink twilight settled over everything. It was beautiful.

A few months before the end of the semester, our engineering professor told the class we would have to submit a final project to pass the course. At first I was excited. It meant this would be my ticket to enjoying the summer.

The excitement faded when he told us we had to work in pairs. I wasn't very good at socializing. I never went to parties, not because I wasn't invited, but because I didn't like that kind of atmosphere. Besides, my parents wouldn't let me go out. I'm what you'd call a lonely, boring kid. Most of the class didn't like me. Sometimes they simply ignored me or forgot I was there. The closest I ever came to socializing with anyone in class was when a girl told me she liked me, but I got so nervous I turned her down. Back then I had social anxiety. I was hopeless at talking to people.

By the time I stopped daydreaming, everyone in the class had a partner, except for one person. He was a boy I'd never seen before, or so I thought. Since we were the only two left, we had no choice. The professor began explaining that the project had to incorporate all the skills we'd learned over the semester.

A virtual assistant. That was the first thing that came to mind. A virtual assistant that could help users when they felt they'd forgotten something important in a text. Something like a summary, except users could ask it any question, saving time and effort. It could be a very useful tool for students like me… although, in the wrong hands, it could do more harm than good.

John. That was his name… John. He wasn't sociable either. In fact, nobody knew him. You didn't even notice he was in the class. There was something very strange about him. He was quieter than I was. Honestly, I even wondered whether he was in our class at all. Getting a conversation going with him was difficult. We settled on my house as the place to work on the project, and, as expected, he agreed.

John… his name was John.

On the way to my house, John didn't speak to me. Not that day, not over the next few days, not the following week. The project was incredibly difficult at first, partly because the first prototype of the program kept crashing whenever I entered a question. The interface would stop responding immediately, and the screen would grow sluggish before freezing altogether. Then the whole thing would shut down with a horrible noise. I tried so many times. I tried fixing the code, and I tried starting from scratch in another program. I was so tired of trying day and night that I began to think it was a waste of time, until John helped me. His voice was very timid. At the time, I thought perhaps he sounded that way because it was the first time he'd spoken to me and he was embarrassed.

I told him to give it a try. The prototype was a lost cause anyway. I imagined the scent of apples, the summer breeze brushing my face, the trees swaying back and forth, the leaves falling.

I was so close… I could feel the summer breeze so close, and yet so far away.

My project was doomed to fail. Maybe if I'd done something simpler, I wouldn't have been in this situation. But the need to impress the rest of the class, to finally be noticed, to finally feel like one of them… was stronger.

I was about to cry when John suddenly touched my shoulder and pointed at the computer. The prototype was working. I couldn't believe it. I'd spent so long changing the code, trying to fix it, and John had done it in no time. I tested the program, and sure enough, it worked exactly as I'd imagined. I immediately looked at John, and he shyly lowered his eyes.

John… his name was John.

As far as I remember, he wore loose clothes, mostly black. Sometimes I wondered how he could stand the heat. After that, he and I tried to talk. It was a little difficult at first, but as the days went by, we opened up more and more.

I won't deny that talking to him intimidated me, not because he was quiet, but because he was so intelligent. He talked to me about so many things: science, writing, politics, society. Intellectually, I felt so small beside him. He could talk about those things for hours and hours. I'd ask, and he'd answer. I'd make a comment, and he'd offer an argument. I'd give an opinion, and he'd debate it. To this day, I don't know how he knew so much.

The days that followed were much the same, the two of us discussing anything and everything. In the end, we never found answers to our questions, and we'd laugh about it. Deep down, I thought he was a nice person. I even began to think I'd found a close friend.

John… his name was John.

Some time before the project was due, I noticed how restless he'd become. I even noticed he wasn't coming to university as often. I waited a day, then two, then four, then a whole week without seeing him.

Under a tree, the leaves falling, the breeze touching my face, the sun lighting everything. Summer was mine, and I wasn't going to wait for John to show up. I'd submit the project without him.

John… his name was John.

One afternoon before the project was due, I received a message. It was him. He sent me a location and told me to come, because he needed to talk to me about something important. I was a little wary at first, since the place was in a forest far outside the city, but then he sent another message saying it was very important.

The journey felt much too long. By the time I arrived, the forest was completely dark and silent. I had to use my phone's flashlight to keep from tripping. As I went deeper, I made out the lights of a house in terrible condition.

John was waiting for me there. The wind began to blow so hard that leaves went flying from the trees. I thought he would tell me why he'd disappeared without a word. He didn't. His face was haggard, with deep shadows under his eyes. He looked as though he'd been up all week and had only just gotten out of bed.

Without a word, he took me by the arm and led me into the house. Outside, the wind kept getting stronger.

Inside, a few candles barely lit the rooms. John switched on a flashlight, and we went down a flight of stairs. I couldn't see a thing down there. I tripped over something, and when I turned on my phone's flashlight, I saw cables scattered all over the floor.

“You're the only person I trust, the only person I can talk to about this without being judged. Promise you won't tell anyone. You might be a little confused at first. I was too. But this could change the fate of humanity, or reality as we know it. With this, we can make a better world.”

That's what I remember John saying before he pulled a lever up and the whole basement lit up. The first thing I saw was that the cables led to several interconnected monitors, which came on with an unbearable blast of static that slowly faded.

“You have to trust me. This can bring about the world humanity has always dreamed of.”

I could make out that the cables weren't only connected to the monitors and the computer towers beneath them. They also ran through a doorway in the corner of the room. John began typing into a program very much like mine, though its interface looked like something from an old computer. Then he entered a command and pressed a key.

The other monitors began blasting deafening static and displaying strange symbols as the room grew a little warmer.

Suddenly, I heard screams coming from behind the door in the corner, and I quickly realized that all the computer cables led into that room. I grew frightened as the screams became louder. John kept typing commands into the computer. Smoke began pouring out from under the door in the corner, and for a few seconds the floor seemed to shake.

When the screams stopped, the basement was full of smoke, and the room was so hot that I was sweating all over. I wanted to ask him what those screams had been, what he was doing. None of it made sense.

“Ask whatever you want. The program will answer. All I did just now was start it up. It's not quite ready yet, so think carefully about what you're going to ask… anything you can think of, the program will answer.”

I hesitated at first, but the curiosity was killing me. I thought it would just be a waste of time, that John was only messing with me. I decided to play along. When I sat down at the computer, I noticed the program looked like a badly made copy of the one John and I had developed. I didn't give it much thought and decided to do as he'd said.

“What is my date of birth?” That was the question I asked the program. After I pressed Enter, it began displaying bands of code in different colors, until the screen froze. Just as I'd expected, the whole thing was a sham.

“You need to put a little more effort into your jokes,” I told John. I was about to leave when the monitors began blasting static again. I covered my ears. It felt as though the noise would burst my eardrums.

When I looked back at the main monitor, there was something written on it. Apparently the program had a small glitch that made it freeze for a few seconds before answering.

August 28, 1999.

No, no, no.

I couldn't believe it. It was the exact date I was born. I thought I must be mistaken, but I wasn't. I closed my eyes and opened them again, and there it was… August 28, 1999.

The program had answered what I'd typed.

I was amazed. I asked John to start the program again and, after a few commands, typed in another question to make sure it wasn't a joke:

“Who was the first man to reach the Moon? If you overtake the person in third place in a race, what position do you finish in? Who was Charles Darwin, and what did he do?”

I refused to believe it. How was something like this possible? Not even the internet back then was as advanced as this program. It answered every kind of question, one after another. Sometimes history, sometimes mathematics. It didn't matter. The program answered in such a human way.

I looked at John, and he told me he had to show me something. I couldn't wait to find out how this machine worked, so I went with him without hesitation. He seemed nervous.

While he opened the door, I grew impatient to understand how this machine, or program, or whatever it was, worked. I thought perhaps it ran on several interconnected computers full of information, and the program searched through all that information to answer the user in an almost human way. But if it was a machine, how had it known my date of birth? I was desperate to see what lay beyond, where the cables disappeared through that mysterious doorway. I wanted to know what John would surprise me with this time, because, after all, his name was John.

When the door opened, I froze. All the cables led to a hospital bed. John switched on a small lamp hanging from the ceiling. On the bed lay a shape beneath a white sheet.

And beneath that white sheet, I could make out a hand hanging down.

What is summer? For some, it's the warmest season of the year. The days seem to last forever, while the nights are short. It's the season when everyone goes on vacation and enjoys themselves. But I think it means something else. Summer means freedom, nature, happiness, love, and a little betrayal.

John said, “You have to trust me. It'll be difficult at first, but all of this will be worth it. You're the only person I trust, and I know you'll stand by me in this.”

John approached the bed and slowly pulled away the white sheet, revealing something I could never have imagined.

The cables didn't lead to the bed itself. They all ended in a large sphere that covered the head of a pale, thin body, so thin you could see its bones. Straps around its wrists, ankles, and stomach held it to the hospital bed, making any attempt to get up impossible.

Anyone else in my position would have run away in terror, but for some strange reason I was far too calm. Curious would be the right word.

The pale body moved as though it were trying to catch its breath.

“A few days before I stopped coming to university,” John said, “I was walking through the forest, taking my usual nighttime walk. I was thinking about how important you'd become to me, someone close I could tell all my problems and secrets to. I was thinking our project would be the best anyone in the class had ever seen. That you and I would finally make a name for ourselves among the people who'd always ignored us. That we wouldn't be those same introverted kids anymore. That maybe some girls would notice us, and we'd ask them out over the summer. The first time we talked was so awkward, but little by little we got better at it. You told me about yourself, and I told you about myself.

“And I was thinking about telling you I'd like to spend the summer with you. I was making up what I'd say and imagining how you'd react. You'd probably be happy about it, because, after all, you'd dared to get to know me, and I'd dared to get to know you. I was so happy. For the first time in so long, I had a friend.

“But it all began when it started to rain.

“That night, the wind began blowing terribly hard, and I was already deep in the forest. The first drops fell on my face, and in the blink of an eye they became a downpour. I tried to run home, but it was no use. I was too far away. Besides, the forest was so dark I couldn't see which way I was going. I tried anyway, but the forest kept growing until it seemed to have no end. I was lost. There was no point in walking any farther, so I sat under a pine tree, frightened and cold.

“I thought I was going to die, until I found him.

“God.

“Like me, he was lost and confused. He asked me to help him. That's how I found this cabin.

“He was so grateful. He told me I'd earned a place beside him in heaven. I couldn't believe it. It was him, God the Father, right before my eyes. He was so beautiful, so majestic.

“I tried to take him home with me, but he refused. He said anyone other than me would die instantly at the sight of his face. So over the next few days, I looked after him, feeding him and tending to his wounds with the few things I brought from home. Little by little, God began to recover, and, naturally, I asked him the questions any mortal would ask:

“Why do we exist?

“What is our purpose?

“What comes after this life?

“And so on.

“But he wouldn't answer me. Part of me understood. I thought perhaps the answers were so complex that the human mind couldn't comprehend them. I had so many questions and not a single answer.

“The night before, God told me he would return to heaven before dawn. In desperation, I insisted once more that he answer my questions, but he refused. I was miserable. Right in front of me was the chance to know everything, absolutely everything. The answers to questions so many great minds had longed to solve. The cure for every disease, the solution to wars, food for the poor. But God simply wouldn't give me any answers.

“I wasn't going to let that opportunity slip away. I wasn't going to leave things like that. I wasn't going to let God go.

“I told God I was going to get food and would be right back, but that wasn't what happened. That night, in desperation, I went to a dump and gathered everything I needed: a hospital bed, tape, harnesses. Somehow I made it back here, and I did it. I felt guilty and damned, but at the same time I was excited.

“Now there was another problem. How was I going to make God talk if he refused? That's when I remembered the program we'd made. Over the next few days, I worked on building a machine that could extract all the information from God's brain. At night, I refined the program that would serve as a bridge between the machine and God's knowledge.

“I failed so many times, but I came a long way. Far enough to build what you saw.”

I was confused and astonished at the same time. God was right in front of me. I touched him, and his body was cold.

I was so curious that I told John to run the machine and the program again, only this time I would watch the whole process.

John left the room and came back with a laptop, the program already open. He began typing the code again, and when he was ready, I started typing my question:

“What comes after death?”

When I pressed Enter and the question appeared on the screen, the program seemed to load normally. But after a few seconds, it began freezing and displaying strange symbols so quickly that I couldn't make them out. The generators grew incredibly loud. The electrical current made the cables thrash back and forth, and the room began to heat up, but none of that mattered to me. I was watching God. His body writhed desperately from side to side, trying to break free of the bed. His screams were infernal. I thought my eardrums would burst at any moment. He looked like an earthworm trying to escape a child.

Apparently John still had a long way to go before the machine could work without making God suffer, although, if this was a prototype built in a few days, I could only imagine the finished version. Deep inside, I felt jealousy and rage. A program like this had been my idea, and now he came along and stole my work. It was as if he'd spat in my face. What would John do with the program? How would he show it to the world? Would the world even be interested in someone as strange as him?

The sphere covering God's head began to smoke until the whole room was filled with it. Then a burst of sparks shot from the helmet, plunging everything into darkness. As expected, the program had stopped working. John tried to fix the generator, but his efforts were useless. He told me to wait, that the lights would come back on any minute, but I left without saying goodbye.

Outside, the weather had calmed. Under a tree, the leaves falling, the breeze touching my face again, the moon lighting everything. Summer was close. I could breathe it in at every turn.

I stopped in the middle of the forest. I don't know how long I stood there, thinking about everything I could lose. I looked back toward the cabin.

And I went back.

Several years have passed since the day I presented my virtual assistant project to the entire department, and, as expected, everyone was impressed. Several classmates came over to see it and asked me questions about it. For the first time, it felt good to know people were interested in what I did. In a big headline, the university newspaper called it the best project a student had ever completed independently. Beneath that headline was a photograph of me beside the prototype of my program.

That summer, I went out with Mary, a girl from university who'd been very impressed by my project. She was incredibly beautiful, and we had so much in common. In time, she introduced me to her friends, and that gesture ushered in what you might call the best period of my social life. My life had changed so much, and I was grateful. Before we knew it, we were a couple. Years after graduating, we bought a beautiful house near a lake, something Mary had dreamed of since she was a little girl. A few years later, our first daughter arrived. She was so beautiful that we named her after her mother: Mary.

Everything in my life was perfect.

Until yesterday, when the police came to my house. My daughter cried as they arrested me. The officers didn't say a single word, but deep down I was certain they'd found out… it had only been a matter of time, and that time had run out. In the interrogation room, they asked whether I knew a man named John Taylor.

I told them I didn't remember very well, and in a way that was true. Or perhaps I haven't told the whole story. After several hours of questioning, they let me go. They didn't have enough evidence to keep me in custody, though the investigation would continue. I don't want to do this because I don't want to remember. I don't want to dig up something I struggled so hard to forget, something that gives me nightmares every night, something that makes me cry, something I simply cannot explain. Every time I turn out the lights, fear runs through my body, and I rush to Mary's bedroom just to see them both asleep. Sometimes I stay awake to make sure they're still with me and no one can hurt them. Especially him.

I know Mary can't see him, but I can. I see him everywhere I go. I've learned to ignore him and get on with my life, but lately his appearances have become more frequent, and he comes closer when the summer nights fall.

In the early hours of this morning, I couldn't sleep. I went outside to get some air and calm down, and that was when I saw him… floating in the lake, in the darkness, watching me. I shouted at him, asking what he wanted, but he didn't answer. Mary came out of the house, frightened, and asked me what was happening. I simply didn't answer her.

For some reason, I felt the need to return to the forest where John had asked me to meet him for the last time. I don't know what I expected to find after so many years, but I needed to go back.

That afternoon, I left home without saying anything to Mary. When I reached the forest, the sun was beginning to sink behind the trees. I walked for quite a while before finding the remains of that old cabin.

That was when I remembered everything.

That summer, before the project was due, I killed John. Why did I do it? Was the answer simple? If John managed to perfect the prototype of the program, he would have everything, and I would have nothing. I was jealous. Besides, it was my program, my idea, that made the machine work. Without my program, John couldn't do anything. It was everything to me. It was mine. I had the right to claim it. That night, while John tried to fix the generator, I was walking away from the house, sick with envy and anger.

Besides, he wouldn't have known how to make the most of that machine. He wanted to help humanity, to find cures for diseases, to give bread to the poor. But the world wasn't made for good, weak people like him.

The program was mine, the machine was mine, the attention was mine, God was mine and no one else's.

I remember going back to the cabin that night. John was still trying to fix the generator. I was so blinded by rage that the last thing I remember clearly is that, in the blink of an eye, I was holding a metal pipe, and John lay dead in front of me, in a pool of blood.

I disposed of his body as best I could. At first I thought of leaving him there, but sooner or later someone would find the house. It was only a matter of time before his parents and the university grew suspicious and reported him missing.

“As they ate, Jesus took bread and blessed it. Then he broke it and gave it to his disciples, saying, ‘Take this and eat; this is my body.’”

“Then he took a cup, gave thanks, and passed it to them, saying, ‘All of you, drink from it. This is my blood of the covenant, poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins.’”

I buried John's body in several different places, each a long way from the others, which would make it very difficult for anyone to find him. And if they did, by then the remains would be unrecognizable, or the worms would have eaten him.

The easy work was done. Now I had to face the hard part: finishing what John hadn't. Throughout summer break, I worked day and night, refining the machine and the program to make them run more effectively.

Meanwhile, missing-person flyers for John Taylor were being handed out around town. Within weeks, search parties were organized. And, as expected, a few police officers came to my house asking about John, since I was the only person who'd been with him in the last few days before he disappeared. He'd told me his parents worked full-time. Besides, he had no friends other than me. I took advantage of that and told the officers John was very strange and distant, that he didn't like talking to me, and that one day he'd disappeared without telling me anything. Apparently they believed my story, because they left, though not before telling me to get in touch if I learned anything about him.

I needed somewhere to hide God, since if they found the cabin, they would find everything. Time was against me, so over the next few nights I moved God and the machine, a little at a time, piece by piece, into my basement. I kept God with me for a while, until the searches ended.

Meanwhile, I kept making progress on the program's prototype, and everything seemed to be going well. I'd improved quite a few things. One of my greatest breakthroughs was extracting all of God's neural information and his consciousness, storing them on several computers. It was difficult at first, but the further I got, the more things seemed to work themselves out, allowing me to get rid of his body and all the heavy machinery. I won't deny that, before disposing of him, I wanted to remove the sphere John had placed over his head. But it had fused to his face because of the electrical shock he'd received when John's prototype was still faulty.

The first version of the program was released in 2021. It was an overwhelming success, crushing the other internet search engines. Everyone was talking about it. Everyone said it was like talking to a human being. I received offers to buy the rights to my program and my brand, but I turned them down. Within a few years, I topped the list of the world's richest people.

It was only a matter of time before humanity began misusing my program. Some used it to cheat on school exams. Others used it so they wouldn't feel alone. The rest couldn't even answer simple questions without asking my program. I suppose, in the end, John had been right about some things. This tool could be used for a greater good, instead of the stupid questions people kept asking it. I felt overwhelmed and disappointed in myself. I'd given everyone the most powerful tool in the world, but they didn't seem to realize it. I'd given everything and, at the same time, nothing. I'd stained my hands with blood that, in the end, hadn't been worth it.

Over these last few years, I've begun to feel that emptiness inside me again, the way I did when I was young. Neither fame nor money had changed my life. Deep down, I was filled with regret.

John. His name was John.

It's been five months since I stopped writing this. I'd even forgotten about it. But now that I've decided to put an end to this suffering, I want to pour all my guilt into this text file. At least before the alcohol wears off and I change my mind.

My trial took place that same winter. The press and all the news outlets were there. It was the story of the moment, and no one could believe it. I stood trial for the murder of John Taylor. According to the news report, a property developer had bought the land, and during the excavations they had found part of John's remains. Forensic specialists examined them. DNA testing revealed that they belonged to John Taylor.

But that didn't prove who had killed him. With no witnesses and insufficient evidence to convict me, I was acquitted, although sooner or later they would discover that everything pointed to me. Mary was terrified and pulled away from me. She wasn't going to let a murderer like me look after our daughter. I haven't seen either of them since that day.

Now I'm alone in the house, standing at the window, watching John float above the lake. He's staring straight at me. His body looks like an abstract painting. His feet are where his hands should be, and his hands are where his feet should be. His torso seems to be made of rotting flesh, with ribs showing through it. His face is unrecognizable. Outside, everything is dark, and there is no one there.

As I write this, I know that, for some reason, he has no intention of killing me. And rightly so, because in front of me an old rope is waiting.

That's all, and honestly, I don't want to write anymore. To whoever reads this, whoever finds it, I have to say that I've made my decision. There's nothing left worth living for. Going to prison would only prolong my miserable suffering. I'd like to ask John to forgive me, wherever he is, and God too, for sinning against him. I know I deserve to go to hell, but I hope you'll understand that I was only someone who wanted a little attention.

I love you so much, Mary. Take care of our daughter. Thank you for making me happy all these years.

John had given that machine a very strange name, something like a play on the words artificial, for the way the machine and the program worked together, and intelligence, for God's knowledge. AI for short. And that was what I named my project that summer.
