You ever wish you could go back in time and change something? Anything at all. Whether its a mistake you made in the past or something you said to someone that you regret. For me it was what I *didn't* say. For my whole life I wish I had just asked him if there was something on his mind.

I was walking home from school with my best friend Josh and it seemed to me it was any other day. I had other friends too, but Josh knew me the most. Josh understood me and I understood him. Our sense of humor complemented each others and we were into the same music, video games and shows.

We had a routine to meet by the entrance after school and walk home together. Our house's were close to our school so it usually wasn't a problem. First stop would be my house and then Josh would walk alone to his house, which also wasn't far. I remember that day well, it seemed average.

Just before the walk.

We both had our skateboards on this day, so that meant we would be skating back home and only occasionally speak to each other. I didn't notice something was off until we turned onto my street. Josh circled me with his board and skited to a stop right in front of me, causing me to nearly jump off.

"Hey watch out man!" I said.

Josh started laughing.

"What are you even doing?" I said ending the sentence with a slight chuckle.

"Stopping you, figured we could talk until we get to your place." Josh said.

I shrugged my shoulders and kicked up my board.

"Okay sure, what's up?"

He looked at the ground in silence for a few moments.

"You ever wish.. things were just different?"

I gave him a confused look and nervously laughed.

"What do you mean Josh?"

"Life would be much easier if it wasn't so.. complicated. You know what I mean?"

I was caught off guard, I knew Josh had a difficult home life but he had never opened up to me about it before. His real parents were never in his life, they gave him up to a foster family when he was still in diapers. Josh swapped between 2 families until he was put into the one he was in now. He never knew the feeling of having real parents, real companionship. My young teenage mind had no idea how badly this could affect somebody and that was truly the worst part, I had no idea how much he was struggling.

"Yeah life can sometimes suck, but we're teenagers now so it'll only get worse." I said and laughed.

I thought it was funny, but it wasn't. If there were any words I wish I could take back, it would be those. I was completely oblivious to the fact my best friend was trying to ask me for help.

Josh didn't respond. We walked in silence until we got to my driveway. Things had suddenly gotten very strange. Josh was normally talkative, very outgoing. But today he was off. Anyone who knew Josh, even just a bit would be able to tell something was wrong. And I ignored it.

I stopped and looked at him when I stepped on my driveway. He looked expressionless.

"Alright ill see you tomorrow dude." I said.

And that's when Josh finally looked at me, and for the first time in our five years of being friends, he hugged me. I stepped back confused, and hugged him back. He let go after a few seconds and spoke.

"You're a good friend man, only good one I've ever had. Don't think I ever told you, so that was my way of doing it."

I smiled at him.

"You are too Josh, I hope I'm alright."

Josh laughed and spoke to me, for the last time.

"Keep doing you man."

With that he was gone, skating down the street without giving me a chance to respond. I was left dumbstruck and confused. I had this terrible sense of dread that I still now can't properly describe. I ignored it at the time, but found out later why it was there.

Josh never came back from that walk.

I received a call from Josh's foster mom later that night. She had asked me if Josh was with me. When I said he wasn't, she went into hysterics and told me he never came home. Josh had turned off his phone location and nobody knew where he was.

That's when it hit me. I realized why I had that overwhelming feeling of dread, why he had seemed so off and why he never skated towards the direction of his house that day.

Josh was missing.

We hung up and I immediately dialed his phone, the call went straight to voicemail. I was in a full panic at this point, full of unanswerable questions. I ran out of my house, ignoring my mothers concerned questions on the way out. I was going to check the only place I thought he might be at, our spot in the woods.

When we were 12 we had found a small opening of trees in our neighborhood woods and spent the whole day building our own fireplace by at. Then we put two chairs next to it. We spent many nights there, just talking for hours. It was our spot.

I was in a full sprint running into the woods, tears beginning to form. Eventually I made it to the campfire.

I paused when I saw it, before I screamed. I had never screamed like I did on that day and the sound of it scared me even then.

I was right. He was there. But he was not alive.

He laid on his back on the ground, face blank with both eyes open, endlessly starring off into the sky. There was not a scratch on him, but the empty bottle of pills laying next to him was all but invisible.

I ran to him, shaking his body and screaming his name. But he was unresponsive. I still remember the way he looked, he had empty void eyes and his lips were slightly turned up. He was smiling. It was a pleasant smile, the smile of somebody who finally found peace.

I screamed his name as if to let out every single emotion I was feeling at once and the tears came full force. I cried for a couple of minutes, unable to process what I was looking at.

Then I saw something on the chair closest to me. A piece of notebook paper folded up and placed under a small rock. Wiping away tears, I unfolded the paper and saw what it was.

It was his note.

*‘I hope life treats you better than it treated me. I'm sorry.’*

I was almost angry. After all the years we spent together, that's all he had to say? I don't know how long I sat there next to his body, reading the letter over and over again before I called his foster mom.

She was there in just a few minutes. Josh was rushed to the emergency room.

He didn't make it.

When I got home I ran to tell my parents. By then it was too late for him. They showered me with hugs and told me they were here for me no matter what. It felt meaningless to me, like they were just telling me things I wanted to hear. But what do you tell somebody who had just lost they're best friend to suicide?

I went up to my room and got into bed. I sobbed there in the dark until my eyes had enough and I fell asleep in tears.

Eventually I woke up, but quickly found I couldn't move. I looked around in the dark, but found nothing. The only light in the room being the streetlamps outside reflecting into the house. I was desperately trying to move any part of my body but it was useless. It was pure silence, more quiet then anything I thought possible. It was maddening. No sound of crickets outside, no AC unit blowing air, nothing. I tried to calm down but each second I spent unable to move caused me to panic even more. I had a feeling something was going to happen.

Then I heard something. The creaking of an opening door, coming from the direction of my closet.

It was quiet again for what must have been a full minute, but I knew I wasn't alone. I felt a pair of eyes on me, but I couldn't see the source. The feeling was more strong than anything I had ever felt. Then soft footsteps. I watched in helpless horror as a shadow of a person began to take form in front of me. It stood watching me for a moment before walking towards me at a patient pace. The shadow took a couple of steps before it stopped. It was hovering over me, and that's when it spoke.

*"this is your fault"*

It spoke in my mind, almost like a thought. The voice was low and raspy. I was using every ounce of my strength to move my body.

*"your fault"*

I had never been so scared in my life, no matter what I did I couldn't move. I wanted to scream.

It moved closer.

*"your fault"*

It was starting to get aggressive. The words kept repeating and repeating.

*"your fault your fault your fault* ***YOUR FAULT***\*"\*

The shadow was now yelling in my mind with a voice unlike anything I had ever heard. It sounded glitched and impossibly deep. My insides screamed at me and with every ounce of strength I could possibly muster, I finally moved a single inch of my head.

It was gone with the blink of an eye. I shot up and looked around my room in a panic, convinced it was hiding. I searched every area of my room before I was convinced I was alone again. I plopped back on my bed. Just a sleep paralysis. I had never experienced something like that before. I never went back to sleep that night.

‘Guilt’ felt like the only word I knew the next following week. Josh was the first thing I thought about everyday I woke up. I couldn't help but wonder if I could have changed his mind, if I could have known. That's the most dangerous part about depression, the longer you spend with it the easier it becomes to hide. Josh hid it well. The only time he ever tried talking about it to me, I dismissed it.

I felt numb. Everything felt meaningless. The words of the shadow echoed in my head every second of the day. I knew what it had meant.

Going to school was possibly the most difficult part. Nobody came to check on me and nobody even talked to me. All I got were sympathetic glances in the halls and in classes. I felt watched and alone.

On one particular day I decided to walk to our spot in the woods after school. It was almost a scary thought, but I wanted to. It may as well have been his gravestone, and I wanted to talk to him even if I knew he couldn't hear me. As I walked into the woods, I wondered if I could ever see them the same way again. Me and Josh claimed these woods years ago, we grew up in them. How was I supposed to go back now?

It didn't take long to get to the campfire. I stood staring at it feeling empty. Bricks stacked in a small circle with sticks crammed inside. I almost felt like destroying it. I sat on one of the chairs with my eyes never leaving the campfire, the sound of nature filling the air. I let the sound overtake me as I began to form words to my late friend.

I spoke without thinking.

"I'm sorry."

I was. I didn't know where to start.

"I wish you told me sooner" I managed to say. When I got no response I suddenly felt angry. I let out a loud grunt and began throwing the bricks onto the ground. Why did he have to do this? Why didn't he write more on his note for me? Why didn't he tell me sooner?

I eventually stopped and fell on my knees in tears. I sat there for a few minutes before I could finally stop. I prepared myself until I could speak again.

"Wherever you are, I hope your well." My words came out muttered but were comprehensible.

"I'm sorry for destroying our campfire, but why couldn't you have told me sooner? I would have helped you."

I looked up at the sky and closed my eyes.

"Look man this is---"

A voice interrupted my sentence. As I was going to say 'this is no ones fault', I heard a soft whisper directly in my ear say “*stop*”.

I jumped and shot my head around, hearting dropping in my chest. The voice was the same one I heard in my paralysis, but this time I wasn't sleeping. Then I heard it again.

*"you did this."*

The voice spoke in my other ear. I quickly turned towards the sound but again didn't see anyone. Where was that coming from? I decided I had enough.

I looked at the now broken fireplace one final time before walking away. The more I walked, the more panic faded. I was clearly just hearing things. Instead I was being faced with thoughts of what the voice was telling me. Was it my fault?

The question loomed over me. It invaded my thoughts, it took over my life. Everything would always lead up to the same question. The same simple question.

That was the last time I went to our spot in the woods. It was hard to go there, even to look at it. I began isolating. I stopped going outside, I stopped seeing my other friends and I barely spoke to my parents. The voice I heard in the woods began to take hold of me, I heard it at random times for the rest of the day. It constantly repeated 'you did this' in my head. It was being thought of against my will. Ignoring it was difficult, but when I did I found myself more at peace.

I would try to tell myself it wasn't my fault even if I didn't fully believe it, but when I did the voice would slow down just a bit. I spent the rest of the day locked in my room, which I was starting to become used to. My room was my shell.

That night I saw it again, the shadow in my sleep. When I woke up and couldn’t move my body, I knew it was going to happen again. Just like the first time, it was pure silence. Nothing was audible except for the constant ringing in my ears that you would only hear in the deafening quiet. I braced myself.

A single knock on my door amped up my fears in an instant and I found myself afraid again. It was a light gentle knock, like whoever was on the other side was patient. I took in deep breaths and tried to move. I felt like I was standing in the eye of a hurricane. A second knock came in a pattern.

*knock knock knock. knock knock.*

It was like it was taunting me. It didn't knock again. It's a terrifying feeling being unable to move while knowing something is after you. I tried to focus on my fingers, if I could just lift anyone of them this would end. As I lay there trying to move, the door handle slowly began to move. It was getting inside. My heart was throwing itself against my chest.

The door creaked open and at first I didn't see anything on the other side, until it stepped into view.  The same shadow I saw the night before stood in front of me, but this time it wasn't a shadow. Its body only matched it. This time it was a person.

It was Josh.

His skin had taken on a greyish color, his hair was soaking wet and his eyes were open wider than I thought possible. He was wearing the same clothes he died in, but his shirt was ripped away in places. His expression was blank but I could still feel his eyes burning onto me. In the dark he looked like some kind of zombie. He looked like a walking corpse. I felt like I was fighting for my life but this time it was even harder to move than before. The voice spoke, but this time taking on Josh's voice.

*"It's your fault you know."*

Then an idea struck me. If telling myself the opposite of what the voice says makes it fade, maybe it would get me out of this. It was a long shot but what other choice did I have?

*"You did nothing wrong"* I told myself.

Josh stepped forward.

*"You did nothing wrong"* I thought again.

Josh's voice spoke.

*"Why did you do this to me?"*

He tilted his head as he spoke, but his lips wasn't moving.

He stepped forward again.

*"You did nothing wrong. You didn't do this"*

That's when Josh stepped back. He smiled as he did. I repeated the same words to myself at a rapid pace. The more I did, the more Josh would retreat. Josh got to the door, and without looking he walked out and closed the door behind him.

Then I was able to move again.

I looked around my room, it was empty. I knew he was gone, but I swear I could still feel those eyes on me. The feeling wasn't gone until morning. Like last time, I never went back to sleep that night.

The next day is when it happened for the first time. The first time I saw him while I was awake. I was in my first period class when it came back, the feeling of being watched. It hit me out of nowhere. I looked around. I looked at each corner of the room, but didn't see anything. It didn't occur to me how dumb I might have looked until I felt a tap on my shoulder. It was Adam. Adam was what I would categorize as an acquaintance. Not exactly a friend, but somebody to talk to in class to make time go by a bit faster.

"You okay?" He mouthed to me.

I nodded to him but as I did, I saw him. Josh was standing right behind Adam, hovering over him. My face dropped and my eyes widened.

How was he here? What was going on?

Adam gave me a confused, yet concerned look.

"You sure?" He mouthed.

Josh's eyes were on me. In the light I could see him perfectly. He didn't seem to be paying any attention to Adam. I looked at Adam and shakily lifted a finger and pointed behind him, but when he turned around he gave me an even more confused expression. He couldn't see him. Josh stood still throughout all of this.

As I realized this, I calmed my face to the best of my ability and whispered to him that I was alright. Josh stood in the same spot until the class was over. All I could do was ignore it. The bell rang and I walked out, as I did Adam caught up to me.

"Hey man what was all that about?" He said.

I didn't want to talk.

"It was nothing, really I'm fine." I said back.

"Are you sure? I know about what happened. I'm really sorry about that."

He needed to stop. I didn't want to talk about it. But he pressed further.

"You can tell me if something's on your mind." He said.

I looked up at him and offered the best smile I could give and told him I was fine and walked off. Who was he to ask me these questions? He didn't even know Josh. Nobody knew Josh, not like I did.

I saw him again in my second period. Standing in a corner this time, looking at me. Nobody even batted an eye in his direction through the whole class. I was the only one able to see him. I spent the rest of the day trying to go throughout my normal routine, but he was always there.

Josh began to follow me everywhere I went from this day forward. Everyday no matter where I was, Josh was there too. Standing somewhere slightly out of view staring at me. It was a constant reminder, I couldn't escape it.

For the next couple of weeks it was like this. I was incapable of thinking about anything else. However the longer he would be around, the more I got used to it. It was horrifying for the first couple of weeks but when something sticks with you for long enough, no matter how unusual it might be, your mind eventually warms up to it and accepts it. Eventually I barely even thought about it.

Sleeping was however the hardest part of all, how are you supposed to sleep knowing theirs somebody in the corner of your room watching you? But even then, after weeks turned into months my mind warmed up to that too.

That entire time in my life is difficult for me to remember, everyday was the same thing. I would wake up, go to school not speaking for the entire day, go home and stay locked in my room until I slept. Josh was there everyday.

One day I got home from school and rushed to my room, but my mother caught me first. I walked to the direction of my room before she grabbed my arm. Confused, I looked around to see her standing there. She had a worried expression, like she didn't want to say whatever she was going to say.

"Can we talk?" She said.

I tried to politely brush her off but as I turned to go, she grabbed my arm again.

"I need to talk to you." She said again.

I decided to talk to her, whatever it was seemed important. But I hoped it would be quick. I nodded and she gestured to the living room. I sat on the couch next to her. She hesitated for a few moments before she spoke.

"I need you to talk to me. I've been worried about you, I've been leaving you alone since it happened because I figured you wanted space but that doesn't seem to be helping you."

I looked down.

"Please let me help you."

There was something about the way she said it. A tone of voice only a worried mother could make. It broke me. I fought back tears and looked over at the corner of the room. There he was. Josh was standing there unmoving. My mother looked too.

"What are you looking at?" She asked.

"It's nothing. Just thinking." I responded.

It was hard to talk about him while he was standing in the same room as I was. By this point though, I doubted Josh could actually hear anything I was saying. It had been a long time since I heard his voice.

We talked then, really talked. I told her everything, only leaving out about how I could still see him. She listened to every word I said without interrupting and by the time I was done she was in tears, but I felt better. I hadn't told anyone about how I felt, and I didn't realize until that moment that I needed to. I looked back at Josh, but he was gone. I stared in disbelief while my mother gathered herself. He was really gone. It didn't take me long to figure out how he disappeared, it clicked in my mind easily.

It was at that moment I decided I wanted to change.  I told my mother I would improve my life, I would get over what happened. I could tell by the look in her eyes she didn't believe me, likely thinking I was just telling her what she wanted to hear so I could be excused to my room. But I meant it. I didn't want to see him anymore, I didn't want his death to overtake me for the rest of my life. If I wanted change, I would have to work for it.

This is when my life changed for the better. After months of misery and despair I finally managed to snap out of it, and all it took was one talk.

Josh was back the next day, but I finally fought against it. I forced myself to talk to my other friends again, I forced myself to think of anything other than what happened to him. Anytime I would forget about it entirely, he would be gone. I picked up hobbies I never thought I'd be interested in, I went out with my friends nearly everyday, and I spoke to my parents everyday for the first time in months. Life was beginning to look better.

Every morning I would still wake up to him standing in my room, but as the weeks progressed into months and finally a whole year, one day he was gone. At this part of my life I was doing pretty well, still not entirely happy but getting there. I still remember that feeling, the feeling of hope that struck me. I shot out of bed and checked my entire room, every hiding place I could find, but I never saw him. It was finally over, I was moving on.

However I did see Josh again that same day, but for the last time.

I had just gotten to my room after dinner but I was feeling tired, more tired than I would usually be by that time. I tried to stay awake but the more I fought against it, the stronger it became. Eventually I decided to give in. I ended up going to sleep in minutes.

I woke up. I couldn't move.

My eyes widened, indescribable panic overtook me. It had been over a year since this had happened. How was it happening again? Something wasn't right, I thought this was over. I had to fight against it, this time I was motivated.

Nothing happened for a few minutes, I felt completely alone this time. I couldn't feel his presence, but that still did nothing to calm my nerves. It even made it worse. When was he going to show up? Every second that went I felt I would see him standing in front of me, but he didn't appear. Something really wasn't right.

As I was trying to calm myself, out of nowhere my bedroom door fully opened. It was sudden and if I was able to speak I would have screamed.

Then I saw him, but he looked different this time. There was not a single scratch on him, no ripped clothing and no terrifying blank stare. It was just Josh. Josh as I had last seen him the year before.

He turned to look at me and slightly smiled. There was nothing off about it, and I didn't feel afraid. I wanted to smile back but I couldn't. I wanted to say so much to him, I wanted to apologize, I wanted to ask how he had followed me everywhere for months, I wanted to just talk to him again. But I couldn't.

He walked towards me and crouched down on one knee to be eye level. It really felt like him, not whatever he was before. I didn't feel the presence of something bad, I just felt the presence of Josh as I knew him. For a moment he was silent and looked around my room. He did this a few times before a satisfied smile formed on his face. He looked up at me with a single tear in his eye and spoke to me.

*"Thank you."*

Then he was gone, and that was the last time I ever saw him.

My life was never the same again without him, but I lived with it as best I could. His death taught me that reality will change at any given moment and there’s nothing we can do about it. We can't control it and we'll never be able to, all we can do is accept it. I reflect on our days often, but I'm okay with it. Thinking about Josh went from painful to almost bittersweet as my life moved forward.

It's been many years since I've seen Josh now, but I still think about him. I even like to think about him now, he was a huge part of my life and I'll never forget him. The days we spent together made my childhood memorable, and that’s more than you could ask from a friend.

Sometimes I wonder where he's at now, but a feeling in my gut tells me he's found peace.

Sometimes I wonder if it has anything to do with me.
