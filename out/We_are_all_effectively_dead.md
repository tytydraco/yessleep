My name is Clark. At least, that is what you should call me.

I am a scientist at a university that, for obvious reasons, I cannot name. For years, my team has researched water—its composition, its behavior, everything we thought we understood about the most ordinary substance on Earth.

Then we received a sample from a swamp in Argentina.

At first, nothing seemed unusual. The water was contaminated with a strange species of algae. 

The biologists working with us discovered something impossible: the algae could break down **any and all** poisons and venoms, converting them into carbon dioxide.

That discovery was fascinating.

What frightened us was what happened to the swamp.

The water level had dropped by nearly one and a half meters in a single year.

The algae wasn’t merely living in the water.  
It was consuming it.

Anna was the other lead researcher assigned to the project. Her first theory was that the algae could dissolve any liquid into carbon dioxide.   
We tested it.  
She was wrong.  
It only worked on water.

So we began studying the sample itself.

Most people believe water is simple. H₂O. Two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. That’s what we are taught in school.

But real water isn’t that simple.

Salt water, distilled water, lake water, groundwater—they all contain different compounds, dissolved minerals, impurities, and microscopic organisms. 

We tested dozens of samples.

The algae consumed every single one.

We still couldn’t explain how.

And, frankly, I wasn’t paid to find out.

Mark, our supervisor, explicitly ordered Anna and me to leave that part of the research to the biologists. Our job was to determine **why the algae dissolved water**.

Then Anna asked a question that changed everything.  
“What if water is a poison?”

Mark laughed.  
Then he stopped laughing.

We tested the theory.  
We took several laboratory rats and separated them into controlled environments. They were given food, but no water.  
They all died.

That should have disproved the theory.

Mark wasn’t satisfied.

He brought in a newborn mouse.  
It was given no water for several days.  
It survived.

Then we gave it a single drop.

It drank.

And after that, it wanted more.  
Every day, it sought water. We eventually allowed it to drink normally.

It became dependent on it.

Then , when we took it away… it died.

We repeated the experiment.  
The result was the same.

Anna broke down when she understood what we were looking at.  
“What if we’re not dying because we stop drinking?” she whispered.

“What if we’re dying because we started?”

I didn’t sleep that night.  
The idea was absurd. Impossible.  
And yet every experiment made it harder to dismiss.  
Water appeared to behave like an addictive toxin. 

The more an organism consumed, the more it demanded. And once the body adapted to it, stopping became fatal.

We had spent our entire lives **poisoning** ourselves.

Mark submitted our findings.

Three days later, government officials arrived.  
They told us the research was classified.  
We were ordered never to discuss it.  
They said releasing the information could cause mass panic. Millions might stop drinking water overnight and die.

They said silence would save lives.

Perhaps they were right.

But I can’t stay silent.

If you’re reading this, I’ve already sent copies of this letter to people across the world. I expect to be arrested.

Maybe killed.

But it’s already too late.  
You have probably already taken your first sip today.  
Maybe you’re drinking from a glass right now.  
Maybe you’re thirsty.  
Maybe you think that refreshing feeling means water is keeping you alive.  
It isn’t.  
That first sip you ever took wasn’t the beginning of your life.  
It was the beginning of your addiction.  
And the moment you swallowed it…  
**you signed your death warrant.**
