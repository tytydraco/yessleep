**Perpetual Calendar Clock**

That perpetual calendar clock would change its time on its own every time the power went out. At first, I paid no attention to it, thinking it was just some ordinary technical malfunction. But as time went by, I began to realize that those changes had never happened randomly.  
It seemed that every number that appeared carried some kind of meaning—cold, silent, and far more frightening than I had imagined.  
There was an old perpetual calendar clock in my house. It had been hanging there for more than a decade, perhaps even before I was born. On the left half was an image of Shakyamuni Buddha, surrounded by small light bulbs that once looked like a halo, but they had long since gone dark. I didn’t know when they had stopped working.  
On the right was the time and calendar display, showing both the lunar and solar calendars. The numbers continued to move silently, steadily, just as they always had for all those years.  
Then one day, when the power went out, I was old enough to start paying attention. I noticed that the clock was always showing the wrong time whenever the electricity came back. And every time, my father would silently stand beneath it, patiently correcting each number.  
At first, I simply thought that it had “stopped” at the exact moment the power went out, so naturally the time would be wrong when the electricity returned. But after noticing it several times, I began to feel that something wasn’t right. It wasn’t just the time that was wrong—the day and even the month changed too, despite the power outage lasting only a few hours.  
I tried to push those wandering thoughts aside, reassuring myself that it was all just a technical error. Those incorrect numbers had to be nothing more than coincidence.  
Until one day, by chance, my whole family sat together and looked back at old memories of my grandfather, who had passed away. On a commemorative CD recording footage of him, I noticed a string of numbers appearing in the corner of the screen:  
**“230713 12:05”**  
Curious, I turned to my mother and asked what those numbers meant. She remained silent for a moment, her eyes falling, before quietly answering: that was the date my grandfather had left this world.  
“Okay… I understand.” — I replied, but inside, a strange and indescribable feeling began to rise.  
After that day, those numbers kept repeating in my head, like a fragment of memory that had become stuck.  
“23… 07… Why do I keep remembering this? It’s not like I normally have a good memory…” — I told myself, a vague sense of unease rising inside me.  
“Time to sleep… It’s midnight already.”  
I pulled the blanket over myself, covering myself from head to toe. It wasn’t cold outside at all, yet an icy feeling rose within me, making drops of cold sweat quietly seep out.  
At 3:20 in the morning, the whole neighborhood lost power again. Even though I had been fast asleep, the oppressive heat caused by the fan stopping woke me up.  
My parents had already gotten up, rummaging around for a few hand fans to cool my younger sibling down. I knew they couldn’t stand the heat, but in the quiet of the middle of the night, their loud crying became unbearably clear—hurried and chaotic, making me even more irritated.  
I turned over, pulled the blanket higher, and forced myself to close my eyes, ignoring everything and sinking back into sleep.  
The power didn’t return until 5 in the morning. Being woken up in the middle of the night and then unable to fall back asleep felt terrible. I tossed and turned from 3:20 until the lights finally came back on, my mind heavy and sluggish.  
“My head really hurts…” — I muttered, feeling restless.  
Out of habit, I walked into the living room and looked up at the perpetual calendar clock.  
“Wait… Dad hasn’t fixed it yet?”  
I froze.  
“But… those numbers…”  
**The 23rd.**  
**The 7th month.**  
**The year 2013.**  
**12:05.**  
My heart suddenly skipped a beat.  
It was exactly the same string of numbers from the old recording.  
“How could this possibly be a coincidence…” — I stood there frozen, a chill running down my spine, trying to reassure myself that perhaps I still wasn’t fully awake… or maybe it was simply an unbelievable coincidence.  
Those numbers haunted me for the next half a month. At first, I still tried to believe they were nothing more than a coincidence. But the more I thought about it, the more fragile that belief became—almost impossible to hold on to.  
“If it isn’t a coincidence… then what is it?”  
The question kept repeating itself in my head. Every time I thought of the clock, my heart seemed to drop with anxiety. The headaches lingered stubbornly. I tried telling my parents, but all I received were evasive looks, as though what I was saying wasn’t worth their concern.  
One stormy night, I sat alone in my room, scrolling through my phone to pass the time. Outside, the wind howled in bursts, and rain pounded relentlessly against the window.  
In the blink of an eye, the lights in my room went out.  
Darkness swallowed everything.  
Then, immediately, the lights came back on as if nothing had happened.  
“Must be because the rain is too heavy… The electricity is acting up again…” — I muttered, but the fingers holding my phone tightened without me even realizing it.  
I glanced at my phone. It was already past midnight.  
“I’ll go outside, pack my books, and then go to sleep…” — I muttered, dragging my heavy steps into the living room, searching for my books and notebooks for tomorrow before hurriedly stuffing them into my bag.  
I tried not to look up.  
I tried my hardest.  
But then, my eyes were still drawn toward that perpetual calendar clock—as if something was forcing me to look.  
**The 7th.**  
**The 5th month.**  
**The year 2026.**  
**11:32.**  
The air suddenly turned inexplicably cold. All sounds seemed to fade into the distance, leaving only the frantic pounding of my heart inside my chest.  
“Is that… tomorrow…?”  
Until then, the clock had only ever changed to dates from the past. This was the only time it showed something in the future.  
I stood there motionless, afraid to blink.  
That night, I stayed awake until morning.  
I lay staring at the pitch-black ceiling, asking myself over and over whether that string of numbers truly meant something. There was only one thought occupying my mind… yet the more I thought about it, the harder I tried to deny it.  
“What… is going to happen tomorrow?”  
The next morning, everything went on as normally as any other day. I went to school, carrying a heavy feeling pressing against my chest. Throughout the entire class, I couldn’t concentrate on anything.  
A few friends turned to ask if I was okay because I looked much worse than usual, but I simply shook my head and brushed it off.  
“I’m fine… I just didn’t sleep well, that’s all.”  
Even as I said it, I could hear how strangely empty my own voice sounded.  
I didn’t know when the time passed. It was only when I looked up that I realized the clock was already nearing 10 at night.  
I anxiously paced back and forth in the living room, looking at my phone before glancing back up at that damn perpetual calendar clock, as though I were counting every passing second.  
Only about an hour remained.  
One hour to find out whether all of this was merely a coincidence… or something far worse.  
My mind was so empty that it ached. I collapsed onto the sofa, unconsciously reaching for the remote before turning on the TV.  
“Watching something might help me stop thinking about it…” — I muttered, though even I didn’t believe what I had just said.  
After flipping through a few channels, I suddenly glanced at my phone.  
**11:27.**  
“Five more minutes…” — I muttered.  
An icy chill suddenly ran down my spine. I sprang to my feet, gathered the books and documents that I had left scattered across the floor the day before, trying to keep myself busy with anything at all.  
“It’s nothing… I’m definitely just imagining things…”  
But my hands were still trembling.  
After sitting in front of the TV for an entire hour, my throat had gone dry.  
“I’ll get some water and then go to bed…”  
I slowly walked toward the kitchen at the back of the house. But the farther I went, the more I felt that something was wrong.  
A very strange smell lingered in the air.  
Familiar… yet enough to make my stomach tighten.  
“What is that smell…?”  
My steps slowed.  
My heart was pounding so hard that it hurt inside my chest.  
**11:32.**  
The small kitchen light flickered.  
Then I saw it.  
My mother was lying motionless on the floor. Around her, a dark liquid was spreading across the ground.  
“M… Mom…?”  
My voice caught in my throat.  
And in that moment, I understood.  
**The clock had never been wrong.**
