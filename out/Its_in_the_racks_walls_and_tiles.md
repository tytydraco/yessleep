You know that feeling you get when you turn the lights off in one room, and you sprint to the next because your brain thinks something is right behind you? I’ve lived in that for the past three months. From 10pm to 11pm, Monday to Friday, something is watching me. I’m not crazy. I’m not. My therapist says I am, my friends are worried about me but I’m not. They just say the job is getting to me, that I’ve worked in retail too long, but it’s real, and it’s fucking dangerous.

I like working retail. I might be the first person to ever feel like that, but I like talking to guests, stocking shelves, and giving my spiel on company policies. It’s simple, and that’s all I ever really wanted. That’s why I was so excited when I got promoted to a manager. They waved a few extra dollars an hour and some benefits in my face and I basically threw myself at it.

I was trained by my old boss, Josh. A nice enough guy, but definitely the type who smoked too much weed for his age. I shadowed him for a week as he walked me through all of my new responsibilities, most of which I had become familiar with during my past shifts. However, I never closed with him until the final day. Everyone else’s shift ended around 10pm once the last box was unpacked, but we had to finish the inventory report.

After waving everyone out, Josh slid a lanyard from his back pocket. 

“Uh oh, new manager alert!” He tossed it over to me. I grimaced at my own half-formed smile.

“Ugh, I hate this picture. Do we ever take new ones?”

He held out his, revealing the face of someone who resembled Josh with a full head of hair.

“Make sure to keep that on. Higher ups get real wound up over the uniform.”

I drew it around my neck as I sat behind him. The next hour was all spreadsheets, but his instruction was cut short when the lights shut off.

“Oh shit, we gotta go.” He said, bouncing out of his chair.

“But there’s still a few-” He cut me off.

“Rules are rules. Lights are timed to go out at 10:50. We gotta be gone and locked up by 11, something about the alarm. The opener usually finishes it anyway. I’ll tell you the rest on the way out.” He had his coat on before he finished speaking.

I hurried behind him, but I had to jog to meet his pace. Our footsteps echoed against the pitch black walls. His instructions were spat out as fast as he was moving. I didn’t notice it at the time, but thinking back, he didn’t move his head. He was only looking forward, eyes completely focused on what was in front of him, rather than what might have been beside, or behind us. We made it to the front door, and slammed it open. Once we were outside, his entire posture fell with a deep exhale.

“Jesus, man, do you always hit the door like that?” I exhaled, shoving my hands into my pockets.

Josh chuckled, pulling his dab pen from his coat.

“Sorry man, I just hate being in the store once it’s empty. A little creepy, y’know?” He took a puff before holding it out for me to take.

It was the middle of February and the wind ripped through my coat, but free weed, you know? I took it from him.

“You’ve done this a billion times and it still freaks you out?” I took my hit.

“Let me ask you something Sam,” He looked back into the store, a poorly hidden scowl flickering across his face. “Are you going to get freaked out? Closing by yourself? It’s a big place, and you’re not allowed to have anyone stay with you after ten.” He took his pen back.

“Josh, I’m a big boy, the dark hasn’t scared me since I was eight.” I coughed out.

He was the manager before I even started working there, and from day one I knew him to be chill. The kind of boss who will let you slack a little, because he would slack a little. Always a laid back guy, even on the clock, which made the sudden seriousness in his face send a shiver through my body deeper than the winter ever could.

“Are you sure?” He asked bluntly.

I threw up an eyebrow at him.

“Uh…yes?”

He sighed out a cloud of smoke.

“Okay man. You just seem like a cool guy. Don’t want to see you trapped at a job like this.”

With that, he fished through his pockets and pulled out his keys. He removed a ring and held it out to me, dropping it into my hand. He gave his parting words with a pointed finger.

“Remember: locked up by eleven.”

The weekend flew by, and once Monday rolled around, I clocked in at exactly 2pm. I know it’s lame, but I was actually excited. Every time I heard the loudspeaker echo through the store asking for a manager, I was on top of it. Whenever one of my coworkers had an issue, it got taken care of. My first day went great. Exactly as expected. Until 10pm. My coworkers waved me goodbye, and it was time for inventory. Stepping into the back by myself, though, it seemed dustier than it was on Friday. The fluorescent lights hummed a little louder without Josh telling me about shipment orders and pay stubs. I must have been a little too caught up in my surroundings, because I was only halfway through the sheet when the lights went out. The sudden silence caught me off guard.

“Oh shit.” I mumbled, grabbing my coat. I thumbed at my phone to get the flashlight on, and headed for the door.

Before I could make it out of storage, something swiped down my back. I yelped and flailed my light, revealing a green sweater crumpled on the ground at my feet. It had fallen, and scared me so bad I nearly swallowed my tongue. I let out an exasperated sigh and picked it up. I folded it and went to place it back when I noticed. Our back storage is all large box shelves, nearly reaching the eighteen foot ceilings, but the entire unit that held these sweaters was rocking, slightly swaying back and forth, as if someone was balancing on top of it. I put my hands out to steady it, and the movement stopped. I restocked the sweater, made a mental note to check it when I got back, and headed for the door.

Now, I meant what I said to Josh. I haven’t been afraid of the dark since I was a kid, but this was different. The walk from the back of the store to the front takes two minutes, tops, but each second that passed I felt colder. My brain alerting me to an unseen danger I was positive didn’t exist. My footsteps echoed louder in my ears as I got closer to the exit. No, not my steps, other steps. Almost exactly in line with my own, like they were trying to match mine. Right behind me. 

I whipped my body around. Nothing. The same store I’ve been working at for two years. I immediately felt how heavy my breathing had become, and the sweat slicking my palms. My flashlight moved aimlessly through the aisles, until I caught it. 

A rack of T-shirts faintly swung, the hangers creaking against the iron rods that held them. “Hello?” I called out. Silence. I stared for a moment, until my thumb grazed my phone’s screen, lighting it up. 10:58.

I spared another look into the emptiness, and shook my head. I jogged to the exit, swinging the door open and locking it behind me. I shook in the night air, only partially from the cold. I backed away, staring into the shadows of my nine to five. I waved my light across the check out counters, the shelves, the walls, looking for anything that would justify what had come over me, when my eyes stopped at a collection of hanging slacks. There was a gap in between two of them. Completely ordinary, but when I looked into that separation, I felt like I had spotted a tiger lurking in tall grass. All movement halted. My hair perked up across my neck, and my breath caught in my chest. I tried to figure out what was happening to me, when the gap shut. Like a curtain being drawn. Someone was in there with me. I sprinted to my car, and called Josh.

No answer. I called again. Nothing. I immediately cursed myself for calling my old boss in this situation, and called the police instead. Surely there was someone in the store. It’s the only explanation for all of that. I told the operator my name, my store, and I told them there was an intruder inside. Now, I’ve had to call the cops once or twice, as I’m sure most of you have. Even in a non-emergency scenario, they send an officer to take information, and I work in a major city, so they definitely had the personnel. I shit you not, the operator said they would send someone in the morning. For an active intruder! I tried to argue, and they literally hung up on me! I was baffled. I sat, staring at my phone. My shock was only broken by a notification. A text from Josh. I read the message, and it replaced every ounce of confusion with fear.

“Don’t quit. It’s in my closet.”

I didn’t sleep that night. I stayed up, pacing through my apartment, replaying the entire night in my head. This had to be some kind of hazing thing. I was the new, young manager. Josh and some of my other coworkers decided they’d freak me out, and it just so happened to be on a night where no police were available for a break-in at a retail outlet. I told myself anything I could in order to be more comfortable with the fact that I was going to do it all again in less than twenty four hours. 

I clocked in with much less enthusiasm on Tuesday. I had received a couple of side eyes from the openers before Irene saw me. A truly sweet lady. She trained me on register, and always had a sympathetic ear for my problems. Just this once, I wish she hadn’t been so kind. She would probably still be alive.

“Sam, sweetie, are you okay? You look tired.”

“I had a rough night Irene.” My eyes didn’t have the energy to meet hers. “Did any of the openers tell you what the police said?”

Her posture stiffened as she shook her head.

“The police? No, what happened?”

“About the break in last night, what did they say?”

“Sam, what are you talking about? I’m on a double. There’s been as many cops today as there have been any other. I haven’t heard anything about anyone breaking in.”

I sat in stunned silence. I had no thoughts to make any words.

“Honey, is everything okay? You’re starting to worry me a bit.” She rubbed a gentle hand on my shoulder.

“I…I’m okay, I think. It was just a really weird night.” I stammered, brushing my hair away from my eyes.

“I’ll tell you what, why don’t I stay after with you, just for tonight, okay? While you get used to the new job.”

I quickly looked up at her. My eagerness must have been transparent, because she smiled at my expression. I looked back down, though, as I remembered the rules.

“No, I can’t. I’m not supposed to keep non-managerial staff past ten.”

“Oh, *pfft*. You’re new to the job, you can get away with a mistake here and there. And if anyone looks at the cameras, I’d like to think whoever’s looking would recognize little ol’ me.” She chuckled, her humor cutting through my worry.

I wanted to accept immediately, but I have always been one to listen to the rules, especially at work.

“What about your kid? Don’t you have to get home to him or something?”

She laughed

“Sam, Hunter is sixteen. He’ll probably relish a night without Mom around.”

My shoulders fell for the first time since last night.

“Alright, Irene.” I looked at her for a moment, and hugged her, too tight for a professional environment. “Thank you.”

The shift moved by far too quickly. I was the only one there checking the clock and dreading each hour that passed. Knowing I wasn’t closing alone allowed me to breathe, at least. I kept glancing at Irene, as if she would vanish and leave me by myself, but eventually, ten came. Everyone else left, and Irene and I headed to the back. Though I was grateful to have her with me, Irene’s nonchalance clashed with the stress I felt. She organized shelves and texted as I tried to focus on inventory. Each stroke on the keyboard made difficult by the sweat building at my fingertips. I gasped when the lights went out. 10:50.

“Jesus, Sam, you scared me.” Irene chastised.

Like Josh, I had my jacket half on once I left my chair.

“Sorry, I’m sorry. Let’s just get out of here quick.”

“Well give me a minute, I have to find my coat.” She muttered, aimlessly wandering for it.

“Are you serious? We have to go now!” I felt my voice spike.

“Sam!” I had never heard her raise her voice. “You have to calm down. The door is right over there, we have plenty of time. If I knew you were gonna be this grouchy I might not have stayed.” 

I bit my tongue, and stood at the backdoor. After agonizing minutes, Irene had her coat, and we were out the back in an instant. I wanted to sprint, but I walked just fast enough to keep her right behind me.

“Slow down, Sam, we’re fine.” Irene panted.

“Please just keep up, Irene, we have to get out by eleven.”

“It’s alright just slow do-”

Her words cut off, and all of my momentum ceased. The sound of her footsteps behind me were replaced with a foul, guttural squelching to my left, like wet static.

“Irene?” I called out. Nothing was returned but the echo of my own voice.

I fought to turn my head. To this day I wish I hadn’t. My eyes met her legs. They were jutting out from a square shelf, the kind that holds t-shirts and jeans. Her body looked as if it had been pulverized through the two by two gap, whatever didn’t fit clogging the corners and painting the outside of the shelf. A pool of what was left began to form at the base of the display. The shock might have killed me if she hadn’t spoken.

“S…Sa-am?” Irene choked out. Wheezing and spitting oozed from above me. I felt a drip grace my cheek before managing to point my flashlight. The rest of her jutted out from the air vent, sealed in with a mangled mix of herself and polyester. Her nose was crushed flat, and it looked as if the sides of her head had been scraped to the skull. I don’t know if she was missing teeth, or if her mouth was so full of blood I just couldn’t see them. She seemed to realize what was happening as I did. She grunted and arched toward her waist, the bones she had left crunching and popping against each other. She pushed at the ceiling, attempting to pry herself free.

“I-I can’t-” *Sklch*. Her body pulled free. Irene’s top half was released from the vent, and fell two stories, straight onto me. I don’t know why I didn’t move. I wish I had. Instead I was struck to the floor, my coworker’s torso on top of me. I’m not sure if the crack I heard was her body or mine, but I felt it regardless. Something popped in my chest. I rolled Irene off of me, and clutched at the pain. I looked up and saw the exit. Safety. I lurched to my feet with a loud grunt, and sprinted. Even in my delirium I could hear them. The quick pitter patter of footsteps almost matching mine, now accompanied by something else. Laughter. Giddy and excited. Directly in my ear, the voice an amalgamation of small child and old dog. It only stopped once I sent my own body careening through the doors.

I tumbled onto the sidewalk and felt a spike of pain through my chest. I can’t tell you why, but the ache reminded me of what Josh specifically told me: “locked up by eleven.”

LOCKED UP.

I spun to look at the door, and saw it slowly creeping open, that horrible giggling catching on the frigid wind. I threw myself against it, fumbling for my keys with hands that wouldn’t stop shaking. I felt something pushing, forcing, then slamming against the other side of the glass, but looking through it revealed no source. The key finally found its way into the slot, and with one final push, I turned the lock. With that, all movement stopped. Nothing pried against the door or banged on the windows. Everything was still. I tried to take a step back, but my leg gave out and I fell on my ass.

I instinctively patted my pockets looking for my phone, but stopped once I saw the solid beam of light illuminating Irene’s corpse. I cried, but I couldn’t look away. I had known her for years. She had a son, her ex-husband sucked, she had a passion for helping people, only matched by her love of baseball. And now she was this. A heap of flesh smattering the floor of a job she never wanted, but made the most of. 

My recollection was cut short as both of her halves began to twitch. I hadn’t realized Irene was still affixed to the ceiling by what I guess were intestines, which were slowly being pulled back. Her body went from flat on the floor to limply hanging as it was dragged back to the vent. Her lower half was being squeezed further into the shelf, the chunks of her waist that didn’t fit ripping free against the polished oak.

My body retched. I turned away, tears freezing to my face. I couldn’t see any more. I had to find help. I would run to the police station if I had to. This was all on the security tapes. Someone had to have seen this. Someone would have to help me. As soon as I had the thought, I spotted a patrol car. Parked at the end of the lot. I ran so fast I basically flew. I hit the window without slowing down, scaring the sleeping officer so badly he smacked his head on the roof. I pounded on the door and pointed behind me. Whatever I said in the moment, I don’t recall.

He didn’t get out. He didn’t call for back up. He didn’t even roll down his window. He looked past me, at the store itself. He pulled out his phone. He squinted as it lit up his face, and shook his head. He held it up to me. 11:02pm. He then started up his car, and drove away. Leaving me, and the store, in the still.

The first thing I did when I got home was turn on all of the lights. I fumbled through my closet to find my sling from high school. I decided the doctor could wait until I sent my resignation, or at the very least had some answers. I had the whole thing typed out, but I couldn’t bring myself to hit send. 

“Don’t quit. It’s in my closet.”

That text spelled out my prison sentence. I was left painfully aware of my dresser. Even now, typing this, I feel like I can make eye contact with it. But at that point, I was still in the denial stage.

“Josh worked there for years. Maybe this thing needs time to latch on. I’ll probably be fine.”

“It probably can’t follow me if I haven’t invested in my 401k yet.”

“I haven’t even utilized my health plan. It definitely can’t get me.”

I told myself a lot of lies to process what I’d seen. Anything to convince myself I could send that email. I stared blankly into my laptop, begging the world to give me an out. But it gave me a push notification instead. 

“Hi Sam! Rebecca from HQ calling! I wanted to reach out regarding onboarding issues, and some confusion as to your closing duties as manager. Do you have time for a chat tomorrow?”

I sat for a second. Hesitation gave way to excited relief. Corporate! Of course! Maybe they could help! They could reschedule me, or maybe move me to a different store. Nonetheless, this was my out!

“Hey Rebecca! I absolutely have time tomorrow! Thank you for taking time to stop in!”

I didn’t sleep much, but the wave of ease mixed with total exhaustion gave me a few hours. I woke up nervous, but excited. I wasn’t going to have to close that God-forsaken store ever again, and I was hopeful for answers as to what I’d been put through. As soon as I walked through the doors, I saw her.

Perfectly pressed suit, brunette hair tied back in a bun that seemed messy in an expertly crafted way. My gaze caught hers through chunky black-rimmed glasses.

“Sam! Perfect timing. Let’s walk, shall we?” She turned with a strut.

“Rebecca! I’m so happy to see you. I wanted to-” 

“Don’t worry,” she cut me off. “Once we’re in the office, we’ll have our little sesh.”

Her words were spoken sweetly, but my brow furrowed as the back door swung open. She followed me in, and sat me in a chair facing the desk. I was visibly nervous. Shaking and peering back at storage, waiting for something to swoop down and tear my head off. All while she displayed a perfect picture of calm.

“So I wanted to begin by talking about expectations, Sam.” She clasped her hands together.

“Wh…what?” I managed.

“When staff are seen as necessary as yourself, we always want to reward that. Upward mobility throughout our stores is not always offered, but once it is, there are certain requirements that must be met, you understand?” She tilted her head to the side.

“No… I- Rebecca you don’t-”

“I understand the operations of an establishment like this can be overwhelming at times, but complaints need to be filed through the proper channels. When handled on one’s own, it can often lead to mismanagement, which I fear we have already seen in your short tenure.” She brought out a note pad and pen from her suit’s pocket. I sat, trying to dissect what was being said to me.

“I would like to open up a dialogue, so that I can confirm what was instructed to you via your past supervisor, and see what I can encourage, and what needs tweaking.” She sat forward, pen in hand, suggesting it was my turn to talk.

“Rebecca…” I put my hands out, pleading. “I don’t know if you guys know what’s going on here. There’s something in this store, or it’s the place itself! Something- it- it got Irene! It-” She put out her hand. Stifling.

“Samuel, if I am left unaware of how you were instructed, there isn’t a path towards betterment for either party, you understand?” She sat upright. “So! What does Sam’s close look like?”

I was dumbfounded. I didn’t know what to expect going into this, but I was expecting something. Anything! Instead I was presented with nothing but jargon through pearlescent teeth. I thought this woman was my lifeline, but she spoke to me like she was bringing an HR complaint against me. She cleared her throat.

“Anything at all could truly be helpful.” She was a statue.

“I… I wave everyone out. At ten.” Rage clenched my fists

“Okay, perfect! And then?” She began writing

“I do the inventory I can…before lights out.” The rage gave way to realization

“Amaaazing. What next?” She didn’t look up from her notes.

“I leave and lock up. Before eleven.” Realization gave way to fear. I began to cry.

“Alright that all looks good! Now, recently, do you believe you’ve fallen short on any of these tasks?” Her eyes didn’t change at my tears.

“I-Irene…” I choked out her name before sobbing into my hand.

“Ah, that’s important to know. You see, Sam, seeing your employees off is very important! Both to make sure that the location is secure, but more importantly to ensure their safety! Parking lots are notoriously dangerous, and whether or not you’re paying attention could be the difference between life and death!”

I raised my head. All of her attention, all of her being, focused on what felt like my soul. That curated smile slipped for just a moment, and revealed something I can’t place, but that strikes a jolt of fear through me to this day.

“But… but I stayed! With Josh! He didn’t wave me out or-”

“Of course managerial onboarding requires hands-on experience! You were scheduled for that closing shift, correct?” She pressed the edge of her pen into my lanyard, clicking it on my picture. “This is the result,” *click*, “of proper,” *click* “procedures.”

Her smile shifted into something that asked, “Any more questions?” I let my head sink back down.

“Am I… when I-” I searched for anything else I could say, and from the safety of my hand I asked, “Can I quit?”

“We can always circle back to that once you’re absolutely certain.” I heard her fuss in her chair. “I’m so happy we could have this discussion!” She stood, sliding her paper back into its crevice. “Due to your shortsightedness, however, we will be assigning you an eight hour workshop. It’s all online, so you can get it done from the comfort of your couch! Or you could get started on your break!” She beamed, as she revealed my phone from the same pocket and placed it on the desk.

“Have a great rest of your shift, Sam. Don’t keep them waiting out there!” 

With that, her heels clicked across the tiles, and through the back door. 

I wanted to run. I wanted to scream, throw up, break a window, smash a car, punch something, kill someone, set this whole fucking place on fire! I wanted to do anything other than what I did. I swallowed that miasma of urges, stood up, and walked to the breakroom mirror. I really did look awful. I flashed a practice smile at myself, and headed out onto the floor.

I’ve tried would-be solutions over the past months. I messaged Josh dozens of times, but he’s in the wind. No social media to speak of since he quit. Not a great sign for me. I started therapy a month into the job, but it doesn’t help as much as a cigarette. I didn’t smoke before I got promoted, but when Irene’s son came in asking about her, I downed a pack. I’ve even tried the police a few more times, but every day is like she said: as many cops today as there have been any other. My collarbone is nearly healed, though, and my birthday is right around the corner, so it’s not all bad. As long as I wave everyone out, do inventory, and lock up by eleven, everything is going to be completely fine. If I’m being honest, whatever’s in the store is only half the reason I stay on top of it. I just really, really don’t want to see Rebecca again.
