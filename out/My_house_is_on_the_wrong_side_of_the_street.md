It’s exactly as the title of this post reads: my house is on the wrong side of the street. Well, I guess it “was”, but I’ll get to that later.

Yesterday, I took my dog for a walk around the neighborhood. I always walked her around sunset and the walk itself was only about a half an hour. However, this time as I walked down my street and the sky shone gold from the setting sun, something wasn’t right.

I’ve often heard that when you become familiar with a place, your brain subconsciously picks out things it remembers and things it doesn’t. That is why sometimes seeing a familiar place from a new perspective makes it seem totally unfamiliar. My street– I hardly recognized it.

Then I saw what it was. My house, which was always on the right side, was on the left side of the street. I approached my house and I actually did that thing they do in cartoons where they rub their eyes and then open them again as if that’ll change anything.

Well, nothing changed.

To the left was my “house” and to the right, where my house should’ve been, was an undeveloped lot. Although a sense of dread had overcome me, I felt myself moving forward slowly, and mechanically. Eventually, I felt a tug. I looked over my shoulder to see that my dog, Kali, hadn’t moved any closer and that “tug” that I’d felt was from the leash reaching its max length.

Kali began to growl as I tugged at the leash gently.

“Kali, it's okay, let’s go,”

But it wasn’t okay, and so I ended up listening to Kali.

“Alright, let’s get the hell outta here,”

And so we walked again. I don’t know why, but I figured that maybe if we just did the walk again something would change. My street is technically in a suburban area, but it’s pretty forested, so while on my street, I can’t see other streets. I’d always thought it nice, but as we walked down our street again, I started to have second thoughts.

“Damn,”

My house was still to the left, and in addition, the lights were all off. That wasn’t how I’d left them. It seemed that my street, in general, was even more fucked up the second time around. Pretty immediately, my mind connected that maybe going for a second walk had actually ruined things further. I didn’t know for sure, but I didn’t try it again.

This time I tried to make a call. I tried to make a call to my sister. Thankfully it rang, but unfortunately I never got an answer. I left a somewhat panicked voicemail asking if she was alright and then tried my best friend. He didn’t pick up either.

I looked around– the entire rest of the street seemed normal besides being oddly quiet. I walked to the top of my driveway.

Do I just go inside? Maybe I was the one acting strange? Has it always been this way? That seemed more likely than that my house had inexplicably moved.

*Bark!*

I jumped.

“Damn, Kali, you scared me,”

Kali was barking at the undeveloped lot that had replaced my house. She kept barking, and I stood frozen. I was trying to make out what it was she might’ve been barking at.

Nothing. There was nothing. No movement, no sound. Just eerie silence. Kali began to whimper and cowered low to the ground. She began moving towards me, never taking her eyes off the lot.

“Fuck it,”

I picked her up and quickly made my way onto my porch and then inside my house. Every light in my house was off, but I made good time turning them all on and locking every possible entrance into my house. Those moments in the darkness, just expecting something to be waiting in one of the dark corners, were the most confused, terrified moments of my life. However, nothing ever jumped out at me. My house was empty.

Kali found a place to hide under my couch, and I sat at my kitchen counter contemplating my next move. I didn’t feel the need to call the police. For some reason I felt that it was possible that I was the one who had this all wrong, but then my phone rang.

It was my sister. I picked it up and asked,

“Hey. Oh, it’s good to hear from ya’. I know this is going to sound crazy, but what side of the street is my house on? Like when, when, you’re walking down it.”

“You need to turn your lights back off,”

“What?”

“Your lights, turn them off,”

Her tone was flat, very matter-of-fact. It almost felt cold.

“I– I don’t understan–”

In my peripheral vision I watched something quickly drop out of view of my kitchen window. I wasn’t sure what I’d seen, or if I’d seen anything at all. I looked down at my phone and the call had ended.

*What should I do? Was that even my sister? If not, maybe I shouldn’t listen to her?*

I looked back to the window. I swore I’d seen something move away from it. Okay, I think I would turn my lights off. I moved towards my kitchen light switch, when underneath my feet I heard the creaking of footsteps. I stopped to listen.

*Creak.*

One single footstep and then they stopped as well. It wasn’t possible that someone was below me, because my house didn’t have a basement. And also, the footsteps sounded as if someone was walking inverted to me. Which definitely wasn’t possible.

*Creak. Creak. Creak.*

The footsteps picked up again, this time quicker and more distinct. I followed the footsteps from the kitchen to the living room. Up until this point, nothing in my house had changed besides its location.

The living room however.

All the furniture besides the couch and wall decor had been cleared out. I must not’ve noticed in my scramble to get all of the lights back on. The floor was just bare hardwood except for a door in the center of the room. And no, this wasn’t a trap door, or some sorta hatch. It was a regular wooden door laying horizontal to the floor around it. I heard the footsteps walk right up to the door, and I heard a knock.

There was a moment of silence, then another knock came, this time in a triplet.

“Kali, Kali, come on,”  
  
But she wasn’t coming out from under the couch.

The door opened. I couldn’t see where exactly the door opened into, but a light sniffle arose from it. Whatever was on the other side seemed to be crying. I backed out of the living room slowly, listening to every sniffle and sob that came from the door.

Suddenly a leg clothed in brown slacks and dress shoes casually moved through the door frame. I immediately moved out of the living room and skulked up the stairs trying not to make a sound. I remember whatever it was, it was holding a briefcase. I remember watching the leg and subsequent body that followed adjust to the shift in gravity in a manner that almost resembled gliding.

I made my way up the stairs and into my bedroom. I didn’t hesitate. I quietly opened my bedroom window and calculated the damage I’d take from the fall.

There was a knock on my bedroom door. Whatever it was, it was moving quickly.

I looked over my shoulder a little lost for what to do. Then I heard the thing’s triple knock, which previously had been the precursor to it just letting itself in, so I lowered myself out of my bedroom window and prepared to drop.

As my bedroom left my view, I saw the hallway light shine in as the door opened. Once again, I heard that damned crying. Moments before I let myself drop, I saw the start of a very tall man’s head wearing a bowler hat scraping along my bedroom ceiling.

I didn’t have much time to contemplate this though as I soon made contact with the ground. Thankfully, I landed okay and the adrenaline got me to my feet. I made a break for the road, and ran up it without looking back.

*Kali, please stay under that couch.*

As I neared the top of the street, I caught a glimpse into one of my neighbor’s houses. The front door was wide open, and hanging by a single hinge. The living room curtains were drawn and I could see blood splatters staining the wall in a morbid display.

*Oh, fuck.*  
  
I picked up my pace again and made it off of my street. However, I kept moving until I found a main road away from the street. It was then that I noticed that during all of this, the sun had never fully set. In fact, it seemed that it hadn’t moved at all.

Eventually, I found a gas station and sat out front for hours. It was just nice to be around people coming and going. I felt secure.

After some time, my friend came and picked me up. I stayed at his house for the night. I was unsure of what to tell him, but he’s a good guy and let me stay without needing a reason.

I slept horribly, and after some painfully long hours, morning came. Around noon we drove over to my house.

It was on the right side of the street. Just like normal.

I went inside cautiously. My friend must’ve thought I’d lost my mind. Kali was fine, but her leash was still on. I walked up the street and to my surprise, my neighbor’s house was empty– a “for sale” sign in the front yard. There was no sign of him. That wasn’t how I remembered it, and something inside me was scared to ask where he’d gone, or why he decided to sell.

I’m writing all of this now to share my experience. I have no clue what happened to me last night. It feels like I imagined the whole thing, but I know I didn’t. I haven’t really known the best way to go on with my day, so I spent the rest of it driving around with Kali.

Although as I’m writing this I’m getting a call from my friend. It seems he sent me a text too. Let’s see. It reads:

*Yo, what I’m about to ask is going to seem crazy, but just please answer me anyway.*
