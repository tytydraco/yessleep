Kerrywood forest burnt down last week. They say a group of teens were out camping when their campfire got out of control and with it being a particularly dry autumn night, the whole thing went up in a matter of hours. There was nothing the town could do but watch it go up and try to make sure it didn't spread to any of the surrounding fields. The whole thing has hit me pretty hard. Most of my treasured childhood memories were in that forest. God, that was all about twenty years ago. Everything is coming back with a particular bitterness now. Especially the whole business with Charlie.

 

 

I've been back in Kerrywood for a week. It's the longest I've been back since I left for college. Ive been staying at my mother's house, I still remember her phone call that night when she saw the forest burning from her window, she had told me she thought the world was ending. She's in her sixty's now and she often gets confused. It's not rare for her to enter a room only to have forgotten why she was there or what she had been doing just before. It's pretty obvious to everyone what's happening. Maybe she was onto something on the phone, it really does feel like everything's coming to an end right about now.

 

 

I took a walk through the remnants of Kerrywood forest the other day. I guess I hoped it would help with some of the stuff I'd been thinking about lately; I don't know how much help it ended up being. Its unrecognisable now. No tire swings or secret hideouts anymore, just charred bark. Although, I do think I saw the stump. The stump where we found that dammed "thing" or I suppose – it found us.

 



The night it happened Charlie had been 13, which would've made me about 14 or so, it's hard to remember. We were out much later than we should've been, and it was starting to get dark. There were 5 of us out that day: Me, Charlie, Matthew, Peter and Sam. We were debating heading back as none of us had any torches and Peter had become pretty adamant about it – he was known to be easily frightened. He once wet himself at a sleepover because of a thunderstorm and hadn't lived it down since. Since none of us were in a hurry to relive that "incident" we obliged and began on our way back. As it got darker it became notably harder to follow the trail back and Peter was sure we had gotten lost. Matthew had to on numerous occasions remind him that this was a walk we had done countless times before in the day and it hadn't changed just because it was now dark. It was about an hour more before we began to consider that Peter had been right. Nothing seemed familiar anymore, despite Sam's incessance that he "recognised that tree". As usual he was lying, every tree in there looked the same; tall and white with their branches and leaves creating a shroud obsucring even the little light we might've gotten from the moon and stars. We didn't have mobile phones at that age, so we were going to have to get out of this ourselves. Peter was in a frenzy now and even Matthew's bravado seemed to have subsided.  Charlie remained quiet however, as he often did, silently observing like he was worried of what he might say. He had on more than one occasion been picked on by Matthew and Sam for being “boring” or "dull".  That night, he just sort of stared into the dark with his wide blue eyes, like he was trying to will us a path back.





"We're never going to make it back!" Peter yelped, he had begun to cry now and refused to keep walking.

 "Come on don't be a dick it's getting cold" Matthew snarled trying to pull him back to his feet. An awful wind had picked up now whipping the branches and summoning an eerie whistle as it passed between the trees.

"Yeah Peter, you crying about it isn't going to help, is it?" Sam insolently added.

"Well, what are we going to do?" Peter continued through his sobbing.

"We're totally lost and it's not like-" He stopped dead.

 

 

There was something in the dark. A noise, like the crunching of leaves. Our lively crowd had now fallen silent and still. Matthew and Sam both looked poised to flee. Peter had retreated behind his hands, peering out into the black from between his fingers. Charlie continued to stare as he had done before, though I do think I could now see tears in those wide blue eyes. The silence continued like this for a time, sending my mind into a spiral, I grew desperate to break it.

"What was that" I pleaded in a hushed whisper.

A long winding moan now, reaching from the same spot in the dark. I was sure I could see something moving out there in the black; an impassable screen projecting my cruellest imaginings. Matthew and Sam skipped back a few steps closing our pack tighter. We were all shaking now.

"A wolf!" Sam rasped. There were no wolves in Kerrywood.

"Shut up!... Just shut up!" Peter was now inconsolable, hiding his face in his shirt and clasping his knees, he was rocking back and forth.

Again, a crunch of leaves followed by a painful whine.

"It's getting closer!" Sam pleaded turning to run before Matthew grabbed him by his sleeve and pulled him back.

"Wait! Did you hear that?" Matthew returned a few steps forward.

Crunch... Moan... Crunch... Moan. It went on like that at nauseum, but it didn't get any closer. Nothing came out of the dark. I was unsure what I was meant to be hearing until-

"Is that a voice?" Peter sputtered, now to his feet. He looked as though he had found his saviour.

Charlie had taken lead of the troop now, descending further into the dark. I tried to call him back, but he didn't seem to pay my pleads any mind; he was like a man possessed.

"I think it said my name"

I believe that silently none of us wanted to follow Charlie that day, but more so, we didn't want to be the one left behind. We all followed.

 

 

Falling into the dark together now; it didn't seem to give way as it did before. I could barely see my own feet. I only had the others panicked breaths to tell me I wasn't alone. The voice to tell me where I was going. I became suddenly aware at how long we had been walking, my legs felt weak and my thighs burned. A few times I felt as though I was falling, giving into my exhaustion, only for my other foot to miraculously find the ground. I wanted to call out again, beg to turn back. But my voice came out a raspy squeak - it felt like someone was sitting on my chest. The voice was clearer now; it was numerous voices. The sound was of a radio quickly skipping between channels. The crunch and whine. Suddenly, the dark seemed to give way, like we'd passed through an ocean of ink. In its place was a tight packing of trees, the centre of which was a stump. Cut so cleanly at its base and atop it was in fact a small portable radio. We did not come out of the dark together in a line as I had imagined we had been just a moment before, but rather from separate points in the shadow as if forming something of a circle around the radio. It hadn’t been each other that we had followed through the pitch-black.

  


"What is it?" I could finally speak. The weight had gone from my chest and with it my exhaustion.

"A radio." Sam impotently replied.

"Well yeah, but why is it playing that song?" Peter was now closest to the radio, his earlier terror seemed now to have happened to someone else.

A song? There was no song. The radio was merely skipping between stations, each as indiscernible as the last, with a voice so toneless whereas I could not place the age or gender of the speaker.  Matthew seemed to share my confusion.

"What song Peter? What are you talking about?"

Peter didn't seem to hear him. He just stayed facing the radio, swaying now. He looked like he was about to throw up.

"Peter! What song! What are you-"

I could no longer hear Matthhew. The radio had suddenly gotten so loud. It became increasingly hard to stand, like someone was beating me over the head. Peter hit the dirt hard. The monotone voice had seemingly grown into one constant pitch in my mind; it sounded like my mother. Everything went black.

 

 

I didn't wake until morning, except perhaps once. Still now I think I may have dreamt it, but that night I was sure I had woken and seen Charlie. Though seeing seems the wrong word. I still felt that my eyes were closed and my head was buried in the dirt yet all the same I was looming over Charlie. He was sat cross legged in front of the stump and that radio. He didn't turn to face me, and I didn't talk to him but still he appeared to answer my question.

"Can’t you hear them? They're screaming"

 

 

The next thing I remember is waking to someone shaking me. My mouth was dry and full of dirt. Above me was my mother jostling me awake and crying. Around her were an assortment of friends and family - each clad in red hi-vis. She told me that they had been looking for us since late last night and all through the day. She asked me if I had seen where Charlie had gone. He was no longer with us, and neither was the radio.

 

 

The four of us all went home to get washed before rejoining the town in the search for Charlie. We were out there till 10 that night. We were only sent home when, deeper in the forest, we had heard screaming. It was Charlies parents – they had found him.
