Dinner has been getting awkward lately.

Dad barely speaks. Mom keeps looking at me with this strange expression, and my little sister has stopped telling me about her day.

I don't know what I did.

Tonight, I made spaghetti. Mom's favorite.

I called everyone downstairs.

Dad came first. He sat at the head of the table, folded his hands, and stared at the food.

Mom followed.

Emily came last, dragging her slippers across the floor.

"You're late," I told her.

She didn't answer.

I served everyone.

Nobody touched their food.

"Is something wrong?" I asked.

Dad looked at Mom.

Mom looked at Emily.

Then all three looked at me.

I laughed awkwardly.

"Okay. What's going on?"

Nobody answered.

I hate it when they do this.

A few days ago, I saw Mrs. Collins outside our house. She's lived across the street for as long as I can remember.

I waved at her.

She immediately went inside.

I thought it was strange, so I went over to ask if everything was okay.

She opened the door just a little.

When she saw me, her hand started shaking.

"Please," she whispered.

I asked what was wrong.

She closed the door.

People have become so unfriendly.

Even the man who delivers our groceries refuses to come inside anymore. He leaves everything on the porch and waits until I close the door before driving away.

I miss how things used to be.

Emily used to follow me everywhere.

She'd sit in my room while I played games, borrow my clothes, and ask me ridiculous questions.

Now she spends most of her time staring at the hallway.

Yesterday, I asked her what she was looking at.

She said, "Nothing."

Then she asked me something.

"Do you ever think about that night?"

I told her not to bring it up.

She went quiet.

I don't like talking about it.

I remember the argument.

I remember Dad standing up from his chair.

I remember Mom crying.

I remember Emily telling me to stop.

And I remember the house becoming terribly quiet afterward.

Sometimes I wish I could go back and do things differently.

But what's done is done.

Tonight, I tried something different.

I bought a cake.

I put four candles on it and brought it to the dining table.

"Come on," I said. "Let's have a proper family night."

Mom started crying.

Dad wouldn't look at me.

Emily just stared at the candles.

I blew them out myself.

For a moment, I could almost hear them laughing again.

Then someone knocked on the front door.

I got up to answer it.

A man in a uniform stood outside.

He didn't look happy to see me.

"Your counselor is waiting."

I sighed.

"Can he come back tomorrow? It's my family's anniversary."

The man glanced past me at the dining table.

Four plates.

Four chairs.

One untouched cake.

Then he looked at me.

"Your family has been dead for eleven months."

I didn't answer.

He waited.

"You're still having these conversations?"

I looked back at the table.

Mom was crying again.

Dad had his head down.

Emily was looking straight at me.

I turned back to the man.

"They're my family."

He nodded slowly.

Then he said something I'd heard many times before.

"You know why you're here."

I looked down at my hands.

I remembered the night the police arrived.

I remembered the three people they carried out of our house.

And I remembered the fourth person they found sitting at the dining table.

Me.

The man waited for me to follow him.

Before leaving, I looked back one last time.

Emily was smiling now.

For the first time in eleven months, she looked happy.

I smiled back.

**I hope they forgive me someday.**
