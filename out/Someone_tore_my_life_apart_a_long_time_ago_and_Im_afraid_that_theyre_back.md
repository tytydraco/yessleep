Hi, Reddit. I’m not sure how to begin, so I’d like to just dive straight in.

A long time ago, someone single-handedly turned my life upside down. Only a handful of people know the whole story because after it happened, I did all I could to disappear. I know I might sound a bit dramatic, but it’s the truth.

I thought what had happened was behind me though. I thought life had moved on… but then, just earlier today, it felt like my past came knocking at my door.

 

Before I get into what happened this morning, I want to explain what happened years ago. For you to understand how terrifying my experience was, I need you to experience it with me. So please bear with me as I relive my most vivid nightmare.

 

\-           -           -

 

It’s 3:49 P.M. on a Wednesday, and I’m at work. I am the producer of the biggest radio show in my city, and I’m fielding answers for today’s *Manic Marty’s Mind Benders*.

Normally Marty—one of the two show hosts—just shows up with three random pop culture questions he came up with earlier in the day, but after weeks of pestering him, he caved and used the questions I came up with. Probably because today’s prize is two tickets to the opera—a Zonk Prize as far as he’s concerned.

I scan a long list of emails, most of which are time-wasters. Messages like, “I’m a big fan of the show,” or “Been listening to you guys since the nineties!” Fan mail. Digital trash.

I separate the wheat from the chaff as quickly as I can. Marty will involve me in their nonsense any moment and expect me to have some clever comeback. Those with real answers are moved to my temp folder. I can begin reading them during the upcoming ad break.

“Oh, she’d love that,” I hear Marty say. He’s not talking about me, right?

I look up from my computer monitor.

He continues, “I think all women love bouquets and chocolates and all the other bull crap Hallmark shoves down their throats. Whaddayou think, Tiff?”

Like the soundboard is an extension of my body, my fingers zip to the unmute button. “Oh, you know me, Marty,” I answer. “I’m too much of a minimalist to want all that.”

Aaron—the other show host—cuts in, “But if it’s shoved down your throat on Valentine’s Day, you’ll tolerate it, right?”

*Prick*, my brain exclaims as my mouth says, “Hallmark and I aren’t that close.”

Marty laughs and says something I do my best to ignore. A few more seconds of the clowns riffing as I review some more answers, and then I cut to commercial.

Marty and Aaron keep joking around as I search for the first email that provides the correct answers.

Wrong. Wrong. Wrong. Wrong. And then an outlier.

Not only did this listener give the right answers, but the email is formatted cleanly. It’s written like a formal letter. Far too sophisticated for one of our listeners.

My upper lip is hooked into a sneer as I look at the correct answers provided in an indented, numbered list, sandwiched between a greeting and closing remarks. It’s not an exceptionally long email, but it’s so official, it could be mistaken for an email from the station owner.

Since I won’t have to review any other submissions, I can’t resist spending a few more seconds to read the winner’s full email.

 

*Dear Tiffany,*

*This is my first time writing to the show, and it is to provide the answers to today’s Manic Marty’s Mind Bender. Normally, the answers are so painfully obvious that I never bother to submit a response. Today’s, however, were thought-provoking. I couldn’t help but suspect you were the one who formulated them. Here are the answers:*

 

Below the numbered list, he continues,

 

*To tell the truth, I had only ever stumbled upon this obnoxious show by mere happenstance. It played in the background at my local barber shop. Oh, how it grated my nerves the way the imbecilic hosts prattled on about whatever nonsense entered their minds.*

 

I crack a smile. It’s far from the way I’d phrase it, but whoever this is has a similar opinion of this show’s “talent.”

The ad break is almost over. I don’t finish reading the email, but I make a mental note to later.

Before the break ends, I give the hosts a heads up. “Got a winner, guys.”

Marty throws a thumb up and Aaron nods, all smiles.

We’re live.

“Alright,” Marty begins, “I’ve been informed by top government officials that we’ve found day’s super genius.”

“That right?” Aaron plays along.

“Yep. Tiff, you wanna key us in? Who’s the latest mastermind to grace our humble, little show?”

I muster a cheerful tone and find the signature near the bottom of the email. “A mister Benjamin Thaliez.”

“Brilliant man,” Marty states dryly.

“Truly,” Aaron agrees.

“Well then, congratulations, Thaliez, my man. I’m sure a man of your caliber, your *wit*, will have an incredible time at the opera.”

“I’m beyond jealous,” Aaron adds.

“Yeah, me too. Guess I’ll just have to suffer through another Sunday night football game.”

The two go back and forth in their usual way a few more times before Marty asks, “Hey, Benny Boy remembered to include his address, right?”

We often have to remind listeners to do so, otherwise we can’t send them their prize. But I had already seen that this submission included an address below the signature, so I reply confidently, “Yep!” At the same time, my eyes return to the address; one final confirmation. But something’s wrong. That can’t be right.

Marty and Aaron continue bantering in the background as I reread the two short lines of text over and over.

The address at the bottom of the email is mine.

My eyes drift up to the final paragraph. The only bit I hadn’t read yet.

 

*But then I heard you and it was as though everything else ceased to exist. You are clever. Composed. Intelligent. And after a bit of research, I see that you are beautiful as well. Let me take care of you. Be mine, and I’ll make your life a dream.*

 

My skin tingles and my blood becomes ice water as questions flood my mind.

*Who is this? Is this for real? How did this person get my address? Is this a prank?*

I read the email again. Check the sending address. The subject line. I scour my memory for the name *Benjamin Thaliez*. Nothing.

Everything around me becomes silent and still. The world halts. Or so it feels.

“Tiffffffanneeeeeeee,” Aaron drones. His voice brings me back. I blink a few times, forcing the email out of view and meeting his expectant gaze. “Do you *know* what song we have lined up?”

Marty snickers. “Do you see the way she’s ogling her monitor, Aaron? If I’ve told you once, I’ve told you a thousand times, Tiff. Do not browse smut on the work computer!”

I force a chuckle and apologize before introducing the next track. It’s already lined up so all I have to do is press play.

As soon as we’re off-air, Aaron’s tone sharpens. “Think you can lock in for two full hours today, Tiff?”

I try to swallow, but my mouth is dry as dust. “Sorry, I—”

“Can you imagine that?” Marty interjects. “Working *that* long uninterrupted?”

They laugh. Even without an audience, they’re all jokes.

I clench my jaw. Is it worth telling them? Would they offer any sympathy? Would they even care?

Finally, I smile. I’m good at that. Pretending everything is fine. “Not to worry, fellas. Little distraction, that’s all.”

In the email account, I make a new folder and drop Benjamin’s message inside. I’ll forward it to someone who might give a shit later. For now, it’s showtime.

While the tracks play, Marty and Aaron chat as they always do, and I push my discomfort down. I push it down deep enough to smother it.

 

When we return, Marty and Aaron make fun of me for missing their cue to play the next song. They start off playfully enough, but their nails start to dig into my skin.

“Well,” Aaron says, “let’s be fair. She’s not in her natural habitat.”

“No?” Marty follows.

“I mean, look around! There’s no oven. No laundry for her to fold.”

“Oh, true!”

“Tiff, sweetheart, it’s not your fault. We have put no effort into accommodating you.”

“Let’s make a few calls and get this fixed today.”

“Yeah, we don’t want this to become a regular thing.”

New bit. Improvised, but I know where they’re going. They’ll want me to field a few calls, get random listeners to join in.

Agitation smolders in my chest. These two grate my nerves like no one else. Normally I can fight back, make an amusing quip, but my mood is shot. My thoughts are elsewhere.

I look back at the computer monitor. More emails coming in. People offering to install a washer and dryer in the studio. Others just being nasty. Cruel. I’m allowed to blacklist those ones, and I relish the opportunity.

Wait… Near the top, second to latest email. The sender’s name makes my throat clasp shut.

 

*From: Benjamin Thaliez*

 

I open it quickly, wishing for the contents to reveal that it’s from an old friend or employee at the station screwing with me. But instead, it reads,

 

*Tiffany,*

*You have grown so quiet.*

*Will you please speak again?*

*Be calm, even if your heart refuses.*

*Mine is racing.*

 

Something heavy forms in my chest and sinks slowly toward my gut. This email is so different from the last one, worded far more strangely; it makes me nervous. And then I see the true message. The one he must have known I’d notice.

*Tiffany… You… Will… Be… Mine.*

My face grows hot. I want to cry. To release the anxiety and anger and fear. But something inside me perseveres. It insists that the show must go on.

After all, if this is some sick joke, crying would let the freak win.

And if it’s real, I don’t want to show my fear. I *can’t* show my fear.

“Oh, absolutely!” Marty shouts. “I say we talk to anyone who thinks they can help.”

“But first?” Aaron says.

“Let’s play a song.”

They made the cue extra obvious this time.

I don’t look at them. I just play the next track.

As soon as I do, Aaron strikes. “Do you have something better to do there, Tiff?”

And Marty piles on. “You know, we could always just sit here quietly if you’d prefer. I’m sure our audience wouldn’t mind.”

My chin trembles and a tear forms in my eye, but I hold it back. “Guys, I’m sorry, but some freak is harassing me. Alright?” My voice cracks, just a little. Just enough that if I was speaking to someone who cared, they’d realize just how upset I am.

Instead, it’s Marty and Aaron.

“Come on, Tiff,” Marty starts softly, “I know Aaron’s a bit clingy, but ‘freak’ is a bit harsh, don’tcha think?”

Aaron isn’t as playful. “Here’s a crazy idea. If someone’s harassing you, then just blacklist them!” He throws his hands up while giving Marty a bewildered look. “I mean, seriously, how hard is that?”

Marty laughs, and the two poke fun at me as I add Benjamin’s latest email to the folder I had created earlier. Before Aaron had suggested the obvious, I had already blocked Benjamin Thaliez. But that doesn’t change the fact that he has my address. Or that he wants me to *be his*. I shudder.

And then I realize that there’s one more email. One that must have gotten through before I blacklisted him.

Curiosity gets the better of me. I need to know what this creep had to say.

I open it.

 

*The one on the living room wall.*

 

That’s it? I look the email up and down. There’s nothing else there. Just an incomplete thought. Was he trying to say something else? Did he know I was going to block him, so he was in a rush?

Marty’s voice is pointed. I sense that he’s talking to me. “Less than a minute ‘til we’re live. Think you can stop drooling over your secret admirer for a li’l while?”

I paint a smile on my lips and feign a laugh. “Oh, I think I can manage.”

Play along. Act like I’m not upset. It’s the best I can do right now. Hopefully it’ll at least drain the fun from it for them.

Phone calls are coming in, but I haven’t been fielding them. Every line is waiting to be answered. I rush to answer this one and that. Marty and Aaron are willing to talk to almost anyone for material, so I just need to make sure the caller has a personality and promises not to say anything inappropriate. The songs are over and the hosts are already rambling when I find the first guest.

“Okay, Harvey,” I tell the man on the phone, “I’m gonna put you through in just a few seconds. Hang tight.”

He starts to say something, but back on hold he goes.

As stressed out as I am, my timing is still perfect. Aaron and Marty try to segue to their bit before I answer the next caller.

“Well,” Aaron says, “let’s find out. Tiff, has anyone called in yet?”

Sounding excited, I reply, “Tons! The lines have been lighting up, guys.” I take the first guest off hold and introduce him. “We have Harvey here who says he has a kitchenette he’s willing to donate!”

The hosts hoot and holler before they chat with Harvey, who eagerly joins in on the ‘fun.’

Even if they talk to this guest for a minute and a half, they’ll want at least one other caller. If he’s boring, they’ll want to bring in someone else quickly, so I waste no time getting to the next line.

“Hi,” I say after flipping to the next line, “thanks for holding. What do you want to contribute to the show?” The question is purposefully vague. It helps me know whether the caller is quick and at least moderately interesting.

A man’s voice grumbles through my earpiece. “The show?” he asks.

“Yep. We’re looking for callers to talk about kitchen or laundry renovations in the studio.”

“Oh,” he mutters, sounding disappointed.

My index finger hovers over the next line. I can already tell this guy won’t fit.

But before I can tell him, he says, “I was hoping to discuss the family portrait.”

I wince as I try to recall Marty or Aaron mentioning anything about that. Could they have lined up another bit when I wasn’t listening?

“Sorry,” I say, “you’ll have to remind me. What family portrait are you referring to?”

The man doesn’t respond immediately. I’d almost thought he disconnected, but I realize can hear him breathing.

When he speaks again, his voice is a stoic baritone. “You know the one.”

At first, I shake my head. This guy is just weird. But then a chill trickles down the back of my neck, and the words from the last email flash before my eyes.

 

*The one on the living room wall.*

 

My jaw becomes slack as I picture my home. An old family portrait from my senior year in high school—me, my sister, my parents, and our dog—hangs on the wall to the left of the TV. It’s the only picture of myself I have on display.

It can’t be seen from outside. The only window in the living room is on the same wall.

You’d have to be inside my house to see it.

My eyes are glued to the base of the telephone. My grip tightens around the handset while the rest of my body turns numb.

I whisper, “Where are you calling from?”

My heart is kicking and bucking in my chest so forcefully, I fear it’s going to burst. The silhouette of a man forms in my imagination, standing in my living room, using the same phone I use when I call my parents.

His breaths become heavier. Faster. He’s getting *excited*.

“Come home soon, Tiffany,” he says before I hear the click of the handset being placed in its cradle.

My mind becomes a hive, a chaotic swarm of thoughts I can’t keep track of. The phone slips from my trembling hand and clatters onto the table.

Muffled voices say something in the background, but I make no effort to hear them. Panic possesses me; it makes me stand up and look around the production room as though some solution is hiding in one of the corners.

The cable attached to my headphones doesn’t stretch far enough. It yanks tight. Something falls before I feel the auxiliary cable snap out of its port.

“Whoa! Hey!” one of the boys shout from the other side of the glass. They’re yelling at me. Mocking me. Insulting me.

I feel like I’m going to puke. I don’t know what to do or where to go. My face grows hot as fresh tears stream down my cheeks.  
“The hell is wrong with her?”

I can’t catch my breath. My chest tightens as I gasp for air.

“It’s weird how she loses her mind around the same time every month, isn’t it?”

I turn and lock eyes with Marty. Then with Aaron. I see their amusement. Their arrogance.

“Just relax. We’ll find a chore for ya in a minute or two. Alright?”

That’s when I break.

Along with numerous pieces of studio equipment.

 

\-           -           -

 

That was over twenty years ago. Later on, the police confirmed someone broke into my house. Whoever was taunting me stole my underwear, a few perfume bottles, my hair brush, and a few other things, but the culprit was never found. Unsurprisingly, Benjamin Thaliez was not the man’s true name.

I moved in with my sister on the other side of the city that night. There was no way I was staying in that house alone after that.

Afterward, the station owner made me an offer. He wouldn’t sue me for the broken equipment so long as I quit silently. I know it was a bum deal, but honestly, I didn’t care. I didn’t want to risk letting Ben—or whatever his real name was—call me again.

It was naive of me to assume he’d leave me alone after I left though. A few days later when I went back home to pick up a few things, I saw that he had returned. There was a note slipped under my door and letters without postage in my mailbox.

Over the course of just a few weeks, this stalker found ways to communicate with me through every possible means. Email, AOL messenger, phone calls, letters. *Everything*.

He was like a shark circling his prey. And no matter how many times I called the police, they kept telling me the same thing.

“Unless you can identify him, there’s nothing we can do.”

Of course, I knew that the day I could identify him, it’d be too late.

I couldn’t sleep. I was terrified to be alone or go out in public. In less than a month, it felt like my entire life fell apart.

So I left it behind.

I moved multiple states away and used a fake name. You can call it rash or cowardly or whatever else, but as far as I was concerned, it was the only way I could feel safe again.

And it worked. I hadn’t heard from him since. Although I was in an unfamiliar city living a life that wasn’t mine, it felt like things were normal again.

 

For all I know, I still am safe. But I received a strange letter today, and it seemed… familiar. Ominously so. I keep telling myself that I’m overthinking it. That the similarities between this letter and the ones from two decades ago are nothing more than a coincidence.

Whatever the case may be, it felt good to finally share my story.

 

I’m including the contents of the letter that I received this morning below. It had no postage.

 

*Ms. \[REDACTED\],*

 

*I hope you are doing well. You may not remember me, but our paths crossed quite some time ago. I found your company to be quite pleasant, and I thought it’d be good that we keep in touch. Sadly, I neglected to offer as much during our exchange, and when it occurred to me to do so, I missed where you had gone.*

 

*Found your residence by complete happenstance just the other week though! I happened to be driving past your lovely home when you were outside tending to your garden. It seemed to me to be kismet!*

 

*You may not agree (especially if you do not recall our prior encounter), but I thought it would be nice to get to know one another. Oh, as I write this letter, I sense how silly and strange it must seem to you. But I hope you give me a chance. I’ll be in touch soon.*

 

 *- Elizabeth Jinman*
