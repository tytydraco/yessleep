I’m sorry to those who’ve been following this story, but this entry is going to be short. Remembering everything that happened is painful, and the sooner I get it out the sooner I can try to deal with the horror I’m experiencing now. My psychiatrist and I have made a lot of progress, but these events wouldn’t make sense to her without putting the thought that I’ve relapsed in her head. So, now’s probably the best time to tell you all I’m thankful for whoever reads these. Even if no one does, it *is* helping me manage by getting everything off my chest.

The following week was awful. 

God, it was hell. Sunday was a buffer from the factory before work the following day. I didn’t mind it at the time, it gave me an excuse to use more of my stash. Work was the same as always on Monday. Coming in late and sleep deprived despite the comatose night before, just for my manager to chew me out.

“God dammit John, I swear one more day and you’ll have to find employment elsewhere.” One of his many empty threats. I didn’t listen, instead tried to remember if I took the time to brush my teeth as I threw on my dirty apron and walked to the grill . I always hated my dead end job, and I hated getting scolded about showing up late to a restaurant that had no busy hours. My one bedroom apartment wasn’t going to pay for itself though, and despite Gerald’s threats having little weight to them, it would be hard to find somewhere else that would hire me.

I made the same vow of showing up on time that I always failed to keep eventually. My mind wandered as I cleaned the grill. Thoughts of concrete and ink swirled around in a quiet tempest as I impelled the scraper across its surface. I hardly noticed my coworker Jane trying to spark conversation with me out of boredom. I usually jumped at the opportunity to talk to her, but my mind was too forcibly occupied elsewhere to pay attention to any small talk she attempted to make. She must’ve realized it was pointless to steer my focus, because she eventually stopped trying and we continued our shift in silence, occasionally broken by the sizzling of the grill or the mechanisms of the register. 

It wasn’t until Tuesday when the headaches started. Scolding hot knives periodically stabbing my temple. I’ve gotten a lot of migraines over the years and I thought I’ve gotten used to them, but these ones were bad. Really bad, some of the most unimaginable pain ever. Each one was accompanied with a strong desire of relief, nothing out of the ordinary there. It was the part of me that knew where I’d find relief that felt unnatural. That large crack in the wall with big bubble letters over it lived at the edge of my subconscious like a faded stain on my frontal lobe. It’d be so easy to take some whiskey collecting dust in my cabinet and drive down to the factory, I thought.

Visions of that dread from Saturday night suddenly hit me. The timer hitting zero, the world becoming a Stygian gray. I had hardly noticed my hands trembling on the cabinet doors. “Just a few more days.” I said to myself. “I just have to last a few more days.” As pleasurable as Paradise was, I just didn’t feel comfortable yet entering its gates alone. I can’t imagine why I felt this way. Maybe the allure was fading with the time I was spending away, being stripped slowly to reveal the horror that it truly was. Maybe the headaches and further suffering I would endure was its way of punishing me. But I am no stranger to withdrawal, and I am capable of going through great deals of pain in waiting for the remedy. So I simply pushed through agony as the week progressed at an eternal pace. 

I used more dope than I normally would throughout the week, but it hardly had an actual effect anymore. Every time I closed my eyes, its gravity pulled at my soul like shackles. My dreams were solely of that place, serving as reminders of the wonders that awaited me in that rotted building. I called off of work on Thursday, the headaches were leaving me bedridden and I struggled to pinch the syringe into the vein of my arm. It did little to mend my suffering, however it did offer me some rest. 

A split second of seared blood vessels, followed by a rush of dopamine. I could feel my limbs slowly warming up, and my eyes fluttered a brief moment before the headache became a distant memory and I passed into a deep sleep. I dreamt I was in paradise again, this time with my brother. We laughed together and poked at the artwork to see what reactions we’d get. I barely remember it thinking back on it, but it was the best dream I’d ever had.

I miss Ricky a lot.

In waking, for the first time in a long time, I questioned how I let myself get to where I was. Through pain streaked tears, I thought of my brother. I fumbled around in my drawer only for disappointment to set in when I realized I blew through my entire stash. I normally bought enough to last more than a week. Shit, I thought and I quickly pulled out my phone. Tapping the screen, I dialed a phone number and listened to the dial tone until Randy picked up. 

“What’s up dude?” His voice came through much calmer than mine.

“Hey, uh, do you think you can bring me my fix tomorrow? I’ll bring cash.” My voice was shaking.

“Yeah, sure. Hey, you mind if I bring my brother with us?” He replied. “What?” I asked confused. “Paradise dude, you mind if my brother comes with us? I’ve been telling him about it and he’s a ‘seeing is believing’ kinda guy.” I heard him produce a sharp inhale from his nose. 

“Yeah, sure, whatever. Just bring my fix, I feel like I’m going through hell.” I snapped back at him.

“Okay okay dude, I got it. I’ll catch you tomorrow, same time right?” I massaged my temple before replying , “Yeah, I’ll see you at 9:00”.

The headache came back, not nearly as harsh as before. Finally, a little relief, even if it was faint. I caught a whiff of dirty socks and body odor, most certainly coming from me. Geez, when was the last time I took a shower? I told myself to get out of bed and my body begrudgingly listened. My laundry basket was a mix of clean and dirty clothes, so with some detective work from the good ol’ sniff test I pulled what I’m sure was wearable and ambled towards my bathroom.

I really didn’t notice it at first. Hell, I had my hand on the doorknob before I heard it. It wasn’t really clear, I put my ear to the door to really make out what it was. But, from the other side of the door, I could hear crying. 

I recognized who it was too, and *that* realization planted my ass on the ground and plummeted my heart into hell. I swear on my life it was Ricky. Each hic and sob inched my spine closer to the opposing wall. Bit by bit I crawled backwards, unable to fully process if the experience was truly reality or not, until my back finally hit the wall. The dull thud it made instantly stopped the crying. The silence that followed was so deafening and long, I wondered if I was in a limbo of suffering. 

“…John?…” His voice was a spear that pierced through the door and straight into my heart.

I didn’t respond.

“…John, are you there?”

I sealed my lips. 

“John? John, I'm not happy. I can’t be happy, I don’t know what’s wrong with me.”

My face flushed with heat and my eyes welled up in tears.

“You can be happy though, you… just gotta go back. You have to go back to Paradise…”

I was too afraid to make a sound, but I was helpless against the muffled cries that escaped my lips.+

“It’s okay Johnny, everything will be okay. You plan on going back, right? Tomorrow? Everything will be fine tomorrow, I know it.”

I knew it was a hallucination, some fucked up symptom of the peculiar withdrawal I had been experiencing. I couldn’t help it though, I wish I could but I couldn’t.

“Ricky, why did you do it? I’m sorry, I-I wish I knew. You should’ve told me- I.. I would’ve done anything.” The words crawled out my mouth in between heavy sobs. The effort it took to say them forced the breath from my lungs, and I brought air back into them with one painful gasp.

“I’m just so sorry, if only I knew. Do you have any idea what you put us through? What you put ME through?!”

Silence…

“Mom barely talks to me anymore, and Dad disregards the fact that he even had children!”

Again, silence…

I was a mess, tears covered my burning face and no matter how frantically I wiped I couldn’t dry it.

“…it’s okay John. Go back tomorrow, all your pain will wash away…” suddenly, his response was cut short by a huge crash that shot me out of my skin. Nearly half an hour passed before I could pull myself up and hobble over to the bathroom. I rested my hand on the knob and gently leaned my ear against the wooden door. 

The faint sound of leather stretching under the weight of something heavy left a note of dread hanging in the air. Goosebumps covered me from head to toe in a cold sweat and my grip on the doorknob faltered.  I dared not to open the door, cause I knew what I’d see. Something I refused to confront again. Something that took years of saturating my brain in chemicals to forget the image burned into the past.



I didn’t leave my apartment, I don’t know why. Despite wanting to be anywhere else, I simply went to lay in my bed. I didn’t sleep, instead I waited until Friday morning before my body couldn’t handle the lack of rest.

I woke up later just in time to enjoy Paradise.
