Entry one

**My boots are wet.** 

  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  


**My foot came down harder than I intended, repetitively stomping on the wet concrete below. Today, the world around me seemed grayer than ever. The clouds in the sky were darker, the people I encountered were meaner, and the world was crueler. Maybe I was just nervous. It is a big day after all. I just had to keep my head down and my feet moving. Everything would be fine. Left, right, left, right, left, right, left, right, left, right, left, right. I marched along the pavement, weaving in and out of the horrors surrounding me, trying desperately to reach the subway station. I’m pulled forward in one slow, shuffling current toward the subway’s entrance. Bodies press close enough that I can feel heat radiating through coats and shirts. A shoulder bumps mine. Someone’s elbow catches my ribs. The person behind me steps so close that the toe of their shoe clips my heel.**

**I try to slow down, but I can’t.** 

**My calves tense to keep my balance, muscles tightening beneath my skin every time someone nudges me from behind. My thighs burn from shortening my stride, constantly correcting myself so I don’t step on the person ahead of me. My shoulders creep higher toward my ears. My fingers curl and uncurl at my sides.**

**I was so focused on trying not to get trampled or squished that I didn't even see the overwhelmingly large puddle at the subway's entrance.** 

**Without warning, there was a soft, yielding** ***splash*****. It swallowed the sole of my shoe with a muted, sticky “glop” before releasing it.**  
**Great.**

**I didn't look down.**

**I couldn't.**

**My body had already committed to the descent, momentum dragging me toward the yawning mouth of the subway. Every step hammered through my legs, muscles pulling so hard they trembled beneath my skin.** 

**The wetness in my shoe spread.**

**I could feel it.**

**At first it was only the front of my sock, a cool pressure barely noticeable. Then it seeped farther, curling around my toes. My foot shifted inside the shoe with a slick squelch. Every movement forced whatever goop I'd stepped into deeper into the seams.**

**Don't think about it. You don't have time.**

**The station echoed like the inside of a cathedral. Somewhere below, a train screamed against the rails; A shriek of tortured metal that bounced off concrete walls. Footsteps cracked around me, multiplying into dozens by the acoustics. Fluorescent lights buzzed overhead.**

**Down** 

**Down**

**Down** 

**Down** 

**Down**

**Down** 

**I went.** 

**Sweat rolled from my scalp onto my eyebrows, eventually reaching my eyes and blurring my vision. It trickled behind my ears and down my neck, soaking the collar of my shirt. My heartbeat slammed against my ribs so violently it felt as though each drum shoved my entire body forward.**



**My attention slowly returned to my wet shoes. The back of my heel had started to slip.**

**With every stride, the stiff edge of the shoe scraped the same strip of skin.**

**Again.**

**Again.**

**Again.**

**At first it was only irritation, a hot line across my Achilles.**

**Then the skin began to soften.**

**The moisture inside the shoe made everything slick except the spot where leather caught flesh. That tiny strip became raw. Each step peeled the skin away another microscopic layer. The friction was relentless and methodical. I could feel the blister forming before it existed: heat swelling beneath the surface, fluid gathering under skin that wanted desperately to tear.**

**Another stair.**

**Another scrape.**

**The sensation sharpened until every stride felt like dragging exposed nerves across coarse sandpaper.**

**I clenched my jaw hard enough that pain shot into my temples.**

**Don't stop.**

**Stopping meant looking.**

**Looking meant seeing.**

**I flexed my toes instinctively.**

**The staircase seemed longer than it should have been.**

**Advertisements peeled from tiled columns in curling strips fluttering in the stale air. The lights hummed overhead with a relentless electrical drone that seemed to vibrate inside my skull.**

**As I descended further down, I became painfully aware of every movement my body made. My hamstrings stretched and snapped taut with each stride. My hips twisted to compensate for the pain caused by my slipping shoe. My lower back tightened from leaning forward, and my shoulders burned from carrying my backpack.**

**Still...**

**That wet shoe.**

**I couldn't stop thinking about it.**

**It wasn't just on my foot anymore. It felt as though it had crawled upward through my ankle, settling into my bones themselves.**

**Each step ended with the same sickening slide of skin against the heel of the shoe.**

**Each scrape seemed louder than the trains.**

**Louder than my breathing.**

**Louder than my own heartbeat.**

**I still hadn't looked down.**

**Because some part of me knew—**

**if I looked…**

**“Hi, how can I help you?”**

**“Um, hi. My name is Jonathan Davis. I’m here for my 9:30 appointment.”**

**“Ah, good morning, Mr. Davis. Thank you for showing up 15 minutes early, as suggested. Here is your paperwork,” the woman at the desk in front of me said as she handed me a clipboard and pen. “Please take a seat and begin filling out this form. When they're ready for you, I will call you over to that door, and we will head back.”**

**“Alright, thank you.”**

**I took my seat and began filling out the “Federal Virtual Defense Administration’s (FVDA) Form 17-B — Civilian Virtual Service Eligibility & Classification Questionnaire.”**
