“What happened, Daddy?” my son asked.

I stared down at the collection of feathers at our feet. 

“Something must’ve eaten the nuthatches,” I said, disheartened.

“Oh no…” he groaned, teary-eyed. 

I knelt and studied a cluster of thick grey fur caught on a tree branch. A raccoon, maybe? Or a cat?

Seeing the birds’ remains made me incredibly sad. I loved our nuthatches.

Every morning I’d wander out and see the male and female building a nest in our birdhouse. It felt like a small promise of life that we could look forward to.

Ever since my wife (and son’s mother) died, I’d needed some sense of hope and promise. These birds gave me that.

But now, because of life’s cyclical nature and a starving predator, that promise had been snuffed out.

“I feel bad,” my son said, looking down.

“Why?” I wrapped his tiny body in my arms. “It’s not your fault. It’s just the circle of life. We’ll build a new birdhouse to honor them.”

***

We built our new birdhouse the following weekend and set it atop the fence that bordered our neighbor’s property. 

They lived in Florida most of the year and had plenty of birdhouses in their own backyard. It seemed like the perfect place to put it. 

Plus, their fence was made of plastic. I figured that it’d be harder for raccoons to climb up and reach any unsuspecting guests.

“It’ll be a new home,” I said, resting a hand on my son’s shoulder. “Another couple will find it and move in soon.”

I was right. The following weekend, we had another pair of nesting nuthatches. We even had some titmice in the trees above our patio.

My son and I went to bed that night, beaming with pride. As we read stories together, he asked, “Do you think more birds will come?”

I looked out the window, imagining the tiny pairs already out there, cuddling together, preparing for their new lives as parents. 

“I do.”

“Good,” he said, smiling. “I like birds.”

***

It must’ve been after midnight when the noises woke me.

*Crunch. Crunch.* 

It sounded like footsteps outside my window. They were slow and methodical, like an animal moving through unfamiliar terrain.

*That’s too big for a raccoon,* I thought, then peeked out the window. *Is it a deer?* 

It was too dark to see.

I got up and ran to the kitchen, swiped the airsoft gun from the top of my fridge, and eased the sliding door open. 

If it was a predator, I didn’t want to kill it. I just wanted to scare it off so it wouldn’t snag any more birds. 

The icy air hit me as I made my way onto the porch. 

Stupidly, I had forgotten my flashlight. 

I stared out into the pitch-black yard. 

“I know you’re out there!” I said, and slammed my foot against the patio, hoping to spook off whatever was lurking in the darkness. 

Nothing responded or even acknowledged my existence. 

I stood there for a long time and waited. I even fired a few rounds into the bushes to see if anything would stir.

Nothing did.

***

The next morning, we found a trail of mangled feathers next to a birdhouse.

“Looks like the raccoons came back, Daddy.”

“Damn…” I groaned and made my way to the birdhouse.

He was right. Once again, there was a strand of grey fur caught on a tree branch.

*So there was a predator out here last night.* I swung an arm over my son’s shoulder. *How did I miss it?*

And then... I noticed something even more terrifying.

Claw marks scratched deep into a tree… they were huge. 

*What on earth?*

I wondered if a mountain lion or a wolf had made them. I commanded my son to go back inside and immediately FaceTimed my uncle, who was an avid hunter.

“Uncle Dave, will you check this out?”

I showed him the marks, but unfortunately, without being there in person, he couldn’t tell.

“Just contact the city and make sure no mountain lions have been in the area,” he said. “And keep your son indoors.”

***

I told our local Fish and Game about the markings. They assured me that there hadn’t been any mountain lions spotted in the past five years.

“They just don’t come down this far,” the officer said over the phone. “It’s probably a bobcat marking its territory.”

I hung up, still not encouraged. I’d seen a bobcat once while hiking. It didn’t seem big enough to make those scratch marks. 

The mystery of all this ate at me, so I ordered two trail cameras online. I set up one by the creek and another by the tool shed. 

I made sure the angles were high and wide enough to cover every inch of the property.

I also installed some motion lights near the creek to spook off whatever was trespassing in our yard.

*If I know what it is, I’ll be able to set traps for it,* I figured.

***

My schedule got busy over the next few days. So busy that I didn’t have time to check my cameras. 

Life as a single parent while working full-time was torturous.

On the positive side, there hadn’t been any signs of the predator since the trail cameras had been installed. The birds were fine, and I kept my son indoors every chance I got.

It seemed like things were finally back to normal.

And then it happened.

Early Saturday morning, I stepped outside and saw… blood. Lots of it. It covered the birdhouse, dripping all the way down to the base of the fence like some demented river.

Like last time, there was an assortment of feathers scattered around it.

I surveyed the area and once again found strands of grey fur.

*It has to be a mountain lion…* I went to the trail camera next to the tool shed. It was off-kilter, like something had tried to rip it free.

I immediately went inside and opened the latest recorded sequence on my computer.

I scanned the footage. From ten p.m. to 1 a.m., nothing out of the ordinary happened.

And then… at exactly 1:59 a.m.… 

… I saw a snout and a pair of glowing eyes…

*What the hell?*

The thing was staring directly into the camera… like it was studying the device, trying to make sense of what it had discovered.

*It has to be a bear,* I thought, shaking with fear. *It’s so big.* But the more I studied its muscular face and long snout, the more I realized, no, this wasn’t like any bear I’d ever seen.

It had distinct, familiar features… eyes that held a personality… a being that almost seemed… human.

At 2:15 a.m., the strange mammal backed away from the trail camera and moved on to the birdhouse atop our fence. 

*How is that possible? The thing’s walking on its hind legs. Like a human.*

The next thought that hit me was, *Why haven’t I seen any footprints?*

The thing was massive, maybe six feet tall. Surely something that huge would leave tracks on the ground.

I clenched my teeth in horror as the creature made its way to the birdhouse, poked its long fingers inside, and fished out the half-sleeping, now-panicked pair of nuthatches.

I nearly lost my breakfast as I watched the mammal shove them into its mouth, chewing slowly, savoring them like a human enjoying a cookie.

Then, the creature made its way back to the trail camera. It was still mashing its meal as it stared into the frame with its glowing eyes.

It reached out with its large, almost monkey-like paw and fidgeted with the camera. The angle jittered, then fell askew as its claws ripped it halfway off.

Eventually, the creature grew bored, wandered toward the edge of the property, and vanished into the woods.

***

I didn’t know what to think. I just stood there, watching the footage, processing, *What on earth did I just see?*

The patter of tiny footsteps alerted me to the hallway. My son was awake. He stepped into the office, yawned, and stretched toward the ceiling with his tiny arms.

“Daddy?” His voice squeaked. “Did you find any feathers this morning?”

“Yes.”

He sat at his toddler-sized desk, the one we’d set in the corner so he could draw and pretend to work.

I noticed… several bird feathers stuck on the leg of his pajamas… and a tuft of grey fur.

“Buddy?” I asked, not even sure how to register what I was seeing. “Did you sleep okay last night?”

“Yes, Daddy…” His voice lowered, sounding deep and gravelly. “I had a yummy snack.”

My voice trembled as the breath left my lungs.

“But you shouldn’t have set up those cameras.” He swiveled his face toward me, revealing those glowing nocturnal eyes. “I don’t like them.”

***

I’m outside now, hiding in the tool shed. I don’t think my son… or whatever he is now… saw me come this way.

I’m trying to rationalize what is even happening.

I can’t tell Fish and Game. Otherwise, they might hurt him…

I just hope that he turns back to his normal self soon.

I can hear his footsteps outside. They seem so much heavier than usual.

His voice sounds sinister and rumbling, distorted with rage. “Where are you, Daddy? Stop hiding from me. I’m hungry.”
