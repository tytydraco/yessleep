I went to the lake this afternoon.

There was no wind.

The water looked black, almost oily, and the surface was so still that the trees reflected perfectly in it.

I walked down the dock it felt frozen in time.

The same dock.

The same boards.

I remembered Daniel sitting on the edge, kicking his feet in the water.

He had asked me once if the lake had a bottom.

I'd told him yes.

He'd asked what was under the bottom.

I'd said dirt.

He'd asked what was under the dirt.

I told him to shut up.

Geez.

I wish I'd just answered his questions.

I just sat at the end of the dock.

“Monkey?”

Nothing.

I waited.

Then something brushed against the underside of the wood.

A long, slow scrape.

I leaned forward.

There was a shape in the water.

squishy looking and oblong.

About eight feet down.

I thought it was a water logged trash bag first.

Then it moved.

A small hand floated upward.

Five little fingers.

The same little scar on the thumb from when Daniel cut himself opening a can of peaches.

I reached toward it.

The hand stopped just below the surface.

I was crying so hard I could barely see.

"Please."

The hand turned over.

Its palm opened.

Not unfolded.

Opened.

A thin seam split down its center, revealing a row of tiny white teeth.

Then another hand appeared beneath it.

And another.

And another.

They were connected.

Not separate arms.

One long, branching structure of wrists and fingers, stretching downward into the darkness.

Each hand was smaller than the last.

Some had fingernails.

Some had eyes.

One was holding a little plastic phone.

I backed away.

The water began to rise.

Not a wave.

The entire surface bulged upward, forming a smooth mound.

Something enormous was pressing against it from below.

And inside that mound, I saw a face.

Daniel's face.

His eyes were closed.

His lips were blue.

He looked exactly like he did in the hospital after they recovered his body.

Then his eyes opened.

"Maddie?"

His mouth moved.

But the voice came from behind me.

I turned.

Daniel was standing at the other end of the dock.

Barefoot.

Soaking wet.

Wearing his green dinosaur swim trunks.

He looked seven.

Exactly seven.

For one stupid second, I forgot everything.

I ran toward him.

He held out his arms.

I picked him up.

He weighed nothing.

His skin was freezing.

His hair smelled like lake water and the strawberry shampoo our mom used to buy.

I buried my face in his little chest.

"I'm sorry," I kept saying.

"I'm so sorry, Monkey.”

He wrapped his arms around me.

And whispered:

"That's okay, Maddie." 

I didn’t know if what I was seeing was real or just some mental breakdown. 

But I felt something move against my back.

His fingers.

They were getting longer.

I tried to pull away.

He held tighter.

His mouth pressed against my ear.

"I waited for you."

I could hear the bones in his hands cracking as they extended around my ribs.

"I waited a really long time."

His voice was changing.

Getting deeper.

Not like a boy growing up.

Like somebody slowing down a recording.

"I knew you would come."

I screamed and threw him off me.

He landed on the dock.

His body folded strangely.

His knees bent backward, and his arms landed at angles no human arms should ever make.

For a moment, he lay there twitching.

Then he started crying.

Real crying.

The ugly, breathless crying Daniel used to do when he fell off his bike.

"Why did you do that?"

I froze.

He looked up at me.

His face was normal again.

His bottom lip trembled.

"I just wanted a hug."

And I almost went back.

That's the part I hate the most about all this.

Even after seeing what he was.

Even if It was all in my head just torturing me for being a piece of shit to him. 

Even after feeling those fingers curling around my spine.

I almost picked him up again.

Because it still looked like my brother.

And I had already failed him once.

I just couldn't. I couldn't do it again. 

I couldn't look at him feeling as if  I was leaving him behind for a second time.

But I ran anyway.

I ran to my car.

The thing followed me.

I could hear bare feet slapping against the dock.

Then the gravel.

Then the pavement.

I didn't look back.

I drove forty minutes before I finally stopped shaking enough to pull over.

My phone rang.

Unknown number.

I answered.

My mother's voice.

"Madison?"

I couldn't breathe.

"Honey, listen to me."

She sounded scared.

Not angry.

Not disappointed.

Just scared to her core.

"Do not bring him inside."

I started crying.

"Mom?"

"Listen carefully. I made a mistake."

I could hear something moving behind her.

A horrible, dragging noise.

"I thought it was him. I thought if I loved him enough, he'd come back."

She took a shaky breath.

"But it doesn't bring them back, sweetie .”

"What is it?"

She was quiet.

Then she said:

"I don't know."

Another noise.

Something cracking.

"But I think it's been down there since before the lake."

I heard her begin to sob.

"And I think it's lonely."

The call disconnected.

I went back to the house because I had nowhere else to go.

I packed my suitcase.

I grabbed the notebooks.

I was halfway down the stairs when I heard something in the kitchen.

A chair scraping across the floor.

Then a little voice.

"Maddie?"

I stopped.

Daniel was sitting at the kitchen table.

His hair was dry now.

He was wearing the pajamas my mother had buried him in.

The blue ones with the stars.

He was eating cereal.

He looked up.

"Are you leaving again?"

I couldn't move.

He smiled.

There were too many teeth.

Not in some dramatic, monstrous way.

Just a few extra.

Like somebody had tried to make a child from memory and gotten the number wrong.

I backed toward the front door.

He put down his spoon.

"Mommy left too."

I reached for the handle.

"She said she'd stay forever."

The lights flickered.

The kitchen walls made a soft, wet popping sound.

Daniel stood up.

His feet didn't quite touch the floor.

"Why do you all keep lying to me?"

He wasn't crying anymore.

He sounded genuinely confused.

The wallpaper behind him began to ripple.

Something pushed outward from beneath it.

A face.

My mother's face.

Her eyes were open.

Her mouth moved soundlessly.

Then another face appeared beside hers.

An old man.

A woman.

A little girl with blue hair.

Dozens of them.

They pressed against the wallpaper like people trapped behind a thin sheet of plastic.

Their mouths opened.

All at once.

And the house filled with voices.

"STAY."

I have to get somewhere safe.
