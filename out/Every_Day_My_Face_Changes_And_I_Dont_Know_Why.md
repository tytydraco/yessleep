To preface this, I was always a sort of normal looking guy. Nothing really did stand out about me. I had the kind of face you'd find on a stock image website. But this all changed a couple of weeks ago.

It was a Friday and I had just gotten off of work and came home at about 7pm. It was a particularly stressful day, from the constant buzzing of phone calls to the whining of my boss demanding I meet my quota for calls today. It didn't help either that I had forgotten to clean the dishes in my apartment the night before. But at least I had the trek out with my friends coming up soon. As I did every day, I went into my bathroom, flicked on the lights, and then turned on the tap to splash some water on my face to liven me up a little. I looked back up in the mirror, expecting to look a little bit more refreshed, but instead was startled when I caught a glimpse of myself in the mirror.

My nose looked a bit smaller - not just shorter in general, but narrower at the bridge with less width. I blinked once, before pinching it; it felt completely normal. I pushed it from side to side, expecting it to feel loose somehow, but it moved exactly as it always had.  
  
 My mind wandered for answers before thinking,

"Must've just been the long day."

So, I cleaned up the dishes from the night before, made some pasta pesto, and watched some Youtube on the couch before falling asleep.

The next morning it was.. *more pronounced*. I went to go have my morning shower, but noticed something different. Now my eyes looked farther apart. It wasn't so noticeable if you just looked for less than two or three seconds, but when I looked straight ahead, I could tell everything about the symmetry was wrong. I took out my phone to check it on the camera there. Face ID didn't work, so I typed my password in manually. It wasn't just from the mirror. Then I checked a photo of me from about two weeks ago. The difference was apparent. I kept looking back and forth between the picture and the camera, trying desperately to make them look the same, but it didn't. It had the scar above my eyebrow, and my clothes, but there was something unsettling underneath which I couldn't quite put a finger on. I tried to just chuckle it off, but the image stuck in my head for the rest of the day.

On Sunday I decided to cancel the road trip out with my friends to the Catskills. I remember the phone call Marcus (who organised everything) and me had exactly.

"Hey Marcus, just wanted to let you know I don't think I'm up for going anymore." I said, a slight shakiness in my breath as I stood in the kitchen-living room.

"Oh, how come? You feeling alright?" He was always the kind to check up on everyone.

"Yeah, well I guess so, just not feeling great these last two days." I tried to reveal as little as possible.

There was a moment of silence from his end before he continued.

"Damn that sucks. Well hey, you want us to delay it till you're better? We can always try again next week. Heard there will be better skiing then anyways."

I abruptly stop him at the end, the thought of me coming out and showing myself to them, or really anyone, was agonizing. I don't know what got hold of me, but that day I suddenly felt deep palpations in my chest from the thought.

"No no," I take a deep breath in. "It's best if you guys just go. Send me pictures!" I try my best to sound energetic.

I hung up before he could protest. Relief washed over me, but it didn't last long. My phone buzzed soon after, Marcus had sent a few concerned messages on the group chat. One friend said "Hope you feel better soon, man." whilst the others just reacted with thumbs up emojis. I stared at the screen for a while before turning my phone off without replying. I let the rest of the day pass by.

I spent the week I was supposed to be on vacation for sitting in my room, pacing to the bathroom every hour, and staring into the mirror. I stopped going outside and used DoorDash as an alternative to getting food from the store. Even then, I only opened the door an inch or so, as to not let the delivery drivers get a good look at me.  
  
The first few deliveries went as usual, but on the third day the same driver who came to my apartment a day before before didn't let go of the bag when I cracked the door open.   
  
"You okay dude?" he asks, with a clear face of concern on him  
  
I mutter something about being alright as I notice him staring at my jaw, the light from the corridor illuminated it. I tugged the bag a little harder and he let go, although he continued to stare as I was closing the door. I slunk back into my room and leaned against the door until I heard his car pull out.

I took the time as an opportunity to go onto Google and looked up

*"facial asymmetry sudden onset"*

The AI overview said something about Bell's Palsy or a stroke. But my face didn't look droopy at all. It was just.. different. Simplified. Flattened, almost. Like a PS1 character rendered in realistic skin without updating the underlying model. And yet nothing else felt off, except for the fact I noticed that my pupils weren't dilating as I stared in the mirror for hours, turning left to right, trying to find the angle that would make it seem normal again. But I never found it. I grabbed my phone and turned on it's flashlight, and shone it directly into one eye. The pupil stayed the exact size. Then I tried the other, and it was the same. I should've gone to the hospital, instead I just kept staring.

It's when I noticed something else. My lips had thinned. It wasn't dried or anything, but it looked like someone just took an eraser to it. I tried to lick them with my tongue, but it found barely anything to touch. I tried going to sleep again that night, but I just couldn't. I kept waking up periodically, until I couldn't take it anymore. At about 5am on a Monday morning, I went back to check myself in the mirror as I had done so many times before. My nose had sunken further, leaving just two small holes and a slight ridge. My eyes had stretched even wider, pupils now far too large and glassy. It didn't feel like me anymore. No, what was standing there, in my bathroom, using my mirror, couldn't have been me. I caught myself smiling. I didn't want to smile, but there it was. It felt almost natural now. The rim of my mouth twitched and jittered for a few seconds, before easing again. I assumed it was just my nerves.

I told work I needed to have another week off.

I tried to ignore my problems. I stopped having the lights on. I turned my phone to "Do Not Disturb" to avoid the calls from my family and friends asking if I was alright. I brushed my teeth in the dark and I draped some cloth over my mirror.

The urge never went away to look. To see if, perhaps this would all just go away. So eventually I took it down and looked.

Now, I looked as if you asked a child to draw a human face when they've never seen one before. My eyes were rounder than they should be - alert but empty. Ears squashed closer to my head. Lips reduced to thin pencil lines that barely moved when I spoke. Cheekbones that sat too high and too sharp. My skin had lost its small indentations, imperfections, and pores. It was now waxy, stiff, even slightly rubbery - like a mannequin someone had forgotten to fully finish. I pushed my fingernail deep into the side of my cheek before letting go. The dent in my skin stayed for several seconds before popping back in place suddenly. When was the last time I had blinked?

I had barely realised, but after this I had simply stared at myself in the mirror for nearly ten hours, unmoving, unflinching, uncaring. I noticed my torso was now also elongated, and my limbs had lengthened and thinned unnaturally. This was further exacerbated by the fact that I barely looked like I had been eating or drinking at all.

By the next day I had lost all track of time. I likely hadn't slept for a day or more. But I managed to go onto my laptop, trying one last time to distract myself from what was happening. The screen was the first normal thing I'd seen in days. Everything was as it was, the folders still in place, the same background of starry night, the same icons. I felt normal for a few fleeting moments. My Gmail was filled with emails from my boss demanding me to go to work, with the latest one informing me I was fired. I didn't even care anymore. A few weeks ago I would've raised a storm, but now there's just nothingness. It was a strange realisation that I had sucked every ounce of energy I had in my life into this. As I continued to scroll through the countless notifications, my eyes suddenly focused on my reflection in the screen. For a moment, I didn't even realise it was mine. It was even worse than a few days ago. The only thing recognisable being my haircut and the scar above my eyebrows which I got when I was 7.

I screamed and punched the laptop multiple times, continuing even after the screen was thoroughly cracked and destroyed. It gave me a second of satisfaction. But then I saw my bloody knuckles; *my fingers were still lengthened and thinned*, alongside every other abnormality in my body. 

Today I went out of my room and looked in the mirror. I noticed the ceiling had grown black mold above the bathroom showerhead. Stains on the wall. The little things that I didn't realise before. It stunk badly, and there were empty wrappers and trash bags littering the floor, enough trash bags stacked up that it looked like I'd been here for months. And I noticed that now, I was stuck in a perpetual smile. I don’t know why. I just smile ear to ear. I tried to force my expression back to normal, digging my nails deep into the top corners of my mouth and pulling down. It still didn't look normal, and when I let go it returned as it was.

I'm writing this in my room using my phone. I don't go outside at all anymore, nor answer the knocks on my door, especially after Marcus tried calling me 6 times yesterday. I wanted to call back, explain what was happening, have some consolation that I was going to be fixed and my life was going to be normal again. But something inside me, deep inside, likes the way I am now, and I can feel it growing. I don't even know the purpose of writing this. All I know is that I never want anyone to see me now.
