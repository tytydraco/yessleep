All-you-can-eat buffets, a lot of people love them, a lot of people see them as the ultimate sign of greed and gluttony, and some people are just confused by the whole thing. 

I want to take a moment to help that last group. Some people are worried that you have to sit there all day and stuff your gullet in order to make it worth the money. Others just aren’t sure what the etiquette is in one. 

Well, I am an experienced buffet-goer, and I recently went with two friends, one of whom was a complete stranger to the concept himself. So, I’ll use that trip as a guide to maximizing your All-you-can-eat experience, and not just how much you eat. 

-

“So, this place is something pretty special?” 

“It’s a local landmark for sure.”

My friends and I, we’ll just keep the names simple and call them Dave and Joe, arrived at around 6 PM.  Dave being the buffet virgin, was cautiously curious about what was to come. 

Tip 1: You pay at the door. Unless you absolutely need an ice-cold soda to enjoy your dinner, do not order a drink! They cost extra and you WILL be bugged for (typically not free) refills throughout your meal. The water is also ice cold, and free. 

We handed over our entry fees and the three of us went straight to the plates. 

“Holy shit, this place is… I don’t know. What the hell?” Dave was looking around in wonder and confusion. 

“It can be pretty overwhelming bro. Just keep your eyes on the prize.”

That right there is Tip 2: Know what you want!

Buffets are designed such that you are overwhelmed by the options and grab one of these, one of those, Ooh! Definitely one of that. As you wander down the lanes, you find a full plate faster than you expected. 

That’s all the cheap stuff. Your plate is full of canned beans and bread. The expensive foods are carefully at the end of the natural walking routes. Don’t just freewheel it. Plan what you want to eat, and grab it. 

“It’s all labeled,” Joe added. “See those signs over each aisle? Just go get what you want.” 

I took one of the mini-bowls and went straight to the soup warmers. Odd choice, I know, but I was really in the mood for some hearty beef stew. I glanced over as I went to make sure Joe was sticking by Dave. These buffets are pretty huge sometimes; can’t have him getting lost. Joe was loading up on curry at the Indian foods; he’s a real spice freak, thankfully right next to Dave grabbing some potstickers just on the opposite warmer. 

My next target was the fried foods. I wanted a drumstick and maybe a country-fried steak. I capped it off with a warm roll, some collard greens, and, of course, I took my time putting gravy and sauce on everything. 

Hey, so I wanted some comfort food. I have to be more than responsible enough during the sixty-plus hours a week I’m working these days. 

I went to meet back up with the boys. Joe was staring at two different spicy noodle dishes like deciding between them would answer all of the questions in the universe. He’s like that, never just splits the difference even though that’s what the buffet is all about. 

“Just grab a pile of both, dude.” 

“I’m trying to decide if I want both.” He insisted, like always. 

“If you just try a little of both, then you’ll know.” I was mostly just making the same argument as always for the fun of it now. 

“Szechuan, I know it’s gonna be good.” He decided. 

“Alright. Dave, you g-“ Dave wasn’t at the opposite warmer anymore. 

“Where’s Dave?” My mood turned instantly.

“He’s been picking at the Chinese stuff the whole- Shit.” Joe went from sure of himself to panic even quicker than I had. 

We instantly leapt to action and started looking around. We heard him before we saw him.

“…the hell?!” Dave’s shocked and horrified voice echoed down the rows. 

That was enough to pick his direction. We ran. It didn’t take too long to spot him, frantically walking the rows. We pushed our hardest to reach him before he did something stupid. 

“Hey there Dave!” I called out to him once we were close. “We told you man, we’ll show you the ropes; this place can be overwhelming.” 

“Overwhelming? What the fuck is this place?!” He was heading into a full-blown meltdown. 

“A buffet, dude.” Joe managed to say it like it was the most obvious thing in the world. 

“A b-? This is not a buffet!” He was just short of screaming. “I must have passed forty rows! And none of this is food!”

“Yeah, it’s big. The whole place is set up to feel bigger; the aisles start to loop. The Chinese you were just at is only, like, five rows back.” I pointed up at the signs, and sure enough, it was there, with me and Dave’s plates left behind easily visible. “We told you to stick with us and know what you want. So what do you want Dave?” 

He looked at the sign and the familiar sections we had just been at right behind us, as if we had barely run at all. His face twisted between half a dozen expressions as his mind tried to make sense of it. 

“This is ridiculous, what the hell did you bring me into?! That was not there! None of this-!” He started to rant again. 

“Dave!” I snapped and cut him off. “Do not make them call the manager. We are not getting kicked out of here. What do you want to eat?”

Dave’s face hovered between confusion and anger. Only when he noticed that lights had started to shut off around us and we all heard a door slam did he stumble backwards and nervously listened to me. 

“Pie! Okay, pie.” 

“Pie’s right there.” I pointed two rows ahead. “Grab him a slice of apple, Joe. And this?”

I gestured at the things he had been walking through. 

“You wandered into the Scandinavian stuff. It just looked weird ‘cause it’s piles of leek goop and preserved fish ‘n crap. That’s it. It’s just a buffet, dude.” 

Dave didn’t look convinced, but did look frightened enough to be mollified. 

Joe came back, handed Dave his plate, and we all went to our table. Three glasses of water were waiting.

“This fried chicken is heaven on a plate.” I managed to speak between bites. 

“Yep, Szechuan was definitely the right choice.” Joe was happily inhaling his noodles. 

Dave just glared.

“Okay, cut the crap guys.” He spoke more quietly than before. “What the hell is this. And if you say ‘a buffet’, I swear to god I will freak out. Look around,” he gestured around with his arms to demonstrate “there are no employees, not one. Who the hell is refilling those warmers? And why are we the only people in a buffet, at dinner time, on a Friday? Are you guys with one of those prank shows?” 

“Dude, just finish your food. We can talk about this more later.” Joe tried to placate him. 

“I’m not playing along with this anymore. Just tell me what the hell this is!” He started to shout again.

Okay, rule number 3 time: The buffet is just like any other restaurant. It doesn’t matter that you serve yourself; it is a public place, behave with decorum! Who cares if the staff isn’t bringing you your food, try being a decent human being anyway. The same etiquette you would apply to any other sit-down restaurant applies to the buffet. 

In short? Don’t make a scene.

“Just calm down, you have good food, it’s quiet, safe, don’t stress about it.” I tried to keep him calm.

“Calm down? You’ve been gaslighting me this whole evening. It loops? The warmers are all in a straight line dumbass. Look over there, where is the goddamn back wall? How can I not see the back of the restaurant even! It just, keeps going. That’s insane. And those… things on the warmers weren’t ‘Scandinavian food’ until you said it. I don’t know what this is, or how you’re doing it, but it’s either a messed-up prank or you’re insane.” 

“Dave, the manager-“ He cut me off.

“I don’t care about a freaking manager! I’m leaving!” He stood up.

“Maybe that’s best.” I agreed. It was risky leaving with our plates full, but riskier staying at that point with how he was acting. 

“You had better explain this stunt.” He seethed. “And trying to have me do that ‘payment’.” He drew the word out with disgust. “I’m not paying a buffet with my dead dog’s old collar.”

I froze cold in my tracks. Joe did exactly the same. We shared a look of horror. 

“You paid, right?” My voice was tremulous with fear. “We helped you pick. You had forgotten that old thing was down there even; it’s not really a keepsake.” 

“It’s not about the collar! It’s that buffets don’t take payment in ‘things you remember’.” He spoke with a mocking lilt, like talking about magical mumbo-jumbo. “This is seriously messed up and weird, guys. I just want to go home.” 

He had almost reached the door by the end of his rant. Me and Joe saw it; no streetlights or shadows from the parking lot. The glass on the doors was pitch black. Dave clearly didn’t, or he didn’t care anymore. 

Last rule: Do not EVER cheat the buffet. You pay your fee, you eat your food. Do not take food out. Do not leave a full plate. Most all buffets charge a waste fee for leaving plates of uneaten food. Ours lets us have a takeout carton for leftovers only as long as we reasonably ate from the plate; that’s more a generous exception than the norm though. Dining and dashing will get you banned.

Never get banned from the buffet.

We tried to pull Dave back from the door, we really did. But, he was already reaching for it before we screamed. I caught the briefest glimpse of something like teeth swimming in oil, black and deep and horrifying in a way I will never be able to properly describe, before I snapped to attention and turned around. I pulled Joe’s arm to spin him back too.

“Crap, we didn’t stack our plates, bro.” I forced it through my teeth as casually as I could.

“Oh man! You’re right!” Joe tried his best to match my tone, but it came out more like a choked sob. “Don’t want to be rude.”

“Guys! What the f-!” I tried to ignore the sounds of Dave scrambling backwards and screaming. “Oh god no! Please! No!” 

We ravenously ate the remains on our plates. There was no point risking the food waste fee anymore. We were willing to for Dave, but we were forced to hear Dave screaming like a child while some wet thing lacerated his flesh less than twenty feet away from us. So, that was a lost cause. 

I hid my face in my food to hide the tears. 

“We shouldn’t have brought him here.” Joe risked a whisper.

“And risk him coming alone? He never would have made it.” 

We both knew why we took the risk, but neither of us felt right either.

We still don’t.

We dumped the scraps on one plate and stacked it on the other two. We placed our silverware on top and drink glasses beside, and we gave the table one quick sweep with a napkin to be kind.

When we turned around, it was over. The entrance was illuminated by the dull orange-yellow of the old streetlights in the parking lot. We took that to mean our performance was accepted. We would be allowed to leave. 

We didn’t run. We walked out to my car with a calm decorum, and then we cried.

My God, did we cry. 

If we ever have to take another newcomer, we will try a different approach.

But me? I will be back next week. 

Because, for the price of a knock-off Oswald the rabbit figurine I managed to pull up one not very good memory of staring at while I had a fever when young, I can eat anything I want for two days, once at the buffet and the next from leftovers. If I want to feel like a king I can eat croquembouche and beluga caviar. Mostly, I just eat well though, and two days a week for free.

In this economy, that matters. 

So, I will pull out another not-quite-forgotten relic from my past, I will pay a fee I cannot understand, and I will sit down in an empty building whose walls I cannot see, and I will eat food that cannot exist. 

And when I need to, I will help others do the same. I hope I helped you. 

Enjoy your meal.
