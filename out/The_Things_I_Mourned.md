I’m sorry I took a pause writing this. It took more of a toll then I thought, March called just before I was about to drive to the bar. Her voice brought me down from the edge. After I told her what I was doing she scolded me, insisting that I stop, that I was safe and there was no reason for me to put myself through hell again. I told her I couldn’t stop, told her that at least someone who will believe this, can know the truth. So that one person won’t make the same mistakes I made. 

I didn’t go back home after the first ceremony. I certainly couldn’t seek refuge in my father. I couldn’t even go to Charles. I had no idea who I could trust, no idea who I would be putting in danger. I pulled off the highway into a shallow ditch adjacent to a golf course. It was four in the morning when I finally considered a motel. When I went to search for my wallet I realized it hadn’t been returned with my clothes. A roll of fifty dollar bills had replaced it in my pocket. My trembling fingers counted ten of them, just as promised. I felt sick. Dirty. I swallowed the taste of vomit in my mouth and resolutely drove back onto the expansive road. A road into nowhere until it led to the red shotty light of a motel. 

The motel attendant looked at me as if I had shouted in his face to wake him up. Which I suppose is fair enough considering the hour. He continued to scowl at me with a sour frown. After I slid him a fifty his demeanor immediately changed and he promptly hoisted himself off of his chair and gathered my room key. When he came back he looked me up and down. My suit was disheveled at this point but I was still wearing all black.  

“Late night funeral?” the attendant sneered as he slid me my key. 

I took the key and looked at him silently. I considered answering, lying perhaps, but thought better of it and headed to my room with no reply. 

Lillian was upset with me the next day of course, saying she was worried sick when I stumbled through the doorway close to the following noon. She tried to hug me to express her relief at first but I backed away. I felt as if I was infectious, and the ice cold fear  returned to my stiff body in an instant. Lillian pulled me close regardless knowing now that something was deeply wrong. I could feel March’s toddler arms wrap around my leg and I began to let out heavy, shaking sobs. I called off sick that day and the day after that. My lie to Lillian and Charles was that there had been a mistake and my father had scheduled me for a funeral of a close childhood friend of mine who had been murdered brutally. It was an open casket funeral. That seemed to explain my state as I was known by both of them to be a sensitive soul. 

The world spun around me with a disorienting normalcy. It almost angered me. How could everything appear to be fine when I had seen what I’d seen? How could I smile at customers when I had seen an innocent woman die in front of me? I constantly lived on edge, flinching at every dim recollection seen in the face of a stranger. Watching for any stalker in the night or car that lingered behind mine for too long. Even so, I saw nothing to arouse my apprehension or suspicion which confused me to no end. I even asked Lillian and Charles if they had seen anything unusual to that effect, and both said no. Whoever or whatever was watching me and my family on behalf of this evil was nothing seen to the naked eye. Which somehow worried me even more. 

One evening while Lillian washed the dishes, she mentioned something that alarmed me. 

“I called your father this afternoon,” she said, “He was very apologetic about the scheduling issue. He said he completely forgot that the departed was your friend. Must have forgotten his name.”

My grip crumpled the edge of the newspaper I read. I felt betrayed and yet terrified. The worst I imagined was most likely true and I had no idea what that might mean. 

“Thank you for doing that dear.”

The phone rang not a day later. I recognized the voice almost immediately. I wanted to scream with rage, tell them that I would be going to the police and that they could keep their money. But I didn’t. I remained silent as the caller told me the address and its subsequent code word. As I got ready, I made sure to bring something that could serve as a blindfold. 

Something that I still cannot explain logically to this day, is that the address to the ceremony location was completely different from the one before, and yet I pulled up to the exact same property. Identical, pristine, green field. The very same large manor, painted completely white. At first I thought it must have been a mistake. That surely the manor must have been at least slightly different, but the more I look back, the more I question that reality. 

Since I wasn’t sedated this time, the butler led me through the mansion as if I were a guest. I saw my surroundings without a mind clouded by drugs, and found it just as revolting. Patterns upon patterns on tile, with blue lines forming geometry on the walls in stark strokes. It seemed as if the hedonists were so desperate to recreate the sacred nature of ancient temples. But instead it translated as an ignorant mess of an eyesore. Dead plants appeared burnt in opulent vases, and upon the walls were mounted several animal heads. Including human. The revelers were already indulging in various activities, many I gasped at being done in such a publicly brazen manner. Even so, I continued to follow the butler. An outburst could potentially cost me everything, and I did not want to push that line more than I already had. 

My guide led me up a large staircase and down a dimly lit corridor with several doors. It seemed to stretch forever, the moans of pain and pleasure echoing off the garish wallpaper. We stopped at a door near the end of the hallway and I was ushered in. I could hear the door lock behind me. The walls of the room were mirrors this time and the ceiling was the same. There was a queen bed on one wall with patterned sheets and upon the duvet were the robes I was to wear. Atop those robes were my golden shackles. I fumbled with the robes as my hands shook. After I was done draping the fabric over my body, I glanced in the mirror. I had never felt more exposed than I did at that moment. The fabric was sheer, I could hardly move my arms or neck, and I couldn’t help but feel that the mirror was watching me back. Perhaps it was, as now I know that there is such a thing as a one sided mirror. 

A knock at the door shook me out of my thoughts. The butler's voice came muffled asking if I was ready. I replied, resigned. Even the crawling feeling of being watched could stop me from being numb. It simply drove me toward the door quicker than I would have otherwise.

The ceremony room stunk of sex. The sounds of copulation surrounded me in droves, like bees humming in their combs. I tried not to look too hard at the people involved in the acts, especially those who were bound in the same shackles I donned. I had to keep up the puppet act, or else I knew I would go insane. The man, the one from the first night and someone I came to call the leader, stood beside me under the Minotaur once more. He did not ring a bell this time. He simply began to talk, his voice cutting through the drawl. 

“Indulge my brothers and sisters,” the leader began, “Be what taboos the lesser bring upon us divine.” 

He kept talking but I resentfully toned it out. I simply stared at the ceiling, witnessing the intricate pattern upon it. Unlike the nauseating nature of the other decor, this ceiling was somehow calming. Hypnotic. It looked like the rings of a tree drawn over and over again. It felt like floating at the surface of the sea and looking down into a deep trench. Depth. One so tantalizing and sensual I became frightened. My eyes flickered from the ceiling to the face of the Minotaur above me. He was carved out of what looked to be obsidian. His face held no malice or malevolence. It just looked like a bull. It was the human part of him, holding a knife in one hand and a child in another, that was evil. 

The sacrifice space was already cleared that night and instead of bringing forth only one person, the militia brought forth a man and a woman. Both naked and bound in shackles. They seemed high on something, pliable and subservient, but I could still see the fear in their eyes. They knelt on the floor without question. 

“Copulate,” the leader ordered. 

Whatever the drug was, it made them immediately obey. Their actions were unnatural though, almost as if their joints were too stiff to move right. Once they touched it was as if the act was two puzzle pieces that didn’t fit together. I watched them, to my discomfort, afraid of what might happen if I didn’t. 

“Eat,” 

My gaze snapped toward the leader. He couldn’t have been serious, but the leader's smile did not waver. It was pure delight that filled him in that moment, and it didn’t take a fool to see it. I couldn’t watch and turned away from the two sacrifices. The leader stomped toward me and grabbed my shoulders. 

“Do not turn away wretch,” he hissed into my ear, “You must witness it.”

“No. No I can’t.” 

“You will.” 

The leader pulled the chains on both my wrists and neck forcefully, holding it short to him so that I was forced frontward. I could hear the slick wetness of teeth against flesh. I clamped my eyelids shut, earning a slap from the leader. 

“Fuck you,” I spat. 

“Keep this up boy and I will have Charles in front of you next.” 

Through my labored breaths I saw him kneeling in my mind's eye. The fear in Charles' eyes as his throat was slit at my feet. In a split second my eyelids blinked open. I will not be describing what I saw before me. Just know that the sight still haunts me, even through the years of cocaine and hard liquor. Yet, despite sobbed pleas for them to stop, the blood of both still flowed into the sacrificial hole.

That was the night I first tried marijuana and whiskey together. Still not a good combination after all these years, I don’t recommend it. But it does get the job done for a rookie. I climbed into bed that morning after a four hour long shower. The water was hot, almost felt like I was searing my skin off but anything to get the scent of sex, blood, and liquor off of my skin. If Lillian could still smell it she didn’t say anything.

I stopped calling Charles after that. I stopped eating too. When I had to keep up appearances I would spit the food into napkins. Soon, the hunger became too much to bear and I found that I could at least stomach some vegetables. I still can’t eat meat though. Lillian asked about it at the time and I said it was the new dieting fad for actors which seemed to explain it well enough to her. Still, I knew she could tell there was more to it. After all, I looked like shit most days and most nights I came home smelling like the bar around the corner. Even so, she didn’t pry. She would never get the chance. 

There were ten more ceremonies on my contract. I don’t know how I stomached seven more without issue but I did. The next sacrifice was three women flayed in front of me. It took hours for them to stop screaming. After them there were two men and two women, forced into iron maidens while making love against their will. The blood formed a pool before funneling into the hole. Next came five, crammed inside a large bronze bull. I thought a voice I could hear from inside was Charles’ but I’ve chalked that up to the amount of meth I was on that night. Four again, stretched until split in two. Three once more, guts strewn across the floor. Two that time, forced to skin each other. One again, decapitated. 

I said it was myself that avoided issues up until that point but it was more like the trouble found me. I don’t know why I was so stupid as to think that if I stopped talking to Charles that he would cleanly cut himself out of my life. One day, one where I had a considerable hangover from the night prior, Charles showed up at the house. Luckily it was when March and Lillian were visiting her parents, and were out of town for a couple of days. I suppose my moodiness got too much for even the patient Lillian to handle, which was fair enough. 

“Why are you here?” I asked, standing at the house's threshold as the beautiful visage of my friend was displayed before me. I missed him, despite myself, and it took everything in my power not to rush and embrace him. The hangover and my lack of shower was enough to delay the urge. 

Charles scoffed, a livid smile forming on his face as a hand combed through his blonde hair. 

“Seriously? It’s been weeks, Ed. No calls, no visits, no signs of you outside of my friends seeing you at the bar. Hell, I thought you were dead!” Charles exclaimed. 

I wished I was, but I couldn’t leave March behind. Looking back now I was slowly becoming a bad father, in spite of March keeping me going. An alcoholic, a budding drug user. Granted, I didn’t become a full blown addict until Lillian’s parents got custody of her, but it doesn’t matter. I was a poor excuse for a man, an abysmal example of a father. Self-pitying aside, I suppose then I should say I had a sneaking suspicion in the back of my mind that the group would not let me. Whatever they were doing, I was a part of it now. I don’t know how to explain the feeling but it was almost instinctual. 

Nevertheless, I gruffly invited him inside. We settled in the living room where Charles looked around at the disarray left in my wake. I hadn’t planned for his visit and that was clear enough with the beer cans crumpled and strewn alongside Chinese takeout containers. 

“I’m worried about you, Ed.” Charles started gently, “And judging by the looks of things, I have every right to be.”

“Right, I suppose you do,” I said through layers of withdrawal.

“That’s all you have to say to me? You always do this, you always agree with me when conversations get too hard.”

“Well what would you have me do Charles? Lash out and tell you to go fuck yourself? Or maybe you’d prefer me to start crying on my knees, begging you for your forgiveness.”

I had never spoken to him that way. The shock was like a slap on both of our faces. 

“I just want you to tell the truth,” Charles replied, his voice starting to shake, “Like you always have to me. What is it? Is there someone else?”

“What?”

“Are you seeing someone else? Someone younger, more fun? I know you’ve been contacting a drug dealer. Whoever this person is, they're obviously a bad influence.”

We were both shouting now, pacing around the empty house like angry ghosts. 

“Yeah you’d like that wouldn’t you? Some drama to add into your life so that you can come and save me instead of the other way around!” 

“Ed, you’re being mean.”

It hurt me to see him cry but the anger I had at first played pretend at, had become the rage of festering years. 

“Right, because god forbid I’m mean to the golden boy. The one who gets everything. A family to pay for his art school, a mother who didn’t die, a dad who doesn’t give a shit if he’s a queer as long as he doesn’t track it into the house!”

“That’s not fair Eddie, stop.”

“No what’s not fair is that I’m getting hunted like prey every other day, being in danger, while you live your single bachelor life fucking whoever you want! Was it fun? Playing with your best friend's emotions because it was easy? Because I already loved you? Or was it more fun to imagine me off with someone else so you didn’t have to let me down easy?” 

There was an impregnable silence that stood between us, sharp and unmoving. His tears stopped and his face turned pale, before he grimaced in the pain of heartbreak and turned to leave. The slamming door was sharp against my eardrums and just as heavy. I stood alone, finally able to break down into the tears I had been holding back. My anguish grew with every moment but so did my fear. I had said something, slipped and fell. Not too hard, but still I trembled. I almost broke as I heard the phone ring. 

I was right to be afraid. They had heard everything. And despite being vague, despite the subtlety of my suggestion, I suppose it was just enough strikes for them to take action. As soon as I entered the door the militia men punched me, beating me on the ground until I stopped moving. I could hear the revelers' laughter all around me, every voice started to sound like Charles’ as I was carried limp up the stairs. I was in the mirrored room once more and the Leader stood in front of me. Tied to the bed and stripped naked, I desperately strained against my bonds.

“I warned you what would happen if you were to say anything, and I did mean anything,” The leader clucked, “Though, since you’ve been such a good boy for us so far, I suppose it is only you we shall hurt tonight.”

He nodded to a masked militia man who stood beside the bed next to a surgical table with a myriad of tools. He held a large pair of pliers, bringing them to the tip of my first finger. The pain was searing white as the militia man pulled off the first of my fingernails. Blood dripped down my arm and onto my forehead as he continued, my voice as loud as it had ever been. Sobs, pleas, horrid promises. As he paced toward my feet my voice had gone hoarse. The leader sat in a chair in the corner, watching me with only one pair of hungry eyes. I knew there were more. At some point I blacked out from the pain but when I came to every nail was gone. The wounds were dressed and cleaned, wrapped with white gauze. I began to cry again, deep curdling sobs as I brought my knees to my chest. Suddenly I felt a kiss pressed to my forehead.

“Don’t waste those tears now. After all, they do grow back.”

They made me walk to the sacrificial area, each step, needles of pain. Eventually I settled for a crawl. This time, a young man was brought before me. With golden hair and a fit frame. He had brown eyes instead of green. His hair was longer. But he screamed just like Charles, when a corkscrew was slowly driven into his skull. 

The next day, I learned what withdrawal was. Every time I put pressure on my hands or feet I felt searing hot pain. No matter how many pain killers I took I would still feel it. I couldn’t very well go to my dealer or the bar to numb anything, I could hardly walk to the kitchen. I slept on the couch for a full day and woke up to the  sound of knocking at the door. I didn’t move at first, I dreaded the idea of it being Charles or the Leader. 

“Edward?” a familiar voice called, “It’s your father.”

My muscles tensed with fear and apprehension, but my mind swam with confusion and curiosity. I limped gently to the door and opened it. Sure enough, it was my dad, standing there, holding a pair of crutches. 

“What are you doing here?” I groggily interrogated. 

“God- son you look like hell,” he replied with almost an air of amusement, letting himself in, “When I heard what happened I thought I’d swing by and bring you some things to help your recovery.”

My mouth went dry. 

“I don’t know what you’re talking about.”

“Ah, I see they’re still as good as ever at gettin’ people to shut up,” my father began, his tone still jovial. That preacher's voice he always put on after Sunday mass, or when he was holding back his anger. “I didn’t raise you to be no’ blabbermouth so I suppose you inherited that from your mother.”

“You knew?” I pinched my brow “All this time, you knew? You knew what you were signing me up for?”

My heart was beating at a breakneck speed. Seeing my nightmares true in front of my eyes was a sight that almost drove me mad. Yet, my father, my terrible father, simply kept smiling. 

“Of course. You didn’t reckon that after how I lied to your wife?”

“What the fuck? Why? You know what they do to people, you know how many they kill, why would you send me there?”

Preacher Tom stepped slowly toward me, placing a gentle hand on my shoulder. 

“I’d have to be a fool to not know what sins you’ve been up to Edward. Since you were a little boy I knew. I knew what you were. Your mother tried to tell me I was just imaginin’ things, that you were just sensitive. I thought I gave you a way out employin’ ya, bringin’ you closer to the church, but no, you just had to go and screw the Williams boy. I had nearly given up, but of course in my hour of need, God gave me an answer. And say what you want about my brothers and sisters, but they sure as shit know how to straighten out a f-”

Before I could think, before the action could even register, I had socked my father square in the mouth. The older man staggered back, steadying himself on the kitchen counter. I immediately winced as my fingers erupted in pain under the gauze. When I looked back my father had righted himself and started laughing. I sneered in disgust and anger. 

“Fuck you.”

He let out one final loud bark of laughter.

“Well look at that, you finally grew a pair,” the preacher said. I paced into the kitchen, trying to get as far away from him as possible. He held his cheek, rubbing it forcefully as if to relish in the pain, and began his exit. “I got ya the strongest pain medication I could haggle. And son,”

He made sure I was looking at him as he made his way out of the door. The gleam in my fathers eyes was immeasurable. I remember seeing it in them all those years ago, when he gave that sermon about Paradise Lost. 

“Give em’ hell.”
