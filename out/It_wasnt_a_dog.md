I saw a dog on the side of the road yesterday. Poor bastard was limping towards a trashbag. When it started tearing through the black plastic, I knew I just had to take her home. 

I'll admit, I had a beer or two on the way home. Not enough to get me hammered, but enough to make me sympathize with the dog's awkward gait. I saw myself in her that night, two lonely souls on an empty road looking for something, anything, that could take us away from it all, if only for a moment. Only difference between us was that I got the privellage of going home to a warm bed and a loving family. 

So I came up to her real slow, readying myself for a chase or a bite, but the moment she saw me she whinpered and ran up to me, licking me all over and shaking like the most cutest, most pitiful engine on earth. She had a collar, thank god. No rabies for me tonight. 

Poor thing might've been a poodle. You couldn't tell from the caked up fur and filth, but she was a good breed. Probably got lost.

I got home around midnight. It was a Monday night and my wife was away for a business trip. The kids had school in the morning. The walk was nice. Empty, quiet roads that hummed with sleepy street lights and cicadas. Just us two. The dog kept shaking the entire way home. 

I shimmied myself from my pants and ruffled through the closet. I was looking for something. Something I promised I'd get to eventually. I rummaged through old dog bowls and chew toys until I found it again. Old Benny's collar. He was a bit bigger than the road dog, but the collar fit her all the same. 

I dusted off the old doggy bowl and got the dog some water and a bit of cold chicken from the fridge. She ate it up like it was the greatest thing she'd ever taste. 

I stayed up with the dog until one in the morning. I named her Baba. I bathed her, introduced her to Benny's favorite toys, and tried getting her to sleep on Benny's bed, which had been sitting in storage for far too long. She couldn't fit. I didn't understand why, it was easily twice her size, but no matter what I did, Baba always seemed to jut out at strange angles. It was like trying to catch a magician in the act, I could never quite understand why she didn't fit. 

And so I decided I would go upstairs, take a shower and go to bed. As I did so, the dog followed me, never straying far from my ankles. She kept licking me too. A thousand wet kisses from a grateful little thing. I wish I could describe my kids the way I describe Baba to you now. 

I took her upstairs to the bedroom and went to take a bath. As I showered, I could hear Baba pawing at the bathroom door. Desperate and quick, with those tiny paws and overgrown nails. It was the quickest shower of my life. I got dressed, closed the door to the bathroom and bedroom, and turned on the air conditioning. 

As I got to bed, she hopped up with surprising strength. It was like she never had a limp. I didn't mind the company, so I slept beside her in the cold dark, holding her against my chest. She was so warm. 

Warm. Until it got cold. Too cold. I woke up. It was three in the morning. Baba was nowhere to be seen. 

"Baba?" I called, getting up from bed and making towards the light switch, making sure she wasn't on the floor waiting to get stepped on. 

She wasn't in the room. And what's more, the bedroom door was wide open, revealing a pitch black rectangle where the door should've been. I make my way out, unsure of what to make of things. I was sure I locked the door, damn sure of it! 

"Baba?" I called out. Nothing. But —wait no, there was something. Something in the dark. A sound. Sound like wet chewing, rough teeth gnawing at something fleshy, like fiber being ripped apart by malicious jaws. I tiptoed my way back to my room and took out the Smith and Wesson beneath the bed. As I made my way downstairs towards the sound, I smelt it. Blood and meat. Raw meat. And there was something else too, a soft glow and a buzzing emminating from down below. 

It was from the kitchen. Something was in the kitchen and it was eating my dog. That was what I'd decided. I ease my way down the stairs, air heavy, my heart in my head thrumming like drums against my temples, my breathing impossible to control. I aimed and made my way down, and finally I saw it. 

There, in the dark, stood the fridge wide open, and crouched beside it, a dark figure, too wide to be one of my kids, too gnarled and long to be my wife. It was eating something. I could see it. Soft flesh giving way to bone. It was eating Baba. 

"Hands where I can see 'em!" I screamed. It stopped.

"Stand up, slowly." I said. As it did, the figure revealed a towering height. Easily 7 foot and wide as a barn door. It raised its hands in the air with eerie gentleness. 

I slowly inched towards the wall, and without taking my eyes off the figure, I felt for the light switch. Part of me didn't want to flick the lights on. I wanted to shoot the intruder right then and there. What the hell was I gonna see? Some hobo eating a dog and rummaging through my fridge? 

Time stood still as fear wrestled with my volition. Seconds felt like minutes, as the dark silhouette of a man was echoed by the incessant buzz of the fridge. 

And then the man heaved forward.

Without another thought, I fired, and turned the lights on. At the report of gunfire, the figure lurched backwards, away from the sink. I missed. 

There, under the light of fluorescent bulbs, I saw something I will never forget. 

I heard footsteps running down the stairs, calling out to me in my ringing shock. 

"Dad!" Called my eldest. "Daddy!"

"Stay up there!" I screamed. "Go back to bed!" My other hand curled up against the pistol as I trained another shot at the thing in front of me. Some Titan of a man, if it was a man. Body leporous and scarred head to toe, bald and with long hair, like a woman's. It was tall, too tall, and its mouth was caked in blood, it's hands still holding on to flesh. 

But that wasn't the worst part. 

It had a collar on it. Benny's collar.
