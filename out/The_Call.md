When I was in college, I took it as an opportunity to reinvent myself.

After my brother, Daniel, died. I was no longer me, I was “the dead guy’s sister”. And in small towns, people talk. They gossip. It becomes impossible to go to the grocery store without an elderly person walking up to you saying something insensitive like..

“I heard you were the one who found him, oh poor thing.”

My first chance I had, I applied for a scholarship as far away as I could get. My parents didn’t have a bunch of money for out-of-state tuition, but they helped where they could. And I would have done anything to earn some extra cash.

Local dog walking, food delivery, babysitting 5 screaming kids at once.. That’s why when the opportunity came to host my school’s late night radio show, I jumped on it. I could make the same amount of money from all of my odd jobs for a fraction of the work.

The shift was easy. 11pm - 4am. The show had easy listening songs already cued up, I just had to take any calls that came through on our “Open Ears” segment. People would call in, ask for advice, but mostly just needed some venting space.

When my new boss Todd explained this to me, I was anxious.

“I don’t know if I’m the best advice giver..”, I had told him.

“Oh don’t worry, people just want someone to listen. I wouldn’t worry about it.”, he responded.

And he was right.

Monday through Friday, I sat in that tiny office, no bigger than a closet. Alone except for one of the producers who sat just outside the glass, helping slot songs and field calls. 

We got a handful of calls every evening. Anything from relationship horror stories to creepy professor experiences. And Todd was right, very rarely were people calling in to ask me for help. It was mostly a lot of sympathetic responses. Soothing words, a laugh when appropriate. Nothing had struck me as being weird or off-putting.

Until my last night.

It was a slow Tuesday, we weren’t getting any new calls so I let the guy who called in to complain about his frat go on without cutting him off. 

He was in the middle of complaining about early meeting times when thankfully, the light to signal a new call started flashing.

“Geo, I hear ya about those early morning socials. They can be brutal! My advice? Early bedtime, lots of water, and a hot coffee to start your morning! Helps to kick those sunrise blues. Thanks for calling!”, I said into the mic, while Andy shut off Geo’s call.

Maybe it was a little harsh, sure. But after 45 minutes of Geo’s world.. I had to get a break.

My producer of the night, Andy, pointed my direction and nodded, signaling the new caller was ready.

“Alright, we have another call coming through.. Hello! You are live on Open Ears, what have you got?”

Silence.

I looked up to Andy in confusion, he looked at his screen and shrugged.

“Hello? Caller are you with us?”

Silence again.

“Hmm.. Must be a bad signal folks. That’s alright, we have plenty of hits cued up for-“

“Hello?”, a small voice sounded through my headphones.

“Yes? Hello! I think we had a rough signal before..”, I say, turning the sound dial up a notch to hear them better.

“Yes. I think so.”, the voice responded. It was frail, like a kids. But we didn’t have kids call in, literally not once.

“Glad we could connect, so what do you want to talk out?”, I asked, crossing my arms and leaning back in my chair. Already daydreaming about the fourth mini bag of Doritos I would eat when the music started.

“I.. I think I need help.”, the voice responded.

“Yeah babe, don’t we all. Let me guess, boy troubles?”, I asked, already in my DJ mode.

“Kind of..”, she said, her voice sounded even quieter now, prompting me to turn up the notch again.

“Lay it on me, what’s his problem?”, I asked her.

“He’s.. not..”, she started, her voice mumbling on the last few words.

I sighed, turned up the volume again, and held my headphones closer to my ear.

“He’s what?”, I prompted.

“He’s not breathing.”, she whispered.

I wasn’t a stranger to prank calls, but my heart did drop for a second.

“Is it because you took his breath away?”, I asked, laughing dryly at my bad joke.

“No.. Because you did.”

My heart started beating erratically, I quickly looked to Andy. He had his headphones around his neck while he chatted cheerily on the phone.

I knew I was on my own.

“Oh this is a nice prank call, but our Halloween cast isn’t for a few more weeks. You’re too early!”, I said in a much cooler tone than I felt.

“I’m too early?”, the voice taunted, “Or is it that you were too late?”

I tried waving my arm to get Andy’s attention, but he wasn’t paying attention to me.

Todd was insistent that we never disconnect an engaging call, and I could see the listener count going up as the call went on. So I decided to suck it up, hang on a bit longer to make my boss happy.

I sighed heavily into the mic, rubbing my eyes with the back of my hands.

“Okay, enlighten me caller. What was I too late to?”, I asked in a monotone voice.

I heard a gurgled whisper.

Exasperated, I flipped the volume to the loudest it could go. Clutching the headphone to my ear.

“Caller, what was I too late to?”

The small voice screamed into my ears.

“DANIEL!”

The shock of volume had me yelp in surprise, twisting the notch down just as the frequencies began vibrating my ears with the most horrible sounds I’ve ever heard. The voice started screaming into the headphones. High-pitched and painful, never ending.

I took off my shoe and threw it at the window, finally getting Andy’s attention and gesturing at the setup for him to get it together.

Once I could tell the screaming had stopped, I put the headphones back on one ear. Expecting to hear silence from a disconnected call, but instead listening to soft sobbing.

I muted my microphone and slammed the headphones onto the desk. I then stood quickly and crossed to the glass partition.

“Why is she still here??”, I demanded.

I could see Andy’s brow furrowed from behind the screen.

“It’s not letting me disconnect. Give me a minute.”, he gruffed, “Just try to calm her down. Listeners are going crazy right now anyways.”

I looked back at the headphones on the desk, staring at them like they were a snake that was going to bite me.

How did this person know about Daniel? I hadn’t told anyone here about him. About the night I lost him. What kind of sick joke was this?

I should have walked out. Stormed out. Left Andy to deal with it. But I didn’t want to lose the coveted position funding my textbooks.

I numbly walked back to the desk, and slid the headphones on. The crying was continuing.

“Caller, do you need us to call someone for you?”, I asked, as indifferent as I could sound.

The crying continued, then slowly, the sobs turned into gasping. The gasping to laughing, the laughing to squealing.

“No, but I can call someone for you!”

I heard a dial tone, then three numbers pressed. Ringing began. I had hoped the call had hung up.

Ring.

Ring.

“911, what’s your emergency?”

“Hello? Hello? Please, I need help, he’s not breathing!”

Oh my god.

“Ma’am, where are you located?”

“My brother.. Oh my.. I think my brother is hurt.. I.. Oh my god.. NO!”

My hands began to shake as I listened to the 911 call I made 5 years ago, when I discovered Daniel in his room. 

I began to scream incoherent sentences to Andy, demanding for him to turn off the call.

“Please hurry, please.. Daniel.. Please.. Please hang on, please don’t leave me.. Please.. DANIEL!”

I listened to myself cry, scream. It’s like I could feel the tears on my face again. The feeling paralyzing me into place.

It was then I realized, that the crying from earlier was also my own sobs from that night. The screaming for my big brother’s life, was echoed back to my own ears.

I closed my eyes, I could faintly hear Andy in the other room ripping chords out of things in a panic, but I felt like I was floating.

“What do you want?”, I whispered, so quietly I wasn’t sure anyone heard me.

The 911 recording stopped immediately.

For a long beat, only silence. And then..

“I want you to never forget the blood.”

The phone line clicked, and then a dial tone filled my ears.

Andy ripped the headphones off my head, out of breath from the last 90 seconds of chaos.

“What the hell was that?? Todd is blowing me up going crazy! Did you put someone up to that? That was insane! We had 2,000 listeners just now! If we could make this a weekly segment I know you would get a bonus…”

I could hear Andy talking, but nothing was connecting. Like a light had been turned to dim in my head.

“What was that number?”, I asked, interrupting Andy.

“Uhhh.. I can pull it, one sec.”, he said, turning to his wireless laptop with the show pulled up.

I waited what felt like eons, counting my still erratic heartbeats while I waited.

It had to be an awful joke from someone I knew back home. It had to be.

“Ah, here it is. Look familiar?”, Andy said, turning the screen towards me.

And once I saw the 10 digits, it did.

Because it was one of the first numbers I ever memorized. 

And to a phone that didn’t exist anymore, with a line that had been cut a month after Daniel left us.

“Are you okay?”, Andy asked me.

I stared blankly at the screen, before placing my keycard on the desk slowly.

“Tell Todd that I quit.”, I said, before walking out of the office.

Andy called after me, but I didn’t stop. I couldn’t. My walking turned into a sprint, and I ran until I hit my apartment door, crying the whole way home.

I never found out who made that call, but they got what they wanted.

I would never forget the blood.
