It’s impressive how sobering a car accident can be. I hardly remember the drive, but I remember looking at my shaking hands hovering above the wheel. 

Small shards of glass resting on my fingers, reflecting the alternating lights. What an idiot.

It was about two years ago now that I was first approached for Project Truman. A bunch of guys in suits sat me in a room and told me that if I agreed to be a test subject, they would knock a hefty chunk of time off my sentence. Of course I agreed; believe me, I know I deserve to be in prison, but I wanted out nonetheless. 

A few days and a dozen signed documents later, I came face to face with Truman. It looked like they were about to execute me. Can’t remember what I was expecting beforehand, but with how official everything was, the room was quite unremarkable. 

One chair sat in the middle of a mostly empty room. It looked like a chair you’d see in a doctor's office, the kind with that really thick sheet of plastic on it. There was a horrid energy to it all; I remember my hair standing on end despite the heavy humidity in the room. Lights were dim and conversation was sparse. 

There was a window on one side of the room; I couldn't see them, but I could feel them watching me on the other side of the glass. Probably all scribbling various notes as I was hooked up to their machine. It was all so clinical. An IV was pressed into my wrist, and sensors were tapped to various parts of my flesh. 

All the while, who I can only assume was a doctor, explained to me what I could expect from the test. He seemed more nervous than I was. Maybe he was the lead behind it all, probably had a lot riding on the success of it all. 

“Dream manipulation.” He called it. The ability to conduct a dream through code that is then transmitted electrically through millions of tiny pins affixed to a helmet they place on my head. “Like a human Tesla coil.” He explained, trying to dumb it down to a level I could understand. 

He told me the IV would keep my body nourished and the chair will stimulate movement so my muscles don't experience atrophy. When I asked how long they planned to have me under, he shrugged and explained it was all up to how the test proceeded. That it should have said as much in the paperwork I definitely read. 

“It doesn’t really matter how long you're sleeping anyways-” He spoke as he made final adjustments to the helmet on my head. I could see the monitor on the table next to us, its measurements jumping as the device started picking up on my brain activity. I could see my growing stress being charted out. 

“Time as you know it is about to become irrelevant.” He finished. That stuck with me, it still does. I wasn’t just surrendering control of my body; my perception of reality was in the hands of someone else. 

They told me it was about rehabilitation. 

Sold me the idea that they wanted to have prisoners serve a sentence in their dreams so they could still contribute to society. Through our untethered perception of time while we're sleeping, they could make it so you serve 50 years in just a few months. 

But they needed subjects to test the limits of this system. To make sure the brain and body could maintain their health throughout the process. So they were going to put me through a series of dreams instead of one long one. Easy right? Go to sleep for a few days, wake up, and get to walk free. 

You wouldn’t be reading this if it was that simple, though. 

The IV pumped in a sedative, and my consciousness began to wane. I watched the doctor as he turned to observe the readings on his monitor. My vision started to get hazy, and my limbs grew heavy. And I was tugged by the weight of my own body into hell. 

It’s nearly impossible for me to place what happened while I was sleeping into any kind of sensical order. There was no first or last, because they’re all just memories to me now. Some of the memories were… nice. Again, I don’t know how any of it really works. If I had to guess, though, what they were doing to me in there, they had to throw some nice dreams in so my brain didn’t shut down from stress. 

Like, waterboarding my subconscious. There were fields of flowers; even now I can recall how it smelled. There was a first date, a pretty girl across the table smiling at me that I could've sworn I knew my whole life. But now, awake, she’s nothing but a stranger. The wine tasted so sweet, and the music was smooth and lively. 

Every dream carried all the sensations you’d experience while awake; it all felt so real and I felt so present. I was there; I could feel the petals of flowers under my fingertips. Those dreams, the sweet ones, never stuck around for long. They were just small refreshers before I was plunged. 

Sometimes, the kinder dreams would melt into madness. There was this water park, I stood at the top of a large waterslide. A long and dark spiraling slide led down to a massive pool of clear blue water. I could see and hear people playing down below. The feeling of water cooled air pressed against my cheeks as an uninterested attendant informed me that the slide was clear. It was my turn. 

Excitement rose in my chest as I sat down and felt the rushing water at my back; with confidence, I pushed myself into the tube. Quickly, I was plunged into darkness, sunlight behind me vanishing as I rounded the first corner. The cool waters carried me down the slide, my body jerking from one side to the other as I hit curve after curve. My heels occasionally smacked against the dark plastic. 

My excited screams echoed through the tube until it sounded like another me shouting back. Then an unease settled in my heart. Each twist and turn I took should have brought me closer to the end of the slide, only I felt like I wasn't making progress. 

It started to feel like minutes had gone by, but still, each turn revealed no inkling of escape. My screaming stopped, and all I could hear was sloshing water and the occasional thump when my body smacked against the plastic. I knew I was moving, if my fingertips rested gently on the sides of the slide I could feel the cool surface rushing by me. But I wasn't going anywhere. 

Panic set in and my breathing grew heavy. Every time my body would tap the slide it was a cruel reminder. I should have left the slide; from the outside there were only a handful of turns, but I had made dozens. If not hundreds. Time was impossible to calculate; distance too. Sometimes I would pick up speed and others I would lazily drift along. I had enough stimulus to remain present but was stripped of will, carried along, in darkness. 

There’s no way of knowing how long I was stuck there for. Beginnings and endings all fall apart in Truman. Now, looking back, it’s like it all happened in an instant, but I feel the truth; I can remember how the dreams stretched minutes into hours like taffy. I remember the lives they had me live. 

Never really put much thought into having kids during college, and it didn’t seem like something worth thinking about in prison. But I know how it felt in the dreams. The memories of my children stick with me. They would create snapshots of a life in the dreams, create a character for who I was. 

Woke up one morning to the feeling of my daughter jumping around trying to wake me up, too excited for her trip to the park to sleep. My wife, child and I sat at the table and ate breakfast together. In this dream, I remembered a life that was never mine. I remembered watching my little girl take her first steps. I remembered standing at the altar, moments away from officially being married. So many memories, none of them mine. 

After our kid gobbled up her eggs as fast as she could, we were on our way. The drive took about two hours. This gave us enough time to play my child’s favorite CD twice in the car, much to my chagrin. The park was fun, a hot day for sure, but the wait times were short, and the rides were enjoyable. It was a really good day. 

Then it was time for the car ride home. Which meant two more plays of the very same CD. The sun was starting to get low, and the CD got about halfway through its list of songs for the second time. And just as the group of singers started to sing about how fun it can be to clean up your room, our car was thrown off the road. 

My knuckles got white as I gripped the wheel after impact, trying to mitigate rotation as much as I could, but once we hit the guardrail, the wheels no longer had contact with the ground, and we flipped. Some drunk prick driving a pickup truck had fallen asleep at the wheel and cruised through the intersection. 

My consciousness faded in and out, fighting to stay awake. The knuckles that had been gripping the wheel were scraping against the roof of the overturned vehicle. My body felt battered but had no noticeably severe pain. It did hurt me to turn my neck. What I saw when I was able to pivot my gaze has cemented itself in my mind. 

My wife dangled in the passenger seat, body limp and mangled, having taken the brunt of the force. A complete ruin of the woman I remembered spending my life with. There was an even deeper pit, though. One I couldn't stomach the thought of facing. I wonder if it was really my choice not to look in the backseat. Or was it scripted? Was anything I did in the dreams they put me through of free will? 

It’s only now that I am able to realize what they had done. How they had made me live through the life of someone who fell victim to the very crime I had perpetrated. Imagine that: a murderer who is forced to dream of being killed in the same manner. Over, and over and over, each time just as fresh and horrifying as the last. 

They can pick and choose how much you remember from one dream to the next. They can even decide whether or not you're aware that you're dreaming. There was a dream that I was treading water at sea as a storm approached. I knew I was dreaming; I was completely aware that my body was relatively safe, strapped to a chair and being monitored. This knowledge did nothing to calm the terror. 

Knowing that I was dreaming didn’t stop the feeling of water filling my lungs. It didn’t stop my nose from stinging as I desperately gasped for air. It didn’t slow my heartbeat when I was pushed under the water by thrashing waves. The clouds still darkened the sky, and my legs still grew tired from kicking. The impending notion of death still gripped my heart as the current became too much to fight against. 

My body sank under the water. Drowning was always a fear of mine. I wonder if they had somehow gotten that information. Knowing someone’s deepest fears and being able to use them as a weapon. Lungs started to ache, unable to draw another breath. Like a rock my body drifted down further. A few desperate flails of my limbs yielded nothing and I was swallowed by the sea. Other times, you’ll have no idea you’re dreaming. And just like when you dream, the brain is completely convinced that everything unfolding before you is perfectly normal. Like that first date I mentioned earlier? I recall that during the dream, I hadn’t thought anything was out of place. It's only now that the cruelty of the dream settles in. 

Sitting across the table, stabbing some lumpy mess of meat with her fork, was the same woman I had gotten into a car accident with in the other dream. Like she was just some reused asset. She sat there, smiling away as if there wasn’t a large shard of gnarled metal weighing down the right side of her head. 

She paid no attention to the dripping of red from her various cuts, all dripping down and staining the white shirt adorned with shattered glass, sparkling like sequin beads. We spoke between us and laughed as the dinner carried on. Her voice was guttural, with frothy red bubbles of spit dripping out occasionally. None of it seemed strange at the time. 

“You’re a murderer.” She said it like it was some inside joke. One that made us both laugh heartily. Until my chest started to burn we laughed all the while each shift of her head drove the metal deeper in like she was smacking against an unseeable wall. 

Mind you, I was just a test subject. They flicked from one dream to the next, channel surfing to find a show they liked. They were testing the parameters they could work within. And again, all I can do is speculate. But still I have my guesses.

That feels like it really happened to me; I can't shake it. Any of them. They ring more like memories. There are so many. And that’s just the tame stuff. There are still all the things they can do in dreams that defy the laws we understand in the conscious world. 

I’ve been alone in the woods, being stalked by creatures that are typically reserved to myths and legends. Large, hulking creatures that want only to rip me apart. Dreams where I was out and about and could just feel something was off. Now and then, I catch a glimpse of something in the distance. Something I knew wasn’t supposed to be there, and my chest would get tighter as it got closer. I’ve watched myself scream hysterically at some force just out of my peripheral vision. Something so awful and foreign that no human mind could conjure it, pure insanity. 

It is all up for grabs in Truman. One of the final dreams was one I was warned about prior and was told was the primary purpose of Project Truman. Serving time. It was a simple dream to create and understand. Just a room. Walls, floor, and ceiling, the same shade of concrete grey. 

The air was just a little colder than you would like. No beds, no sink or potty. They gave me nothing. I was always tired but couldn’t sleep. I was always hungry but was provided no food. It was a dream, so my biological functions were ultimately unnecessary, and so all I could do was stand up or lie down. 

I cannot possibly put a number on how long I spent in that room. It felt like ages. My mind started to go so numb I stopped feeling like a person. Just some strange approximation of life floating around an empty room. No idea when it would end, and slowly beginning to believe that it never would. I knew I was dreaming, but not knowing when the dream would end was a constant grip of fear. 

When I think back on that dream, my body stiffens up and I feel a lifetime of wear wash over me for a moment. Eventually I woke up again in the hospital chair to the doctor removing the sensors off my body. I could feel how ragged my body had become. They kept me nourished and kept my body from rotting but there was still the stiffness of laying in one position for several days. 

The doctor then ran through some questions with me that I stumbled through to the best of my abilities. My brain was still piecing together what was reality and what was manufactured and before I really got my bearings, I was told it was time for me to leave. One of the guards entered and helped me out of the chair before leading me to the door of the room. 

We walked through together and my heart sank. Looking around me I came to realize I had just walked into the same grey room I believed I had woken up from. Looking behind me I found myself alone again. The door I walked through and the guard had vanished. It was just me again. 

Seconds turned into minutes, minutes fed to hours, and my second stretch of time in that grey room grew larger and larger. All sense of self melted away. Just a body, nothing human about it. 

When I woke up for a second time, my trust in reality was gone. I stuttered through the same question the doctor asked me in the dream. There wasn’t a moment that passed where I wasn't waiting for the shoe to drop. Every door I opened, every blink of my eyes, I was waiting for the room to return. I didn’t trust the feeling of the bed in my cell, or the words spoken to me by lawyers and guards. I had become a shell. 

Even when I was finally released from that prison, given my early freedom, I can't shake the feeling that I'm still dreaming. That’s the point, though, isn’t it?
