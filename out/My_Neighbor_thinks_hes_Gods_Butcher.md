The “story” I’m about to tell you is 100% true. I moved into an apartment building. It wasn’t the nicest, but it was what I could afford. Shortly after I moved in—within an hour, actually—I had my first interaction with a neighbor down the hall from me.

Not a direct interaction, but he was screaming out his window. A lot. He would go on and on, nonstop, screaming things like, “God is coming! The end is here!”, “All shall burn and perish, repent for your sins!” and “Die, f\*\*got, die!” and “Kill yourselves, ni\*\*ers!”

This was basically nonstop. I don’t know when the man slept, because it was an all-day, all-night thing, every day and every night. There were times when he would stop for a few hours. I imagine that must have been when he was sleeping, but it wasn’t a lot of sleep if that was the case. Sometimes he’d even throw glass bottles at people on the street below, screaming that same bullshit.

A few days after I moved in, I found out he also marched up and down the hall, shouting at people. The first time I saw this, I went down to the apartment manager. She told me that he had been diagnosed with paranoid schizophrenia and that, as scary as he might seem, he was perfectly harmless. As far as she knew, he’d never hurt anyone. She also told me that his parents lived in our apartment building and would be able to take care of him.

Mind you, this was at the start of COVID. I’d lost my job and really had nothing to do, so I was watching pretty frequently as all this was going on, which was nearly daily at this point. I never ONCE saw his parents. Usually, he’d end up going back into his apartment on his own, or the cops would have to get involved. Usually the latter.

There was one time it got particularly bad. I think we had two Black families living on my floor, and there were two groups of people he absolutely hated: gay people and Black people. He saw the father of one of those families as the man was leaving or entering his apartment. I’m not really sure which. They were on the same side of the hall as me, but at the opposite end, so I couldn’t really see what was going on unless I opened my door and stuck my head out—which I knew better than to do at this point.

He charged down the hall after him, and things got pretty heated. I don’t know exactly what happened, but I know guns got involved. The cops came and got my neighbor, but he was right back in his apartment the next day.

Months of this went by, and eventually, I decided to move. Not just to a different apartment, but to a different state entirely. I couldn’t handle state as a whole anymore.

Two days before it was time for me to move, I had one last interaction with him. This particular interaction will haunt me until the day I die. It was also the one that made me the happiest to be leaving.

He was making his rounds up and down the hall, as he always did. I was watching through the peephole when he stopped in front of my door. He looked directly at the peephole, as if he knew I was watching. It was like he was staring straight into my soul through that hole.

And he said, “Men are like cattle, meant to be slain and eaten, and I am God’s butcher.” Immediately after saying that, he pressed himself up against my front door for a minute, then continued on his way.

One of the neighbors called the cops, who stopped him in the hallway, and I could hear the discussion they were having. When speaking with them, he sounded calm, coherent, and logical. And he said some things that me realize that he was completely in touch with reality, which made the comment about slaying and eating men even more unsettling.

The cops got him back into his apartment, but he was at it again later that day. This time, my curiosity got the better of me, and I decided to follow him. I stayed well behind him, at what I thought was a safe distance, and for the first time, I found out that he didn’t just stay in our hallway. He went to other parts of the building.

I followed him through the back staircases, by the elevators, and down to the basement level. There was a very unpleasant smell down there. It wasn’t ridiculously strong or unbearable, just unpleasant. Underneath the stairs was– well, I don’t know, I couldn’t actually see it. Whatever was under the tarp was probably between 5 and 6 feet long and 4 feet wide. I was behind him, so I couldn’t see much. But I could see his arms moving, he was reaching under the tarp and moving and bring something up to his face. I could hear a quiet, somewhat wet squishing sound, and I could hear him chewing. Whatever, perhaps whoever, it was under that tarp, was being devoured. 

I ran and got my stuff and moved out of that building, so that was the last I saw of him. Thankfully. 

I try not to think about what was under that tarp, but I do still wonder if his parents ever came around to take care of him, like the apartment manager said they would. Somehow, I have the feeling they couldn’t…
