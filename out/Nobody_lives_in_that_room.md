I rented a room in an attic a few years ago. The room at the top of the house was mine and I was overjoyed to get it. It was big, quiet, and I technically had my own bathroom at first on the floor below, that was supposed to be shared with the person who lived on that floor. But the room there was not rented.

But after half a year a woman named Mary moved in. She rented the room in the second floor and we shared the bathroom. I never saw her use it or come out of her room except to cook and do laundry for herself. I never saw her talk to the other tenant who lived on the first floor.

When spring came, I thought it would be nice to invite Mary to plant some things in the garden boxes out back so I went to her room to ask her. I rarely saw her save for the times she came down to cook and do laundry. She was always in a nightgown in the morning and I never saw her in the evening, so I figured she worked later than me or worked from home. She didn’t talk much, but that was ok, she just moved in.

It was evening and dark out, the door to her room was closed but the lights were on and I could hear her walking around inside so I knocked on the door to ask about the garden thing. She didn’t answer. It kind of bothered me. I knew she knew I was there.

The next morning was a Saturday. I hoped to see Mary in the kitchen as she usually prepared breakfast at the same time as me on weekends. But she was not there. I assumed she was at home since her light was on in her room. I decided to knock on her door again, but again no answer.

The Monday next week, I went downstairs to get ready for work. Mary’s door was open. Her room was empty. Vacant of furniture, it was completely cleared out. Just an empty room. Wood floors and a large window facing the sun. 

I rushed downstairs to see if the other tenant was downstairs and knew what happened to Mary. “Where did she go?” I asked, nervously. Dan, my downstairs roommate was making breakfast for himself and looked up at me with a look of total confusion on his face. “Who?” he asked. “Mary, she’s gone.. did she move out?” 

Dan looked at me with wide eyes, “huh?”

“The lady in the room on the second floor!” I yelled. 

“Nobody lives in that room, Emily. You know that.” He said. Angrily.

I walked upstairs to my room after - walking through the middle floor where “Mary’s” room was. The room that now stood empty. 

That night I tossed and turned, contemplating what had gone on with Mary - was I delusional? I had to go to the bathroom that night well after midnight. I tip toed down the stairs to the floor to where “Mary’s room” was but the door to her room was shut. I could see a faint hint of light from beneath the door. And, the shadow of footsteps from within.

I approached the door on my tip toes. My hand grasped the handle to Mary’s room door and I twisted it. It was open, not locked. I opened it a crack and peaked in. At the centre of the room was a silhouette - a woman’s figure in a nightgown. The figure was almost transparent. She was suspended just above the ground. Feet pointing towards the wood floor. The light I’d seen from beneath the door before going in was the moon, a full, almost fluorescent moon that lit only the shapes if faced, but cast dark shadows behind that which it lit.

Looking up I saw that the silhouette of the woman was hanging from what I could make out appeared to be the shape of a rope. I could see the moon outline the outline of a noose around her neck.  Hanging from a banister from the ceiling of the room. I froze. Then ran back to bed. Paralyzed with fear. I then fell into a deep, unsettling sleep.

The next morning, I woke up hoping the entire ordeal was a dream. 

I moved out shortly after. 

A few months later I found a book detailing some of the town’s history that included a chapter about a woman who hung herself in one of the homes in the same neighbourhood as the one where I met Mary.

The circumstances were unclear, but a woman had apparently hung herself in one of those houses. 

Her husband was a man about town, his personality brimmed with booze and spirits during the hours he entertained clients and friends, leaving his wife at home. A business man who inherited a successful company his parents started. He entertained himself and clients every night after work. Until the pubs and saloons closed, and every bottle on hand ran went dry. 

He’d womanize, gamble, and on nights he came home, he’d have drunk himself senseless. The clothe’s his wife had laundered, starched, pressed for him untucked, stained, soaked in the sweat of dark liquor. 

If he’d not found company in a woman his money or booze could solicit that night, he’d try to force himself on his sleeping wife. She’d try and push him off and if she could he’d punish her. Drag her off the bed by her hair. Fists to her ribs and stomach on the floor as she screamed. Until she was able to flee to the room on the second floor. A room she’d changed the lock on herself without telling her husband.Who himself was too drunk to resolve to get the door lock changed again so he could get a key. 

But he was a man about town. A successful business his parents started that he’d inherited. A company run by his staff, not him. The profits lining his pocket and sustaining his habits. 

One morning she’d locked herself in that room before he came home that morning. He’d have no one to throw himself on, no one to unleash the resentment he had for himself on, either. After the mirth and mania of his liquor wore off, the misery and shame of his own decisions found release in menacing and violent expressions aimed at his innocent wife.

That morning she locked herself in the room before he found her in bed. She loathed the smell of his breath and hated his slurring speech as he forced himself on her, if she got away he’d drag her violently out of the bedroom and bunch her stomach and ribs for rejecting him. She’d usually find a way to get into the room on the second floor. The one where she had the only key. The one he was too stupid to realize she had the only key. Where he’d finally give up and go to bed once she found safety there after yelling and berating at her from behind the door outside. 

“I know you’re in there, you’ll come out eventually! When you do, expect double what you got last time!”

This morning she was in the room already before he came in. He knew she was because he could see the light coming from underneath the door. He banged and banged and yelled but she didn’t answer. He could hear her walking around. He was furious. 

After few minutes he heard something move around like a piece of furniture. He then waited and heard something fall on the ground, a table or stool or something. He kept banging on the door, threatening to kill her if she was breaking anything. But he eventually stopped banging and yelling at the door. He couldn’t hear her footsteps anymore. There was silence, for hours.

“I know you’re in there! Open the door!” He tried again after a few hours dozing off some of the previous night’s libations. When there was no response he finally lost it.

He so furious that he broke the door down. There in the room Mary hung from a light fixture, a noose around her neck, her feet just a few inches from the ground, lifeless.

A note sat on a small table by the window. “No one can live in this room.” He clenched it in his fist.

The scene left him breathless, and then a pain in his chest. He grabbed his heart and collapsed to the floor. There beneath Mary’s body he pleaded out in agony, “someone, help me!” But there was no one else in the house to hear him. There was only Mary’s lifeless body in the room with him, hanging from a rope.

A few weeks later I ran into Dan, the tenant who lived downstairs. He still lived there. “So, do you remember that ghost I saw?” I asked him. “Yeah,” Dan said. “I do.” I went on to tell him about the story I’d heard about Mary and the monster she’d had as a husband that I’d heard about a house I that neighbourhood. “Yeah, I know,” Dan said. “SO you didn’t believe me about the ghost, but knew the story?” I asked. “No that’s not what I meant.” He said. “I said no one lives in that room!” I didn’t understand why he was yelling at me. “No one lives in that room!” He said again. “Don’t you understand, Emily?” I finally did. 

No one lives in that room. 

They all die.
