“Ace! We’re ready. But, you still have –” I interject, hand on my chest in apology. 

“Hey. Ha–sorry, I apologize. Quick question.“ The words came as a broken spurt. Like a faucet running out of water. “Nearest bathroom?” I throw a hand up in a point, aiming in various directions, waving it around like a broken human compass. 

“Behind ya, boss. Anything else? Good luck tonight by the way! The garden.. Wow. I’m so happy for you guys. Break a leg out there!” She put a finger to her headset, listening to someone speak, continuing with her job. Lost in her work. 

“Nope. Thank you kindly.” I turned and left for the restroom. Hoping I’d make it in time. I raised my voice without losing momentum towards the bathroom. 

“Don’t call me boss.”

Getting to the bathroom corridor I noticed it’s the same hallway with the window to the Atlantic. I entered the Men’s room. 

Focus.

I quickly scanned the bathroom, choosing to take the big stall with its own sink. The last stall on the right. I froze and analyzed a new sensation. Vibration? Oh, my phone. Another text came through.

Whatever. Not right now. I ignored the phone. Deep breaths, relax. My thoughts raced. The nausea was rampant now, requiring me to lean against the stall wall. 

I reached for the sink and turned on the water. Noise. Something to ground myself. I splashed my face. Gripping the sides of the sink I took another deep breath. Where did my water bottle go? Whatever. 

I grabbed the towel from my pocket and proceeded to soak it, before squeezing it dry, and draping it over my neck. I cupped my hands under the stream and gave my face another few splashes.

What the hell is going to happen next? I close my eyes and sigh. A fucking dragon?

Focus.

The bathroom entrance door swung open.

“Five minutes, Ace.” A female voice echoed over the piss-stained urinals. 

“ –Thanks. Thank you Veronica.” The door slammed closed before I could say her name. She didn’t wait for a reply. That’s odd but understandable. She’s always keeping busy. 

Her and Alan have been talking about things quietly lately. Not really avoiding me? But not including me. I think they are buying a new house. Their old apartment must be getting too small. 

Veronica. That’s someone that can make me feel better. She’s real. She’s reality. Just outside that door. 

All this crazy shit is in my head. I’ve taken my medication. No change in the routine. No ears ringing. No little black and white lines zig-zagging through my view when the depression rolls in. I can tell the damn difference between crazy and something else. This was all something else. 

If I take the meds? I’m fine. It’s why I haven’t mentioned it. The stereotypes and preconceived notions are too thick to navigate in a story like this. I’m depressed due to an abundance of understanding, anyhow.

You need to know what happened to me, real or not. Four minutes to go by now. 

I left the bathroom stall, opening the door carefully, looking around. Nobody. I left the bathroom. Opening the door, the sounds and smells of the stage hit me at once. Where’s Veronica?

I checked my phone. Two missed texts. I leaned against the wall next to the bathroom doors, ignoring passersby. 

Fred:  
Ace where did you run off to? Going to see Willow play a bit, then I’m out of here. My brother ended up in the hospital. Not sure why yet! I swear dude. –Fred

Yes, he signs his name after every text. And in each voicemail. Okay, second text.

Veronica:  
I can’t get to you. You are on in 6-8 minutes.

It took me a moment to put it together. What does she mean–the realization hit me at once. The hair on my skin stood on end, a chill went down my back. 

In hindsight? This was the moment I should have cancelled the show. Or at the very least, consider it. But I didn’t. I assumed everything would be fine when on stage.
