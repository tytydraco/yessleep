My nephews asked me to tell them a horror story—but on one condition: it had to be true. I do not know why children these days have grown so fond of horror stories without ever seeming afraid. They do not tremble at tales of jinn, devils, and those malevolent spirits that might be sharing the very same room with you, unseen... watching you intently, perhaps even staring straight at you while you sleep until their breath brushes against your face. You wake up, feeling that a strange hand was reaching toward you—or had already pulled the blanket away—yet you find nothing at all. To me, that is the most terrifying thing in the world... indeed, in all existence.

But children these days no longer seem to care. Is it because they are braver than we ever were? Or are we simply the naïve generation that still believes in what cannot be touched? We are the ones who never dismiss the gentle sway of a curtain as nothing more than the wind teasing it, but imagine instead an evil spirit hovering nearby, causing our skin to crawl and our hair to stand on end—to the point where we might lose the ability to move and close the window completely, to stop that mischievous breeze from slipping inside to tamper with our peaceful dreams.

Or perhaps children today simply feel nothing at all. They have become desensitized; if the Devil himself were to manifest before them in plain sight, they would probably play hide-and-seek with him, and invite him to return every single night!

At any rate, the children wanted a frightening story, and their primary condition was that it had to be personal—something that had actually happened to me, or to someone I trust completely. As a matter of fact, I have many stories of that kind.

So I said to them: *"Very well... One night, we were walking when, suddenly, the power cut out and the whole town was swallowed by darkness. We happened to be passing near a place called the 'Old School,' which people claimed was built over an ancient cemetery. Not an ordinary cemetery, either, but one for strangers who were found dead in strange and brutal ways: hollowed-out bodies, or those drowned in the river."*

Whenever someone drowned, the women would run through the town screaming in terror: *"Drowned! Drowned! Drowned!"* Every eye in town would turn toward the corpse drifting with the current, yet no one dared approach it, as though the dead might suddenly return to life and take revenge on the very first hand to touch it.

At last, after the body was recovered by the fishermen—who possessed vast experience in dealing with the river's dead—it would be buried in that cemetery over whose remains the Old School was eventually built.

The disturbing—and revolting—thing was that those graves, before they ever became graves, had served as an unofficial dumping ground. You could throw anything there at any time; no one would stop you, and no one talked about the environment or the foul air. Even if you threw a corpse into that place, nobody would care.

And one day, that is exactly what happened. People found a corpse in the rubbish, its features mutilated. They stood around it for a while, asking foolish questions such as: *Who is this? What brought him here? Is he a friend or an enemy? Maybe he’s a stranger—a thief.* His mutilated features were unlike anything they had ever seen.

In the end, after hours of flies gathering around the corpse and the stench of decay spreading through the air, the jackals that descended at night from the nearby desert hills gathered and devoured it completely. That is why people say that if you stare into a jackal's eyes for too long, you begin to feel as though you are not looking into the face of an ordinary animal, but into the face of another human being.

For that reason, whenever my eyes fall upon a jackal, my whole body shivers, and I feel a chill in my legs—even if it is nothing more than an image in a wildlife documentary on television. I always feel that it is watching me, looking at me alone, seeing me as a dead body it intends to tear apart at any moment, just as it did to the corpse of that unknown man.
