 I was never a “party girl” growing up, so I never expected to be working as a bartender in my twenties. My college years, like high school, have so-far been filled with extra-curricular activities, honors classes, and unfortunately, not a lot of time to work a steady job. That was, until a few weeks ago. My Uncle Rusty and his wife, Gaila have always been very close to me growing up. For years, they have owned a little hole-in-the-wall saloon on XXX Road called “The Rusty Tap.” Gaila suggested I take up the late shift as a bartender, because it would allow for me to take on all of my classes during the day, and Uncle Rusty wouldn't mind if I finished up my homework during my shifts at night. She also added that the patrons were mostly just Uncle Rusty’s old college friends, so I wouldn’t get a lot of slack from the men there, but I *would* get lots of tips. I took Uncle Rusty and Gaila up on their offer, and for one whole month, it was the perfect gig. Until it wasn’t.   
Last night was like any other, at first. I suited up in my all-black outfit, strapped on my apron, and began doing my homework before the regulars trickled in. Ed, the head chef, and I would be the only ones working tonight from 4:00 PM to midnight. After that, we would be free to leave when the cleaning guy came. Usually, Uncle Rusty checked in on the place, but Gaila had caught a case of Covid a few weeks ago, and as an ex-cancer patient, her immune system wasn’t taking it very well. Rusty told me he was going to stay the night in the hospital with her, and she should be discharged sometime the next day. He made sure to let me know that I could text him if I needed anything, because after all, “it’s just a 5 minute drive away!”  
The evening was slow, but that wasn’t surprising. Most of the guys didn’t come in until around 5:00. For the next hour, I sat on the sticky floor beneath the bar and finished my homework. When I was done, Ed and I passed time by tossing some darts. Ed may have been the tap’s cook, but he never really cooked much. He and I had gotten to be friends, and I was thankful that the job offered both good pay and good company. At one point, Ed turned around, back toward the dart board, and held his throwing arm above his head. Eyes closed, he wobbled like a wild animal. 

“What are you doing?” I laughed as he rared the dart back a couple times, facing the wrong direction.

“Kicking your ass at darts,” he remarked, throwing the dart in an awkward, backwards motion behind his head. The dart zipped through the air and “thudded” stiffly when it landed. It missed the dartboard by about six feet, instead, sinking deeply into the home-made wooden sign that Uncle Rusty had proudly fixed to the wall, which read:

“**The Rusty Tap: We Don’t Shut Down Until You Do**.” 

“Nice goin’, Ed,” I snorted at him. He laughed, both of us knowing we would have to hope Uncle Rusty didn’t notice. Uncle Rusty hung that sign there because even though we closed at 12:00, he never locked the doors until the last customer had had their last glass. It was as close to an official “company policy” that The Rusty Tap had, and the customers never abused that rule.  
Around 5:00, the regulars started showing up. Per usual, I never had to make anything more complicated than a beer, and Ed didn’t even have to fire up the grill. The guys came, had a few drinks, asked me how Gaila and Rusty were doing, and eventually all left. By 11:30, all of the regulars had come and gone, and that is when the weirdness began.   
Around that time, a stiff little customer entered the building. He seemed to float through the doors, leaving nothing but a jingling doorbell in his wake. I immediately felt uneasy by his presence. He was short but somehow lanky in stature, and had a receding hairline that appeared to sit unnaturally far back on his pale head. His facial expression was polite… but it felt stiff. Almost as if his tight-lipped grin was plastered widely across his face with cement. His eyes were small, yet opened very wide in a glazed-over type of look. I tracked him cautiously as he made his way to the furthest table from me, sitting impossibly pin-straight in military fashion. He settled in his chair without a word, staring at me with that glossy, wide smile.   
I almost ignored him. We were *just* about to close. But then I realized that, since I didn’t recognize him, he must have been new here, and he probably expected me to take his order. After taking a shaky breath, I walked over to him, menu in hand.              

“Welcome to Rusty’s, can I take your order today? Maybe a beer?” I said, hoping a conversation would make this man seem less… *mechanical.* Instead, he turned his head just so, as if to track my voice. His eyes were empty, looking through me rather than at me. Smile still wide, he began to speak. 

“No thanks. I will have one  steak, please. Rare.” His voice reminded me of the dryer buzzers at the laundromat, monotone and whining. I shivered, the way he spoke sounded wrong in my ears, like a disembodied voice, or an English-dubbed foreign film, it just did not match up quite right with the way his lips moved. I made my way to the kitchen window behind the bar and shot Ed a desperate look. He sighed.

“Real weirdo out there, huh?” He whispered. I nodded. “Look, he doesn’t seem dangerous, just weird. One steak and I gotta get out of here, it’s my daughter’s birthday.” 

 “What?” I hissed, “Can’t you just stay until the cleaning guy comes? Don’t leave me here.”

 “Linds, I gotta go. Call your uncle. He’ll know what to do.”   
And with that, Ed began firing up the grill. Rare steaks didn’t take long to cook up, but I was praying that the cleaning guy would show up before it got done. I checked my phone, but it wasn’t even quite 11:45 yet. I cursed in my head before looking up at the strange little man. He was looking right at me, that tight, polite smile beaming through me like a laser. Something was wrong with him, I knew it. As he glared blankly ahead, sitting as poised as a statue, I averted my gaze. My foot tapped anxiously and my stomach churned as I felt his stare on the back of my neck. I knew he had never been here before. I would have remembered that awful smile, that cold, pale skin.   
My mind began to wander for I'm not sure how long. He was wearing a stark, white collared shirt and khakis.   
It was a Tuesday night, why was he dressed so pristinely? He didn’t ask for a menu, but he somehow knew we had steak. That means he must have come here before, right? Surely, If I looked at him again, something about his distinct face would seem familiar.   
I turned to the man, who had not moved an inch. He looked to be about fifty, judging by his receding hair and wrinkles, but his skin looked so youthful and soft… maybe he was in his late twenties. I turned back around in fear he would see me, tears welling up in my eyes. 

*Don’t leave me here, Ed.* I thought the words to myself, but I couldn’t muster the courage to actually say them.   
I heard the gas stove ‘click’ off, and watched Ed plate the steak, hang up his apron, and walk out of the kitchen doors and into the bar. He sat the steak on my counter and bid me goodnight. I was so angry that I could have killed him at that moment, but I didn't even *move*. I just stood there, completely helpless, and watched Ed slip out the door. I turned to the little man, still frozen, his fingers crossed politely on the table. I cautiously picked up the plate and walked to him, setting it down. I made my way back to the bar, neither of us speaking a word. He was staring into his plate now, just looking over it, and I dare not take my eyes off of him. I was terrified of looking away. He stared down into his plate for what seemed like hours, smiling at the bloody steak.   
I quickly popped in an airpod and called my Uncle Rusty, but there was no answer. I thought about slipping out the back door. I know Uncle Rusty wouldn’t blame me for feeling unsafe here, but then my eyes shifted up to the sign on the wall, now dawning the dart Ed had foolishly graced it with. “**The Rusty Tap: We Don’t Shut Down Until You Do**.”   
If anything happened to the bar, it would be my fault, regardless of if Uncle Rusty cared or not. This guy didn’t appear to be dangerous, just a creep. ‘I was overreacting,’ I convinced myself, and looked back up at the man.

My heart dropped to the floor.

The man was now salivating over the untouched steak… beaming into the plate with a smile that opened up further than logically possible. A long web of drool was dangling off of his disgusting, unnatural grin. I checked my watch. It was 11:55, with no sign of the cleaning guy. It was probably good that he *didn’t* come in, because I would have ruined his day if he had. I knew I had to be dreaming, or possibly even going insane.   
Ed obviously didn't find the guy as weird as I did, or he wouldn't have left me here all alone, would he? My mind was a swirling pool of fear and anger. My throat tightened and my fingers went cold. I had no idea what to do, and the pressure of that uncertainty bore on my shoulders like an impossible, heavy anvil.

*‘I could call the police,’* I thought to myself, ‘*but for what? A guy drooling over food? I would be the laughing stock of the police department, and possibly even get in trouble for harassment. Maybe this guy has a medical issue?’*

Then, the man finally moved. I watched as he picked up the steak with bare hands, cramming the entire bloody thing into his mouth. Ruby juices dripped onto his pristine, white, button up and painted his pale face. For a moment, his jaw seemed to unhinge unnaturally wide, and his eyes seemed to bulge— but only for an instant— leaving me wondering if they actually had. I watched his fingers dig into the steak in a primal fashion as he crammed all ten of his fingers into his mouth, pushing the hunk of meat down his throat. His hands followed, disappearing into his maw. I couldn’t take it. I ducked behind the bar.   
Was this real? I wondered if this man was even human. Everything about him was wrong. The image of his contorted face burned into the backs of my eyelids, and it was only then that I realized my eyes were shut.  
I began to cry and dial 9-1-1, not knowing what else to do. Right as my finger hovered above the glowing green “dial” button,  I  heard the gentle *‘ding’* of the doorbell. I popped up and saw that his table was empty. Through the tinted window, the silhouette of the stiff man was rounding the building corner.  
 I wasted no time. I jumped over the bar and sprinted toward the door, locking the deadbolt in what seemed like one, quick motion.   
I collapsed into the floor against the door and sobbed. Now that this was all over, it felt far away, like a fever-dream. I was so scared of… what? A man smiling? A weirdo not using a fork?   
My hands were shaking. My throat was burning. I felt relieved, but also more frightened than before. I couldn’t help but wonder if I had imagined the whole thing.   
I brushed myself off and began to buss the man’s table with fingers trembling, which despite his sloppy style of eating, was left perfectly clean. The plate looked as if it had been freshly washed. There was no sign anyone had even sat there at all, except for a small, handwritten note. It was scribed in such neat penmanship that I thought it had been printed at first. Upon closer inspection it read:

I DIDN’T LIKE THAT MUCH.

I threw the note away. I would have rather had no reminders that this had ever happened. All I wanted to do was go home and sleep this nightmare off. I locked down the bar and grabbed a bag of trash on my way out. It was usually the cleaning guy's job to take out the trash, but he obviously wasn’t coming, I didn’t want it sitting there all night.   
I slipped out the back door, in a hurry, just in case. I walked into the back alley where my car was parked, conveniently by the dumpster, concealed by a ply board gate. I heaved the trash bag over my shoulder as I brought a hand to the gate, about to push it open, when I heard a strange sound.   
There was a rustling noise behind the dumpster gate. I stepped back slowly, ready to abandon my trashbag in the alley, when something furry rushed past my feet. My eyes locked in on the creature, now sitting across the alley. It was just a gray tabby, shyly licking its paws in the dim light.   
I let out a relieved breath. The alley cats that were having a heyday of our scraps. I pushed open the door with a false sense of security. Nothing could have prepared me for the reality of what was *actually* making the rustling sound.   
There, stooped over in the garbage and covered in rotting trash, was that little, plasticy man, grinning ear-to-ear at me with a mouth full of rotten waste. In a few short seconds, I took in the horror in detail. Fecal matter, food sludge, plastic and grime stained his bloody clothes and pearly white grin. Maggots wriggled out from between his smile. His eyes were like two, stark moons, glowing in the dark. The smell burnt my nostrils and my eyes began to water.   
I watched, horrified, as he rummaged through the rot and sucked his fingers animalistically before speaking in that terrible, monotone voice:  
“Now this is *delicious.*”

Uncle Rusty called me this morning. He apparently had checked the tap’s security footage and saw some “weird stuff,” last night and wanted to make sure I was okay. The truth is, I can't stop thinking about that guy. I’m going over to Rusty and Gaila’s house later to explain what happened at the restaurant last night, but to be honest, I’m not even sure *what* I saw. I can’t stop imagining his twisty little face every time I close my eyes. The way he crammed his hands into his mouth and swallowed the steak whole. The smell of rotting garbage still burns my nostrils. I am afraid it may never go away. Worst of all… I’m a little afraid that this was all in my head, or that I’m somehow just being an asshole. Maybe the customer last night was just some weirdo, and I let it get under my skin. Maybe I’m being unnecessarily cruel to someone who needed help. In the moment, I was certain that the guy wasn’t human. But it all seems so impossible now, and I don’t know what to think.
