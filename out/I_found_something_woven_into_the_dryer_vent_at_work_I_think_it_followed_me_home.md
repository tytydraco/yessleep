I work the closing shift at a laundromat. It's not an exciting job. Mostly I wipe down machines, empty lint traps, mop the floor, and occasionally explain to people that putting six comforters into one dryer isn't going to save them money.

Last Thursday, dryer number eight stopped heating.

It still spun normally. Clothes came out damp, and there was a weird smell whenever the machine ran. Not burning, exactly. More like wet cardboard left in a hot garage.

My boss told me to check the lint buildup before he paid somebody to come out.

I unplugged the machine, pulled it away from the wall, and disconnected the flexible exhaust duct.

There was something caught about a foot inside.

At first I thought it was a rope.

I used a pair of long pliers to grab it and pulled out maybe eighteen inches of tightly braided material.

It was hair.

Mostly dark brown, with some gray and blond strands mixed in. There were little knots along its length, and some of them had things worked into them. A pink shirt button. A piece of green thread. Half of a plastic hair clip.

It looked like somebody had spent hours making it.

I actually laughed because I figured one of our regular customers must have done something disgusting with hair extensions, and they'd somehow ended up in the machine.

But when I pulled again, the braid wouldn't move.

It went completely taut.

I could feel tension on the other end, like somebody was holding it.

I let go.

The braid slid back into the duct so quickly that the loose end slapped against the metal opening.

I stood there for a second, wondering if I'd somehow managed to snag the exhaust fan.

Except the dryer was unplugged.

I took out my phone and recorded the inside of the duct with the flashlight on.

It was dark beyond the first bend. There was nothing unusual in the video, apart from a little bit of lint stuck to the metal.

And the braid was gone.

I called my boss, who told me to leave the machine unplugged and put an OUT OF ORDER sign on it.

That should have been it.

On Friday morning I came in early because the repair guy was supposed to arrive before my shift.

His name was Dennis. He's an older man who's serviced our machines for years, and he's generally not impressed by anything.

I told him about the hair.

He asked if I'd been drinking.

I showed him the video. There wasn't much to see, but I'd taken a picture of the braid while I was holding it.

He zoomed in.

"That's not lint," he said.

"I know."

He studied the picture for a while.

"Who braided it?"

I told him I didn't know.

He walked behind the machine and looked at the disconnected duct.

Then he asked me to get him a flashlight.

When I came back, he was crouched behind the dryer with his head tilted sideways.

He had the duct in one hand.

"Come listen to this."

I knelt beside him.

At first I heard nothing except the other machines running.

Then, from inside the duct, came three soft clicks.

A pause.

Three more.

It sounded like somebody tapping their fingernail against the inside of a drinking glass.

Dennis straightened up.

"Probably something in the vent line."

"Like an animal?"

"Maybe."

He looked uncomfortable.

The exhaust pipe ran up through the ceiling and eventually outside through the back wall. Dennis said he'd have to inspect the whole line. He didn't want to reconnect the dryer until he knew what was blocking it.

While he went outside to check the vent outlet, I stayed behind and started cleaning the lint traps on the other machines.

That's when I found the second braid.

It was in dryer six.

This one was much shorter. About four inches. It had been pushed through one of the little holes in the drum, so that it hung down into the machine.

There was a silver earring woven into the end.

I recognized it immediately.

It belonged to a regular customer named Marlene.

She wears these enormous silver hoops, and she'd complained the previous week that she'd lost one somewhere in the laundromat.

I hadn't found it.

I pulled the braid gently.

Something pulled back.

Not hard. Just enough to make me stop.

Then I heard a sound behind the drum.

A person clearing their throat.

I backed out so quickly I hit my shoulder against the open dryer door.

"Dennis?"

No answer.

The braid moved.

It slowly disappeared back through the little perforated hole, taking the earring with it.

I went straight outside.

Dennis was standing behind the building, looking up at the exhaust vents.

He said none of the outlets were blocked.

Then he showed me something on his phone.

He'd taken a picture of the back wall.

One of the exhaust outlets had a thick clump of hair hanging from it.

Not tangled hair. Another braid.

And tied into the end of it was a small brass key.

Dennis told me he'd never seen anything like it. He said he was going to shut down the affected machines and come back Monday with equipment to inspect the ductwork properly.

I told him about the earring.

He looked at me for a long time.

Then he said, very quietly, "Don't put your hands in there again."

I didn't.

I finished the shift with both machines unplugged.

At closing, I was the only person in the building. I turned off the lights over the washing machines, grabbed my bag, and started toward the front door.

Then I heard the dryers running.

Both of them.

I turned around.

The drums in six and eight were spinning slowly.

No lights on the displays. No numbers. No sound from the motors.

Just the drums turning.

I could see something pale moving inside number eight.

For a moment I thought it was a towel that somebody had left in there.

Then it pressed against the glass.

Five fingertips.

They were pressed against the inside of the door, and I could see the nails.

The fingers were far too long.

They slid down the glass and disappeared.

The drum stopped.

I didn't go closer.

I left through the front door, locked it, and drove home.

My boss thought I was having some kind of breakdown when I called him. He said he'd review the security cameras and we'd talk on Monday.

I spent most of Saturday trying to convince myself I was exhausted and had mistaken something ordinary for something frightening.

Then Marlene called me.

I don't know how she got my personal number. We aren't friends. I know her first name because she's been using our laundromat every Wednesday for about two years.

She asked if we'd found her earring.

I told her I hadn't.

She said she'd been having a strange problem since she'd lost it.

Every morning she was finding hair in her bathroom sink.

Long, braided pieces of hair.

She lives alone.

She asked whether anybody at the laundromat had been playing a prank on customers.

I told her she should probably call someone to check her apartment.

She went quiet.

Then she asked, "Did you touch it?"

I didn't answer.

"The hair," she said. "Did you pull on it?"

I asked why.

She started crying.

She said she'd found a braid hanging from the bathroom faucet on Friday morning. She'd pulled it out of the drain until she had several feet of it coiled on the floor.

Then it had pulled back.

She'd grabbed the sink to stop herself from falling forward.

Something had tightened around her wrist.

She said she could feel individual strands working their way between her fingers.

She'd managed to get free by cutting the hair with scissors.

There were still red marks around her wrist.

I asked if she'd called the police.

She said yes.

They hadn't found anything in the pipes.

Then she asked if I'd noticed an unusual smell.

Wet cardboard.

I told her I had.

She hung up.

I called her back three times. No answer.

That night I took a shower before bed.

I was washing shampoo out of my hair when I felt something brush against my ankle.

I looked down.

There was a single strand of dark hair coming out of the drain.

It was moving against the water.

Not floating.

Moving.

It slowly dragged itself across the floor of the shower until it touched my foot.

I jumped out, nearly fell, and slammed the bathroom door.

I could hear something scraping against the other side.

A slow, dry sound.

Like a person dragging a comb across a wooden table.

I left the apartment and spent the night at a motel.

Yesterday I called my boss and told him I wasn't coming back until the machines had been inspected.

He said the repair company had already looked at the dryers.

They'd found no mechanical problems.

He'd also watched the security footage from Thursday.

He told me he couldn't see anything unusual.

I asked if he saw me pulling the hair out of the vent.

He said yes.

Then he hesitated.

"What?"

He said it looked like I was pulling on something for a few seconds, but whatever it was didn't show up clearly on camera.

I asked about the picture on my phone.

He said he couldn't explain that.

Then he asked why I'd been standing behind dryer eight for almost eleven minutes.

I told him it couldn't have been eleven minutes.

He said he checked the timestamps.

I'd walked behind the machine at 9:14 p.m. and hadn't come back out until 9:25.

For most of that time, I was crouched behind it.

Not moving.

He said my head was tilted toward the exhaust duct like I was listening to somebody.

I remember pulling on the braid for maybe thirty seconds.

Nothing more.

I went through my phone last night.

The picture is still there.

So is the short video I took inside the duct.

But there's another recording I don't remember making.

It lasts ten minutes and forty-three seconds.

The screen is almost completely black.

At the beginning, I can hear myself breathing.

Then there's a faint rustling sound and something that might be a voice.

For several minutes, nothing happens.

Around the nine-minute mark, I hear myself whisper something.

I've listened to it over and over.

I'm pretty sure I say, "I'll keep it."

Then there's a sound like somebody laughing with their mouth closed.

I haven't slept much.

I'm at my sister's house now. She's at work, and I've been sitting on her couch trying to work out what to do.

About an hour ago, I found something caught in the zipper of my jacket.

A few strands of hair.

I started pulling them out.

They were attached to a tiny braid, no thicker than a shoelace.

There was a little pink button woven into it.

The same one I saw on Thursday.

I dropped the jacket in the kitchen and went outside.

When I looked through the window a few minutes later, the jacket was still on the floor.

But the braid wasn't.

I've locked myself in my sister's bedroom.

I can hear something moving in the hallway.

It's making the same dry scraping sound I heard through the bathroom door.

And a few minutes ago, somebody gently knocked three times.

I haven't opened the door.

The worst part is that I can feel something brushing the back of my neck whenever I sit still.

I keep checking, but there's nothing there.

I just found a single gray hair on my keyboard.

It's tied in a knot.
