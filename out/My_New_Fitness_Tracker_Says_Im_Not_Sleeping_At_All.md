Okay so I finally got one of those fancy fitness trackers. You know the ones that tell you how many steps you take your heart rate all that jazz. I got it mostly for the sleep tracking honestly. I always feel tired so I figured maybe it would give me some insight into my terrible sleep habits. Boy was I wrong.

  
I set it up last Tuesday evening. It was a new model sleek black band nice little screen. Pretty standard stuff. I charged it up synced it to my phone and went to bed feeling optimistic. Maybe finally I’d figure out why I always feel like a zombie.

  
Woke up Wednesday morning feeling just as groggy as usual. The first thing I did was check the app. It showed zero hours of sleep. Zero. I figured it was a glitch. New device new tech maybe it needed a night to calibrate or something. I mean I distinctly remembered falling asleep. I had dreams even. Nothing too vivid just normal dream stuff.

  
So I tried again Wednesday night. Made sure it was snug on my wrist. Didn’t mess with any settings. Just went to bed like normal. Woke up Thursday feeling a little worse if anything. Checked the app again. Still zero hours. This is where it started to get weird.

  
I actually felt a little panicked. How could I not be sleeping? I close my eyes. I lie still. I don’t get up. I don’t remember anything happening during the night. Yet the tracker insisted I was awake the entire time. It even showed my heart rate was elevated for hours during what should have been my deepest sleep.

  
I decided to troubleshoot. I checked the company’s forums. Lots of people complaining about inaccurate sleep tracking but mostly it was about missing REM cycles or light sleep. No one was reporting zero hours. I watched some YouTube reviews. Everyone raved about how accurate it was.

  
Friday night I was determined. I took a sleeping pill. A really strong one my doctor prescribed for occasional insomnia. I was out like a light. I remember thinking as I drifted off that there was no way this tracker could say I didn’t sleep now.

  
Saturday morning I woke up with that heavy drugged feeling. My head pounded. I stumbled to my phone dreading what I’d see. Zero. Still zero hours of sleep. My heart rate graph was a chaotic mess of spikes and dips. It looked like I’d been running a marathon all night. But I was in my bed. The bed hadn’t moved. I hadn’t moved.

  
This continued for another few days. Every night zero sleep recorded. Every morning I felt worse and worse. My coworkers started noticing. I was forgetting things. My eyes were bloodshot. I kept zoning out during meetings. I even almost fell asleep at my desk one afternoon.

  
I started setting up my phone to record audio at night. Just in case I was talking in my sleep or something. All I ever got was dead silence. No rustling no breathing no nothing. It was like the phone wasn’t even in the room. But it was right there on my nightstand.

  
Then I got desperate. I bought a cheap webcam. I set it up facing my bed Monday night. I wanted to see what the hell I was doing. Was I getting up and pacing? Was I sleepwalking? Was I just lying there with my eyes open staring at the ceiling? I needed answers.

  
Tuesday morning I felt utterly drained. The worst yet. I hadn’t even bothered checking the tracker anymore. I just went straight for the webcam footage. I scrubbed through the night. The first few hours were normal. Me getting into bed. Turning out the light. Lying down.

  
Then around 1 AM it started. I was just lying there completely still. My eyes were closed. But then my mouth started to open. Just a little at first. Then wider. It wasn’t like I was gasping or screaming. It was just slowly opening. Like a fish.

  
And then I started to tilt my head. Slowly. To the side. Then the other side. My head was rotating like I was trying to look around the room without moving my body. My eyes were still closed. My mouth was still open. It was disturbing. Like I was a mannequin being manipulated by invisible strings.

  
This went on for hours. My head would turn. My mouth would open and close slightly. Sometimes my fingers would twitch. But I never sat up. I never got out of bed. It was like my body was performing a slow motion dance of unease.

  
The most terrifying part was when the camera picked up a faint whisper. It was barely audible. I had to turn the volume all the way up. It sounded like a raspy dry voice. Not my voice. And it was saying something. I couldn’t quite make it out. It was too muffled. But it was there. Whispering right next to my head.

  
The tracker has been confirming it all along. I am not sleeping. I’m just lying there playing host to something. I don’t know what it is. I don’t know what it wants. But it’s using my body. And it’s keeping me from rest. I took the tracker off. It’s sitting on my desk now. I don’t think I can put it back on. I don’t want to know anymore. I just want to sleep.
