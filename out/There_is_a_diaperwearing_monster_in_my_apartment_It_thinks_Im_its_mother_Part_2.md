[Part I](https://www.reddit.com/r/nosleep/s/Qn6GOGUZjf)

After much deliberation, I decided it best to take the government’s help with my man-baby situation.

I left my signed pamphlet on the desk just before I was discharged. Apparently, I was free to go “home.” Police were notified of my decision to move forward with Suzie’s offer and deemed me “not a threat to the child.”

Their words.

I planned to hide out in a hotel until this was all over but I needed some things from my place. A rational person wouldn’t have gone back, but part of me still hoped this was all a dream. Maybe I’d open the door and find my pristine bedroom. No crib. No man-baby.

Even now. After all of this. I had hope.

Of course, I shouldn’t have.

He was there waiting for me. I opened the door and the lights were still on. The smell hit me first. Then the sound.

My senses were overwhelmed. Through the cracked door I could see a tapestry of diapers and waste covering the apartment in foul shades of brown and green. The tv was on. Some WWII documentary was blasting the sound of gunfire throughout the place.

I saw the glint of keys hanging out of my purse on the table and decided that would have to do. All I needed to do was grab it and be on my way. Hopefully Suzie would have answers for me soon. I won’t be back here until she does.

The door slid open and I tip toed around various messes on the ground until the table was within my reach. I glanced frantically around the house as my hand wound up the purse strap, careful not to allow the metal to clink against the wooden table.

That’s when I saw him. He was sitting across from me behind the arm of the couch. A mischievous eye looked up at me from the dark corner of the room. Deep, childish giggling escaped him, sending chills up my spine.

I started to turn, but then something unexpected happened.

I started laughing.

Suddenly, I wasn’t scared at all. The sight of him made me hysterical, like a mom coming home to find her child trying to be sneaky but failing.

He lifted his head and started to approach me. A fountain of drool fell from his dumb, smiling mouth as he stumbled over to me. I looked up at him and smiled. Memories flooded my brain.

I giggled again.

I pictured myself playing with him at the park. He was too large for the equipment, so I watched him tumble down the grassy knoll instead. We both laughed as he went up and down the hill.

Another memory. It was cold outside. We stayed in and I put some children’s show on the tv. I had never seen it before but he seemed to enjoy it. He pointed at the tv everytime the horse character came on-screen. I decided to take him to the petting zoo next week so he could see a real horse.

Another memory. He was having a tantrum and I couldn’t figure out what he wanted. I cried and begged him to calm down. I watched as he turned the place upside down but still wasn’t slowing. That’s when he jumped on me. He tossed me around the apartment like a rag doll until I was unconscious. I woke up choking on my own blood with three broken ribs.

What the hell?

I don’t understand. None of those memories were real. None of it happened.

I stumbled and nearly tripped over a mountain of garbage to my left. He giggled and jabbed my side with a big meaty finger.

“No—no get away!” I screamed, swatting him away with my purse.

He grimaced. I watched as he balled his hands into fists and started babbling in frustration.

“Wha…who…” he mumbled.

I turned and made for the door.

“Ha…WHORE!” He screamed. It was a deep, manly bellow. Nothing a child could utter.

He tossed the dining table as he scrambled to close the distance between us. It shattered against the drywall, sending a blast of splinters across the messy apartment.

I turned and screamed as his meaty hand grasped for me but it was too late. The door slammed shut just before his clumsy limbs could get hold of me.

I was shaking. My breath was thick and heavy as I gripped the railing to keep myself from falling over.

A neighbor was seated on their balcony across from me watching my despair with a cheeky smile.

“Kids, am I right?” he said, raising his beer can in jest.

I didn’t respond.

The sound of cursing and fists faded as I descended the apartment stairs towards the parking lot and practically leapt into my car.

I made my way to a hotel and laid in bed with all the lights on. The man-baby was far away, but I knew I’d see his face the moment I closed my eyes.

The sound of his fists against the door rattled away in my mind.

And those memories…they felt so real. For a moment, everything seemed right in the world. If I didn’t snap out of it, I’m not sure what would’ve happened.

Tomorrow is my first session with Dr. Han. I prayed he would have answers.

Unfortunately, I left the session with more questions.

“It’s really is quite simple,” he muttered, scribbling symbols and graphs onto the dry erase board. It was hard to follow his train of thought. The fluorescent lighting in the crappy government facility made his eccentric appearance even more jarring. It was distracting.

Dr. Han was middle-aged. A wiry halo of grey hair sat upon his balding head like a silver bird’s nest. He was tall, grizzled, and wore an ill-fitting lab coat that he was practically swimming in. We had just finished meeting and exchanging awkward pleasantries before he broke into a lecture. I was anxiously awaiting my turn to ask what the hell is going on but I wasn’t even sure where to begin.

“It’s like a stack—you see.” He scribbled a set of vertical boxes and tapped each one. “Less like a linear set of steps. More like a computer execution stack. As deviations in the flow of reality reconcile, the stack picks up at the previous recursion. You said you were a computer scientist, correct?”

“Uh—no. Sales actually.”

“Oh….very interesting.” He turned back to the board and wiped away a third of his work.

He explained his theory that reality is like an ocean we all sit within. Our lived experience gives reality a shape. Sometimes, shapes form in the negative spaces between our perceptions. This creates “deviations” that reality tries to rectify.

“So, my man-baby is some sort of deviation in reality?” I scratched my head, failing to absorb Dr. Han’s poorly explained hypothesis.

“Well, that’s the tricky thing. I don’t know. Nobody does—well, except you of course. Here—let me give you an example.” He began drawing a surprisingly well rendered dog on the board.

“What is that?” He asked, pausing for my answer.

“Uh—a dog?”

“A dog? I’ve never heard of that. There’s one in my house right now—eating my furniture. Biting me. I don’t know what to do with it.”

“You’ve never seen a dog?” I chuckled.

Dr. Han laughed. “Of course I’ve heard of a dog. We all know what a dog is. This is how you sound to people when you talk about your uh…deviation.” He quickly scribbled a baby with a mustache on the board. It felt insulting.

“So, everyone else believes this man-baby thing is normal? And I’m the only one that knows it isn’t?”

Dr. Han smiled. “Exactly. It’s a lot like the Mandela Effect. Only, instead of Mr. Peanut wearing a monocle it’s a giant baby in your apartment.”

“Who’s Mr. Peanut?”

“Never mind.”

My head was spinning but I nodded like I understood. “Im starting to have these…memories. Only, I know they didn’t happen—they couldn’t have happened.”

“Ah, yes. I call those ‘mends.’” He clasped his hands together as he spoke as if he was bridging a gap.

“Reality isn’t working against you, Kara. In fact, it wants these anomalies to reconcile just as much as you do.”

I felt a chill run up my spine. There’s a lot I still don’t understand, but this was crystal clear to me. If I’m not careful, this nightmare could become my reality. I could forget my true life even existed.

“So, what now?”

“Well…” Dr. Han shifted uncomfortably in his chair. “Now, we evaluate. Once we have everything we need, and if we decide to move forward with treatment—“

“Wait—if you decide? What is there to decide? I’m trapped at home with a monster. Do you think I’m making this up?” I was starting to shout. Fear and anger started to rise within me.

Dr. Han didn’t react, as if he’s heard this hundreds of times.

“I may not be able to see what you see due to the nature of this situation. I do, however, see the symptoms. I believe you Miss, and I will do everything I can to help. Unfortunately, there is a process we must follow.” He waived a stack of papers tucked into his Manila envelope. “And that process takes time.”

I was at a loss for words. This session wasn’t going how I imagined at all.

After a long pause, Dr. Han patted his thighs and stood from his chair. “Well, this is our first time meeting so I’d like to keep today’s session brief. You have a lot to think about.” He made his way to the door and held it open, signaling to me that our time was up.

“Just remember, our organization is tasked with handling cases like yours and bridging the gap between your reality and what is true. This is just one step of many on that journey. Try not to feel discouraged.”

Defeated, I started to make my way to the next appointment. I walked down the beige hallway towards the entrance labeled “PSYCH.” It was time to meet up with Suzie.

This was our first psychology appointment. I used to believe this was meant to alleviate my mental distress from the horrors I’m facing. Now I understand it is just a process to root out the wackos and fakers.

The thought left a sour expression on my face. Suzie picked up on the energy immediately. “I was happy to hear you decided to take our offer and move forward with this investigation. Although, something tells me you aren’t thrilled with this decision.”

“I guess I just expected…more.” I said in a sullen tone.

“More?”

“Yeah—more answers. More help.”

“Was Dr. Han not helpful?”

“No—I mean yes. He was. I just don’t understand why I’m being questioned.” I said sharply.

Suzie nodded sympathetically. “I see.”

“I understand your frustration honey. I really do. But you must understand what we’re dealing with.”

She rustled some of her notes around until her fingers fell upon the files she was looking for.

“Luke. Says his girlfriend is a dragon and he needs help slaying her. Tested positive for meth and LSD at the police station.” She picked up another stack.

“Marge. Claimed aliens changed her reality so her children could be abducted. All her kids are alive and accounted for.” She tossed Marge’s report onto the desk.

“James. Filed a report with the police claiming his ex-wife was the devil. Warrant is still out on that one. Her criminal record alone makes her sound like a goddam demon.”

She discarded James and dropped another stack of paperwork for additional curious characters. It landed with a loud thud on the desk in front of me.

“Now these I just received this morning. I haven’t read them yet but I’m sure it will be more of the same.”

Silence filled the room. I know I couldn’t blame her for this. I’m sure I sound just as deluded as the next wack job to walk through that door.

Still, it didn’t hurt any less to hear.

“Listen Kara, for what it’s worth I do believe you. Still, I’m bound by process. Let’s just work through this together quickly so we can get to helping you alright?”

There’s that word again. The “process.” I sighed, and asked her to proceed. Then, we were off.

She asked a few questions about my life. Friends, family, coworkers. We walked through the whole man-baby ordeal from the beginning. What she did after that was…strange.

She reached for a camera perched between two ferns and switched it off. I didn’t even know it was there until this moment. The red light at the center of the camera dissipated.

“Just to clarify, the man-baby didn’t exist in any form in your reality, correct?” She was no longer scribing with her writing machine. This line of questioning appeared to be off-book which made me uncomfortable.

“Uh-how so?” I asked.

“You didn’t have a child at before this? Or some other responsibility? This…anomaly…just came into existence?” She appeared very focused as she asked. As if this was critical information. And yet, none of it was being recorded.

“Oh—yes. I have no children or anything resembling that…thing.”

“Interesting. Thank you, Kara.” In one swift movement she flipped the camera back on and continued her note taking.

We worked through a few more novel questions before things took another uncomfortable turn. Suzie began prying into my past relationships. I’m sure the reports came up during their screening. The law was involved, after all.

“I’m not sure what this has to do with anything?” I asked plainly.

Suzie cleared her throat. “I just want to understand the nature of this previous relationship.”

Everything with Tod was still fresh. I really wasn’t ready to hash it out with anyone just yet. Especially not with someone I just met. What does this have to do with any of this?

“We’ve been broken up for almost six months now.”

“I am assuming things didn’t end…amicably?”

Memories of Tod became front and center. The abuse, and his eventual imprisonment hung heavy in my mind.

“No. Any more questions?”

Suzie shifted in her chair. It’s clear she wanted to pursue this line of questioning but didn’t know how.

“Kara, these questions are just a part of the process. I am required to ask them. But I apologize if they make you uncomfortable.”

“I am uncomfortable.” I adjusted myself and took a sip of water. “But proceed.”

“Does the thing in your apartment look like Tod at all? Any passing resemblance?”

I felt a pit grow in my stomach. There was a time Tod and I talked about having kids. Back when I thought he was a better man. Before he…

I could see myself on the ground again—but Tod wasn’t there. The false memory of the man-baby breaking my ribs on the carpet flashed in my mind again.

“No—absolutely not.”

“Hm. So there’s no chance your feelings toward the child are influenced by your feelings towards Tod?” She said in the most sincere tone she could muster.

“What are you trying to say?”

Suzie set her writing assistance tool on her desk. “I’ll speak plainly. In order to go any further in this process, I need to rule out any possibility that your current…situation…is not a form of dissociation caused by your past trauma.”

I audibly scoffed. How dare she? I am going through literal hell and she has the audacity to accuse me of making this up? I felt a rage boiling in me, but I couldn’t argue against her point.

Memories of Tod and the monster swam in my mind and I couldn’t deny there were….similarities. The face, the hair—even the voice…I could see traces of Tod. It wasn’t a strong resemblance, but enough to make me consider her point.

But no—she couldn’t know that. I needed to get this over with. It’s all just a coincidence. Reality tricking me again. Yeah, that’s all it is.

“No Suzie. None at all.” I looked down and noticed my knuckles were white from gripping the sides of my seat. I relaxed and fixed my shirt while Suzie pondered my response.

“Understood. Again, I am sorry for the questions.” Suzie picked up her writing device and got back to work.

“Now then, let’s get you home. I’m sure you’ve got a lot to think about. Let’s continue tomorrow.”

There’s that phrase again. Of course I have a lot to think about.

I drove by my place on the way to the hotel. I couldn’t see anything from the road, but I knew it was still in there. Waiting. I felt a chill and thought it best not to linger. As I drove away, I saw a large shadow flit across the window.

Today was not as productive as I’d hoped. I left both appointments with more questions than answers. The hopelessness was starting to settle in. 

Were things ever going to be back to normal? I walked through the day in my mind over and over again. Dr. Han and Suzie promised they would try to help. I just had to take their word for it. Not many other options anyway. 

As I lie in bed wrestling with memories that aren’t mine, I start to question my sanity. 

The ceiling—it’s changed.

It’s not something anyone would really notice. Unless you’re me. Unfortunately I’ve had the pleasure of staring at this ceiling for hours at this point. 

Looks like Dr. Han and I have a lot more to talk about tomorrow.
