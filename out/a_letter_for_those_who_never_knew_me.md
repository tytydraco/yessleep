i hated his thin-lipped smile. i still do. i think even once i die, i’ll still hate it from beyond the grave.

“we don’t really have a name for that entity yet. they won’t give us one to call it, anyway- so i’ve been calling it lucy.” what kind of fucking name is that for that thing? “it feeds on brain activity. uses fear as a stimulant to get more out of you. it’s not so different from a mosquito, when you think about it.”

he ended up going on this whole long spiel. the entity is only doing what it has to do to live. we don’t hate cats for hunting mice- so how is this any different? all creatures have to do what they have to do. and it least it doesn’t try to kill you- it holds you still to avoid accidentally lobotomising you.

you’re humanising that thing, i wanted to say. it doesn’t do that out of compassion! it just wouldn’t want to cut off its food source! 

“collecting data on how it affects people isn’t easy, but we know its favourite demographic. it’s easiest to invoke fear in traumatised people. so we designed our products, and we sought out our target demographic, because at least one out of the fifty of you would have to end up being targeted, surely.” he exhaled out of his nose. “that support group really made the job easier.”

i still remember how dry my throat felt as i barely managed to croak out, “you said that the test included twenty people.”

“oh, yes. i lied.”

i don’t know what i expected him to say in response to that, to be honest. it’s the type of thing i would find humorous if it was in a tv show or a book. but it wasn’t. i only felt dread.

“we’ve got good reason to believe that its feeding habits can reduce the cognitive lifespan in its host. early dementia symptoms, eventual brain death…”

“so you have what you need to stop it, right…? you… you want to prevent it, right…?”

he smiled thinly again and my stomach dropped.

“not exactly. think about the prison system, christine. some people just really deserve to die, but when the death penalty is used, everyone kicks up a fuss. some people have to be given an extra push if we want to get any answers out of them, but those extra pushes are seen as inhumane. it’s all too gentle. it leaves no room for actually fixing the world. but look, christine… we have a free pass for those prisoners that deserve death to ‘die young, from natural causes’, but only if we learn to use it for our benefit. this research has been approved, you know. by our government. we’re onto something groundbreaking and it’s allowed, so we’re not really doing anything wrong even if you don’t agree with it.”

i’d never felt so disgusted. if he’d been anything like the man he pretended to be, i know he would have been against such a blatantly misanthropic and horrible view. there’s a million things wrong with it, for god’s sake.

“i really, really hate you, andrew.” i’d hissed out through gritted teeth, and he smiled sort of sadly, like he’d seen this coming. not that i feel any sympathy for him. he could have easily not done this. he actively chose to involve me.

“now you know everything you wanted to know. i’m afraid we can’t let you leave, though. i am sorry, christine. but we have a policy about this kind of thing. you’ll have to stay here, and… there’s no nice way to put this, haha. but it’ll be swift and painless, i promise. we’re not monsters.”

“...you are.”

that was pretty much where the conversation ended, and he led me to some other room- a small one. all that intelligence of his is for show, i suppose, because he didn’t even notice when i pickpocketed his phone from him. that’s how i’m writing this.

“you can either do it yourself, or you can wait for us. there’s no painless way to do it yourself, though. i’m sorry, christine.” 

that was the last thing he told me before he left me in here to wait, and god knows how long i’ll be waiting for. it could be hours, days, weeks, or months, but i don’t really care at this point. i’ve been given more than enough time to type out this whole story, and maybe it doesn’t sound real. maybe i brought up anecdotes that don’t need to be brought up, but forgive me for not wanting to die totally unknown, just another person fucked over. at least there’ll be some trace of my personality here. at least something will be known about me, rather than just me being dead for stupid, inhumane reasons.

none of this truly sounds real, but one day i’ll know if i hallucinated this whole thing, because the illusion will eventually end. then, i’ll speak again. don’t keep an eye on the news- they won’t report on me disappearing, of course they won’t. but look out for a report from a joe osborne or a marisa kelly on a missing sister. then you’ll know. and if i never am heard from again, just know i’m dead. 

and whatever that entity is, you’ve got less reason to be afraid of that thing than your own species, as fucked up as that is.

it was nice meeting you all, even if i didn’t ever talk to you, and i never will. it practically feels like we’re good friends already. :)

\- christine
