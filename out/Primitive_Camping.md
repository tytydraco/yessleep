My family camps a lot.  
I feel like I need to start with that, because otherwise this story makes me sound like someone who went into the woods already expecting something creepy to happen.  
I’m not.  
My husband is former military, I’m a pretty vigilant mom, and we’ve been primitive camping more times than I can count. We know what the woods sound like at night.  
That’s important.  
This happened several years ago, when our kids were still little. I’ll call them Jack and Emma. We also had our dog, Molly, with us.  
We hadn’t been camping in a while, so everybody was ridiculously excited.  
The site was beautiful. Remote, primitive, right on a lake. We had our generator and our little pop-up camper, the kind with beds that fold out on either end with canvas around them and a solid section in the middle.  
We pulled in during broad daylight.  
The kids immediately started running around with Molly while my husband and I unloaded.  
And the second I stepped out of the truck, I wanted to leave.  
There was no reason for it.  
The sun was shining. The lake was gorgeous. The kids were laughing.  
But it felt like I’d walked through an invisible wall.  
One second I was excited, and the next every part of my body was telling me something was wrong.  
I remember staring at the tree line with this overwhelming feeling that something was looking back.  
I told my husband the campsite felt weird.  
“How?”  
“I don’t know.”  
Obviously, not very convincing.  
He told me I was being paranoid, and honestly, I agreed with him. We did this all the time. There was nothing unusual about this place except the feeling I couldn’t shake.  
So I ignored it.  
By evening, I was doing better. We made dinner, built a fire and let the kids stay up late roasting marshmallows.  
Then I heard something in the woods.  
Just a rustle.  
My husband didn’t hear it.  
A few minutes later, I heard it again.  
This time Molly’s ears went up.  
“Probably a rabbit,” my husband said.  
Fine. Probably a rabbit.  
Eventually I put the kids to bed. Our plan was to sit outside afterward, have a drink by the fire and enjoy some kid-free time.  
Except I suddenly didn’t want to leave them in the camper.  
My husband reminded me that we were literally sitting right outside. We’d done this dozens of times.  
He was right.  
So I went back out.  
Then a branch snapped.  
Not a twig. A branch.  
My husband heard that one.  
Another noise came from somewhere off to our right.  
Molly stood up.  
Then she growled.  
Molly wasn’t a growly dog. Her whole body went stiff, the fur along her back stood up, and she stared into one specific part of the woods.  
That was enough for me.  
“We’re going inside.”  
This time my husband didn’t argue much.  
He slept on one end with Jack, and I was on the other with Emma. Molly stayed in the middle.  
Sometime later, I woke up to movement outside.  
Leaves. Branches. The occasional crack.  
Then I realized it was getting closer.  
I’d hear something farther out.  
Silence.  
Then closer.  
Silence.  
Then right outside the camper.  
I whispered my husband’s name.  
“You hear that?”  
He listened.  
Another branch snapped.  
“Yeah.”  
Then something touched the camper.  
Tap.  
A few seconds later, another.  
Tap.  
Then one against the canvas near my bed.  
I got up and stood beside Emma.  
Across the camper, my husband sat up.  
“I’m going to look.”  
“No.”  
“There’s obviously something outside.”  
“Exactly. So don’t go outside.”  
We whisper-argued while Molly paced between us, whining under her breath.  
Finally my husband grabbed the flashlight.  
“I’ll walk around once.”  
I begged him not to, but he went.  
The second he stepped outside, I picked Emma up and moved her into Jack’s bed so I could stay close to both of them.  
I watched my husband’s flashlight move around the campsite.  
Then it stopped.  
He was staring up into the trees.  
When he came back inside, he told me there was nothing out there.  
I knew by his face that wasn’t completely true.  
“What did you see?”  
He hesitated.  
“Eyes.”  
Two eyes reflecting back at him from the trees.  
They were high. Too high to be a deer standing on the ground, but lower than where he would’ve expected a bird to be perched.  
He couldn’t see a body.  
Just the eyes.  
He decided it was probably an owl.  
I think mostly because *owl* gave us something normal to call it.  
Neither of us got back into bed.  
We sat together on the bench in the middle, near the kids.  
For a while, nothing happened.  
I actually started feeling stupid again.  
Then something moved outside.  
Molly’s head snapped toward it.  
Another crack came from behind us.  
Then one from the other side.  
It felt like something was circling the camper.  
The tapping started again.  
Canvas.  
Metal.  
The solid wall beside us.  
Then silence.  
Molly started growling.  
And something outside growled back.  
I still can’t describe that sound properly.  
It was low and rough, but there was something almost metallic underneath it. Like two sounds layered together.  
I looked at my husband.  
He was already looking at me.  
And that scared me more than the sound, because he wasn’t brushing it off anymore.  
Molly backed herself against the cabinets, shaking with her hackles raised.  
Then she peed on the floor.  
That’s when I started crying.  
Not screaming or panicking. Just silently crying while I grabbed paper towels and cleaned up dog pee in the middle of the night.  
Molly had camped with us before. She knew what animals sounded and smelled like.  
I’d never seen her that scared.  
The commotion woke Jack.  
“Mom?”  
“I’m right here. Molly had an accident.”  
My husband stood up.  
He wanted the flare gun.  
We kept one in our emergency bag.  
Unfortunately, the emergency bag was in the vehicle.  
Outside.  
“No.”  
“If that’s a person out there, I want something in here.”  
That was the first time either of us had said *person*.  
Somehow that was worse.  
He went outside again.  
I cleaned up Molly’s mess as fast as I could, then pulled blankets and pillows onto the floor in the solid middle section of the camper.  
I brought both kids down with me.  
Jack was awake. Emma barely stirred.  
I sat there with both of them against me and Molly pressed against us.  
The beds suddenly seemed horrifyingly exposed.  
Nothing but canvas between my children and whatever was outside.  
My husband was gone maybe a minute.  
When he came back with the emergency bag, I knew before I even asked.  
“You saw them again.”  
He nodded.  
Same eyes.  
Same general area.  
Same strange height.  
Still no body.  
He locked the door.  
Eventually the kids fell back asleep on the pallet. Molly lay beside them but kept her head up.  
My husband sat on one side.  
I sat on the other.  
We stayed awake until morning.  
Every once in a while we’d hear movement outside.  
Then, sometime before dawn, it stopped.  
The second there was enough light to see, we packed.  
No breakfast. No playing by the lake. We shoved everything wherever it fit, hooked up the camper and left.  
In daylight, the campsite looked exactly like it had when we arrived.  
Beautiful.  
Quiet.  
Completely normal.  
There were no obvious footprints. No claw marks. No damage to the camper.  
Nothing that explained the tapping, the growl, Molly’s reaction or the eyes in the trees.  
Maybe there is a normal explanation.  
Maybe the eyes belonged to an owl. Maybe a raccoon was messing with the camper. Maybe several different animals were around us and, once I got scared, my brain connected things that had nothing to do with each other.  
I’ve considered all of that.  
There’s just one part I’ve never been able to explain away.  
I knew before anything happened.  
Before dark.  
Before the noises.  
Before Molly growled.  
The second I stepped out of our vehicle in broad daylight, with my kids laughing and the sun shining on that lake, every instinct I had told me to put my family back in the car and leave.  
I ignored it because there was no logical reason to be afraid.  
We’ve camped for years since then. Primitive sites. Remote places. We’ve heard coyotes, had animals wander through camp and woken up to things moving around outside the camper.  
I’ve never felt that feeling again.  
We’ve never been back to that campsite.  
I still don’t know what was outside our camper that night.  
But if I ever pull into another campsite and feel that wall hit me again?  
We’re leaving.
