 It was quite a shock when my mother called me over from across the country (at 6 AM, mind you), crying, telling me rather vaguely that "something bad" had happened to my father.

 As much as I pressed her, she wouldn’t get into any detail over the phone, every time I tried to get details, she kept insisting I need to come and talk to him myself and even more strangely, that it needs to be today, no matter what. Lest to say, that made me worry quite a bit.

 I told my wife about the call and apologized profusely for leaving her alone with our newly born baby boy for the next couple days, when I made sure my wife was going to be alright, I booked the nearest flight to my parent’s state.

 When I had arrived at the small village where my parents lived, at the old house that was in our family for what must have been 10 generations by now, I noticed some big changes.

 Of course, some things were the same as I remembered them, the dirt road that always got muddied to hell when it rained, the chicken coop where me and Jack hid when we wanted to skip homework, and the criminally creepy scarecrow that was made intentionally to look like a man, hanging from the largest tree on our property, I will get to that later.

 Some things changed quite drastically as well, the scarecrow had new clothes for once, and seemed a bit smaller than before, I thought that they made another one, it turned out I was right, though not in the way I had thought. In the moment however, more pressing than the scarecrow was the fact that the forest on the border of our property had completely overtaken around half the wheat field, some of the roots stretching to the point of touching the house’s backside, backside whose window was now smashed to shit.

 I initially thought that my older brother was just slacking off (that’s who Jack is, by the way), not cleaning the porch, not maintaining the fields, not repairing the windows, it was in his character to be fair, he was always a bit of a slacker, but letting things get to that point just because our parents were too old to do the upkeep was plain wrong.

 I was already preparing to give him a good earful and then help him cut out some of the trees and pull out some of the roots.

 But obviously, more pressing than all that was finding out what had happened to my dad.

 I opened the front door to see my mother crying on the sofa, when she saw me, she tried to get up on her feet, but she tripped in the process, I managed to catch her by her arm and helped her straighten up, I hugged her.

 “What’s wrong, mom?” Having seen the state she was in, I was starting to get worried. “Where’s dad?”

 My mother awkwardly stumbled her way to the bookcase and proceeded to grab an old tattered book.

 “It was real.” She said, slobber and snot covering her mouth. “We thought it was just an old superstition, a superstition from when people were less civilized, your dad and I decided it ends with your grandfather, that’s why we never told you kids anything.”

 “Where’s dad???” I was crying too by that point.

 She shoved the book into my hands and asked me to read.

 I will give you a copy of what was written in the book.

July 27

 Mathew told me the other day that only our village has a scarecrow.

 Or that our scarecrow is one of a kind or something, can’t really remember exactly what he said now.

 Truth is the fact never occurred to me, how do them other villages keep the Forest away from the wheat?

 After all, where there’s wheat, there’s crows, and crows eat wheat, so there should be a scarecrow to eat the crows so they don’t eat the wheat.

 Obvious, right?

 Matt told me that the folks in the other places he went to have some fake scarecrows, he said they look the part, but they stay all in one place, and they don’t do much to scare away the crows, they just sit there and look all mean he said, sometimes you see one with a crow perching on its head.

 Now that made me chuckle when I heard it, don’t that defeat the whole purpose of a scarecrow now?

 And they don’t hang from no tree either, they have some sort of stick up their asses or something like that.

 But those fake scarecrows from outside don’t need no upkeep, they don’t even got someone whose job is to look after them in their village.

 If you ask me, those people are just asking for their wheat to be all eaten by the Forest.

 And that’s what I told Matt too, but he said them crows over there are fake too.

 They don’t bite, they don’t have no murder in their eyes, they just eat the grain, don’t do much to the land neither.

 Not that just eating the grain is nothing at all, it just seems those folks have it a lot easier than we do.

 Makes you jealous don’t it?

 And by you, I mean me, of course, this being my journal and all.

 Well, I guess that’s enough for today, I’ll be seeing you tomorrow I reckon. (and by you I mean me again, of course).

 

July 28

 Dad told me today that at this pace, I’m never gone be able to take over the family business, and that it’ll all end with him.

 I don’t know what he got so worked up for, it ain’t like I spilled the whole pot, I just dropped my ladle.

 He got all teary-eyed and he screamed at me all mean and angry.

 “DO YOU EVEN KNOW THE RATIOS?

DO YOU KNOW WHAT TEMPERATURE TO PUT THE FIRE ON AND FOR HOW LONG?

HOW LONG DO YOU LET IT COOL, BOY?

WHERE DO YOU APPLY IT?

DO YOU EVEN KNOW THESE THINGS AFTER ALL THESE YEARS?”

 I told him as good as I knew, I reckon I was right too, he made me memorize this stuff a long while ago, he’d be right to be angry if I forgot any of that.

 After I told him, he slapped me anyway, I can’t for the life of me figure out why he’d done that.

 And on top of that, when we got home, mama gave me an earful too bout not stressing out my father and all.

 Now I knew better than to talk back, I had one red cheek already, I had no need for the second.

 And when we all sat down for dinner, dad told me that tomorrow, I’ll be the one looking after grandpa, all by myself.

 Mama told him that I’m too young, and to call up uncle.

 He said we don’t got the time to teach uncle.

 I don’t know what he meant to say by ‘no time’.

 Truth be told, journal, I’m kinda scared.

 I best get going to bed, journal, tomorrow’s gone be a tough one.

 

July 29

 I started just the way we always do.

 I got up, got dressed, had a cup of water and the bowl of porridge mom left out for me last night.

 When I got down, he told me I’m late, even though the sun wasn’t even up.

 I didn’t manage to get no sleep, I told dad I really wished we had some coffee in the house, like the city folks do, Matt told me it keeps you all awake and alert, he told me to man up, that ain’t none of us ever had coffee and I'm the first one complaining about it in our family's history.

 I got bummed when he told me that, but after what happened yesterday, I think I get why he’s so mean these days.

 Today we needed a new batch, I think dad chose today on purpose, so he’d see if I’m up to the task.

 He told me once that before his grandpa’s time they used honey and liquor, and that back then, they lasted less than they do now, hell, I’m glad we don’t waste honey and liquor on the dead no more, if I had honey I’d much rather eat it, and if I had liquor, I’d much rather drink it.

 We went to the stables and got the cauldron out, damn thing’s hard on your back when lifting by yourself.

 I made a fire and got all the sacks out and put on the gloves.

 I mixed all the powders the way I had seen dad do what must have been a hundred times by now, then I mixed in that nasty vinegar-smelling acid to cover up the smell, and then some clove oil, to cover up the vinegar, I got the ratios right, I got the temperature right, heck I even counted the seconds it took to cool down.

 I did everything properly this time, I ain’t even drop my ladle.

 Then we got the cart and had to search up for grandpa, we found him on the other side of the village, dad made me drag him back all by myself like I was a mule or something.

 Well now, I guess he must have done that by himself for some years until he had me too.

 I got the needle ready, got the solution in him all nice and proper, brushed him with it too.

 He had a big gash on his leg, something tore up his clothes so bad you could see his flesh all green and mushy by now, and the sack on his head was damp too, he must be melting.

 I guess that’s why dad’s been mad these days, hell, I’d be mad too.

 

July 30

 The Forest’s been acting up these days, I think it figured out grandpa’s on his last leg, we had a lot of wolves looking at him from the woods, glaring something nasty, but they don’t get to come out the Forest at day, no they don’t, only at night.

 The crows been stirring up a racket as well, they can come and go as they please, but even during the day, they don’t dare to come close to the wheat.

 Mama’s been crying every night these days, dad ain’t doing too good himself, hardly eating, and truth be told I’m not all sunshines neither, I kinda hoped this journal will make me make sense of it, but I don’t think there’s anything to make sense of honestly.



July 31

 Dad took me to the city today, after we took care of grandpa, of course. He heard Matt speaking with me about when him and his folks went last week and dad said we oughta go there too.

 I don’t know what the city was called or where it was to be transparent, dad probably had no clue either, guess he just wanted to take his son out the village for a change, about time I reckon, I’m 16 and it’s my first time stepping foot outside the village, something bout not being able to get back in time, but I guess today was worth the risk.

 He brought me to one of them fancy clothing shops and bought me a mighty fine pair of boots, paid a mighty fine price for them too. I heard him asking the keep for ‘something that will last a lifetime’. I reckon he wants me to remember him by those boots.

\*this place on the page had a spot that looked like a dried tear\*

 Then, we went to a salon, told me it’d be a shame to go all the way to the city and not have a drink with his one and only boy, he bought us two pints of beer, we drank them fast and then I started to cry, he hugged me and we headed home.

 I sure done embarrassed my dad in front of the fellas at the salon, didn’t I? Now they’re gone think that us country folk can’t hold our drink.



August 1

 My dad always said grandpa was a tough fella, he had this story from when he was a kid.

 They used to have one of them big draught horses back then, now grandpa was a head taller than other men, and his horse was a head taller than him.

  Now one day, the horse was acting up, it got grandma all scared that he was gon run off into the Forest, so grandpa went to tie him down, when he got near it, damn thing turned around and smacked its hoof right into his jaw, now my grandma, naturally, fainted when she saw this, and if my dad wasn’t there to catch her, she might have gotten a nasty bump on her head from the fall, but he was there alright, after he put her down gently, he went outside to try and drag his father from there, or at least scare the horse away, but when he looked, the man was already on his own two legs, and he was angry alright, he was fuming and cussing at the horse, talking about some how dare he hurt his master or something long those lines, so he grabbed that huge horse by its mane, and shoved it to the ground with a single hand.

 Now the horse ain’t never raised another hoof at him for as long as he lived, what I’m trying to say is that if there ever was such a thing as a strong man, my grandpa was him.

 With that in mind.

 Grandpa was missing a leg today. Dad fell on his knees when he saw it.

 Grandpa was a good scarecrow, mighty fine, lasted a whole 30 years, he really was sturdy, if my family had a record book, my grandpa must have been top of the list.

 But we expected grandpa to last through the week, we don’t got all we need right now, delivery boy’s coming tomorrow, I guess we just have to hope grandpa’s up to one last dance.

 I know what needs to be done, Dad taught me good. Mom is sewing the clothes, keeps poking her fingers with the needle, can’t really see much of nothing through the tears.

 There ain’t gone be no sleep in this house tonight, I’m going downstairs to hug my dad while I still can.



August 2

 I did in fact go to sleep, against my wishes, in my dad’s arms no less, haven’t done that since I was a babe. He wrapped me up with a blanket and by the time I was awake, the delivery boy already came and went, dad ain’t wake me up either, I reckon he wasn’t too eager to get on with it himself.

 When I went to the table, my mom and dad were hugging, there was 3 cups on the table, one for each of us, dad went and bought me coffee. Just like the city folks drink in the morning.

 We sat down to drink it and he told me:

 “It ain’t too bitter, boy? We ain’t put anything in it, I hear that’s the fancy way to drink it.”

 And I said that it ain’t bitter at all, I said I ain’t taste nothing bitter in it, matter of fact it’s too sweet for me.

\*there were many dried tears on this part of the page\*

 Then me and dad went outside to search for grandpa, mom passed out on the couch , I reckon from exhaustion, I guess coffee ain’t all it’s cracked up to be.

 We found what remained of grandpa right on the border of the wheat field in a ditch, all torn up, legs and arms and head and chest all a few feet from the others, dad shed a tear, it must have hurt to see your dad die twice, I sure as hell would have done anything to not see it the first time.

 The Forest won a foot of field that day, dad said he expected a bigger loss, grandpa must have fought it off right till before dawn, he really was a sturdy fella, he went unbeaten for three decades and then some, but tonight he lost, and there wasn’t no rematch waiting for him.

 We gathered him all up, put him in the cart.

 The vinegar and clove oil wasn’t covering jack shit today.

 We went to the graveyard, and buried him.

 Ain’t it funny? We’ve been embalming him everyday for thirty years, but on the day we bury him, that’s when we stop.

 We buried him next to his own dad, who was buried next to his own dad, and so on, we had ourselves a little corner of the village graveyard just for our family. Dad’s tombstone was already in place, he told me it’s custom for each one of us to buy his own so he don’t bother his son, I didn’t get that, why worry bout the stone? What bother could be bigger for a son than seeing his dad go away, what’s a stone to that?

 Then it was time to actually do the deed.

 We went back home to take dad’s new clothes and the chair, mom was still sleeping, dad kissed her on the forehead, he told me to tell her he loves her when she wakes up.

 We got to the field, my dad put on the last set of clothes he’ll ever wear, we got a noose that he put around his neck on one end and the other on the tree. He told me to take care of my mother, and that he will take care of us, then he kicked the chair.

 When it was done, I got him down and said the prayer over him, the same one he said over his dad, and the same one my son will say over me.

 Then I embalmed him, just like we used to do with grandpa. I felt awful, was that how he felt every day embalming his own dad?

 One day my oldest is gonna have to do the same to me, I reckon.



August 3

 Dad’s doing fine for himself in the field, I found some crow carcasses near where I found him today, all bitten in half, dad told me the Forest always tries something  the first day when you get on the job, guess it ain’t work on him.

 He must be a good scarecrow as well, it’s in our blood after all.

 Someday I hope to be a good scarecrow too, hell, maybe I’ll last thirty years like grandpa, maybe even more if they come up with some new methods or chemicals.

 But I shouldn’t bet that dad will last this long, I best get started on my own family, so that I have the time to pass on the craft to my eldest, like my dad did. There’s this Suzy girl I’ve been sweet on for a while now, maybe I’ll pull her aside next time we cross paths, tell her bout that time my dad took me to the big city, maybe show off my new boots as well, that’ll get her all over me, I bet.

 

 When I was done reading, I looked over to my mom, and believe me, if it wasn’t for the overgrown forest, the broken window, and most of all, the look in her eyes, I wouldn’t have entertained this was real for a single second.

 “Where’s…”

 I wanted to ask about my father again, but I pieced it together right then.

 I knew where he was, he was hanged from that tree.

 “Mom, I don’t get it, what does this all mean?”

 Then through tears and snot she told me, she told me about my family’s duty, how every father must be made into a scarecrow by their eldest son, that this is the only way they can keep It back, she told me how my dad decided to just let the Forest do what it wants, thinking it can’t be something worse than what they do to prevent it, how they fought with grandpa over it when he was alive.

 About the things that they heard last night, the wolves, the crows, the trees, and other things that couldn’t be rightly called animals, things that other forests don’t have.

 She told me dad had caught a single glimpse of It and made up his mind to become a scarecrow, he did all the preparations himself, he hung himself from the tree just before I arrived so that I wouldn’t have to see it.

 “You have to say the prayer over him.”

 “I don’t get it mom, why does have to be me who says the prayer? Where’s Jack???”

 When I mentioned my big brother’s name, her legs gave out, she fell into my arms and I placed her softly on the sofa.

 She told me they how they were all hiding in the kitchen, that they huddled underneath the table, that the scratching and howling and cawing outside got louder and louder, that it shook them to their core, and just when the howling had reached its peak, the window shattered, a root breaking through it like a tentacle. She told me how it grabbed my brother by the foot and pulled him towards It, my dad managed to grab him by his arms, but the Forest was too strong, It pulled them both towards the broken window, and that was when dad saw it. She told me how his arms gave out instantly. After it took my brother, It settled down.

 My mother says that after my dad saw whatever it was that dragged my brother away, he looked shell-shocked, he wouldn’t listen to a word out of her mouth, he had decided the instant he laid eyes on that thing what needed to be done, it makes you wonder what he saw, right??? What thing could there be out there in that Forest, to make a man give up his life after a single glance.

 After she told me all that happened, her eyes looked like they were about to burst from the swelling, she wasn’t even crying anymore at that point, just dry retching, her tears had dried up from mourning both her son and husband, all in one long day.

 I, on the other hand, simply froze. I couldn’t cry. I couldn’t scream. I couldn’t move.

 I wanted to, but I couldn't.

 This was too much for me to process, I wanted to just drop to my knees and weep.

 But I was scared too, because I realized what this meant.

 “Mom… If Jack’s not around to do it, I’ll be the one that has to do it in his place someday, won’t I?”

  My mom looked at me through her tear, nodding sheepishly, she told me:

 “I’m so sorry, I’m so sorry.” while hugging her (now only) son tightly in her arms.

 I told my wife that my father and brother had died, I told her to move to my mother’s house with me to take care of her, at least for a year, I told her it was a car accident, I told her that that’s why there were no bodies at the funeral, I did not tell her anything about the Forest and the Scarecrow, she wouldn’t believe it and I don’t blame her, I wouldn’t believe myself either.

 This is why I am posting this here, I beg you, if you have heard of anything that could possibly be related to my situation, tell me, I am willing to try anything.

 I've searched every webpage, every book online, every book in the village library, every exorcist, priest, fortune-teller I could find, and no one know anything about the Forest or the Scarecrow.

 Every day I have to embalm my deceased father, and every night I have to hear his reanimated corpse patrolling the field.

 But worse than that is knowing that I will be in his place eventually, and worse than both is knowing that my own son will have to be in his place someday.

 If there is anything you know that might help me, or more importantly, that might help my son, please give me a message, I am begging you on my knees.
