There’s a plastic kid at Orange Orchard Elementary School. 

I saw it the first day I worked there. I’m a janitor. Got the job because the boss is my uncle. I’m not a responsible person. My parents threatened to kick me out if I didn’t start working, so Uncle Clark hooked me up. The hours were early and late, but I’ve always loved being in places I wasn’t supposed to be. Being in an elementary school before it opened and after it closed was like getting paid to trespass. 

But I’m not excited now.

The morning of my first day, Clark made me go around to the rooms and make sure that all the trash had been taken out. The guy right before me was a slacker and cut corners on everything. It’s actually why I was being hired. He’d bailed on my uncle without so much as a text or a resignation letter. Just stopped showing up for work. So that meant I was taking care of the evening shifts.  

Anyway, I went around to each room, poking my head in and getting a look at the trash can. The hallways were dark except for the emergency lights, and everything was quiet. Like funeral silent. When I was younger, the place had felt so much louder, so much bigger. Now the seats could only fit half of my ass. 

Out of nowhere, I opened a door, and there it was. Sitting at a desk. 

It made me jump. It looked like a real person. When it didn’t move, and my heart stopped trying to kill itself, I went to get a closer look. It was a serial killer's art piece, like five mannequins torn apart and then smashed together. Mismatched blue and brown arms, black and white legs. A tan torso. I guessed it was a weird school project, but I didn’t  know what evil teacher would tell their kids to make a fucked up thing like that. 

But after I got used to it, it was more crazy than creepy. Except for the head. Oh god, the head. It was so real, like one of those baby dolls you give grieving mothers. It had the blinking eyes and everything, its face pulled into a creepy half smile like a barbie doll. It sat like one too, arms and legs out, since it didn’t have knee or elbow joints. Its head was pointed towards the door, which was a diabolical joke.  

I had to give the kids who made it credit. Scared the shit out of me. 

So I decided it would be funny to play a joke on them. 

I looked around for a good hiding spot. There weren’t a lot of places to put a four foot tall mannequin, but the ceiling had those cardboard squares that are easy to push out. I picked up the kid. It weighed like five pounds, max. I pushed out a square, and tried to get it into the hole. The arms and legs kept getting in the way, so I pulled those off so it was just the torso and creepy kid head. I threw the parts into the ceiling, and then adjusted the body so its head was peeking just over the edge. When I was putting the cardboard square back, I made sure to leave a little gap. The plastic eyes peaked through, dead and staring. I laughed when I thought about a little kid glancing up and screaming.  

But to be honest, it made my skin get cold a bit too. 

I got the trash the guy before me had left. Then I locked the door behind me. 

The rest of the day was pretty boring. I was expecting some sort of hullabaloo over the kids seeing the plastic face in the ceiling, but nothing happened. I kind of forgot about it. Mostly because kids are fucking gross. They will shit literally anywhere but the toilet. I don’t know why we even bother feeding them, since they’re just gonna puke it back up again like thirty minutes later. By the time lunch was over, I was dirty, sweaty, and two seconds away from jumping off the jungle gym headfirst. 

I was in the cafeteria bumming an orange soda and a slice of cold pizza, when I saw it again.  
The plastic kid, put back together, sitting at one of the lunch tables. 

Even in the light of day, the thing made me jump. The lunch lady, a fifty-something named Delores, just raised an eyebrow, her unlit cigarette jumping in her mouth. I had caught her just before her break. “You alright, junior?” 

I made sure to think about what I would say carefully. Obviously they had found the doll. I didn’t want anyone knowing I had messed with it, since I had a feeling I was gonna get yelled at if they did. I hammed up my fear a bit. “What the hell is that creepy doll at the table?” 

“Watch your mouth. We got kids cleaning up here.” Delores leaned out from behind her station and squinted. The doll wasn’t trying to hide. It was in one of the center tables. I was confused why she was making such a big show of trying to see it. It was literally right there, its plastic head turned in our direction. “You been smoking, son?” 

“No.” I had, but that wasn’t what this was. “Come on, it’s right there.” 

“Sure it wasn’t a kid?” 

“I can still see it. Right there.” I was about to go over to grab it, when for some reason I started to panic. Like you do when someone in a horror movie goes into a pitch black room. Even though I had taken it apart a few hours ago, I didn't want to touch that thing anymore.  
Delores raised another eyebrow at me, and her jaw clamped down on her cigarette, breaking the filter. “You’ve got a lot to learn if you think you can pull one over on me.” She took back the pizza, took a swig of the soda, then left out the back door to smoke. 

When I looked back, the doll was gone. 

Not getting lunch sucked, but I was more worried about the plastic kid. How could Delores not see it? Where did it go? It felt like a rock was sitting cold in the pit of my stomach.  

I went to Uncle Clark. He’d know if I was going crazy. 

He was busy restocking a toilet paper roll in the staff bathroom. I kept an eye out for the teachers, hoping that my questions wouldn’t peg me as the one who moved the doll. 

“Is Delores a kidder?” 

Clark looked up from the toilet paper dispenser. “You ain’t been messing with her, have you? I’ve seen that woman refuse lunch a week or more for a ‘boo.’” 

“No…I just saw some weird stuff and she acted like she couldn’t see it.” 

“What weird stuff?” 

I thought about telling my uncle. But I was spooked. Knowing that Delores didn’t really have any space for nonsense made me wonder if she was telling the truth about not seeing the kid. For a moment, the way my uncle was looking at me, it felt like he knew what I was thinking about, that plastic doll. But if he did, he didn’t say anything. “I don’t know. Just some stuff a kid painted on the wall.” 

“Oh goddamn it, where?” 

I assured my uncle I would take care of it. I wasn’t a complete idiot. I could paint a wall. I pretended to get supplies, but went to the lunch room instead. Already the tables had been pushed up so that the P.E. class could take up the gym space. I waved at the teacher, who didn’t see me. She was too busy making the kids do high knees. I looked for the doll, scanning the edges of the room in case someone just threw it to the side when they were putting everything away. 

No sign of it. It had just vanished. 

I might have just been on edge, but all the way to the janitor's closet, I felt like someone was watching me. Even though the hallway was empty. 

School ended for the day, and Uncle Clark decided that I had been trained up enough to finish the nightly routine by myself. He framed it as a “test” but I’m pretty sure he just wanted to get a head start at Clooneys, the local bar. I didn’t want to work alone, but I couldn’t tell him that. I’d never hear the end of it. Scaredy Bryan, shitting his pants in the dark.  
I just took the list and started doing things as fast as I could. 

All that stuff might have lasted me the rest of the night if I was taking my sweet time. But there was no way in hell I was lazing about. I finished most of the list after two hours. It was winter, and the sun went down early. I didn’t want to be in this place after dark. I was already regretting my little prank in the morning, imagining the doll watching me from every nook and cranny. Whoever was getting me back was clearly better at this than me. 

I was on my last trip to the closet for a roll of toilet paper when I turned a corner and nearly ran into it.  

The plastic kid. 

It stood in the middle of the hallway, fully upright, face pointed directly at me.

I couldn’t move. I had been down this hallway like twelve times. Not once had I seen it there before. It wasn’t like I had just missed it in my rush either. It was right, fucking there. Dead center of the space, nowhere to hide. Even though it only stood as high as my chest, I felt like I was the short one.

“Very…very funny, Uncle Clark.” That had to be the explanation. He was in the building and messing with me. Hell, this whole thing could’ve been started by Clark in the first place. The mannequin could be his, a hazing thing. But I got no answer. 

I figured he must be hiding somewhere, and the only way to get through this was to get it done quickly.

I eased around the doll then sped walk to the janitor's closet. I got my toilet paper, and went back down the hall.

My heart stopped again.

It was gone.

I needed to go. Even if it was just Clark being an ass, someone was here with me where I couldn’t see them. I didn’t like that one bit. I sprinted down the hall to the exit, toilet paper in hand. I took a moment to stop by the bathroom. It was on my way out and I figured I’d just lean the thing up against it. I’d put it in the dispenser during the morning shift. But just as I put it up against the doorframe, I heard it.

*Donk. Donk. Donk. Donk.*

It was a familiar sound, but I couldn’t put my finger on what it was. That being because I was scared shitless and everything in me froze up. It came from a hallway that was between me and the exit. With no other option, I slipped into the girls bathroom. I turned off the light, then cracked the door a peek so I could see what was coming.

*Donk. Donk. Donk.*

The sound stopped just at the junction between the hallways. It was quiet for a while. The doorway blocked my view, and I strained my neck trying to get a view of what was there. Still, that noise nagged at my brain, it felt so familiar. What was it?

I got both my answers in the same moment.

When I was a kid, there were these little plastic tubes that you would hit against stuff to make funny sounds. I used to love those things, smacking 'em on anything and everything. They had a hollow, funky tone to them, like a baby xylophone.

*Donk. Donk. Donk.*

From my crack in the door, a plastic foot came into view. It all clicked for me. Hollow. Plastic. Hit. Donk. 

The doll walked past the bathroom, all on its own. 

No one was moving it. No one was holding it up. It moved on its own in a strange, smooth style, like a giant invisible kid hand was playing with it. Each time its feet would hit the floor, there’d be another *donk.* Its head swiveled on its joint a full 360 rotation, like an owl. It was looking for something. 

I held my breath, not entirely sure I wasn’t hallucinating. 

It stopped a little ways down the hall, and turned. The plastic kid stared at something on the ground. 

The toilet paper against the door. I wanted to punch myself. 

*Donk…Donk…Donk…* 

I tried to psych myself up. I could take this thing. It was just plastic. I had chucked that thing up into the roof just this morning for christsake. I never was that good of a fighter, but maybe I could just push it over, tackle it. 

I decided I would jam the door straight into its face when it got close. 

*Donk…Donk…Donk…* 

I leaned back, and put a foot on the door. 

*Donk.* 

It stopped. There was quiet for a long while. 

Then there was something else… 

Squeaking? A bird. 

I moved to the crack to look. A small robin was bouncing on the far end of the hallway. I had no idea how it got in. It must’ve been living in the roof, coming down in the evenings to pick up crumbs and things. The doll, just outside the door, was staring at it, unmoving. I wondered what was happening. Was it scared of birds?  
The plastic kid lunged. 

In one leap, it flew head first towards the bird, arms extended. There was the sound of slamming. *Donkdonkdonkdonkdonkdonk.* The bird squeaked, screamed. There was a crunch and then no more noise. 

After a moment, there came the slide of meat on plastic. 

Once my stomach calmed down, I realized that this was my chance. It was distracted, with its back to the bathroom. All I needed to do was get to the emergency exit at the end of the hall. 

I slowly opened the door, tiptoed out. The doll was doing something to the bird. I heard the squelch of flesh, and something bubbling. I tiptoed down the floor, being careful not to squeak against the concrete I had polished literally an hour before. It was the longest five minutes of my life. 

Five feet from the door I made the mistake of turning around. I was curious. What was it doing with that bird? My eyes fixed on the plastic kid and all the breath left me. 

The kid was smearing an utterly ruined bird corpse against its rigid lips. Red coated its jaw and neck, and dripped down to its torso. But something else was happening too. The thing was…*melting* into the bird, the corpse already half absorbed by drippy lines of plastic. It’s jaw was gone, replaced with a cascade of mozzarella-like strings. The bird twitched, and disappeared further into the kid. 

I stared. Blinked. The kid’s head swiveled so it was staring right back at me.

I ran. *Donkdonkdonk* came from behind me. The splat of liquid plastic on concrete. I slammed against the door bar, got out, then  put my full weight into closing it. There was the sound of plastic on glass, and the door jumped with surprising strength. Holding on as best I could, I plunged the key into the lock and turned it. I heard more banging and didn’t even bother to double check if I had locked the door. I sprinted to my car and drove as far away as I could. 

There was no sleep that night. Even though I blocked up all the windows and pushed my dresser in front of my door, I couldn’t relax. I don’t know if it was some exhaustion induced hallucination, but I swear I could hear a faint *Donk. Donk. Donk.* coming from my window in the middle of the night.

Uncle Clark got mad at me when I told him the next morning. He said I would need to try harder if I wanted to get out of work. He told me not to talk about it anymore, and gave me a orange soda to calm down.

My parents didn’t believe me either. They threatened to turn me homeless if I quit.  
Can’t do that. The last thing I need is for one of those bastards to show up when I’ve got only tent walls to keep me safe.

I’m still working the night shift. For a while, I didn’t see the plastic kid again, except from the corners of my eye. I’d catch a glimpse at lunch tables, in the back of a classroom, in the ceiling of the teachers lounge. It would always be gone when I went to get a second look. Sometimes at night I’d hear a *Donk. Donk. Donk.* somewhere around the corner. But nothing more than that. No chasing. I kept my head down, constantly looking over my shoulder.

Today, as I was locking up, I turned and saw a face peeking at me through the school's front door. There the kid was, half-smiling like an idiot. 

And there was another face in the next window. 

Two plastic children raised up their hollow arms and waved to me. I could tell what they were saying even without them saying it.
