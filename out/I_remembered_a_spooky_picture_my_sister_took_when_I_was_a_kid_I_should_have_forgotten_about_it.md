Lately, I've felt empty. I'm not sure if it's just the current state of things, having just lost my career over a drunken bar fight, or having so recently lost my closest friends. 

Time moves slower and slower, and I just exist, waiting for the inevitability of death. I think that's why I'm here, today. Like a shitty coping mechanism.

I want to say it all started maybe 23 years ago, when I was around 3 or 4 years old. The details have always been a bit soft as I wasn’t one to pay much attention to my surroundings growing up. I was staying at my aunt Kelly's house with my sister Grace, cousin Nicole and their friend, Valerie. I was playing on the Game Boy Advance, a sketchy port of a hardcore BMX game complete with bit crushed punk rock. It was the time of my life at that age.

The girls, each around 10 years my elders, played some obscure midnight game all the while. I don't remember exactly what it was, and I really wish that I did, as it would help bring me some closure, but it involved candles, repetitious phrases, mirrors and total darkness, the usual Bloody Mary deal.

At one point, the old Polaroid camera was brought out. Giggles of excitement filled the room. The girls were clearly up to something as they all gathered round to take their ancient form of selfie.

Ka-chunk. The photo is snapped, and all is well for the few seconds it takes for it to develop. But then it does, and my sister turns pale in the face. She starts to hiccup and cry. Nikki sweats and takes a step away to clear her throat. Val, the only one not totally drained of color, takes the picture downstairs to my mother and Kelly.

Mom tells them to get rid of the picture immediately. How it's "satanic" and "wrong." She's always been a hyper-religious type, so that sort of response wasn't much to go off of. It could have been anything wrong with the picture's development. For all I knew, it just came out way overexposed. But I never found out, because my sister listened, and the photo was burned that evening before I ever got the chance to look.

I carried that missing image in my head for years after that point, always wondering what they could have possibly seen to make them react that way. But eventually, like most memories from that age, it faded into obscurity. That was, until last January, when we got news that Nicole had passed away from a heart attack.

We cousins had always been as close as siblings, growing up together, so the news came as a shock and quite the tough one at that.

It reminded me, weirdly enough, of that funny childhood tale of something spooky potentially appearing in the dark behind the girls. Was it real? Was it just their minds playing tricks on them, a quirk of the lighting? One of the only people who would have known just died, and I hadn't heard about Val in years at this point.

I know it's awful, but, I was more curious than ever now that Nikki was dead.

Anyways, I figured now that we'd had a few months to mourn, that it would be a good time to reminisce on some memories and finally ask the question that had been on my mind since that night; what exactly was in that Polaroid photo?

The perfect opportunity comes just a month ago, when my mom and I decided to drive to her new apartment to help her unpack. We get there, knock on the door, and wait around for nearly ten minutes before my 8-year-old nephew, Zeke, opens the door. He says that she's in her room sleeping. The apartment itself looked like a hurricane had gone through it recently.

Sure enough, she was passed out on the bed next to a bottle of Coors. Mom and I helped her get cleaned up. It took a few hours, a few arguments, and a few charmingly pointless conversations, but we got the deed done (or at least, cleaned things up to a more manageable mess) in time for sundown.

My mom went outside to go have a smoke. That's when I decided to pop the question on Grace. Something along the lines of "what was with that cursed picture you took as a kid?" It took a bit of back and forth for her to figure out what I was talking about, but as soon as she did, her breathing stopped.

There was no way of getting the words out of her, not at first. She tried to change the subject, and fought with me on it, telling me to forget about it and that it wasn't something she was comfortable with. I kept pushing, though, because I was curious. Stupidly so.

I told her about my crisis of faith. How I needed to know if she had really seen a demon.

"A demon?" she asked. "You thought I saw a demon?"

Well, yeah. That's what the implication had been all these years.

No, though, she explained. It was no demon, it was just...her. Her, Nikki and Val, but all three of them different. Aged, some by a little, some by a lot. Some had gained weight; some bore heavy bags under their once-youthful eyes. It was like a projection into the future, she said. Her future.

She said the photo didn't look too much off from how she was now. Disheveled, depressed, eyes dilated and groggy. How she felt like her life had been spinning out of control more and more since Nikki, her one and only real friend, passed away so suddenly.

It makes no sense to me at the time. I ask her for proof, to which she gets upset. I can't help it, though. With how she'd been acting lately, missing work, going on manic fits against my mother in her texts, I just, well, didn't believe it. That, and the fact that what she was telling me was along the same vein as many of her other gaslight stories meant to shock me.

Tangent aside, I asked her for proof, she got upset, and after another painful back and forth, we finally come to the conclusion that she actually *kept* the Polaroid camera she used to take the picture, all these years. All we would need is some standard film reel from eBay or something.

So I took the camera home with me, sent an order for the proper film online, and waited.

About a week later, Grace passed away from an accidental overdose. This was three weeks ago, today. The funeral was last Sunday. I still feel sick to my stomach, but maybe that's just the migraine.

Last week, the specific roll of film I needed arrived in the mail. Took them long enough. I snapped a photo of myself, and after staring at it while it developed for a few minutes, I noticed...nothing. Nothing really out of the ordinary, beyond a weird splotchy artifact right above my lip. Nothing about me was changed, otherwise.

So now I sit, waiting, because this photo has brought me zero closure. In fact, it's only made me more scared. Because what if the lack of change means it's already started? What if my time has already come, and I just don't know it?

I can't decide on what to do anymore. The past few days have been maddening, like Chinese water torture levels of maddening. I feel like at any moment, something's going to reach out from the beyond and drag me with it.

My head is screaming. My nose is bleeding.

I don't know what else to do besides write it out.
