This is my first time writing here, but I didn’t know where else to turn or who else to tell who’d even listen. I’ve never experienced anything paranormal or even anything remotely scary until this. I was always a skeptic, not believing in ghosts or ghouls, but this experience changed me for better or for worse.

I grew up in a small, quaint village. For context, there are more sheep in my hometown than residents, even my neighbor was a shepherd. There isn’t a shop within 10 miles, only a village bar, which goes to show what type of people live there. We didn’t even have cell towers here, and the only phones that worked were landlines. My house isn’t large by any means, but we do have a huge backyard. Due to that, my parents decided to build our garage detached from the main house, making it its own smaller 2 story building. There isn’t any staircase in our garage though, just a rusty metal ladder leading up to a dusty overhang directly above where we would park our old car. We always had a garage fridge where my dad would keep his beer, and I would keep my sodas. I remember playing video games late at night after my parents would go to sleep, then sprinting to the garage through the pitch dark of the yard to grab a coke. We didn’t have any lights in our backyard, and the only light in the garage was an ancient lightbulb hanging from the ceiling. I would flick on that light, watch it stutter for a few seconds, then hum to a low glow before I would start making my way to the fridge each and every time.

Last night, however, the light faltered. I had woken up in the middle of the night, with a thirst only an ice-cold soda pop could quench. I forced myself to get up out of bed and slide into my sandals to go downstairs, making sure to grab my phone just in case. I made my way down the steps to the backyard, using the small remote we had at the entrance to open the large garage door. I turned on my phone flashlight, then started walking the 30 or so feet to my garage. It didn’t provide too much security, though it would do for the time being. As I stepped onto my patio, I heard the chirping of crickets and a light breeze blowing through the branches of our walnut tree. I took a deep breath, taking in the chilly midnight air and sighing peacefully, when everything got quiet. The crickets seemed to have disappeared, and the air was stiller than I’d ever felt before. Somehow, it felt like time had stopped, yet I was the only thing exempted. My heart started beating faster, and my breaths became shallow and fast. I spun around, using my phone flashlight to identify what was going on, though everything seemed fine. Then, as if nothing had happened, the crickets started chirping again and the breeze came back.

I was confused but chalked it up to being half-awake still. I shrugged it off and started trudging through my backyard towards my garage. Since I was wearing sandals, I made sure to watch my step carefully with my phone flash. It had rained earlier that day, and there was still a faint trace of water on the ground, as I felt the wet dirt squelching with each step. I could see maybe 3 feet in front of me, which definitely didn’t make me feel good, but I was used to it after all these years. Out here, away from the bustling of the city, there were no streetlights, and the only illumination I would get came from the moon itself. However, due to the rainy weather prior, it was blocked out by a thin sheet of gray clouds, so I had to rely purely on my weak phone flashlight. Because of that, it took me a while to notice the footprints in the dirt.

I was almost at the garage when I noticed them. They could’ve easily been animal prints, since stray cats and dogs and sometimes even wild foxes or deer would pass through our yard at night all the time, but for some reason, these prints felt different. It was hard to tell, as the remaining water in the dirt had faded them slightly, though they seemed too long and wide to be an animal. The only viable option would be a bear, though they were really only in the mountains. I felt uneasy looking at those prints, and I felt like something was off. I turned my head around in the dark, trying to see if anyone or anything was around, but it was inky black all around me. I considered going back to the house to wake up my dad, but then I felt my throat begging for some soda again and decided to keep going. I finally made it to the garage after what felt like a lifetime, turning on the light switch and hearing the familiar hum of electricity make it to the lightbulb. It flashed once, twice, then steadied, giving the garage a warm glow of orange. I turned my flashlight off on my phone, then looked around the garage, the rusty old car in the same spot as always.

We hadn’t cleaned in a while, and there were spiderwebs in the corners, though the spiders never really bothered me too much. I saw a small daddy longlegs crawling along the ceiling, then let my eyes lower towards the overhang. It was darker up there, since we had a lot of old boxes and suitcases blocking out the light, though I still saw it. Peeking at me from behind a box of my old toys was a figure with eyes darker than the night, a pale hand wrapped around the box as if looking around a corner. I stared at it, not daring to move a muscle, and watched it retreat deeper into the overhang. Right at that moment, the lightbulb started stuttering once more, though this time, it didn’t turn back on. I was plunged back into the darkness, this time not alone. I fumbled with my phone, finally turning on the flash, then used it to look around everywhere, from the car to the fridge to the rusty ladder leading up to where that thing was, finally resting it on the overhang where I had seen the figure. I didn’t see anything, so I slowly backed out of the garage, forgetting all about my thirst. Once I passed the threshold back into the night, I started sprinting towards my house to wake up my parents. I tripped. I tumbled onto the grass, my phone flying out of my hand and landing a few feet in front of me.

I got up quickly, picking my phone back up from the ground, then shining it towards where I tripped. I saw something lying on the ground, something breathing. I took a tentative step forward to get a better look at the object, and noticed it was one of the neighbor’s sheep. It was still alive, even though there was a chunk taken out of its stomach, its intestines pouring out. I could hear it taking shaky breaths, barely clinging on to life. How it had gotten into our yard, I had no idea, but the more pressing matter was what happened to it. I kneeled down next to it, gagging at the stench of blood. I took a look at its wounds, seeing claw marks along its snout and a broken leg, probably what grounded it in the first place. The bite on its belly seemed fresher, as if it was for the kill. Whatever hurt it hadn’t eaten anything though, it seemed to have taken its life for fun. The sheep couldn’t have gotten here by itself either, so the culprit must have dragged it all the way here, and I had a strong suspicion whatever was in my garage is what did this. The sheep looked at me, still holding on. It made a faint “baa” and kicked its legs weakly. I looked at it pitifully, wishing I had a way to take it out of its misery, when I was knocked onto my back. I craned my neck forward, seeing the sheep get dragged away into the dark, bleating desperately. Whatever that thing was, it had come back to claim its kill. It must have followed me out of the garage, stalking me.

Luckily, I didn’t see it anywhere, so I got back on my feet. The crickets were long gone, and the breeze that comforted me when I first stepped outside had left me out to dry.  I sprinted again in the direction of my house, or what I thought was the direction of my house. Instead, I found myself hitting the concrete wall surrounding our yard. I had gone the wrong way. I must’ve lost my sense of direction when that thing knocked me down. I looked around, realizing I was on the opposite side of the yard. I was at the back, where the previous owner of our house had a rusty old bike and my dad had built a shed. “The shed!” my mind told me. It was full of tools, even a buzzsaw. That was probably my only hope, though that thing wasn’t really hostile towards me. My fear didn’t take account of that however, since seeing something that looked human but not completely wasn’t something I could wrap my head around. I pushed hard on the wooden door of the shed, and it creaked open. I coughed as a cloud of dust billowed upwards. This shed hadn’t been used in years, since my dad had started putting his newer tools in the garage, though I didn’t dare go back there. I saw the rusted buzzsaw next to the door, then turned my phone to the back of the shed.

Hanging on a hook on the wall were some tools, including an axe. I lifted it off of its hook, tossing it in my hands, feeling the weight of it. I don’t know what that thing was, but hopefully this was enough to protect myself against it. I turned around to head back out of the shed, when I saw the figure once more. It was peeking around the corner of the door of the shed, staring at me blankly. I stared back, fear wracking my bones. It had once again wrapped its hand around the doorframe, and I got a better look this time. It fit the description of horror stories I had read online before, but not exactly. It was pale and thin, bipedal, with clawed, humanoid hands. Its eyes were extremely dark, and I could see half of its mouth baring its bloodstained teeth at me. It would’ve passed as a person if I saw it from afar, but it definitely wasn’t one. The biggest difference between this thing and the monsters I had read about before was that this one was small. Maybe 4 feet in stature, I looked down at it rather than up. That didn’t reassure me too much, however, as I saw what it did to that sheep. At this point, I was sure this entity was out to get me. I felt adrenaline pumping in my heart, finally taking away some fear. I took a quick look to my left, picked up a pair of rusty pliers and flung them at the thing at the door. It made a high-pitched screech and disappeared from the door faster than should’ve been possible, slamming the door hard behind it.

Once again, I was plunged into darkness, though this time I was reassured by the fact that thing wasn’t in here with me. I walked towards the door, prying it open. Using my flash, I looked around in the dark once more. There were many smaller trees back here that grew pears, apples, and other fruits. In the back corner there was a huge cherry tree, ripe with freshly growing berries. There it sat, looking down at me from one of the upper branches. Luckily, I had brought the axe with me, so I wasn’t as scared as before. It started moving on all fours, scampering around the tree as fast as a squirrel but 50 times its size. In the hand that wasn’t gripping the shed’s doorframe it held a bloody part of the sheep it had killed like a trophy, carrying it around wherever it went. “That poor sheep.” I thought to myself. My neighbor sometimes let me visit and help him feed the sheep, so I was somewhat attached to them. This monster didn’t care clearly, it just killed it for fun, not even for nourishment. Right as I was thinking about this, it dropped down from the tree. I jumped backwards, though it didn’t head towards me. I watched it run across the ground and towards a smaller plum tree, scrambling its way up the trunk and towards the upper branches. I couldn’t see its body anymore, though I was able to see its large, pitch-black eyes staring at me through the leaves. “What does this thing want?” I asked myself. It hadn’t attacked, though it sure as hell had many opportunities to do so.

I decided to handle things myself, so I started walking towards the tree it was, brandishing my axe. I made it to the base of the tree and looked up. It was gone. In its place was a stray cat, licking itself. I looked around, bewildered. Had everything I just experienced been nothing but a hallucination? The only explanation for that would’ve been sleep deprivation, but it was so real. I made my way back to the garage, making sure to head in the right direction this time. I looked around where the sheep carcass was, but there wasn’t even a speck of blood in the area nor any sign that there was something that large nearby. The footprints were still there, but they were in the shape of my sandals rather than the weird form I had seen them before. At this point, I felt like I was going crazy. There was no sign of anything I had just experienced, and there was no way for me to prove to myself that it was real. I walked to the garage, once again feeling the dryness in my throat. The lightbulb was humming on as if it had never turned off in the first place. I went to the fridge and pried it open, hoping to grab myself an ice-cold soda. In their place laid a tuft of sheep’s wool.
