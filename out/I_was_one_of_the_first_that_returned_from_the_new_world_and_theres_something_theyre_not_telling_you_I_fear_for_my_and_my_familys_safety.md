I'm keeping my name off of this letter for obvious reasons: They've been spreading the word of the new world, that new forest we discovered, like the plague in the cities.. and ya, that's the right word for it. Plague.

I know it's backwards to get my word out here now in that I'm spreading it further anyway, but it's better than their beautification of what is dangerous. And chances are the news of it will spread throughout the world anyway, so hopefully my version will too.

Some of you outside of the cities may not have heard of it yet, but I doubt my message will spread further than theirs faster, but I hope so for the sake of any man, woman, or child that may go through the torment that their shiny advertisement may sway you or anyone else you love to enlist to their settling of it.

The land **was** beautiful, but **it is not** safe. Way more dangerous than their sway of adventure might hold you. Not the danger of wildlife, not the danger of snakes, not the rushing rivers, or large landscapes, no.

It's the people, the.. things.. that were there before us.

If you can even call them that.

Before these elites decided to publicize the knowledge that we found the new land and advertise it's upcoming enlistment of a militant settlement program there, we were actually a secret mission against the Veritun enemy.

They selected around two hundred of us from the fatherland's military to sail through the horizon eye of the ocean to see if there were a backward flanking position into their main port cities that were less guarded, and yes, that's how desperate we truly were. But what we found was this whole new land out there. At first it was beautiful, intimidating the closer we got, but it was so much worse than we expected when we got there.

I ask that if you do find this letter, spread the message smartly. Scribe these words down on a seperate letter, and spread the location of where you discovered it by word of mouth, only to those you trust, everything else by parcel, by parchment, there is no other way:

**Trust anyone from the Fifth Regiment with your life.**

I will arrive back to read and reply to your messages when I feel it is safe to do so, I have some people on my side here, and my family is in a safe location. It may take some time until I find and read them as I have to lay low for the time being. I have to keep a keen discretion on whose I reply to as well.

Within the spread of this message I would also like to add any of those around New Lincoln that have been effected by the recent wyvern fireraids, that if you need assistance the church on **Herald** has supplies. They're trying to help everyone, but with their church crumbling from the blasts it's difficult.

Talk to **Head Pastor John** if you're looking to and able to volunteer and help them out.

Anyways, the stories you've heard **are** true.

The land is large, trees were as tall as the sky, beautiful, pulling at the imagination within you when you looked into the many wood: but it was like you were an ant near a fleet of knight boots, too small amongst such large beings.

Spurring fear in us at first, but now seen as adventure, as they say. Which I could understand.

I wanted absolutely no parts in it, but the officers needed to report a situation, and that was it.

The first thing that set me off about the whole excursion, beside the colossal size of the trees, was a lone boat that was moored upon the flat beaches. We had three boats in our fleet, and two smoke signals were sent up over the backward horizon, so what was a boat doing there?

The trees there were too colossal of size on our research upon the Veritun's land: and upon further inspection- the boat was one of ours.

The crew of our boat had found no sign of any men there. No camp, no tents, nothing- the boat was even weathered and barnacled. Not standard for a military crew and its vessel especially one that was cleanable upon land.

The main point being, there was something fishy happening, we don't get lazy.

The captain of the ship and the officer had us pick lots, and much to my dismay I was selected. A group of four of us, sent with one handgun, was sent to investigate further inward while they awaited and signaled the other boats.

And that land was eerie. My steps in the grass seemed to suck my feet within, the size difference between the foliage and the shadowy trees, and the breathing in of the fog of the marsh and bogs made my head feel weird and like I were in a dream or that it wasn't real.

I swear I kept seeing movement in the small breezes and I kept feeling presences in the large bark and vine. I had to halt the squadron multiple times just to make sure nothing was there, and we yelled out for anybody, anyone. No one, nothing. Silence. Echoing silence. For what seemed like thirty minutes we yelled out for them, nothing. And we decided to head back.

On our way back though one of the crew, Thomas, a former prior, who was one of the meanest right assholes I've ever met (may God rest his soul), saw and pointed out some wooden structures to the right of where we entered that we had not seen before.

A town and clearing of log cabins that was now overgrown, with vine and forest.

We did a preliminary check, yelling and searching, but no one within, the buildings hosting nothing living, except for the greenery I suppose.

Back on the beach we told the captain and the officer what we found as the other ships made way toward the beach. That night our fleet made home there.

~

At this point the men and I were rightfully questioning what was happening there.
The officers and captains didn't know, not even the admiral, or at least that's what they were saying. I, and the other men had thought different, divisions were being made and, I have to admit, I was one of the main culprits of that occuring.

I didn't trust that they had no idea what was happening, the trees and the smell of the fog had been getting to me, I hated it, it felt like my brain was burning from the inside; there was one of our ships on that beach, a small town in that foggy god-forsaken jungle, there was some answer and I knew they knew it. The talks became more than I supposed, their ears heard mutinous whispers over a burning distrustful skeptical pricking.

I had been assigned hunting duty at a lone cabin found nearby that next night with two of their priors.

That night, that night was when I knew those officers had nothing to do with this. That though the true word has not been spread of the danger of that land, that they had not known that night either what was truly out there or what was occuring.

I was still scared shit-less going into it, I thought the worst I was going to get was a beating that night, but the look in their firey eyes said otherwise, this jungle was getting to them too. Oddly, I accepted my fate on the walk there. I thought even if I were to die, I would be safe, and I wouldn't have this burning in my mind anymore. I was never a holy man, neither do I believe I am now- but that night when I was being led to that cabin, I felt a profound sense of calm, and protection.

The moments before sleep, were silent by candlelight, the men that took me there seemed mad I wasn't upset, that I wasn't fearful. Their wrath was patient though, if not hateful. The moment they started talking again though, my fear rose up again; words full of hate against my peace, words that were serrated, covered by the innocence of what they were actually saying, but obviously apparent in what was about to happen, and also that they knew I knew their true meaning too. That man had that handgun the officers had gave me before- he turned his head and I thought to leap at him but there was a heavy slam on the closed front door of the cabin.

I remember that night we looked at eachother, and suddenly those men and I were boys. Terrified, out of mind, children. We creeked that door open and nothing was there in the dark forest. We stood out on that porch- but, something **was** out there. A faded bright bluish-white light, way out in the distance, farther into the forest, with the shadow of a man.

\~

I say all this not to elicit ungrounded fear in my common man but to expose the truth of what happened that night. For us three, we went out there in that night expecting one of those lost men out there, but found nothing but a bewitching light, and death.

For that was no man, but a murdering shadow of a monster, double our size- and I came back to that log house village with that handgun in hand, and the shakes of the blood and bone of those comrades on my face expecting judgement of something I did not, nor could, do.

The reports they would send to the higher officials in the military when we had arrived back would be ones of those knight's bodies hanging from trees, splayed, flayed, and tethered by sinew.

By the way of light and beauty they found next to it, they would wish to enlist more young men to journey.

Do not send your sons there, do not trust their words, do not trust the beauty they mark of it. There is only pain there, and suffering.

I don't know how much longer I have here before they get me, foregoing my name was only one small barrier to them finding me.

But death to the Veritun.

And damned be that native.

Glory be to the true fatherland, not what they are making of it, not what those recruiters are sending our men out to find.

Don't trust them.
