The first message came from a number I didn't recognize.



It said, “Sarah, are you there?”



I ignored it. A few minutes later, another message appeared. “I need help, Sarah. Please answer.”



I had no idea who was texting me. But before that night was over, the person behind those messages would be telling me exactly where I was standing, what was parked in front of me, and where he wanted me to go.



This happened on Thanksgiving in 2014. My name is Sarah, and I've never been able to forget that night.



Thanksgiving was always something I looked forward to. My family enjoyed having people over, and that year was no different. We had invited some friends and relatives for dinner. We spent most of the afternoon preparing food, getting the house ready, and making sure there was enough for everyone.



By the time everyone arrived, the house was full of conversation, laughter, and the smell of Thanksgiving dinner. Nothing felt unusual. We ate together, talked for a while, and eventually decided to watch a movie. Most of us gathered in the living room. Some people were still finishing their food, while others settled onto the sofas.



I remember checking my phone when I noticed a message from an unfamiliar number. “Sarah, are you there?” There was no introduction. No name. Just that question. I assumed it was either a wrong number or some kind of promotional message. Around the holidays, I often received random advertisements and messages from numbers I didn't recognize. So I ignored it and went back to watching the movie.



A little while later, my phone vibrated again. It was the same number. “I need help, Sarah. Please answer.” That second message caught my attention. The first one could easily have been a mistake, but this person was using my name and asking specifically for me. I looked at the number again, trying to remember whether I had seen it before. I hadn't.



I considered asking someone in the room if they recognized it. But everyone was enjoying the movie, and I didn't want to interrupt them over something that might turn out to be nothing. Instead, I replied. “Yes, I'm here. Who is this?”



The response came shortly afterward. But it wasn't a name. It was a picture. Someone had sent me a photograph of the street outside my house. I recognized the area immediately. It was taken from one side of the street, looking toward the houses nearby. For a moment, I stared at the image, trying to understand why a stranger would send it to me.



Then another message appeared. The person explained that he was outside, somewhere near our street. He said he needed food. Since it was Thanksgiving, he was hoping someone might be willing to help him. He asked whether I could bring him something to eat.



I remember feeling uncomfortable, but also a little guilty for being suspicious. We had just finished a large family dinner. There was plenty of food left, and this person was claiming he had nothing. I knew there were people who genuinely needed help, especially during the holidays. And the fact that he had contacted me by name made me wonder whether he might be someone I'd met before. Maybe someone from the neighborhood. Maybe someone who had received my number through a mutual acquaintance. I couldn't figure it out.



I thought about telling my family, but I was an adult, and at the time, it didn't seem like something I needed to make a big announcement about. I wasn't planning to go anywhere far. I thought I'd step outside, give him some food, and come straight back. So I went to the kitchen. I found a box and put together some sandwiches and leftover pies. Then I quietly made my way to the front entrance. I didn't tell anyone where I was going. That was something I would regret almost immediately.



When I stepped outside, I expected to see someone waiting near the gate. But there was nobody there. I looked along the street in both directions. I didn't see anyone who appeared to be waiting for food. I checked the number and decided to call.



The person answered. I told him I was outside with the food but couldn't see him. He said he hadn't been able to come all the way to my house. Then he asked if I could walk farther down the street. He explained that he didn't feel comfortable accepting food directly outside someone's home. He wanted me to bring it to him near the end of the lane.



I hesitated. I remember looking back toward my house. Everyone was still inside. I could have gone back and asked someone to come with me. I could have left the food at the gate. But at that moment, I was still thinking of him as someone who needed help. The distance didn't seem particularly far. And I convinced myself that if anything felt wrong, I could simply turn around. So I started walking.



I kept the phone to my ear while carrying the food in my other hand. As I moved farther away from the house, I kept looking for someone standing by the roadside. But I couldn't see anybody. Eventually, I reached the area near the corner. I stopped and told him I was there.



He responded immediately. “I can see you.” I looked around. There were houses on either side, a few parked vehicles, and parts of the street that were darker than others. But nobody was standing where I could see them. I asked him where he was. Instead of answering directly, he told me I was standing near the corner. Then he said something that made me feel sick. He mentioned a red pickup truck parked nearby. He told me to get inside it.



For a second, I couldn't understand what he meant. I thought I must have misunderstood him. I told him I wasn't getting into anyone's vehicle and that I'd only come to give him food. Then I turned toward home.



His voice changed. He told me not to go back. He repeated that he could see me. And this time, it didn't sound like a request. I suddenly became aware of how far I'd walked from my house. I'd left a room full of family and friends without telling a single person where I was going. And now I was alone on the street, speaking to a stranger who knew exactly where I stood.



I started looking for the quickest way back. That's when I heard movement behind me. I turned. Two men were standing a short distance away. Both had their faces covered. One of them appeared to be speaking on a phone. I couldn't be certain, but I believed he was the person I'd been talking to. The other man was carrying what looked like a revolver. I froze. One of them gestured toward the red pickup.



That was when I understood this had never been about Thanksgiving dinner. The messages, the picture, the request for food, even the instructions to meet at the end of the street. They had all led me to that spot. I still didn't know what these men wanted. I didn't know whether they intended to rob me, abduct me, or do something worse. I only knew that I didn't want to find out.



I thought about running, but they were between me and the way back toward my house. One of them had a gun. I was terrified that any sudden movement would make him use it. So I began moving slowly in the direction of the pickup. I was trying to think of anything I could do.



Then another vehicle approached from behind. I heard its engine before I looked. It was coming down the street toward us. For a brief moment, both men seemed distracted by the approaching car. And I realized that might be my only opportunity. I didn't wait to see what happened next. I ran. I turned away from the pickup and sprinted toward another street. I could hear movement behind me, but I didn't look back. All I could think about was getting somewhere they couldn't follow me.



I ran as hard as I could. Somewhere during those moments, I lost my shoes. I honestly don't remember when they came off. I only remember the feeling of running on the road without them. My phone was still in my hand. I managed to call my brother, James. When he answered, I was struggling to get the words out.



I told him I'd been tricked into leaving the house. That two unknown men had approached me, one of them was armed, and I thought they were following me. I tried to explain where I was while continuing to run. James immediately understood that something was seriously wrong. I knew he would come looking for me, but I couldn't stop.



I kept moving through the neighborhood, trying to put as much distance as possible between myself and the men. Then I noticed an open doorway. I recognized the house. It belonged to the Henderson family, who owned a local supermarket. I headed straight for it. I went inside and closed the door behind me. I was frightened and struggling to catch my breath.



I told the people there what had happened. I explained about the messages, the red pickup, and the two men who had approached me. Someone looked outside. But the men were no longer visible. Not long afterward, James arrived in the area with some of our cousins. He'd come out looking for me as soon as he realized I was in danger. Seeing him was the first time I felt anything close to safe.



We contacted the police and told them everything. The officers checked the surrounding area, looking for the men and the vehicle I'd described. But they couldn't find them. The men were gone. We gave the police the number that had contacted me. Later, we were told the number couldn't be traced to a reliable identity. I don't know how it had been registered or who had been using it. I just know it never led us to the people responsible.



News of what happened gradually spread through the neighborhood. People were frightened by the idea that unknown men had apparently been watching someone near their own home and using messages to draw them outside. For a while afterward, police patrols became more noticeable in the evenings. As far as I know, there were no further reports of the same men approaching anyone else in that way. And I never heard anything more about them.



But there were questions I couldn't stop thinking about. How did they know my name? How did they get my phone number? Had they chosen me specifically, or was I simply someone they believed they could trick? And how long had they been watching before sending that first message? I don't have answers to any of those questions.



What frightens me most is how easily everything happened. I was surrounded by people who cared about me. My brother was nearby. There were friends and relatives sitting inside our home. But I walked out without telling any of them. I thought I was helping someone who needed a Thanksgiving meal. Instead, I ended up alone on a dark street, being directed toward a stranger's vehicle by two men whose faces I never clearly saw.



Sometimes I think about the car that came down the road at exactly the moment I needed a distraction. I don't know what would have happened if it hadn't appeared. And I don't want to imagine it. Since that night, I've been much more careful about messages from unknown numbers. I don't respond to strangers simply because they know my name. And if someone needs help, I make sure I can offer it without putting myself in danger.



That Thanksgiving changed the way I think about trust. Because the person who contacted me didn't begin by threatening me. He began by asking for help. And that was what almost got me into his truck.
