I wrote everything down while Hannah looked up the Morse alphabet online. Then we translated the first code: P-A-P-A.

My knees went weak and I collapsed on the floor. Is this really happening? Or am I losing my damn mind? Is any of this real? Is Hannah real? Is this house real? While my thoughts spun out of control, Hannah reached for the pen. 

“Theo, is that you?” she asked out loud. 

Moments later we had our answer: Y-E-S. 

“Is Noah with you?” 

Again the answer was: Y-E-S.

Hannah and I fell into each other’s arms and cried. Then I asked the question that had been burning in my heart for three decades: “Where are you?” 

The faucet answered: I-N  T-H-E  H-O-U-S-E.

“How is that possible?” 

The faucet went silent. 

“Theo! How is that possible?” 

The water started dripping again: W-E   A-R-E   T-H-E   H-O-U-S-E.

“What is that supposed to mean?”

 I saw the same helpless look on Hannah’s face that I must have had, and I thought of the spiritual medium my mother had hired twenty years ago. Back then she had said Theo was “in the walls,” and we had all written her off as a quack. 

“I... I don’t know. The house somehow swallowed them,” I said in a thin voice. 

“How can a house swallow people? That’s insane!” 

“Do you have a better explanation?” 

Hannah turned back to the sink: “How did you get in there?”

For a while it was quiet. Then the drops came at different speeds: S-A-C-R-I-F-I-C-E.

“Sacrifice...” My head spun, my stomach cramped. For a moment I thought I was trapped in a nightmare and would wake up any second. But I didn’t wake up. I was sitting on the cold bathroom floor, talking to a sink with my brother trapped inside. The faucet kept dripping: N-U-M-B-E-R  5. 

Our eyes moved to the house across the street, the one with the number five.

“How well do you know the woman who lives there?” I asked Hannah. 

She shrugged. “I don’t have much to do with her. She’s pretty plain. But actually quite nice.” 

Then I remembered a detail so small and so unrelated to our fate that I had almost forgotten it. “She had severe cancer. And a few weeks after Theo disappeared, she beat it.” 

“Sacrifice,” Hannah said quietly. “That also explains why the tornado spared this area.” Hannah rubbed her forehead.

Neither of us dared to say out loud what we were both thinking about this woman. Who she really was. I got up and ran for the exit. Hannah followed me while every faucet in the house went crazy and water sprayed from all the pipes. The floor filled with water, the pipes in the walls burst open. Thick drops ran down the walls, as if the house were crying. Only later did I understand that Theo and Noah had been trying to warn us. But it was too late.

I pounded on her door while Hannah stood behind me. And when she opened it, I noticed she had barely aged in thirty years. She should have been around seventy. But she looked exactly the way she did the last time I saw her. 

“What did you do to the children?” 

She smiled thinly. 

“What children?” 

“You know exactly what I mean,” I said and pushed past her, straight into the living room. 

“I’m not leaving until you tell me how to get my brother out of there.” 

Her house was bright, tastefully furnished, and smelled of lavender everywhere. And yet I could feel the presence of evil. Something was clearly wrong here. Hannah felt it too and grabbed my hand. 

“Please, let’s go.” 

“I want answers!” I screamed. 

“And what if you can’t handle the answers?” the woman said and sat down on the sofa. 

“What if I told you the sacrifices were necessary?” 

“Necessary? Are you damn serious?” I screamed, shaking with rage. The woman turned to Hannah. 

“If the tornado had come, it would have destroyed everything. Your son and your husband would have died. I saw it.” 

Hannah froze. 

“This way, both of them are still alive. So you should actually be grateful to me.” 

“You’re insane.” 

“You just can’t see the bigger picture, and it’s driving you mad.”

Her smug grin and the offhand tone she used to talk about our tragedy made me so furious that I charged at her. I wanted to grab her by the shoulders when an invisible blow suddenly hit me and flung me against the window.

Through a blur, I saw the woman get up and walk toward me.

What happened next, I can’t explain, only describe.

I lay tied to a table in a windowless room. There were candles all around me. Hannah lay tied up next to me. It smelled of sage and other herbs I couldn’t identify. The woman walked around the table, whispering something in a language I didn’t know. Then she pulled out a long knife and sliced open my forearm. But I felt no pain at all. She laid a cloth soaked in sage over my face.

My whole body tingled. I could feel her start cutting off my hands and feet, but I felt no pain.

Everything turned bright.

And when I could move freely again, I saw the living room of Hannah’s house. No, I didn’t see the house, *I was the house.*

I sensed that Hannah was here too. And I sensed that Theo was with me. I couldn’t see him, but I felt him. He was a grown man by now, and we were both the house. We were reunited. Even if not the way we had hoped.

What I saw was Hannahs husband searching the whole house for his wife while the house was still full of water. He called the police. The officers came, searched the house, and left, once again without a single trace.

Hannahs husband cried for a long time. And when he came into the bathroom the next morning, Hannah sent him Morse signals through the faucet.
