In my drowsy state, I run my shoulder full into the corner of a wall on my way to the coffee pot.

“Shit,” I mumble as I stumble backwards. I make it to the coffee pot.

The steam from my battered travel mug drifts through the light of the single lamp alight on my kitchen counter. The everchanging patterns of the steam hold my gaze for a few moments while I try to wake up. 

*Time to go.* 

After hurriedly gathering my uniform and equipment, I get out the door.

It’s 5:00 in the morning and today is the deer archery season opener. Not 30 seconds after I sit down in my patrol truck, the speakers of my truck start blasting my ringtone. I nearly hit my head on the roof. 

That was loud enough to wake the neighbors I think to myself, despite not having any neighbors.

 I look at the screen on my dashboard. “Incoming call, Garver”

 I sigh and hit accept. 

“Mornin’ Garver,” I say half-heartedly. 

“Sup motherfucker!” 

I crack a smile as I reply, “Just tryin’ to get to Holloway before shooting light but I'm gettin’ bothered by an annoying morning person.”

“God, I hate when that happens,” Garver replies seriously. “No one's gonna do anything in your county ‘cause they heard Lyle’s the new sheriff in town.”

“Yeah, well, you won't know if anyone in your county is poachin’ because you'll be at the Bellstore flirtin’ with the seventy-year-old attendant.”

“It's a tough job but somebody’s gotta do it,” says Garver. I can practically hear him grinning through the phone. “This way when Brian calls me askin’ if I caught anyone I can say I didn't see a single hunter do anything illegal.”

I put the truck in drive and pull out of my driveway. There's always been a bump at the end of my driveway. Today is no different. It surprises me for the hundredth time and I spill my steaming coffee on my pants.

“Fuck me!” I say with as much contempt for myself as I can.

“Nah, I’d rather do that to Marge at the Bellstore. Thanks for the offer though,” I hear over my truck speakers. I bark a genuine laugh and hear a similar laugh returned to me.

Once the laughing subsides, I ask Garver what he wants.

“Can't a guy call his friend just to shoot the shit?” Garver chortles.

“Not at five-thirty in the morning!” 

“Yeah, I suppose that’s fair. I just knew you would be having a bad morning and would need some cheerin’ up from your good pal Garver.”

I snort, “How’d you know I’d be having a bad morning?”

“Because you always have a bad morning you miserable fuck.”

“Now that’s fair. Some people just have shit luck I guess.”

“Are you ever gonna stop saying that?”

“Well it's true! Especially for me.”

“Me and luck are like me and my ex-wife. We used to have a good thing goin’, but now she won't even look at me,” says Garver. “Plus the whole havin’ sex with another guy thing.”

As I try to dry my pants with a coat from my backseat, my smile returns.

“No, I'm just checkin’ on you. Everything been alright lately?”

“Yeah, I'm fine. Same old, same old.”

Garver and I both sit in silence for a few moments. 

“Can’t complain,” I say. 

Another pause.

“You doin’ alright?” I ask Garver to break the silence.

“Yeah, I'm doin’ good. Sadie got second in her competition this weekend so we’re pretty excited about that.”

“That's awesome! Tell her I said congrats from me.”

“I’ll do no such thing!” says Garver. “There will be no words of praise for second place in my household.” 

I laugh again and turn down a dirt road. The star-speckled, early-morning sky creates a dark gray canvas for the silhouettes of trees lining the road.

“How are things goin’ with Holloway?” Garver asks.

“‘Bout how you'd expect. Political bullshit and every higher up wantin’ the credit for making the property a thing.”

“I’d expect nothing less from our trusted, fearless leaders.”

“Right?” I say. 

“I still wonder about what happened to old man Holloway.” 

“Yeah, real shame.”

“Have you been out there yet?”

“Only once. They just finished the paperwork.”

“Good huntin’?”

“Supposedly. It's apparently barely been touched for years. I think it'll be good to have the state managing it.”

“Word is poachers have been runnin’ rampant while it's been in limbo for the last couple years.”

“Heard the same. Haven't been able to get a good look at it until today.”

“I’ll have to go out there with you sometime. Dude, I really gotta go, just pulled up to the Bellstore.”

“You called me!”

“Semantics”

“Tell Marge I said hi.”

Garver hangs up and I'm left in silence. A sign is visible up the road a little ways:

*Holloway Wildlife Area*

*Public hunting, trapping and fishing are allowed on this area*

*Vehicles permitted only on designated roads. No ATVs.*

*All hunting, trapping and fishing regulations must be followed* 

“Well, here we are,” I say to no one as I roll down my windows.

Large hickories and oaks line each side of the old dirt road. Branches reaching across like they're trying to touch. The hills frame the Appalachian night sky. Clouds have rolled in and covered the stars making the trees blend in with their dark canvas. All is quiet.

I flip on my high beams.

Two glowing eyes appear far down the road, just out of reach of the headlights. 

My binoculars come up. 

I look more closely. 

They stare back. 

I wait for it to run. 

Another moment passes.

I hear it blow air through its nose and the eyes disappear. Brush is knocked aside as it runs in the opposite direction. The darkness behind the first line of trees swallows both sight and sound of the fleeing animal. The quiet resumes.

“You're in for a stressful day,” I say to the deer.

My foot comes off the brake and I slow roll down the road. 

The starless night sky slowly turns to a motley of greys. The shift to gloomy morning is so subtle that I don't realize the sun has risen until I hear a wood thrush calling in the distance. The birds have started their morning chorus. My eyes drift in the direction of the bird song and I notice a man standing a little ways off the road in camo. He's in a clearing in the woods. The man has a compound bow on his back and is looking at the ground. No, not the ground, a deer carcass. 

The truck rocks as the passenger side tires dip into the side ditch. 

“Dispatch, 1003 call southeast”

“*G’head 1003”*

“Can you start a hunting incident at my location, please. The closest intersection is Burr Oak Road and Wildlife Road 1.”

*“Copy”*

The deer lies motionless in the grass through my binoculars. The hunter has seen me now and raises a hand in greeting. I step out of the truck and begin walking through the tall, dew-covered grass. My pants begin to soak through and stick to my leg. The grass is trampled down in a path from the road to the deer and I decide to follow this path instead.  The clearing is strangely bright in comparison to the darkness of the surrounding woods. 

“How we doin?” I say as I approach the scene. A bloody stump of a neck is visible where the deer’s head should be. 

“Not too bad. Got out here later than I planned and found this on my way to my spot.”

The blood is dark on the neck. Dried blood clots stick to the dark, shredded meat. The flesh looks like it was hacked apart with a saw rather than a knife. The vertebrae isn't cut, however. It sticks out from the meat slightly and the entire bone is intact. 

*Twisted the head off then. This ain't their first time.* 

A glance at the hunter’s hands and boots shows me no sign of blood. 

“Seems like it was killed a day or two ago,” I say in response.

“Thought the same. You been havin’ issues with folks poachin’ since the state bought this place?” 

“Yeah, from the looks of it. They barely got this place in the state’s name in time for the opener. I'm as new here as you.” 

There's a circular hole in the side of the deer right below its front shoulder. Entry wound.

“You the new game warden for this county?”

“Sure am. I'm Lyle Ward.”

“Huh. Like Warden.”

“Must mean I'm destined for greatness or somethin.” 

The hunter snorts. The nitrile glove makes a smacking noise as it envelops my left hand. I press two fingers into the flesh of the neck stump. The feel is reminiscent of sourdough bread. Crusty on the outside but soft underneath. I flip the deer over and see the exit wound on the other side. Much bigger than the entry wound and not as clean. The sky begins to drizzle on me from above. 

“Of course,” I mumble to myself. 

“Is it okay if I keep headin’ to my spot?”

“You mind if I take a peek at your license and tag quick? That way I'm not bothering you later?”

“Not at all.”

I confirm the information on his license and tag and send him on his way.

“Good luck today.”

“Thanks, same to you.”

The sound of his steps recedes as I stare at the carcass. I retrace my steps back to the road and look in the dirt where the trampled path begins. The glint of brass catches my eye. A shell casing. 

My eyes drift back to the woods. 

The morning is still, no wind moves the trees. 

The wood thrush has stopped its calling.

 The trees hold my gaze a moment or two longer before pulling my eyes back to the casing. I pick it up, bag it, and slap an evidence tag on it for good measure. 

I make my way back to the carcass. The shoulders of my shirt are now cold and wet against my skin. I put a second glove on my right hand, grab the deer's back legs, and start dragging.

—

The drizzle hasn’t stopped as I pull off the dirt road and reach for my lunch. An apple and some beef jerky. I opened the bag of beef jerky. Mold.

 “Guess just the apple then.” I say to no one… again.

 I finish the apple and wash it down with some more coffee. The wind has picked up as the morning has gone on and leaves are rustling in the trees. Fallen leaves tumble across the road in a desperate scramble to reach the other side.

My eyes close for a few seconds. I hear the sound of an engine in the distance.

 *That's an ATV*. 

My eyes snap open and I put my truck in drive. I creep further down the old road. Once I crest the next rise, I spot a red four-wheeler at the bottom of the hill. Through my binoculars, two men sit on the ATV, a deer being dragged behind it. I watch as they come out of the woods and stop in the road. Both men hop off and walk back to the deer. A doe from the looks of it. My foot comes off the brake.

As I descend the hill, the two men hear the dirt and gravel beneath my tires and look up. They stare at me as I get closer. 

“Dispatch, 1003 call southeast”

“*G’head 1003”*

“Can you start a hunting incident at my location, no close intersection, I'll be on Wildlife Road 2 in the middle of Holloway Wildlife Area.”

*“Copy 1003, we're unable to track your location at this time.”*

*Of course*

“How we doin’?” I say as I exit the vehicle. 

“Alright”

“Looks like you had some luck.”

“Yeah”

Both men spit dark globs to either side like they planned it. The man on the right is a skinny rail of a man with squirrely eyes. The one on the left is much shorter with a bit of a gut. His hands won't stop moving. 

“Who shot it?” I ask, noticing the blood on Squirrely Eyes’s hands. 

Silence.

I sigh.

He pulls the brim of his hat down lower.

His left eye won’t stop twitching. 

Twitchy Hands draws my eye. 

He’s looking everywhere but at me. 

“Listen, if you don't have a hunting license or somethin’, there's people doin’ worse things. If you work with me I can work with you.”

Again no answer.

The rifle on the back of the ATV is a little too close for my liking. 

The leaves stop rustling. 

I can't stop looking at that twitching eye.

His hands are clenched, one tucked behind his hip.

 He keeps staring at me. 

The nervous one is turned to the side looking into the trees. He still won't look at me.

My hands come together at my waist. 

My heart beats loud in my chest. 

I consider how long it would take me to get behind the engine block of my truck.

Several dragging moments pass as we stare at one another.

“It weren’t me!” says Twitchy Hands.

“Son of a bitch Jerry! Couldn't you just keep yer mouth shut for once.”

“Well I ain't tryna get into no more trouble Coonshit!”

Coonshit, apparently, turns and looks at me.

“Yeah, I shot it.” 

He spits a dark glob again. This time in Jerry’s direction. Both men give me their licenses. 

—

“Coonshit?” I ask while filling out a ticket. “Why do they call you Coonshit?”

Coonshit pulls up his sleeve to reveal a tattoo of a raccoon on his upper arm. The raccoon is shitting.

“Been my name since I got this when I was 17.”

“Mmm,” I say.

“You know you're not allowed to have ATVs on the area either?” 

“No, how would I know that?”

“There's a sign at the front of the… nevermind. I'll cut you a break on it since this is a new wildlife area but your gun and that deer’s coming with me.”

Jerry tries to help me load the deer in the back of my truck but ends up only making it more difficult as he can't get his side up above the tailgate. He's thanking me over and over for not arresting him. Coonshit just watches. 

Coonshit drives the four-wheeler up the hill, Jerry behind him with his arms wrapped around his waist. I shake my head. 

As the ATV drives off into the distance, I lean against the front of my truck. I take a deep breath. Several chickadees are calling to each other around me. The sun is finally peeking out from behind the clouds. I listen to them for a few minutes.

 “Would I rather die from black lung or from gettin’ shot by a guy named Coonshit.”

I shake my head again. Working in the mine wasn’t a fun job but it was predictable. Another wood thrush has joined the chorus of singing. I take another deep breath. 

At least I get paid to listen to birds now. I take my weight off the bumper of my truck. The sun poking out between the clouds accentuates the darkness of the inner woods. Like a gaping maw, it drains the little light filtering through the trees. The darkness holds my gaze.

A crow gives a warning call. 

The chickadees go quiet.

The thrush cuts his call short. 

I pause and look in the direction they were singing. 

My eyes search for movement. 

I see nothing.

I pause my breathing and listen.

I hear nothing.

I feel something.

“Must be a coyote,” I mumble.

The sun goes back behind the clouds. 

My eyes stare at the same patch of darkness. A jolt of unease runs from the top of my head to my toes. A set of antlers poke up above the shrubs. I watch as the buck takes a step out of the brush.

It looks directly at me. 

It takes me in. 

It seems to consider me.

Long moments pass as we stare at one another. 

The hair raises on the back of my neck. 

It doesn't run.

I wait.

The deer flicks its tail, looks away from me, and takes a step into the road.

 I expect it to bolt.

Its careful steps take it into the treeline on the other side.

It doesn't run.

*That's weird.* 

It looks back at me once more. 

The woods are quiet. Only the sound of rustling leaves infiltrates the silence. 

 I notice I've stopped breathing. 

*Why did the birds go quiet for a deer?*

I can't take my eyes away.

My truck radio wakes me from my reverie. 

*“1003 from dispatch on call southeast.”*

I snap my head towards my truck and feel heat course across my body. I look back at the deer. It's gone. I stand in silence staring at the void where it had once been.

The thrush starts calling again.  I shake my head and put one foot in front of the other.

*The Holloways must've been feeding these deer like they were pets*.

 “On to the next call,” I say to the woods.
