I was walking my dog last Thursday. It was pretty late, 11pm or so. I like to do that sometimes. My dog’s name is Scooby. I think it’s funny, no one else I know thinks it is though. Scooby and I left the house and went around our usual route, just a few blocks around where I live in the suburbs. 

It’s always nice and serene to be out late, and I think Scooby likes it too. It’s a good little ritual I’ve developed. The walk started out very ordinary. I was heading along my street and ran into my neighbour, Jamie. He’s always a great guy. No one really has any issues with him. He’s just a friendly bloke all the time. 

We got closer to each other. He kindly raised his hand at me. 

“Nice time for a stroll,” he said warmly. 

“You know it J. What are you doing out?”

“Had an event down at the RSL, stayed a bit longer than I told Lauren, walking back home. Lovely warm night isn’t it?”

Jamie is a pretty average looking guy, middle aged, in his late forties I’m pretty sure. The only peculiarity Jamie has is a quite visible red birthmark on his left cheek. It resembles a horse. He’s often self deprecating about it. It’s not really a big deal though 

“Ah. Going crazy Jamie. Yeah, beautiful night,” I responded to him. 

He raised his hand again. 

“Gotta get back soon or I’ll be in the dog house. I probably already am. Catch you Rory!” He said. 

I gave him a little nod, laughing, and said, “Don’t want to hold you up J, see you around.”

And with that we parted ways. Love that guy, truly. He’s minded Scooby while I’ve been away a bunch of times. I made my way down the street and set off around the neighbourhood. I passed the park nearby, the shops, the petrol station which was still open. Eventually, I came to the overpass which I walk by all the time. We stopped underneath it, out on the footpath. Scooby shuffled onto the grass to do his business. 

I was there, waiting near the overpass, when I saw it. I thought it was a mirage or something at first. It was a blood red shape in the sky. I couldn’t quite make it out. I focused on it more. It was a cloud, a deep red cloud. The sky was bright and starry so you could clearly make out it’s colour. It hung very low in the sky, probably being the lowest cloud I could see. 

Scooby shook himself off. The cloud started to drift slowly.

I moved back onto the footpath. I turned and looked up. It was a very interesting thing to me. I saw the cloud drifting in my direction, gliding very steadily. It looked alien, out of place in the rest of the sky. I thought it was pretty cool. As I got to the middle of the overpass, it came over the top of me. 

When it reached me, I stopped. I couldn’t help but look up directly at it. I was very curious, you know. Almost in an instant, it started to rain down on me. The rain hit me and it hurt. It was as if it was acidic. I looked around, it didn’t seem like it was raining anywhere else in the area. I winced from the stinging of the water and almost let go of Scooby.

I instinctively closed my eyes as the rain hit me, it was surprisingly painful and it caught me off guard. I rushed forward to get out of the rain. When I opened my eyes again however, well… I…

I struggle to describe it. I struggle to even recollect it. Everything had changed. The whole neighbourhood had morphed. I was frozen. It was beyond anything I could… anything I could imagine. The whole place was totally destroyed. It was a wreckage. It was like a post apocalyptic scene. The houses were tossed up and broken into pieces. Strange, giant black trees burst out of the earth and tangled themselves in the shattered houses, reaching up unnaturally high in the sky. Above me, the sky had turned a bright red, thin black clouds periodically streaked across it. 

It was hot, uncomfortably hot. Scooby barked, and kept barking, as if there was a threat in front of us. But nothing was really there. The threat was everywhere, everywhere I looked. I was completely overwhelmed. I took a couple of steps forward. 

I could recognise the street, Bell Street. It was parallel to my own. I was familiar with some of the houses, but everything was twisted up in a Bosch-like way. Most of the houses were destroyed and scattered on the ground, like there had been some bomb, or natural disaster, or I don’t know what. 

I looked ahead of me, and saw a car, a Toyota Yarris, walking on the broken road in front of me. Yes, walking. The body of the car, which seemed like it had been torn in half, was attached to two towering black legs. As the car stomped along in front of me, inky black goo dripped from the legs onto the cracked road. 

Scooby went silent at this. I turned away, in an almost catatonic state. My shock wore off, and I began to grow incredibly anxious. 

I picked up the pace near one of the houses. I looked up at the sky again, that red cloud had disappeared. I saw something crawling towards me in the distance. It was some sort of animal. No, it wasn’t an animal. 

When I reached it, I looked down and couldn’t do anything except let my mouth quietly hang open in disbelief. It was a man, kind of. It was some fleshy mass resembling a human. It was a twisted contorted torso, with a head and arms, dragging itself along the dry grass. The lower half of it’s body was taken off, or never existed. It had no eyes, two nostrils stuck on its face instead of a nose, and it’s mouth was agape with decaying teeth placed in there in nonsensical, jagged angles.

Scooby started barking again. I quietened him down this time. 

The thing started making a kind of faint, pathetic moan. I stood in front of it. It suddenly looked up at me. I was completely still. It let out another moan, louder.

As I kept staring down, I spotted something which immediately chilled me. It had a red birthmark on it’s left cheek. It was in the shape of a horse. I leaned forward towards the thing and felt myself speaking. 

“Jamie?” I said, barely keeping it together. 

It kept looking up at me. It reached out at me with a limping arm, grasping for I don’t know what. It let out a feeble moan again, and it didn’t stop. Soon, it’s random sounds coalesced into rough words. 

“Rooo… Roor… Rooorrryyy” It cried out weakly, still clawing at me. 

I stood dumbfounded. It was him. It was Jamie. 

“Jamie? What, what’s going…”

It’s spidery hand grazed my leg, and it started to grab at me desperately, like it needed help. 

“Rooorrryyyy,” it eked out in another loud, pathetic wail. 

I instinctively stepped back, pulled my leg away from the bloody thing, took Scooby and started to leg it out of there. 

I sprinted now, as much as I could. I was beside myself. I had had enough. I ran out onto the broken road, darted my head around frantically.

Another walking car…  a BMW… strode by me. My sense of dread reached an unbearable peak. 

“Help! Help! My God! Help me!” I cried out, hurting my throat. 

I caught sight of another red cloud, floating over part of the road up ahead, low in the sky. Scooby was barking like crazy. I ran towards it, not knowing what else to do. 

I reached it, yelled up at it like a maniac. 

“Help! Help! What do you want?! Please! Somebody!” 

The cloud continued to creep slowly In the sky. I yelled and yelled, hoarse, at the cloud, at everything, at nothing. I was about to break into tears, to tell you the truth, when the red cloud stopped above me. 

I craned my neck up at it, my face in absolute anguish. It started to rain. It stung me again. I recoiled, crouched to the ground. My eyes couldn’t help but shut tight. 

Very slowly, I opened them. I looked around. It was raining lightly around me. Scooby barked slightly and tottered over to me. He rubbed himself on my back. The rain felt so soothing. I looked up, the red cloud was nowhere to be seen. I immediately started to sob with relief. I was back at the overpass.
