Several weeks ago, in response to a string of package thefts in my neighborhood, I (40F) purchased and installed my first doorbell camera. Coincidentally, the thief was apprehended before I had even finished setting up the app on my phone, but I enjoyed the added security regardless. My ex-husband and I divorced two months ago, and while the separation was amicable, it still felt strange and sometimes eerie to be alone in the house at night. Even on weekdays, when my fourteen-year-old stayed with me instead of with his dad, I often went to sleep at night with a terrible knot of anxiety in my gut. I often felt, irrationally, like there was another person in the house with my son and I, and I could not tell if these sentiments stemmed from my own nostalgia, or if they were an omen of things to come.

For the first week that I had the camera, I constantly woke up in the middle of the night to movement alerts on my phone. We live in a rural area, and the other side of our residential street boasts a thick patch of woodlands. We get a lot of critters running across our yard as a result. After a few days of waking up in a cold sweat to an alert, only to watch a playback of a skunk waddling down my driveway, I turned down my phone's ringer and stopped responding to the notifications with such urgency. 

One Friday, I got up at 5:30 as usual, went through my morning routine, and then rounded out the quiet hours before my son awoke by reviewing the previous night's camera footage. There was already a notification waiting for me when I turned on my phone. 

"Review activity from 03:46 AM?" 

I clicked into the app and navigated to the flagged recording, hoping to catch a glimpse of the skunks. I hit play and spent a few seconds watching the still, silent street before there came movement from the corner of the frame. 

Someone came walking down the middle of the road. They had a strange gait, almost a limp, and could not seem to move in a straight line—veering to one side, then overcorrecting and drifting to the other. Despite this, they moved with surprising speed, walking as quickly as an average adult might jog. The result was so bizarre that I had to stop the playback to confirm it wasn't sped-up. 

For some reason, just as it seemed that the figure was about to pass by my property without incident, their head snapped toward my house. Their body followed a moment later, and they changed direction—lumbering with that unnatural rhythm in the direction of my house. As they got closer to my porch, I could make out more details in the grainy footage. The person was tall, well over six feet; they had dark hair which cascaded all the way past their waist; and they were clad in a long, thick overcoat. Despite the bulky silhouette their clothes gave them, I could see, once they got close enough, a pair of thin wrists poking out of the ends of their sleeves. Their ankles too, which were not quite covered by the hems of their pants, were so thin that I briefly wondered if the person were on stilts, and if that could be why they were moving so strangely. 

The person cut across my lawn, making a beeline for the front of my house. They staggered up the steps, and once they reached my porch, I was able to make out their face for the first time. I believe that the person was a woman, but I'm still not quite certain, even after countless rewatches. She seemed to have some kind of medical condition—her skin looked puffy and tight, as though swollen from some kind of allergic reaction. Her dark eyes were extremely deep-set, buried in the thick flesh that ringed her eyesockets. 

After an unsuccessful attempt to turn my doorknob, the woman dropped into a crouch and lifted the welcome mat, then quickly dropped it. She then spent a full minute poking around my porch—looking under flower pots and decorations, and even peering into my mailslot. Then, she turned around and descended the steps of my front porch. Instead of fleeing into the night, however, she got up close to the exterior wall of my home and started to round the perimeter of the building. Once she disappeared from view, the highlighted portion of the recording ended. No other motion activity was flagged for the remainder of the night. 

My backyard is gated off on both sides, but there's a small gap between the bottom of the gate and the ground. With enough motivation, an adult could potentially wedge themselves through. The room just on the other side of that gate belongs to my son, Max. 

My son, who often likes to leave his window open at night.

The second the video ended, I raced to Max's bedroom, throwing the door open and turning on his light. I almost burst into tears of relief when I saw him sound asleep; a part of me truly believed I would open that door to an empty bed or a dead body. His window was, indeed, wide open. I crossed his room and looked out into the backyard, now visible in the light of dawn, and saw nothing amiss. Regardless, I slammed the window shut, which earned me a "What the Hell, Mom?" as Max woke up. I explained the situation to him as best I could as I checked around his room—in his closet, under his bed, behind his bookcase—and then called the police as I swept the rest of the house. 

They arrived 20 minutes later, but there was little they could do aside from take my statement and, at my request, search my backyard for me (there was no way I was checking the garage or the garden shed by myself). I don't know if the woman ever even entered my yard, but if she did, she didn't leave any trail to follow. Aside from the doorcam video that I emailed to the police, there was no evidence that anyone had paid my house a visit.

Max and some of his friends from the neighborhood usually ride their bikes to school along a trail that runs parallel to the woods. On that morning, however, after calling his friend's parents to tell them what had happened, I drove him to school. Some of the mothers I spoke to were as concerned as I was, though many brushed off my story. 

*Someone came up to your door, couldn't get in, and then left? That's it? No attempt at forced entry? No broken windows? Why are you even calling me about this?*

Some of the conversations I had honestly made me question myself. It was true that nothing had "happened", exactly—I had seen something spooky, sure, but the unwelcomed visitor hadn't tried to force their way in. 

I was just starting to think I might have blown the incident out of proportion until last Tuesday, which was just about two weeks after the woman came by my stoop. By that point, I had let Max bike to and from school again. He rode in a group with a few other kids from the neighborhood, the school was pretty close, and they always came back before it got dark outside. Still, I found myself nervously waiting for Max to return home in the afternoons. At least I'm lucky enough to have a work-from-home job so I could confirm he was getting home when he said he was. 

That afternoon, I was sitting in my home office with the window open, when I heard a group of boys stopping in front of my house, bike brakes squeaking and leaves crunching under their tires. I let out a small sigh of relief and prepared to return my attention to my work before—

"Holy shit man, holy *shit*," laughed one of the boys. "That chick was crazy!" 

"Yeah, Max, she was looking at you like she was gonna fucking *eat* you!"

I heard a sharp *shh!*, followed by my son's voice chastising his friends, telling them to keep it down. The group got quiet after that, speaking in hushed voices that I could no longer easily make out, but I'd heard enough. I exited my house through the front door and made my way down the driveway to where the group had congregated, despite Max's labored groan. He shot his friends a look that seemed to say: *See? Look what you've done now*. 

"Everything alright, guys?" I asked the group of bikers. The older boys looked between each other uneasily, but the youngest boy in the group, a sixth grader from down the block, was quick to spill what had happened. 

"Oh it was crazy, ma'am! This lady chased us through the woods!"

"What??"

"Yeah! She really scared the cra—the crud outta us. She kept up with us for a really long time even though she was just walking, but we managed to get away. But once we started pulling ahead, we looked behind us and she was just standing at us and pointing all creepy-like. She was pointing right at Max!" 

"*Bullshit*." Max snapped. "She could've been pointing at any of us." 

"What did this woman look like?" I asked. 

"She had long brown hair and pale skin, and she was really tall and skinny, except in the face. Her face was like … It was bigger. It was—"

"Bloated?" Volunteered one of the others. 

"Yeah, bloated."

Once the others biked off down the road, Max and I walked back inside. I kept checking over my shoulder as we walked up the driveway, looking at the trees in the distance. Nervous that this woman might somehow figure out where we lived, I tried asking Max for more details. *Are you okay? Do you agree with the others' description? Did she say anything to you? Why did the others think she was looking at you specifically?* He practically stonewalled me the whole time, grunting noncommittal "I dunno"s after every question. Sighing, I zipped up one of the pouches on his backpack (he has a bad habit of leaving them a little open) and followed him into the house. 

I called the police station that evening to give them an update and urge them to find this woman, but again, I felt like I was the only one who had a sense of urgency about the situation. Everyone else seemed fine to wait, to sit back twiddling their thumbs until something really bad happened and it was too late to stop anything. Did you know that 54% of female homicide victims reported stalking to the police before they were killed by an intimate partner? Fat lot of good that did them. Sometimes, I think most people would prefer taking vengeance after a crime than stopping that crime from happening in the first place. 

Anyway, I've been a little overprotective of Max this past week, and to say he hasn't been thrilled with me would be an understatement. I've been driving him to and from both school and soccer practice, and I've been pretty relentless with my reminders about stranger danger. I think it was a relief for him on Friday night to be picked up by his father, and in some ways, it was a relief for me too; I knew he'd be safer one town over in case there really was some crazy person out in the woods chasing after kids. 

What I did not consider until later was how terrified *I* would be, holed up all alone in that house. I got a lot more "Motion Detected" alerts from my doorbell camera than usual, and each one made me flinch, but the camera was only catching squirrels and skunks. That didn't stop me from peering out the windows every chance I got, each time expecting to see that strange, eerie face staring back at me. 

On Saturday night, as I was doing my pre-bedtime paranoia-patrol around my house, I lifted the curtains on my living room window and looked out onto the street. Or, at least, I *tried* to look out onto the street. Strangely, it seemed completely pitch-black outside. Like I said, we live in a rural area, and cloudy nights can get very dark, but even so, I couldn't even see the road. I looked out of a back window and experienced the same thing; even with the porch lights on, it was difficult to see more than a few feet into my backyard. I thought that some kind of heavy fog had suddenly descended upon the neighborhood, but when I checked again a few minutes later, it was gone. I could easily see the woods across the street; I could clearly make out the way the brush rustled now and then as the wind—or perhaps the animals—swept through them. 

The recordings on the doorbell camera looked like someone had used a dimmer to incrementally turn off all the light in the world; the video showed my street normally, then the recording suddenly got darker and darker until it was solid black. A few minutes later, the night faded into view again. I still do not understand how to explain this phenomena, although I do have an inkling as to what caused it. 

Mark, my ex-husband, dropped Max off later than normal on Sunday night. I was watching reruns in the living room, anxiously checking the clock on the wall every few minutes, when I heard a car stop in front of the house. It was around 10:00 PM. Mark had probably taken Max out for dessert somewhere; fine by me, although I wish he had communicated the late drop-off to me earlier. 

I remained seated, waiting for Max to let himself inside. I heard footsteps shuffling up the porch steps, but I heard no jingle of keys. Instead, the sound of knocks filled the quiet house. When I looked through my peephole, I saw my son standing on the porch, weekend bags in hand. I opened the door for him, and he walked inside with a casual, "hey, Mom." I gave Mark a wave, which he returned before driving off down the street. Then, I shut and locked the front door, calling out after my son: 

"Why didn't you let yourself in?" 

"Oh. Well, Dad was worried about some of the things I told him—about the weird stuff in the neighborhood, you know. So he wanted to see that you were home." 

That made sense. I was glad that at least one other adult in town was taking the situation seriously. I asked Max if he wanted to eat ice cream and watch *The Walking Dead* with me, but as I suspected, his dad had already taken him out to eat. Feeling only a little deflated, I focused on the show while Max unpacked and prepped for school the next day. He said goodnight to me at 11 and I switched to a book so as to not make too much noise. 

I couldn't focus. I thought having my son at home with me would have dispelled some of my unease, but it seemed to have the opposite effect. I felt strangely nervous. 

After re-reading the same few pages over and over again for a little while, I pulled out my phone and texted Mark. I didn't really want to bother him, but since the sunken-eyed lady potentially impacted our son's safety, I wanted to get his read on the situation. 

*I heard Max told you about that creep. Thoughts? We have a doorbell camera now and I've been driving Max everywhere.*

I set my phone down. It took Mark over half an hour to respond, and every minute I spent waiting only made me more anxious. Why did I suddenly feel so tense? The response from my ex, which arrived just a little past midnight, read:

*Creep? What? Max didn't mention anything about that. Everything okay?*

Immediately, I understood. I sprang up from the couch, making my way to Max's room. As I walked I remembered my hand reaching out, my fingers closing around the backpack zipper and pulling it shut. 

When I opened the door to my son's bedroom, I saw him awake in his bed, watching YouTube videos on his phone. He tried to stash the device under his pillow, already making up some excuse, but I didn't care about that. I flicked on the light. 

"Max." I said. "Where's your house key?" 

"Oh, yeah …" He replied. "I realized I forgot it at Dad's house, but I know exactly where I left it. Maybe he can drive it over tomorrow after work, or maybe I could just wait for—"

"Max." 

For a moment, he seemed to think about maintaining the lie, but after a few seconds, he sighed heavily. He told me that his key had fallen out of his bag when he and his friends were chased through the woods. He hadn't had it for nearly a whole week, and since I had been chaperoning him everywhere, I had been none-the-wiser.

"Look, I'm sorry. I didn't think it would be a big deal. Even if that lady finds the key, she doesn't know where we live. It's not like she's gonna try every house in the neighborhood 'til she finds us."

I opened my mouth to respond, but before I could say anything, my eyes drifted to the window. There was something strange happening outside—a barely noticeable shift in the ambient light. The night was slowly growing darker. 

Panic surged through me. I told Max to grab his backpack and follow me. I shut down his protests as I grabbed my phone, keys, and wallet, and made my way to the garage. I thanked my lucky stars that our garage was connected to our house, and that Max and I would not have to go outside in order to get in the car. Before I drove away, I took a moment to look at my house from the road. Despite my leaving a few lights on inside, the interior was pitch-black. 

I drove us to a hotel in a neighboring town. A part of me was unsatisfied with the distance and wanted to keep fleeing forever, but the dark country roads didn't make me feel particularly safe either. Only when we settled into our hotel room did I start to feel less nervous, comforted by the densely populated building and the prospect of a door with an uncompromised lock. 

At two in the morning, as Max slept in the bed next to mine, I reluctantly tapped the *Motion Detected!* notification from my doorbell camera app. The recording started just a few moments after Max and I left the house. The first few seconds were similar to the video that sparked this whole ordeal in the first place: a calm, windless night, a moment of peace on an unassuming residential street. 

And then: *her*. 

This time, she came not from down the street, but from the woods. She staggered across the street, pale and thin as the bare birch trees behind her, moving more quickly than that horrible gait should have allowed. She was at the edge of my lawn in seconds, then on my stoop in a few seconds more. 

This time, she did not have to look for a key. She fiddled with the door handle for a moment, then paused. Her dark eyes settled on the camera, and I felt, despite the miles and the hours between us, that she was looking straight into my soul. Her lips stretched across her bloated face, expression morphing in a way that looked painful. She gave the camera a wide, closed-mouth grin. 

And then she let herself inside.

I do not know how she knew which house was mine. I do not know what she did after coming inside of my home. I do not know what would have happened if I had brushed off my concerns and gone to sleep that night. 

But I do know, with a certainty that I have rarely felt before in my life, that whatever came to my door that night was not a human being.
