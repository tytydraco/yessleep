I feel it is important to note upfront that many of the details of this story are going to be very vague. I know that can be annoying especially to the youth of the internet but I genuinely couldn’t give less of a fuck. Even if most of the malevolent party mentioned and involved are dead by now – part of the reason I’m opening up about this. I’ve finally made a good life for myself, one where I can finally look at fire without flinching. Besides, even if I told you the true names of those hedonistic bastards, you wouldn’t believe me. The therapist said I shouldn’t write anything that’ll make me relapse either, so forgive me if I leave some things out. Or don’t, I don’t care. Knowing the current social climate, by the end of my story, you will most likely condemn me as a coward at the least. But I suppose with all of that out of the way, let's begin. 

It was 1982 when I received the call from my father. He told me that an independent client of his was requesting my services and that I was to meet with them at the church that afternoon. Most job inquiries went like this. My father would get contacted, he would review the person's request, and then he would hand it off to me. This sort of system flowed best for my work as a professional mourner. Especially since it was my father who supported me in the profession and it was easier to run my services through the mouth of an established church. Pastor Tom was a hardworking, faithful, and loving man of God. He saw my talent for acting as something I could use to serve God, and when my pursuits began to fall through more often than not, he proposed the idea of becoming a professional mourner in association with the church. At the time I was very receptive to the idea, as my managerial job at Macy’s could only provide so much for my wife and daughter, especially while auditioning in my spare time. To boot, I had always been good at crying on command. 

I remember having to pull my pants up and quickly explain the situation to Charles who was lounging on the couch. Afternoon meant an hour away, and considering the precocious position we had just been in, I needed Charles out of the house. He politely obliged like he always did;

“Called off to the frontlines again. What would our church do without your services?” Charles said, straightening his tie into place atop his once unbuttoned shirt.

“Just about what you would do without my other services, dickhead,” I laughed. 

“You’re the dickhead for makin’ me kneel on this abysmal carpeting.” 

I pushed him on the shoulder playfully as we both left through the garage door. He stumbled, turning back to push me with a smile. Just like when we were kids, up in the woods together playing wizards. He always picked the pyromancer while I oscillated between controlling imaginary lightning or earth. I could never make up my mind back then. Even at that time, when we were adults, I couldn’t – climbing behind the wheel I couldn’t decide whether to blow off my dad or do as I was told. Especially looking over to Charles with that beautiful sparkle he always had in his eye. 

The drive to the church was cold without the warmth of my best friend, making me wish I could pause those tender moments between us and rewind the tape over and over. Even then I knew what was more important; making enough money to support Lillian and March. The whimsy and warmth had no place in practicality, while cold hard cash did. And boy did the client provide in that respect. A month-long contract to a familial funeral home up west, due to their influx of unpopular businessmen dying in their old age. Mostly from drug overdoses and the like, becoming a warning to all older individuals; be careful about what vices they keep. Or at the very least discreet until even after death. In any case, each funeral would earn me $500, which to modern wealth would be about $2,000. They estimated to have around two or three funerals a week where my services were necessary. Doing the basic math made it apparent to me that I would be a fool to turn down such a proposition. Even though the man they sent was admittedly shifty, plucking at his sleeve and tugging at his collar any chance he could get. 

My wife didn’t agree, saying that the expected late night shifts and proximity to the drug scourge going on in the neighborhood, would take quality time away from my daughter and myself.  She got awfully upset when I persisted, asking me if I knew what I would be doing to our family's reputation. Lillian was a sensitive woman and we would often get into arguments like this. Though, to be fair, I was also a similar creature. I just had differing priorities. Despite that, I still cared very deeply for March and acknowledged that Lillian was right. I had never been gone at that hour before as it was the only time we would gather together as a family during the week. 

“Do you have another big audition daddy?” the bright eyed six year old with freshly missing baby teeth said, a smile plastered across her face as she did her math homework with an orange crayon. I chuckled softly. 

“No honey, this is for my other job. The one where I get to help sad folks at those parties where everyone has to dress in black, remember?” I replied softly while cleaning the last dish.

“But that’s no fun, they don’t let me watch you dance on TV. Will you ever get to be a chimney sweep with Rick Van D?”

Lillian laughed along with me as she made her way into the kitchen, hair in a fresh towel and silk robe wrapped around her small, stout frame. 

“Sweetheart, his name is Dick,” her mother said, nudging me on the shoulder playfully. I smiled at her warmly and she did so in kind, almost as if the argument prior was nothing more than a passing conversation. And it was, as we were both rational, knowing we would compromise somehow. That’s why I knew deep down that she knew what I was. What Charles and I really were. She was intelligent to know that it was of no consequence, as we held our own type of love for each other. But perhaps that was just me being selfish, imagining what was a perfect agreement between the two of us, when in our community such a thing couldn’t even be uttered in hypotheticals. Not even to Lillian. 

“Mary Poppins calls him Bert,” March huffed with a pout. 

“Yes, very good March,” I affirmed, wrapping my arm around her in a comforting hug, “Now let’s see that math homework.” 

Those were the tender moments that I cling onto when I’m low. The kind I wish I hadn’t taken for granted when they were happening. The ones March still thanks me for, despite what followed. 

The next morning I called up my father to tell him I would take up the offer. He was stern, like he always was, acknowledging his pride with a short hum and the number of the owner for my reference. The one piece of advice he gave me before he hung up was the fact that all of the funerals would be held in different private locations separate from the mortuary center. I had done private wakes in the past so this was not a surprise. I supposed they would tell me the location of the cemetery once I arrived at the wake location as prior homes had done in the past. Instead of a particular identity instruction, I was given the odd piece of direction. Each time I was to be a mourner, I would only get express knowledge of this, and where the wake was, 3 hours before the event along with a password. My first reaction was apprehension, but, as it was with many things, I had a knack for rationalizing the strange. The funeral home must be renowned for their discretion surrounding mourners, and the password was just another level of security. I was nothing if not acknowledging how touchy my profession was. 

It was one in the afternoon when I received my first call. I had to run from the mimed tea party I was having with March to the landline where my mood immediately switched to somber. The speaker had a low raspy voice, but a polite countenance. They gave me the address to the letter, and the password was ‘Gotze’, pronounced in an almost pristine German. I tried to repeat it back to the person but the dial tones tinnitus rang through the receiver before I could utter an attempted syllable. I wrote down the password quickly and shoved it into my pocket. March wasn’t pleased at the interruption and even less pleased at my need to prepare for the evening. 

Before we begin with the true horror, I need you all to truly acknowledge something. I am not a good person. I will never claim to be a good person. I lie, I cheat, and I run. I am not a good person, but I am a very good coward. A bystander. My therapist doesn’t like me using those terms, she would prefer that I acknowledge my victimhood. But I think deep down she knows that calling myself a victim would be spitting in the face of those who truly suffered. Those who didn’t get out alive. I simply did as I was told, and I cried. The worst part of that act is that I wasn’t doing it for them, the dead, but I was crying in pure unadulterated fear. Every time. 

The first time is terribly fuzzy in my memory. Like a broadcast under the blanket of TV static. I don’t remember the drive there or the moments before I left the house. I only remember that I must have cooked March and Lillian dinner before departing, as it was something that significantly calmed me. So when I rolled through the winding drive in and past the open gates, a steely resolve had settled over me. The nerves of a new client always appeared but I routinely tried to cope with them. After all, you never knew when a casting director would be in attendance. Stage fright was a subtle killer. 

What wasn’t obscured by time in my mind, was the appearance of the wake venue. Or, I suppose, the funeral venue, as I would come to find that they were one in the same. It was a strikingly large estate, one I thought could have easily made its home in the Scottish countryside, much less a wooded marsh plot in Florida. It was surrounded by a vast, pristinely cut, grass lawn with willow and palm trees skirting the side like a large crowd of watchers. The estate itself was a colonial style mansion that wrapped around in a ‘C’ format with two wings flanking the vast front. Adjacent to its visage was a typical fountain, except it didn’t have any water running through it despite being completely spotless. The estate was painted a stark white, with only flashes of color coming from the copper drainage and the large dark door. The paint was tacky, I could almost feel the rubbery texture on my palm just by looking at it. Somehow the gravel driveway was empty, despite all the windows at the front of the house being filled with a glowing orange life. When I heard the nature of the voices drifting out of those windows through the humid air, I knew something was wrong. My heart sank to my stomach. 

There were so many voices, and all of them sounded happy. Now I know that may not sound unusual to those who have only been to two or three funerals in their lifetime, but if you work at them in any capacity you know how one sounds. It’s a silence set above a murmuring fog. Sobs, sniffles. Even at celebrations of life, though those were relatively rare in 80’s America, the happiness was less boisterous and more respectful. This sounded like a party, one of the parties Charles talked about from his early days in University. I could even make out the drunken slurring. 

I didn’t have much time to think about the unease I felt before a lanky, balding man in a butler's uniform stepped out of the massive mahogany doors. I swallowed hard and stepped out of my car, closing the door behind me with an odd sense of finality. The man stepped toward me and appeared at my side in due course. 

“Hello sir,” the butler said, “May I ask for the password?”

I stammered, a bit taken aback by the directness.

“Gotze,” I slowly garbled out. I was never good at accents or pronunciation. 

The butler winced.

“And your blindfold?” he said, peeking around and examining me like I was a dog hiding a ball. 

“Blindfold?”

“Ah I see, your boss did not tell you,” The butler said, his eyes becoming cold and steely, “No matter. I came prepared.”

Without a moment's hesitation, the butler reached into his pocket and grabbed something. I was in the process of retorting in confusion but he scowled at me and lifted the hand that had once been in his pocket in front of his face with an open palm. He made sure to step a few paces closer to me before blowing whatever he had in his hand in my face. In quick succession I began to lose consciousness, feeling my head fall hard on the gravel. I have come to know that what was in the butlers hand was what they call Devils Breath. 

I woke up with a splitting headache and in a room completely unfamiliar to me. In clothes unfamiliar to me. Soft linens clasped together with golden shackles on my wrists and ankles. For lack of a better term, I was uncomfortably exposed. I panicked immediately but the shackles binding me only allowed me to shuffle back on the bed I was on so far. It was only then that I realized a man was sitting on the end of the bed staring at me. He was smiling, but there was no happiness behind it. I screamed, loud. Loud enough that I hoped anyone around would hear me. 

“Help! Fuck-” My body crashed to the floor as I frantically made my way to a door I spotted on the left wall. “Somebody help me!” 

“I would refrain from that if I were you,” The stranger said, circling around the bed to me like a shark, “My companions become all the more hungry when they sense fear.”

“What the hell?”

I immediately began to shuffle toward the wall trying my best to get away from this malevolent force before me. Again, everything about that night is so obscured that I can’t even remember the man’s face. But I know that I see him in the faces of so many opportunistic smiles on the news. 

“Don’t worry though, you won’t be the meal tonight. You will simply do as you were hired. Honestly, this whole mess would have been a lot easier if your employer simply gave you all the relevant instructions. Lord knows why he blindsided you on that specific detail.” 

He laughed at his own joke with veneered teeth lashing like a wolf. 

“Absolutely not. I am not staying in the house of a psychopath who just kidnapped me!” 

“Oh but you will! After all, you’re still getting paid. And money is oh so important to help support little March and Miss Lillian. Isn’t it?”

My blood ran cold. He smiled even wider as I failed to answer, failed to even speak. Lillian I could understand my father telling employers about but, March? How could this man I had never seen nor even heard of know the name of my daughter? 

“No? Well perhaps a few bits of privacy is more paramount then.” 

He knelt in front of me at that moment, reaching into his suit jacket pocket and pulling out a stack of Polaroid's. Charles at my front door. Charles and I kissing. The two of us in bed. 

“How?” is all I managed to stammer. 

“Oh we have our ways. Not just of securing your little acts of sodomy, but of your daughter and wife as well. Would you like me to show you those pictures too? The lighting in one of them beautifully accentuated the wallpaper in your daughter's bedroom. Lillian does always have a knack for picking out the best florals isn’t that right?”

The breath had been stolen out of my lungs in a matter of minutes. 

“Who the fuck are you?”

“Someone you would be wise to obey,” the man said whilst standing. He appeared to finally put on a serious tone, as if the literal blackmail waved in my face was just an effortless ace up his sleeve. “You will mourn at the ceremony we hold tonight, and all of the ceremonies after. You will do so without causing a scene or trying to run, though with those darling shackles you won’t have much room to do the latter. You will then proceed to come back for your entire contract. And throughout your entire contract and into the rest of your miserable life, you will never utter a word about anything you see here. If you don’t do even one of these things, there will be dire consequences. Warnings. Perhaps those beautiful Polaroid's will find a good home on your wife's desk. Or a journalist’s. My my, imagine the headlines. ‘Preachers Son; a Blasphemous Sodomite’”

“Stop,” I murmured desperately, “I get it. I understand.” 

The man appeared satisfied enough, with a toothy grin plastered along his cheeks once more.

“Ah, good. I knew you were a reasonable man.” 

The next thing I remember was standing in a grand room. It was dim and dark, blurry from the lingering Devils Breath in my nostrils. There were so many people. Too many people. I could hardly make out what all of them were doing, though I would come to know of their revelry in the future. Everyone was choking on each other's air and absorbing each other's heat. The crowd all huddled around a large statue that I stood beneath. It was that of a bullheaded man. I came to remember him as the Minotaur, though I’m sure that’s not what they were worshiping. The man from before stood beside me, shaking the hands of the crowd as they passed. I could only make out so much of what they were talking about through the din, but I could parse together conversations concerning donations, funding, governmental relations, scientific studies, oil, gas, fossil fuels, burning. I stood there in a numb state of shock, even as the man beside me rang a small hand-held bell to call the room to attention. 

“Good evening esteemed guests. Friends. Family,” he said in a booming voice much louder than what his mouth implied, “Tonight signifies the beginning of the Final Rite. A momentous occasion where we all should secure what we have sought for years. The spirituality of the bright. The holiness of the incomprehensible. Over these next three weeks, we shall spill the blood that grants us the warmth of our god. Beginning tonight with our first.”

He rang the bell again and the crowd parted to make way for two men in military grade uniform dragging a woman across the floor. Her hair was long and matted and her right eye was blackened. She had shackles identical to mine, but hers were much more faded and bloody. The ankles around them, deeply wounded and festering with infection. She kicked and screamed through the crowd, spitting curses and profanities. Once she reached the front of the statue the men clamped her to a chain on the floor by her shackles and exited. Still, she thrashed, when her eyes met mine I saw a glimmer of hope.

“Please, please, you have to help me. I have a baby- a newborn baby. One of these fuckers has her and God knows what they’ll do to her- Please!” 

“Behold!! The vermin of the world! The lesser. The inferior. She will die and her dirty blood will be burned clean by our god to create a holy fire. One so bright, one so grand, it shall engulf us all in everlasting light!” the man beside me declared. “Let her screams invigorate you!”

The crowd began to chant in a language I still can’t attribute to any widely used dialect. The stranger beside me did so as well, in his booming voice. He continued loudly, even as he took an axe from one of the militiamen. It was sizable, old in its make and model. He trudged down the stairs, holding the axe as if he had done so one hundred times before. The woman began to cry and panic even more, trying to kick and squirm out of her bonds in the dim red light. I could hear one of her bones break in the struggle, even above the crowd of voices. 

Then I watched as the stranger lifted the double headed axe above his head and cleaved the woman down the middle. 

Her guts and viscera cut in an uncanny precision with no struggle. The blade fell through her like butter. Both pieces of her body fell to the ground in a thud while the crowd grew silent in anticipation. The woman's blood pooled, draining into a hole at the base of the statue I hadn’t noticed until now. I watched it flow in a stunned, shocked, and terrified silence. 

“Cry,” I heard the stranger whisper beside me in frustration. 

The tears came easily. Vomiting came even easier which seemed to delight them even more. My sobs and shaking screams echoed through the night for what seemed like hours. Even after the stranger left. Even as the reveling continued.  Somehow, no matter how much pain, I could not scream loud enough.
