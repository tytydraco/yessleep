When the world gets too dark, I turn to places I find comfort. But what happens when the places we call home become empty shells of what they used to be?

I had just gotten back home from work; I felt the tension in my neck ease away as I jolted the back of my head, letting out a nice pop from the base of my nape. My leather handbag and keys toppled onto the kitchen counter as I kicked off my heels, clanking onto the cold wooden floor of my apartment, before proceeding down the hall with nothing on my feet but the mesh of my stockings padding the ground with each step.

It should’ve been a miserable day, and in theory it was; I spilled my coffee on the break room sink, my lunch was cut fifteen minutes short, I had to print a hundred pages of documents, then reprint them again because it wasn’t colored, and got my pay threatened for wasting ink, said printer’s ink, but nothing got me down today.

Throughout my shift, I hadn’t realized I’d been smiling—I was happy—no, I was *excited*.

Because for the first time in a long while, I’ve been feeling a thrill I hadn’t thought was still possible to feel; the kind of excitement from Fourth Grade, when I would count down the time until dismissal, because for the first time in a long while, I had something to look forward to at home.

After freshening up, I was in my comfortable pajamas, with a pint of leftover strawberry Greek yoghurt I still had in the fridge; I whipped up a proper bowl with nuts, chocolates, and berries, before calling it dinner, and scurrying back into my room like a blue-pajamaed hobgoblin. The bowl sat precariously on my desk as I prompted my computer to open.

And just as I left it the other day, it had all my other tabs open; my documents, browsers, music, and emails. Closing them all properly, I paused on the email once again, rereading the personalized letter I had received from PelaGames Studios;

**From**: noreply-devteam

**Subject**: Merrantis Horizon: Legacy Beta Invitation

**Date**: July 11, 2026, 3:14 AM

Dear User (Olivilivvia)

We are excited to announce the upcoming return of Merrantis Horizon!

Since the official closure of our game in August 2014, we have heard the community’s demands over the years to bring back the games of yesteryear. We’ve begun to test run the familiar world the players immersed themselves in for years, in a newer engine due to the closing of services for Adobe Flash.

And would love nothing more than to have our legacy players participate in helping us bring our world back to life. If you would like to participate in the beta testing of Merrantis Horizon, we have provided a link where you can download the game;

It is only available for systems running Windows currently.

In Eden I Rule—A.B

\[ DOWNLOAD\_BETA\_CLIENT\_SERVER276.EXE \]

When I first read the email, I thought it was spam—right off the bat, until I started to verify the validity; I checked the sender’s email, and it seemed legitimate. I even ran my link checker to see if it was just a ploy to steal cookies off my browser, but it all seemed clean.

But then I began to ponder—how did they find my email?

That made me sit and think for a while until I realized that I was reading off of my old Gmail, which my dad created for me so I could have my own Facebook account at the age of 8; I know that sounds a bit off-putting, but he was only forced to make it when I kept using his account to share game invites to family friends and relatives, although he wasn’t very pleased when I kept changing his avatars as a woman.

I checked the game launcher I left to install that morning; it was a hassle at first considering I didn’t remember my password for the old account I used to have, but after a quick reset, I got in, and immediately I felt nostalgic to the design, as it displayed the seasonal packages that the game used to have; it showcased the different wardrobe options for the characters, but the display in particular promoted he Fall’14 collection, which never got released.

Looking down at the bottom of the screen, I was pleased to see that the game had fully installed in my computer—*ready to play*.

The game loaded faster than I remembered, but considering computers were faster now than a decade ago, it made sense. The opening intro and loading screen were still the same. I’ve always thought it oddly resembled the Starbucks siren logo, as bubbles emitted from behind, as beyond the game’s logo was the blue sea, lighter from the top of the screen, then gradually getting darker down to the bottom, where the in-game tips and quotes usually were displayed;

“Spends your Seashells and Pearls wisely!”

“Seahorses and *Ferblins* are your best friends, don’t forget to feed them before your big adventure together!”

“Avoid *angel silver* at all costs, they sting!”

It felt warm seeing those texts. I’ve spent countless hours on the loading screen to the point where I’ve practically memorized most of the little quotes.

After loading, the game began in a pitch black screen before a complete white, and there it was in a mix of blues and purples, followed by the sound of woodwind and chimes, amidst the rush of bubbles was the title itself—*Merrantis Horizon*.

My character loaded in the saved game selection, where she floated above the seabed surrounded by corals; her majestic tail glistened iridescent purple, the idle motion of her slowly floating in the water as her tail moved in mimics of ocean waves.

The other two empty slots remained vacant; it was an option to make a new character, but it would mean starting over at level one, so I never truly touched that feature, as my main character was already in Level 84. But for the sake of trying again, I wanted to see the character customization menu if it still worked as well as before.

The camera rushed past my main character and formed a blank figure standing behind a rush of bubbles, as the screen asked, “Merrano or Mermaid?” I stared at the selection for a while, and without careful consideration, out of curiosity, I clicked Merranos. Immediately, what appeared was a humanoid sea creature with two legs, webbed fingers and toes, with skin decked in iridescent blue scales blending the natural human tone in a gradient mix. Its head appeared normal for a male character, but the ears were fin-like and blue.

I began to play around with the customization for a while, trying out different hair, facial features, and clothing, before eventually going back to my main character and starting the game from her perspective.

I spawned in the middle of the town center, the map looked the same as I vaguely remembered it to be; Merrantis captured modern architecture while blending the beauty of underwater dilapidation, where some buildings imitated coral reefs with holes resembling windows of apartments, the shops selling in-game items, and goods were portrayed inside modernized shopping centers, alongside non-intractable cafe’s and bistros serving as a spot where players commonly role-play in.

But there were none. What made the bustling world feel alive were the players, and yet, I was *alone*.

In the game, I found myself to be mesmerized as my eyes settled on the sight before me, the game looked far below from what the graphics of newer generation consoles and computers can already depict,  the distinction of what I’ve been used to seeing now and before felt like a blaring contrast—*but in a good way*.

It felt like coming back to your childhood home, and realizing nothing really changed, the furniture was still there, and the pictures were still the same, but you weren’t, and you haven’t been in a long time.

I roamed the map, visiting the shops, the shopping center, the ‘beach’—which I always found ironic even then. Yet, I hadn’t seen a single player, except the NPCs, they were all still present, the merchants, the characters dressed in outfits you can’t buy off of the clothing stores, yet infinitely looked better than some other items; they were all still there, repeating the same idle motion they’ve had for more than a decade, as if cursed to repeat a loop while they degraded through time.

Although, to my surprise, I found the quest booth beside an NPC, that was the spot most users would go to so they could collect their daily login rewards, and complete the daily tasks that eventually became routinely predictable.

Approaching the board, I pressed E on my keyboard to interact with the NPC;

“…”

A blank chat box appeared below his name—Korbin Jeeves; a Merranos dressed like a pirate, his left eye had an eyepatch, while one of his hands was a hook; I vaguely remembered fan art about this character on DeviantArt, and it slightly makes me shudder thinking about it; I clicked ‘Continue’.

“…”

*Click*

“…”

*Click*

“…”

Usually, the character would give game updates to the players, but I guess there was nothing to update about—Click.

“The *Hexagon* has taken over. *Pelagora* is falling.”

‘No New Quests Available’

The camera sent me back to a 3rd person point of view as I left the quest booth, leaving the pirate NPC. He returned to his idle motion, but even as I navigated away from the area, I could’ve sworn from the corner of my screen his eyes—well, eye—shifted to follow my avatar.

I thought of it being most likely a trick of the eyes, but I was already taken aback by his words echoing through my mind.

From what I can recall, the Hexagon was the enemy of the game’s story, some kind of military government organization that wanted to take over Merrantis and take down the queen, Pelagora. I remember doing multiple quests back then, where I had to fight soldiers that tried to invade the land, which mainly took place in the story quests, but they got a bit too difficult, so I stopped and just played to hang out and role-play with my friends.

Eventually, the novelty of roaming through the game began to wear off; there was really… nothing else to do. I couldn’t do the quests, I couldn’t meet new people since nobody was present, and it all just felt pretty empty. I was used to playing single-player games similar to this, but Merrantis Horizon wasn’t built to be single-player, and because of that, the silence felt louder than it should.

Although I was a bit disappointed, I was still happy to revisit the game I once loved so dearly, and with that in mind, I was ready to log off. I was a cursor away until I realized I hadn’t visited one more place—*The Drunken Coral*.

It was a hub for players to hang out inside; the building resembled a midnight club out in the town, or what they thought kids envisioned it to be. A Merranos bouncer stood outside by the lines of NPCs waiting to get in, repeating the same animation as though they were speaking to each other or the person they were with.

Floating into the venue, my whole monitor went dark, before a loading screen appeared on screen. It was different from the opening; this one was promoting the Merrantis Horizon gift cards.

*\[TIP: Explore the 8th Continent of the world in style! Grab your Merrantis Horizon Membership Gift Card at your local Best Buy or 7-Eleven today!\]*

I let out a sad chuckle; I used to force my dad to buy one of those every time we went on shopping trips. I felt a slight tug in my chest seeing those again, as the memories came vividly flashing through my mind; I sniffled and brushed my nose before watching the shiny loading bar fill all the way up to a hundred.

The game took me to a secluded map, as I noticed on the circular map at the side of the screen, the rest of the pathways were gone, only leaving the area of the club for exploration. I floated through, going down the underwater hall with walls and ceilings that resembled an aquarium, as 3D fish swam and passed through.

Stopping in front of another door, another bouncer was waiting with crossed arms, but this one I could interact with. I pressed ‘E’.

“…”

*Click*

“…”

*Click*

“All hail73524672willreturn. They will pay.”

The screen blinked briefly, fading to black before taking me inside the club.

I barely had any time to even process what the bouncer said, as I froze at the sight before me;

The club had bustling music, strobe lights beaming overhead as it changed colors, beaming against the dance floor full of players—real players; every Merrano or Mermaid had their own different appearance and style, as their usernames flashed above their avatars’ heads;

‘kellyclarksonfan2014xoxo’

‘samyaya05’

‘oiNoellaPlaysYTio’

‘BatmanBateman321’

I was about to rush and speak in the chatbox, only to realize it was still empty. In fact, as I took a closer look around the characters, they weren’t moving at all; they froze mid-action, as some had a chat bubble floating above their head, only for it to load repeatedly. Others were frozen in a walking stance, while some mermaids froze floating mid-water.

The only movement I caught was from the bartender behind the counter; I floated onwards, passing through the color-changing dance floor and frozen players to observe more closely. He was wiping the counter before floating elsewhere, preparing drinks for nobody, before setting it on the bar and repeating the animation in a loop. I couldn’t interact with him, although his silhouette emitted a halo-ish light; the ‘E’ wasn’t working.

It was strange roaming around the club, the upbeat music continued, but the crowd full of players looked frozen in time, like living statues—a memorial for what was at the moment, a digital snapshot in time, a perfect capture of what took place before the game pulled the plug on this server’s world. Leaving the players at that time stuck forever through their avatars in a time capsule.

Passing through the halls as I continued to explore, the music faded as I found myself heading to the club’s washroom. I paused, my character floating mid-water as I spotted at the end of the hall a statue of a mermaid.

I swam onwards.

She appeared to be different, a bit odd; although she had similar features to every other mermaid in the game, the stone carved statue had a long pole protruding from her chest, pinning her against the platform below her. From a far the looked to have wings, three on each side, and her tail was pointed upwards as she held the base near the fins, while her other arm was slightly raised, along with her dainty fingers forming a shape or a sign.

The halo formed around her silhouette.

I felt my heart skip a beat as my finger hovered over ‘E’.

*I knew then I should’ve left, that something didn’t feel right.*

My fingers brushed against the key,

*Click.*

The camera shifted to face the statue closer—too close; her head covered the whole screen, her frozen stone eyes sent shivers down my spine as it stared glaringly into my own. We stared at each other for what felt like minutes, as the empty chat box finally appeared below.

“The Siren sees what the world chooses to hide.”

*Click.*

“In Eden I Rule—A.B”

*Click.*

“Hex. ago.n will. F.all.”

My heart sank as the upbeat music looped the same note, repeating over and over until it didn’t sound like music at all anymore, but as an electronic wail. My screen froze as I tried to exit the game, spamming ‘Space’ to leave, but it felt like minutes before it actually did anything; but when it did, the Windows loading screen stated the app wouldn’t respond anymore. I tried Ctrl-Alt-Delete, exiting properly, but the laptop hung.

I couldn’t stand to look at her stone face, plastered across my screen; I just couldn’t take it. As I scrambled off my seat, before pulling the extension plug completely off the wall. In the darkness of my room, I fell back onto my bed, looking at the computer.

Her face is still ingrained into my vision as an afterimage. My skin felt sticky as the air in my room had gone humid and hot; standing with my feet on the carpet, I froze, because even if it was for a split second, for one singular moment in that time I stood from my bed to the floor—I didn’t feel *alone*.

My fingers twitched as I felt the darkest corner of my room grow the size of a shadow thrice, until I ran towards the light switch, my body slamming against the door as I fell back, feeling my hair stick to the barnished wood, as I sat on the floor.

I admit, I cried that evening. I couldn’t sleep in my room or alone, so I found myself in my friend Monica’s apartment. I told her everything, and… of course, she thought it was all in my head, and that games did ‘weird shit’ all the time; and it was just a little spook over nothing, and I guess I could see that.

But when I did try to draft a review for the game developers to let them know about whatever that was, even if I wasn’t even sure what it was or couldn't explain it, the website flashed an Error 404. I tried to check online news about the game being back in development or revamping, but nothing recent came up. It was the same old articles about the game officially shutting down, and other Reddit threads talking about how they want the game back up again.

I was going to try and ask other players if they too received an email, but I wasn’t even sure where to start; I wasn’t in touch with my online friends anymore, and it had been a decade since we’ve last played the game; but one thing is for certain, I won’t be playing *Merrantis Horizon* again.

*At least, not alone.*
