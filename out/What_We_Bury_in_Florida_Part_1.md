I’m not sure what it feels like to be cooked alive in an air fryer, but if I had to guess it’d be akin to the summer heat of central Florida. Everyone likes to say how much they hate their hometown, and I’m no different, except that when I say my hometown is Hell, I think it carries a little more weight. Heat indexes that rival the sun-blasted rocks of Death Valley, pools of stagnant filth acting as the primordial ooze of the twenty-first century, humidity that clings to every fiber of your being and turns your gentle stroll into fording an invisible, boiling river. I could go on forever about the ugliness that stains this overpopulated sandbar, but there is also a beauty to this land.   
From the tortoises plotting their long voyage from one end of the yard to the other, to the setting sun hanging gently over a lethally gorgeous swamp; Florida isn’t for everyone and maybe isn’t even for me, but if you stick around long enough; God, Gaia or whoever it could be will paint you a pretty picture from time to time. 

These silver linings are often the few reasons I enjoy my walks with Maggie, besides her company. Most of the time a Summer walk consists of swatting bugs and cursing myself for drinking the night before. But the dog needs to get outside, and I need to slow down the curse of getting fat and looking like my father, so I’d still be walking even if it was only misery. 

Today I really didn’t feel like going, but Maggie looked up at me with her big, human eyes and I couldn’t say no. I figured it’d be good for me to walk now instead of later. The story wasn’t going anywhere, and a good hike usually gets my thoughts moving around with me. I’d been staring at the blinking caret for almost an hour when I finally grabbed Maggie’s pink harness and made my way out the door. 

I always drive to the walking trail. You have a better chance at catching the subtle beauty of nature the further you are from other people, and this was the best I could get. My nature trail was about twelve miles in length beginning outside a nearby country club and making its way North to finally end at the boil of Blue Springs. It was mostly quiet, but you’d still get the occasional other dog walker, bicyclist, or sometimes even a horse with its portly jockey plopped atop. Maggie always loves those days, but I get nervous. Horses are majestic and awesome, and I’m sure you had at least one girl in your middle school who could tell you way too much about them, but I just think they are too big and too muscular. I’m one of those guys who likes to think they can kill more types of wild animals without a gun than is reasonable, but I’ve also seen horses kick things and I think they’d have my number. 

By the time we arrived at the trail head Maggie was already panting. I got her ready, took one last swig of water from my bottle and started us off. I always try to get through the first hundred yards quickly. There’s the aforementioned country club on one side and a water treatment plant on the other, sandwiching the trail head. I stopped bringing doggie bags with me because the trail offers no trash can. The first time I walked this trail I was proper and ready, but after walking with a bag of dog shit in my hand for an hour waiting for a bin to appear, I decided to leave the bags at home from then on. I mean every other animal on Earth takes dumps that no one else is picking up, so I figure one dog can’t make that much of a difference.

I tried to rush through the beginning, tugging at Maggie as she shamelessly let loose in the most conspicuous of spots. I could hear a bunch of working men on the other side of the fence and foliage grunting, with the occasional beeps of a reversing truck. As we walked, slowly the sounds became replaced with the screaming buzz of high-voltage wire dangling above our heads; we rounded a bend of young slash pines and came upon a solar farm that the cables were connected to. Along the perimeter was a Spanish-style stone fence, painted a salmon pink I could only describe as “farm-raised”. As we got closer it became apparent there was silver graffiti at the corner of the stone fence reading:

*Gaylord’s Factory*.

I let a sharp exhale through my nose. “Fuckin’ kids.” I mumbled in amusement, shaking my head. “Must be a new addition, huh, Maggie?” She kept her gaze fixed ahead.

The walk was pretty standard so far, we made it into the woods proper and only encountered one other person, a cyclist in full yellow, skin-tight gear. I had sweat pouring down my face, only stopping for a quick reprieve on the summit of my brow. I rounded the corner which changed our direction of travel from the West to the North, parallel with the train tracks. The bugs had started following us immediately upon exiting the car, but now they were in such numbers that I couldn’t help but think of my skin as the Great Wall protecting my insides from the Mongol horde that is sub-tropical insects.

We were about halfway to where we usually turn around, a little under two-miles from the entrance we started at, the dog was stopping at every sizable shadow for a quick rest. After the third time I dropped the corded leash; she wasn’t going anywhere. The leash dragged across asphalt, sand, and grass as we made our way deeper into the trail. Maggie’s hips swayed along with the tops of the trees as she trotted along, keeping pace so that she was always a couple steps ahead of me.

Pebbles started to rock, nestled within the dirt. A vibration sprung up from the ground; it was off in the distance. I pulled out my phone and dialed Jason; I figured I might as well get some calls out of the way and see how people are doing.

\*Ring…\*

\*Ring…\*

\*Ri-\*

Jason answered with heavy breathing, “Hello?”

“Hey bud, it’s Ken.”

“Yeah, I got caller ID.”

“Damn dude, it’s great talking to you too.”

“No. Sorry man, I didn’t mean it like that. I’ve just been super busy, how’s the writing coming along?” he apologetically changed subjects.

“It’s fine, I’m on a walk with the dog right now,” I said, changing subjects further. The subtle rumble of the Earth became a violent shake. I could hear the grating screech of metal on metal; I picked-up the leash just to be safe.

“Fishing for ideas?” he asked, not letting me out that easily.

“Yeah, I guess you could say that. I’m basically in the same spot as when you saw me last.”

“Endings can be hard… I would imagine, I mean I haven’t written anything since college. Shit, I can’t even remember the last time I’ve read.” I remembered him telling me that he had read the opening of my story a couple weeks ago, but I let it slide. Writing is my hobby and there’s no reason I should get upset for others not caring as much as I do. I think he noticed what he said though, because he quickly asked, “So why’d you call?”

“Oh, just thought I’d get in touch and see how people are, I feel like I’ve been in a cave all week.” I rolled my eyes, only for myself.

“That’s good, everyone’s been asking about you.” Half concern and half obligation permeated his voice.

“Yeah?”

“Yeah. You gotta admit you haven’t really been yourself lately, I guess Kayla and the guys just want to make sure you–” The excruciating wail of a horn drowned out the last bit of what he had to say, but I understood well enough, and the insinuation made me shiver in disappointment.

“Well, tell them thank you for their concern but I’m fine. I just needed some time.”

“I get it… But hey, it’s Wednesday! That means there’s a lady’s night tonight at DaVinci’s. You should swing by.”

“Oh, you know I’m trying not to-” My left arm lurched forward, pulling me off balance and sending my right foot clumsily into the other ankle keeping my weight up. I tumbled forward with my phone flying the other direction. The leash dropped, and I caught myself on the pavement, scraping my palms. The blood started pouring out immediately, running around the small pebbles stuck into my hand, creating an isthmus of gravel and garnet. I went to grab my phone at first, but I saw a bunny dart left into the thin tree line with Maggie shortly in tow. 

My eyes widened at the realization, and without any thought I began a dead sprint toward the adorable predator and her unfortunate prey. 

I could hear the train get closer as I bashed my way through the stiff branches and spiderwebs, revealing the short field of sugar sand between me and the tracks. Maggie and the rabbit were already halfway across the sand pit, the train barreling closer, unable to see the mutt and unable to stop even if it did. I pushed through the sugar, driving my feet down as they buried into the amorphous ground. My feet were plungers stuck to the Earth, barely able to be lifted by the ungreased hydraulic pistons that were my legs, the lactic acid pooling like hot magma in my calves. 

“Maggie!” I screamed through heavy breaths, each one getting a little quieter and less forceful. The train was fifty yards away, the bunny crossed the tracks, and the conductor must’ve seen the dog and I, because the train let out a deafening blow of its horn which caught me off guard and threw off the rhythm of my already severely hindered strides. The train was twenty-five yards away now; Maggie was just reaching the rails, unable to understand her impending doom. Fifteen yards and the dog was jumping the first beam.

“Jesus Christ…” I redirected my gaze into the sky, waiting for the inevitable.

The cars rushed past me, close enough to lose a finger or two if I’d wanted. I held my breath as the tempest draft of the train consumed all the air around me, coating my dripping skin with a layer of dry sand which was now slowly forming into mud. 

The train was uncomfortably long, it left me a lot of time to expect the worst. Getting hit by the train would be gruesome, but there’d also be nothing left for me to really see and sob over. Truly, I was worried that her leash got caught. I wasn’t the most squeamish guy, but I don’t think I would’ve been able to shake seeing my buddy get decapitated or otherwise ripped apart. When all the train cars had passed, I didn’t stop looking up for a few seconds. I eventually figured it was time to check, because nothing could be done now.

There was nothing. No blood, viscera, or fabric from her leash. The dust had finally settled all around me, but off about hundred yards across the tracks was a little dirt cloud followed by a long, flapping piece of pink fabric.

Once I caught up to Maggie she wasn’t running anymore, but not from a lack of trying. Her leash was wrapped around a jagged root and stretched in the direction of where the rabbit went. The anchored line kept the meat vessel from putting herself in more danger. I thanked God and told him I’ll be back in church soon, which I meant in the moment, but subconsciously knew was a lie. After a scolding which made Maggie look genuinely scared and made me feel genuinely mean, I figured that was enough adventure for today. The prey had successfully evaded capture, and my phone was still back at the trail, waiting to be destroyed or stolen.

“Shit.” I untangled Maggie’s leash and held it firmly, but I knew she was too tired and scared of getting yelled at again to run off. Just as I was gearing to turn around and head back, I caught the glimpse of a structure in my periphery. In the tree clearing to my right. I looked closer and realized it was a cave. “What the hell?” 

Above ground caves in Florida are uncommon because most of the state is just sand and limestone underneath, but that wasn’t the only reason I was surprised. I had ridden dirt bikes all along the train tracks and trails over here as a kid and never once did I see this cave, I swear I’d even been in that exact tree clearing before. 

I took a couple steps forward, but Maggie stayed in place. The look on her face said that she definitely wanted to go home now, almost looking spooked. Maybe I was too hard on her, she is a dog after all.

“C’mon big girl, let’s see what this is.” Maggie reluctantly followed, she was practically under my legs. I emerged my head from the trees into the clearing. The cavern sat idly in front of me but felt as if it’s moving. Breathing? It looked like how plants did when I took a hero’s dose of mushrooms and freaked out about time looping in on itself. I don’t really do drugs anymore, and that was a big reason why. The hollow had a sign on the top of the entrance. I couldn’t read it from this distance. I got my other foot out of the brush and as if a switch had been pressed somewhere, rain began to pour down on me.

Hadn’t it just been clear? I mean Florida’s weather can be bipolar, but I don’t remember seeing a cloud before this moment.  I decided this cave can be explored tomorrow; I was tired enough after the dead sprint a minute ago.

“Yeah, I’ll take another too. Whiskey and diet,” I replied, hiding myself with a smile. Kayla began to turn toward the brick, one-story building which housed the inside bar and restrooms, opposite the rotted wooden stage. “By the way, Ernest Heming-Gay is lame…” Kayla turned back toward the table. “You can do better than that.” Kayla snickered and began jostling more puns and dumb names within her blonde head. She then continued to her destination inside, pushing through the thin crowd of drinkers now younger than us, if only by a couple years.

Jason held his mouth agape, brows furrowed, “And that’s it?” The dissatisfaction was palpable.

“Well shit, I told you the exciting part. What else do you want?” My voice pitched higher on the last syllable.

“I hope your writing is more thrilling than that, you should’ve drawn it out better if that was the peak.”

“I feel ya, but I mean we work out and stuff.”

“That’s great, but drinking nearly everyday doesn’t get canceled out with bro splits.”

The next day was much of the same, except for some missing posters printed along the trail. Maggie and I ventured out with a mission in reaching the cave; the temperature was still a bitch, but no brushes with death or running. We took our usual route until we ventured off the path where I remembered picking my phone back up yesterday. You could still see the three sets of footprints in the sand going towards the rail. Each set behind the other was larger in area and further in depth. It took us just under an hour to make it from my car to the clearing with the cave. This time I made sure to leave earlier, so we would miss the afternoon rain. The dog and I were awake and driving toward the trail before ten, but even with our extra time it still poured down with fury. If I wasn’t such a skeptic, I would’ve thought that the cavern was guarded by some angel or entity, which was trying to keep us away from it.

Back in high school we would go to this town close by, Cassadaga, that has the moniker of being haunted, and at one time hosted the most palm readers and spiritualists of anywhere in America. We would sit in the “Devil’s Chair”, a throne of cabernet bricks fixed in the middle of a silent graveyard, and try our hand at the Ouija board; a game made by Milton & Bradley. One time we even went to a haunted (abandoned) hotel and searched around for hours at night, looking for any sign of paranormal activity. Every time, nothing happened. Unless you count a homeless tranqzombie chasing you and your buddies down a dark hallway, because you disturbed his meditation as “paranormal”.

But I mean, the rain started the second I stepped into the clearing, not a moment sooner or later. I had to admit this phenomenon was weird, but it still is Florida, and rain isn’t any reason to sound the alarm. So, after a long stare down with the formless opening, we packed it in again and headed home. I still couldn’t read the sign. 

Friday I was thoroughly prepared; I was going to see what was inside this cave. It was probably nothing, but I had to see for some reason. It was just so odd for an above ground cavity to be in Florida, let alone this region. I decided to pack a bag to bring this time; I didn’t want the rain or anything else getting in the way of me seeing what was inside. I packed a raincoat, two flashlights, a collapsable dog bowl, an extra bottle of water, some snacks like kolbas and some peanuts, seven Jim Beam nippers, and my .38 Smith & Wesson. 

My choice of supplies may have been short-sighted if not down right foolish, but I figured I’d make a day out of it. I’m a teacher and this was summer, so I didn’t have work or much else to do during the day. There was little drink to carry me on if the rain got bad and I had to shelter under the cave, enough food and water to last me and the dog at least a day if something got really bad, and the gun was for safety. I know I already talked about my potentially misguided confidence when pitted against wild animals, but this theoretical fight isn’t sanctioned and I’m sure as hell not about to fight a coyote or black bear on equal terms, not that the gun would do much to a bear.

The plan was to just head to the cave, look around, and be back within a couple hours. I brought all the extra because… Well, I’m not sure but I just had a feeling I’d be there a while. Longer than planned no matter the plan. It started with the raincoat and flashlight, but then spiraled to this backpack. Not like it was a whole lot, but the navy Jansport was full enough that I felt like I was hauling books in it again.

I made it to the trail at 9:17 AM; the sun hadn’t been out too long, so it was actually kind of pleasant still. Some of the water plant guys were just arriving at work, Maggie and I had to wait for one truck to pass before being let in front of the next one. I really wished I brought a doggie bag this time, too many witnesses. 

We passed “Gaylord’s Factory” and worked down the trail, it sure was a difference with the bookbag on. Maggie was pulling as we walked this time; she seemed more excited than usual, it must’ve been the morning air. 

It was overcast, so the raincoat was probably a good idea, whether Tlaloc was watching the clearing or not. We passed the tracks and made it to the clearing with the cave by 9:53 AM. I felt like the trip was longer the last two times, but maybe knowing the route took off a few minutes, even with the extra weight.

Before I stepped into the clearing, I took the last few moments to gather in my surroundings. I was facing West, toward the cave, and had practically walked in a straight line from where I turned off, that means I just have to walk East should I get mixed up and I will eventually make it back to the trail. There was a tree directly to my left, one of those big Southern oaks which have probably been around long enough to have seen conquistadors walking around. I decided to mark the tree just in case. East was easy to find because it was where the sun rises and opposite of where the Earth opened, but it’s better to be safe than the asshole who dies in a wilderness that’s just a short walk from civilization. In my front left pocket were my keys. Scratching an *X* into the tree with a little house key was like scraping a pan of burnt cheese which had been left out all night, not easy but not impossible. I worked up a little sweat.

When the *X* was finished, I stopped to proudly gaze at my work for a second. “Not bad huh, Maggie?” The dog looked up toward my direction upon hearing her name; she returned to staring at the mouth of the cavern. The hair on the ridge of her back stood at attention, Maggie’s tail did not wag. I think she could sense my uneasiness and it affected her too. I ran my right arm through the strap of my pack and let it sink slowly to the ground with my left. I couldn’t turn around now, but I still took my time before starting up the rain and entering the cave.

I rested upon a fishhook shaped tree. I sat by the old oak with its fresh carving and my bag, the shape was steep enough to sit in the curve like a seat. I pulled some liquid courage from my bag, twisted off the small cap and took a second to smell the sweet corn nectar, then shot back the first dose. The liquor slid down my esophagus with a tender caress, warming my blood more than the heat of Florida ever could. I took my time to see if anyone or anything would exit the hollow; nothing ever did. I looked down to see five of the seven airplane bottles empty and on the forest floor. It took a few, but my nerves had finally calmed down enough for me to think. I packed the trash back into my bag with a deep sigh. It was time, my phone read 10:37 AM.
