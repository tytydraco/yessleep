I have always been a curious man, but I realise now that it has killed more than just the cat.

As a young boy, I had questions and the passion to find the answers to those questions. "How do planes fly?"  "Why wasn't i accepted by the other kids?"  "Why are some people bad?" 

My mother usually avoided my questions, drowning herself alive in her knitting; and so did my father, coming home late from work and immediately dozing off on the couch after dinner.

I was ridiculed in school for my excessive need for knowledge, by classmates and teachers alike. 

Most days I would visit the local park where I spent most of my time when I wanted to take a break away from home, from school, from everything.

Always laying underneath the biggest tree, thinking to myself, "What will happen when i die?". And I always tossed a coin in the well across it when I inevitably thought about it.



After graduating, I slowly disassociated with my friends, didn't need them anyway. Got a well paying job at a research institute, and moved into an apartment. I gathered my stuff from my parents house, even if they did neglect me a little, I still loved them.

When I first moved into this old shithole to get away from my parents and have my own freedom, I was relieved. I could finally think to myself without any disturbance, stirring my thoughts in the pot that was my brain.



I set up my computer and searched the web for the questions that I wanted answered. Most I managed to find answers that were "good enough" for me. but ofcourse you can't answer such questions as, "why are some people bad?" or "When will I die?". It's a mystery that we won't know for our whole lives. I wish I could say the same for myself. 

After I couldn't get a reasonable answer for such subjective questions, I started looking for a solution, I wanted to achieve absolute knowledge at all costs. I checked the Web for a higher power or an artifact, no answers.

 Then I visited the local Library. I asked the Lady at the desk for antique books and she showed me to this thick dusty tome, It had these weird glyphs and runes on the greyish leather cover, and a big letter that resembled an F but it's branches had angled down.

I thanked the Lady and went home with the book, sat at my desktop and opened the cover after blowing off the dust. I couldn't read a single word of it but I looked for a translation online, it was exhausting translating every letter one by one, but after the first few words I didn't need my computer anymore. It started making sense to me, as if it was my mother language. Page after page, it was like this book was made for me, everything i had wondered; It was there.

At first, it was things I thought about but couldn't get clear answers. "How do planes fly?" "How do computers work?" "How does the body function?", and I had fun reading the answers, for such little words it taught me what no man could teach, It was like living through the entire lifetimes of the most knowledgable people on the subject. After skipping through the earlier questions, the questions i saw made me nervous,

 "Why didn't mom love me?"  "Why didn't father care?"  "Why were they so cruel to me?"  I read and read and it made me cry, yet i kept reading, even with teary eyes i had clear sight of the writing on the leathery pages. I had only thought about such questions after getting a yelling or a bad day at school, I didn't know that it would actually answer.

"When will they die", "How will they die?" I had learned what no man should. I didn't know mother was going leave me so early, I dared skip the next questions but I couldn't stop my eyes from gazing at the answers, the horrible truth to the secrets of the universe.

I took the pencil next to me, and stabbed my eye. I was going to take notes with it but this would be way better than its actual purpose.

Even with the stinging pain and the blood leaking onto my clothes and the pages, I still couldn't stop reading. I accepted my fate, that I would be omniscient. My damned curiosity had doomed me into a terrible burden. Putting my hoodie on, as to hide my scarred eye, I walked to my favourite spot, the biggest tree in the park, still reading the book, now only seeing out of one eye. I sat at the base of the tree, enjoying the slight breeze, then I glanced at the question, "What will happen when I die?" 

without looking at the answer, I searched my pocket for a coin to throw in the well, as per tradition. I looked deep in the well, seeing all the dull oxidized copper I dirtied the water with. I walked back to the tree, continuing on from the question,

There was no answer.

What did it mean? Would there be nothing after I died? Would I live forever?

I had to know, I had to know what it meant.

I unbuckled my belt, tying it around my neck.

I had to know,

I had to know.
