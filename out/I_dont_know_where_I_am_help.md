Don't get me wrong, I love my wife. She's an incredible and beautiful person. The moment I met her, I knew, and I think she did too. There was time before her, and then after her, and yada yada, how it goes on for so many couples. While it felt like a profound moment for me I'm sure plenty of other people know that same feeling. It was like waking up. Every moment since I met her has felt like my eyes were opened, like someone was finally looking at me straight on with no intent other than to create and experience pure bliss with me, regardless of whatever else was happening around us. Someone who believes in you at every moment and cheers you on no matter what. It's a feeling I wish everyone could have at least once.

Her eyes shimmered at me in the light, and I always felt overcome by how beautiful she was. Not just once, like in a movie, where you get the whole falling in love montage and the two of you grab hands and spin around before jumping into a lake at sunset. Every single day. Every moment with her felt incredible and deeply satisfying in a way I still can't fully explain. The curve of her shoulders calmed me down when nothing else could. The way her hair fell differently every single day inspired me somehow. The depth and honesty in her voice struck me down to my most vulnerable, most stripped down self, and yet I never shrank in front of her, not outwardly anyway. It felt like submitting myself to something, and knowing that if there was anything I could do to build a better life for her, I would do it without question. I could only hope she felt something similar about me, about our life together. You never really know what's happening behind someone's shimmering eyes though, do you.

Like I said, I love my wife.

We spent five years together before we got married. Not five years of just fooling around before some sudden elopement. It was intentional. We spent the first few years enjoying the crust of the earth together, then got progressively more serious. We made sure to enjoy it along the way too. I'm talking trips, socials, events, concerts, swimming, eating, indulging in pretty much everything we could together. When you meet the right partner it's like getting to experience life all over again, because you see the joy in their eyes and it's unmatched by anything you'd feel on your own. We had to do everything again.

It was almost like she beckoned me along some path and I happily played along the whole way. I made sure there was always plenty of sweetness and little pockets of playfulness scattered along that path too. Getting married made sense eventually, financially I guess, if nothing else. Then we had our son. As it goes. Our little guy.

That's enough of all that though. I'm writing this now because I need to share it with someone. Anyone. Everyone, if that's what it takes. I need contact with someone outside of this. I've always kept a separate phone of my own, I know, I know how that sounds, like I'm so enamored with her but somehow still keeping one foot out the door. Why can't I just fully commi- let's just stop there. I need to explain this. I need to get it out of me. It's eating away at whatever is left of me at this point. I'm not sure where to go from here, honestly. I'm not even sure where I am anymore. And I'm not sure my wife is still my wife.

Right now I'm sitting in what I would have called our basement three days ago, so that's still what I'll call it for now. It's a basement, at least in the sense that it's a basement in the best way a basement can be. We decorated it together with bright 90s and 80s stuff, neon colors and zigzag patterns, a little grunge mixed in here and there. We're both 2000s kids technically but we always liked that older aesthetic more. It used to be fun down here. It used to be my favorite room in the whole house, before I noticed something. Before I started noticing things.

We picked out all the art down here together, every single piece.

About two weeks ago I was with my wife and our son. We'll call them Vanessa and Chris. Chris was calm that day, Vanessa was getting tired after lunch, and I'll basically always take an afternoon nap if given half a chance. I get up early most mornings and go for a three mile jog around our neighborhood. We live near a lake that glistens in the morning sun in this incredible way, it just calls to me every single time. I never feel more clear headed or strong than when I'm out there in that morning air. It always tires me out afterward though, and she knows how much I love a good nap. Vanessa suggested we both nap that day, right as I was already nodding off, and I agreed, told her I'd grab the blankets from the dryer and meet her in the bedroom.

I walked down to the basement, and right before opening the dryer I stopped and stared at a piece of wall art hanging above it. Same bright color scheme as always, but different. Instead of the palm tree silhouette we'd had there for years, it was now a brightly colored paper cup, tipped over, spilling out some neon green liquid.

Walking upstairs, I called out to Vanessa, and by the time I reached the bedroom door I mentioned it, standing there in the doorway with the blankets in my arms, asking if she'd bought new art for downstairs. She was already lying on the bed, blinking her eyes open toward me before closing them again, and said oh yeah, the neighbor brought it over and she thought it looked kind of nice so she just put it up without thinking much of it. Honestly, I hated it, but I said alright and let it go. 

I hadn't really talked to our neighbors much. They'd always been sort of distant for some reason, I don't think I'd ever actually seen their faces, just the backs of their heads as they walked into their house or drove off somewhere. It didn't take up much of my energy or thought at the time. I set the blankets down and joined her in bed, not thinking any more of it.

I went back downstairs after our nap. That same art piece was still there above the dryer, and everything else looked mostly the same, except it all seemed shifted a few inches from where I remembered. We'd had recessed lighting put into the ceiling down there, little round lights embedded flush with the drywall, but when I looked up they were square now. Was it possible I'd just forgotten? Standing in the home improvement store picking those out, had I forgotten choosing square ones instead of round? But I installed them myself, with my own two hands.

That's pretty much where it all started, right there in our basement.

I started noticing other things. The kitchen table was an inch too short one day, an inch too long the next. The couch was a shade darker than I remembered it being. The rug in the hallway had all the same basic colors but the pattern itself was different than what I remembered choosing, then back to normal a few days later. Even the family photos in the hallway seemed off somehow, not the images themselves exactly, more the frames around them, like they'd all been swapped out for ones that were almost identical but slightly heavier, slightly darker at the edges. I stood in front of our wedding photo one night for a solid five minutes trying to figure out what was wrong with it, and I still couldn't tell you exactly what it was. Just a feeling that the version of that day hanging on our wall wasn't quite the version I remembered living through.

I couldn't tell if I'd hit some kind of breaking point, between the new responsibilities of being a dad, the lack of sleep, maybe running too much, or if something actually real was happening around me. I decided to take a break from my routine, stop running for a bit, just stay home with the family and see if I could recover some energy and whatever sanity I had left.

I remember calling my buddy Marcus around that time, one of the only people I still talk to regularly outside the house. I didn't tell him everything, just mentioned offhand that some stuff around the house felt different lately, like Vanessa had been rearranging things without telling me. He laughed it off, said something like that's marriage for you, and then, almost as an afterthought, asked if everything was really okay, because I sounded off on the phone, sounded far away somehow, even though I was standing right in my own kitchen. I told him I was fine. I don't know why I told him that instead of the truth. I think some part of me already knew that saying it out loud, really out loud to another person, would make it real in a way I wasn't ready for yet.

There was this sinking feeling in my stomach one day, standing in our living room, when I heard her behind me in the kitchen open the refrigerator door once, then close it, then open it again, then a third time, and again. On the fifth time I turned around to see what was going on and she was just standing there with her head hanging low, not even looking into the fridge, more staring down at the floor in front of her, opening and closing the door over and over on nothing at all. There was this vacantness to her movement that I can't fully describe. Then she suddenly snapped upright, unblinking, staring right at me, then smiled and asked if I wanted a snack.

The longer I stayed in, the more I noticed changes in the way she moved. Sometimes her walk turned jagged, instead of her knees bending normally and her legs just moving forward the way anyone's would, they'd jolt out at strange angles, and her hands would squeeze shut and release, over and over, at her sides. I got so thrown by it that I went through our bathroom cabinets looking for anything new she might be taking. She hadn't mentioned anything to me. I didn't find anything.

I started watching her sleep too, which I know sounds strange to admit, but I needed to know if it was just a daytime thing, some kind of stress response maybe. Most nights she slept the way she always had, curled toward me, breathing slow and even. But twice that week I woke up around three in the morning to find her sitting straight up in bed, completely still, eyes open, staring at the wall across from us like she was reading something written there that I couldn't see. When I whispered her name she didn't answer right away. The first time it took almost a full minute before she blinked, turned to look at me, and asked why I was awake, like she hadn't been the one sitting bolt upright in the dark.

That night the sun set in a color grayer than usual, like the whole sky had been drained a little. Walking toward our bedroom after putting Chris down, the hallway lights blinked off, then on, then dimmed to a red orange color, then snapped back to normal.

Half my body slammed into the doorway entering our room after that, and Vanessa was standing at the foot of the bed with dull, limp hair and a pale face pointed straight down at her feet. Just be normal, I told myself. Stay calm. 

Maybe she's going through something and hasn't told you yet. She's normally so open with me about things like that. You never really know what someone's keeping locked up inside them though, do you. I reached out and caressed her cheek to get her to look up at me, and her eyes were dim, lips chapped, skin dry and flaking. I frowned and tilted my head toward her, asking if everything was okay. She said everything was perfect, then bounced off toward the bathroom to get ready for bed like nothing had happened at all.

Chris changed a little in all this as well, though I keep trying to convince myself I imagined that part more than anything else. He'd started sleeping longer stretches than a baby his age really should, six, seven hours without a single sound from the monitor, when before he'd wake crying every two or three hours like clockwork. I remember standing over his crib one night just watching his chest rise and fall, counting the seconds between breaths because something about the rhythm felt wrong, too even, too mechanical, like a metronome instead of a baby breathing. I told myself I was being paranoid. New dad anxiety, everyone gets it, I'd read that somewhere. I wanted so badly for that to be the explanation.

It had been a while since I'd been outside at all by that point, a few days, maybe longer than that even. I sat on the edge of the bed thinking I just needed some fresh air, maybe a short walk would help clear my head. That's not usual for me, normally I stay in once the sun goes down and get up early instead, but I hadn't gone for a morning jog in days at that point either. I pulled on my sweatpants and a hoodie and called out to let her know I was heading out for a short walk to get some air. No response came back from the bathroom, just the buzz of an electric toothbrush running. That struck me as odd. I walked over and eased the door open, gently said her name into the room. She wasn't in there. The light clicked off on its own a moment later, like it was on a motion sensor and no one had been standing in that room for the last five minutes or so. Only I don't remember ever installing a motion sensor light in that bathroom, not once.

I turned and checked the baby monitor next, we'd set up a little camera in Chris's room so we could watch him at night. There he was on the screen, calm, sleeping, and then a moment later the whole feed cut to static. I jolted up and ran across the hall to his room. She was standing there with the power cable bitten clean through, staring right at me with it still hanging from her mouth. How was that even possible. There was no time to ask questions though. I moved faster than I even knew I could toward the crib.

Chris was gone.

I was scared enough at that point to wonder if I was hallucinating the whole thing, not sure what to do next. I just knew I needed to get out. My heart was pounding, my mind racing through every possible question. What was happening, where was Vanessa really, how had so much changed without me noticing it happen. I didn't engage with her at all, just turned and left the room. Walking back out through the hallway, the distance between the walls started to stretch out longer than it should have been. 

I got to the front door and grabbed my keys off the hook by instinct, except they weren't my keys anymore, they were bronze and sharp edged in my hand. I grabbed at the door handle and it wouldn't budge at all, wouldn't twist, wouldn't turn no matter how hard I tried. I looked toward the living room window instead, the curtains were moving like there was a breeze coming through, so my next plan was to get out through there and just take a look at the outside of the house, figure out where I actually was.

I pulled back the curtain on the living room window and there was a huge, tall, gray wall sitting just an inch away from the glass. No way to get out through there either.

Behind me, Vanessa was standing there. I screamed at the top of my lungs, louder than I think I'd ever screamed in my life, WHERE AM I.

She walked toward me holding out a set of keys in her hand and said these are our keys.

No they're NOT, I shouted back at her, backing into the wall, and that's when it hit me, all at once, like every piece I'd been stepping around for two weeks finally landed in the same place at the same time.

The naps. It was always the naps.

Every single time, she was the one who suggested it. Every single time, right as I was already halfway under, she'd bring me something, a glass of water, a cup of tea, her hand resting on my chest until my breathing slowed all the way down past normal sleep into something heavier, something I never once questioned because it felt so much like her just loving on me. I thought about the mornings after, how groggy I always was, how much longer it took me to feel like myself again compared to before we got married. I used to blame the running. I used to blame Chris. It was never either of those things.

She'd been taking me somewhere while I was under. I ran to the basement and started throwing things off the walls, then I found it. Some kind of seam in the world, a portal, I don't even know what else to call it folded into the wall behind that stupid paper cup painting. I left it hanging to cover the seem.

 She must have pulled me through while I was too far under to fight it, set me down in this other version of our life she'd been building somewhere on the other side, and every single time she built it she got something a little bit wrong. A light fixture. A couch cushion. The pattern on a rug she must have bought new because she couldn't bring the real one through with her. She was furnishing a whole second house to match ours exactly and she kept missing by an inch here, a shade there, because you can't actually copy a life down to the last detail no matter how badly you want to, no matter how long you've been planning it.

I don't know how long she'd been switching me back and forth before I finally noticed. I don't know how many mornings I woke up in the real house thinking everything was fine, when I'd actually spent the whole night somewhere else entirely. I don't know how many naps I've taken in the last two weeks that weren't naps at all.

She appeared in the doorway at the top of the basement.

WHO ARE YOU, I screamed at her again, and she just kept coming toward me, keys held out, smiling that same soft smile she's had since the day I met her, and she said, so calm it made my skin crawl right off my bones, you're already here, baby, you don't have to keep fighting it.

I grabbed the lamp off the side table and swung it at her without even thinking, and it passed straight through her like she wasn't fully solid on this side yet, like she was only halfway dragged through with me. She flinched back from it anyway, shrinking away toward the stairs like the swing had actually caught her somehow, and then she turned and went up, quick, quiet, gone. 

I ran after her far enough to slam the basement door shut and turn the lock, both locks, then stood there with my back against it until my legs gave out and I slid down to the floor. I've been sitting here since, writing all of this down because it's the only thing keeping my hands busy enough not to shake.

It's quiet now. That's almost worse. I keep checking the lock every few minutes even though I know I haven't moved from this spot. I don't know which house I'm in right now, I don't know if this is the real basement or the one she built, I don't know if Chris is real or if he's some other small thing she got slightly wrong, I don't know if I'm even the version of me that's supposed to be here. 

A few minutes ago I started hearing it, low, coming from the seem in the the wall where that painting used to hang, a deep warping sound like something being pulled and stretched at the same time, and now the wall itself is starting to ripple along with it, small waves running through the drywall like it's water instead of something solid, and I don't think the lock on this door is going to matter at all.
