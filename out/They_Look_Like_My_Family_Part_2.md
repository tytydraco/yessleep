Lisa asked, "Hey, are you okay?"

Without answering a single word, I rushed to my room to try and pull myself together. The room was illuminated only by the sickly blue glow of my laptop screen. My fingers trembled slightly as I moved the cursor, reading through medical forums and archived threads on post-traumatic psychosis one by one.

Capgras Syndrome. PTSD. Dissociative Episodes.

The medical terms kept popping up on the screen. Numerous anonymous users on the forums replied to post-trauma sufferers with reassuringly logical explanations explaining that after enduring severe trauma, the human brain can project extreme fear onto the faces of loved ones, making them appear alien or horrifying as a strange coping mechanism.

"It's just a psychotic episode," I whispered softly into the quiet room, desperately trying to anchor myself to reality. "Your brain is just making up terrifying hallucinations."

I shut the laptop immediately. As soon as the screen went dark, the room turned pitch black and felt suffocating. The overwhelming exhaustion finally overtook my fear, until my consciousness faded away and I drifted off to sleep.

"Swish... Swish... Swish..."

A faint, rhythmic dragging sound forced me out of sleep.

I woke up abruptly, gasping for air, my heart pounding violently against my ribs. I grabbed my phone from the nightstand; the screen read 10:30 PM. The house was dead silent, save for that strange scraping sound outside.

I stepped out of my room into the pitch-black hallway. I tried pressing the wall switch no reaction. I tried the hallway light, then the bathroom switch. The power was completely dead.

I aimed my phone’s flash down the corridor, but the battery was low, leaving the light flickering weakly. As I passed my parents' bedroom, the door was slightly ajar. I peeked inside. Their bed was made with unnatural precision the blankets folded so stiffly that not a single natural wrinkle showed, as if no human body had touched the mattress all night.

Stepping onto the fifth stair leading down, my foot struck something hard. A small metallic object tumbled down the wooden steps. When I aimed the weak beam of my phone screen downward, I saw a wall light switch plate lying on the wood, its internal wiring snapped clean not from wear and tear, but pulled violently out of the drywall from the inside.

I swallowed hard and called out into the dark, "Mom? Dad? Lisa?"

No answer. The air in the hallway felt heavy and stifling, carrying a weird smell like rusty iron mixed with damp earth.

Seeking fresh air, I went downstairs and opened the front door. A bone-chilling cold hit my skin right away, accompanied by a fog so thick it swallowed the entire yard whole. I couldn't even see the walkway, let alone past the front gate.

Standing right in the middle of the fog-covered lawn was a rigid female figure. Her right arm moved slowly in a mechanical rhythm, dragging a broom across the bare ground.

From her height and frame, it was Mom.

"Mom?" my voice sounded raspy in the freezing air. "What are you doing standing outside this late?"
The moment those words left my mouth, the sweeping stopped completely.

The figure froze for a few seconds. Then, her head began to rotate toward me not a smooth movement, but a sickening, mechanical jerk, like a broken mannequin neck being forced around.

A wave of pure terror hit me physically. Every nerve in my body screamed one command: RUN.

I turned around, sprinted back into the house, and slammed the door shut. Through the frosted glass of the front door, I could still see her silhouette standing out there in the fog, completely motionless, without even a hint of breath.

The moment I turned back toward the dark hallway, my ears caught new sounds—the soft chewing of someone eating, mixed with the rustle of turning magazine pages coming from the living room. A sickening wave of déjà vu hit me instantly. I had lived this exact sequence of events before.

Panic completely shut down my logic. I ran upstairs, locked my bedroom door tight, and pressed my back against the wall.

Wanting to check if I had truly lost my mind, I slowly crept toward the window and peeked through the blinds.

She was standing right beneath my window. And the moment my eyes locked onto her, her neck snapped upward. Her face caught the dim moonlight, stretching her lips into a grotesquely wide smile that went way beyond normal human limits.

Then came the footsteps.

Not just one pair. Dozens of heavy, uneven footsteps started marching toward the front porch. Distorted voices sounding like corrupted voice recordings of my family began chanting my name in a creepily slow cadence.

"Ja-a-ack... Come... out..."

Panicking, I dropped to the floor and crawled under the bed, pulling my knees tight against my chest.
The calls outside turned into deafening, hysterical screams. Heavy thuds battered the front door downstairs, followed by the sound of splintering wood moving up the stairs.

THUD! THUD! THUD!

My bedroom door buckled violently. Wood splinters rained onto the carpet as the lock snapped.

From the narrow gap between the bed frame and the floor, I saw them step inside. Their feet moved with weird, rigid gestures, dragging their steps across the floorboards one foot stepping on its toes while the other dragged heavily on its heel.

The stench that invaded the space under the bed was no longer the familiar smell of our home. It was a suffocating mix of fresh wood varnish, wet soil, and a sharp, metallic odor that made my throat itch to cough. I pressed both hands over my mouth, shutting my eyes tight.

The closet doors were yanked open empty. The bathroom door was kicked off its hinges also empty.

Heavy, rasping breaths not the warm exhalation of a human, but a cold, mechanical hiss echoed directly above me. The weight of something heavy pressed down on the mattress foam until it bowed downward, brushing against my back.

A shadow slowly crept downward, blocking out the remaining light on the floor. The bed skirt peeled back slightly. In the darkness, its forehead emerged first, followed by a pair of wide, unblinking eyes. Its gaze locked onto mine in the pitch black. It pulled a faint, sinister grin, then whispered softly right in front of my face.

My chest locked up violently, my breath completely cut off until everything suddenly went dark.

07:00 AM

I gasped awake, my shirt soaked in cold sweat.
I scrambled out from under the blanket and immediately looked at my bedroom door.

The door was completely intact.

No broken hinges, no splintered wood. I walked over with shaking legs and ran my fingers over the frame. The wood felt smooth, but my fingertips picked up something strange fine sawdust clinging around the hinges, along with a faint smell of fresh varnish that hadn't completely dried yet.

See? I reassured myself, wiping the sweat from my forehead. It was just a nightmare. Just a psychotic episode. What I read on the forum was right.

I changed my shirt and headed downstairs. Morning sunlight poured through the living room windows, wiping away the heavy darkness from last night. Dad was sitting casually on the sofa watching the morning news on TV, while Lisa sat on the rug, lazily flipping through a stack of magazines.

"Oh, Jack, you're awake?" Dad turned around with a warm smile, though the corners of his mouth curved up so symmetrically it looked almost painted on. "Go grab some breakfast. Your mother made your favorite."

I walked over to the dining table. On a tin plate, thick slices of dark meat covered in heavy gravy were already served. The aroma was rich and savory.

I took the first bite. The texture was incredibly tender the meat fibers melted easily as I chewed. There was just a tiny hint of a sour taste at the back of my tongue, super faint, to the point I thought it was probably just from Mom's marinade.

"Finish it up, Jack," Dad's voice broke the silence from the living room. He turned around briefly with a warm, fatherly smile before turning back to the TV.

Not long after, the front doorbell rang. Lisa immediately jumped up from the sofa and opened the door.

"Oh, hey Carl!" Lisa called out from the front.
My heart felt a whole lot lighter seeing Carl step inside. Carl had been my best friend since childhood. He knew my house and my family inside out, so having him here felt like an anchor pulling me back to reality.

"Man, you look pale, Jack," Carl said, sitting next to me at the table.

I just smiled faintly, trying to brush it off. Mom served a plate with the same meat for Carl right away. The two of us ate right there while chatting casually. Carl devoured his meat hungrily, complimenting Mom on how her cooking kept getting better.

"Your mom’s cooking is great, Jack," Carl said, chewing. "Reminds me of those summer barbecues your dad threw when we were kids. Remember how he’d burn the sausages and just laugh?"

I smiled weakly. "Yeah... except Dad hasn't touched the grill in years."

Carl nodded along, but his eyes lingered for a solid three seconds on how Dad was holding his coffee mug—his fingers weren't looped through the handle, but cupped tightly around the boiling ceramic rim without a single flinch. Carl let out a nervous chuckle. "Uh... Mr. Mark? Isn't that mug burning your hands?"

Dad simply maintained his symmetrical smile. "Old skin gets tough, Carl."

Halfway through the meal, my stomach suddenly felt a bit uneasy. I stood up to excuse myself. "Hey Carl, I'm heading to the upstairs bathroom for a sec."
"Yeah, take your time," Carl replied, taking another bite.

I walked upstairs into the bathroom and splashed cold water on my face, staring at my reflection in the mirror. The warmth of Carl being downstairs made the weird suspicion in my head slowly fade away. See? They're just my family taking care of me, I thought.

While wiping my face with a towel, the casual conversation from downstairs suddenly cut out. The only sound left was the quiet murmur of the morning news broadcast on the TV.

Then came a low whisper Lisa and Dad speaking at an impossibly rapid tempo, sounding like a audio tape being fast forwarded at double speed.

I froze, leaning my ear close to the bathroom door. Carl’s voice cut through, sounding startled and slightly shaky: "Uh... Mr. Mark? Why are you standing so close?"

Before I could even turn the door handle—
SMASH!

The sound of a plate shattering into pieces against the floor, followed by a heavy silence that instantly wrapped around the entire house. No chatter, no footsteps.

I froze in front of the mirror. A bad gut feeling hit my chest again. I opened the bathroom door right away and walked quickly downstairs.

When I got to the dining area, Carl was gone.
On the tile floor, Carl's plate lay shattered. Pieces of meat and sauce were spilled everywhere. Mom was kneeling on the floor, picking up the broken bits of the tin and glass plate one by one with very steady, calm finger movements.

"Wait... where's Carl?" I asked, confused, looking around the quiet room. "How did the plate smash like this?"

Dad turned slowly from the sofa, pulling a friendly, perfectly symmetrical smile.

"Oh, Carl had to rush out suddenly, Jack," Dad answered in a smooth, easygoing tone. "Said he had an urgent matter come up. He was moving so fast when he stood up, he tripped and accidentally knocked his plate off the table."

I stood frozen on the stairs. My eyes shifted to the front door, which was shut tight, and then back to the broken plate pieces in Mom's hands.

In a rush? Without even saying goodbye to me first?
