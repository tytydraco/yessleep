I've been wearing a night guard for about eight months because I grind my teeth in my sleep.

It's a clear plastic thing that snaps over my upper teeth. Cost me $340, which seemed ridiculous at the time, but I was starting to wake up with headaches and one of my molars had developed a crack.

For the first six months, it worked perfectly.

Then I started finding it in strange places.

The first time, it was on my pillow. I assumed I'd taken it out while half asleep.

The second time, it was on my nightstand, sitting neatly beside my phone.

A few nights later, I found it on the bathroom sink.

I live alone. No pets, no partner, and I've never had a history of sleepwalking.

Still, taking out a mouth guard while sleeping seemed like a much more reasonable explanation than anything else.

At my next dental appointment, I mentioned it.

My dentist, Dr. Patel, examined the guard under a little magnifying lamp.

"Do you use anything to remove this?" she asked.

I told her I just hooked my fingernails under the back edges and pulled it off.

She turned it over.

"That's what I thought. But these marks aren't where your fingers would normally go."

She showed me three tiny scratches along the inner edge, right where the plastic sits against the gum behind my upper front teeth.

They looked like somebody had been scraping at the plastic with a needle.

She asked if it ever felt loose. I said no.

She put it back over my teeth, checked the fit, and told me it was still fine to use.

Before I left, she suggested recording myself sleeping if I was worried about taking it out unconsciously.

So I did.

I leaned my phone against a stack of books on my dresser, connected the charger, and recorded myself for about six hours.

The next morning, the guard was on my pillow again.

I watched the video.

At 3:17 a.m., I sat upright.

My eyes stayed closed.

I reached into my mouth, removed the guard, placed it on my pillow, and lay back down.

That's it.

I felt stupid for being worried.

But while watching, I noticed something else.

I didn't remove the guard immediately.

For about twenty seconds before sitting up, my mouth kept opening and closing.

Not like I was grinding my teeth.

More like I was trying to chew something that kept moving.

I replayed that section a few times, then decided it was probably just another weird sleep habit.

The following night, I put the guard in its case instead of wearing it.

I wanted to see whether the chewing would happen without it.

I woke up at 4:30 with the worst jaw pain I've ever experienced.

Both sides of my face ached. My tongue was sore, and there was a little blood on my pillowcase.

I checked my mouth in the bathroom mirror.

There were two small cuts underneath my tongue.

They were almost perfectly parallel.

I assumed I'd bitten myself somehow.

Then I noticed the night guard.

The case was still closed on the bathroom counter, exactly where I'd left it.

But there was something wet around the edges.

Not water.

Saliva.

I opened the case.

The guard was inside, pressed into its little plastic holder.

There were fresh scratches along the inner edge.

Four this time.

I didn't sleep much after that.

The next day I bought a cheap indoor security camera. It had night vision, motion detection, and continuous recording. I set it on the bookshelf facing my bed.

I also moved the night guard into my kitchen drawer.

I put a strip of masking tape across the drawer and wrote the date on it.

Then I went to sleep without wearing anything.

I woke up at 3:42.

My mouth was open.

I couldn't close it.

At first I thought I was having some kind of jaw spasm. I tried to move my chin with my hand, but it felt like there was something hard wedged between my upper and lower teeth.

I reached inside with my finger.

I touched plastic.

The night guard was in my mouth.

I pulled it out so quickly that I scraped my gum.

I remember sitting there, staring at it in my hand, trying to understand how I'd gotten it from the kitchen without remembering.

I went to check the drawer.

The tape was unbroken.

The guard's case was still inside.

And the case wasn't empty.

There was another night guard in it.

I stood there holding one in my hand and looking at the other.

Both had the same little blue manufacturer's mark near the back molar.

Both had the small chip on the right side from when I accidentally dropped it in the sink a few months ago.

But the one in my hand was warmer.

I put it down and went straight to the camera.

I don't really know how to describe what I saw.

The recording showed me asleep on my back.

At 3:38, my mouth opened.

Very slowly.

My chin kept dropping until my mouth was open much farther than I could comfortably open it while awake.

Then something moved.

At first I thought it was my tongue.

There was a small pale shape behind my lower teeth.

It moved upward.

I paused the video.

I still have the screenshot, although I've stopped looking at it.

It was a finger.

Not my finger.

Both of my hands were visible on top of the blanket.

This finger was coming from somewhere inside my mouth.

It was thin, with a tiny rounded nail, and it moved like someone trying to feel around inside a dark room.

A second finger appeared beside it.

Then a third.

I watched them reach up behind my upper teeth.

They seemed to be searching for something.

The fingers disappeared for a moment.

When they came back, they were holding the night guard.

They pushed it against the roof of my mouth.

It didn't fit at first.

They turned it slightly and tried again.

Then I heard a click.

Exactly the sound the guard makes when it snaps over my teeth.

The fingers withdrew.

My mouth closed.

Four minutes later, I woke up.

I watched the recording three times.

I checked the camera's position, the timestamps, everything. I wanted there to be something wrong with it.

There wasn't.

I packed a bag and went to my brother's apartment.

I didn't tell him the whole story. Just said I'd had a weird night and didn't want to be alone.

He let me sleep on his couch.

I didn't actually sleep.

Every time I started drifting off, I felt something shifting behind my tongue.

Around five in the morning, I started hearing a faint clicking sound.

It was coming from my own mouth.

I went into the bathroom and opened my mouth in front of the mirror.

Nothing unusual.

I checked under my tongue, where the cuts had been.

They were still there.

But they weren't cuts.

They were little crescent-shaped impressions.

Like the marks fingernails leave when someone presses their fingertips hard into their palm.

Except these were underneath my tongue.

Facing upward.

I called Dr. Patel's office as soon as they opened.

Her receptionist said the dentist wasn't working that day, but offered me an appointment tomorrow.

I asked whether they could check something about my night guard.

She said of course.

Then she asked a question that made me feel sick.

"Is this about the second appliance?"

I asked what she meant.

She told me their laboratory had called that morning about a duplicate guard that had apparently been ordered under my patient number.

There was some confusion because they only had one approved order on file.

I told her I hadn't ordered anything.

She went quiet for a few seconds.

Then she said, "That's strange. The laboratory says they received a new impression."

An impression is the mold or scan they use to make a guard fit your teeth.

I asked when they'd received it.

"Yesterday."

I was home all day yesterday.

I asked her to tell me exactly what the laboratory had received.

She put me on hold.

When she came back, she sounded uncomfortable.

Apparently the package had contained an old-fashioned dental impression made from putty. It was labeled with my name and patient number.

But the technician had flagged it because the teeth were unusually small.

And because there were too many of them.

I asked how many.

She said she wasn't sure, but the laboratory had counted forty-two.

An adult human normally has thirty-two at most.

I asked whether she could send me a picture.

She said she'd have to check with the dentist.

I hung up.

Then I remembered the two night guards.

The one in the case had been my original.

The one I'd pulled from my mouth was the new one.

I'd assumed they were identical because of the little blue mark and the chip.

But I'd only compared the outside.

I hadn't looked at the impressions on the inside.

I made my brother come with me to my apartment.

I didn't want to be there alone.

Both guards were still on the kitchen counter.

I picked up the original and put it over my teeth.

Perfect fit.

Then I picked up the other one.

The inside looked completely different.

The front teeth were narrower.

The molars were tiny and crowded together.

And there were more impressions than there should have been.

The strangest part was that the guard wasn't shaped for a normal upper jaw.

It was much too narrow.

I couldn't have worn it over my teeth.

But it had fit something inside my mouth.

My brother asked me why I was shaking.

I showed him the video.

He didn't say anything until the fingers appeared.

Then he physically stepped backward.

He watched it once and made me stop.

We left the apartment.

He's driving me to an emergency department now. He thinks I need to show someone the recording, even though neither of us knows what a doctor is supposed to do with it.

I'm writing this in the passenger seat because I need to put down exactly what happened while I still remember it.

There's one thing I haven't told my brother.

On the drive, I've been feeling something move behind my lower teeth.

Not my tongue.

Something smaller.

Something that keeps pressing against the inside of my cheek.

And about ten minutes ago, I heard that clicking sound again.

I think I finally understand what the night guard was for.

Those fingers weren't trying to take it out.

They were trying to put it on.

Whatever is in my mouth has been practicing how to wear teeth.

And I just felt it close a second jaw.
