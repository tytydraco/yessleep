Alright, I need to share something. Something you are not going to believe. I didn’t dare go public with this information, so I posted it here instead, as it felt safer. Do what you want with this information – it’s out of my hands now.

So, this discovery, as it would turn out, is quite shocking. I’ve only told this to about a handful of my friends. Some have not gotten to hear about it, and some never will, unless they find this text. I’ve not told any adults – that’s parents, guardians, teachers, or any others. They wouldn’t believe me, the same with the rest of my friends; they’d think this is just some bad joke.

I should clarify a couple of details first.

I’m a 19-year-old from Norway. I come from a small town on the south-eastern coast. I don’t expect you to know about this place, of course. It’s about an hour’s drive from the capital, Oslo, if that gives you anything to go on. Most likely you’re not that familiar with Norway, but please, bear with me.

Just to quickly make you familiar with the surroundings. I’d say my hometown, and the part of Norway I’m from feels a bit old. Nearly everything’s looked the same since I was born. No skyscrapers, no restaurant chains – not even a cinema. As for one who’s been raised in this place, it’s what I’m used to. Now, my hometown is a small town, to be fair, and quite agricultural as well. It’s located within the county of Vestfold, an agricultural region of Norway. In Norway, we’re divided into counties (kinda like states or provinces), and within them, there are the municipalities.

I’m sharing this first because, while this all may come to surprise you, it surprises me even more, as this discovery happened in my own home county, Vestfold.

I’ll describe my discovery in brief: I’ve found a lost and seemingly unknown municipality – a town essentially – hidden amongst the others, located in the rural plains of my county. As far as I can tell, nobody else knows about it. I must admit I got a little spooked about this discovery. All this comes off as very unnatural and quite un-Norwegian.

You may wonder how I came to find out about this.

First of all, I’m fascinated by a lot of things – a lot of them regarding history. Everything from ancient history to local history that I can get my hands on. A thing that occasionally happens in Norway is that municipalities merge. That’s when two or more get combined under one name to form a new and bigger municipality. The merging of municipalities shapes the edges of my hidden discovery.

So, I was reading up on former municipalities. I’d left Wikipedia some time ago and was now on really old and what seemed to be local websites for each county, and then again for each municipality. It was quite fascinating, as I hadn’t even heard of most of the former municipalities in my own county.

Nothing was out of the ordinary yet. Names like *Hedrum* and *Tjølling* went by – useful information for another day. But then, at one point, an article referred to 27 out of the 26 municipalities in Vestfold in the 60s.

That’s when things started to get weird.

It could’ve been a typo, but it was referenced several times throughout the same article, though never by name, of course. This resulted in another round of searching. What was this 27th municipality?

I’ll just cut right in and tell you that the municipality I happened to discover was known as **Glenne**. But should you try and look it up, you’d find nothing; it’s virtually non-existent, or existed – which seems to be the big idea. Later, this is what I’ve managed to gather on Glenne, summarized:

>Glenne was a former municipality in the county of Vestfold that bordered the municipalities *Andebu*, *Lardal*, and *Hedrum* (another former municipality). It was located within the interior of the county. Glenne seems to have been the smallest municipality in Vestfold that was itself not a city, and at the time, one of the smaller ones in Norway. It was granted official status as a municipality back in the 1830’s! Yet today, nobody knows about it. Some time in the 60’s, it got merged with Lardal, and today it’s nothing more than what seems like an empty forest on the map.

Now, a municipality doesn’t just vanish into thin air. Municipalities merge, true, but this feels different. This feels more "redacted" than just simply forgotten.

I could never imagine I’d end up on a discovery or a journey like this, but when I started to see the contours of something so out of place, I just had to dig deeper.

As you’d expect, nothing progressed. I couldn’t find a single webpage that referred to the existence of a mysterious 27th municipality. It took some time to try and dig things up, and I was close to giving it up and acknowledge it as a typo after all.

But then I stumbled upon a map.

Yet, a map without names on it, or really just like the biggest few cities at the time, nothing more. But on the map, you could clearly see more than just the eventual 21 municipalities from the 70s. All the other maps I’d found showed just the usual 14 municipalities I’ve grown up with. So, as I didn’t have a map I could cross-reference with, it became too difficult at the time to figure out which was the supposedly 27th one.

I went to try the museum in my hometown. Unfortunately, the museum’s attention was only on *my* town’s past, not the other parts of Vestfold, so at the time it felt like a dead end.

Some time went by, and the case went cold. I didn’t see any reason to tell my friends – what did I have to tell them? That I’d found a misleading map that couldn’t point me in any direction, and an article that hadn’t been updated since the owner likely got his first access to the internet, presumably back in the late 90s or early 2000s.


By happenstance, some time later, I stumbled upon the former municipalities again, and my interest was refueled. Before this, I’d already been to the museum, with nothing, and then later the local library, also nothing. Now, I’d started at a university in another city called Drammen, not far from where I’m from. One day, I had some extra time on my hands and decided to try the local museum in Drammen. But yet again, no information there either.

My campus also houses the city’s local library. I figured, why not, and decided to look there as well, and this time I actually found something: an old book.

It was quite serendipitous, but it seemed to have fallen behind some shelves and drawers, and was only a shadow that I noticed by the local history section. It was a book on Viken, which was a large region by the south-eastern Norwegian coast during the Viking Age. The book detailed all the counties and municipalities in the area at the time the book was published, which was in 1957. Vestfold had a really minor section, but yes, yes, finally I had a list! A list of names of municipalities before the mergers in the 60s and 80s! Translated excerpt:

>"…here you find the cities \[…\]: *Svelvik*, *Holmestrand*, *Horten*, *Åsgårdstrand*, *Tønsberg*, *Sandefjord*, *Larvik* and *Stavern*. In addition, you find the district municipalities from along the Dramsfjord down to the Skagerrak: *Skoger*, *Strømm*, *Sande*, *Botne*, *Våle*, *Borre*, *Sem*, *Nøtterøy*, *Tjøme*, *Stokke*, *Sandar*, *Tjølling* and *Brunlanes*. Inwards into the county you find the agricultural municipalities: *Hof*, *Ramnes*, *Andebu*, *Lardal*, *Glenne* and *Hedrum*."

"Glenne".

A name I’d definitely never heard of before, and I guess few others have since the 60s as well. The book didn’t mention anything more than the name, and no map in regard to its location, but it was more than enough. I finally had something.

I tried to look up everything I could on Glenne, but it wasn’t much. The problem from before was that I hadn’t had any name to look up, but now I finally hoped for answers.

I found one page that made vague references to something akin to Glenne and decided to follow it.

It went several pages deep, so far that I think nobody ever cared to look for it, and that was my chance. It became evident at this point that someone or something had tried to cover up Glenne by some means. No school books, maps, papers, or books in the last decades mentioned anything about Glenne. And the weird thing was that I couldn’t even find any of those from before the 60s either. The only exception had been the randomly forgotten book I’d found at the library. This only further strengthened the theory about this seeming like some sort of cover-up.

Luckily, this webpage was it, and a whole new but hidden world opened up to me. It was through this page that I found the information about the place that I’ve already shared here. And I found where it was located.

I finally realized I’d found a hidden municipality that can’t be traced back to books or papers. Something that the majority of people, I’d assume, don’t know about.

It’s hard to explain why it’s so… "spooky", I guess you’d say. It’s so out of the ordinary of what I’m used to. Here I had the guidance to something that was just waiting to be unearthed, and it was closer than I’d come to realize. Did I dare to do this? Not just stop here and tell people?

No. Something is weird; you don’t just "close down" a municipality like that. I had to find out why. I just had to go there myself. A real and yet unknown place that presumably just sits out there in the woods.


I went there on a Tuesday, which is the day of the week we’re allowed to study from home, a perfect day for a hike out. I borrowed my father’s old Ford and drove off to Lardal.

It was a chilly morning at the end of summer. It had rained the night before, so the road and grass were slick and wet as I went. A few puddles showed up here and there. I’d never been to Lardal before, so I had some initial confusion about the right way, but in the end, I made it there. The plan was originally to bring along a partner in crime, a friend I’d made at school in Drammen, but the day before the trip, he bailed due to sickness. I was really determined to go, so I ended up going alone and didn’t want to bother anybody else about it.

I was in the right municipality, now it was just about finding the place. According to the documents I’d found, there was a detail to take notice of.

Back in the 60s, when Lardal and Glenne merged, the old road that went into Glenne was rerouted to go past just south of it. It didn’t disclose what had happened to the part that originally went through Glenne, but trying to follow it would thus lead me the wrong way.

The map I mentioned was outdated even by the time it got published. The municipalities at the time would merge after the book I’d found was published. The borders have changed over the years, even before the map was drawn, so I didn’t have any precise location for my destination, but it had to be in the area.

When I first came to the outskirts of the supposed area, I thought I’d found it right away, but it was too good to be true. I’d mistaken a bare area behind the meadows for a road and thought that it could be the old road, but no. All the roads that went off to private properties didn’t help either. If I couldn’t find it, then I couldn’t, but that didn’t stop me from trying. I was here after all.

And eventually, I found the traces of it.

Along the road was an old dirt road that turned sharply to the left and continued well into the forest. It looked far older than the other dirt roads I’d passed.

I parked the car by the main road. I couldn’t get any further by car because a boom barrier was blocking the way. I left the car by the barrier and walked around it, and began my venture into this almost paranormal forest. The barrier did not seem new.

I’d already walked so far into this forest along the old dirt road that I couldn’t even see the car or the barrier behind me any longer. This place really creeped me out. First of all, I didn’t even know what awaited me, and second, this forest was spooky, man! Constantly, I’d look behind me, not in fear of someone or something, but to look for my way out.

Shortly after, the path and the old tire marks in the ground started to disappear, and turned to a narrow slope. I presume you’d walk from here, whatever it was. Within just a few meters in front of me, I came over my first real obstacle. A giant fence.

A tall chain-linked metal fence that stretched far into the deep ends of the forest, and as far as I could see. Now, if I hadn’t been creeped out already, this was definitely the time. Fear struck me – what was this? My heart started racing, and my legs were getting wobbly. What was this long-ass fence doing out here in the middle of the forest? Just an empty metal fence. I held my breath. You could barely hear it, the quiet sound of metal shivering as the wind blew through the holes.

Like the boom barrier, the fence wasn’t new either. It was rusty and flaky, but was still intact, in good shape, and didn’t seem to have been bent or pried open.

From where I stood I could see a door in the fence. I had already made up my mind about doing this thing and therefore wanted to continue; it might be my only chance. The door was locked, of course, and the lock hadn’t been opened in a couple of years it seemed, though it looked like a newer model. It was all rusty and worn, and even with a gentle pull, it came right off. Just think if that lock had been there since the 60s, though it was hard to tell its age.

Inside the fence, there was nothing more than grass and trees, as I had seen from the other side. I closed the door behind me. Not locking it, but made sure that it was easy enough to open, should I need to make a hasty escape. Far in front of me, the forest turned dark from the overgrown trees, almost as if the trees were closing its roof off from prying eyes.

It took some time, but now I was really far into the forest – in the deep heart of Vestfold – and every two minutes or so I’d start to overthink and wonder if I should turn back the way I came, but I tried to shake it off. Suddenly, the dark grass wasn’t just grass anymore.

Right in front of me, an especially overgrown road turned up. It had to be it, the old dirt road, it was still here, and was all mossy and cracked from all the sprouts. This was a major breakthrough. The old documents seemed to be right; something was here before it got cut off from the rest of the world.

I walked on the road, and let it guide me to what would be Glenne ahead of me. Even if all the signs were there, I was still uncertain if the place was actually real. Still, no matter what state the place was in, I had found proof that something used to be here, at least. I felt so close as if I could almost hear the sound of cars driving in the distance, or did they really?

Then, as if to prove my speculations, I found what I considered to be *actual* proof of the place. I got to what must’ve been an old intersection, where another road just went straight off to the right, into some trees. I saw some poles that I guess originally used to be signs. The tops were either bent and broken off, or possibly sawed off. I guess it was for navigating the intersection.

However, the big one was that shortly after, an actual house turned up! I got so hyped. This was real; it was all real, and I might just be the only one who knows about it!

The house, or the remains of it, was all dark, rotten, destroyed, and reclaimed by nature. In truth, it might’ve been a shed of some sort for all I could make out. Even though I was so happy that this place turned out to be real, a part of me quickly came to its senses and realized the possible dangers. I remembered that this was in no way normal at all, and that I should stay alert. I had just gone through a huge fence on the way here, as well as on a road that had been dug up and rerouted.

I walked up to the building, unsure if I should enter it and see the state of the interior or not. I decided to do it. The afternoon sky grew darker, and the uneasy overgrown roof of the forest made it all the more dim.

The inside was all upside down. Some furniture was still there, though kicked around. The living room smelled rotten, and I didn’t stay for long. Though I couldn’t make my way over, and it was all decayed, it looked like pictures and a few family portraits still hung on the walls. A lot of debris lay spread on the floor. Animals had most likely been there in the years the house had stood empty.

After following the road some more, I hit the jackpot, an actual street, with more houses located along the road I was walking on. A few cars lied around, some in more peculiar positions than others. I walked to a car that was upside down. The roof was all dented, and the car looked like it could crumble from just a gentle breath. I backed away in fear of crushing something that potentially lived inside in.

I tried to cross the garden on the way to another house. However, the ground was not a solid plain any longer; and as soon as I stepped on it, it turned to a mush, and I jumped back onto the road. The recent rain hadn’t helped the ground either, and the overgrown road was full of puddles.

The houses all looked the same: overgrown by plants, rotting, falling apart; with stained, broken windows that showed nothing but dark, empty spaces. I could hear the wind howling through every creak, which when combined made it sound like a haunted, out-of-tune choir. My heart started racing out of fear.

I heard some leaves rustle behind me. My body stiffened, and I could hear the thundering heart in my ears. My mouth went all dry – and my hair tightened its grip on my scalp. I turned around slowly. I couldn’t see anything, but then, far out behind a house, a large bird was jumping around, minding its own business before flying off. My body sank to the ground in relief, and my mind went all dazed.

Although harmless, I didn’t take any chances; for all I knew, the waters in the area could have become toxic over the years and changed all the animals.

I tried to shake it off me and dared to continue a bit further. Continuing along the road were overgrown streets with houses that were either broken down or just about to. And all these cars that just lay around. The houses weren’t many, and there was a considerable distance between them. This could not have been a heavily populated area. As I got closer to the houses, I noticed something that definitely should not have been there.

Sounds. But not just any kind of sounds.

The sound of something electric, something with the power still on. I couldn’t place it, but somewhere, something emitted a low electric hum that stood out from the howling winds. I freaked out. Normally, I would been running all the way back home from seeing the metal fence, and in a split second, I couldn’t understand how I’d managed to stay for so long.

Though with the panic alarming in my ears, I tried to discuss with myself what to do next, trying not to make a sound. I agreed with myself to just walk around the corner, to try and see if I could get a better view down along the road, and then turn back and call it a day.

I tried to look if I could see where the sound was coming from. I walked up to what looked like it had been some sort of gas station once, but through the years, there weren’t really any signs left of it actually being one, except for all the broken glass lying around – even the pumps were gone.

I neared a door; the sound became louder with every step, though it was still faint. The door had some signs showing something about power and electricity. I carefully touched the rusty handle, and after a second of building up nerves, I opened it.

Behind the door was a room or cabinet I guess, full of electrical units and fuses. This was definitely the source of the electric hum. Then it all went quiet.

It took some seconds for me to realize, but the sound had actually stopped.

Before doing anything drastic, I tried to listen if it was just me, or maybe if the sound had gone quieter, but no, it had stopped, as if it was turned off.

I backed away in terror. Almost tripped on a plank in the crumbling driveway. It was getting hard to breathe. I had to make it out of here. Everything was getting darker around me, as if the sun was already setting.

I sprinted around the gas station and turned down onto the street I’d come from when I saw something in the distance. Around the corner of a house, something flickered. I thought that it might be the sunlight that shone through a couple of trees, but I was wrong.

It was a light. First, I’d found power; now I’d found sources of light.

There was a light on in one of the windows. On the second floor, in one of the few houses that still had an upper floor that still stood upright. I had just walked by that house, and there had definitely not been any light on in there. It flickered, as if it was just turned on, or was a bit indecisive to whether it should turn on or not.

As it turned out, several of the houses I had walked by now had lights on in their windows, both floors, simultaneously! Living room lamps. Porch lights. Lamps in some of the windows. Even some from the windows in the basement, barely visible amongst the overgrowth. It felt like nighttime under the cover of this forest. What I hope was another bird or a squirrel moved from one tree to another. The lights flickered in the reflection in the puddles. All these lights, all these hums.

I didn’t even think any longer. I’d had enough! I was really scared. Scared for my life.

Ever since I first came here, nothing had seemed right. I looked for my way back. No matter what may pique my interest, I was out of here; running!

I left the lights in the windows behind me. As I ran, I happened to catch the look of something, something that unknowingly made me slow down to look. Something glimmered in a puddle on the road; it was a reflection of something from a house. The wind blew over the water and made gentle waves, which distorted the reflection. I tilted my head, trying to decipher it. I couldn’t make out what it was.

A branch snapped by the house. I instantly turned my head to look. I realized that I’d stopped in my tracks. Almost expecting to be launched at by something, I saw nothing. I turned back, and the thing in the window was gone.

It was in this moment of silence, this moment of solitude, that I heard it: the whispers.

Behind me, I could hear some faint whispers that I couldn’t make out, but it sounded like a conversation, and it was not the howling wind. It was certainly not a mechanical sound; it was organic. It could have been the wind blowing through something and maybe emitting an echo, but no. The conversation lasted for barely a second or so before it stopped. The only word I could make out as an actual word was "somebody".

I ran for my life. I don’t think I’ve ever run so fast before, certainly a new personal best. My legs hurt, my laces had come undone, but my mind convinced them to continue on. The whispers disappeared behind me, and I couldn’t hear anything anymore.

I left all the houses and the streets, left them here to rot. At this point, I didn’t know what to do; there was only one thing on my mind: escape!

I couldn’t stop running until all I could see was the empty forest again. The old, unpaved road started to disappear underneath me, and I knew I was close to getting out. The sound of hard steps on gravel soon changed to the sound of grass hurriedly trampled on.

Far in front of me, the fence thankfully started to appear. I was so happy. I’d had enough of this loneliness. I didn’t even process it at the time, but I think I heard some sounds of what I hope was distant animals that ran away in terror from my sudden appearance, but I don’t know for sure. Whatever it actually was, at least they ran away from me and had not followed me from within the depths of the forest.

I reached the fence. My body was shaking after the running, but also from the fright. The door stood wide open as if it welcomed me to leave. This was for sure a ghost town. I could still hear the whispers in my head. I hastily made my way through the door, and as I did so, I remembered that this was not the state I’d left the door in.

I had closed the door; now it was wide open, and no wind could have pushed this heavy door open. I quickly closed it behind me and looked for the broken lock lying in the grass. I found the lock and slammed it in place, trying to lock it, even though it was broken. My hands trembled as I tried to do everything as fast as possible. I was already sweating from both the running and the panic, and I felt lightheaded.

I backed away as if I was expecting something that had chased me to crash into the fence. But nothing, just the wind squeezing through each hole in the metal fence. Through the wires, there was nothing more than a silent, empty forest that housed a deep secret.

I understood it; the fence, the power, the lights, the whispers. Something lived here. Something was locked in here.

After barely catching my breath, I continued to run on the path and did not dare to look behind me – this was goodbye. After running and later jogging, I could see the boom barrier where I’d left the car. My mind and body were still struggling to come to terms with what I’d seen: a hidden, abandoned ghost town with power still on, lights on in the windows, and the voices, whatever they were.

It was a moment of relief when I finally exited the forest and could take a deep breath of fresh air in the daylight. The sun was still up, though it hadn’t really felt like it under the tall trees in there.

By the barrier, I turned to look back into the forest; there were no signs of me actually having entered it at one point. For everyone who’d pass by, nothing would seem out of the ordinary; nothing would seem to be missing.

I walked up to the car, unlocked it, got in the driver’s seat, and started to shake and tear up.

I drove off, taking quick glances behind me in the rear-view mirrors. I drove a couple of miles maybe, before I suddenly slowed down and stood still on the road. I was still all alone and felt as far away from civilization as I could come. This was too much. This whole thing was all fucked up. What should I do? After witnessing all this, I didn’t dare to think of actually telling anybody about this, something that I’d already done, unfortunately.

I sighed and started the car again. The radio played outdated hits from another time. I left this place, hopefully for good. It was only later, after I’d driven for a couple of minutes, that I came to realize that the car had been facing the opposite way from where I’d left it.

I try not to think about it too much.
