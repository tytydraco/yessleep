I’ve never been good with money. Shit, there’s an understatement. It’s not really my fault though, I tell myself. I’m part of that class of people who did everything society asked of us. Call us what you will, the lost generation, millennials, zillenials, gen-z, zoomers, gloomers; the point is we got served a shitsandwich for our efforts, along with $69,075 in student debt—which doesn’t get forgiven even when you declare bankruptcy, by the by, but I digress.

Evidently, I didn’t invest in silver or gold or oil futures, didn’t park a cool 10% of my monthly income dollar-cost-averaged into the S&P 500 back when the going was good because I was too busy being four years old. Apart from being handpicked by God to be too young to ride the dotcom boom and just old enough to be financially obliterated into AI redundancy, I’d say things are going pretty swell. These are the things that go through your mind when you’re apartment hunting—well, my mind anyway.

I navigate the bureaucratic labyrinth of credit checks, loan approvals, mommy and daddy’s cosign, come up with a cash deposit—a sweaty $100 bill that I gingerly slip to an austere building manager, pennies and bits of lint spilling out of my pocket in the process. When I walk into the apartment building, I’m greeted by a sea of nauseating green carpeting, decades of cigarette burns and bum piss enmeshed into the fibers, a faded array of yellow lamps overhead. I believe the architectural term is ‘shithole’, but I’d have to look that one up. Elevator’s out of service, of course. I heft my belongings up seven flights in three trips.

There are three roommates, which isn’t too bad, and one of them might not even be a complete sociopath. In my cloistered room, I unwrap a folded piece of paper—the ad for the apartment that I ripped off the bulletin board not two weeks prior—and curse myself. The overworked inkjet left a devious splatter mark, but on closer inspection I see it now; what I thought said ‘furnished’ was actually ‘unfurnished’. My room is nothing but a pile of boxes, contents untidily strewn across the floor, the smell of freshly-laid rat poison ambling into my sinus cavities.

The next day, with my back screaming, I walk into Joe’s Mattress Acropolis on 35th and Fitz. Motors whirr, mocking me as the automatic doors slide open. Would you be surprised to hear there’s mattresses here? Mounds of them. Big zigzag-cut yellow stickers assure me of the low-low prices.

A man emerges from the back of the store—the eponymous Joe, presumably, though he doesn’t look particularly Greek. He’s wearing maroon slacks, a short-sleeve button-down shirt tucked into the waistband, straining against a gut that spills over the belt line, and a toupée that isn’t fooling anybody.

“Anything I can help you with?” he asks, a salesman’s crescent smile.

For a moment, I look around and it’s only now that I realize the store is empty. Under the flickering fluorescence, Joe waits patiently for me to answer, his grin seemingly growing as the seconds pass.

“Yeah, need a mattress.” I say, as if they sold anything else.

Joe—though I don’t see a nametag—claps his hands together. “Indeed I can help you with that, young man.” He fires a swift fingergun in my direction. “Say, what kind of sleeper are you?”

“Just…regular, I guess.”

“Are you a side-sleeper?”

“No.”

“Belly-sleeper, then? Back-sleeper? Topsy-turvy? Hooded wombat, perchance?”

“What was that last one?” I shake my head. “Back-sleeper, I guess.”

“Aha!” Fingerguns again. “I thought you had that look about you. How about any ailments, special needs? Scoliosis?”

“Really, I just need a mattress.” Looking around, the low-low prices don’t seem to dip under $299, I realize with flowering anxiety. My eyes find the charlatan’s again and I’m almost too embarrassed to ask. “You don’t have any used mattresses, do you?”

“We prefer the term, ‘pre-owned’,” Joe says, suddenly deadly serious. The smile comes back. “Of course. If you’ll follow me.”

Joe waves me past the styrofoam ionic pillars and we walk toward the back of the mattress store.

There's a stack of bare yellowed mattresses balanced on top of each other like some sort of an unholy jenga tower, teetering. A get the faint smell of sandalwood—cheap air freshener. Another odor underneath it too that I can’t quite place.

As he watches me, Joe pushes up his glasses with an index finger. “A single starts at $99,” he says and as he does the light flickers overhead just for an instant. A sense of dread creeps through me, so subtle that I don’t even realize it at first. “Whoops, sorry about that.” Joe chuckles. “Been meaning to see about that tube.”

“Look, man, I have like maybe sixty on me. Can you help me out or what?”

“Hmh.” He strokes his chin for a moment. “I might have just the thing.”

Deeper into the bowels of Joe’s Mattress Acropolis, I follow, and again there’s that creeping dread. It pitches as we turn down a drop-ceilinged hallway. Then we’re in a stockroom, dust motes suspended in a sliver of light from a murky window.

“If you can haul it off, you can have it,” Joe says, all the affectation of the earlier salesman gone and I have to look at him to make sure I’m still talking to the same person.

“The place is empty.” The words come out harsher than I mean. Is this some type of sick joke? It’s bad enough I’m broke, now this asshole’s got to pull my leg.

Joe clears his throat and points half-heartly to the corner. And I see it—a queen-size mattress, barely illuminated. I take a closer look and apart from a layer of dust, I don’t see any noticeable shit stains or streaks of jism. I wouldn’t want a blacklight anywhere near this thing, though.

I get my buddy Julio to load it into his pick-up, give my thanks to Joe, and that’s that. But as I’m riding in the passenger seat of Julio’s 2004 Toyota Hilux—the mattress propped between a weed-wacker and half a bag of cement—I remember something; the look in Joe’s eye just before I left. There was no charlatan character then, just…something. Probably nothing. And then there was that creeping dread I felt, announcing itself, building the deeper we pushed into the store. Again, probably nothing there.

Julio stops at a bar and I forget all about it after my first sip of Keystone Light as we work through the sixty bucks I just saved. Like I said, I’ve never been good with money.

Later, we clumsily schlep the mattress up the seven flights of stairs to my apartment, though in my stupor it feels more like seventeen. We plop it down to my bedroom floor with a thud. A drift of dust mists off of it to a chorus of coughs. I spend the next ten minutes fruitlessly beating out what I can. Then finally, sleep.

Just as I doze off, the radiator waxing an elegy of white noise, the remaining ethanol molecules wrestling with the enzymes in my liver, I can tell something is wrong.

An undulating purple sky; that’s the first thing I see. It seems to be breathing—something alive. There are other colors too; periwinkle? No, some unearthly spectrum. I can’t say how, but it knows I’m here. There’s something else I feel.

*Don’t look behind you.*

The firmament ripples. Wobbling bands of light warp and coalesce in concentric rings, coming to a point in the center of the sky. The breathing slows.

Okay, too much Keystone Light. It’s a dream, and dreams can be woken up from. I look down. Ghastly white willows writhing in an endless field. There’s a faint cackling, a laughter. I hadn’t heard it up until now. It’s inhuman to the ears—a whining voice that hits me in multiple octaves at once. It grows closer, and it’s now that I realize the cackling belongs to the sky. It’s pulsing in time with it; a pendulous throbbing as the rings in the sky fold in…and in…and in.

And I see that it’s beautiful.

The laughter lulls me. I find myself swaying along with the willows, enraptured. Everything belongs to the sky; I see it now. Entwined in the rhythm, I don’t care that a root coils around my leg or how loud the laughter has grown. I want to laugh too—laugh and laugh, join in the tenors and squeaks. The willow wants to play. Oh, here’s another one. The branch caresses my hand as we sway languidly arm-in-arm. My body is weightless. I could do this forever. All is well. All is—

Beneath the hypnotic sensation, beneath the pulsating sky and the wash of laughter, I feel it again.

*Don’t look behind you.*

But I do look. In the treeshade afforded by the naked willows, brisk movement. It doesn’t want to be seen. All I see is a glimpse of antlers, and it’s gone, occluded by the denser woods where the willows are taller, but writhing all the same. Then again, a glimpse—a tendon, a hoof? I understand it’s the source of the cackling, of the laughter. It breaks my trance and now—Christ.

Barked tendrils feel their way all over me—wrapping, constricting. I pull back and hear the sky roar. It shakes the trees, pulsing, beating faster. They are afraid, the willows—stiff now where they were fluid and free. It buys me a second to get away.

I beat through the field, away from the dense woods, away from the source of the sounds that I now know to be terrifying. Tall grass bristling underfoot. The cackling wanes the further I run—along with its hold on my mind. And even as the angry sky chastises me, concentric rings strobing now, I weave through the sparse woods. My shoulder brushes past a protruding branch, a dagger of pain roils, but I run. The world fades.

I awake in a sheen of sweat. That was some fucking dream.

I take a shower, a torrent of tepid water covers me. It feels like I haven’t slept at all; body pains all over. But what can you expect from a free mattress? I step out for my towel, and a bolt of fear goes through me as I catch my reflection in the mirror. A pink gash on my shoulder right where the branch clipped me in the dream.

An image returns; white willows dancing beneath a purple sky.

Looking in the mirror, I bat my eyes as if to clear the illusion, but there it is—a trail of broken skin across my deltoid. This doesn’t make any sense.

It’s extremely common for physical sensations in the real world to manifest themselves in a dream. It’s a state called hypnopompia. Waking sleep. Still, that doesn’t explain the cut. I must have scratched myself absently. It’s not unheard of, only it’s never happened to me before. I look all this up at work, ingesting three cups of coffee by lunchtime. By 3PM, I can hardly keep my eyes open; I swear I’ve had better sleep on the bare floorboards.

By the time I clock out, the world is a hazy blur. My eyelids feel like they’re made of lead and, at this point, I’m about ready to crash and burn. A homeless man on the block rattles a can of beans at me. Sorry, guy.

At home, I fall into the mattress. Really fall—asleep-before-you-hit-the-pillow fall.

And awake to a purple sky.

A jolt. I’m here again and I am scared. I must remember that I am scared.

I don’t look up at the sky this time—the hypnotic throbbing. Instead I focus on the willows and their sickeningly curled branches. They’re moving again, wormlike. My shoulders follow their rhythm, almost as if tuning in. No. Have to make a run for it again. I spy last night’s trail through the tall grass, a frantically painted path, the dips and twists exactly as I made them. It shouldn’t be there. No, it shouldn’t be there. Of course, I didn’t actually run through that path, it was only a—

*Don’t look behind you.*

There’s that feeling again. How long have I been swaying? And how long has the laughter been back for—that demonic cackling? It’s at this point that I look down and see the ground, but too far below me. I am lifted by the trees. It’s…it’s actually kind of soothing, yes. A gentle caress; a touch and a kiss, really. Truly floating. Oh, and the sky—purple and fuschia and mauve, rippling, ready to receive me. We all belong to the sky. Everything belongs to the sky.

A turn and a twist of the branch, just a sprinkle of pain. Little rough there, boys. Hehe, it’s funny up here. I’m laughing with the woods, with the creature I saw and that I know is there now, inching ever closer.

Twist and twist and—ah!

The willows pull on all of my limbs, splaying me wide, further and further apart. The pain comes in spurts, small icicles shooting up my leg, my arm, and I forget what was so funny. Still, the creature throws its *yips* and *squawks*; an inside joke between itself and willows and the sky above.

The pain works itself up to a height and icicles now feel hard and fibrous. And I can hear the footfalls coming up behind me; soft staccato padding through the rustling grass. I remember now; I am scared.

The look of him—or it, whatever eldritch thing stands before me—is hard to describe. What I catch immediately is the sweet, sick carrion smell of death. The beast is tall, hunching eye-level with me even at this height where I’m lifted by the branches. And yes, it is laughing, laughing at me. So many mouths; a mouth next to an ear, another mouth where an eye just was. I watch the milky eyeball roll and get gnashed into a goo. Every mouth is laughing, howling, cackling, all in a different pitch. A hundred mouths, a thousand; they develop on its grotesque body, growing in and out of it. Mouth’s inside mouths. Peeling flesh everywhere, and it’s all I can do to keep from retching arcs of vomit through the air.

A cloven hoof reaches for me, and just past the rotting, twisted form, I see a glimpse of the sky. It’s absolutely purring in joy. It wants this to happen. Actually, this is good, it says.

I find myself.

And I scream. My one mouth against his millions. It is a sound I can’t recognize within myself as I empty my lungs. The shape of the sky changes—angry again. That’s good.

The trees shirk away in fear, and even the creature takes a step back. A chance.

I pull, I yank.

One toe, one foot, one leg. Free.

The creature, in the dim, ghoulish light, rages. A hoof comes again and I kick with my free leg—sickly, slippery contact. It feels cold, but it staggers the beast. I work an arm loose, then another, and fall, careening upside down. A willow holds me by the ankle. God, it looks so much more horrific upside down. And from here, I see that the sky is scrambling. I do not belong to you—I hurl my thoughts at the sky. It doesn’t like my answer. You will see, it groans back at me.

I wriggle against the branch. Yes. I fall fast.

A crash upon the grass and then I haul fucking ass.

I move in a blur, just making out my previous path, and retrace it through the thicket. But the creature—the disgusting hooved thing of mouths—charges after me. I can hear its trotting steps, feel them reverberating through the false earth. And of course the laughter—an angry, wailing thing now. I rush and my footing is true. I weave and I sidestep, whisking past the willows.

I don’t know how long I run for, but eventually I see the world fade at the recesses of my vision; a cone narrowing.

I return.

It takes a few moments for me to believe I’m back, the city skyline glowing assuredly through the bedroom window. My head is heavy as I roll off the mattress and slowly my heartbeat quiets itself. I’m really back. There’s a coating of slime on my foot, right where I struck the creature. The slime is oily and saccharine, cold to the touch as I run a finger through it.

A couple hours have gone by and I still can’t make any sense of what happened. There are only two things about the mattress that I know to be true. They come to me in a series of thoughts.

First, I’m sure none of it was a dream. Everything was too visceral, too real. I know that I was there. The cut on my shoulder, the slime. You can’t bring things back from a dream. The mattress is a portal to the purple sky.

Then the second thought comes, and even in my sleep-deprived state it curls the short hairs on my neck.

I must not go back to sleep.
