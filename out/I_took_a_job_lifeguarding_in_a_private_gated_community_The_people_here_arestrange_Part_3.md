I was working a night shift the second week of August, a few days after the incident at the Warren house.

I was getting a little stir crazy, so I decided to walk around the pool a little bit. I was fine if I didn’t touch the water, and there was no service that night. I had gotten into the habit of doing so as of recently, and I found it to be rather refreshing.

I was about halfway across when I noticed a movement in the haze.

A long black shape, gliding slowly far below, far below five feet.

Looked almost… like a tentacle or a tendril, as if something was feeling around. 

Kind of just… probing.

I stood there, frozen.

Couldn’t move, even if I had wanted to.

Couldn’t blink, either.

The shape slowly rose up though, and began to take shape as it emerged from the depth.

A woman, with a slim build. My blood ran cold as I realized she seemed to be wearing what looked like a ceremonial gown, black and heavy. A serious safety hazard, but that was the least of my worries now. Mainly, it was the fact that I had somehow missed a woman entering the pool this late at night. That she was still alive despite it being fatal at this hour was an entirely different matter.

She took one or two strokes, then glided into the wall before coming up for air. I would say she was in her mid-30s. Red hair. Attractive. The dress looked less like a gown now and more like a casual black cover-up dress. Like she’d gotten there, realized she’d somehow forgotten to put her bathing suit on underneath, and decided to get in anyway.

“Hi- can I help you?”

I realized I had been staring.

She looked up at me. A kind, but expectant expression on her face.

“Sorry. Just - you can’t be here this late at night. We closed hours ago, and the system we use to clean this part of the pool is incredibly dangerous.

Oh, and I’m sure there’s, um, a reason for it, but that dress is a serious drowning hazard, especially in the deep end.”

She nodded, seeming to understand.

“I’m very sorry. This is what I usually wear to swim - I have very sensitive skin, you see - so I figured I would be fine.

Give me one moment. I’ll get out, dry off, and be on my way.”

I walked back to the guard office and sat down, wanting to give her some privacy. But something didn’t sit right with me.

For one, she said that was what she usually wore to the pool.

But I hadn’t seen her once since I’d started there, and I was at the point where I recognized everyone.

That, and the whole matter of her being in the pool in the first place this late at night.

My train of thought was interrupted by the woman, who now stood in the doorway.

“Hi - I wanted to apologize again for startling you.”

“You’re quite alright. Don’t worry about it.”

“I also wanted to introduce myself. I’m Abigail. Pastor Josiah - you may have met him - is my husband.”

She extended her hand. I got up and shook it.

“It’s very nice to meet you, Mrs. Patterson.”

She smiled pleasantly. “Likewise….?”

“Luke.”

“Enjoy the rest of your evening, Luke.”

With that, she left the pool.

No longer wanting to sit in the guard room, I found a lounge chair and set it in front. Lay back, looked at the sky, and tried not to think for once.

Unfortunately for me, a thought rose to my mind immediately - an unwelcome one at that.

When Mrs. Patterson approached the deck, and shook my hand, it had been maybe five minutes since our initial interaction. Sure, maybe she had changed into a dry dress on deck, and she could have had a portable hair dryer of some sort with her.

No, no. None of that logic worked.

Because when she approached me, and we shook hands, she was bone dry.

No hint of even the damp cling on her dress when you get changed quickly after swimming.

Not a single damp curl in her red hair.

As though she had never gotten in the pool in the first place.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

The rest of August passed without incident. Cody’s absence was felt, but we all tried to ignore it the best we could. As far as Vitaly was concerned, he’d never existed in the first place.

Things went smoothly enough that we began discussing a year-end party, for which we were even able to get Vitaly on board. He thought he might be able to pull some strings and have the deep end open that night. Since there were only about eight of us, he would even see about allowing us to invite people. I don’t know who he thought he was kidding with that - my social circle for the last 2 months had largely consisted of him, Cody, Vik, and Ricky - but I decided to let him run with his little fantasy.

After a little bargaining with Craft, they came to an agreement.

Craft’s wife’s 50th birthday was coming up.

She’d apparently grown up in a strict, deeply religious household, and because she was homeschooled had not gotten to have a prom. So, we’d all be throwing her an adult prom for her birthday. The pool would be closed, but it would be all hands-on deck, because the party was going to be an all-out affair. Open bar. Appetizers. Open buffet. It sounded more like a wedding reception than a 50th birthday party, but who was I to judge? For all I knew, they were going to renew their vows that night too.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

The night of the party came. There was a funny juxtaposition to it - the 50 or so guests, all in their tuxes and gowns, milling around the pool with tiny plates and drinks, and then the eight of us guards, all matching in our forest green Riley Estates polos, black dress pants, and black dress shoes.

The night went by without incident. I could tell just from the way they looked that both Craft and his wife - Janie - were having a dream of a night. She was glowing, and he was basking in her spotlight.

That all changed when it came time for the birthday cake, in the clubhouse.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

Yes, I know what you’re thinking.

And no, no one tried to smash her face into the cake when she went to blow out the candles. She had had her hair and makeup done professionally. Had someone tried that, the shotgun from the mantle would have been in their face the second they so much as moved in her direction.

They cut her the first slice, as things like that usually go.

She took a fork and went to cut into the slice.

And there was a tiny, but audible *clink* as her fork suddenly struck something hard, sucking the air out of the room.

As if not sure what had just happened, she again went to cut into the cake, and her fork was once again stopped.

*Clink.*

With a perfectly manicured hand, she slowly pinched, then plucked an object out of the cake as her face wrinkled in confusion. It was covered in cake and frosting, but it wasn’t hard to make out what it was.

A ring.

Specifically a class ring, from the looks of it one of those overpriced ones they try to scam you with your junior year of high school, as though a $300 piece of jewelry is the perfect way to commemorate it.

In a way, it was weirdly appropriate. 

*Happy belated prom night! Here’s a class ring!*

Without so much as skipping a beat, Craft strode over and plucked it from her hand as though it were nothing more than a plastic fork that had broken inside the cake. Quickly walked over to the sink across the room, cleaned it off, then dropped it in his pocket.

He let out an upbeat “please, continue!” As though nothing had happened.

And the night went on. 

As though, indeed, *nothing had happened*.

The party ended right around 1am. As we cleaned up, I saw him hug Vitaly, drink still in hand, and thank him for helping to make the night so special. Couldn’t tell whether he genuinely was happy, or if he was silently seething and trying to figure out who was responsible for the sudden appearance of the ring.

And if another one of my coworkers would go missing as a result.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I wound up getting the last Saturday shift of the year - the week before the party. I had both the morning and night shifts, but the afternoon off. I found myself almost wishing I was just working the full 24 hours.

I arrived just as Vitaly was leaving the office. He nodded at me, then continued in his way. Not a word. Made me wonder if I was being scapegoated for the ring, and they figured this was a good punishment.

I walked in, noticing something immediately - Vitaly’s email was still visible on the screen. Unable to suppress my curiosity, I walked over.

I only glimpsed a few things before the email window closed.

“Enhance the system for next year.”

After that, “responsible for the incident with Tim’s ring.”

Finally, “already been three months!”

Three months was right around when we started.

It wasn’t hard to connect the dots after that.

I had a strong feeling we would be down another lifeguard after that night.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I noticed something else on the screen shortly thereafter.

A small icon on the bottom, that looked like a camera. I clicked it, and gasped.

A small screen filled the display, showing the space immediately in front of the guard office.

I now seemingly had a way to watch the weekly services. But did I have the courage to watch, knowing something could happen to me?

A noise from outside the office interrupted my train of thought.

Chanting.

Dread washed over me as I realized the service had started. It covered me like a blanket, and I realized I was frozen in place when I tried to turn off the monitor, suddenly wanting no part of what was going on.

It seemed my decision had been made for me.

I watched the screen.

I watched as the sound of chanting slowly made its way through the building, coming outside.

I watched as it made its way down, coming closer.

And then I watched, unable to perceive what I was looking at, as the sound came to a stop immediately outside my window…

….and yet nothing appeared on camera.

It went on for a full hour. 

The chanting. 

That awful keening, which by now I knew deep down meant that someone’s chapter was coming to a close.

I sat there, frozen in place.

Able to hear but not see.

Able to think, to feel, but not move, no matter how badly I wanted to.

When it finally ended, I blinked.

Felt something damp on my face. Put a hand to my cheek.

Realized I had silently been crying.

I sat there silently for God knows how long afterwards, trying to make sense of what had just happened. Noted with a grim lack of surprise as the early dawn light began to blind my eyes that the blinds had, once again, somehow been raised without my knowledge.

Looking back at incidents like this, and the loss of Cody and other things, I’ve asked myself why I never once considered quitting and taking a new job or just swallowing my pride and moving back home, and while simple and somewhat stupid, I think it’s something like this:

I had spent so much of my life running away from things as soon as they got hard, that I decided that for once I was going to finish something out, no matter what it cost. As for…practical reasons, anything seemed better than facing my parents after throwing $30k down the drain.

When Vitaly came to relieve me, I walked past him without a look or a word. I think he knew what to expect.

From the brief glance I gave him, I think he also knew what had happened the previous night, and who it had been.

I didn’t ask.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

I went home. Crawled into bed without showering.

I slept for 12 hours.

I didn’t dream.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

The guard party came, on schedule, a week later. It almost felt as though I had gone to sleep and slept for seven days straight.

We closed the pool early that day, around 5pm, although the residents didn’t seem to care. It was overcast and cloudy.

Ricky and I got there about an hour early - 8 or so - to set up everything. The kegs, the wine and liquor coolers, and the food.

The rest of the guards arrived at 9 or so. I couldn’t tell, but I had a bad feeling we were down a person.

I looked at Ricky. “I count seven. Shouldn’t there be eight?”

He did a once over. Nodded grimly. “Yup. Seven.”

Then, at 9:15 - fashionably late - Viktoria walked in.

She looked like a dream.

Loose curls flowed down her back. Her face looked as though she had hired a professional to do it. Her grey dress hugged her like a second skin.

It was only later that I realized she looked identical to that dream I had had, so long ago.

She had left me totally and completely entranced, to the point where I didn’t realize I had been staring until she waved a hand in front of my face.

“Uhhhh… hello!” She laughed. “Eyes up here, Luke!”

“…sorry.” I said, blinking. “I’m just not used to seeing you this dressed up.”

“I know.” She said, flipping her hair over her back. “But this is my last chance with Vitaly. I don’t want to blow it.”

My stomach dropped like a stone at the mention of his name. I guess I had hoped that, after the incident with the date, that whole thing was done.

I guess she could tell by my facial expression that I was over her fawning over him, because she rolled her eyes at me and sighed.

“Get over it, Luke. He’s apologized countless times. I got over it. So should you.”

Ricky piped up.

“Hate to break it to you Vik… but I think you might have already lost it.”

We turned around to see Vitaly walking toward us with a girl. An attractive brunette. Dressed almost the opposite of Viktoria, in a tank top and short cut-off denim shorts.

Viktoria’s eyes narrowed, almost to slits. It reminded me of one of those nature documentaries where a lion sees an injured gazelle across a field.

This girl had done nothing worse than accompanying a man to a party, and yet I could see Viktoria was out for blood. 

At the very least, she was considering pushing her into the pool at some point. Marking her territory. Something like that.

“Hello, you guys.” said Vitaly. “This is my girlfriend, Sara. I flew her here for this. We are going on a trip to Cape Cod once this is over.”

Ricky and I nodded hellos. Viktoria just glared. At who, I wasn’t sure.

“It is very nice to meet you all.” said Sara. “You look absolutely stunning.” she added, smiling at Viktoria.

Viktoria smiled softly at her. If you could call it that. It was more like, the curve of her frown rose a little higher towards a straight line.

“… you as well.” She choked it out like a piece of rancid meat.

Vitaly, either utterly uncaring or blissfully unaware of her animosity, bade us farewell and guided Sara towards the keg.

Viktoria waited until they were out of earshot before turning towards us, seething, her eyes blazing.

“That… *motherfucker* led me on for three fucking months….while he had a girlfriend this entire fucking time….” She hissed.

I had never seen her this angry, not even when she had thrown open our door still soaking wet from her impromptu swim. I made a mental note to never find myself on the receiving end of it.

“… what’re you gonna do about it?” asked Ricky, beating me to it and taking the target of her anger in the process.

“How should I fucking know?”

He shrank back, and even she knew instantly she had reacted a little too severely.

“Sorry… I’m just a little shaken up right now. She seemed so sweet, too. That… slimy fucker … just mark my words. I’ll think of something.”

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

As it turned out, whether because of spite or a desire to give him a taste of his own medicine, Viktoria’s plan of action was not above playing homewrecker.

Ricky and I watched her get progressively drunker - which was no small feat, since we’d learned that she had the tolerance of a grizzled 50-year-old man with 35+ years of drinking under his belt.

Before long, it was like taking care of a toddler. Anytime we turned away for more than 30 seconds, or Sara left Vitaly’s side, she’d be right there. Flirting. Touching.

And the worst part? That fucker enjoyed it. Encouraged it, even.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

It happened right around the stroke of midnight.

Ricky and I were standing by the guard stand next to the deep end, finally having a long overdue discussion about possibly taking Viktoria home. By this point, she was almost blacked out, and it was a minor miracle she hadn’t managed to sloppily stumble into the pool yet, or “accidentally” push in Sara.

In retrospect, you’d have thought it was strange as well that, despite Vitaly very explicitly saying the system was off and the deep end open, not a single person had gotten in that night.

We were standing there, sipping beers, when there came a *slap-slap-slap* followed by a *thunk-thunk-thunk*. We looked over.

Viktoria had set down her drink, kicked off her flip flops, and was now ascending the ladder to the guard stand.

We rushed over and stopped her.

“What the fuck do you think you’re doing?”

She looked down at us. Her hair hung down over her face. In the dim light, I couldn’t tell whether her makeup was starting to smear or if she’d been crying.

“Vitaly and Sara just went into the bathroom. When he gets back, I’m gonna show that dumbshit what he’s missing out on. Deep end’s open! Why not take advantage of it?!”

Ricky and I looked at each other, and I could tell we had the same thought on our minds.

She was about to make a fool of herself. That dress was going to be a serious drowning hazard in her condition. And one of us was going to have to make a save.

That brief pause was enough time for her to get up the platform. We looked up at the sound of her voice.

A drunken slur, as she turned to face the pool deck. Her back was to the pool, her arms out wide as though she was about to start conducting an orchestra.

“THANK YOU ALL!”

And, with that, she turned around.

Stepped onto the edge.

And dove.
