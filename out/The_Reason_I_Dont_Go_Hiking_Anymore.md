Up until 9 years ago, I was a big fan of hiking.  I was in my high-school walking club, Any time I visited my Aunt we'd go hiking in the woods on her property, and I knew the woods behind my childhood home like the back of my hand.  All of this is to say, The woods was where I was happiest.  I honestly still miss it.  One of the times I'd been hiking with my Aunt, I think I was about 13 at the time, she told me about "Road Doors".  A local spooky story that sat alongside Bolter Street and Mr. Feets as stories you tell your kids to make sure they don't do anything stupid when they're out and about.  The story goes "Sometimes you'll be out hiking or on the road driving, anywhere where a path might be, That's where you find a door sat where it shouldn't be.  You don't go through, Even though it wants you to, Because if you do, They'll never find you.  Walk around it, be on your way, That's how you live another day."  Honestly it'd always freaked me out, Mostly because there's never been a clear answer about what's on the other side.  I've had people say it's a "gateway to hell" or "It's actually a mouth", but they usually just say "Nothing.  That's why you don't get found."

Nine years ago, the day it happened, I was out hiking with my friend Kayleigh.  We'd been friends for a long time, and honestly I'd been developing a bit of a crush on her.  We both loved being outside, but she was never quite as scared of all the spooky monsters as I was.  Any time we'd be out there after dark she'd scare me, I'd get upset with her, and she'd laugh her ass off.  We were out hiking, it was around 3 pm, and we came across a door.  It was sat in the middle of a pretty well-worn path, and if you'd seen it in any other situation you'd find it utterly forgettable.  A wooden door, with a long handle, sat in a white painted frame.  I immediately started telling Kayleigh not to mess with it, and Kayleigh did what she always did and messed with it anyway.

As we got closer to it I started feeling sick, and when I told her that she started calling me a wuss.  "It's just a prank Pattie, There's probably a camera set up somewhere to record people getting scared by it or whatever.  It's for some dumb Youtube video."  She knocked on it in an overly dramatic way, and when I told her to just leave it, she turned and flashed me a big stupid grin as she reached for the handle and opened it.

I freaked out and started telling her to stop, but it was just forest on the other side of the door.  Clear as day, Nothing was there.  She looked at me and rolled her eyes, "See?  It's just some stupid prank." and walked through the doorway closing it behind her.  That's when the woods went silent.  No wind, no leaves crunching, no bugs or animals, Dead silent.  "Kayleigh?" I called out after what felt like a full minute of standing there.  "Kayleigh this isn't funny.  I swear to god if you jump out at me when I walk by I'm gonna punch you in the nose."  Nothing.  Not even a giggle or a poorly stifled laugh, Just silence.  I started getting worried so I walked around the door with my fists up ready to sock her one for jumping out at me, and she wasn't there.  Nothing was there.  The door was gone.  I stumbled back, then moved around to the front, and it was just gone.  Like the second I'd stepped past it, it'd vanished into thin air.  I started screaming for her, running around demanding she quit fucking around, begging for her to just come out, that I wasn't gonna be mad.  By the time the search party had found me it'd been just over two days.  According to them, they found me collapsed in the dirt ranting incoherent about how "It ate her.  It ate her and she's gone."

The official story is that an animal took her.  A mountain lion, a bear, something like that.  When I told them about the door, I was written off for being "An exhausted young lady who saw something terrible."  Clearly I'd seen something I couldn't handle seeing, and my mind built a delusion that was easier to accept in an attempt to cope with it.  Nevermind the fact that no one ever found a trace of her.  No blood, no bones, no animal tracks, nothing.  I never really got over it, but time passed.  I moved to the other side of the country, got into therapy, got on medication, and I started to convince myself that maybe they were right.  Maybe she had been taken by an animal and I just needed to accept it and move on.  I mean what was more logical right?  An animal getting her, or her stepping out of reality through a door that wasn't really there?  The world is logical, and I'm just traumatized, That's what I'd started to believe.  Until Yesterday.

See, I'd started thinking that maybe if I ever wanted to make peace with things, I needed to go into the forest and prove to myself that things could be fine.  That, despite what happened, I could still go out into the woods and be okay.  That I Could get over it, if I just gave it a chance.  I started doing research on the area, checking out places that are woodsy but nothing too far removed from other people.  "A controlled environment" my therapist called it.  I found the perfect spot, waited for the perfect day, and I went.  It was nerve wracking, but halfway through I started to relax and it started to feel great to be back out there.  Then I turned a bend in the path and it was there.  Sat there in the middle of the path like it'd always been there.  Caked in dirt, the door handle crooked, the white paint chipped and cracking.  Unmistakable.  I stood there frozen for what felt like hours.  No one came by, Nothing happened, I just stood there staring at it.  I tried to walk towards it, and I just couldn't.  I couldn't make myself do it.  So I turned around to to leave, and that's when I heard it.

A knock.  A single knock.  Exhausted and weak, but I heard it.  I froze, I spun around, and for a moment I let myself hope.  I let myself believe she'd be there.  That I'd fling the door open, and she'd be there like time had barely passed for her at all.  I ran to the door and wrenched it open, and no one was there.  She wasn't there.  She was gone.  Of course she was.

I left it there like that.  I turned and left, feeling like my insides had been hollowed out.  I can't help but wonder what'd happen if I went through.  Some part of me still wants to think she'd be there, waiting for me.  Maybe she would be.  Or maybe there'd be nothing there at all.
