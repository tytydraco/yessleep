“Sorry, Sport. You’ll have to stay late tonight.” Damn, guess I’ll be missing Friday night at the bar with my friends.

I just completed my first week as a custodian at Riverside Memories retirement community when my supervisor called me into his office and sat me down.

“Don’t worry, you’ll be getting overtime and double pay for the night, along with some training and guidance from another worker. He’s done this for a while, so he can go over everything more in-depth.” Drew’s watch sounded as soon as the clock hit 6, and he clicked the side buttons to turn it off. The mention of double pay made the FOMO of not seeing my friends disappear.

“You’ll be hosting the monthly bingo night this evening,” Drew said, returning his attention to me from his watch. I already didn’t like spending time with the residents; their horrible aim in the bathroom gave me reason enough, but having to spend hours with them in a cold, smelly, and nearly lifeless room? It was like my worst nightmare coming true, but the silver lining of double pay made it sound manageable.

“I know this wasn’t in your job description, but management would recognize you for standing up and going above and beyond.  Can we count on you tonight?” Drew said, his eyes flickering from his watch to me.

“Yeah. Yeah, I can do that.” I said, nodding in agreement. Drew clapped his hands together and stood up. 

“Great! I’ll explain some things as we walk down to the event center.” Drew stood up, grabbed his raincoat and his leather laptop bag, and ushered me out the door.

We headed towards the elevators silently. Drew kept fiddling with his watch and turning his head to look at me. I kept my head forward, afraid of making eye contact with him. I really had no idea what type of person Drew was. I only interacted with him in my initial interview and moments ago when he called me into his room. 

He seemed to pick up on the fact that his constant fiddling made me nervous, so he finally spoke again.

“Don’t worry. I’m nervous too. We’ve never had someone this new run a bingo night, but I’m sure you’ll be fine.” His words didn’t do much to quell the anxiety.

“The person who usually ran it quit so suddenly that it shocked us all! Even the residents. Not to hold you to too high a standard, but she was a great announcer, and she knew how to make the elderly laugh. She quit last month, but everything has been so busy around here, from dining services to event planning, that I didn’t even remember we had lost her until today.” Drew pushed the elevator button and quietly waited.

“May I ask why she quit?” I voiced a question that made Drew harden in hesitation. 

“She got dealt an unlucky hand in life. Stuff outside of her control. I don’t think I can share anymore with you,” He said as we entered the elevator. 

“Oh, no worries, I get it.” I figured some employer or employee act was made to protect her privacy. The rest of the ride was quiet as Drew continued to move his watch around with shaking hands.

 The elevator doors opened right near the event center, where the wide glass double doors were open, and I saw a tall, dark-complexioned man setting up long, folded plastic tables and chairs.  

It had a little stage with curtains and a projector, with a few closet doors on either side of the stairs and ramps up to it. The room was large and bare, with plaster white walls adorned with generic pictures of mountains and creeks. Each wall was also adorned with paintings or artworks from some of the residents, some of which were hard to believe they weren’t kept in a museum.

The carpet was thin and sounded more like a tile floor as I walked across it.

The tall man came out from a large closet with a few chairs under his arms. Once he noticed our presence, he attempted a wave but only managed to drop a few of the chairs. He was wearing a black hoodie with the company name and logo plastered on the front. He had long grey jean shorts and shoes that gave him another few inches past his already biologically allowed height.

“That’s Jayden. He’s been doing this for a while, so he can tell you all of the specifics. I have to talk with him really quickly, so it's best you set up some more chairs and the ball cage. Thanks again.” Drew hurried off to help Jayden pick up the chairs and talk with him, a conversation that got heated fast.

I couldn’t hear what Drew was saying, but Jayden was clearly animated. His voice was harsh and loud, as if he were scolding Drew. I could hear him protest even when I was deep inside the closet, struggling to hold on to a large folding table. 

“You just keep bringing younger and younger people in, don’t you? Why is that? You know the type-” The remnants of Jayden’s voice echoed into the closet, as well as the loud and demanding shush of persuasion by Drew. I didn’t hear any more of Jayden’s complaints after that.

As I exited the closet, opting to drag the large table into the event room, Drew called for me one last time. I hurriedly set up the table and walked over. 

“I almost forgot to tell you the rules of bingo night here! Jayden was *kindly* reminding me about rule two.” Jayden rolled his eyes and returned a second glance at me. As Drew spoke, Jayden did his best not to make eye contact while desperately trying to look at me, almost as if I were a car wreck.

Drew pulled out a folded piece of paper from his breast pocket and began to read.

“Rule one: Once the games start, no one is allowed to leave until the third game has concluded. No bathroom breaks, no smoke breaks, nothing. Residents know this already and won’t need to be informed. Jayden will enforce this rule at the start of the night. Drew spoke as if he were teaching me how to mop floors for the first time, despite it being my lively hood..

“What’s with the rules? These are rules for, like, high school tests, not a bingo night?” I asked in confusion, unsure about why there were such tight standards for such a boring game.

“Well, unfortunately, I cannot tell you more, as Rule Two states. Rule Two: Do not discuss the events that take place during the games outside of game time. Failure to follow this rule will lead to… Well, I can’t say that part. Strange how it's written down, what a loophole.” He flipped the paper around for a second while he shrugged. There wasn’t enough time to read any of the words, but I could tell Rule Two was the longest.

“Rule Three: Everyone in the room must play, including the announcer and the helper. Two employees must always be present. Refer back to Rule Two if this requirement isn’t met.” Drew mumbled something under his breath about updating the rule sheet while Jayden spun on his heels to face the opposite way. His foot tapped in nervous anticipation, no rhyme or rhythm.

“Final and fourth rule: all standard bingo rules apply to the match, and most importantly, have fun.” Drew’s face wrinkled as if he sucked on a lemon. His obvious distaste for the rules must have been applied to my face, as he swiftly reminded me of the overtime and double pay.

“If anything happens, Jayden will be here to help. I have to go now for a dinner date with the missus, but don’t be afraid to contact me either. Happy wife, happy life, am I right?” He said with a chuckle and an elbow nudge before stuffing the rule paper back into his pocket and tossing his jacket on.

Drew waved us goodbye and ran out of the room, as if he were already an hour late for dinner. Both Jayden and I sat in silence for what felt like an eternity before he finally cleared his throat.

“We should finish setting up; bingo starts in an hour,” Jayden said softly before returning to the utility closet. I almost spoke up to ask more questions, but caught myself. Rule Two forbade Jayden from telling more, so what was even the point? I followed behind him quietly and finished setting up for the night. 

The next hour went by agonizingly quiet and slow. I tried my best to talk to Jayden during that time, but he either gave me short one- or two-word answers or completely cold-shouldered me. This didn’t stop the awkward staring. I’d catch him in a thousand-yard stare sometimes when I turned around, causing him to snap out of it and flick his head away.

The first resident showed up as soon as we finished setting up the bingo cage and set out three bingo sheets at each chair. Jayden was still placing a few daubers at each of the tables when she strolled in. 

The frail woman placed all of her weight on her blue stroller. Her pink glasses barely sat on her long and slender face as she walked, and the little basket of items that dangled off the stroller looked like it could fall at any moment. I jogged over to help her find a chair.

“Oh, hello, sweetie! You're a young and new face! What’s your name?” She asked as I got close. Her knitted sweater and long brown slacks all smelled of tomato soup.

“I’m Mickey, and you, ma’am?” I said with a wide smile. The old woman fiddled with her kitten sweater and showed me her little name badge that all the residents and workers had.

“Paulliene. Will you be the new announcer? Shame what happened to Patricia. Smart young woman.” She asked as I walked her to the nearest seat. She had a collection of bright silver jewelry on her wrist and a silver key on a string around her neck to accompany it.

“I think so; Jayden will do the announcing. May I ask what happened to Patricia?” I let the noisy part of me win. I knew it was probably an incentive or a taboo subject around the crowd she was closest with, but the lack of any idea about what was going to happen made me only more curious about anything I didn’t know about.

“Oh, I can’t tell you that, honey. I’m sure you read Rule Two!” She said with a crooked laugh. My thirst and comfort in knowledge only backfired and made me hunger more. 

We approached the nearest table before she began pulling away and veering off in another direction. 

“Ma’am? Where are you headed? This table is much-” She flapped her hand in a dismissive manner and spat in disgust into the air.

“Bleh! That spot is bad luck! The losers always sit there! The best seat in the house is up here!” She lifted her bent and skinny fingers to the table right below the stage. She moved so quickly to the spot that when she lifted her hand, I thought she would topple over.

As she sat down, she grabbed the small basket of items off her stroller and set it on the table. She took out candles, small wooden cat carvings, a framed photo of Jesus Christ, and small, round coins that looked like they could be currency from foreign nations.

Ms. Paulliene started to set up each item in a circle around her small collection of bingo cards. Candles on either side where her hands would rest, the cat's carvings would face inward and start the semicircle to its peak, where J.C. was placed perfectly, making eye contact with Ms. Paulliene. She would complete the semicircle with the coins, finishing her shrine of luck.

While I was watching her crooked hands craft her totem, I failed to realize that the event center started to fill up with more residents. Men and women, strollers and canes, mobility scooters and oxygen tanks began to fill the floor and chairs.

The smell of the room darkened, dead skin and pungent ammonia filled the air, making my eyes tear up. 

Not a single resident looked happy to be here. Nearly no one talked to each other, and all had the face of pure focus. At this point in their life, I don't think they had much more to worry about than winning at bingo night.

Jayden and I helped people to their desired seating and watched as they created their own luck shrines on the tables. A man had brought a collection of fake eyeballs and banana peels to place them on, and a woman brought a collection of old, stained Beanie Babies adorned with bright blue feathers. One man even started pulling out long, tangled strands of hair from his bag, placing the matted, rust-colored hair around his sheets.

Each resident who came in had the same key around their neck that Ms. Paulliene had. Even the string it was attached to seemed to be from the same spool.

“What are they not allowed to bring?” I whispered to Jayden, hoping to make the distance between us just a little shorter.

“Weapons. Or anything that is weapon-adjacent. Basically, anything that could make a hole in a person.” Jayden answered back. I snickered at his comment and finally felt like the ice started to break. He laughed too, but quickly caught himself and returned to stone-cold silence.

This, combined with the confusion from the past hour of my life, sent me over the edge into paranoia. I grabbed Jayden by the arm and dragged him over to an empty part of the room. He tripped over his long, skinny legs as I pulled him.

“What the hell is going to happen tonight? And why are you being so weird about it?” I harshly let out. Thankfully, none of the residents seemed to have that good of hearing. Jayden hesitated to answer, his eyes bouncing from mine to the sea of residents behind us.

“Just… Just wait until game time. Please, I can explain more then. I’m trying not to…” A horrid screech filled the room and cut Jayden off. Both of our heads spun around and tried to locate the source.

“Get out of my spot! You know it’s mine! I’ve sat here ever since I’ve been here! Get out, you bitch!” A woman screamed. I let go of Jayden, and we both rushed to the front of the room.

The screaming came from an older woman at Ms. Paulliene's table who was flailing her cheetah-print purse around, hitting the table and smashing good luck items and nearly colliding with Ms. Paulliene herself.

“Early bird gets the worm, Beatrice! Should have shown up earlier like me!” Ms. Paulliene shouted in Beatrice’s face. Beatrice turned red with rage. She held her purse tight and smacked Ms. Paulliene across the face with it.

“Ladies, please! It’s not worth it!” Jayden shouted as he grabbed Beatrice’s bag and held it still. I ran to check on Ms. Paulliene, who was laughing like a witch.

“Oh, you’ve done it now! You won’t get away with this! I’ll be reporting to the board!” Ms. Paulleine howled. Her glasses had a small crack in them, and the side of her face turned a faint purple.

“Are you alright, ma’am?” I asked after coming out of pure shock.

“Oh, I’m just fine, honey; it just seems we have a sore loser back here!” Ms. Paulleine shouted back to Beatrice.

“I hope you report me! Maybe that way I don’t have to play these stupid games anymore!” Beatrice shouted back, who rested uneasily in her chair in the back of the room. Jayden walked to the wide double doors and closed them at the back of the room. I checked my watch and saw we only had a few minutes left before game time, so I hurried over to him to call the game off after the tussle.

“Jayden, hey, we need to cancel tonight. Ms. Paulleine could have a concussion for all I know, and we need to go get a nurse from assisted living upstairs to make sure she’s alright.” Jayden ignored my pleas and pulled out a silver key of his own. It was bigger than the residents, and it fit perfectly into the wide double door, where it then locked us in.

“What the fuck are you doing? You saw what I saw, right? Are we just gonna ignore it?” I shouted, getting the attention of a few residents.

“We have to. It's game time now. We can’t break the rules,” Was all Jayden said before he turned around and walked back to the stage, where the bingo cage awaited like a Guillotine; Jayden, it’s executioner. 

I followed him, screaming at him the whole time. He didn’t flinch or stop his crusade up to the stage. The only sign he wasn’t a robot was the silent quiver in his lip and the watery film that covered his eyes. I started to panic; my breathing became erratic, and my heartbeat was off-tempo.

“Just give me the key then! I can leave, and you can play the game! I can bring in a nurse from upstairs! Please, Jayden. Anything? Anything!” I shouted, finally stopping him in his tracks.

“I can’t. Rules One and Three. I’ll announce this first round so you can see how it's done. Just walk around and play. Make sure no one is cheating. I’m sorry.” One of the watery films on his eyes broke, and a tear streamed down his face. My eyes were inspired and began to cry out in confusion.

Jayden made his way up on stage and stood behind the table with the bingo cage and tapped the microphone, causing the room to ring.

“Sorry about that, everyone. Does everyone have three cards, a dauber, and their key?” No protests from the crowd, except for my soft whines, which I was desperately trying to hide. Jayden walked to the corner of the stage with the supplies in hand and gave them to me.

The bingo cards were nothing special, same with the dauber and the key. I shoved everything but a card and a dauber into my pocket and made it to the back of the room to watch over everyone.

I was closest to Beatrice, whose death stare was directed towards the front of the room at Ms. Paulleine, who was more focused on her card. Beatrice’s shrine was adorned with bright, shiny objects. Small glass marbles and small lockets sat loosely around her cards. They all seemed to be placed in some type of bird feed or grain to keep them from rolling away.

“Alright, here is the first ball of the evening…” Jayden said into the microphone, letting a ball fall into his hand. The room was deathly quiet; everyone was either so focused on listening or deathly afraid of what might happen next, but no one was shaking quite like me. My card was flapping like a flag in the wind as my hand shook.

“B-7.” Jayden acknowledged with no flair or excitement, only a small wobble in his voice. I checked my card, nothing. I looked up to see the tides of residents checking their cards, some dabbing, and others taking a deep breath.

“O-25.” Another number rang out. This time I had it in the top right corner. The little purple dot sat soundly on top of the number, making the free space slightly less lonely. I saw Beatrice dab her paper, Ms. Paulleine as well. 

“N-8.” The majority of people in the room dabbed their paper, including Jayden and me. I placed my purple dot in the top middle.

“G-90.” The dauber sat still in my hand. 

“G-39.” The dauber placed a small dot next to the free space again. 

“I-83,” I saw Jayden turn his head away from the edge of the ice and take a deep breath. A sigh of relief washed over his face. The same couldn’t be said for me. I placed the purple dot adjacent to the free space. Only two more until bingo.

“B-55.” Beatrice made a sound as if she were holding in rage as she dabbed the paper.

“B-71.” Nothing from those I knew the name of, but the other residents dabbed their papers.

“O-34,” I plotted the small dot in the bottom right.

“G-20,” I plotted another small dot next to the one. A bead of sweat dripped from my face and hit the paper.

“I-33,” Nearly no one in the room dotted their paper, except Jayden.

“N-41,” Jayden dotted again.

“O-88,” And again. He let a cough out into the mic, his voice dry.

“Sorry, everyone. B-60.” I dotted my paper, only one left in a bingo.

“B-bingo…” A weak voice called nearby.

“Hey! Hey! We have a Bingo! We have a bingo over here!” Beatrice screamed at the front of the room. Jayden motioned for me to check.

The voice that claimed bingo came from the small man sitting next to Beatrice, who had not crafted a shrine like the majority of people. He only had a framed picture of what seemed to be his grandkids visiting him, all playing a board game.

 He wore a plaid shirt and jeans, with oversized glasses. He had a distinct sniffle in his voice.

“Keep- keep it down!” He tried to tell Beatrice, his hands weakly trying to keep her flailing arms down. I walked up behind him to check his card. The card was a winner after all, a straight horizontal row.

“Congratulations, we do have a winner!” I said loudly but not confidently. I wasn’t sure what I was expecting, but no one in the room clapped. Silence from the whole room, except for the start of the old man’s sobs. Everyone stared at the man as he stood and grabbed the photo.

“Please collect your belongings, sir, and exit the room,” Jayden said over the microphone. The man nodded weakly as he began to walk to the stage. 

I couldn’t look away as the whole room stared at him, slowly shambling towards the stage stairs. As he passed by plastic tables, those who sat at them reached out to touch the man. Their wrinkly fingers grazed his wrinkly shirt as he passed, almost as if they were mourning him as he walked to the electric chair.

Some seemed to do it out of pity, some seemed to do it out of solidarity, and some seemed to do it for luck, as Ms. Paulleine wiped both of her hands on the man, unlike the gentle touch every other resident did. 

“Get me out of this seat immediately! I told you this table was bad luck!” Beatrice snarled at me in a quiet tone. I didn't bother to respond as I watched the old man pull out the key around his neck. He placed it in the palm of his hand as he approached the set of double doors near the stage stairs.

The old man brought up the photo of his family to his face and kissed it, tears making the paper damp.

“I love yous.” He said before unlocking the door. The door opened into a dark room; no lights shone from inside, and none of the lights inside the event room seemed to penetrate the darkness.

The old man took a deep breath and stepped inside. He closed the doors as he entered, and the click of the lock sounded again. 

A few moments of silence passed; then the old man’s slow footsteps could be heard walking away. The footsteps echoed loudly into the event room and went on for minutes. I couldn’t tell if he was walking away and the echo carried the sound back, or if he was going in circles.

I snapped out of my daze of staring at the doors when Jayden tapped me on the shoulder.

“Mick? Mickey, you alright?” He asked, sweat pouring off his face. I took a moment to compose myself.

“What is he doing?” I asked, breaking the sound of the repetitive footsteps.

“He won. That’s what happens if you win. That’s why there’s no prize table. Winners have to go in there. It’s what happened to Patricia in the second round last month. I never saw her again.” Jayden told me, staring into my eyes but past my soul.

“What happens to you in there?” I asked, afraid of what he might answer.

“I… I don’t know. Only those who go in know.” Jayden joined me as we watched the door, the sounds of footsteps echoing from the opening underneath.

“I can keep reading the numbers if you’d like. I really don’t mind. I…” He went silent as the footsteps went silent, the room’s only noise the heavy and labored breathing of the elderly.

“No, no, I can read it's fine,” I said, trying to stop my head from spinning. I started for the stairs before Jayden stopped me again. Maybe reading numbers could distract my brain from thinking too hard about the scenario in front of me.

“Look. I’m sorry for being so distant, I… Patrica was my best friend and… I can’t have that happen again.” His lip quivered; he looked like he wanted to say more, but I understood. I patted his shoulder and headed for the bingo cage.

I sat down in the seat and pulled the microphone close. I pulled out a crumpled card from my pocket and placed it on the table.

“Hello everyone.” My voice was soft, quiet, unsure. “If everyone could get a new card out and toss the old one, we could play game two.” I wobbled into the mic. The crowd of residents grabbed their cold cards and crunched them into a ball. Those with shrines added the ball of paper to it; those without tossed it on the floor. 

“Everyone, ready? Alright.” The first ball fell into my hand.

“O-92.” I looked at my paper, nothing. I looked up to survey the crowd. Jayden was busy arguing with Beatrice about her seat while some of the crowd dabbed their paper, including Ms. Paulleine.

“B-1.” The second ball came out, and this time I splotched my paper with purple. Jayden did the same, and Beatrice followed suit, her red and bubbling face easy to spot from far away. 

“N-68.” Nothing on mine again, a few dabs from the crowd, but nothing remarkable.

“I-77.” Luck was on my side this round, as only two marks sat on the paper; Jayden’s paper didn’t seem to be so lucky. Maybe being near the table wasn’t so much a superstition after all. 

“G-4.” No one I knew marked their paper.

“O-5.” Everyone I knew marked their paper, including me.

“B-54.” Another mark for Jayden.

“I-76.” Another mark for me.

“G-98.” No one except two people in the crowd marked.

“Bingo.” A smoker’s voice called out. A woman from Ms. Paulleine’s table stood and held the paper above her head. 

The woman was slender and wore a tank top with flowers stitched in. She had a navy blue cap on with countless pins stuck in it. She moved as if she were younger, but her voice sounded as if it had been talking for a century.

“Come down here and check it.” She said, putting on a strong front. Jayden was already making his way over to her. 

After a second, Jayden gave me a nod. My stomach tightened, and my mouth grew sour. I took a few deep breaths before I spoke again, to avoid throwing up over complete guilt. I announced into the microphone the commanding order for her death march. 

After the last of my words left my mouth, I refused to look her in the eye. I couldn’t. I didn’t even know if she was looking back at me. I heard her shrine of twigs and soda cans being scooped up.

“Y’know. We used to do this to your people. They never complained. They did what was right.” I looked back to see that she was facing Jayden. Jayden just nodded and pressed his lips together. The woman broke away from Jayden as she walked to the door. Residents held out their hands to touch her.

Ms. Paulleine rubbed her hands on the woman, who swatted them away, dropping a few sticks in the process. Ms. Paulleine pushed the sticks away with the base of her stroller.

The woman grabbed her key with a free hand and unlocked the wide double door. 

She stepped inside with her items, locked the door, and walked. She walked and walked, footsteps echoing for minutes well into the next game.

Jayden and I looked at each other.

“I’m sorry.” I mouthed, he shrugged, and mouthed something back.

“You good?” I think that was what he said. I nodded and started to crumble my piece of paper. The room sounded like a paper shredder as everyone, in unison, crumpled their paper up and discarded it. 

The footsteps were much more noticeable with the door so close to the stage. I imagined the old woman walking in a straight line in pure darkness, nothing for miles. I saw her traversing a long tunnel system. I saw her finding the old man from earlier, and I imagined them finding Patricia. I tried imagining all of them alive.

“Are we going to play?” Ms Paulleine’s voice croaked out, and I found myself back in reality.

“Uh, yes, we are. X’s for the last game.” I announced as the cage began to spin.

“Ms. Beatrice, would you like to come up to the front for the open spot?” I called to the crowd. No response was given, only a glare. I decided to stick to announcing the numbers.

“B-12,” the ball read. I called the number into the air and watched the crowd respond accordingly. I marked my sheet.

“B-10,” I marked my sheet again.

“O-71,” I marked my sheet once more.

“I-43,” My sheet started to turn more purple than black and white. My mouth grew dry, and my face moist.

“I-22,” I started to curse under my breath the more I marked my card.

“G-95,” I marked again, trying not to panic. The footsteps in the background started to taper off.

“O-20,” The ball read.

“What the fuck?!” I whispered to myself, some of the words being picked up by the microphone. I marked my paper again. I was one away from a bingo. 

I looked up to look over the crowd. Many faces seemed to share my dread, Jayden’s being the most noticeable. His mouth was agape, staring up at me. His eyes seemingly shook in his head.

I spun the cage, and the next ball came out.

“N-90,” I let out all of the air I held in my lungs, the sigh of relief showering the room with noise. 

 “I-79.” A voice crack read the number aloud. The only thing keeping me from the dark hallway or room or whatever the hell that place is was an I-24.

“N-28.” Any number that wasn’t the one that would lock me away didn’t do much to quell the anxiety, but it helped, even if it was just for a second. 

“B-63,” A few marks, including one on mine that didn’t matter.

“I-5.” I nearly vomited right on the microphone. A few sounds of anger came from the crowd.

“O-10,” Jayden marked his; none on mine.

“G-11,” I marked mine. I didn’t care about looking at the crowd anymore.

“G-69,” No dabs on my end.

“B-9,” I marked mine again.

“I-16,” Another near accident. The beat of my heart now mimicked the sound of the missing footsteps.

“G-23,” Jayden marked. I didn’t.

“N-81,” No marks.

“O-15,” No marks again on mine.

“G-45,” My heart started to slow down, my breathing calmed, and my stomach settled. As bad as the thought was, I hoped someone else would win. That someone else would lose. That anyone would say bingo before we got to,

“I-24,” I read the number and letter on the ball, replaying it in my head. People marked their sheets and got ready to listen to the next number.

“I-24…” I read it again, like tasting wine in my mouth. Rotten, putrid, vile wine. Despair and hopelessness swam in my veins. 

“Bi…” I gagged as the word tried to roll out the back of my throat. I didn’t know what was on the other side of that door, but I knew it was nothing good. 

I couldn’t run. Could I fight? Could I break my way out? Jayden would have felt pity, but whatever sort of fucked up ritual I was partaking in was his top priority, for reasons he probably didn’t even know, and there was no way I could win in a fight against him.

I figured I could at least try. I took a deep breath and balled my fists. All I needed to do was grab the key from him and book it.  I just needed him close.

“Bin-” I started to speak into the mic before an explosion of anger boiled over from the back of the room.

“This is all your fault, you fat bitch!” Beatrice screamed as she pointed to Ms. Paulleine. Ms. Paulleine’s face was painted with shock.

“You took my lucky spot! You took it from me, and you know it! You stole all of my luck!” She screamed again, getting to her feet with shocking agility.

“I’m not going in there! She should have to! If I had sat there, I wouldn’t have gotten a bingo! It’s not fair! I’m the winner here!” She screamed at the room as everyone stared in silent awe. Her face was covered in tears and snot, making her mascara run.

“It’s the fairest system we have ever used, Beatrice, and you know it,” Ms. Paulliene said in a mix of fear and snarkiness.

“Fuck you! Ahhhh!” Beatrice started to move as fast as her withered body would let her, which was faster than I would have ever thought. She made it to Ms. Paulleine’s table in seconds and lunged for her, tackling her out of her chair.

Both women seemed to scream in pain and rage. Each of them exchanged weak blows. Jayden rushed over to pull Beatrice off, and I dropped off the stage to make sure Ms. Paulleine was still breathing.

Before either of us made it to the brawl, the double doors near the stage slammed open, sounding like a gunshot and grabbing everyone’s attention. Jayden and I froze in fear; both of the elderly women looked up to see the void staring back at them.

A soft but warm breeze blew in from the doorway, bringing the faintest smell of overripe fruit. A few of the residents got to their feet and slowly backed away from the doors, walking until they hit the farthest wall away. Besides the soft mumbles of prayer, no words were spoken.

I almost didn’t believe my eyes when a shape emerged from the doors. Neither did anyone else, as the room stayed silent, except for the screams of Beatrice. I tried to scream, I tried to move, but everything in my body was frozen in shock.

As the alien object slowly emerged, it revealed its horrible, amalgamated body. It had patches of brown, white, and black fur that periodically appeared and then turned into hardened chitin that looked like it belonged to a crab. The chitin fought the fur for control of the surface area, but it also had another offensive that it was battling. 

Jelly-filled ant-like abdomens pulsated on the body of the object, covered with a smooth black material. Hairs emerged from the pus-filled exoskeletons that touched the feathers that flanked them.

Small, colorfully black feathers like a rainbow in oil dotted the limb like poppies in a field. Larger patches of black feathers mixed with patches of deep purple and navy ones.

All the different clans of skin and body had other features that mimicked the species they belonged to. The fur regions had snouts that flexed and twitched in the air, sniffing the room, drowning out the silence in harsh inhales.

The chitin biomes had crab claws that would stretch as far as their wrong anatomy would let them, as they would grab and reach for shrine parts on the tables. The beaks of the feathered areas did the same as the eyes observed photos and small trinkets. The antennae in the insect zones jolted and snapped, sensing the slightest of movements.

The antennae all moved in unison as soon as Beatrice started screaming. The limbs of all the different species that lived on craned themselves over to her, towering over everyone in the room. 

“Get it away! Get it away from me!” She screamed as she tried to scurry away from it. She crawled as fast as she could on all fours, hiding underneath tables as it slowly inched closer to her.

No one dared to move or speak. No one dared to help. Most didn’t dare to even look.

The beaks and claws grabbed hold of the tables and picked them up with ease to gain access to Beatrice, who was now curled up in a ball, crying out all the liquid left in her body. 

Ms. Paulleine laughed a broken giggle as the tail-like limb got closer to her bingo rival. The limb began to curl around Beatrice, embracing her in a twisted, animalistic squeeze.

“Ahhhhhhhhhh!! Get off me! Please, I beg!!! Please!!!” Beatrice screamed. The limb didn’t obey her sorrowful commands as the peaks and pincers held on to her skin, clothes, hair, anything they could grab. 

Beatrice screamed in agony as she was lifted into the air by the limb, small drops of blood dripping from her flailing body. The limb began to recede into the darkness.

She reached her hands out to Jayden, then to me, then to Ms. Paulleine. None of us reached back, either from shock, fear, or pride.

As the limb receded, the antenna took an interest in me. They all turned to me as the beaks and claws passed Beatrice down the limb’s body like an assembly line of ants with a crumb of food. They stared inquisitively for a second, as if they were judging the character of my being, before returning to mindless squirming.

Beatrice yelped and begged for help, only intensifying as she got consumed by the darkness of the doorway. No one did anything as she disappeared. 

Beatrice's screams went on for a while after she disappeared. They got quieter and quieter at a slow and agonizing pace. You could still hear them when the ambulance came to take Ms. Paulleine to the hospital for the injuries Beatrice gave her.

You could still hear them as the residents exited the room.

You could still hear them when Jayden and I collected the trash.

You could still hear them when we put away the tables and chairs.

You could still hear them as we locked the event room for the evening and headed straight to a bar.

We both got hammered as fast as possible, drowning out the ringing of screaming and the weight of guilt in our hearts. We drank for hours, until the bar closed. We went to the local liquor store to keep the day from materializing in our memory.

Jayden and I sat on a street curb and laughed at very little innocuous things that happened until his drunk brain randomly remembered something.

“Did you know… Mickey… Mick, did you know?! That I am a birdwatcher?” He looked very proud of himself. I burst out into laughter. 

“No! It's true, it's true! I think their colors are very, very, very pretty!” He exclaimed, pulling out his phone and failing his passcode a dozen times before finally getting in. 

He was typing for a long time and giggling to himself before falling over onto his back, letting out a melancholy sigh. He held his phone up to me.

His notes app was open with a very confusing line of letters. I tried my hardest to decipher his drunk typing.

“The… feathers…” I started to say aloud before Jayden shushed me.

“Read it in your head!” He said, back to the earth, face to the sky, still.

“The feathers and peaks belong to birds. Lots of birds. Different species. Woodpeckers, corvids, Bowerbirds. They all collect things. It collected Beatrice.” The note said.

I felt the alcohol of the night scalding the back of my throat. I purged all over the street as Jayden laughed. 

“I used the loophole. Hehehe.” He giggled to himself.

All of the numbness left my body, and my stomach ached, and in its place, every little detail came rushing back. I began to drunkenly sob next to Jayden.

“I’m putting in my two weeks tomorrow! Fuck that, I’m quitting now!” A proclamation into the night air, causing Jayden’s joy to cease. 

“You can’t leave me there alone. I never did that to Patricia. She never did that to me.” Jayden said, his voice stern.

“You won’t leave me, right? I don’t want to be…” Collected. I knew what he meant.

I didn’t say anything back.

“You’ll get better pay! I get paid 40 an hour on bingo days!” He offered; I said nothing back.

“It's… so addicting to be there. The rush of chance. But I can’t be alone if I lose. Please!” He pleaded, his voice cracked as he spoke. His true motivation for staying surfaced.

“Don’t. You can’t. Promise me.” He whispered. That’s where the memory of the night ended before I woke up in my bed the next day, head pounding.

I rolled over to look at my phone, knocking over a PowerAid bottle. One text from an unknown number.

“Hey. It’s Jayden. Thanks for staying. See you next month.”
