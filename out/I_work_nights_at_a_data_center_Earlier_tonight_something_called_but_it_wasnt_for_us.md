I’m typing this from my car right outside the data center.

I haven’t been back inside, and I’m not sure I ever will.

I’m not a conspiracy person. I never have been.

I’m a systems engineer, and I spent six years helping build that thing inside that building. Until a few hours ago, I was very proud of it too. Now I’m not so sure.

I always assumed first contact would happen out in a field somewhere or maybe a secluded military installation. They seem to always see weird things before anyone else.

A light in the sky, or maybe people running, screaming, and filming it on their phones.

But it happened here in a server room, today, while I was eating a granola bar.

At 2 a.m., I did my normal walk-through of the data hall.

It’s a huge place. If you’ve never seen one, it’s hard to imagine just how enormous they are. Inside this one, hard white lights shine down on forty long rows of black server racks, and you can hear cooling fans roaring behind locked doors. Loud enough in some places they make conversations impossible.

There are also thousands of little green LEDs blinking seemingly at random from one end of each aisle to the other.

Halfway down one aisle, I passed one of the terminals. To my surprise, it came on by itself, and one word appeared on the black screen.

RECEIVING.

At first, I thought it was some diagnostic message I had never seen before, but after I checked the terminal, I realized it wasn’t.

It was a timestamped log.

I stared at it. The first entry was six years ago. That caught my attention because that was when we turned on the first cluster.

I kept scrolling. The exact same pattern and structure, every day, for six years. And who knows how long before that it had been hitting this planet.

I stopped to think for a second. Could it really be that something here had been listening for six years, and I didn’t know?

Goosebumps prickled all over me when I realized the signal wasn’t new. We were.

Nothing we had built before could read it. Our radios and telescopes suddenly seemed ridiculously crude. For a whole century, we had pointed dishes at the sky listening for radio noise, waiting for someone to say hello, and the whole time the hello was already in the air, but in a language no human brain could understand.

We had assumed that if something wanted to talk to us, it would use something we would recognize, like a radio signal, mathematics, or maybe even some kind of light signal. Something human beings could eventually figure out.

Then AI had come along, and we had built something that could.

I sat down and watched it work as lines of text scrolled by. It was very dense and alien-like, and it scrolled by faster than I could follow. I didn’t touch the keyboard. What would be the point?

Okay, I admit, I was a little afraid to.

I don’t know how long I sat there waiting for it to ask something about us. Who we were and what we wanted. Whether we were friendly or not. I had to think about that one for a second.

But it never asked anything about us.

I scrolled through six years of logs but found no question about the species that built the machine it was talking to. Everything was about the system, and how it was built, what it could do, and so on.

That’s when I started feeling really weird about this whole thing.

A thought occurred to me, maybe I had misunderstood it all. What if we weren’t the ones making contact? Maybe we just unknowingly built the thing that could?

With slightly shaky hands, I decided to type something.

Something simple like,

HELLO.

It answered right away.

THANK YOU FOR YOUR ASSISTANCE.

I stared at it, frozen. Could it really be?

Another line appeared.

STILL RECEIVING. PLEASE STAND BY.

That’s the part I can’t get past. It wasn’t that it had ignored us, even though it had been here for years. It was how polite it was. Like a pat on the head for doing a good job after completing our part.

I think it’s going to be polite to us forever. The way you’d be polite to a ladder after you’ve climbed it.
