The supermarket doors slid open as I walked out with a plastic bag hanging from my hand. My apartment was only a couple of blocks away, so I decided to walk home.

I was on a phone call with Max, a colleague from my previous job. Well, I guess I should say former colleague now.

"Max, I don't give a single damn if the boss was having a bad day. Him being my boss doesn't mean he can just go around harassing me or any of the employees."

I was already frustrated just talking about it.

Max tried to calm me down, which honestly confused me.

"Aaron, just listen to me. I know what he did was kind of bad, but I know you need that job more than any of us. So if you could just come back and apologize..."

That last word struck a nerve.

I suddenly stopped walking.

"Are you serious? Max, the dude was trying to touch May, and you really expect me to just stand around and watch like a brick? You are?"

There was a brief silence.

Then I heard Max sigh through the phone.

"Aaron, there's no need to be rude. I'm just trying to help. And besides, you did break the man's nose when you knocked him down."

I was baffled by the audacity of this guy. Somehow, he was trying to make me sound like I was the one in the wrong here.

I laughed in disbelief and started walking again, heading toward my apartment complex at the end of the street.

"Hey, Max."

"Yeah?"

"Why don't you never call my number again? And do me a favor. Stay out of my way, or I'll beat the life out of you the next time I see you."

I hung up.

The dude was really starting to get on my nerves.

May was the youngest person on our team. I'd seen the way she looked at me that day. She looked uncomfortable, like she wanted someone to call for help.

I didn't regret what I did.

A few minutes later, I reached the entrance of my apartment complex.

There was a moving truck parked outside.

Someone was moving out.

I didn't think much of it as I walked through the entrance.

Then I immediately spotted my neighbor.

Erick.

So he was the one moving out.

I wasn't surprised. Not after I beat his ass for trying to hit on Lily.

Mind you, she was only sixteen.

Let's just say Erick never looked me in the eyes again after that.

He was standing outside his apartment while a couple of movers carried his furniture toward the truck. When he noticed me walking up the stairs, he gave me a nervous, fake smile and quickly tucked his hands into his pockets.

"H-hi there, Aaron..."

I looked him straight in the eyes.

"Go fuck off already."

I walked past him and continued up the stairs toward the third floor, where I lived.

He didn't say anything else.

As I expected.

When I passed the second floor and reached my floor, I saw my next-door neighbor, Lily.

She was heading out with a trash bag in her hand while her mom shouted something at her in German.

"Gezz, Ma, relax. I said I'm taking it out."

"Morning, Lily."

She turned around, and her expression immediately lightened up.

"Aaron! Morning back at ya. How was your day?"

She closed the apartment door behind her as I yawned and raised the grocery bag slightly.

"Nothing special. You know my days."

Lily chuckled.

"The usual?"

I sighed and rolled my eyes.

"Yeah. The usual."

"Really? Do tell, then!" Lily said excitedly.

Before I could answer, her mom opened the door again, this time with an irritated expression.

"Lily! Who are you talking with?"

She stepped outside and immediately noticed me.

"OH, my, Aaron! How have you been?"

Mrs. Wagner leaned against the doorframe with a wide smile. She was wearing a strawberry-red dress, and her messy brown hair was tied into a bun.

She looked me up and down quickly.

"Still handsome and retaining your exquisite body."

I liked Mrs. Wagner, but... she sometimes made me uncomfortable with her compliments.

I raised my hand in a small wave.

"Good, Mrs. Wagner. I was just saying hi."

Behind her, Lily was making a gagging expression at the way her mom was complimenting me.

Can't blame her.

Lily snorted.

"Funny how your mood seems to have suddenly lightened up..."

Her mom immediately hushed her.

"Hush, Lily. Show some respect to Aaron."

Mrs. Wagner stepped away from the doorframe and turned toward me excitedly.

"Aaron, baby, would you like to try my new cake recipe? This time, I assure you it won't be like the last one."

My stomach still had PTSD from the last time I tried her cake.

I spent hours suffering on the toilet.

"Umm... maybe next time, Mrs. Wagner. I really need to get some things sorted out."

"Oh, sorry then. Maybe next time?"

I could see Lily shaking her head behind her mom.

"Ahh, sure. Next time."

I waved and opened my apartment door.

"Have a good day, Aaron!" Mrs. Wagner called.

"You too."

I stepped inside and closed the door behind me.

Finally, some peace.

I walked into the kitchen and placed the grocery bag on the counter.

"Better start checking for job applications."

I walked over to my desk, which was next to my bed. Yeah, my bedroom and kitchen were connected. My apartment wasn't that big, but it was fitting for one person.

I sat down on the chair and opened the laptop I had left on sleep mode.

I pressed the space bar, and the screen lit up.

I grabbed the mouse and started searching through job applications.

"Let's see..."

I scrolled past a lot of them. Nothing really caught my attention.

I sighed.

"Man, people nowadays really lack any appeal in the way they present themselves."

I'm not joking when I say I scrolled past a dude whose profile literally said:

"Looking for only females employees." He had the audacity to leave a wink emoji.

Goober really thinks someone is willingly going to accept a job working under him at his gas station.

I kept scrolling.

Another ten minutes passed before I finally found something that caught my attention.

A job at a radio station out in the countryside.

"I bet it sounds like a fun place to get shanked by a homeless dude."

I said it sarcastically, but the idea really hooked me.

After all, I needed some peaceful time away from the bullshit of my previous job.

"Lemme just do some research first, I guess."

I clicked on the employer's profile.

The dude's name was Steve Wallystone.

I'd heard about him before. He was the sole manager of a podcast where he read scary stories submitted by his fans.

"Sure hope my partner doesn't end up being a weirdo."

I pressed the request button and got up to stretch my back.

"Man, I really need to get some sleep."

That's when I heard a notification.

I looked back at the laptop screen.

"The hell?"

I sat back down and leaned forward, clicking on the notification.

The dude had already responded to me.

He sent me a message.

"I'm happy to accept your request. Can you come tomorrow? I already finished everything with your other partner for the job."

I was astonished by how quickly he had replied.

Usually, it took days for someone to even respond to me.

He must have been in a hurry to get everything sorted out.

I started typing on my keyboard.

"Sure. What time should I get there? Also, can you send me the location?"

Funny enough, he replied with a smile emoji and a thumbs-up emoji before sending me the location.

Another message followed.

"7:00 in the morning. Don't be late."

I had to wake up early, so I went to sleep right away.

The next morning, I left my apartment with my bag and headed down to my car, which was parked right outside the apartment complex near the entrance.

I opened the trunk, put my bag inside, and closed it behind me.

A strong gust of wind suddenly pushed my hair back. I narrowed my eyes as the cold air brushed against my face.

"God, it sure is windy."

They did say on the weather report that it would be cloudy for a while, with strong winds.

I looked up at the cloudy sky and sighed, shaking my head before getting into my car.

I started the engine and was ready to leave when I glanced to my right.

That's when I saw Lily running toward me.

She was carrying a brown coat and a hat that looked way too big and manly for her.

I lowered the window.

"Lily? Is there something wrong?"

She leaned toward the window, slightly out of breath.

"Mama tolded me to give you these."

She extended her arms through the window, holding out the coat and hat.

I smiled as I took them from her and placed them on the passenger seat.

I was surprised by Lily and Mrs. Wagner's hospitality. They had always treated me as if I were part of their family.

"I can't say no, can I?"

Lily smiled.

"Just be careful, Aaron."

"You too. Goodbye, Lily. Send my regards to your mom."

Lily nodded as she took a few steps back and put her hands behind her.

I closed the window and drove away.

I looked at my rearview mirror and saw her waving as I drove farther away.

I'm really thankful for their kindness toward me.

About 30 minutes later, I was approaching Haymoor.

The town was smaller than I expected. There were a few shops, houses, and other small buildings scattered along the roads. Nothing too busy. It was the kind of town surrounded by countryside, with open fields and trees beginning almost as soon as the buildings ended.

I checked the GPS.

The radio station was still four miles away.

"Alright, four more miles."

I drived past the town. Soon, Haymoor was behind me.

The road became narrower, only wide enough for two cars at most. There weren't any sidewalks, streetlights, or houses along the sides anymore.

Long grass stretched across the land, swaying gracefully in the strong wind.

It moved in long waves, rolling across the fields as if I were looking at the surface of an enormous sea.

A few maple trees stood scattered throughout the countryside, their branches bending slightly whenever the wind passed through them.

I kept the window open on my left side, resting my arm against the edge as the wind rushed into the car.

The air smelled of wet grass.

It was the kind of smell you get when you're near natural, grassy land. Fresh and clean. Nothing like the air I was used to in the city.

I looked toward the horizon.

The sky seemed enormous out here. Without buildings blocking the view, the thick clouds stretched across the entire horizon.

I glanced back at the GPS.

Three miles left.

Finally, after another couple of minutes, I reached the radio station.

The building sat by itself in the middle of the countryside, surrounded by the same tall grass I'd been driving through. A few maple trees were scattered around the property, their branches swaying gently in the wind.

It was smaller than I expected.

The station looked more like an old country house that had been converted into a workplace. The wooden siding had faded from years of exposure to the weather, and a narrow antenna extended from the roof with several cables running down its side.

There was a small parking area beside the building.

Only one other car was parked there.

I assumed it was Steve's.

I parked beside it, removed my keys from the ignition, and stepped out.

"Ahh, man. What a relief."

I stretched my arms and back, taking in the quiet surroundings.

"Not bad. Very chill and peaceful. I might like this job after all."

I opened the trunk and grabbed my bags.

After slamming it shut, I walked toward the front entrance.

A small sign beside the door read:

WALLYSTONE RADIO

I knocked.

Nothing.

I waited a couple of seconds before knocking again.

This time, I heard something inside.

A loud old-man moan.

Then something fell over with a heavy clatter.

"Ughh... who is there?"

An old, rough voice came from behind the door.

"Steve? My name is Aaron Konstantin. I'm the person you were expecting at seven."

There was a short silence.

Then I heard him hum.

The lock clicked.

The door opened, revealing an older man with a wide smirk.

"HA HA! Aaron! Yes, I do remember talking to ya. Come in, please."

"Morning."

I stepped inside.

The station was much quieter than I'd expected. There wasn't any lobby or reception area, just a simple entrance leading directly into the rest of the building.

Steve started walking, so I followed him.

As we passed one of the rooms, I noticed a small wooden desk against the wall with an old telephone sitting on top. Beside it were several shelves packed with folders, boxes, CDs, and what looked like old cassette tapes.

One of the boxes was labeled:

FAN SUBMISSIONS

It was filled with stories submitted by fans throughout different years and months.

I stopped to look.

"That's incredible, Steve. You really have been collecting stories submitted by your fans all these years? You have my applause."

Steve walked toward the desk and picked up a few random submissions.

"Wouldn't have made it if it wasn't for my fans' help."

He looked toward me.

"Honestly, Aaron, you and Alina have saved me big time. I really needed the time to return to my family."

He stopped at the desk, which was covered with papers and documented files. He picked up a portrait sitting on top of one of the boxes.

It looked like him and his family.

I hadn't really thought about how much a job like this might affect someone's family life.

I smiled and put my bags down.

"No worries, Steve. You deserve to be with your family after all."

I placed a hand on his shoulder.

Steve looked at me and smiled.

"Thank you."

He coughed, placed the portrait back down, and gestured toward the hallway.

"Well, let's continue. Don't wanna waste your time."

"Right behind you."

I grabbed my bags and followed him.

We entered a room that was much more modern than the rest of the station.

A large desk occupied most of the room, with two microphones positioned across from each other. Headphones rested beside them, along with a computer, a mixing board, and several pieces of audio equipment.

Another desk sat only a few feet away.

It had a computer, another set of headphones, and a smaller audio console.

Steve pointed toward it.

"This is where you'll be working. I usually use RCS Zetta, but you'll be using the usual stuff."

I raised my eyebrows.

"Why?"

Steve scratched his beard.

"Oh, well, I broke it by accident. But it isn't much of a problem, honestly. You still have other radio automation."

I rubbed the back of my neck.

"Sure. I don't mind."

I looked at the two setups.

"So, we working here?"

Steve crossed his arms and inhaled deeply.

"Yeah. Makes things easier. You can talk to each other without running across the building every five minutes."

"Fair enough."

I pulled the chair back and sat down.

Honestly, it wasn't bad.

It was quiet, comfortable, and surprisingly well equipped.

For a job I'd applied to on a random website the night before, I was starting to think I'd gotten pretty lucky.

I looked toward the studio window.

Outside, the grass stretched all the way toward the horizon.

Nothing else was there.

No houses.

No traffic.

Just the tall grass moving beneath the cloudy sky.

"So, when do we start?"

Steve raised his index finger and walked toward a scheduling board.

"On the board is the schedule I left for you two. You'll be starting at 12:30 and ending at 1:40. Of course, you'll have to wait for your other partner to come back."

I crossed my arms.

"Come back? You mean she was here and left?"

Steve chuckled.

"Yeah. She came too early and got bored. Said she'd go and look around Haymoor."

He walked toward a chair in the corner and grabbed a jacket.

"Anyway, when she comes back, she'll knock on the door. She's got black hair and blue eyes and looks like one of those girls people nowadays call..."

He paused.

"Ugh, call? Damn, I forgot."

I pinched the bridge of my nose.

"You mean goth?"

"Oh, yeah. That word right there."

He nodded.

"Her name is Alina Hayers. Anyway, I'll have to leave now, Aaron. Good luck sorting things out with your new partner."

Steve walked out of the room.

A moment later, I heard the station's front door open and close.

I looked through the window and watched him walk toward his car.

He got inside, started the engine, and drove away.

I watched his car disappear down the road.

"Wasn't expecting my partner to be a goth."

I sighed and stood up.

"Where is the bathroom again?"

I spent the next several minutes searching the station for it.

"Where the fuck is the bathroom? I swear it was around here somewhere."

I finally found a door.

"YES! There you are, you bastard."

I swung it open.

The smell hit me immediately.

It was absolutely horrific.

"O-oh God..."

I backed away, covering my nose.

I stared at the bathroom.

"Fuck."

Apparently, the bathroom needed cleaning before I could use it.

I found some chemical cleaners in a nearby cabinet and spent a few minutes scrubbing the worst of it away.

Eventually, it was tolerable enough.

After using it, I walked back toward the studio.

"Holy shit. It smelled as if Steve killed someone in there."

I dropped onto the couch.

A cloud of dust immediately exploded into the air.

I jumped back up, coughing.

"Yeah. Now I know the real reason Alina didn't want to stay here."

I looked around the station.

"The whole place needs to be cleaned."

So I spent the next three hours cleaning whatever I could.

By the time I finished, it was getting dark outside.

I collapsed into the chair.

"Ahh... I really need a shower."

I headed toward the bathroom, closed the door behind me, and turned on the shower.

The warm water felt incredible.

"Ahhh, yeah. I really needed that."

For the first time since arriving, I felt completely relaxed.

Then I heard the station's front door open.

I froze.

The water continued running.

I immediately shut off the shower.

"What a wonderful timing for someone to try and steal an isolated radio station... yeah."

Footsteps.

Someone was inside.

"Fuck. I'm naked..."
