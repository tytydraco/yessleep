"For all of your trusted funeral needs. Adams Funeral Home." That was the slogan my grandfather came up with when he started this business almost 100 years ago. He passed it on to my father, and when my father passed away, it was given to me. It was a business that I never wanted. Hell, I didn't even want a job ever in my life, just so I could avoid red tape and bullshit. I pretty much run the funeral home all by myself. Occasionally, a part-time employee named Tiffany helps answer the phones and assists me in the occasional funeral. Other than that, she's practically useless.

Useless is good because let's just say my business practices aren't orthodox. I am the only funeral home for 50 miles. People from neighboring towns come to me for all of their funeral needs. I have a specific policy that I like to call the "closed casket clause." No matter the funeral, that lid cannot be opened. This is a cover that I use to dispose of unwanted body parts or at times unwanted corpses. People think I'm burying Grandma, but sometimes it could be a homeless man that was dumped at my doorstep.

If I run out of space and you want your great uncle buried, I'll cremate him and put rocks in the casket. I get paid handsomely for the work I do and I couldn't care less about what happens to the dead because as far as I'm concerned, they can't pay me. Call me a monster, call me anything you want, but you could never call me broke. One afternoon, Tiffany strolled in only two and a half hours late this time. She really dresses to impress for this job. She usually wears these Goth platform black leather boots, a pleated black skirt, and a band T-shirt — today it's My Chemical Romance — and always the same black choker. She could easily be mistaken as Wednesday Addams's body double, with long black braided hair and black lipstick. She attends the community college the next town over and this apparently was the only job that would give her a chance. She usually answers the phones for a couple of hours and then comes up with an excuse like she has to do homework, get an oil change, or drive her father to work because he doesn't have a car. However, I know the main reason she leaves early is so that she can go see her boyfriend. I was born on a Wednesday, just not this Wednesday.

I usually don't mind her lying to me because it gives me more time to do what I need to do alone. I've become very well versed with a chainsaw. I cut up bodies like logs from a tree. If I'm able to use the crematory, I get rid of them quickly. However, sometimes it's on the fritz and I have to throw them in the woods behind the funeral home. Past the woods is the cemetery. I own the entire land and I know exactly how to utilize every inch of it.

A couple of days later is where this story truly begins. I had a man come in by the name of Jonathan Watts. He was a business owner — something about concrete, I think, though I wasn't really paying attention because I didn't care. He came by because his mother had passed away and he needed to work out her funeral arrangements. Even though he was a middle-aged man, he looked like a lost puppy dog to me. Whatever business he was in, I didn't care. All I cared about was the business he was going to give me, and that only produced dollar signs. He had picked out one of the caskets I had displayed around the parlor. Little did he know I kept the cheap ones in the back and put only the expensive ones out front. They took up an entire room and covered the floor. Above them were shelves stacked with urns, also on the expensive side, with the affordable options hidden away in the back room. He picked the most expensive one — a cherrywood glossy finish. He gave me some sob story about how he was the last member of his family left and that he trusted me with fulfilling his mother's final wishes.

Before he left we set a day for the following week to bury his mother in my cemetery. This gave me a few days to prep the casket and her body before the service. Little did he know I had already begun stuffing the casket with some of the body parts that had really begun to smell, and his mother would soon join them. He did agree to the closed casket clause after all.

A couple of nights later during a thunderstorm, I was in the back — let's just discreetly say using a chainsaw — when I heard somebody ring the doorbell. Tiffany, who was supposed to be there, was nowhere to be found. I had to change out of my dirty coveralls that were soaked in blood and what I could only describe as other bodily fluids. I rushed to the door to find Jonathan standing there in the rain. I groaned under my breath and rolled my eyes as I opened the door. "Jonathan, it's late. Is everything alright?" I said to him, as if I actually cared. I deserved an award for that performance. He took a deep breath and sniffled as he said, "Yes Mr. Adams. I'm alright. However, I just miss her so much and I was wondering if I could see my mother one last time before the funeral in the morning." He said as the rain steadily dripped from his soggy work uniform. "I know you have a closed casket clause, but I just miss her so goddamn much." He concluded by throwing his face into his hands and sobbing uncontrollably.

On the inside, I was boiling with rage, but on the outside I had to have a little bit of courtesy. I put my hand on his shoulder and explained to him, "I'm sorry Mr. Watts, part of my closed casket clause is that I weld the caskets shut. I apologize that you'll never be able to see her again, however, I would feel more comfortable if you would wait inside while the storm passes. I can get you a towel and make you some hot tea." He just hung his head low and agreed to come in after turning around and looking at the pouring rain. I told him to stay put while I went and fetched him a towel and started a kettle on the stove in the break room. I was already pissed because he was drenching the freshly shampooed carpet, but again I had to have some tact. I hastily grabbed him a towel from a utility closet and popped the kettle on the stove. I had forgotten my phone in the back room so I decided to go grab it while I was waiting for the water to boil. There was my phone next to a mangled corpse and a chainsaw. I grabbed my phone and put it in my pocket.

In the time it took me to do these three tasks, I turned around and there was Mr. Watts. "I don't know what kind of shit you're playing here, but I just went into my mother's room, and the lid was in fact, not welded shut." Mr. Watts stood there, pointing his finger at me, and if it were possible, steam would've come out of his ears. I stood there sweating because I had been caught for the first time. He then continued his rampage. "Why in the fuck are there mangled body parts in my mother's casket?" Before I could even utter a word, his eyes swept over to the workbench. His eyes began to tear up when he saw it. "Mama?" Was all he could say.

I didn't know what else to do so I shoved him to the ground as he stumbled backwards onto his ass and ran out the back door. Trudging through the mud, I felt like I was running in quicksand. I headed straight for the woods and decided to hide. When I heard Jonathan yelling my name in the darkness I ducked behind a tree. That's when I remembered that I had my car keys in my pocket. My car was about 500 yards away. If I did it quickly, I was sure I could make it there in time before he could catch me. I swiftly waded through mud and trees in the forest before I was in the clearing of the cemetery. After dodging gravestones I could see my car in the distance. Next to it was Mr. Watts's cement truck. I was almost there. That's when I heard a shot ring out like thunder piercing the sky. All of a sudden, I felt a sharp pain in the back of my right knee. That son of a bitch had shot me. I fell in agony while clenching my fresh wound. I looked up and there he was standing over me with a six-shooter.

"I got you now, you sorry son of a bitch." He said, looking down at me on the ground. "Mr. Watts, this is a big misunderstanding. That room was inaccurate. Your mother's room was the one next to it. That room I use for body parts that need to be cremated." I was pleading to him with lie after lie dripping from my tongue. However, he wasn't buying it. "Not only are you a sorry son of a bitch, you're a lying one to boot." He said, not wavering the gun pointed directly at my chest. He then looked over to a hole in the ground and began to smirk.

"Is that the hole you were gonna put my mother in?" He asked me bluntly. All I could do was vigorously shake my head that it in fact was. "Great!" He said as he shot my other leg. As I began screaming from the hot lead that had just entered my other leg, he picked me up, put me over his shoulder, and tossed me into the would-be grave of his mother. "Now, mister, this here is yours." As I begged for my life he just walked away. For a moment, I thought he was just going to leave me there. That's when I heard an engine kick on. It got louder and louder, indicating that it was close. That's when it started beeping as it was backing up. As I looked up, all I could see was what looked like a chute at the top of the hole. A car door opened and shut as I could hear footsteps in the mud. He looked down on me once again and began to speak. "You see, running my own concrete business has its perks. Since you took it upon yourself to defile my mother's corpse, yours will never be found again." He then walked over to the truck and I could hear him flip a switch. That's when wet cement started covering my body. After a while, my screams were muffled by the wet mix and I could hear Mr. Watts laughing as the hole began to fill up.
