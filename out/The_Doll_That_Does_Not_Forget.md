I never believed in superstitions. I always thought that all stories about abandoned houses were just exaggerations invented by people to entertain themselves. Until the day I was forced to pass by Haj Ammar's house.

The house has been abandoned for more than ten years, since its owner died alone inside it. It is located at the edge of the village, surrounded by prickly pear trees, its windows are broken, and its wooden door is always open like a mouth waiting for prey. I was returning from work, it had rained heavily, and the electricity was cut off in the whole village. The only shortcut to my house goes through the courtyard of that house.

I hesitated, then convinced myself that I am a rational man, and I entered.

The smell inside is indescribable. A mixture of humidity, rotten wood, and old dust. The floor is covered with yellow newspapers dating back to 2011. I walked carefully, using my phone flashlight, looking for a back exit.

In the living room, there was something I did not expect.

In the very center, there was a small wooden table, suspiciously clean in the middle of all this dust. And on top of it, three cloth dolls.

They were not ordinary children's dolls. They were handmade from burlap and straw, sewn with thick black thread. Each doll had a face drawn with charcoal, and on its chest, a small paper attached with a pin. I came closer and read.

The first paper had my name on it: "Salim".

The second had my deceased brother's name: "Mourad".

The third had: "The Mother".

I felt a cold shiver run down my back. No one knows that my mother is very sick these days except my family. I picked up the doll that bore my name. It was unnaturally heavy. And when I turned it over, I felt something moving inside it.

I opened the stitching with my nails. Inside, there was a lock of black hair, a piece of a nail, and a small rolled paper with a spell written in a language I did not understand, but I recognized two words: "Bind" and "Dwell".

At that moment, I heard a door slam shut loudly on the upper floor, although I had not seen any door.

I went up despite my fear. I wanted to prove to myself that I was imagining things. The upper floor consists of two rooms. The first was empty. The second was closed, and from under its door came a flickering candlelight.

I opened the door.

I will never forget the scene as long as I live. The entire room was drawn with large circles made of salt and charcoal. In each circle, a similar voodoo doll, hundreds of them. And on the walls, the names of all the villagers, their old photos, their hair, their teeth. And in the middle of the room, an old woman was sitting, her back to me, combing long hair and humming.

She turned to me. She had no face. Her face was made of burlap stitched like the dolls.

I screamed and dropped my phone. I ran downstairs, but the front door I had entered through had disappeared. The walls had become fabric. Everything had become stitched cloth. Small hands grabbed me from everywhere, the hands of the dolls.

I felt a needle entering my chest, in the exact same place as the heart of the doll I was holding.

I woke up.

I was in my bed. The sun was shining. My mother was calling me for breakfast. Everything was normal. I laughed at myself; it was just a silly dream caused by exhaustion.

I got up to wash my face and looked in the mirror.

On my chest, there was a small red spot, like a needle prick. And in my pants pocket, something was moving.

I put my hand in and took it out.

It was the doll bearing my name, still warm, and the paper on it was still dripping with blood.
