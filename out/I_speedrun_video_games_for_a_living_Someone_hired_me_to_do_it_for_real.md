Arbitrary Code Execution, I blurt without even thinking.

The old man’s face splits open into a smile full of yellow teeth and pink gums. He nods up and down, the beads dilating into fat marbles now.

ACE, I say. It’s when you turn your own data, like your movements, the X, Y, and Z values of a body in space, into code. Mop Top was going to inject his own code into the room. He was going to warp reality to his liking.

The light, I explain. He was going to keep glitching through that light over and over and over until the physics of the room broke down.

And then my head starts to throb. Oh god. I bring my right hand to my temple. When I press on it, I feel like my ears are going to blow out. But I’m starting to remember. 

Back in time, I murmur. He wanted to go back in time.

The accident. My little sister. My mom. The black truck. The crash. Both of them gone forever. But not now. Not anymore. Now they could come back.
