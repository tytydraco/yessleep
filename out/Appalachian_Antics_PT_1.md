STORY #1: The Infinite Cornfield  
Witness: Norman F.  
————————————————————

This story is an interesting one. Long, but interesting.

I was sitting on a bench at my local park, sulking after a failed interview. The old couple that I had approached originally seemed like a good idea, but they were just as mean as the kids around town.   
Just as I was about to leave, someone sat down next to me. 

I’d be lying if I said it didn’t startle me, but I took a deep breath to steady the building anxiety in my chest and met eyes with them. It was a man. He was old, very old. If I had to guess he was in his late 80’s to early 90’s. He had a soft, kind face and his eyes told me that he had a story. 

I cautiously struck up a conversation with him, and surprisingly, he was very chatty. Here is the story he told me.

A.R: “Alright, Norman. How old did you say you were?”

N.F: “I’m 94 years old…”

A.R: “Alright, and you said you had a story for me. What exactly happened, Norman?”

Norman exhaled slowly. The sound isn’t very audible on the tape, but it was audible enough to send chills down my spine. 

N.F: “Yes, yes. This happened… 30 some years ago now? This was back when this town was just known for mining and farming. My father was a miner. He would disappear for days at a time in those mines, then reemerge covered head to toe in coal dust and grime.”

A.R: “So, your dad was a miner. How does this tie into your story?”

N.F: “Well, it made me realize that wasn’t the life I wanted. Once I got older, I knew I didn’t want to be underground like my old man was, so I went into farming instead.”

Norman coughed lightly. Then he cough again, this time harder. Soon enough he’s hacking and coughing so much I can feel small balls of spit and mucus land on me. Then, just as soon as he started, Norman stopped and continued. 

N.F: “S-Sorry, young lady. Now, where was I? Oh, yes. The cornfield. This happened when I had just turned 18. I had been working on the farm going on 3 years at that point and was pretty acquainted with the land. Well, one night my boss asked me to head up to the barn and check on the cattle. Apparently, he’d heard a ruckus and wanted me to check it out.”

A.R: “Okay. Once you got to the property, did anything stand out to you? Did you see any signs of an attack or that someone was there?”

N.F: “Yes. When I got there, it was obvious that *something* had broken into the barn. There was blood everywhere. Something ripped the barn doors to shreds and then did the same thing to most of the cattle. Some of them were ripped from their neck to their guts, while others were just ripped in half or mutilated beyond recognition.”

The air of the conversation changed within an instant. I had never gotten anything like this before, and it was very evident in the excitement in my voice over the tape. 

A.R: “D-did you see who did it? What happened after you saw that?”

N.F: “After I made sure the barn was clear, I started to check around the outside of the place. For the most part, it was clear. Sure, there were cow parts, blood, and guts everywhere, but I didn’t see anything other than that. Just as I was about to ride up to the house to tell my boss about the cattle, I heard a rustling sound.” 

It was at this point in the story I noticed the change in his demeanor. Earlier he was calm, open, ready to tell me just about anything. But, I could tell this next part bothered him. 

N.F: “I stopped dead in my tracks, listening for the noise when I heard it again. It was coming from the cornfield. I spun around and saw… something. It looked like a person, but, it looked wrong. Its arms were all twisted and wrong. It had legs, but I don’t think it knew how to use them properly. I didn’t know what to think, all I knew was that I needed to get it off the property. So, I raised my gun and fired off a round. It hit the thing in the ribs, and the sound it made didn’t sound like any animal I’d ever heard. Then, it ran away.”

A.R: “That’s it? You shot a skin walker and it ran away?”

N.F: “Well, yes, but I didn’t kill it. That was the bosses rule. If it didn’t belong in the farm, it didn’t belong anywhere. So, I chased it into the cornfield. It didn’t take long before I realized I’d lost the thing and it had probably wandered off the property. So, I turned around and started back towards the clearing. I walked, and walked, and walked. I walked for what felt like 2 hours straight, yet I never broke through the corn stalks.”

A.R: “So what? You just got lost?”

Norman shook his head. 

N.F: “No, I knew those fields like the back of my hand. I’d worked in them for years and knew every way outta there. Something wasn’t right, and I knew it. I kept walking, but every so often I would hear noises I couldn’t explain. An extra pair of footsteps. Corn moving off in the distant. The low growl of something. Something that wasn’t human. At this point, I’m panicking. I’m sweating bullets, convinced that whatever I shot was there in that cornfield with me. Hunting me.”

A.R: “And was it?”

N.F: “I was going on what I thought was my 5th hour of walking when I heard another voice. I stopped dead in my tracks, listening closely and sure enough, someone was crying. I moved closer towards them, calling out softly. The second I spoke, the crying stopped. The wind stopped howling, the bugs stopped chirping. The entire field fell silent. I slowly started to inch away from whatever was in front of me, hoping I could just slip away, and then…”

Norman paused. 

I waited for him to say more. But he didn’t. 

A.R: “Norman? Are you—“

N.F: “I-I’m sorry young lady, but I have to go.”

Then, with surprising speed, Norman got up and began to walk away from me. I tried to call out to him, the desperation in my voice evident in the tape. But he didn’t turn around. He didn’t even say where he was going he just up and left. 

Now, most people would’ve left good enough alone. I am not most people. A few days after talking with Norman, I looked his name up online and found that his daughter actually lives in town with her husband. So, being the journalist that I am, I decided to ask her about it. Thankfully, this transcript isn’t as long as the other.
