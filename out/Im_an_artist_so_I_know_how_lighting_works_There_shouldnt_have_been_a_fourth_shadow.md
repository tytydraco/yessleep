I’m an artist, so I know how lighting works. There shouldn't have been a fourth shadow. 

I get that it's because of how different points of light cast different shadows. I'm an artist, I should know, but sometimes it just… doesn’t feel right y'know?

For some context, I just came back home after grabbing some snacks at the nearby 711. It’s very late, 11:41 right now as of writing, so the events earlier happened at maybe around 10:30-ish? Night time here on the streets in my area’s very dangerous, lots of activity going on at this hour so whatever errand you run, you gotta make it quick.

The orange lamps and the foggy after-rain vibe made the late night walk more eerie. The fog was so thick I could barely see my own shadow under the lamplight. It was awfully quiet, I was walking looking down watching out for deep puddles and I could see my shadow shift as I moved in between the rows of streetlamps. Usually, depending on the angle of the light, there’d be 3 under you, one from the upcoming lamp whose light is already casting a shadow on you, one from the lamp behind you that still is casting one you, and one in the middle from the lamp you're currently under. 

For the streetlamps in my area, the ones I always walk back and forth under, there should only be 3 max. There's always been and should ever be 3. 

But tonight there was a 4th.

It was opposite the middle shadow and seems to have been cast from another light source to the left of me, opposite of the streetlamps to my right. It doesn't move and shift like the other ones, like it's supposed to. Instead it’s stuck in place, permanently right of me. There was no light source of any sort to the left of me, in fact, there literally wasn’t any sign of anything to the left of me. The fog was so thick that all I could see was a bit of the road in front and the dim orange glow of fog from the upcoming lamps ahead. I actually stopped walking because I assumed there was a passing motorcycle or someone's porch light. I looked left. Nothing. 

Everywhere around me was dark and gray and by this point, I was already walking for way longer than what should've been just a 5 minute quick grab, and was already scared shitless. I kept walking. Maybe it was just another light source. Maybe I was tired.

I started to panic then ran, I could see nothing in front, to the left and right, and behind me. I was looking for street signs, waiting for cars to pass, any turns in the road, fences, houses, a person walking by, anyone, anything, please. 

I was getting tired, it was getting harder to breathe, my legs were starting to hurt. I’ve almost slipped a couple of times, my slides barely grabbing onto the wet asphalt. 

Eventually, I had to catch my breath, and as soon I stopped running, the odd shadow under me was getting darker and thicker at a noticeable rate, like something bright was coming closer. My shadow then got bigger and bigger until I could no longer see its body past the fog to my right but the legs. 

For anyone who knows lighting, a shadow growing that fast and in that manner means the light source is moving rapidly, dropping down from above. What it meant in my situation, absolutely no idea. Then just as fast as it grew, the shadow then shrunk under me till it was gone completely. I looked up, finding the source, and only as the streetlamps around me slowly dimmed, I saw it. A huge glowing ball of orange light above. It was so high up and far behind the thick mist, but it was so bright I could start to feel the heat. 

I was compelled to approach it. I knew I shouldn't get closer. I remember thinking that if I took another step, I wouldn't be able to find my way back. Then I took another step anyway. I followed.

As I got closer, I realized it wasn't light. It only looked like light from a distance.  
It pulsed. Its shape wasn't quite round, and every pulse made it subtly change. It swayed gently. Whatever it was hung from something impossibly high above, disappearing into the fog.

Something grabbed my arm. I screamed. "Hey, kid. Watch where you're going."  
An old lady frowned at me. "You'll trip." I stared at her for a second, too stunned to answer.  
She glanced past me, shaking her head. "Kids these days..." She kept walking.  
It took me another second to remember where I was. I looked back up. It was gone.

No lamp in the sky. No fog. Houses to the left and right. The asphalt was bone dry. There was no sign of rain or mist anywhere. The puddles I'd avoided earlier didn't exist, and somehow I was standing in the middle of the street, right in front of my house.

What the fuck.

I mean the fog didn't even fade like it would normally. Just gone.

I couldn't believe what happened earlier was real, but it sure as hell didn't feel like it was all in my head either. I checked my security camera as soon as I got home. It showed me that there was zero fog and haze to begin with and I was about to walk straight past my driveway without even slowing down. Then the old lady reached out, grabbed my shoulder, and snapped me out of it. I stopped and looked around. Then turned and walked back toward my house.

  
Every few seconds, though, a fourth shadow appeared beside mine.

  
The camera wasn't pointed at anything that could've cast it.
