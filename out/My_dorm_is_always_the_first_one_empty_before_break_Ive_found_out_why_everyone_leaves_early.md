For the last week I’ve been the only person in my entire dormitory. This isn’t normal for my college. All the other dorms have been normal; it’s just mine that everyone left early. By the time I noticed, the residence office told me I wasn’t allowed to transfer buildings.

It started a few weeks ago when announcements for winter break began, which you’d think to be normal I guess. Everyone wants to get home and away from classes when a good vacation is right around the corner. But the thing is that, in my dorm, people started leaving early, very early. It was the upper classmen who went first, the seniors and the juniors. From there our hall advisors started suggesting everyone leave. Not in the casual way when you started alluding to the break and asking everyone to start packing, It was passively aggressive. A few too many notices on the bulletin board, a nervous, panicked smile in the hall when they ask when you're leaving, and a visible chill going through their posture when you say you won’t be able to.

I wanted to leave early, believe me, but I’m on a very tight scholarship, I can’t afford to leave my final exams, on top of some group projects I need to finish. Bunch of scholarly bullhockey and what not. The point is, as much as I caught on that it was highly recommended I leave, I couldn’t.

It’s startling how uneasy a place can be when all the people who once filled it leave both you and it behind. to bear the size of mass infrastructure all by yourself. Doors lining the halls to empty rooms, a large bathroom with nothing but a single echoing drip, and the only voice being yours which has no chance to find any response.

Maybe I’m just being dramatic, but when you experience the weight of it yourself it makes sense. Now if isolation was the only issue, I could deal with it. I’m pretty reclusive myself, so when my roommate left I was actually excited. Not to say he’s not a nice guy but a room to myself is a luxury I just couldn't help but cherish.

No no no, the real issues started to show a few days ago, when I was truly left alone in the ghostly husk of the building. Yes, the janitorial staff left too, with just me left I guess they reckoned there wouldn’t be much to clean up anymore. Maybe they didn’t even realize I was staying before they left. Anyway, the issues.

I was taking notes after returning from one of my classes, sat at my desk with an overhead light on to make up for the fading light of a near winter evening. As I typed some notes I had a scratch in my ear. Not a physical one, but feeling the hint of a noise that begged me to notice it even while I had something else to focus on. The itch of a sound probed my ear a couple more times before I lifted my head from my computer. 

In an active dorm a noise would do little to pull me away, but, when I heard it again, I instantly turned away from my desk. It was the sound of distant shuffling on carpet. With how dead the air had been, the shuffle, even being so gentle, projected its way to my room demanding attention.

Naturally I got up and, assuming it was nothing extraordinary, went to the door. The subtle static shuffle of the carpet fiber continued as I placed my hand on the door handle and turned. As the door hinges squeaked and I looked to the right down the hallway. It was quiet. The only thing left was the dead air of hermitage which I had just begun to get used to. I shrugged it off and went back to studying.

I woke up in the middle of that night to use the restroom, making the hazy, sleep clouded walk through the fluorescent lights that broadsided my readjusting eyes with their artificial glow. When I got to the restrooms, it was warm, humid, and the long mirror opposite of the showers was fogged up. It was fogged up. I needed to do my business before I truly confronted it. If anyone had been using the showers I would have heard it. I would have noticed as the sharp and constant flow of water cut through the veil of silence.

Standing there and questioning it, I felt my heart drop, not even out of fear. It was a raw feeling of anguish. Like how you can feel yourself being watched. That primal, metaphysical sense of knowing that something was dearly wrong. It weighed me down as I gathered myself and left. I looked over my shoulder as I returned to my room and locked the deadbolt with a passion. Although I hate to admit it, I slept like a child hiding from the boogeyman under their blankets that night.

As I made my way down the hallway to leave for classes in the morning, I noticed some subtle dark spots on the carpet. Wet spots in the shape of footprints. Or almost in the shape of footprints, but flatter, like some of the refined details had been sanded down. I disregarded my curiosity and left, savoring the moments I got out and about before having to inevitably return for another night, even if it meant having to do my finals.

I returned back shortly after five in the afternoon for dinner in the form of some frozen meals I had stocked in my floor's communal kitchen fridge, which I enjoyed no longer needing to worry about anyone taking despite the name labels I usually put on them.

After the microwave had done its job, I had a paper towel forming the thin defensive barrier between my hands and the heat of the plastic dinner tray. I walked down the hallway, passing the bathrooms and getting to the hall of my room. As I turned right and looked down to the end where the open staircase was, I saw something dip down the stairs, like someone had been peaking around the corner. I froze in my tracks and almost dropped my tray.

I rationalized that I was just paranoid after last night's shower incident and the uncanny footprints on the floor. But paranoid or not, my room is the second to last in the hall, making it very close to the staircase and whatever it was that I may or may not have seen. Being both stunned but also aware of the hot tray in my hands, I pressed my back to the left wall of the hallway, hoping to have the best glimpse down the stairway as I inched my way to my room.

Step by step, going at the pace of an anxious snail I made my way. Once I was a skip away from my room, I had a good enough viewpoint down the stairs to see that there was nothing near the top steps and allowed myself to finally let out the tense breath I’d been holding.

I opened the door and got in my room. The instant the door closed I threw the tray on what used to be my roommate's work desk as I could vividly hear footsteps echoing back up the staircase. I locked the deadbolt as fast as I could. The moment it clicked the footsteps stopped. The last step being on carpet.

I silently backed away from the door and spent the next several minutes battling with my heart rate as it not only thundered in my chest, but in my ears, possibly being the only sound in the entire building. As my hands gathered sweat I crept my way to the door. Not even daring to breathe I pressed my eye to the fisheye of my door's peephole. There was nothing. I ate my dinner in silence, though I didn’t really feel like eating anymore.

That night I was awoken again by the conveniently timed needed to relieve myself. The second I came to consciousness; I made the resolution that I wasn’t going to leave the room until it was light outside. If the hallways felt inhospitable when it was day out, I quaked at the thought of how I’d feel having to make the insurmountable trek to the bathroom at night, and then having to double it on the way back. I, doing what was necessary, decided I would just go in a bottle if it got too crucial to hold.

After an hour trying to go to sleep, I started scrolling on my phone before finally getting up. The pressure to go to the restroom was getting severe, and the prospect of going in a bottle was so unappealing, that I was considering sucking up my fear and making the miserable journey down the corridors. When noise suddenly filled the hallway while I was debating with my hand to the door handle, I retracted my hand and all thoughts of the restroom left my mind.

From down the hallway I could hear what sounded like a group of people taking and loudly thumping their way along, similar to the sound of rowdy college students and fraternity brothers completely forsaking the idea that others might be sleeping.

This sounded like that, but somehow different. It was hard to place at first but soon it became apparent as whatever was making the sound came closer to my room. The voices that were talking didn't sound right, the words they were speaking didn’t sound like actual words. It was like how foxes or mountain lions sometimes sound like someone screaming, but more complex. The more I listened to it, the more I noticed the pitches of the voices being off, like autotune going up and down. Feminine and masculine voices peaking a little too high, or sinking a little too low. Before I realized it the voices were right outside my door, casting shadows underneath it. I froze in place, not wanting to even step back as the noise might give me away. I hoped they didn’t already know.

The shadows swayed and shuffled as the voices laughed together. Goosebumps raised on my skin at the sound of the emotionless, and unsteady mockery of laughter. The laughing died down and the talking started again. The voices walked to the staircase where I heard some go up and others go down, then the other way, before they returned to the hallway and went back the way they came. Coming back to my senses in the dark of the room, I realized I didn’t have much need for the bathroom anymore.

Today was the last day of classes and I just completed my last final. I pre-packed last night and wheeled everything to my car. However, to my detriment I am the type to worry about not locking a door after I’ve left, or thinking I might have left something behind. Just to be safe, I went back up the elevator to go and make sure I didn’t leave anything before I said goodbye to this nightmare.

When I stepped out of the elevator, I could instantly hear running water. Walking out of the elevator lobby onto my floor, I realized it was the showers. It was a lot of them, being accompanied by abnormally frequent toilet flushes, hand dryers, and running sink taps. Listening closer I noticed the fake voices again, but not just from the restroom. Even if softly, I could hear them coming from the hallways and the rooms on either side of me.

Despite the absurd horror of it, I hadn’t been hurt, I shouldn’t have been in any danger as far as I knew. No one had ever been killed by a ghost. Believing this, I continued on the way to my room. I took a right down the hall, then a left. I didn’t get any further than that as my feet entrenched themselves to the floor the moment I looked down the hallway.

At the end of the hallway was, I guess what was supposed to look like a person. Its skin was a soft tan, but looked a bit too tight for the frame it was on. Its gray-brown hair looked more like carpet, and what looked like clothes seemed to mold into its skin. The words on its school spirit colored shirt were sloppy gibberish. As I looked at it more, the more offensive it became. As I looked at its face, I noticed its eyes were like shallow, vacant divots into the skin. The same could be said for its mouth, shaped like an open smile without any interior.

I had my vision stuck on it, and it had its stuck on me too. I don’t know how it could see, but it did. It stuck one of its fingerless mitts out to me, its arm lifting up as it pointed. Then it shouted, distorted but almost discernible as a, “Hey!”

All at once the showers, sinks, toilets, voices, everything, stopped. Then I heard the doors start to open.

I started running faster than I ever had before. As the doors croaked open and a loud cacophony of voices built on top of one another in crescendo, I was already near the stairway. I could hear noise coming from the other floors as I passed them. Doors opening, loud stomping on the carpets, screaming and shouts no one could ever understand.

I didn’t look as I passed the floors; I only saw the shapes of the not-people in my peripheral as I flew down them. I could have skipped whole flights with how fast I was going. With the screaming behind and around me I very well might have. In my rush, I didn’t care if I skipped steps, if I stumbled, even if I rolled down the stairs, as long as I was going to make it to the bottom before they did.

I hit the bottom hard as I launched myself down the last flight. I sprung up from my hands and knees, scrambling for the exit door as I pushed into the lobby. The battle cries of the onslaught behind me filled the silence of the ground floor as I barreled through and barged through the main doors.

As the first breath of fresh air filled my burning lungs the noises all stopped, safe for my sound of the concrete steps under my feet. I got into my car and locked the doors. From where I’m sitting now, writing this down on my laptop, there's only one thing in the windows. In the dead middle of the third floor, a sign with colorful letters reads, “See you after break!”

When I get back home, I’m transferring schools.
