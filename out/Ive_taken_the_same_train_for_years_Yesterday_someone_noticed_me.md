We all accept a certain social contract when we enter public transit. 

When you’re on the same train and become a regular, you sit in the same car, in the same seat, and more or less the same people around you. Nobody talks to each other. You barely even look at each other.

So when someone new sat in my section of the train, and actually talked to me, I couldn’t stop thinking about it. I’m probably overthinking it, but I wanted to tell someone else to make sure.

I digress. Let me tell you what happened yesterday.

The train pulled into the station right on time at 6:12am, as it always does. 

When it came to a rest, the doors lined up with the sewer grates in front of me. Not quite symmetrically between them, unfortunately. It never is. 

I stood before car 11 waiting for the doors to open and the fluorescent lights to beckon me inside.

As they opened, I moved like clockwork. Two steps up, three steps forward, left turn to meet the next set of stairs. Four steps up, slight left, three steps ahead, and there it was: my seat. I learned its contours and creaks over the years. It’s absolutely perfect.

The seat faces forward, so I can take in the view of the oncoming and passing cities. It’s tucked away on a level between the upper and lower floors, so there are only 9 other seats in the area. They never fill up.

I relaxed and eased into the seat, phone in my hand ready to send my coffee order at 6:37.

The keychain bulged from my jacket pocket. She didn’t know what it did to pockets, since it mostly lived in her purse.

I looked around. The same people were there that day. The same people are there every day, sitting in the same seats.

There’s The Accountant who sits in the seat pod ahead of mine. He gets on before me, but I’m not sure at what stop. He’s been riding the train at least as long as I have. Probably longer. I saw him on my first trip two years ago. He mostly works on his crossword. I’ve never seen him look up, even when the sun rises on winter trips. His eyes are always pointed at the ground.

Then there’s Lady Seat-Saver, or LSS as I call her. She sits in the seat pod to my left. She also gets on before me, and always makes sure the seat next to her is taken by her bag. I don’t remember the first time I noticed her. LSS doesn’t read, scroll, or sleep. She sits with her hands in her lap, staring at nothing in particular.

The newest addition to this corner of the train is The Kid. He first showed up a month ago, sitting in the same pod as LSS. He mostly looks out the window like he’s taking in the view. His eyes still have life in them, like he believes things matter and that life isn’t just a series of meaningless repetitions.

The doors closed and the train car lurched as we started moving. The automated speaker system announced our trip.

*Now departing Hornbeam Station.*

I went through my regular routine as the train clanged along. I got my coffee order ready on my phone, took the book out of my backpack, and put my reading glasses on.

I took a final look around before I started to read, and that’s when I saw him.

He was just…grey. You could miss him if you were looking around too quickly, or if you weren’t looking too hard. He just sort of blends into the world like a fog. Everything about him was dull, like someone tried to blur him out. Not from your line of sight, from existence itself.

But I saw him. 

I saw him as clearly as I could see my own hands in front of me.

I saw his grey suit. Three piece, classically cut so it hung from him.

I saw his grey beard and hair. Perfectly maintained, not a strand out of place.

Grey Man. That’s what I decided I’d call him.

I could have sworn the seat was empty when I sat down. It normally is. Did he just get there? Has he been there the entire time? I don’t know how long he’d been sitting there before I noticed.

His presence was unexpected, but that’s not what unnerved me. People have sat in my section before. The Kid was one of them, though most new faces disappear after one or two trips.

It was the look he gave me when we made eye contact. Like he knew I would notice him. Like he expected me to. Like he wanted me to.

I looked away quickly and focused on my book for the rest of the trip. 

By the time we got to our last stop, I had completely forgotten about him.

Until I got up to get off the train.

I collected my things and started shuffling slowly to the exit.

*“Same time tomorrow.”*

I figured he was probably just making conversation, or being friendly in a weird way. People do that sometimes.

Today, I got on the same train, at the same time, in the same spot. I waited for the same doors to not quite line up with the same sewer grates before the same chimes went off, opening the same doors through which I took the same two steps up, three steps forward, and left turn.

I started taking the same four stairs up to the next level, and halfway there I saw him.

I didn’t want to believe it was him at first. Lots of people have grey hair. But as I crept up the stairs and saw more, I realized it was undeniably the Grey Man.

I stopped at the third step, staring with my mouth open for what felt like an eternity. A mildly annoyed “excuse me” from my rear snapped me out of it. I was holding up a line of commuters undoubtedly in the middle of their own routines.

My seat beckoned. The same seat whose contours I memorized over the years. I stumbled out of everyone’s way and sat down, sighing.

I didn’t look over at the Grey Man. I willed myself not to. I focused on what was in front of me. 

Phone in my hand. Check.

Keychain in my pocket. Check.

Book taken out from my backpack. Check.

I couldn’t help but spare a glance around to make sure the others were there. The Accountant was buried in his crossword, as focused as I’ve ever seen him. LSS sat emptily with her bag firmly on the seat next to her. The same bag as yesterday, from the looks of it. The Kid stared off outside, likely marveling at how planes can actually fly. Thankfully, he closed his mouth for a change.

And the Grey Man, staring right back at me. I broke gaze immediately, hoping he wouldn’t think I was looking for him.

The doors closed and the train got going again. The overhead speaker announced:

*Now departing Hornbeam station.*

I told myself I only had to bear his presence for three, albeit long, stops. Forty-two minutes. 

I was reaching into my backpack for my reading glasses when he spoke.

“*She used to see you off, didn’t she?”*

My wife used to complain about how early I left for work.

Not complain, exactly. 

She would wake up a few minutes before I left. Set her alarm every morning, precisely timed so that she would catch me just after I had packed up my things, but just before I made it to the door. Her face was the last thing I saw before I left for work every morning.

But how could he know any of that?

I snapped back to awareness. I furrowed my brows and stayed silent. He just nodded, like it was exactly what he expected me to do.

I looked him over properly for the first time.

His suit was the same as yesterday. In passing, it looked pristine and flattering. A closer look showed fraying edges, worn elbows, and at least one missing button. It had definitely seen better days.

He didn’t have a briefcase or any sort of bag with him. His left hand held an extremely worn train ticket, which looked like it had seen much more than the one trip it was valid for. His right hand clutched a folded newspaper, the kind nobody reads anymore. It was folded and turned at just the right angle for me to read the date on it.

It was the paper from three months ago.

It took a few seconds for it to register. Maybe he was just sentimental for newspapers, or he kept an old paper by mistake. Maybe he’s a collector. It’s weird, but not unimaginable.

I looked around to see if anyone else had noticed. The Accountant continued staring straight down. LSS kept her focus squarely on the wall in front of her. I thought the Kid would have noticed for sure, but for the first time since he joined the train, he wasn’t looking out the window. He was staring at something on his phone.

I turned my head back. I already knew what I’d find before I saw it.

The Grey Man was looking at me again.

I worried what that might mean.

I said nothing, though my face might have betrayed more than I wanted to say.

*Now arriving at Holloway station.*

I was saved by the announcement.

I got up to walk off the train and make my way to work. As I did, the Grey Man spoke to me again.

*“Easy to miss a stop if you’re not paying attention, isn’t it? Happened to me. Sometimes it’s hard but you can still go back. Other times it’s one way.”*

I’ve heard people ask or say weird things on the train before, but this was something else.

I thought about it, and I’ve decided to confront him tomorrow about continually disturbing my routine. If that doesn’t work, I’ll report him to the transit authority. This kind of thing isn’t appropriate.

Have any of you had an experience like this?
