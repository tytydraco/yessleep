Both my partner and I were completely baffled as to how this “hospital” was still in operation. It was built in 1894 and it looked every bit its age. Although come to think of it, the whole campus was state-owned property, and when has the government ever been eager to replace such things? Especially if it was going to cost money. I digress…

As the elevator doors opened on the third floor of the east wing, I could see specks of dust linger in the sunlight that broke through the barred windows. The teal blue tile that lined the hallways was wildly offensive. So much so, that if I was a long-term resident in this hospital, I might go insane myself.

When we arrived at the patient's room, the already timeless feeling intensified. There was temperature but no air. Does that make sense? The coolness enveloped you so that you became one with the room in a way that made me wildly uncomfortable. No other patients or staff were around. I announced our presence at the doorway, but there was no response. Speaking of doorway, their door opened directly into their vanity area. A door, directly across from a mirror. In a state facility. An old state facility. Fabulous. I rolled my third eye and began announcing myself again. Every time I did so, I walked closer to the patient in hopes that the lack of response was due to their inability to hear me well enough. That was not the case.

The patient looked to be sixty-five or so. Their hair was salt and peppered in a disheveled braid that reached halfway down the back. They had on a red sweater and pajama pants that did not match in the slightest. The slippers? They were worn. Almost to the point of being transparent. Light purple… I think? The patient was tiny in frame, but something about them spoke volumes as they faced the corner like a scorned child. They didn’t blink. At all. Not even once. They took calm, controlled breaths and stared into the void. I say the void because they were most definitely looking at something, even though to me, it was just a hideously teal-tiled wall.

Medically speaking, the patient was awake and alert, but the orientation was still unknown. Orientation in this regard meaning: did they know who they were? Where they were? Why we were there? As I stood in the line of sight, I gently reached out to begin my physical assessment. Before I could make contact, the patient grabbed my wrist with the strength of a thousand warriors and redirected their daunting gaze. This person looked directly into my soul and chanted “sangre de Cristo” over and over again as their grip tightened. I tried asking for help, but my partner had gone down the hall to find someone to sign the paperwork. It was just the two of us, and I felt like I was running out of air.

At the exact moment that my partner returned to the threshold of the patient's door, they stopped. My partner witnessed me exasperated, but didn’t ask any questions. They just assumed I was having trouble transferring the patient and asked if I needed help loading them onto the stretcher. I said yes.

My partner and I laid the patient down gently in bed and transferred them over with a sliding sheet. Their abundantly intentional form returned to that of a frail older person almost immediately. They were light as a feather and landed on the stretcher with much ease. The walk to the elevator and out the door was uneventful as well. I asked my partner if I could drive. I said something about how I needed fresh air, and they agreed to hop in the back.

As I closed the doors, my partner distracted by paperwork, the patient looked directly at me one last time. They smiled in slow motion, with the corners of their mouth curling up at the end, and mouthed “sangre de Cristo” (blood of Christ). The lights flickered. I felt my color drain as I sat in the driver's seat.

I have never driven so fast in my life…
