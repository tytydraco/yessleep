My sister left her daughter, Elie, with me to babysit. She said she wanted to take a few days to rest. I didn't object. I like children, and this was my own niece. She was five, so it wasn't like taking care of a baby. She was a small, petite girl with blonde hair and blue eyes.

I was only worried that Elie might cry the first night and want her mother, but nothing like that happened.

“Aren't you sad?” I asked her.

She laughed and said no.

I put her to bed in the guest room and went to bed myself.

In the morning, when I got up and went to check on Elie, she wasn't in her bed. I nearly had a heart attack when something grabbed my hand from under the bed.

It was Elie.

“What on earth are you doing under there?”

She crawled out and said with a smile:

“I'm sleeping. There. What else would I be doing?”

I tried to explain to her that she had to sleep in her bed. Then I went to make her breakfast, but she didn't touch it.

We went shopping and bought her some toys to keep at my place, and when we got home, she told me she wanted to play in the garden.

“Okay. I'll make lunch in the meantime.”

I could see her through the glass doors leading straight into the garden. She was sitting on the ground with her back to me, surrounded by toys.

I finished cooking, and Elie was still sitting there. I went outside to tell her that lunch was ready.

“Elie, your food is ready.”

Nothing. No answer.

So I walked around to stand in front of her and saw something hanging from her hand. I focused on it, and I realized what it was.

A bird's claw.

She was chewing on it, and blood was running from the corners of her mouth.

I screamed, pulled her to her feet, and immediately took her to the bathroom, where I washed her mouth and sent her to her room. I grabbed my phone and immediately called my sister.

“The person you are trying to reach is unavailable.”

Of course. Why wouldn't she be?

I went into the room and saw Elie crying.

“You don't love me anymore, Auntie?”

“What? Of course I love you, sweetheart, but this... You just can't eat that. Okay? What's your favorite food that Mommy makes?”

Elie thought for a moment and asked:

“What about you? What's your favorite food that your mommy makes?”

“Pancakes,” I said.

And she smiled and said:

“See? Mine too.”

“Okay,” I said. “I'll make them for you tonight, okay?”

She nodded and went to play in her room.

That evening, I went to tell her that dinner was ready, and I heard her talking about something.

“Auntie said I'm not allowed to eat it.”

“I know. It's the best.”

“But she was really angry.”

There was a brief silence, and then she spoke again.

“You should show yourself to Auntie.”

“Why would she be scared of you?”

“Oh. She probably would be scared.”

I carefully knocked on the door and heard the last sentence:

“She’s here. Hide.”

I opened the door, but Elie was nowhere to be seen.

“Elie?”

“Yeah?”

Her voice came from under the bed.

She slowly crawled out, smiling.

“Come on. The food is ready.”

We sat down and ate pancakes.

“Elie, who were you talking to in your room?”

“That's a secret,” Elie said seriously, furrowing her brow. “I promised I wouldn't tell you.”

“And why can't you tell me?”

“Because it's a secret.”

There was no point in asking any more questions.

When she finished her pancakes, she ran to the bathroom and threw up.

“Shit,” I said to myself. “Elie, I think we're going to have to see a doctor. I think there's something wrong with you.”

Elie looked at me and shrugged.

“Do you want to sleep with me tonight since you're not feeling well?”

“Okay.”

We fell asleep, and when I woke up in the morning, Elie was no longer in bed.

I went into the kitchen and found her stuffing raw meat into her mouth. It was meat I had prepared for lunch.

I squeezed my eyes shut and hoped that when I opened them, the image would be gone.

It wasn't.

I took the meat from her hand, and she looked at me angrily before walking back to her room.

“I have to take her to a doctor,” I told myself.

But there were no documents in the bag my sister had given me. Nothing.

I spent the entire day trying to call her, but her phone was still switched off.

I searched online to see what it could be and asked people online for advice. The suggestions ranged from mental illness to “maybe your sister is abusing her.” But until my sister came back, my hands were tied. There was nothing I could do. Damn it.

Elie stayed in her room all day. I went to the door and heard her talking again.

“She’s a bitch. She won't give me anything to eat.”

“Yeah, I know.”

“You should hurt her because I'm hungry because of her!”

My heart was pounding like crazy.

I knocked on the door, and Elie's head peeked out from under the bed. She was smiling.

“Hi, Auntie!”

She looked completely normal.

What if I'm the one going crazy? What if something is wrong with me? So many thoughts were racing through my head.

“Nothing, Elie. I was making dinner. Do you want some?”

She just shook her head.

“Okay.”

That evening, I put her to bed and went to bed myself.

In the morning, I woke up to horrible pain. Pain in my leg.

I opened my eyes, turned on the bedside lamp, and looked to see what was happening.

Elie was standing in front of me. My leg was covered in blood, and a piece of flesh was missing.

I looked closely at Elie. Blood was running from the sides of her mouth. She didn't say anything. She just kept chewing loudly.

I immediately got dressed, ran to my car, and drove away. I didn't know where I was going, but when I was as far away from the house as I could get, I pulled over and tried to calm myself down.

I picked up my phone and dialed my sister's number.

She answered after a while.

“Oh my God, you finally picked up!”

On the other end, she sounded confused.

“Finally?”

“I've called you like a hundred times.”

“But Kori, I don't have any missed calls from you.”

“Never mind,” I interrupted her.

I started explaining what had happened and told her she had to come and pick up Elie immediately.

“Kori, I don't know what's going on with you, but I don't know who Elie is, and I don't have a daughter.”

A little while later, she drove to where I was. She looked at my leg and was horrified.

We called the police, and they went with us to my house.

Nobody was there.

There was no sign that a child had ever been there.

After the police left, I looked at my sister.

“I swear to you, she was here. And she kept hiding under the bed.”

“Then we'll turn the bed over,” my sister said.

We turned it over, but there was nothing there.

“Kori, shit,” my sister said, horrified. “Look at the scratches on the inside of the bed.”

I packed my things and went to stay with my sister. I never went back to that house.
