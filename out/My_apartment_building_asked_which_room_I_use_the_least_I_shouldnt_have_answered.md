Last Monday, I got an email from my apartment building about something called a Residential Space Utilization Survey.

Apparently management was collecting information for an upcoming energy efficiency assessment.

The questions were pretty standard at first. How many people live in your unit? Do you work from home? Which rooms are heated during the day?

Then came one I thought was a little odd.

"Which room in your residence remains unoccupied for the longest continuous period?"

I live alone in a two-bedroom apartment. The second bedroom is basically storage. It has an old desk, some boxes, a broken exercise bike and a mattress I keep promising myself I'll replace.

I selected "Second bedroom."

The next question asked whether anyone regularly slept there.

No.

Then whether the door was typically kept closed.

Yes.

I submitted the form and forgot about it.

Tuesday morning, my second bedroom was gone.

Not empty. Gone.

I walked down the hallway to grab a clean towel from one of the boxes I'd been keeping in there, and there was just a wall.

Same beige paint as the rest of the hallway. Continuous baseboard. No doorframe, no hinges, nothing.

I stood there for probably a full minute trying to understand what I was looking at.

My apartment is small enough that there was no possibility I'd mistaken the location. The bedroom had been directly across from the bathroom for four years.

I took out my phone and found a picture from when I first moved in.

There it was. White door, brass handle, little scratch near the bottom from dragging my desk through it.

I called my friend Daniel.

He'd helped me move the exercise bike into that room last summer. He remembered the room perfectly.

At first he thought I was messing with him. Then I showed him the hallway over video.

"Did your landlord wall it off?"

"While I was asleep?"

"Well, what the fuck do you want me to say?"

Fair question.

I called the management office. The woman who answered insisted that my unit was listed as a one-bedroom.

I told her I had a lease describing two bedrooms and photographs to prove it.

She asked me to email them.

Twenty minutes later, I got a response saying someone would investigate.

That afternoon, Daniel came over.

We measured the apartment.

The hallway was about eleven feet shorter than it should have been.

Here's where things got worse.

My apartment is on the ground floor. The missing bedroom had a window facing the parking lot.

We went outside.

The window was still there.

Same cheap white blinds. Same little crack in the lower-right corner.

I could even see the cardboard box I'd left on the windowsill.

Daniel started knocking on the glass.

Nobody answered.

He cupped his hands around his face and tried looking inside.

"Your stuff's still in there."

I pushed him aside.

He was right.

Everything was exactly where I'd left it. The exercise bike, the desk, the boxes.

Except there was a chair in the middle of the room.

One of those cheap metal folding chairs.

It wasn't mine.

And somebody had placed it facing the wall where the door should have been.

I went back inside and started hitting that section of wall with my fist.

Daniel tried to stop me, but I wasn't thinking straight anymore.

After about thirty seconds, someone knocked back.

Three slow knocks.

We both heard them.

Daniel grabbed my arm and pulled me away.

Neither of us said anything for a while.

We stayed at his place that night.

Wednesday morning, I received another email from the management portal.

"Your reported unused space has been successfully reassigned. Thank you for helping us improve residential efficiency."

I called the office again.

Different woman this time.

She couldn't find any record of the survey.

When I forwarded her the email, she told me their system didn't send messages with that wording.

I asked her what "reassigned" meant.

She said she didn't know.

Then she suggested I contact the police if I believed someone had entered my apartment.

So I did.

Two officers came out Wednesday afternoon. I showed them my photographs and the lease.

One of them looked through the window while the other checked the hallway.

They didn't know what to make of it.

The older officer suggested there might have been an undocumented renovation or some problem with the building's original layout. He said he'd never seen anything quite like it, but there wasn't much they could do without evidence of a crime.

They left after taking a report.

I wasn't surprised they couldn't help.

What bothered me was something the younger officer said before leaving.

He'd looked through the bedroom window too.

He asked me who'd been sitting in the chair.

I told him nobody.

He looked genuinely confused.

He said there'd been a man sitting with his back to the window when he first looked inside.

By the time he'd called his partner over, the chair was empty.

I didn't go outside to check.

That night I stayed in my apartment because I was furious, exhausted, and increasingly worried I was losing my mind.

Around eleven, I heard furniture moving behind the hallway wall.

It sounded like someone dragging my desk across the floor.

Then came footsteps.

Not random footsteps.

Someone was pacing the length of the missing bedroom, stopping every few seconds at the place where the door used to be.

I sat on my couch with all the lights on.

At 11:47, something scraped against the wall.

A long, slow scratching noise.

Then I heard a man's voice.

It was faint, but I understood him perfectly.

"Is this one available?"

I didn't answer.

He asked again.

"Is this one available?"

I grabbed my keys and ran.

I spent Thursday at Daniel's.

Yesterday morning, we drove back together because I needed clothes for work.

Before going inside, we checked the bedroom window.

The blinds were open.

Every single box had been unpacked.

My old desk was covered with neatly arranged objects I didn't recognize. Keys, watches, framed photographs, a child's red mitten.

There were dozens of them.

The folding chair was still facing the wall.

And this time there was a man sitting in it.

I couldn't see his face.

He was wearing a gray sweater and holding something in his lap.

I knocked on the glass.

Daniel told me not to.

The man slowly raised one hand.

He wasn't waving.

He was holding up a piece of paper.

It was my apartment's floor plan.

I recognized the layout immediately.

He pointed at the missing bedroom.

Then at the living room.

Then he made a small motion with his finger, like he was crossing something off a list.

Daniel pulled me away from the window.

We drove straight to his apartment.

I've been here since.

About an hour ago, I checked the management portal again.

There was a new survey waiting for me.

Only one question this time.

"Which room in your residence could you comfortably live without?"

There were four options.

Kitchen.

Bathroom.

Bedroom.

Living room.

I haven't selected anything.

I don't intend to.

But while I was staring at the screen, the status changed from "Awaiting Response" to "Response Received."

The selected answer was "Bedroom."

I called Daniel into the room and showed him.

He stared at it, then asked me something that hadn't occurred to me.

"How does it know you're here?"

I don't know.

But I just went to get my phone charger from Daniel's guest room.

The door is gone.

And somebody is knocking from the other side.
