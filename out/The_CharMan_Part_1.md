Part 1: Incipient



This is a confession of guilt. One that has been with me for a long time. Something I thought that I would take to the grave with me. But I’ve realized I can no longer hide this secret. It’s a secret that has destroyed lives. Not just my own. But the lives of people that I care for. I don’t know how to start this. I suppose with memories. With the start of everything.



It started when I was a child. I lived in Ojai, California. A small artsy town about 80 miles away from Los Angeles. It’s somewhere where the creative minded go. A place where you can wake up and see the mountains turn into an elegant shade of pink. I was lucky enough to live there. Despite all that has happened. It is still a beautiful town. Just not one I can go back to. 



From what I can remember, I was always a loner as a child. Someone who kept to themselves. I never felt like I belonged. I loved to get lost in worlds of fantasy. To imagine myself right there along with the characters. Run along with the heroic protagonist. Or be one myself. Run through the forests and pretend to have legendary sword fights against the evils that the world could conjure. I had a very active imagination. I miss that child like wonder.



Because I was a recluse of a child, I struggled to make friends. I would change subjects very fast, stirring it to things that I was interested in. Attempting to talk with people about a fantasy world I was living in. I was awkward and shy. It didn’t help that I was also bullied. Pushed around, mocked. Something I didn’t really defend against because “snitches get stitches.” 



But there were always a few people by my side. My older sister Miranda. Some people don’t like their siblings. But Miranda was always good to me. She was 13, about 7 years older than I was. Despite that, she would always do things with me. Play video games with me, take me out for ice cream, movies, etc. Most of all, she didn’t make me feel like the things I liked were stupid. One moment I can remember clearly, was when I was really sick and mom wasn’t able to stay home and take care of me, Miranda did. Making me soup and turning on the Lord Of The Ring’s movies. It was the small things.



The other person was my best-est friend. Jackie Coleman. Jackie was different from the other kids. He came up to me around the second or third day of kindergarten and just asked me if I wanted to be friends. I was excited to have a friend. So was my mother and sister. I think that they were worried about me struggling to make friends.



Jackie was a lot more friendly and extroverted than I was. He was a good kid. Always kind to my mother and my sister when we went over for play dates. His mother was good too. She became a close family friend after Jackie and I became friends. 



I remember we spent endless hours playing video games, card games. Playing pretend out in the woods. I think it was also nice for my sister. She no longer had to feel like she had to take care of me all the time. She could have her own life and friends. To put it in simple terms for Jackie, he became a brother to me. I long for those days. Back when everything was so much simpler. Back when the world felt like this big adventure. Instead of what it really is.



My life changed when I was 10 years old. It was the beginning days of 5th grade. The hot augusts signaling that time of transition to another year of school. 5th grade felt different. Special. That time where you were the top dog of school so to speak. Jackie and I were at the “top of the food chain.” 



It was the first or second week of 5th grade. I was sitting in that simple classroom with plastic chairs attached to desks and inspirational posters of quotes from famous people. Presidents, Activists, Actors, Musicians. My teacher had us starting out with math for the day. Fractions. I stared at the sheet of paper as my teacher, Mrs. Elm, talked about them. I was still confused on how any of this worked. I nudged Jackie.



“Do you get any of this?” I asked.



“Eh, a little bit.” He shrugged.



“Dude I’m so confused, I don’t understand any of this!” I hissed.



“Hah, good luck on the quiz.” 



“We have a quiz!?!”



“Yup. Next Friday." Jackie spoke.



“Shit!” I hissed.



I must have said it to loud because Mrs. Elm turned around and looked at me. 



“Conner!” She exclaimed.



“Oh, uh, sorry Mrs. Elm, I meant shoot.”



She gave me a knowing look. I looked to Jack for help. He smirked.



“You’re lucky I have a quarter Connie.” He mocked.



I squinted my eyes at him and stuck out my tongue. He handed me the quarter and I went up to the swear jar and dropped it in.



“Now Conner, please do not use that kind of language in my classroom.” She said lowering her head a little so she could be on the same level as a very short 5th grader.



Even by normal child standards, I was pretty small. I had gotten mistaken as a 2nd or 3rd grader a couple of times. I went back to my desk and she continued teaching the class. I sulked for a moment, feeling embarrassed about Jack having to give me a quarter. He nudged me with his elbow and started snickering.



“Shut up.” I whispered.



“Guess Mrs. Birrd never put soap in your mouth.”



“Why do you still call my mom that? You’ve known her for like– so long.” 



He shrugged. “It’s polite I guess.”



About 20 minutes later the bell rang for lunch and recess rang. I immediately shot out of my desk. As did a couple of the other kids. I may have been a quiet kid, but that didn’t mean I didn’t have lots of energy. Jackie got up in a more normal way. The two of us headed to the cafeteria. Today was pizza Friday and I was excited. I walked there at a pace that was almost a run. Jackie did so, if only to keep up with me. I was the 4th or 5th person to get in line.



“Better be careful Conner, Don’t wanna get fat like Ryan.”



“Screw you, don’t compare me to that guy!” I exclaimed.



“You’re still mad because he bullied you in kindergarten? I think you got the better end of the stick not ending up looking like a balloon.”



I looked around to make sure that Ryan Teller wasn’t anywhere near. He didn’t really bully me anymore, but if he heard that the two of us were making fun of him, he probably would get mad. After making sure that the coast was clear, I was finally able to expel the laughter that I had been holding in for about a good 10 seconds.



After getting our pizza, we sat down at an empty table. It was normally just the two of us who sat at this table and today was no different. However a group of kids came over to the table.



“Yo Jack, what’s up.” The kid in the front said. 



“What’s up?” Jack asked with a smile. 



“You wanna play kickball with us at recess?” The kid asked.



I looked away for a second. I knew these kids. Isaac, Malcolm, Tim and Liam. The other kids were some 4th graders who I didn’t really know. I tried to play with them last time. But as it turns out I was about as good at running as a snake was at walking. I was slow and lost pretty much every time.



“Depends, does Conner get to play with us?” Jack asked.



“Skelator?” Isaac said.



The other kids started laughing and I felt my face go a bright red but I didn’t say anything. 



“Why would we let Shrimp over here play, he’s slow!” Malcolm said.



“Then no.” Jack said simply.



“Pff, why do you even hangout with him? You could be cool and popular, you know, people actually like you.” 

Isaac said.

I felt my fist tighten and I looked away. I could feel my stomach turning hot from the embarrassment. 



“Popular? You mean the dipshit club?” Jack started laughing.



“Whatever. Enjoy hanging out in the loser club.” Isaac retaliated before walking off with his friends.



There was a moment of silence before I responded to Jack.



“Dude, that was so cool!” I exclaimed. 



“I know, I’m so cool, man.” Jack laughed. 



At recess the two of us hung out at the tree in the playground. Other kids were on the slide or on the swings. But the two of us always hung by the tree. It was a nice quiet place away from the other kids. I walked around, starting to explain a plan for the weekend.



“You wanna have a sleepover this weekend?” I asked.



“Yeah sure, we're gonna explore more of the forest?” He asked.



“Yeah! We can map it out! Maybe we’ll even find a cave or bridge.”



“Or maybe a bear.” Jack puffed out his chest and let out his impression of a bear.



“Tch, I’m not scared of bears.” I lied.



“You are such a liar, I bet if you saw a bear you’d pee your pants!” Jack exclaimed.



“Tch–well, so would you!” I countered back.



“No way, I’d scare it off!”



“Well so would I!” I exclaimed.



“Well, if it’s the two of us, I’m sure we could take a bear.” He said.



“Yeah, we’re way stronger than any bear.” I said.



“Yeah, we’re like Aaragorn and Gimli.” Jack said.



“Am I gimli?” I asked.



“What do you think?” Jack mocked.



“Hey! Gimli is cool! He’s really good at killing orcs!”



He tilted his head to the left with a face confirming I was right. Gimli was cool.



“I think there’s a bridge around by your house. I don’t remember what it’s called but apparently it’s super haunted!” Jack said, starting an entirely new conversation.



“Haunted? Like ghosts?” I asked.



“Yeah, like ghosts. I heard some of the other kids talking about it. I don’t remember what they said, but I bet we could check it out!”



“Y-yeah.” I said.



“What are you scared of ghosts?” Jack asked.



“No! I’m not a chicken.”



“Okay then we’ll try and find it tonight! Then we’ll see some ghosts. Then we can really show 'em who's boss!” Jack proclaimed.



“Ya know what? Yeah! I’m not afraid of no ghosts!”



Without missing a beat, Jack and I started singing the Ghostbuster song. I saw some other kids look at us and I stopped. Jackie hit me on my side lightly with the back of his hand. 



“Ignore em, they’re the real losers. They can’t have any real fun!” Jackie said.



I nodded and kept on singing. Then the bell rang for us to go back inside. I was excited for the next subject we were doing. Science. I had always had a deep love for science. Especially animals. I remember my sister took me down to the beach when mom was working a couple of weeks ago. Normally we would swim, but there were so many sea lions on the beach and we weren’t allowed to get near them. 



However, this didn’t bother me. I sat and watched the sea lions. Laughing at their goofy noises. My sister standing there with me as we gave them names and voiced what they were doing. Something along the lines of, get off of me frank, you’re too fat. It was fun. We didn’t end up going to the beach but it was still an amazing time. 



I sat at my desk, this time actually excited to learn. Mrs. Elm began to explain adaptations for animals. Things like how a penguin was black and white because it was a kind of camouflage for them. When they were swimming the light from above would make them blend in. And when swimming low, they’re black would help them hide in the darkness of the ocean. 



Antarctica as a place was so cool to me. There wasn’t a lot that lived there of course. But the animals that did, were some of the coolest things around. Penguins, certain types of whales, seals and birds. I looked over and saw Jackie sketching the outline of a penguin on his notebook. Jackie, even at that time. Was a really good artist. 



Despite my excitement about learning of the animals, when the bell eventually rang, I couldn’t get out of my seat fast enough. Even Jackie got out of his seat relatively fast as well. We ran outside. Miranda was picking us up today because my mom was busy down at the hospital again. My mom worked a lot, so this was a normal occurrence. My mom worked a lot of 12 hour days. Jack pulled out his flip phone and made a bit of a frown as we sat on the grass waiting for Miranda. 



“Guess my mom isn’t picking me up today. She said to get a ride with you.”



“Eh that’s okay, see if she’ll let you spend the night!” I said excitedly.



He tapped on the tiny keys of his phone. He waited for a little while before there was the sound of him getting a notification. He looked over towards me with a smile.



“She said yes!” He smiled happily.  



I pumped my fist in the air. “Yes!”



After about 5 minutes, Miranda’s blue car pulled up to the side of the road. The two of us opened the door.



“Hi Miranda.” Jackie said politely.



“Hey guys.” She said, “I assume you're gonna spend the night with us Jack?” Miranda asked.



“Yep, as long as it’s okay with you and Mrs. Birrd.” He said.



“Of course. You’re always welcome. I’ll order some pizza tonight. Mom left some money.” She said.



She started the car and started driving away from the school. Heading home.



“You two have any homework?” She asked.



I let out a groan. “Yeah…math homework.”



“Okay well mom said you gotta do that first before any video games.”



“Ughhh fine.”



“Jack, you’re good at math, think you can help him?” She asked.



“Yeah, I understand it alright.” Jack said.



“Alright. Now don’t tell mom, but I’m gonna play some good music. None of that poppy shit.” Miranda said.



Jack and I looked at each other and smiled. Miranda had influenced a lot of my music taste. As a 10 year old, I knew some bands that I probably shouldn’t have. Things like Slipknot, System of a Down, Korn, etc. Mom didn’t know of course but she would’ve had a fit if she knew her 10 year old boy was listening to stuff like that. And Miranda would certainly get in trouble. 



She pressed the 3rd button on her CD player while stopped at a red light. I could hear the metal whirring of the CD starting up and as the first note hit, I knew exactly what it was. My sister had educated me on music. Stuff from the 90’s that was really old. But I knew this, as toxicity, by System of a down. And it rocked. HARD.



Me and Jack attempted to sing along but the words were too fast and the two of us just kept jumbling up the words. It really didn’t matter though as long as we were having fun. My sister smiled as she looked at the two of us from the mirror and tapped to the beat along with us. Jack and I attempted to head-bang like the metal heads did, but after about 30 seconds of it, it got tiring. After seeing how Jack looked while head banging, I started laughing. We really did look goofy. 



After about 10 minutes of driving, Miranda pulled into the driveway of the house. She turned off the music before turning back to talk to me.



“Remember, homework, then ya’ll can do whatever you want. Ya know. Besides burning down the house.” She said sarcastically. 



“Why would we do that?” I asked.



“Ya know sarcasm?” She asked.



“Ohhhhhhh. Okay.” I said.



“Doofus.” Jack said.

I elbowed him and gave him the stink eye. The 3 of us got out of the car and headed inside. I was kind of in a distracted mood. I really wanted to go to the forest. Keep on exploring with Jack. But of course. Homework had to come first. Miranda made sure of that.



“We can all sit down together and do what we gotta do.” She said.



“Yeah, we can help you!” I said.



She let out a bit of a laugh.



“You can try, but this is some pretty complicated stuff.” She shrugged.



Jack and I sat there for about 30 minutes. Jack was able to get his done pretty quickly, and then started giving me vague ways on how to solve it. I gave him multiple pleading looks and eventually tried to get him to just show me how to solve it. He did not oblige.



“Mrs. Elm will see we got the same answers dude!” He exclaimed. 



“I’ll get a few wrong on purpose then!” I said.



Miranda got closer to me and put her arm around my shoulder.



“Sometimes you gotta struggle through a hard thing. Also, Jack, this isn’t tarot card readings. You can be a little less vague. Just show him how to show your work.” She said.



Jack gave her a blank stare before continuing to help me. Eventually. I was able to get them. The fractions were hard. Almost immediately, the two of us quickly started going towards the backyard where we could go to the woods. Miranda gave us a quick look before talking to us. 



“Hey, make sure you don’t go too far!” She called.



“Okay!” I said quickly.



“Yep.” Jack said. 



The two of us started going through the trees. Sometimes we got deep into those woods. And sometimes I would get scared. I remembered this Urban Legend around the town. The Char Man. A local tale. It wasn’t something I would believe now. But as a child, the thought of it terrified me. The story went as follows.



In 1948, the lewman family, consisting of a son and a father, moved to Ojai. They lived in a small cabin far from the others in the town. The boy would walk miles to get to school. He was often picked on. But the real tragedy of the story began with the father. One day, while he was in town, he came across a dead girl's body. Thinking the girl was alive, he tried to save her. But from what a man passing by saw. It looked like the father was murdering the little girl.



The father was arrested. However, eventually they managed to clear his name because similar murders kept taking place. The people of Ojai however, were on edge as ever. They still thought it was him. The father and his son’s name were tarnished. The boy would suffer brutal beatings for classmates. Convinced that his father had killed their friend.



Then one quiet night, the town struck. Having spilled tall tales of the man using his cabin to commit his child murders. They didn’t have any facts. The fact of the matter was this man was a murderer in their eyes. They lit the house on fire. The boy and his father were trapped. The father did everything he could to save his boy. Adrenaline kicked in, and he managed to rip off a wooden panel just big enough for the boy to slip through. Not before fire dropped from the ceiling. Permanently mangling his face with severe burns. The child dropped to his knees and began to sob. His father was gone. 



The town folks cheered as they heard the fathers pained screams. The man begged God. To somehow save him and his boy. God did not listen. The man died in that fire. His son was permanently disfigured. The boy went almost insane from the burns. He started screaming at the people. Roaring how god would slaughter all of them for what they had done. They ignored the child and left him with the burning house.



He sat there for days. Not eating. Not drinking. Eventually, the pain of everything that had happened destroyed his mind. He ripped a hatchet out of a stump his father had cut down, and began hacking at his hand. Taking off the pinky and index finger of his left hand. Then he ran off into the forest. Years passed. The boy became a man. Living with the insanity and the rage. Wanting nothing more than to punish the people of the town. Eventually. He made his move.



He began to slaughter everyone in that town. Sneaking into their homes and either lighting it on fire, or slaughtering them with the hatchet. Eventually, the police shot and killed him. Now the story goes that he haunts these woods. Looking for his next victim to exact his revenge on. Looking at it now. It was just a spooky story. But 10 year old me was terrified that if we went too deep into those woods. The Char man would kill us and take our fingers. Miranda had told me that story when I was much too young. I’m pretty sure she did it to spook us, but then I started crying and she had to comfort me. Tell me it was just a story and that everything was okay. Mom wasn’t happy at all. 



We walked through the forest. I remembered telling myself that if I ever smelled smoke, I would turn tail and run. A 10 year old me was not about to get murdered by the ghost of a disfigured axe murderer from the 50’s. We simply walked. Talking about nothing in particular like more kids do. That was before it started to get into the more dark territory of the forest. That’s when I started to get scared. Jackie was much more confident then I was. We walked through the forest.



In a calm, almost stoically emotionless voice. He told me how if there was anything out here, he would protect me. That brought me a little bit of comfort, but not much. Looking back now, it was dangerous to go that deep into the woods. There could have been an animal, but the more likely outcome of 10 year olds going that deep into the forest, was we could easily get lost. That’s what almost happened.



As we were walking through the woods looking for a cave or bridge. Or just something interesting. I realized that I had been walking behind much more slowly then Jack had. I would turn my head quickly if I heard the rustling of trees or the sound of a bird flying off. As I looked back. I realized that I had lost Jack.



“Jack?!” I called. Beginning to panic. I couldn’t hear anything in the forest now. It was dead silent. And that was even worse then the tiny noises I would hear. I walked around, trying to find Jack.



“Come on Jack, if this is a prank it’s not funny!” I called.



There was no answer. I could feel my heart beginning to race. I felt scared. I didn’t know what to do. I didn’t want to leave the path. I didn’t want to get lost in the forest looking for Jackie. I felt tears begin to well up in my eyes. Suddenly there was the sound of footsteps running towards me. I panicked for a moment before Jackie ran out of the bushes. He was out of breath and I could tell he was scared. He was shaking a lot.



“Jackie!” I said quickly, running over and hugging him. The shaking on his part stopped and he managed to catch his breath.



“Let’s go back please!” He said, with a tone of fear.



“Okay, come on, I thought you got lost!” I exclaimed.



“I did for a little bit but I managed to get back on the path cuz of you. Thank you Conner, I thought that I was gonna get lost out there.” He said through a shaky breath. 



The two of us turned back on the path. Turning our backs to the forest and at a much faster pace then before, began to start heading back towards the house. I had never been more happy to see the light return to the trees as we got back home. Back to safety. The two of us went back inside. I don’t know how long we were gone, but it didn’t seem like it was long enough for Miranda to worry. She was probablyup in her room, talking with her friends. I let out a sigh of relief.



“We’re safe.” I said, going to the couch and sitting down.



“Yeah…” Jack said, also letting out a sigh of relief.



The two of us looked at each other and started laughing. In hindsight, maybe the two of us hadn’t gone that far into the forest, and we were freaking out over nothing. The two of us spent the rest of the afternoon doing normal kid stuff. Mostly playing games. We began to forget about the incident. It didn’t really matter anymore. It was only a couple of minutes where Jack was gone. And I’m sure it was much more scary for him than it was for me. 



The afternoon turned into evening and my sister came down from her room to order us the pizza. She turned on a movie for us. One that mom would approve of. I think it was one of the Spider-man movies. Harmless enough is what I assumed her thought process for that one was. There were only some mature things she let her 10 year old little brother in on. Mostly just music, which as long as I didn’t make it a big deal, it wouldn’t become a big deal. 



I wish I could say the rest of that night was normal. But if it was. I wouldn’t be here, sitting in this room. Remembering all the memories of my life. Where it all went wrong. The moment that changed everything. Jack and I went to bed around 10. Mom still wasn’t home yet by that time. I could tell that Miranda was annoyed about it, but she couldn’t do anything about it. Someone needed to pay the bills. 



After a full day of school and play, I was tired. I remember how fast I went to sleep. I fell into the pillow and passed out. Sleep back then was easy. But after that night, I would never sleep soundly ever again. I remember back then I was a deep sleeper. It was difficult to wake me up. 



The quiet night changed in an instant. Several sounds immediately woke me up. Almost every sense of mine was overwhelmed. The first thing I noticed was the awful smell. The smell of smoke. The groginess of sleep was still wearing off. I didn’t get out of bed before Jack began hitting my arm quickly.



“CONNER WE NEED TO GO!” He said loudly. 



That’s when I began to realize how difficult it was to breathe. I got out of bed quickly. Falling onto the ground. Something they don’t tell you is how fast flames spread. How fast they begin to take a house. I opened the door to my room. All I could see was walls on fire. I stopped in a moment of fear. One where I couldn’t move at all. Like a deer in headlights, I couldn’t physically move. It felt like the fear physically pinned me to the ground. 



Jack started pounding on my back with his hand.



“CONNER!” He pleaded.



Then I heard one of the worst sounds I have ever heard in my life. One that made my blood run cold. A blood curdling scream. My sisters scream. I almost instinctively tried to run towards it after I heard it.



“MIRANDA!” I yelled. I sprinted towards her room.



“CONNER-!” Jack called.



I didn’t answer. At that moment I wasn’t really thinking. A 10 year old doesn’t really think about how to get out of that situation. I just knew that my sister was screaming. Banging on her door. I didn’t understand. Why couldn’t she get out? She could only lock it on her side. 



“MIRANDA!” I screamed. I began to try and push at the door.



There was a pause in her screaming as she began to cough. 



“Conner–you need to get out of here!” She said with a voice of urgency that I had never heard from her.



“But what about you?!” I exclaimed.



“Conner–I’m hurt…I’ll…fuck–.” She panted. She started wheezing. I could already tell that with whatever had happened, she was far too exhausted and in pain to scream at that point. How did this happen? My mind couldn’t even fathom what was going on. 



“Conner–please get help–.” She pleaded.



I myself began to start wheezing. Struggling to breathe. I finally managed to gain some control over my mind. I could only think of two words in my mind at that moment. \*Get Help.\* I started running through the house. I assumed that Jack had already made a break for it. Running to get help. That’s what we needed. I just needed to make it out–



As I reached the stairs, a large piece of flaming wood fell and slammed into my right shoulder. I let out a scream as pain surged through my body. It felt like a white hot iron being pressed into my skin and a sledgehammer smacking into my shoulder at the same time. I lost my footing and fell down the stairs, my shirt now caught on fire. I felt the intense heat burn through my shirt almost instantly as it began to singe my shoulder and began to move rapidly. With the wind having been knocked out of me, I couldn’t even scream. But that was some of the worst pain I had ever felt in my life. I tried to move my left arm but pain shot through it.



I tried to do anything. To scream. To beg for help. But at that moment it didn’t feel like I could do anything. I felt the heat creep up towards my cheek and all I could do was cry. I managed to pull myself up. I sucked in as much air as I could without coughing before screaming for help. There was no answer. I looked around me. There were only flames. I didn’t even recognize the house anymore. The layout of it felt foreign to me. 



I stumbled forwards. Tensing up as the pain crawled down my arm as well. I tried to scream but no words came out. I thought that I heard them come out. But I didn’t know if that was my thoughts overpowering everything about the situation. I felt so disoriented at that point and I was doing everything in my power to not faint as I stumbled forwards through the burning house. While it was only probably a couple of minutes, it felt like I was stuck in that hell for hours before I finally fell over and went unconscious. I thought that was it. I thought that was how I would die. 



That kind of unconsciousness makes you feel like you’re living in a horrific dream that you can’t seem to wake up from. I could still smell the smoke. I could still see the orange flames in my mind. For however long I was unconscious, it truly felt like I was living in my own hell of what I had just gone through. Sometimes when I go to sleep I still go to that place. The smokey world with orange lights.  



Even thinking about it right now makes me feel sick. I remember I woke up in the hospital. I had no memories of how I had got there. I tried to pull myself out of the  bed but a jolt of pain shot through my arm. I tried to move my neck but the right side of it sparked with hot pain, shooting up to the top part of my jaw. My mom quickly ran over towards me. Jack by her side. She threw her arms around me, gently. 



“Oh my baby, I’m so sorry I wasn’t there!” She began sobbing almost immediately. 



I felt so confused. I barely had the strength to speak. “Mom–what–what happened?” I managed to say through a very tensed and dry throat.



“Honey there–was a fire…” She started.



“Where’s–where’s Miranda?” I coughed.



She stopped in her tracks. I moved my eyes over towards Jack. He had this blank face. He looked away from me after he realized I was looking. 



“Mom–”



“She’s…\*She’s gone honey.”\* 



I apologize. I can’t write anymore. I’ll continue this later. Because this isn’t the end. Far from it. If it was just the fire? I might have been able to move on with my life. But it goes deeper than that. In ways I can hardly explain without telling the full story. Until then...this has been Conner Birrd
