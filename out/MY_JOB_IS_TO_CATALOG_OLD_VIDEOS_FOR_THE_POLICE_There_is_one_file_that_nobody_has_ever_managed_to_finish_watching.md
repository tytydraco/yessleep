I know this sounds made up.

If I hadn't seen it with my own eyes, I wouldn't believe it either.

I work digitizing old VHS tapes that are used as evidence in investigations. Most of them are useless: security cameras, interrogations, accidents...

Three months ago, an unmarked box arrived.

Inside, there was only a black tape.

No label.

No date.

No case number.

The protocol said only:

"DO NOT DUPLICATE."

My supervisor acted strangely when he saw it.

He said:

— If it freezes... turn everything off and leave.

I asked why.

He replied:

— Because when it freezes, someone always looks behind them.

I thought it was a sick joke.

I put the tape in.

The recording began.

A stationary camera pointed at the hallway of a house.

No movement.

No sound.

Five minutes.

Ten minutes.

Twenty minutes.

Nothing.

I was about to fast-forward.

That's when my supervisor came running in.

He grabbed my hand away from the controls.

— Never fast-forward it.

I asked why.

He stayed silent for a few seconds.

Then he answered:

— Because what matters... neverappears in the recording.

\---

I kept watching.

After forty minutes, I noticed something.

The clock hanging on the hallway wall read 1:17.

But it never moved.

Even after almost an hour of footage.

It was as if time had stopped.

I checked the file duration.

There were still more than six hours left.

That's when I heard a click behind me.

I turned around.

It was just the air conditioner.

When I looked back at the screen...

The door at the end of the hallway was open.

I was absolutely certain it had been closed.

I rewound the recording.

The door remained closed.

I fast-forwarded to the same point again.

Open.

I rewound it once more.

Closed.

A third time.

Open.

The tape was changing.

Depending on who was watching.

\---

The next morning, I asked the other employees.

They all knew about the tape.

One of them quit after watching it.

Another never entered that room again.

A man who had worked there for twenty years said only:

— The recording doesn't change.

You do.

\---

The following week, I decided to finish it.

I brought coffee.

Food.

I closed the door to the room.

After almost four hours...

Something finally happened.

A woman walked through the hallway.

Very slowly.

She looked frightened.

She kept looking behind her.

But there was no one there.

She approached the camera.

She looked directly into the lens.

And moved her lips.

There was no audio.

I opened a program to enhance the image.

I could clearly read what she was saying.

"Turn it off before he notices you."

My blood ran cold.

I paused the video.

I took a breath.

When I looked at the screen again...

The woman was still staring at me.

Even though the video was paused.

She blinked.

I turned everything off.

Ripped the tape out.

And left the room.

\---

I told my supervisor.

He asked:

— Was she still near the camera?

I said yes.

He lowered his head.

Like someone who already knew the answer.

Then he said:

— Then you only have a few days left.

I asked:

— A few days left for what?

He replied:

— Until he realizes that you can see him too.

\---

That night, I went to sleep.

At 3:12 a.m., I woke up for no reason.

My room was completely dark.

I picked up my phone.

The front camera opened by itself.

I thought I had pressed something by accident.

The screen showed my face.

Behind me...

The hallway of my apartment.

Empty.

I sighed in relief.

But there was a message at the bottom of the screen.

"Motion detected."

I looked behind me.

There was no one there.

I looked back at my phone.

Now there was.

At the end of the hallway...

An extremely tall man.

Standing still.

Completely motionless.

His face was too dark to make out any details.

I got up to turn on the light.

When I looked at the screen again...

He was closer.

Much closer.

I ran to the light switch.

The light came on.

The hallway was still empty.

I stayed awake for the rest of the night.

\---

The next morning, I quit.

My supervisor didn't try to convince me to stay.

He simply handed me an envelope.

Inside was a sheet of paper containing the names of everyone who had watched the tape until the end.

There were eighteen names.

Next to seventeen of them was a date.

The date they died.

The eighteenth name was mine.

The date was still blank.

At the bottom of the page was a handwritten note:

"When you stop seeing him on the screens... look behind you."
