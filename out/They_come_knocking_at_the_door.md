The knocks will come sharp at midnight, my mother always told me. They'd be light, nothing startling. You must answer them. The knocking won't stop unless you do...Just know to answer them.

Also never look through the window, she'd add as an afterthought.

I grew up listening to those strange raps at the front wooden door--nothing unhinged, simply pointed, demanding as if the world depended on acknowledging them.

My mother would answer them. Of course. A hiss, few whispers and my mother would go back to sleep as if nothing out of ordinary makes a habit of disturbing us every night.

"It's a habit," she'd try to placate me whenever I'd lose my color. "You grow used to it."

But I couldn't..

So I ran away from my home, at age eighteen, as soon as I finished high school.

Away from my ancestral cottage as an heir, away from my eccentric mother who'd never come with me, away from the corn field stretching away from our home with those crypts in the periphery, where all our nearest to great-great relation were buried deep with proud crosses erected to placate them.

So when my dying mother telephoned me to come back and 'take charge', I--as always inclined to prevaricate any excuse to postpone the jaunt to that accursed land waited until I was informed of her death.

So now I have returned home. After burial, I stayed in the living room with the local pastor and some of old acquaintances till the crowd thinned and just my choked breath remained.

I tiptoed around the house, inhaling the left over lingering perfume of my mother's smell till time diffuses it out of the house.

At sharp eleven, I chose my mother's bed to sleep with falling tears when my brain just numbed down and before I knew I was under the spell of night, I woke up.

Disoriented at first, I heard a rap at the door.

*A guest?* I thought.

I glanced at the clock. It was past twelve.

Another knock told me my worst fear. I was handed down this cursed ritual that I found not only nauseating but irksome.

The knocks came again and again after some intervals. I lingered in the room, thinking of not entertaining the mischief maker till the caller departs, disappointed.

More knocks came: forceful than before.

"Go away," I muttered under my breath.

The knocks became loud and demanding then.

I took slow steps to the door and tried to listen, tried to think...

The window was closed, the curtain was drawn over them.

For a second, I decided to peek, and then remembering my mother's warning, I decided against it.

The knocks came pounding hard and the door shook with an impact.

I snarled lightly.

The door began to move with a force, like a hurricane was out there or multiple people were pushing against it. But there was no noise outside, no breaths, no grunts, no swearing...

I came closer to the door, and realized my breath was short and the air was turning chillier.

"Who is it?" I whispered.

The incessant knocking stopped.

"Where's the North?" Came a heartbroken whisper from outside. It was a voice of a girl's and in my hindsight, I could see my cousin Angela who broke her heart for a sailor crying before jumping over the cliff...or as the legend says.

I shook my head and ran my tongue over my dried lips.

But Cousin Angela had died a long ago...

"It's, um...right above and beyond." I don't know why I said it. There was a sigh and silence. I stopped there for an hour, afraid to move any muscle. It took me a while to move my frozen body and I went to bed, with hair raised in alarm.

I decided to stay another day.

When the second night came, the knock came too: light and shy.

I was ready as I pounced at the door, and before I could turn the knob, my hands froze. A call of warning made me cower back and I asked who it was.

"Why you never came back?" It was odd that those words were like my mother's, but the tone and the light voice wasn't hers.

I went to the window then and peeked through the curtain.

There was nothing on my doorsteps.

I went back, shaking my head and there was an urgency in the air. I had to answer, so whatever it was should go away...

"I came but late..."

And silence of nothingness...

I sat where I was, on the floor, with my head on my hands.

It's the next day. My boss is calling me to come back while the house, the door, the corn field and the crypts all waits on me.

I don't know what to do.

To keep on answering those raps at the door.

But if I go away what are the odds of these callers finding me on the opposite side of another door?
