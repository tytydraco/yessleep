*Throwaway account because people I know might recognize the details.*

I’m a loser, yes. Like those people you barely notice passing you in the classroom or the school lab. Over the past three years, I’ve practically drowned myself in my dorm and rarely gone to class. Don’t wonder why I haven’t been expelled. I’ve been paying other students to attend lectures for me. Not for exams, though. 

Luckily, even if I only cracked open my textbooks and started studying two days before finals, I still passed every course. Maybe that’s just because my professors are lenient.

I hate myself. My friends said I should see a psychologist — that I might have ADHD, depression, something like that. But I can’t afford it. All my spare money went toward alcohol and paying people to skip classes for me.

Thankfully, I finally pulled myself together in my final undergraduate year. There were stretches where I thought about killing myself. My mom is the one supporting me, even after she got cheated on and hurt badly by my dad when I was little. I know I’m a total asshole. Every time she asks if I’m doing well at school, I lie and say I am.

The job market is brutal right now, and honestly I don’t even know why I’m sitting for the postgrad exam. Maybe I’m just trying to run away from looking for work. But at the very least, I’m making an effort. I go to the library now instead of wasting my days on alcohol or video games.

My friend — well, I’d like to call her J here — was happy to see the change in me. She’s sweet, and she tried to pull me out of my terrible routine. She always likes bringing me small gifts and snacks, even though I never have money to pay her back. Last weekend, she brought over a scanner. She said she found it at a flea market. The seller told her it could bring good luck. I thought she’d gotten tricked, but I couldn’t say that to her when she looked at me with those puppy eyes. After all, scanning with a mobile requires a membership, doesn’t it?

But now I’m sitting here writing this. It clearly didn’t bring me good luck.

*It brought* *it**.*

The scanner, sold as something that would “bring luck”, is an ordinary flatbed scanner — well, maybe not so ordinary. It’s old, bulky, boxy, made of faded cream plastic. Years have stained the corners a dull brown. The lid has a foam pad on the inside; it’s aged and crumbles if I touch it. On the front, there’s only one tiny dim green LED and a single power button. Its glass top is hazy, marked with faint scratches and spots that won’t wipe away. When it’s plugged in, it hums this low, quiet drone that sinks into the background.

“But it’ll be helpful,” I told myself. “C’mon, J cleaned it up.”

I lifted the lid, set my Pharmacy Mistake Collection down on the glass, closed the lid, pressed the button, and waited. Nothing happened at first. I grew frustrated and tapped it impatiently. The noise startled J’s parrot, and it squawked.

Then it worked. I pulled up the scan on my phone. Well… it wasn’t perfect. The text came out clear enough, but there was a faint, rounded shadow right in the center.

“What’s fucking wrong with you?!” I snapped, even though there was nobody there. J was out with her boyfriend. “Fine, calm down. Just send her a message and tell her this is stupid.” I lay down on my bed, texted her, and waited for a reply—Alright, maybe she was busy. I locked my phone and shut my eyes.

Suddenly, I heard the scanner hum, followed by a notification ping from my phone. I sat up, checking my phone, hopeful.

It wasn’t her. It was from that old, ugly scanner. Unease twisted in me. What the hell was wrong with it? I tapped the preview. There were even no notes this time at all, only a silly single shadow sitting dead in the center. It was no longer just a rounded dome. The shape had lifted, an uneven oval marked with soft, hollow indentations.

“What?” I lifted the lid again. The scratches and blemishes on the glass were still there. I grabbed a towel and tried wiping it clean, but it did nothing.

A ping came from my phone. I unlocked it. It was her.

“Hehe, maybe it’s not so perfect, but it can bring you good luck. XX”

I stared at the “XX”, then lay back down on my bed.

I woke up a little late the next morning. J had just finished her morning jog and brought me a sandwich.

“Hey, ready to head to the library?” she asked. I tilted my head while brushing my teeth to answer her.

As my eyes drifted over her, I thought I glimpsed something squatting right beside her. A chill ran down my spine. When I looked again, there was nothing there.

“Oh, are you alright?” She gave my shoulder a light pat.

“I’m fine — I just saw another shadow, y’know, like before.” I finished brushing quickly. I grabbed the sandwich and opened the door.

“You really should see a doctor, darling,” she said from behind me.

*I found myself thinking back to how J and I got close.*J is kind and pretty, well-liked by both students and teachers. She was everything I was not. We used to be roommates. Once she even tried to drag me to a party, but I was in a terrible mood back then and snapped at her. “Why should I go do those meaningless things, just like you?”

It wasn’t even a lie; I really thought that way. Thankfully, she forgave me. Just being her friend was already more than I deserved.

While I studied, weird shadows kept tugging at the edges of my vision. But every time I turned to look, they were gone. I wasn’t sure if it was just my hallucinations acting up again. *Maybe I really should see a doctor, like she said.*

I packed my things and checked my phone for messages.

There were new notifications. They weren’t from my mom or any of my friends. They were from the scanner.

I bit my nails and opened them. The first image looked just like the one from the night before. Then the second, the third…

*There were eleven scan files in total.*

I scrolled down, and cold dread twisted in my gut. When I reached the last one, I nearly dropped my phone. It was wrong. Those faint hollow indentations were sharper now, spread evenly across that oval shape — no, it was no longer just a shadow. It looked like an unfamiliar face, staring straight back at me.

My blood turned icy. I was seconds away from screaming, but I was still in the library. I had to leave. Everyone would think I was crazy. And maybe I was.

I hurried toward the elevator. It arrived fast, and the car was empty. I could not stop shaking. Then my phone slipped out of my hand.

I bent down to grab it. “God, I don’t have money for—”

*No. No. No.*

Something was on my right. It was squatting in the elevator corner. Close enough to touch my hand.

I collapsed onto the floor and threw up. I didn’t pass out. When my focus came back, I was lying in my bed, and J sat beside me. There was this odd, floating ache, not in my heart, somewhere between my lungs and my diaphragm. Like a wave trapped in place, only able to shift within a tiny space.

J asked if I felt any better. I said nothing. I couldn’t.

Her phone rang, and she answered it. I couldn’t make out what she was saying. Then she left the room. Probably her boyfriend again.

*You can’t just lie here. Come on. Get up and go see a doctor.*

I tried to twitch one finger. To my relief, it moved. I had no idea how long it took — maybe ten minutes — before I finally got to my feet.

However, before I went to see a doctor, I had to yank the plug out of that shitty scanner. There was definitely something wrong with it. I leaned against the wall and shuffled toward the machine. Pull the plug out.

*Alright. Nothing happened, right? You’re just stressed out from the exam.*

My phone pinged again.

*Wait. Did I turn the sound back on?* I could barely move.

Maybe it was J. Maybe she unmuted it so I wouldn’t miss her message.

I forced myself to move again. At last I reached my phone and unlocked it.

*No. It couldn’t be this.*

A surge of anger flared up inside my head. I lunged for that damned machine and flipped its lid open. But what I saw dumped a bucket of ice water over me, from head to toe.

It was a face. Like whatever it belonged to was squatting there, lifting  head to stare straight at me. It looked as though it had been soaked in water for days. Its features were bloated, blurred and hard to make out. I couldn’t hold back a scream.

It felt like my throat split open. I had to stop, the pain was too sharp. Then rage flooded my mind once more. I grabbed the stool beside me and slammed it down hard onto the scanner. And then it vanished.

I sank to the floor, gasping for air. The scanner’s glass was shattered completely. Shards had cut into me. The parrot kept screeching nonstop — god, couldn’t it just be quiet?

The door swung open all at once. It was J, carrying food and some medicine. She froze where she stood at first, and then her expression shifted. She shouted something at me, but I couldn’t hear her. My ears were ringing, the same low hum the scanner used to make.

She slammed the door and stormed out. But I felt calm. At last, the machine was dead. *It* was gone.

*I was wrong.*
