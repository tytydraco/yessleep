I’m sitting here typing this on my phone because I absolutely can’t go back to sleep. I’m afraid of what will happen next if I do. 

On Halloween night I went into the ER because my cheek had ballooned to twice its normal size and I was having some pretty severe tooth pain. I won’t bore you with the details except to say that Halloween at a small-town ER was much tamer and much more expensive than I expected. A week later,  I ended up having a tooth yanked because it had rotted down to the nerve. 

I only mention any of this because it’s the reason I’ve been sleeping in a chair in my living room at night. I tried sleeping on the couch, but apparently my head wasn’t elevated enough, because I woke up bleeding from my mouth and nauseated from my antibiotics.

Since then I’ve slept upright just to be safe.

It hasn’t been particularly comfortable, but I’ve managed.

Until tonight.

I was sitting in my chair eating a bowl of yogurt. I was *eating* so that my stomach wouldn’t be empty when I took my midnight dose of meds. I was eating *yogurt* because I had read online that the probiotics would help replace the good bacteria that were inevitably suffering friendly fire at the hands of the antibiotics. 

Either way, I felt nauseous. 

I had the TV on low, watching one of my favorite YouTube channels. Across the room on the kitchen counter, I could see the manuscript for the novel I’ve been working on. After spending weeks trying to edit it on my computer, I finally decided to shell out the cash to print the whole thing out. I find it so much easier to work with a physical manuscript.

As I sat there, I wondered if working on something active, like editing, would take my mind off the way my stomach was swirling. I doubted it. 

I was just about to take my medication and try to nod off when something interrupted my otherwise peaceful night.

Something tapped against the window. 

I turned toward it sharply, and the simple movement made my head feel so heavy that I imagined it dropping off my shoulders and rolling across the floor. Nothing hurt–I was too doped up for that–but the whole world felt tilted, like a cube resting on a single point. 

A gust of wind sent a splatter of rain against my window. At first, I was relieved. I told myself the tapping had actually been a combination of rain and wind. But then I saw the curtain move. 

I sat up straighter. It shouldn’t be moving. I didn’t have the window open–it’s the middle of November in Washington state–so it couldn’t have been wind that disrupted the fabric. 

I blinked. Maybe I imagined it. I watched for a few more seconds. Just as I was about to settle in again, the curtain rustled a second time.

It looked like a balloon inflating slowly beneath the velvet. I watched as the bulge pulled the curtain away from the window. Light slipped past the room-darkening fabric, casting a street-light glow on the wall across from me. 

Something there caught my eye–pulled my gaze from the window to the wall. In the light, there was the shadow of a branch waving lazily.

Only there are no branches outside my window. 

My eyes darted back to the curtain, only when I looked, the balloon shape was gone and the curtain–though gapped–was as still as if it had been glued in place. When I turned back to the light on the wall, the shadow of the branch was gone, too.

Uneasily, I shifted in my chair. I waited a few minutes to see if anything else would happen, and then I closed my eyes, and tried to chalk it all up to the drugs. That’s what you have to do in those situations. You have to just act like there’s an explanation for whatever happened to you and move on. Otherwise you get obsessed. You start seeing things in every little shadow.

I woke up again in the middle of the night. That’s not uncommon when I sleep in a strange place, but this wasn’t the normal kind of waking up. As I sat straighter and fumbled for my glasses, I was well aware that *something* had woken me up. I’d been in a deep sleep only to be pulled out of it by…what…a sound? A feeling?

I looked around the room. The twinkle lights above my windows bathed everything in a golden glow, but there, with the shock of awakening still fresh, the light no longer felt safe. I could hear my heart beating inside of me. 

The shadows looked long, warped and stretched in strange ways, doubled up in some places. You know that feeling you get when you’re absolutely sure you’re not alone–when something you can’t quite pinpoint is so off that you aren’t even sure you’re in your own house anymore–maybe not even in your own skin? That’s what I felt then. 

I sat motionless and silent, scanning the room with my eyes only, for several moments. My back was to a bookshelf, and I could see the rest of the room clearly. The only corner of the apartment that was obscured was the doorway to the entryway. I must have forgotten to turn on the porchlight, because the entry was a single dark shadow, so solid that it almost looked like a physical shape filling up the room. 

Then, in the darkness, I saw the last thing I wanted to see. 

Movement.

I couldn’t see shapes, or direction, or anything else, but I was so sure that something moved. 

*It’s just the meds. You’re just exhausted,* I told myself.

Even so, I stared at the entry way for a long time before I finally lost the fight with the pain meds and fell asleep again. 

The next last time I woke up, it was to a loud swishing sound. I opened my bleary eyes and blinked, expecting the sound to fade away with my dreams. Instead, it grew louder and more intense. It turned from a faint rustle into something between crusty leaves in the wind, and bird wings beating the air. 

The sound was so unfamiliar, and so loud,  and this time I was so positive that it was real that I jumped out of my chair on instinct alone. I spun around, looking for the source of the sound. The sudden movement made me dizzy, and I grabbed the arm of my chair as floaters and fireworks obscured my vision. 

The sound faded to a whisper as my eyes cleared.

The shadows all looked normal again. The curtains were still. I turned toward the kitchen. Everything looked fine there too.

Except…

I looked up at the counter and realized that my manuscript wasn’t there. The realization that my book was gone set off another realization in me–somewhere deep down, I recognized the sound after all. It was the sound of paper scattering.

This time, I knew I had to see for myself. I couldn’t just go back to sleep and pretend everything was normal. Slowly, feeling the dread turning my stomach, I walked to the kitchen. The first sheet of paper had floated all the way to the doorway. I stared at the paper by my feet. Then I crossed the threshold into the kitchen, terrified of what I knew I was about to see.

The floor was plastered with all 158 sheets of paper. It wasn’t like something had simply knocked it over, either (I live alone, and I don’t have any pets, and yet my brain was desperate to find some sort of explanation). No, the paper had been spread out over the entire kitchen in what appeared to be an even layer. The pages were rumpled and creased in places–it looked like a whirlwind had barreled through here and left the paper to settle in its wake.

That’s when something caught my eye in the sea of identical white papers and black text. In the center of the room rested a single sheet of paper with a blood-red scrawl. I took a step forward. My jelly-legs threatened to collapse underneath me, but I put my hand on the wall and took another step.

I knew this page. It was page 315–the last page of my novel. 

I approached it upside down. Slowly, heart pounding, I reached down and picked up the paper. 

In my hands I held the last paragraph of my story. Beneath that, I had typed the words “THE END” in all caps.

The next words–slashed across the page in handwriting I didn’t recognize–were “is here.”

THE END   
is here

I stood there, back to the wall, holding the page in trembling hands for far too long. 

I haven’t gone back to sleep. I’m not going to sleep until the sun comes up, and even then I’m not sure I can stomach it. Instead, I’m sitting here, watching YouTube. That paper is sitting next to me, face down on the end table. I’m doing everything I can to block out the thought of it, but watching videos of people eating disgusting things isn’t the distraction I need it to be.

I’m waiting. I don’t know what I’m waiting for. And I don’t want to find out.
