With a snip of my shears, a white lily gone wilted fell from the branch of one of the many bouquets surrounding the casket of one Pastor Graves. I made my way along, plant to plant repeating the process for roses and carnations until all semblances of rot had been stripped. Taking a few steps back I eyed my work and smiled. The casket was dead center with the adorning bouquets, now purged of any blemishes, flanking each side in perfect symmetry. I went to the back of the chapel and shut the entryway doors before returning to Mr. Graves’ side. The gnarled old man laying before me had lived a full life, dying at the ripe age of ninety. It felt a bit odd to be hosting a pastor in the funeral home’s chapel, but from what I had heard through the grapevine, Morning’s Light Church of God wasn’t exactly a blossoming haven of worship. The Graves clan accounted for almost half of the church's membership and apparently weren’t exactly the ‘God is love’ types. When their Patriarchal leader vacated the pulpit, the rest of the family slowly followed suit. The worship center shuttered not long after. Even now in the eternal embrace of death, Mr. Grave’s face portrayed hints of a permanent scowl that denoted fury rather than peace. 

A grimace of my own formed as I took in the dour countenance of the deceased minister. We had an outstanding mortician in our employ, but a harsh fact of life is that some people just aren’t pleasant to look at. Unfortunately, Mr. Graves fell dead center into that category. A job was a job, though, and I prided myself on the service our funeral home provided. A quick glance at my watch told me that the visitation was upon us. The rest of my employees would be joining me shortly in preparation for any early arrivals so my time alone with the man was growing short. I checked behind me once more making sure the chapel doors were still closed then I leaned down and gave Mr. Graves forehead a nice, long lick. 

The dead have no taste, yet I still can’t help but shudder when my tongue roves across the wrinkles of the Pastor’s brow. There’s a stiffness that presses back against me, a pseudo-resistance to my advances and a cold wet sensation that makes me feel like I’m lapping at raw dough. Each time the experience is different, yet never is it pleasant.

In my youth I wasn’t exactly the brightest kid on the block. Not dumb per se, but as I stared up at the ornate jar on the mantle that bore the name of my late Grandma Myrtle, the only thing that came to mind was: cookie jar. Sure it was in the living room far away from the kitchen, and the powder it contained didn’t look like any sugar I had ever seen; nevertheless, cookie jars were for sweets. Every kid knew that. So despite all the evidence to the contrary I plucked out a handful of powder and popped into my mouth expecting a sweet sugary glaze to coat my tastebuds. The bitter chalk that choked me instead was a shock to the system, but not as much as what came next. 

“Jamie! Put me down!. Naughty boy!” A shrill voice squawked at the back of my head. 

I turned around to see Grandma Myrtle standing in the living room wearing the same violet church outfit she had been cremated in, her skin the pallid white pastel of death. She had passed several months prior, yet here she was clear as day, catching me red handed in the act of devouring her remains. 

I screamed. The urn I had flung in surprise clattered to the ground and shattered into a pile of tiny pieces. Bits of clay chips mixed with the grandma dust and displaced the air in a puffy cloud of smoke. 

My parents found me curled into a ball with tears in my eyes. I babbled and pointed at the apparition in the room, but my mom was only concerned with the now scattered remnants of her dearly departed mother, whose ghostly visage was in turn concerned about the shattered pieces of her precious urn. 

Grandma yelled at me. Mom cried. Dad eventually spanked me and sent me to my room. Neither of them saw Grandma, nor believed me when I told them that I did. Not the happiest of core memories. You could say it left a lasting impression. 

It became clear to me very quickly that talking about seeing ghosts was frowned upon. By the time I was in my early teens it had been years since I mentioned the incident, but it still stuck in the back of my head, keeping me up at night. A life changing incident I could neither talk about nor replicate. At least not until I met Billy. 

Billy’s dad was a long haul trucker who had made the unfortunate mistake of being too reliable. The company worked him to the bone and one night it caught up with him. He fell asleep at the wheel and now rested eternally on the mantle. I clocked the urn the first time I ever went over to Billy’s house. The memories from my childhood playing on repeat in my mind. When I finally joined Billy for a sleepover my mind had already been made up. As soon as I was sure my buddy was asleep I snuck into the living room and had a taste. 

I didn’t scream this time and neither did the apparition. Billy’s father wasn’t quite as uptight as Grandma Myrtle. He just sat in the big recliner that I assumed had once been his oasis of respite. Even in death, he looked so tired. For a while I just stood in silence while he gently rocked in the recliner. It felt like a dream. After a moment, the rocking slowed and he gave me a sad smile.

“If you can, always be a good friend to my boy. I know he’s a handful” 

Then he was gone.

The grim countenance of Mr. Graves stared at me while I rolled my tongue in my mouth, trying to rid it of the clammy sensation of his flesh. Mr. Graves clearly didn’t share the same sentiments about love and marriage that I did. He would get over it. Four hours from now I would close the casket and he would never have the displeasure of doing business with me again. For now though, I had a funeral to direct.

That night in Billy’s living room had swayed the course of my future. The weary smile his father gave me with his parting words was all it took. I knew I had to somehow use my gift for the greater good, but it took me some time to work through the kinks.  Was I an empath? Not exactly. A medium? Maybe. Either way this connection to the dead was related to my mouth. Consumption took things a little too far. Eating a corpse always resulted in a full bodied apparition and not all of them ended up being agreeable. After some experimentation I had found that a lighter approach, like a lick for example, would provide a more tame experience. Licking a corpse gave me a glimpse into the thoughts and feelings of the deceased. Knowing just the right thing to say to a grieving loved one makes all the difference when saying goodbye. I used my insider knowledge to provide words of comfort to those left behind. Honestly, it  sounds so childish saying it out loud. Here I was potentially the only man in the world who could genuinely see the dead and I wanted to use it for the equivalent of reassuring a crying child: 

“Grandma loves you.” 

Maybe I should have been trying to do more, but to me this was what felt right.

The Graves clan was just as dour as their departed Patriarch. Men with dusty suits and sunspotted skin. Sunken faced women adorned in dresses of sackcloth, I felt like I was in the presence of a cult. They all looked sad, but it seemed as if that was just their base level of existence, not a result of being in mourning. Mr. Graves clearly held no love for his flock. Feelings of superiority and suspicion flowed through my body as the men and women passed by the casket. The old man really had just been a hateful minister vomiting messages of fire and brimstone at a family he held in utter disdain. More taunting than warning them of the future he thought they deserved. Usually by now I had gained something, some piece of insight or emotion that would allow me to bridge the gap between life and death and bring a moment of peace to those in mourning, but this spirit was listless and hollow. I felt nothing but contempt. Then, tucked under the arm of a stern faced woman,  a young girl was dragged to the front of the coffin.

Lust. Dominance. Power. Death. Ruin. The wave of emotions crashing into me threatened to drown me in the swell. My breath caught in my throat at the erection that I could feel growing in my pants. Front and center the decrepit Pastor stood, escorting the girl by the hand to a threadbare mattress that occupied the otherwise empty basement. I felt the fingers again, digging into my spine and overwhelming my faculties. I stood stiff,  puppeteered to attention. Beside me, the stern faced woman also stood entranced.
