I’m a 34 year old male from Florida. The hurricane took everything from
me. I had about ninety thousand dollars in my bank account. I had enough
of the weather, the stress, the constant fucking dread. Everything was
gone. All I had left was the money.

I took myself to a small town in Nevada about fifty miles from the
closest city. Bought this little shack. Holes in the walls. Floors so
filthy the dirt had its own layers. When I walked in the first time the
roaches didn’t even scatter. They just sat there looking at me like I’d
interrupted something. I was never handy. Never good with tools or my
hands. But I fixed it up. Made it into something that felt like a home.
For the first time in a long time I felt happy. Spent most of my days
fishing. It was one of the only things that ever made the noise in my
head quiet down.

The town didn’t have good jobs. Met a few people. One of them was Jimmy.
Similar interests. We’d fish together. One day he asked if I wanted to
start a little store. Sell the fish we caught and fishing tools we made
by hand. I only had forty-five thousand left by then. I was hesitant as
hell. But eventually I couldn’t refuse. Fresh start, right? He already
had the building. Just needed me to make it work. We did. For a whole
month it was good. I fished. He carved the tools. It felt almost normal.

September 23rd was the day it stopped being normal.
Four days. No Jimmy. Phone went straight to voicemail every single time.
The only place I could think he’d go was the woods behind his cabin. I
drove out there as soon as the sun started dropping. His place looked
abandoned. Like nobody had lived in it for years. Door creaked louder
than my footsteps when I pushed it open.

Inside smelled like something had been left to rot in the heat for a
week and then someone decided to stir it. The closer I got to the
kitchen the worse it got. Thick. Sweet and metallic at the same time,
with this undercurrent of shit and spoiled meat. There were animals all
over the table. Not cleaned. Not butchered clean. Mangled. Deer,
raccoon, something that might’ve been a dog once. Guts spilled out in
ropes that had dried into black tar. One of them still had its eyes
open, filmed over white, mouth stretched in a permanent silent scream
with the tongue half-chewed off. Flies were so thick they made a soft
humming sound over everything. I threw up pure water. Felt dizzy. Legs
went soft.

Then I heard it. Metal scraping something wet. Slow. Getting closer from
the backyard.

I hid in the pantry. Worst place I could’ve chosen.

“Who’s here?”

It sounded just like Jimmy.

I peeked through the crack. Someone was standing in the doorway staring
straight at the pantry. They knew I was in there. Wearing a deer head.
Fresh. Antlers still sticky with brain matter and clotted blood. The
hide was sewn on crooked so the neck flaps hung loose, dripping. I could
hear each thick drop hit the floorboards. Plop. Plop. Plop. The smell
coming off him was the kitchen smell, mixed with sweat and something
sour like an infected wound.
I ran. Made it three steps toward the front door before something hit me
hard enough that everything went black.

I woke up outside. Couldn’t move. Eyes crusted shut with dried blood and
whatever else they had smeared on me. I forced them open. At least fifty
people standing in a half-circle staring down at me. Every single one
wearing an animal head. Pig. Buffalo. Coyote. Things I couldn’t name.
Some of the heads were still twitching. One of them had maggots working
into the empty eye sockets, white bodies squirming in and out of the
dark holes.

I was tied to a wooden stake. Rough wood digging into my spine. Right in
front of me was the thing with the deer head. Jimmy. Or whatever was
left of him.
I begged. “Please. I was just looking for my friend. Please let me go.”

Nobody answered. He held out both hands. Fishing hooks. Dozens of them.
Different sizes. Some still had bits of old fish scale and dried blood
crusted in the barbs. He took one and jammed it into my thigh. Slow.
Twisted it. I felt the point scrape along the bone. Then another. And
another. And another. Each one went in with a wet little pop and a fresh
gush of hot blood that ran down my leg and soaked into the dirt. I was
screaming so loud the forest threw the sound back at me.

He grabbed my face with hands that smelled like the animals on the
table. Forced my mouth open. Jammed three hooks through my upper lip and
down through the bottom one so they locked together. The metal tore
through the soft tissue like it was wet paper. Blood poured down my chin
and into my throat. Every time I tried to scream the hooks pulled and
the holes stretched wider. I could feel the barbs catching on the inside
of my lip every time I moved.

Behind me was the first lake I’d ever fished at in this place.

They started chanting. Low. Wet. Sounded like words made of wet gurgles.

They shoved me under. The water was freezing and full of silt. I fought
the ropes until one leg came free, then an arm. The hooks in my thighs
dragged through the water and caught on underwater branches. Every pull
felt like someone was trying to peel the muscle off the bone. I swam
until my lungs burned and the blood from my mouth turned the water pink
around my face.

Somehow I made it back to my place. Tried the truck. Wouldn’t start. So
I ran. Hooks still in me. Feet raw. Every step made the metal in my
thighs grind. Blood kept slipping under my fingers when I tried to yank
the ones in my mouth free. The flesh had already started to swell around
them.

Town was full of them. Animal heads. Walking toward me from every
direction. Same chant. Same wet, throaty sound. They closed the circle.
I had nowhere left to go. No family. No friends. Nothing.

I decided if I was going to die I’d take some of the pain with me.
Ripped the hooks out of my thighs and my mouth in one hard pull. Felt
the barbs tear fresh tunnels on the way out. Screamed “FUCK YOU” through
a mouthful of blood and ran straight through them, stabbing with the
bloody hooks still clenched in my fists. Kept running until the chanting
faded and the road was empty.

I’m writing this from a rundown apartment three states away. Couch
smells like old cigarettes and someone’s sweat. I still have the scars.
The ones on my thighs look like someone tried to sew me shut with wire
and gave up halfway through. My lips never healed right. When I talk you
can see the shiny pink tunnels where the hooks went through.

I can’t fish anymore. The second the line hits the water I feel the
metal in my mouth again. Smell the deer head. Hear the wet chant.

I still check the locks three times.

Still jump when I hear the slightest sound.

If anyone out there has ever gotten free of something like this and
actually stayed free…

please.

Tell me how.

Because some nights I swear I can still hear the hooks scraping.
