Part 1

The timer started screaming.

“Uh…”

I looked over. Gage pointed toward the oven.

“I don’t think that’s supposed to make that noise.”

“Oh.”

I yanked the tray out. The bottoms of the pretzels were already
blackening. He stared at them for a second.

“…Those are definitely cooked.”

I ditched them in the trash.

“Sorry.”

Gage grinned.

“So, like…”

He pointed toward the trash can.

“Employee meal?”

I didn’t answer. I slid a new tray of pretzels into the oven when Gage
piped up. 

“You’ve seriously been out of it all day. Are you sick or something?”

“I don’t know.”

That was the first honest answer I’d given anyone all day. He stared at
me for a second.

“…Huh.”

The rest of my shift blurred together. I burned another tray, forgot a
customer’s drink, and rang up the same order twice. Every mistake felt
automatic, like my body had clocked in without me. Every few minutes I’d
look toward the fountain. Just shoppers moving from one store to the
next. He never came back.

I glanced across the food court. Claire was cleaning her windows, moving
with the same routine she’d probably done a hundred times before. She
was the only other person who hadn’t looked straight through the
walkers. If I didn’t tell somebody… I wasn’t sure I’d make it to the end
of my shift.

“Gage.”

He looked up.

“Can you hold the fort down for a minute?”

He frowned.

“Why?”

“I just need a break.”

He rolled his eyes and groaned, but I was already walking away before he
could chirp anything else back. As I approached, Claire looked up. She
set the spray bottle on the counter before I said a word.

“You look awful, Jerry.”

“I know.”

“You okay?”

I almost answered the way I had all day. I’m fine. Instead, I shook my
head.

“No.”

She waited. Not impatiently. Just… waiting. I looked past her toward the
windows. I wasn’t sure how to start.

“I saw one of the mall walkers yesterday.”

“You see them every day.”

“I mean… before he was one of them.”

She didn’t interrupt.

“There was a security guard.”

I swallowed.

“He slipped in the men’s restroom. He hit his head. There was a lot of
blood. I tried to call for help.”

I didn’t. I don’t know why I said that.  

“When the mall’s management got there…”

I hesitated.

“…he was gone.”

Claire didn’t say anything.

“So I thought maybe I’d imagined it.”

I laughed once. It sounded hollow.

“But then today…”

I looked back toward the fountain.

“I saw him.”

“The security guard?”

I nodded.

“He was walking with them.”

A silence dragged. Claire folded the rag in her hands.

“Are you sure it was the same guy?”

“No… Well, yes. I broke into the security office to find out.”

“Jerry…”

“I know. There was a transfer form. Security guard. Dated yesterday.”

She didn’t say anything for a while. Then she looked over her shoulder,
toward the bookcases that walled off the back of her store.

“Management sent someone to inspect that back area last month,” she
said. “Twice. Both times after I turned down the buyout. They told me it
was a structural issue.”

“Did you go back there and look?” 

“No. It’s technically not my space. Well, it’s not on my lease.” She
twirled the rag. “I might tonight.”

“Tonight?”

“It’s my store, Jerry. If something’s back there, I’d rather know.” She
set the rag down. “Come with me.”

I didn’t answer.

“You’re the one who’s been following them around for a week. You saw
what happened to that guard. Come look with me.”

“I can’t.”

“Why not?”

My words started coming faster. “I’ve been trying to stop thinking about
this all day. I’ve burned pretzels, forgotten orders. Every time I start
doing something, I see him again. I just needed somebody else to know.
That’s all. Somebody else had to know.”

I realized how loud I’d gotten. The whole café was looking at us.

Claire’s voice stayed quiet. “Somebody does know now. I’m asking you to
help.”

“I can’t.”

“Jerry…”

“Forget it. I don’t even know why I came over here.”

“You came over here because,”

“I know why I came over here.”

That was a lie; I didn’t. I walked back to the pretzel stand, pissed
off. At Claire, at myself. Mostly myself.

Gage looked up from his phone as I approached.

“Damn. Rejected already?”

“Shut the fuck up, Gage.”

“Jesus. Okay.”

I snatched my apron and went back to work. Or, at least, tried to. 

I couldn’t stop replaying the conversation. Not what Claire said. What
I’d said. I’d told her everything. Randall. The bathroom. The walkers.
The security office. The security office. Why the fuck did I tell her
that? Claire owned a business here. She had a lease, customers, and a
reputation. I was some guy from the pretzel stand who’d just admitted to
breaking into a security office because I thought a dead man had joined
a group of elderly mall walkers. What if she told somebody? They could
fire me. Ban me from Briarwood. Then I’d never find out if Randall was
real. I’d never find out whether the mall walkers are just old people
counting down the days or something more sinister. 

I looked across the food court. Claire stood behind the counter, her
phone pressed to her ear. I couldn’t see her face. My stomach tightened.
Who was she calling? I watched her disappear behind one of the shelves.
Was it mall management? Security? The cops? A few seconds later, she
reappeared, still holding it, but now I could see her face. She was
smiling and laughing. Then she hung up the phone, as if she was talking
to a loved one. I stared at her. Then I stared at myself in the
stainless steel reflection of the warmer. Jesus Christ. I’d just watched
a woman from thirty yards away and convinced myself she was reporting me
because she was on the phone. 

I looked like shit. I couldn’t concentrate long enough to know whether
to salt or sugar the pretzels. Maybe something was wrong with Briarwood.
I knew something was wrong with Briarwood. But something was wrong with
me too. And I know that now.

When I got to work around twelve-thirty the next day, Claire’s shop was
gone. Not closed, but abandoned. The wooden sign that had hung above the
entrance was missing. So was the little chalkboard she kept outside with
the day’s coffee specials. Even the hours she’d taped crookedly to the
glass had been peeled away.

The gate was shuttered. Behind the gate, the store looked as if someone
had picked it up and shaken it. Books scattered the floor next to a
fallen shelf. Several of the mismatched chairs had been knocked onto
their sides. A table had been pushed against another shelf, spilling
whatever had been on top. Behind the café counter, cups and lids covered
the floor.

I wrapped my fingers around the metal and looked through, shaking the
gate a little. Her espresso machine and cash register were still on the
counter. A laminated sign was zip-tied to the gate.

SPACE AVAILABLE
COMING SOON: THE LOUVRE AT BRIARWOOD.

If Claire had decided overnight that running a bookstore in a dying mall
wasn’t worth it anymore, she’d apparently decided none of her stuff was
worth taking. 

“Claire?”

My voice echoed to the back wall.

Something had happened here. My mind immediately jumped to the mall
walkers. Did they do something to her? Was it because she knew of them?
Did she confront them? Did they confront her? Was it my fault?

“Bro.”

I nearly had a heart attack. Gage was standing behind me. Whatever was
in Claire’s old shop must’ve masked his stench.  

“What?”

“Cmon bro, I need you to lock in for a second and work.”

I looked at the time: 12:34 pm.

“Yeah.”

He glanced through the gate.

“Damn. Guess Barnes & Homeless finally went under.”

I stared at him.

“Because it’s like Barnes & Noble, except,”

“I get it.”

“Oh.”

He nodded.

“Then yeah.”

I spent the rest of the afternoon glancing at the vacancy. Not
constantly. I was getting better at pretending I wasn’t. Nobody stopped
by all day, except for confused customers: no management, no
maintenance, no one. But apparently somebody found the time to hang that
sign up.

Around 9, all of our lights inside shut off. Well, most of them. Some
scattered emergency lights are always on. When I was taking out the
trash, I noticed a light on in the back of Claire’s store. I stopped.
The single light in the back cast long shadows between the shelves. I
pulled out my phone and turned on the flashlight.

“Claire?”

Obviously, she didn’t respond. I shone it through the gate. The beam
passed over the tables and the books scattered across the floor.
Something on the floor at the bottom of one of the shelves reflected in
the light. A dark puddle. At least I thought it was a puddle. It was
thick enough not to have spread far. I moved the flashlight higher.
Something had been smeared across the back wall, disappearing behind the
bookcase. She’d said she might go back there tonight. She’d asked me to
come with her. 

I aimed the flashlight into the narrow gap between two faux walls, and
for a second, I thought I saw something move. I stuck my phone through
the gap to light up the area more. Nothing. I stood there for another
minute, maybe longer, when I heard Gage yell from across the food court.

“Jerry!”

I pulled my phone back and turned off the flashlight. Each storefront
backed onto the same maintenance halls I walked every night to dump mop
water.  As I headed back to the pretzel stand, I looked back towards the
shop. It was so dark. But I thought about the store’s faux walls. And
what was back there. For the first time since I’d started watching the
mall walkers, I wondered why Claire was only ever allowed to use so much
of the old store in the first place.

I waited until we finished closing. I hadn’t planned on bringing Gage
with me, but I realized that going back there alone was a terrible idea.
Maybe that meant I was finally thinking clearly. Probably not. 

Gage didn’t take much convincing. I told him I wanted to get into the
back of Claire’s store. 

“Wait. The abandoned Books-A-Million? At night?” He was already checking
his battery. “Bro, that would go so viral.”

I wondered if that was the only time someone asked him to “hang out,” if
you wanna call it that. 

We carried the mop bucket with us in case someone saw us. The
maintenance hall was quieter than I’d ever heard it. Without the echoes
of the food court bleeding through the walls, I could hear every
fluorescent light buzzing overhead and pipes running. We passed the
utility sink where I’d usually dump the mop water and went farther than
I normally had any reason to.

The doors weren’t labeled with store names, just numbers. I counted them
as we passed. Claire’s should’ve been the fourth, but it wasn’t. It
jumped from three to five with a gap in between.  I ran my hand along
the wall. Then my fingers caught something. A seam. I followed it
downward. Someone had painted over the outline of a door, and it seemed
fresh. There wasn’t a spot for the handle anywhere, so Gage and I
cracked the seal and pried at the door. It turned out to be a panel that
popped off easily once we ripped off all the paint. 

Warm air rolled through the opening. The smell hit me next. It smelled
like a mix between a coffee roaster, a metal scrapyard, and a hoarder’s
fridge. I turned on my phone’s flashlight, the beam reaching only a few
feet into the darkness. The space on the other side was enormous. This
was the rest of the Books-A-Million, or what was left of it. Pieces of
drywall littered the floor. An old FICTION sign hung crooked from the
ceiling.

I recognized the faux walls ahead of us. Claire’s shop was on the other
side. We moved toward them when I saw what I could only assume was the
puddle I saw earlier. And up close, there was no mistaking the color. It
was blood. The blood had collected beneath the wall and followed the
uneven floor toward us in a narrow stream. When I saw it, I stopped in
my tracks, but of course, Gage did not. He stepped around me and stepped
into the stream. I followed him with my flashlight. I noticed something
next to the stream. At first I thought it was some sort of fabric. As I
focused my eyes more on it, I realized what it was. A long strip of
human skin that lay crumpled against the base of the shelf. It was long
enough that I couldn’t tell what part of a person it came from. Red hair
was at one end.

“Claire.”

I don’t remember if I said her name out loud or just thought it.
Something shook the faux wall. We both froze.

The wall bowed outward, as if something on the other side had leaned its
weight against it. Then a hand pushed through, as if the wall were moist
and moldy. The wall popped as it poured liquid around the wrist, almost
like it had pushed its way out of an incubated egg. Gage stumbled
backward, falling into the blood, getting completely soaked. Another
hand emerged. Then a head. The thing pushed itself through the wall like
it was crawling through wet paper. An old man stumbled into the room. It
was one of the mall walkers. 

His face hung loosely from his skull. The skin beneath his eyes sagged
too far. One cheek folded against itself when he turned his head. His
neck looked several sizes too large, bunching above his windbreaker
collar like a scarf. His face didn’t fit. That was the only way I could
describe it.

Another walker pushed through behind him. Then another, and another, and
another. None of them moved like old people; they moved like Olympic
gymnasts. They were faster than normal. They even had better posture.

More blobs gathered behind the torn wall. Then I saw Randall. I knew it
was him immediately. But whatever had been wrong with him the other day
was worse now. The skin around his mouth hung loose enough that his lips
moved slightly when he breathed. His eyelids sagged beneath his eyes. A
fold of skin gathered beneath his chin like someone had pulled his face
down and forgotten to straighten it. He looked like someone wearing
Randall.

Another figure stepped through, smaller than the others. My flashlight
found her face. My breath immediately halted. It was Claire. She was
wearing the same clothes she’d worn yesterday. Her skin hung loosely
around something underneath that wasn’t shaped like her. One eye sat
slightly lower than the other. Her jaw was too narrow. Every time her
head moved, her cheek moved a fraction of a second later. She looked
unfinished, like someone had studied Claire from across the room and
tried to put her back together from memory.

Gage slid back across the tile floor. Every head in the room turned
toward him, all at once. The walkers moved for him. They began grabbing
his wrists and ankles, while Gage struggled. 

“Jerry, Jerry, help! Please–”

I watched. I should’ve helped him. I knew that. Just like I knew I
should’ve helped Randall. I’d spent three years waiting for somebody
else to handle things. Somebody whose job it was. There was nobody else
now. It was just me. And I just stood there. Part of me hated Gage,
maybe enough to just watch. 

Claire dug her hands into Gage’s skin. I could see her fingers sink into
his flesh like she was peeling an orange. Blood gushed out of his torso;
his screams were ear-piercing. They’re still ringing in my ears. His
crimson blood soaked his shirt, making it almost purple instead of blue.
The other walkers were holding him down, and they must’ve been strong.
He could barely squirm. 

Once Claire got deep enough, she started to rip. Rip his chest open. I
could hear every tendon and muscle give. At this point, Gage seemed to
have gone into some sort of shock. He wasn’t screaming anymore, just had
a slouched face with a lifeless stare. Once his chest was opened, Claire
started to pull each organ out, tossing them over her shoulder across
the room. The other mall walkers started pulling on his limbs,
stretching the skin attached. I heard his shoulders pop as they
dislocated. They pulled his arms off, but not cleanly.  His legs soon
followed. The walkers then started peeling the skin off, first in one
little strip, then peeling it off whole.

Claire stood up, her hands pointed toward the now-flooded room. Her
fingers split off into dozens of small protrusions that reached the
slush and began to suck. She made a gulping sound as the liquid
disappeared, as if it had never been there, just like what happened to
Randall.

One of the other mall walkers, whose skin looked the loosest, shuffled
over. It began tearing its own flappy skin right off of its body, with
ease. Before I could see what happened next, my foot slipped. Not enough
to make me fall, but just enough for the mall walkers to notice me. They
all snapped their heads at me, just as they did with Gage. 

I backed away, but the mall walkers started moving toward me. I ran
faster than I’ve ever run. But it wasn’t fast enough. As I reached the
door, a mall walker was already waiting for me. It grabbed my shoulder;
it hurt so much. I’d never felt anything like that in my life. But my
adrenaline finally kicked in, and I ripped away from his grip, but so
did a part of my shoulder. The pain didn’t hit immediately. But when it
did, it felt like someone had pressed a branding iron into my shoulder
and left it there. I grabbed my shoulder instinctively and immediately
regretted it. My hand came away soaked in blood. 

I heard something slam into the wall behind me. I didn’t even bother to
look back; I just kept running. I thought I knew these halls. I’d walked
them up and down for three years. Straight ahead should’ve taken me back
to the food court. Should’ve. I turned the corner and almost
face-planted into a wall.

“What the fuck?”

I stopped. I’d been through here less than half an hour ago. There
wasn’t a wall. Something crashed behind me. I turned around and took the
other hallway. Red LED exit signs glowed on my skin as I ran beneath
them. My shoulder burned with every step. I kept one hand pressed
against it, trying to slow the bleeding.

The hallways seemed longer now. Much longer. Something moved at the far
end; I couldn’t quite make it out. I turned to another corridor and ran
some more. As I ran, I noticed cracks in the paint and brick, with
crimson glistening in between them. A drop of red liquid plopped on my
forehead. I looked above me at a ceiling tile sagging downward. Thick
liquid collected at its edge before another drop fell onto my face.
Behind the tile was a web of wires, pipes, and veins. As I stood there,
I saw something pulse behind the tile. The vibration traveled down the
wall and beneath my feet. I could feel it through my shoes. I felt it
every day. The endless hum beneath the music. The vibration in the
floors. The pipes running behind the walls. The heat in the halls. I’d
always assumed it was an ancient HVAC system. I then realized something.
There wasn’t something living inside Briarwood. Briarwood was
something. 

A crash suddenly echoed through the hallway. I turned. Randall stood at
the other end. Claire stepped out behind him. Then another. And another.
The loose skin around Randall’s face bounced as he ran towards me. I
booked it again. The hallway seemed to tighten around me. Maybe it
actually did. I don’t really know.

I turned left. Then right, then left, then right.  The lights behind me
shut off one by one. I could hear them closing in. Their feet pounded
against the tiled floor. Something hit the wall beside me hard enough to
shake it. I didn’t look back; I didn’t have the guts to. I turned
another corner. Dead end.

“fuck.”

As I turned around, one of the mall walkers rounded the corner. Randall
was behind it. My only choice was to run towards them, so I did.

Randall reached for me, but I ducked. Its fingers caught the back of my
shirt and tore through it, scraping my skin. I kept running when a door
appeared ahead. I grabbed the handle and turned it.  Locked. I pulled
again. Nothing.

The footsteps grew louder and faster. I yanked the door again and again,
but it didn’t budge. Then I realized something. I pushed the door, and
it opened. It had never been locked. I’d just been pulling.

I almost laughed. I threw myself inside. This was a closet. I slammed
the door behind me. I barricaded the door with a metal shelf; bottles
flew everywhere. I grabbed a mop bucket, a case of paper towels, and
even a spare toilet. I threw my weight against it. Anything to keep them
out.

The footsteps stopped outside, replaced by frantic pounding on the door
and the handle jiggling. Then, it stopped. Suddenly. I held my breath.
They were gone.  My shoulder throbbed. Blood ran between my fingers and
dripped onto the floor. Then…

Shhh. Shhh.

Footsteps passed the door. Who, I assume, were the walkers passing by.
Then silence again. This kept on for… a long time. I stayed against the
shelf for… I don’t know how long. Eventually I looked down. Blood had
pooled beneath my shoe. The puddle streamed towards the wall at the
other end of the closet, where it disappeared beneath the baseboard. I
pulled my foot away. Another drop fell from my shoulder. It followed
suit with the rest.

The mall is drinking my blood. 

The mall walkers aren’t hunting. They protect, repair, replace, and
consume whatever they get their hands on. The shoppers. The repairmen.
The security guards. The tenants. The employees. They keep Briarwood
running when nobody else will. They never call in, never complain, never
ask for a raise. The perfect employees and the perfect regulars. 

They’re Briarwood’s immune system. And to something in constant decay,
everything’s a threat. 

And that’s where I am now. Waiting to get absorbed into this Behemoth.
My phone’s battery is at 1%, and I’ve used most of it writing this
instead of calling anyone. I guess that tracks. I keep coming back to
this: I could’ve left. Yesterday, or last week, or every morning I sat
in that damn parking lot. Nobody made me stay. Briarwood didn’t trap me.
I just never left. Every morning I opened that car door and walked in. I
always did. 

They’re passing by again. I think I’m finally going to do something.
Open the door and walk out. And if I don’t make it, at least I won’t
remember any of this. I don’t think Randall did.
