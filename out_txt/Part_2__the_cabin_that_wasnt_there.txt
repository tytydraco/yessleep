PART 1

While still walking back across the open field, I couldn’t even say a
word to Molly.
She met me halfway across the field and asked me what happened.
I told her,
“I’m not sure what happened back there.”
“He asked me things, but he was me. So… I asked myself to do something?”
I was still trying to digest everything that had just happened while we
walked back toward Molly’s car.
She looked at me.
“Well, that was weird. Why did we even have to go there?”
I opened the passenger-side door of Molly’s car and sat down next to
her.
I answered with confusion.
“What do you mean? I was on my way to my dad and—”
I stopped.
I looked into the side mirror.
For a second, I couldn’t understand what I was looking at.
I remembered putting my bike behind her car near the open field.
A few minutes ago.
It had been right there.
I could picture it perfectly.
The black frame.
The handlebars.
The little light on the front.
But there was nothing behind the car.
I opened the door again and stepped outside.
I looked around.
Nothing.
I walked a few meters back toward the field.
Still nothing.
“Molly?”
“Yeah?”
“Where’s my bike?”
She looked at me strangely.
“What bike?”
“My bike. I left it right here.”
She shook her head.
“Joel, you didn’t have a bike.”
“I did.”
“No, you didn’t.”
“I rode here.”
She stared at me.
“You didn’t.”
I looked back toward the field.
There was no bike.
Nothing.
It was as if it had never existed.
Completely confused, I told myself I was probably just tired and needed
to sleep.
Molly started the car and put on the radio.
The drive was short, but I remember driving past the other open fields
and through the woods.
It all looked the same.
Somehow, everything felt normal again.
I must have been so tired that I completely forgot Molly, my friend, had
picked me up after she was done with her late shift at work.
I was so dreamy that I dozed off while we were driving.
At least, that’s what I told myself.
When we arrived at our houses, which were next to each other, Molly
parked her car on the driveway and looked at me.
“I think I’m sleeping in my own bed tonight. I need the rest. Is that
okay with you?”
I answered,
“Uhm… of course. Do what you need to do.”
I thought it was a weird question because why would I care if she slept
in her own bed?
Where else would she sleep?
The couch?
“Thanks for driving me,” I said in a chill tone.
Molly walked toward me, smiling, and hugged me tightly.
“Of course, neighbourboy.”
I didn’t say much because this was new for me.
Molly never hugged me like this.
She always tapped me on my shoulder with her flat hand and said,
“See you, slowpoke!”
Not that we had talked much lately.
I think the last time we had an actual conversation was six months ago.
I said goodnight to Molly and walked through the gate of my dad’s
garden.
I could see him through the window, standing in the kitchen, almost like
he had been waiting the entire time.
My dad always made sure there were extra snacks and drinks when I came
over.
And he always had some new movie installed on his PC.
When I opened the door, he turned around.
“Back already?”
I laughed it off, thinking it was a stupid joke.
I hadn’t seen my father for a week, and he made stupid dad jokes all the
time.
“I’m good too, thanks for asking.”
“Normally I would watch a movie with you, but I’m too tired and it was a
weird night,” I said while opening the door to the stairs.
He looked at me strangely.
But I was too tired to care.
I walked upstairs to my room, opened the door, threw my bag onto the
chair, and lay down on my bed.
I sighed and felt a little dizzy.
I closed my eyes for a second.
Before I knew it, everything around me started to become vague and
blurry.

Someone throwing pillows and clothing at me woke me the next morning.
Molly was sitting on my chair, yelling at me to wake up.
She had made me coffee and lit a cigarette.
She passed it to me, and I asked her, confused,
“How did you get in here?”
“And how do you know how I start my mornings?”
She didn’t answer immediately.
Instead, she looked at me seriously.
“I want to talk, Joel.”
She paused.
“Something isn’t right.”
The feeling I got was weird.
She had a point.
Something was completely off, and she was definitely involved in this.
But somehow, besides all that, I couldn’t understand how she was sitting
here in my room.
I got distracted by her cute dimples again, and she continued.
“What happened last night? I don’t know if I want that again. I know
you’re into science and all that, but this was just weird and scary.”
She was serious about this.
Almost like this was a discussion we had already had once.
While I was stretching my arms and yawning with my mouth wide open, she
said,
“I know we’re fighting a lot lately, and I really miss our old
conversations. You’re so closed up and don’t tell me anything anymore.”
Confused, I answered shortly,
“What do you mean? Can I wake up first? Leave me alone for a minute and
I’ll come downstairs as soon as I’m ready.”
She looked surprised.
Almost as if I had said something totally wrong.
“Okay… see you there.”
I stood up, put on my shirt, and walked to the bathroom.

While splashing water in my face, I looked at myself in the mirror.
My thoughts went everywhere.
Why was Molly in my room?
How did she know I start my mornings like that?
What the hell happened yesterday?
When I came downstairs, my father was in the garden watering the plants
and flowers.
Molly was sitting at the kitchen table.
Her hair was falling down her face, and she was looking through the
window on the side of the house.
She heard me walk in, looked at me with a smile, and said,
“Good morning, neighbourboy.”
I didn’t know what to reply, so I went straight to the point.
“I’m sorry for reacting like that. I felt a bit overwhelmed and couldn’t
find the right words.”
“That’s fine. I just felt like talking about what happened yesterday.”
“What happened yesterday?”
My dad said it while walking in.
Molly quickly said,
“Oh, not much. Just another science project Joel and I were trying out.”
I nodded.
But I don’t know why.
Because my thoughts about yesterday were completely different.
What does she mean?
She picked me up after work, right?
Or did I bike here?
And I didn’t like science at all.
What is going on?
I was freaking out, but I didn’t show that to them.
I asked Molly if she wanted to go for a walk.
“Sure! You want to get some ice cream too?”
“Ice cream? I never liked ice cream. You don’t remember that? I told you
many times.”
Molly frowned.
“Oh… yeah, I forgot, I guess.”
I was tying my shoes when Molly stood behind me and hugged me tightly,
her arms wrapped around my body.
“I’m sorry, Joel. I’m just trying to understand you better sometimes.”
I blushed, not knowing what to say or what she meant.
We stood up and started walking toward the nearby woods.
“I just want to ask you some questions, Joel.”
“Yeah, I have questions too, but you can go first.”
“Okay, thanks. So… why did we go there yesterday?”
“You asked me to go check it out together, after you almost hit me with
your car.”
“No… that never happened, Joel. You sent me a message to come to the
field, so I drove there and you were already on the field, waiting.”
Surprised by what she said, I stopped walking.
“What do you mean I messaged you? I haven’t messaged or even talked with
you for six months.”
I said it while trying to feel which pocket my phone was in.
Now that I thought about it, I hadn’t touched my phone the entire day.
“Can you show me? I think my phone is still at home.”
“Can’t you just believe me? I’m not making this up,” she said with
frustration.
I stared at her.
“I do believe you.”
And I meant it.
That was the scary part.
I believed her.
But I also believed myself.
Both of our memories couldn’t possibly be true at the same time.
Unless…
Something else was going on.

We went back to the house and searched for my phone.
Nothing.
Under my bed.
Inside my bag.
In the pockets of my clothes.
Nothing.
It was simply gone.
Molly stood in the doorway.
“You really can’t find it?”
“No.”
She looked at me for a moment.
Then she pulled out her own phone.
“Joel…”
“What?”
“I want to show you something.”
She opened her photos.
At first, I didn’t understand what I was looking at.
There were pictures of me.
Hundreds of them.
Me and Molly.
Together.
At restaurants.
In the garden.
At Christmas.
On holidays.
Random pictures of us sitting together.
Laughing.
Holding each other.
Looking like a normal couple.
I stared at the screen.
“I don’t remember any of this.”
Molly looked at me.
“You don’t remember us?”
“No.”
Her face changed.
“You really don’t?”
“No.”
She scrolled further.
There was a picture of us sitting on the old bench between the trees
behind our houses.
I knew that place.
We used to hang out there when we were younger.
But I had never sat there with Molly like that.
Not as a couple.
Not in that picture.
“Who is that?”
Molly looked at me.
“That’s you.”
“I know.”
I zoomed in.
“But it’s not me.”
She stared at the picture.
Then at me.
“What do you mean?”
“It looks like me.”
I swallowed.
“But I don’t remember being there.”
Molly slowly took the phone back.
She looked at the picture.
Then at me.
“You’re not my Joel.”
The words hit me harder than I expected.
“What?”
She looked like she was trying to convince herself as much as she was
trying to convince me.
“You’re Joel.”
She pointed at the picture.
“But you’re not the Joel I know.”
I didn’t answer.

We spent the rest of the afternoon comparing things.
Not huge things.
Small things.
The kind of things that shouldn’t matter, but somehow felt incredibly
important now.
My favorite food.
Her favorite movie.
The name of the teacher we both had in school.
The places we used to hang out.
Little things about our childhood that only the two of us should have
known.
Some things matched.
Some didn’t.
And some were so completely different that we both just stopped talking
afterward.
The more we compared, the clearer something became.
Neither of us was lying.
Molly really had been with a version of me for years.
And I really had never been with her.
I was the same person.
But somehow…
I wasn’t the same Joel.
That thought stayed with me for hours.
At some point, we ended up sitting on my bed.
Molly was leaning against the wall with her legs stretched out in front
of her, while I was sitting near the edge of the bed with my back
against the headboard.
Neither of us really knew what to say anymore.
Even though all of this was driving me crazy, i kinda liked being with
molly like this..

She looked at the pictures on my desk.
Some were old.
Some were from when I was younger.
There was one of me and my dad at a barbecue.
One of my mom.
One of me standing next to my bike when I was probably around fourteen.
Molly picked up the picture of me and my mom.
“Your mom looks nice.”
“She is.”
I smiled.
“She always was.”
Molly looked at the picture for a few seconds longer than I expected.
Then she put it back down.
“You’re lucky.”
I looked at her.
“What?”
“To still have her.”
I laughed slightly.
“Yeah.”
I didn’t think much of it at the time.
I was too tired.
The whole day had felt like one long dream, and my brain was struggling
to keep up with everything.
Molly looked at me again.
“What happens if we can’t find the cabin?”
I turned my head toward her.
“What?”
“What if it’s not there tonight?”
I shrugged.
“Then we look again tomorrow.”
“And what if we can’t?”
I didn’t answer immediately.
She looked down at her hands.
“What if we can’t find it again?”
Her voice had changed.
It wasn’t curiosity anymore.
She was genuinely scared.
“And what if we can’t go back to how it was?”
I looked at her.
For the first time that day, I really noticed how tired she looked.
The little bags under her eyes.
Her hair wasn’t as neat as it usually was.
She looked like she hadn’t slept properly in days.
I moved a little closer.
“Don’t worry about it.”
She looked at me.
“We’ll figure this out.”
She smiled.
A small smile.
The kind that didn’t completely hide how worried she was.
“Promise?”
“Promise.”
She leaned her head against my shoulder.
I didn’t move.
I just sat there.
For some reason, it felt completely natural.
Even though according to my memories, it shouldn’t have.
We talked for a while after that.
About random things.
School.
Our childhood.
The little group of trees behind our houses.
The time we almost got caught sneaking into the old barn near the
fields.
Things that belonged to both of our memories.
Things that hadn’t changed.
Eventually, neither of us realized how tired we actually were.
I remember Molly saying something.
I don’t remember what.
I just remember closing my eyes for a second.
When I opened them again, the room was dark.
Molly was asleep beside me.
Her head was still resting against my shoulder.
I looked at the clock,
It was almost midnight.
I carefully moved so I wouldn’t wake her.
She mumbled something in her sleep and pulled the blanket closer around
herself.
I smiled.
For just a moment, everything felt normal.
Just me and Molly sitting in my room like we had done a hundred times
when we were younger.
I almost wished I could stay in that moment.
But then I remembered why we were there.
I gently woke her.
“Molly.”
She opened her eyes.
“Hm?”
“We have to go.”
She looked at me for a second before remembering.
“Oh.”
She sat up and rubbed her eyes.
“Right.”
We got ready and quietly walked downstairs.
My dad was already asleep.
We left through the back door so we wouldn’t wake him.
Outside, the air had become colder.
The garden was completely silent.
Molly looked at me.
“You ready?”
I nodded.
“As ready as I’ll ever be.”
We started walking.
The same road.
The same forest.
The same fields.
But this time, neither of us spoke much.
I kept thinking about what Molly had asked me.
What if we couldn’t find the cabin?
What if we couldn’t go back?
I wasn’t even sure what “back” meant anymore.
We eventually reached the forest.
The trees swallowed the moonlight above us, and the road became darker
with every step.
I remembered riding this road back then.
I never thought twice about the darkness.
I knew every turn.
Every little bump in the road.
Every place where the trees grew closer together.
It used to be just a road to me.
A road between my mom’s house and my dad’s.
Now it felt like a border between two completely different lives.
The air inside the forest was colder than it had been outside.
The trees stood close together on both sides of the road, their branches
stretching over us and blocking most of the moonlight.
Every now and then, a gap between the branches would let a little silver
light through.
It reminded me of how I used to ride through here as a kid.
Back then, I would race through the darkness just to see how fast I
could get to the other side.
I never wondered what was between the trees.
I never had a reason to.
Now every dark space between the trunks looked like it was hiding
something.
Molly stayed close to me.
Her hand occasionally brushed against mine as we walked.
Neither of us talked for a while.
Eventually, she whispered,
“Do you think we’re going to find it?”
“I don’t know.”
“That wasn’t very convincing.”
I smiled slightly.
“I’m trying not to lie to you.”
She laughed quietly.
It was the first normal sound either of us had made in a while.
But even that small laugh disappeared quickly.
A few seconds later, we heard a branch snap somewhere deep in the
forest.
We both stopped.
I looked toward the trees.
Nothing.
“Probably an animal,” Molly whispered.
“Probably.”
Neither of us believed it.
We continued walking.
Then we reached the open field.
I stopped.
The moonlight spread across the grass, making everything look almost
silver.
The cornfield moved slowly in the wind.
The road behind us was completely empty.
I looked toward the back of the field.
Nothing.
I took another step forward.
Still nothing.
Molly walked past me and looked around.
“Joel?”
I didn’t answer.
I was staring at the exact spot where the cabin had been.
The same place where everything had changed.
I took a few more steps into the field.
Molly followed.
We looked around.
But there was no cabin.
We walked closer to where it had been.
Still nothing to see.
Then my eyes stopped at the path that led to the woods.
“The path! It’s still there, leading to the gate.”
Molly said it while she started walking more quickly.
But something stopped me.
It was a sound.
It sounded like breathing.
Not normal breathing.
Loud and deep breaths, almost as if the lungs making them were meters
wide.
You could hear it echoing over the field.
It came from behind the gate.
“Molly! Wait!”
I shouted.
Now standing in front of the gate, she turned around and said,
“Hurry up, slowpoke!”
I laughed and yelled back,
“You already—”
A loud woosh stopped me from finishing my sentence.
A huge tree that looked like it had just been pulled out of the ground
flew over our heads.
It landed with a massive bang on the open field behind us.
I watched it roll and slowly come to a stop in the grass.
With my eyes wide open, I turned back toward the gate.
Molly stood there.
Completely still.
While all of this was happening, I didn’t notice that the breathing
sound had gotten even louder.
“Molly, let’s go! We need to go now!”
She tried running, but it wouldn’t have mattered.
An enormous hand attached to an arm as huge as a windmill reached out
through the dark, dense trees behind the gate.
The fingers were thick enough to wrap around tree trunks.
The skin looked almost grey underneath the moonlight, covered in dirt
and small scratches.
For one second, the hand just hovered there.
Then one finger moved toward the gate.
With almost no effort, it pushed through the metal.
The entire gate bent inward like it was made from a thin sheet of tin.
The sound was horrible.
Metal screaming.
Trees cracking.
The ground itself seemed to shake underneath my feet.
Then the hand disappeared back into the forest.
I couldn’t move.
Molly couldn’t either.
We just stared into the darkness.
Then something enormous stepped forward.
I couldn’t even see its entire body.
The trees were too tall and the darkness too thick.
But I could see enough.
Two legs.
And a body disappearing into the trees above us.
“Molly…”
I barely managed to say her name.
She looked at me.
“Run.”
We both turned.
The ground suddenly moved beneath us.
A wave of pressure slammed me backward.
I hit my head on the ground and think I was out for a few seconds.
Dimmed sounds and blurry vision made it impossible to understand what I
was experiencing.
I tried looking up and called for Molly.
“Molly? Are you there?”
Molly was screaming.
Even through my muffled hearing, I could hear her.
My vision slowly became clearer, and I looked toward the gate.
I heard more screaming.
And the last thing I saw was the hand.
Holding Molly between its thumb and finger.
Pulling her into the dark forest.
“No! Molly, no…”
I fell back into the grass, slowly closing my eyes as I blacked out.
I remember opening my eyes sometimes.
Just quick moments where my body tried to wake up but couldn’t.
I saw myself moving deeper into the trees.
The sky above me.
The tops of the trees flashing by.
Sometimes I could feel something hard underneath me.
Sometimes I could hear Molly.
Sometimes I thought I could hear myself.
I don’t know how long I was out, but when I finally woke up, it was
still nighttime.
I felt pain everywhere.
Bruises were forming quickly, and every movement hurt.
I opened my eyes further to look around me.
I was lying near a campfire.
Around me were ruins.
Weird symbols and a language I didn’t understand were drawn across the
walls.
It looked ancient.
Some of the stones were covered in moss.
Roots had grown through cracks in the walls, twisting around the old
bricks like the forest had slowly swallowed the entire place.
There were broken pillars around the clearing.
Some had fallen over completely.
Others were still standing, but they leaned at strange angles.
The fire in the middle of the ruins was the only source of light.
Everything beyond it disappeared into complete darkness.
I was tucked into a blanket, and a bucket of water was standing next to
me.
My mind was scrambled.
I tried making sense of at least something, but there wasn’t anything to
make sense of.
I couldn’t remember being carried here.
I couldn’t even tell if I had been unconscious for minutes or hours.
Then I heard a voice.
“Joel?”
Molly’s voice echoed through the ruins.
“You’re awake?”
“Molly, where are you?”
“It’s too dark to see, but we just have to hold on, okay?”
Her voice sounded shaky.
“Don’t panic when he gets back.”
“Who will come back, Molly?”
She didn’t answer.
Instead, I heard something moving somewhere beyond the ruins.
Then came the sound.
Thud.
The ground moved.
Another one.
Thud.
The bucket next to me started shaking.
The water inside it rippled.
Then spilled over the side.
THUD.
The sound was getting closer.
The trees beyond the ruins began moving.
Not swaying.
Moving.
As if something enormous was walking between them.
I tried to sit up.
Pain shot through my back and I immediately fell back down.
“Molly…”
“I’m here!”
“What is that?”
“I don’t know.”
There was fear in her voice.
“But he brought us here.”
“Who?”
She didn’t answer.
The trees suddenly separated.
Something enormous stepped through them.
A figure taller than the trees slowly emerged from the darkness.
Its enormous head covered the sky above me.
I couldn’t understand the size of it.
The trees reached somewhere around its chest.
The ruins looked like toys around its feet.
The giant slowly lowered himself.
The ground shook when he put one knee down.
The campfire suddenly looked tiny.
Almost pointless.
Then his face moved closer.
The flames lit up his face just enough for me to see it.
I froze.
It was me.
My face.
My hair.
My eyes.
Everything.
Just enormous.
His skin was covered in small scratches and dirt, and there were leaves
caught in his hair.
But it was still my face.
My own face looking down at me.
The giant frowned.
Then he let out a deep laugh.
The sound was so low that I felt it in my chest.
“Molly…”
I swallowed.
“Why am I looking at myself?”
Molly laughed nervously.
“Apparently, you’re also a giant.”
I actually had to stop myself from laughing.
Not because it was funny.
But because the entire situation was so ridiculous that my brain didn’t
know what else to do.
If I started laughing, I probably would have cried afterward.
I was lying in some ancient ruin, covered in bruises, while a giant
version of myself was staring down at us.
And honestly, my head hurt too much for either.
The giant slowly sat down against a group of trees.
Several of them bent backward under his weight.
The branches above him shook and leaves fell around his shoulders.
For a moment, he just looked at us.
Then he carefully reached behind one of the ruined walls.
His hand disappeared from view.
A moment later, he brought it back.
Molly was sitting in his palm.
He lowered her carefully and placed her next to me near the campfire.
Then he spoke.
His voice was mine.
But much deeper.
It sounded like my voice coming from somewhere underground.
“Joel.”
He paused.
“Nice to meet you.”
He looked at Molly.
“Sorry for hurt.”
He pointed toward himself.
“Too strong.”
He looked back at me.
“You are very small.”
Molly giggled.
I looked at her.
“You’re laughing?”
“A little.”
The giant smiled.
Molly came closer to me and whispered
“I think he likes us.”
She looked up at him.
The giant smiled again.
Then he looked toward the forest.
His expression became more serious.
“Listen.”
Neither of us said anything.
“I don’t know why we are here.”
He looked down at his enormous hands.
“But I was happy with life.”
He spoke slowly, like he had told himself this story many times.
“I had a job.”
“I had friends.”
“I had a home.”
He looked at Molly.
“I married Molly.”
Molly stared at him.
“You…”
The giant nodded.
“Yes.”
He looked back at me.
“I had a good life.”
I didn’t know what to say.
He continued.
“One night, I went to visit my father.”
My stomach tightened.
“Your father?”
He nodded.
“Same road.”
“Same forest.”
“Same field.”
He pointed toward the darkness behind us.
“And then I saw it.”
“The cabin.”
“Somehow you, or me? Whoever I was or who i have been, was trying to
take over my life.”
“Or his life, its hard to explain.”
Now i have been hiding here, for so long..”
“Waiting for the cabin to return someday.”

I didn’t know what to say.
Molly was already falling asleep again and i
Felt my eyes getting heavy too.
The last thing the giant said was.
“I got my back joel, or yours i mean” while letting out his heavy deep
laugh.
