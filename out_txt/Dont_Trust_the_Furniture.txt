I don’t know why we even went into that damn antique shop. Neither of us
were particularly into antiques, or even furniture for that matter. It
was just something to do as we wandered through town, something to pass
the time on that beautiful autumn day.

“Miles,” Louise said. “Check out this chair!” 

I peered through the dust as my eyes adjusted to the dim lighting of the
store. Victorian Folding Chair–75% Off  “I didn’t know they had folding
chairs around back then,” I said. 

The chair was certainly unusual looking. It was made of some type of red
polished wood. It had carved claw feet, a high back that was slightly
crooked as though it had been broken at some point, and long spindly
legs.

Louise’s face lit up. “We have to get it!”

I was skeptical. The chair looked uncomfortable, and it didn’t match any
of our other furniture.

As if reading my mind, Louise plopped into it. The chair accepted her
weight with a satisfied little sigh. “It has character!” she insisted.
“It’s perfect. Pleasepleasepleasepleaseplease.”

Louise shoved the chair unceremoniously in the corner of our living room
and I didn’t think about it again for the rest of the day. We had a nice
little evening. We met up with friends to play games, and got dessert at
the new Vietnamese bakery around the corner. Louise finished the Tuesday
crossword and I went to bed early.

I woke up the next morning in an awful mood. I had slept restlessly,
tossing and turning all night. I fixed myself a bowl of cereal, but when
I grabbed the carton of milk from the fridge, it was empty. “Lou!” I
said, exasperated, but she had her headphones on. She just smiled and
gave me a thumbs up.

On my way out the door to work, I tripped over the new chair, banging my
shin pretty badly. Louise must have dragged it into the hallway for some
reason. And she’d scratched up the floor in the process! Shaking my
head, I carried the chair back to the living room and dumped my dry corn
flakes in the trash.

That evening was probably the first time I started actually paying
attention. Louise and I were on the couch, half-watching an old movie,
when I heard an odd noise. It sounded just like someone clearing their
throat. But it wasn’t coming from Louise. It was coming from across the
room. I paused the TV, and it happened again, more quietly this time.

“I think it’s coming from that chair,” I said. We both crept over and
put our ears against the warm wood. We waited and waited, but all was
silent. 

“Maybe it’s termites,” Louise said, grinning up at me. “Or bed bugs!”

“Lou, no!” I stared at her in horror. “You better not have brought bed
bugs into this house!” She was laughing now, but I was serious. “They
get into everything! If this chair has bed bugs, they’ll be in all our
furniture before we know it! They are impossible to get rid of!”

I had trouble sleeping again that night. I felt on edge for some reason,
each creak and thump of the house settling jolting me awake. At one
point, I thought I heard the sound of a door closing somewhere upstairs.
I sat up in bed and tried to shake Louise awake. She hadn’t heard it, of
course. She always slept like the dead. 

“A gust of wind,” she mumbled without opening her eyes. 

I waited several minutes, before I eventually drifted back into a fitful
sleep.

The next time something happened was two days later. I was taking a
shower, and Louise came into the bathroom to brush her teeth. “Lou,” I
said. “You used up my shower gel.” I was pissed, already soaking wet,
and with nothing to clean myself. The lack of sleep, I’m sure, was not
helping my mood. “If you finish something, please remember to replace
it! The same with the milk–”

“What milk?” Louise retorted, eyes flashing. “I didn’t use up your
stupid shower gel, Miles. Why would I want to smell like Fresh
Sandalwood?” 

I scowled, but I didn’t want to fight this early in the morning, so I
toweled off in silence. We headed downstairs. 

I stopped short when I saw it. The chair. It was inexplicably damp, a
small puddle of water pooling beneath. From it wafted the unmistakable
scent of Fresh Sandalwood.

Louise and I just stood there for a moment, looking at it.

“Are you fucking with me?” I asked. It was hard to tell sometimes.
Louise did have an odd sense of humor.

“Are you fucking with me?” she said. 

We exchanged an uneasy glance. Then…

”Beddd bugggggs!” Louise said in a spooky voice. That broke the tension
and we both laughed. But I didn’t feel better.

Nighttime and yet again, I couldn’t fucking sleep. Distant sounds, like
someone setting a plate down, a sticky drawer closing, footsteps.
Footsteps? 

I opened my eyes. Yes, there were definitely footsteps walking down the
hallway. Uneven footsteps, bare feet. A slight limp. Footsteps, coming
closer and closer. 

I held my breath.

The bedroom door opened and I saw a man enter the room. He was a
strange, crooked man with long toenails and a nicely trimmed mustache.
He looked me right in the eyes and nodded once, appraisingly. He folded
himself into the corner of the room and crouched there, almost
disappearing into the darkness. And then he didn’t move.

I stared back at him in disbelief. Then, with a yell of complete terror,
I leapt out of bed and flung on the light. 

“What! What! What!” said Louise, finally waking all at once and spitting
out her nightguard in a panic. I stared into the corner. 

There was no man. 

Just that damn chair lurking up at me.

Louise tried to convince me that it was a dream, a night terror, a trick
of the light. “It happens to everyone,” she insisted. “You see a pile of
laundry in the dark and you think it’s a serial killer. Yesterday I
screamed because I saw the coat rack out of the corner of my eye!” 

But she hadn’t been there. This was different.

“Tomorrow I am getting rid of that chair,” I declared. I folded it up
and put it in the very top of our closet. I wedged the closet door
closed with a clothes hanger. We did not discuss how the chair had
gotten into our bedroom. That mystery hung between us, unspoken.

The morning I gave away the chair, I woke up feeling at peace for the
first time all week. I wasn’t surprised that it fell on me when I opened
the closet to get it down, and I wasn’t surprised that it pinched my
finger, drawing blood, when I picked it back up, either.  Not much
surprised me any more.

“I’ll return it to the antique shop on my way to work,” I said, rubbing
my sore head. “Lou?”

Louise had left her tea steeping on the kitchen table. Cinnamon, with
two teabags, extra strong, just as she liked it. I heard a toilet flush
from the upstairs bathroom. “Bye,” I called out to her.

The antique shop, it turned out, did not accept returns. So I put the
chair outside on the street instead for the garbage collectors. Within
minutes, a family drove up, inspected it, and loaded it into their car.
I almost felt like I should stop them, but what would I even say? As
they drove away with the chair, I could finally breathe again. 

I went for a long bike ride after work, and when I returned home, it was
already getting dark.

The first thing I noticed was Louise’s tea on the table, cold and
undrunk. The water had turned an odd crimson color. It must have been
steeping all day. 

The second thing was the unmistakable sensation that I was being
watched.

“Lou?” I called. I wandered through the house, swallowing hard.
“Louise?”

The third thing I noticed was the sound, like people whispering just out
of earshot. Was it my imagination? I hesitated outside the little side
room we had converted into an office. Right as I flung open the door, I
swear I heard someone say “Shh!”  But the room was empty, just the desk
and our cracked white bookshelf.

Louise was not in the kitchen, or the upstairs bathroom, or the
downstairs bathroom, or the bedroom, either.

Our house was not that big.

So I took out my phone and called her. After a moment, I heard her
ringtone, strangely muffled, and after another several minutes of
searching, I found her phone in the couch. It was not wedged in the
crack between pillows, where it presumably might have fallen out of her
pocket and been forgotten. No. It was placed neatly beneath the seat
cushions, nestled against the couch’s bony frame.

As I was standing back up, turning the phone over in my hands, it
happened: 

Someone behind me said “Eh eh eh eh.” 

It was a toneless, creaking voice, devoid of all meaning. It made my
blood run cold. 

“Eh eh eh eh,” the person said again, rhythmically. 

I froze, my heart in my throat. I did not dare turn around. Instead, I
very carefully raised my eyes to the landing mirror and checked the
reflection.

Squatting behind me, swaying from one foot to the other, was a large
woman I’d never seen before, wearing a pale floral dress. She met my
gaze and nodded once, with an unsettling certainty. 

“Eh eh eh eh,” she said.

I whirled around. There was no woman behind me. Just my creaky old
recliner, the one I’ve had for years, gently rocking back and forth.

In a total panic, I ran into my bedroom and locked it. I pushed my
dresser against the door for good measure. 

That’s where I am now, where I’ve been for the past two hours. I don’t
know what to do. There’s no way in hell I’m going back out there. Should
I call the police? They’ll think I’m crazy. Maybe I am. All I know is,
I’m hiding under my covers, trembling from head to toe, trying my best
to stay calm.

But beneath me, I swear to God, I can feel my bed breathing.
