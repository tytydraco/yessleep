I waved goodbye to Trevor, sending him off with half a box of pepperoni
pizza and a handful of leftover beers. It should prove to be more than
fair payment for the few hours of manual labor that it took to help move
Faye and me into our new home. He was the last of our motley crew to
leave, staying well into the evening.

“We did it.” I said after he left.

“We fucking did it.” Faye replied with a cheeky smile.

“What now?” I asked, sliding my hand around the curve of her hip. “We
don’t have much furniture, but we do have a bed.”

Faye slipped away from me, playfully skirting around my kiss.

“Not tonight, tiger. I am absolutely exhausted.” 

She began to make her way up the stairs. The stairs of our house.
Finally, after putting up with a long distance relationship for nearly
two years we had something together.

Faye paused, draping her hand over the banister. “But there is something
about this place that I have been dying to figure out.”

“And what is that?”

“If the shower is able to fit two people.” She responded before climbing
the rest of the stairs without turning back.

An hour or so later, Faye was tucked under our comforter and I was
brushing my teeth. The bedframe with a mattress on top of it was the
only semblance of furniture in our entire bedroom. Unless you counted
the mounds of clothing bags that desperately needed unpacking.

“Hey, Ryan, would it bother you if I play white noise from my phone?
It’s the only way that I can fall asleep at night.”

I spit out a glob of minty saliva.

“Uhh, sure. But what about all those times I visited you in Seattle? You
didn’t have any white noise then. And is this going to be an every night
thing?”

I had no problem with the concept of a white noise machine. I found the
consistent fall of rain to be quite soothing and I should have no
trouble falling asleep with it on quietly in the background. What was
more concerning to me was the fact that I didn’t know any of this about
Faye before moving in together.

Faye looked at me sheepishly with half of her face hidden under the
blankets.

“Whenever you visited or when I came to you… I didn’t sleep.”

“At all?” I put my weight down on the bed at her feet.

Faye shrugged. “On longer visits I would sometimes sneak in short naps.”

Today had gone as perfect as I could have asked for. There was no use
arguing over a petty squabble now.

“Yeah, it’s no problem to me.”

“Thanks roommate!” Faye leapt from under the covers and kissed me on the
lips.

I could hardly keep a straight face as I tucked myself in on the other
side of the bed. Naturally, it was the side closest to the door.

“Let’s make sure that nickname doesn’t stick.”

My roommate had fallen asleep in a matter of minutes.

I had been incorrect in my assumption that the white noise would not
bother me. I was expecting the ocean, or something subtle. What I got
instead was just a little too… rehearsed?

There was nothing natural about the sound at all, but it seemed to do
wonders knocking Faye right out. Watching her next to me, angelic and
beautiful, I was hit by the realization that I had never seen her
sleeping before.

I shrugged off the oddity. I turned on my side and closed my eyes, eager
to bring respite to my exhausted body. I was almost asleep when I heard
something from Faye’s side of the bed.

“Faye, you’re sleep talking.” I groaned, kicking her gently under the
covers.

The words were louder now. Loud enough for me to hear that they were not
coming from Faye, but rather her phone that was playing white noise.

“Veni mecum in hunc somnum aeternum.”

“What the?”

In her sleep, Faye repeated the gibberish.

“Faye, wake up.” I shook her with moderate aggression.

“Not now, Ryan. We’re almost at the good part.” She responded softly,
her eyes remaining closed.

The “white noise” grew louder. The unintelligible chanting was backed by
sharp orchestral music that filled me with dread. Faye sat directly up,
her eyes tightly shut. Her head spun towards me and she repeated the
chanting that came from her white noise player.

“Somnia nostra spiritibus cibus sunt.”

I recoiled, nearly falling out of the bed.

“Faye, you’re freaking me out!” My volume rose as did the tremble of my
voice.

Faye reached for my arm and grabbed me with imperceptible strength. She
pulled me in and pinned me against the bed. I fought against Faye’s
weight, but I couldn’t break free as she repeated the strange ramblings
from the white noise maker. 

“Mens porta est inter regna.”

My heart felt as if it were about to burst out of my chest as the
ominous music rose to a crescendo. I was consumed by panic, battling for
any chance of escape. And then… nothing.

I didn’t fear for my life. I didn’t see Faye anymore. I wasn’t aware of
the mountains of clothes scattered around the room. I only heard the
sweet, peaceful white noise that lulled me swiftly to a dreamless sleep.

In the morning, I awoke to a kiss from Faye. We spent the rest of the
day unpacking and settling into our new home. Tonight, I’m excited to
fall asleep next to my roommate, the sound of white noise, whisking us
away together.
