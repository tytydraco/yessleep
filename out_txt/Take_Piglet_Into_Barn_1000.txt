I arrived on the silo-dotted property as a rosy film of atmosphere
cradled the horizon like a hide and was skinned by the gloaming. Its
centrepiece was my destination: a run-of-the-mill, tin sheeting-topped
barn.

I’d found the classified serendipitously whilst leafing through an old
newspaper at the coffee shop. The gig paid handsomely, for next to no
work at that. The instructions were to the point: Take Piglet Into Barn,
$1000. I was to see myself in. An envelope at its entrance would hold
the cash and key.

But first, I had to catch a piglet. They ran and ran with all their
might, and I chased them round and round in circles in their pigsty
until one tired. This farm only had piglets, bar a pair of mature pigs
that looked like they didn’t want to be alive anymore and watched
silently from a separate enclosure. The piglet resigned itself to me
once I picked it up, its head dropping with a short-lived groan.

I then ventured into the barn.

Buzzing filled the space. I should’ve set the piglet down then and left,
but something was wrong. There was a… shape in the middle of the room,
barely illuminated by a lone plane of light percolating in through a
slit in the barn boards from the main farmhouse’s porch light outside.

The piglet jittered as I crept forward, the movement soon compounded by
my own jittering arms.

…It was a porky man lying motionless in a child’s pram, torso marinating
in a rainbow-tinged puddle in the carrycot, limp limbs protruding from
it like flaccid oars. A purple network of veins pressed up against the
translucent skin, streaking it like rain did windows on wistful days.

The buzzing was louder here. It reeked of death.

Both the piglet and I jumped out of our skins as the man yawned before
arduously propping himself up on his elbows.

When he finally took notice of us, his eyes lit up like fireworks, and
his gargantuan sausage-tongue made its expeditious appearance,
carouselling slipperily over his bulbous slug-lips and wetting them to
no end.

“PIGGYYYYYYYYYY!” he squealed. His outstretched hands groped the air
continuously. His shallow panting accelerated.

Fuck this. I threw piggy into the pram, where it crashed with a
squelchous SLAP. It immediately audibly gagged before being engulfed in
a bear hug. The pram creaked wildly and I heard something burst. I
couldn’t take it. I scrambled back to the door like a possessed rag
doll, the taste of bile bubbling up to the surface with the fervour of a
geyser on Enceladus. A quick glance over my shoulder I was certain had
burned itself into my brain for good: the piglet’s hind legs
backpedalling frantically on the beads of condensation lubricating the
man’s bloated gut, and the man’s beady eyes gleaming like those of a
spoiled brat unwrapping a Christmas present.

Outside, the blood-curdling screams came, then abruptly ceased. I saw
the other piglets there, huddled together at the back of their pigsty.
They whimpered when I approached.

I unlatched their gate, tears streaming down my face.
