I’m typing this from a cheap burner phone in the back of a 24hr diner
off Route X (i’m not comfortable with sharing my location any further) I
cannot stop shaking dude, and every time headlights sweep across the
blinds, my stomach drops thru me. I don’t know how much time I have
before they track this MAC address, but the public needs to know what
really happened at the development on Vineyard Drive, or Crest Lane. The
news is calling it a domestic terror incident. They are lying.
My husband and I are normal people. We usually ignore the neighborhood
fliers, choosing a quiet night on the couch over awkward small talk. But
last night was different. The entire block was there. Family we hadn’t
seen in months, old church friends from three towns over, coworkers,
uncles, sisters—it looked like a massive, unprompted reunion packed into
a pristine, white-brick suburban dream home. The air smelled heavily of
expensive catering and something else—a sweet, chemically synthetic
undertone that made the back of my throat itch. Rainbow LED tracks lined
the ceilings, throwing pulsing, nauseating pools of red and purple light
over the crowd.
Right as we crossed the threshold, pushing past a flimsy screen door, a
woman intercepted me. I’d never seen her face before, but it’s burned
into my retinas. She was beautiful in an artificial, unnerving way, her
skin glowing with a thick, iridescent perfume oil that caught the neon
lights, making her cheekbones shimmer like crushed diamonds. She locked
her eyes onto mine, grabbed my forearm with a grip that was entirely too
tight, and hissed, “I need to talk to you later.”
Before I could pull away or laugh it off, the crowd shifted, swallowing
my husband.
When I finally spotted him near the kitchen island, he was completely
cornered. A dozen neighbors were screaming over each other, their faces
inches from his. “WHERE IS IT?!” a man from down the street bellowed,
his veins bulging against his neck. “Where’s the green pill?!” My
husband was pale, his arms raised in defense, screaming back over the
music, “What green pill? I don’t know what you’re talking about!”
I fought my way through the wall of bodies to get to him. The moment the
crowd noticed me, their attention snapped. They pressed in around us,
demanding answers. To buy time and gather intel, I forced my voice to
stay level. “Yeah, tell me about it,” I said, pretending to be in on the
secret. “What does it actually do?”
A woman from our old parish smiled, her eyes completely bloodshot. She
explained that it was a clear capsule tinted sea-green, filled with a
swirling, heavy liquid powder. She said it unlocked the absolute
geometry of the cosmos. It gave you the answers to every secret in the
universe for a temporary window. The catch? You die exactly four hours
after swallowing it.
“But it’s soooo worth it,” she giggled, her fingers twitching.
I backed up in pure horror, scanning the living room. People were
whispering in corners, carefully passing out single green capsules from
hidden pockets, ensuring every single guest got their ration. Through
the flashing lights, I watched in absolute desperation as my husband,
completely overwhelmed and frantic from the pressure, raised a green
capsule to his lips and swallowed it.
“Give us the rest of the supply!” someone shrieked, blocking our path to
the door. They were entirely convinced that we were government plants.
They kept yelling that my parents were deep-cover spies and that my
husband and I were using a local military installation to manufacture
bioweapons to eliminate foreign threats. To them, this lethal toxin was
just a high-stakes party drug, and they thought we were hoarding the
stash.
Then, the clock hit the four-hour mark. Down to the exact second.
The music was still thumping when the front row of guests simply folded
forward. People started dropping like dead weight onto the hardwood.
In the absolute madness, I dragged my husband out of the house. We ran
all the way back to our place. The moment we slammed our front door, I
broke down, sobbing, begging him to stick his fingers down his throat
and throw up the capsule. But something had already changed in his eyes.
He didn’t look at me. He looked through me. He completely ignored my
tears, sprinted out to his Amazon delivery van parked in the driveway,
and cranked the engine.
“I’m going back to the military base,” he muttered coldly through the
open window, his friends already piled into the back. “They’re testing
it on mice. I have to find the rest of the supply.”
Before I could scream for him to stop, the van tore down the street,
leaving me completely alone in the dark.
I turned back toward the interior of my house, my chest heaving. I
walked down the narrow hallway, and my heart stopped. My grandparents
were lying directly in the middle of the floor. They were dead still. My
grandmother was face down, her right arm outstretched toward the front
door, her fingers curled into the carpet like she had tried to crawl for
help before her respiratory system locked up. They hadn’t even been to
the party. They were perfectly fine when we left.
Was this foul play? I whispered into the silence. They would have never
taken a drug like that willingly. The air in our hallway carried that
same sweet, synthetic chemical smell.
My pocket vibrated. A restricted number was on the screen.
When I answered, the voice on the other end was icy, professional, and
completely calm. They identified themselves as CIA operatives. “Act like
you don’t know what they are talking about,” the agent instructed, his
voice cutting through my panic. “They will kill you for the green pills.
Go back to the party right now so the ‘everything’ can be taken care
of.”
I didn’t ask what “taken care of” meant. I was too terrified to argue. I
obeyed.
When I walked back through the unlocked screen door of the suburbia
house, the dream home had transformed into a slaughterhouse. It was a
live-action horror film. The drug had driven the remaining survivors
into a violent, self-destructive psychosis. People were throwing
themselves out of second-story windows, tearing at their own flesh, and
turning on one another in brutal, visceral ways.
The shimmering perfume girl materialized from the smoke of the living
room, blocking my exit. Her eyes were completely blown out. “They sent
you back here, didn’t they?” she whispered. She had taken it. She knew.
“I don’t know what you’re talking about,” I lied, backing into the wall.
She stepped closer, leaning her face inches from mine. A massive,
twisted, deliriously happy smile split her face—the kind of look someone
wears when they are standing in the front row of their favorite concert.
“I know you have the fucking pills,” she smiled, her teeth stained pink.
“You’re gonna give them to everyone here, and we’re all gonna dieee!”
I forced a horrific, awkward chuckle and slipped past her, my boots
sliding in something wet on the floor. I ran from the kitchen to the
living room, desperately looking for a way out, but she was always
there, appearing at every turn like a recurring nightmare. “Girl, you
know I love you,” she begged smoothly, her voice dripping with
desperation. “Just help a girl outtt.”
A loud screech echoed from the driveway. The Amazon delivery van swept
over the curb. My husband was behind the wheel—he hadn’t dropped dead.
He slammed the passenger door open, and I vaulted inside, leaving the
house behind. Through the rear-view mirror, I watched the final moments
of the party. The guests were bleeding profusely from their eyes and
noses, collapsing from massive, sudden cardiac arrest, while the festive
rainbow LED lights kept swirling lazily over the bodies.
We made it back home, rushing inside to find my grandparents exactly
where I had left them. Dead still on the hallway floor.
The grief never got a chance to hit. Within two minutes, blinding red
and blue lights illuminated our living room walls. The front door was
splintered off its hinges by a tactical team. They threw us to the
floor, heavy boots pinning our shoulders down as the handcuffs clicked
into place. We were being formally detained for the possession and
deployment of “bioweapons of mass destruction.”
As they dragged us out into the night, the freezing reality clicked into
place. The CIA operatives didn’t call to protect me. They sent me back
into that house to ensure I was at the scene of the crime. They needed a
clean, believable scapegoat for a catastrophic chemical leak that
originated directly from their own military installation.
My husband and I managed to slip our zip-ties when the transport vehicle
swerved to avoid a pileup on the highway. We’ve been running ever since.
If you see an iridescent green capsule, or if someone tells you they
have the answers to everything, do not run. Do not look back. Just hide.
They are cleaning up the witnesses, and I don’t know how many hours we
have left
