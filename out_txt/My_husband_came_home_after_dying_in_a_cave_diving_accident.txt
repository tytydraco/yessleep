My husband died in a cave diving accident two weeks ago. Well, at least
that’s what I thought until the night he showed up at our front door.

A billowing wave of vertigo washed over me when I first saw him. Of
course, there was relief somewhere in there, but so much about his
return didn’t make any sense. After all, the authorities had searched
for him for days. Ultimately, it lasted a week. It was hard to reason
with the idea that he had miraculously survived being stuck within the
cave system for that long. There was the question of air supply, food,
and managing his body temperature.

Considering it was only meant to be a cave mapping expedition his team
hadn’t decked him out with a surplus of gear. Even so, they had pressed
the matter of safety. In total, he was accompanied by 14 other divers.
The teams were paired together in groups of three. Everyone called to
the task was an experienced diver and all their equipment was inspected
the day of. On paper, it wasn’t supposed to end up the way it did, but
it had.

I’ve always hated this hobby of his. Nothing could ever make me
understand why he couldn’t just do something else, something less
perilous, but I digress.

Initially, the cavern had been mapped by a submersible, but the
surveyors decided it would be best to send down volunteer divers to
confirm the measurements and put guidelines on the routes. Jordan and
his team were swimming through a passage when he told one of his
partners he had seen something. Apparently, there was an offshoot cavern
that wasn’t detected by the submersible. I can picture him now, eager to
see it for himself, ready for the discovery and ever so keen to explore.
Jordan was a pioneer in his own right. He loved a good adventure, loved
the idea of finding something wondrous and new. It’s one of the reasons
I fell in love with him, but he didn’t have to be so reckless about it.

I’m told that in his blind pursuit, he disregarded the advice of his
diving partners. They say he told them he’d check it out quickly and
that he’d be right back. Right back turned to a minute and then a minute
became minutes and by the time it was an hour they had already called
for a rescue team. Before the rescue team arrived, one of his partners
went in after him, but that diver had come back explaining that further
into the canal was too tight for her to fit. The rescue team persisted
regardless, but sure enough, the canal was too small. They couldn’t
understand how Jordan had managed to fit, because there was absolutely
no trace of him elsewhere. So they attempted to look inside with a
camera.

I got to see that footage myself.

There was nothing. Nothing, but a vast expanse of a suffocating void.
The camera ventured around for hours, but still, there were no new clues
to the whereabouts of my husband. Not a trace of his gear or rubble or
anything. Not a single trace of my Jordan. He was gone. It was as if he
had just vanished. A few days after that, I was left with the ultimate
decision, and with a heavy heart, he was pronounced dead. We held a
service by the place he loved most, his final resting place. The ocean.

I was just as hollow as the casket I tried to imagine him in. Surrounded
by loved ones mourning him yet disoriented because he wasn’t actually
there. That night I kept picturing him walking through the door, kissing
the top of my head and telling me about the dive and everything he
might’ve seen on it. But that didn’t happen. I wept and slept alone. His
side of the bed felt unbearably cold. His absence left its very own
cavern within our home, within me.

But then he came home.

On such an ordinary day no less. Insubstantial when compared to the
hassle of funeral and estate planning that had occurred the days prior.
My body was huddled into a piteous ball, rotting on the corner of the
couch as I scrolled through my phone reminiscing over old videos of us
together. Then, the doorbell rang. It was late, much too late for a
delivery driver or the press. My thumb tapped the security app and when
I saw what was on my doorbell camera I practically threw my phone.
Standing in the fisheye lens frame was a figure clad in full scuba gear.
It was as if I had seen a ghost. I rushed to the door, hands trembling
as I turned the knob. What kind of sicko plays a prank like this, I
thought. Did they view my suffering as some kind of joke? My shoulders
tensed as I opened the door, but when I did, when I looked up, it was
truly him. Jordan stood there, hunched over in the doorway wetsuit still
dripping.

“I’m home, Dear.”

The wail that escaped me bordered on inhuman. I lurched forward into
him, my body quaking with frenzied sobs. His heavy hand patted at my
back before sliding down, over and over. The movement felt stiff, almost
practiced. I was barely done embracing him before he pushed past me,
stumbling into the foyer. He had begun to strip off his gear, peeling
out of the second skin that was his wetsuit until he was completely
naked. It fell to the tile with a poignant thwack.

“Oh, Dear. I am famished. Love, what’s for dinner?”

“Jordan,” my voice hushed and gravely from days of crying, “Where have
you been?”

“On my dive, of course. I’m just coming back a little late.” He stumbled
around looking over our walls and shelves like a kid on a field trip to
the Smithsonian.

“A little late? It’s been two weeks!” He halted as I exclaimed.

The turn of his head was unsettlingly rigid as he peered back in my
direction.

“Should I not have come back, Dear?”

The question floored me and instantly I was overcome with guilt. He was
right. I had been provided with a miracle, something so many others
could only dream of. My husband had found his way back to me and here I
was fussing over the details.

“No, no, no. That’s not what I meant.” I wiped the tears from my eyes
and straightened out my back, “I’m sorry, you’re right. How about you
just sit down and get comfortable? As a matter of fact, I’ll whip up
your favorite. How does salmon sound?”

His movement as he descended upon me was mechanical, expression flat as
he approached. For some reason I froze, my body stiffening as he stood
before me. I couldn’t gauge what he was about to do. Thankfully, he
simply snaked his arms around me pulling me in with an unsure squeeze.
His lips were upturned into a thin-lipped smile. The kind of smile you
give your dentist, not your wife.

“Dear, that sounds exceptional! I cannot wait to consume this.” I was so
relieved to be released from his clasp, but his scent still lingered. It
was briny and smelled of damp limestone.

Without another word, he continued to peruse around our home. I walked
over the trail of his gear,  making my way to the kitchen. Our fridge
was empty for the most part. I found it hard to eat over the past couple
of days. Thankfully, we had some frozen fillets and vegetables. I tried
to keep my mind on cooking and just being grateful that my husband had
returned, but something wasn’t right. I saw that video. I saw how small
that canal was. Jordan was, is, a big man. Tall and well-muscled. The
partner who sought him out was a five-foot-nothing woman, thin and
lithe. Even she couldn’t fit through there, so how did he? And how had
he gotten himself back out?

My eyes trailed him as he ventured around, picking up and putting down
whatever he could get his hands on. I wanted to think he was just
excited to be back. That he was just relieved to have survived that
crazy ordeal. But he wasn’t acting like someone safe and sound back
home. He was behaving like it was all, well, new. As if he had never
seen it all before.

After I finished preparing dinner, I set the table for us. Normally, we
would just eat on the sofa together with food tray stands pulled out as
one of our shows for the week played on the TV. But this wasn’t a normal
dinner and I didn’t feel normal and Jordan wasn’t acting normal. We sat
across from one another at the table. I was in pajamas I had worn for
days and he was still completely naked. Vacant eyes scanned over the
food, his tongue darting out to lick the corner of his lips.

“This looks divine, Dear. Let’s dig in!” He ignored the fork and knife
he was previously holding up like tuning forks. Instead, he chose to dig
his hand into the fillet, greedily shoveling it down his throat.

Jordan wasn’t a stickler for table manners, but this was unusual. The
more I watched him the more I realized that something was deeply off
about him. His skin looked unusually tight. Maybe it was dehydration?

I picked up the water pitcher before taking his glass and filling it.
The sound of the ice clinking was barely audible in comparison to the
gulping erupting from his gullet. I gently slid the cup towards his side
of the table. He took a pause from his meal, eyes honing in on the
water. He dropped the food letting it fall from his mouth and back onto
the plate, before grasping the glass and guzzling it all down with a
delighted hum.

“Jordan, how was the dive?” My breaths felt uneven, but I ignored it. I
tried to ignore it.

He held the glass away from his lips, face smeared with fish and ice
water.

“Oh, it was splendid, Darling! A real success I’d say.”

“Really? Did you just get lost down there then? How did you find your
way back?” My throat felt so dry. It was as if I was suffocating, but I
knew I could breathe. I could breathe and everything was normal. But it
wasn’t. I knew it wasn’t.

“Dear, I was never lost. I was just looking around. That’s all!” He
filled his mouth with all of the ice that sat at the bottom of the glass
and began chewing it robotically then swallowing.

“Looking around? …Okay.” Something is very wrong. “Well, what did you
see then?” 

The glass cracked as he bit into it.

The cup shattered as his teeth came down again and again, sharp shards
slicing into the roof of his mouth, gums, and lips as he chewed it
absentmindedly.

I screamed.

He had the nerve to look at me shocked, before putting it down and
spitting out what fragments he could onto his plate.

“Oh my god! Oh my god!” I leapt from the table. I wanted to call an
ambulance, call the police, the news, anyone.

“Oh, Dear! I didn’t mean to startle you. I was just so thirsty. I must
have gotten carried away.”

“You need to go to the hospital. We need to go to the emergency room.”
My words came out weaker than I intended. My hands trembled at my side
as I paced back and forth.

“No, no. I am perfectly fine. Everything is fine.” The chair creaked as
he stood from the table, blood still staining his lips, “I just need a
good night’s sleep. We can talk about all that stuff in the morning.”

My eyes were trained on my feet now, but I could hear him rummaging
through the kitchen. Yet, I was still too shocked to care. Once he was
finished with whatever he was doing in there, he made his way over to
the couch and plucked the throw from it to drape over my shoulders and
keep me still. It was decorative and usually I would’ve fussed about
using it, but it was the most comforting thing he had done to me since
being back.

His cold, damp hands ushered me over to the stairs when suddenly he
stopped behind me. His eyes were fixed at the top of the steps, his
shoulders wound up like tight springs. Perched curiously at the peak was
our pretty little Persian, Luna.

“She’s missed you too. She’s been holed up under the bed since you left.
She must’ve come out when she heard your voice.” I continued onward,
petting Luna as I made my way to our room.

“Oh, yes. Our little Luna.” His voice seemed to lose its previous gusto.
Hesitantly, he followed behind me, but when he reached for Luna she
bowed her back and hissed with a ferocity entirely unlike her. Her paws
rolled forward as she bolted away from him, diving into the room and
under the bed. For a moment, he was frozen in place.

“Oh, geez! What a welcome, wouldn’t you say?” As though nothing
happened, he continued past me and that’s when I saw what was clutched
in his other hand. It was salt. He had brought up the full container of
table salt.

“Jordan, why do you have that?”

“Hm, oh this? Well, I just thought I’d take a bath is all. Rest my
muscles and such. Don’t mind me, Dear. You just get in bed and then I’ll
be right there.”

I mindlessly nodded, but his answer only proposed more questions. My
fingers weakly clutched at the throw, the beadwork uncomfortable against
my skin as I sat myself down on the bed. Jordan waltzed into the
bathroom, closing the door behind him, and sure enough the sound of
rushing water followed soon after.

I couldn’t possibly understand why he would want to take a bath after
being lost at sea. Hadn’t that been enough water? And why was he using
table salt instead of Epsom? Why had Luna hissed at him with vitriol
that I hadn’t even seen her offer to strangers? Luna loved Jordan.

But Jordan wasn’t acting like Jordan.

My refusal to think about it only made me want to think about it. Maybe
he had hit his head down there. Whatever he went through it must have
been traumatic, so it was ridiculous to expect him to just be himself
right after. We could talk about it once he was done bathing. I heard
the water from the spout shut off with an abrupt squeak and as though I
was anticipating it my shoulders relaxed.

My back sank back into my headboard as I stared off looking over at his
nightstand. It was the same as he’d left it, completely untouched. I’m
sure he’d make it all make sense to me and then we could go back to the
way things used to be. Maybe he’d even quit cave diving and we could do
something together instead. Everything was going to be okay. That’s what
I told myself as I waited for him to climb into bed. But as I started to
drift off to sleep, the bed remained empty.

I was jolted awake by an abrupt yowl erupting from the hallway. My feet
stumbled frantically as I rushed out of bed to inspect what was causing
all of the commotion. There was nothing that could prepare me for what I
saw.

Jordan’s jaw was dislodged at an unfathomable angle and Luna was halfway
down his throat, her body writhing and thrashing in a feeble attempt to
escape. Only the white of his eyes were visible as they were rolled back
into his head. Instantly my own wailing melded into a chorus alongside
Luna’s as I began to beat at my husband with the beaded throw. A
thunderous growl erupted from his throat as he disgorged and released
her. Luna fell to the carpet, fur sticky and flat against her body. Once
she got her bearings she ran yowling the whole way in horror at the
ordeal.

“What is wrong with you?! What the hell were you doing?!”

“I was just getting a good look at her, that’s all. I didn’t mean to
upset you, Dear.”

Bewildered. I felt completely bewildered. I couldn’t even begin to know
how to respond to him.

“Oh, darn. I’ve gone and upset you again. How about I make us breakfast?
Let’s get this morning started the right way.” I couldn’t stop imagining
the way that his jaw unhinged like a hogfish around our Luna. The man
that walked down the stairs felt like a stranger to me.

Thankfully, I was able to find Luna and she let me check her out without
too much of a fuss. She was fine for the most part, just covered in
spit. I don’t know. It had that smell. That funky, salty smell that’s
coming from Jordan. It’s so hard to describe, but it reminds me of an
aquarium touch pool or the beach when the tide is low. He smelled like
seawater but specifically when it was teeming with life. Like a fishbowl
that needed a good cleaning.

The chair rocked under me slightly as I bounced my foot nervously; my
fingers played at the stray thread of my sweater’s sleeve. The fire
alarm was blaring and I could see him frantically waving the smoke away
from it. I could’ve helped, but my mind was elsewhere. There were more
pressing matters at hand. I had questions and I needed them answered.

After some time, Jordan came from the kitchen and presented me with a
bowl. Not a plate. A bowl. Underneath the heap of runny eggs and
blueberries, I could make out what I could only assume to be a pancake.
His eyes gazed over me expectantly as I timidly combed through my
“meal”. I took a hesitant bite from a piece of my pancake and
immediately wretched at the overwhelming taste of baking soda. Across
the table, Jordan was shoveling down his meal like the night before.

“Mighty fine if I do say so myself. What about you, Dear?”

“We should tell everyone you’re back. Your mother needs to be the first
to know and then we’ll need to go down to the hospital so they can-”

“Let’s worry about all of that stuff later, Honey. Today can just be
about us.”

“What does that even mean? I don’t think you understand, Jordan. We held
a funeral. We thought you were dead. We don’t just get to pretend all of
that never happened. You need a medical evaluation. Hell, maybe a
psychological one. We have to go see someone-”

“NO!!!” He erupted from the table and for just a moment I am certain I
saw his body swell in size. His expression remained eerily flat, but his
voice. Oh god, his voice. Just a deep, guttural croak. I was frozen
there as he walked up to me. He knelt down and leveled with my eyes, his
hands rested on my face and they were cold. So, so cold. And finally I
could see how glassy his eyes were. Milky, hollow husks.

“No, Dear. Just you and me. Just how it should be. How about we watch a
movie?”

He started to swirl and blur in my vision. I held onto that image. The
image of him swimming amongst my tears. His comforts just as empty as
they had been the night before. Pat. Drag. Pat. Drag.

Pat.

Drag.

The day was rather uneventful. We watched a movie and caught up on
chores. He didn’t talk about what happened in the cave and I didn’t ask.
It was probably the most time we’ve spent together in months. We moved
here for Jordan’s work and he’d been so busy ever since between that and
diving. It felt nice just spending the day together, like old times. I
had almost forgotten about the morning when we were just about to go to
bed. Instead of going to his side of the mattress he made his way to the
bathroom.

“Just gonna take a bath. I’ll be right out, Dear.” Before I could
protest, he closed the door behind him.

I don’t know why I did it, but I know now that I wish I hadn’t. I waited
for the faucet to stop and then I waited and waited for him to come to
bed. Then I was sick of waiting. Quietly, I slipped from the covers my
steps certain as I neared the bathroom door. The silver of the knob
glinted against the light of the moon as it washed in through our
bedroom window. Open me it said. Come see for yourself.

And I did.

Our master bathroom was dressed in lunar blue. The tiles plain mottled
slate danced with the shuddering shadows of leaves in the night breeze.
Little ghosts that taunted me to come closer. The drip of the tub’s
spout echoed rhythmically.

Drip. Drop. Drip. Drop.

From the entrance, I could see the tub was full, but there was no
Jordan. At least, not at the surface. I crept closer until I was
standing over the bath and that’s when I saw him. Swaying about like
laundry in a basin was Jordan or at least the shell of Jordan. Grey eyes
gawked at me but were completely devoid of consciousness. His mouth was
agape, but not a single breath was taken. He was empty, just a bag of
skin that drifted aimlessly within the water, except I could see
something rippling to and fro within him. I watched the mass as it
traveled rolling around in his gut, then crawling through his chest and
throat until I was met with a singular polychromatic eye. And then he
screamed. That guttural croak of a scream. His body expanded almost
instantly and suddenly he had burst to the surface.

My throat was hot with panic as I wailed. My hands grasped for anything,
anyone as I flew from our bedroom and down the stairs and into the
kitchen. I threw open our drawers searching for a knife and grabbed the
first I could find. But my phone. Where did I leave my phone?

“Dear, don’t you know it’s rude to walk in on someone bathing?” His
voice was still somewhere between Jordan’s and that thing’s. As if it
hadn’t completely settled yet.

I clutched the knife behind me, turning towards the stairs. He stood
there, still dripping. My heart pounded hard in my chest threatening to
burst out. The pressure in my head was a warning that I may soon pass
out. My phone. Where did I leave my phone?

“Oh, I get it now. I know what you want.” His lips pulled back into that
tight unnerving smile, “I got just what you need.”

He began to saunter down the stairs.

His shoulders shimmied and his hips began to turn and sway as he made
his way over to me and the laugh that escaped me was one of unnerved
horror and complete utter disbelief. I think he took it as delight.

“Honey, I’m gonna give it to you and I’m gonna give it to you good.” A
flimsy tendril dragged over his eye. He strolled closer until he was
just an arm’s length away. My knuckles tensed as I clenched the handle
of the knife still hidden at my back.

He raised a single hand, wiggling his fingers before sensually sliding
it down his chest and stomach until he was over his penis. He gripped it
at its base and then he began to pull. His fingers dug into the flesh,
nails biting into skin and he pulled and pulled and I could see old
brown blood beginning to ooze as he tried to tear away at the flesh.

Losing all the composure I had initially been able to muster I broke out
into a fit of wailing. This seems to have broken his concentration as I
brought the knife to the front clutching it to my chest with fear and
confusion.

“Oh! Oh, geez! Dear, what’s wrong? I thought this was what you wanted!”

“Why the fuck would I want any of this?! What are you doing?!”

“Well, you came looking at me and you had the knife! I was gonna give it
to you! You know, so you could-”

My knees gave out as I fell to the ground sobbing. I’m certain I was
having a panic attack.

Jordan stared at me, expressionless, but his own confusion was evident.

“Let’s get you to bed, Dear.”

I don’t know why, but I didn’t fight when he helped me up. The knife
fell to the tile with a cold clank and I felt so embarrassed at the
version of myself I saw within the metal. Warped and barely visible but
still a mess all the same.

Jordan sat me in the bed, tucking me under our duvet and draping our
sofa’s decorative throw over top. I lay flat on my back, staring up at
our ceiling, and this time he sat on his side of the bed. His hand
rested on my thigh. Pat. Drag. Pat. Drag.

“You’re not my husband are you…”

I was met with silence. My jaw clenched tight and I was just about to
force myself to ask again, but then he spoke. Not aloud, but within.

No. No, I am not.

When morning came I was catatonic. I could still hear and see everything
around me, but I couldn’t bring myself to do anything about it. I
couldn’t bring myself to speak. I couldn’t bring myself to move. I
couldn’t bring myself to cry. I just didn’t want to feel. Once again, my
world had been turned upside down. Only this time I didn’t know what up
or down really was.
Jordan. No. Whatever puppeteered Jordan came in to check on me. It tried
to get me out of bed, tried to get me to eat. But I refused. I didn’t
want to play this game. Whatever it was. I just wanted my husband back.
But not like this.

When night came, I had finally found the strength to get up. I had
questions and I needed answers. But he was nowhere to be found. I
searched everywhere in the house, including the bath. I searched under
the bed, but only Luna came out. I scoured the backyard, but was met
with nothing. Then finally, I knew where he would be.

The drive to the beach didn’t take long. I parked the car and stepped
out, welcomed by the sound of the night tide and the light of a glorious
full moon. I could see a trail of steps in the sand, going towards the
shore. Standing with his back to me at the water’s edge was Jordan. His
hands were held pensively behind his back. He didn’t look at me as he
spoke.

“I believe it’s best that I leave.”

My chest was tight. Wound up with a tsunami of emotions.

“You…You are so selfish. So that’s it? You just drag me here away from
my friends away from my family and this is how it ends? I gave up
everything, everything to be with you. I gave up my career. I gave up my
home. I gave up myself and you think you just get to leave? What about
everything we made together? What about Luna? What about me?!”

The muscles of his back flexed, but otherwise he stayed still. His
silence was as palpable as the salt that lingered in the air.

“You know, when you said we should have a baby I thought that me saying
we should wait until you get more time to be home would make you

But you didn’t. Instead, you just threw yourself into more work, into
more anything, just to not be with me. Why? Why do you do that? You act
as if I said no, but you knew what I meant. Why am I always the one
compromising Jordan? Why am I always the one giving up pieces of myself
to hold onto you?”

The waves thrummed against the sand like a melancholic metronome.

“And that woman. When she told me she went after you, she was crying so
hard. At first, I thought she was so sad because she thought I was mad
she couldn’t find you, but then she showed up to the funeral and she was
just bawling. Just absolute waterworks over my husband and I don’t even
know who the hell she is. Who is that woman Jordan?”

Tears painted my face, but I wiped them. As he turned to me I knew the
importance of seeing him clearly for the last time.

Not Jordan faced me. His expression remained even yet his oil-slick eyes
were solemn as he spoke. His voice was as steady as the tide before us.

“In his final moments there was nothing he regretted more than
traversing by my lair. Just you and him. That is how he wanted it to be.

Without another word, I watched as it ripped itself out of my Jordan. It
unhinged his jaw like a door, pulling itself out as his body fell to the

patterns screamed at me the way a diamondback’s scales yell out at a

Run away. Don’t come near me. I will kill you.

When the next tide came it took to it and into the sea it went, washed
away with the brine. I flew over to Jordan, my Jordan. His body now a
truly empty husk, but I held him to me and I wept. The sounds of my
cries crescendoing amongst the song of the tides. The shell of my
husband so empty and lifeless. But he was mine. All mine.

Beach security found me cradling Jordan’s slough. I gave the authorities
quite a hard time as they tried to seize his remains for an autopsy, but
in the end they succeeded. They still don’t know what to make of it and
nobody believed me when I told them the truth. They’re saying I must
have had a fit of trauma-induced psychosis due to the state I found his
body in. My parents have since stepped in and have me seeing a grief
therapist. She’s supposed to help me make sense of my feelings and

my account of the ordeal. Though I’m certain she will write this off as
further delusion and recommend that my psychiatrist increase my
prescription dosage. But I know what I experienced and I certainly know
what I saw. I still don’t understand why that thing decided to slide out

it then chose to trail back to our home and attempt normalcy, but what I
can say is that in a way I am grateful for it.

Because, it let me get to see my Jordan one last time.
