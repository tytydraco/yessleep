​

I never believed in ghosts.

Even after moving into Apartment 314, even after the strange noises
started, I always had a logical explanation.

Old building. Settling pipes. Thin walls.

That’s what I told myself.

Then I started hearing someone breathing under my bed.

The first time it happened was around 2:00 a.m.

I woke suddenly and lay still in the darkness. At first I thought the
sound was my own breathing echoing strangely in the room.

But when I held my breath…

It continued.

A slow inhale.

A slow exhale.

Directly beneath me.

I froze.

The sound stopped after a few seconds.

I laughed nervously at myself and checked under the bed with my phone
flashlight.

Nothing.

The breathing happened again three nights later.

Then every night.

Always between 2:00 and 3:00 a.m.

I stopped looking under the bed because it was always empty.

Eventually, I convinced myself the sound came from the apartment below.

Until the night I heard it whisper.

My eyes snapped open.

The room was black.

The breathing was louder than ever.

And from beneath me a woman’s voice rasped:

“Don’t look at me.”

Every muscle in my body locked.

I didn’t move.

I didn’t breathe.

Hours seemed to pass before exhaustion finally dragged me into
unconsciousness.

The next morning I searched the entire apartment.

Nothing.

No hidden spaces.

No speakers.

No explanation.

I didn’t tell anyone.

Because how do you explain something like that without sounding insane?

Instead, I bought a security camera.

That night I aimed it at my bed and let it record until morning.

I almost threw up when I watched the footage.

At 2:13 a.m., I sat upright in bed.

But I had no memory of waking.

On the screen, I swung my legs over the side and slowly crouched down.

Then I crawled underneath the bed.

The rest of the footage showed my feet sticking out from beneath it for
nearly three hours.

At 5:31 a.m., I crawled back onto the mattress and lay down.

I remembered none of it.

Not a second.

I watched that recording six times.

Then something caught my attention.

There were two sets of eyes under the bed.

One pair belonged to me.

The second pair didn’t blink.

I moved out the next day.

I didn’t even pack most of my belongings.

I just left.

A week later I received a call from the landlord.

He sounded confused.

“Why did you leave all those photographs?”

“What photographs?”

“The ones under the bed.”

I felt cold.

“I didn’t leave any photographs.”

There was silence.

Then he said, “They’re all pictures of you sleeping.”

My stomach dropped.

“How many?”

Another pause.

“Hundreds.”

The landlord mailed me one of the photos.

I still have it.

The picture shows me asleep in bed.

The timestamp is from three years before I moved into Apartment 314.

Three years.

Before I’d ever seen the building.

Before I even lived in that city.

Most people think that’s the scary part.

It’s not.

The scary part is what I noticed afterward.

In the darkness beneath the bed, someone is crouching beside me.

Smiling at the camera.

And every few months when I take the photo out to look at it again…

The figure is a little closer.
