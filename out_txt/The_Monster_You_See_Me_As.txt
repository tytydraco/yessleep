Have you ever heard of the drowned house?

The text lit up my phone. At the top of the screen read 3:01.

Outside snow fluttered down, sparkling like stars in the night against
the street lights. Another text pinged my phone.

Come on, I know you’re awake.

I should have pretended I was asleep—could have, easily. She wouldn’t
have believed it. 

What do you want? I texted.

She responded almost immediately.

I want to show you something.

I sigh and roll over in bed. My bedroom was bare and cold as the
outdoors. Window partially ajar, small flurries carefully whisked
through the opening. All the posters and flags and trophies sat in boxes
in the closet. Didn’t mean anything anyway. Her purple sweatshirt still
sat on the foot of my bed. I couldn’t bring myself to get rid of it.
Some spectre of  a warmer time. Another ping.

Carter, please. I’m sorry. I miss talking with you.

I dug my palms into my eyes. I shouldn’t respond. She’ll take the hint.

Please, I’m outside.

Snow flurries stuck in my hair and clung to my pajama pants. Julia was
out there, standing under the oak tree at the end of my parents drive
way.

She was an awkwardly tall, skinny girl, almost to eye level with me. Her
long auburn hair was not in her usual messy bun but braided down her
back—just how I liked it. She wore a beanie and a long blue coat that
ran almost down to her ankles.

She smiled when she saw me. A blade turned in my gut. I took a deep
breath.

“Hey you,” she said.

“Hey,” I mumbled

“You’re looking warm.”

“What do you want, Julia?” It came out gruffer then I meant.

“I-I wanted to see you,” she stuttered.

I raised my arms, “here I am.”

“You walk with me? I want to show you something,” She stuck out her
hand, 

Don’t follow her. Don’t follow her.

Our parents were friends; as such, Julia and I practically grew up
together. Our folks always gave each other knowing smiles, little elbow
nudges. They were so happy when we made it official—our mothers were
already planning the wedding. I never told them the truth about why it
ended, maybe I should have. They hate me. I hate me. I shouldn’t, but I
do. 

Julia led me across town, trying and failing to make conversation. We
stopped near the Scott Bridge, an old truss bridge that spanned the
river cutting through town. 

“You really haven’t heard of the Drowned House?” Julia asked, leading me
down a small foot trail that took us closer to the water. 

“Mm-mmm.”

“Well, there’s an old urban legend around it, back when our parents were
kids. My mom told me about it actually; it’s where her and my dad used
to make out. She said it’s haunted, but, if you’re lucky, one of the
ghosts there grants wishes!”

“Cool.”

Why was I following her? What was there to gain? Closure? I had closure.
I saw the truth with my own eyes. Couldn’t I just enjoy the night?
Forget all the memories?

Something about hearing her talk again, the little glances she spared
back at me, it made my heart flutter like it used to. It wouldn’t last.
I knew that. Like a final meal on death row. Why couldn’t I just enjoy
the moment?

Icy water rushed past us mere inches from our feet. The water level was
low, a dry autumn and a summer drought had done numbers on the river.
Still, the current was strong enough to whisk someone away if we weren’t
careful.

“It’s up here,” she said.

In the darkness of the starless night a small island appeared in the
middle of the river. No, not an island. The lines were too sharp; not
the gentle curves of God but the straight cuts of man. It was a house.

“Ta-Da!” Julia said, doing jazz hands as if completing a magic trick.

A few boulders peeked out from beneath the grey water, convenient
stepping stones to cross the current. They looked slippery, either from
mud or water. Julia led the way, getting halfway across before waving
for me to follow her. I did.

In the end, the Drowned House was nothing special. Just an old  single
room brick building now covered in river mud. What wood or glass there
was now long gone. The walls were thick and tall, well out of the water.
There was a small section of wall with a blanket draped over. 

Julia took a seat, letting her legs dangle off the wall and patted the
space next to her.

“I wonder if we’ll see any ghosts,” she teased.

I didn’t like scary stuff. She knew that. 

I sat beside her. She smiled and stared down at the water, kicking her
feet like a little girl. 

God she was beautiful. I remember when I confessed my feelings for her.
The way she blushed. That day I was the happiest man in the world. Even
then I could still feel that familiar warmth blossom in my chest looking
at her. 

The wind picked up. A high pitch whistle blew through the Drowned
House. 

“Ooooh, must be the ghost,” she teased, bumping my shoulder.

I smiled. I wanted to enjoy the moment. Forget what came before and
enjoy her presence. Like old times.

“If you could wish for something, what would it be?” She asked.

“I don’t know,” I lied.

“Oh come on, there has to be something.”

I chuckled, “like what? A big purple unicorn that farts flowers?”

She laughed. God I missed making her laugh like that. A pit settled in
my stomach as an ugly realization struck me.

Despite everything, I still loved her. 

Her hand rested on the wall. Slowly, it inched closer to mine. She
reached out, hooking her pinky with mine. It was like being struck with
lightning. I recoiled.

“What?” She asked.

“What are you doing?” I asked.

“Nothing. I just thought…”

I scoffed. “What? You thought what?”

“I don’t know! I thought we were having a moment.”

“A moment? What the hell does that mean?”

“I don’t know! I just…I don’t know…okay?”

“What? Did you think you could lure me out here and we would–what?–make
up?”

She blushed and turned away.

I chuckled. “Oh my god, you did didn’t you?”

“Carter, please. I miss you.”

I pinched the bridge of my nose and stifled a laugh. It was the best I
could do to not cry,

“Sure doesn’t feel like it,” I muttered.

“I made a bad choice, okay? I messed up.”

“Oh you messed up huh?”

“Yes!” We were screaming now, “I fucked up! Is that what you want to
hear?”

“You didn’t just fuck up, Julia. Jesus Christ, I saw you!”

Tears rolled down my cheek. Hot against cold skin. 

They both said the same thing. I was never supposed to find out. One
time. Mistake. Didn’t mean to hurt me. But I saw them together. Their
bodies tangled together in the back seat of Logan’s Bronco. A deformed
spider of human legs and arms and bodies. The sounds of pleasure they
made. The way she moaned his name. The fear in her eyes when they
slipped from his face to mine. 

All I could do was laugh. He was never interested in girls and she told
me she wanted to wait till marriage.

What a fool I was.

“I told you, I fucked up!” She was crying now too. Ugly, body raking
sobs. She’d never cried in front of me before that day. She’d done it a
lot since.

“You know my parents’ barely talk to me?” I ask, “They think I broke
your heart.”

“I’m sorry,” she managed meekly.

“What would they think if I told them the truth?” I would never. But I
wanted to hurt her, wanted her to panic.

Her eyes went wide, “No! No, you can’t!”

“What do you want from me, Julia? What do you want?”

She opened her mouth to say something, flem making her lips smack. “We
could…please, Carter, we could try again.”

The world seemed to slow. Soft flurries still fluttered to the ground
around us—glinting like stars. But it was silent. No distant sirens. No
howling wind. Just two tear streaked faces, sniffling through shaky
breaths. 

Silently, I stood up.

“Carter, please,” Julia quietly managed. She grabbed my hand. She was so
cold, fingers like ice. 

“Julia, please let go.” I couldn’t look at her.

Wind whistled through the drowned house.

“How can I fix this? Fix us? Please, just tell me!”

Don’t look at her. Don’t look at her.

“Goodbye, Julia.” My fingers slipped free from her hand.

“Please, Carter! I wish I could be the monster you see me as! But I am
not, I am just a person. A person who—” She started coughing, “A person
who still loves–”

Julia started to gag. I turned only to see her clawing at her throat. 

“Julia?”

Her eyes were full of panic. Her hands moved from her throat, to her
stomach, and back to her throat. I took a step forward but before I
could react, Julia fell backwards into the Drowned House. With a THUD
she hit the ground and began convulsing in the mud.

“Julia! Shit! Shit!”

I fumbled for my phone. There was little service but I still dialed 911.
I couldn’t steal my eyes away from her as she seized.

“I’m sorry, the number you…” I hung up and tried dialing again.

Slowly, something began to press against the inside of her throat. It
wormed its way up her esophagus like a child in the birth canal. Tiny
red worms crested in her mouth. Pulling at her tongue, her teeth. No,
they were not worms. Fingers. Long and skinny and blood covered fingers
pulled their way free of her mouth, stretching her lips so far apart
they began to split.

I couldn’t breathe.

Julia wept. She screamed. Muffled and choked as it was. She grasped and
pulled at her coat. Her stomach started to roll like something pushing
and worming around beneath her flesh. Something pierced through her
skin. A fingernail. Arms burst from her gut. Tearing free from her
winter coat. Dripping blood, grasping and clawing at her, the mud, the
walls. 
My phone slipped. Tumbling from my hand and splashing in the water
below.

I cried. The fingers grew and multiplied in her mouth. Snapping of bone
and popping of muscle. More limbs tore free of her body. Fingers and
arms and shoulders and legs and spines. 

Tears spilled down her cheeks. Her eyes, crazed and wild and scared,
swirled in her skull. I watched in horror. Too frightened to move. Too
cowardly to help.

What could I even do?

The wind picked up, the whistle returning with a rage.

I couldn’t steal my gaze from her face. The pain, the terror; writ large
across her body. Oh God. I should have never come here. Her gaze, wild,
frantic, glossed over mine before locking onto me. There were no words
shared, but a thousand thoughts conveyed.

I closed my eyes and took a deep, shaky breath. Julia…

A text pinged my phone.

Have you ever heard of the drowned house?

Tears still dotted my eyes and soaked my cheeks. A light breeze whistled
through my open bedroom window. I rolled over in bed and swung my feet
out from beneath the covers; wood floor cold beneath bare feet.

Another ping.

Come on, I know you’re awake.

I worked my way towards the window. There she stood. Outside, beneath
the pine tree at the end of my parents driveway. The purple sweatshirt
she wore speckled with snow. I watched her for a time, letting the pang
of sorrow and regret flush through my body until I was numb. A third
text lit up my phone.

Carter, please. I miss you. I just want to talk.

My heart beat thudded in my ears. I loved her, right? I could have her
back. Have what we lost. I want that. Don’t I? 

I blocked her number and closed the window.
