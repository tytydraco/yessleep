   I am a person of science and facts. I do not believe in any
supernatural otherworldly things. Most stories I find with ghouls and
ghosts and curses seem to have some logical explanation. However, I
cannot deny what my own eyes have seen. 

   I have seen many horror movies-few that scared me-but in this case,
the setting did not fit. It was a fairly sunny yet bleak day in my eyes.
I believe it was a Monday, the third day of school- as you cannot forget
something like this.

   I had been new to that school, and made friends quite quickly unlike
my future self today. One friend I had made was Jenessa- a friend I
would later quickly abandon out of fear and denial. She was the one who
told me about Marie. 

   The day would’ve been great. The year would have been great, had I
been kept in denial. I lived that year in great fear and had been drawn
to religion as my only hope, wearing a crucifix every day. You fight
fire with fire as they say, so I fought supernatural with supernatural. 

   It was only that day that I heard it. Never again would I have such
an encounter. 

   Fifth grade feels so long ago- yet I remember this quite well. I
remember our teacher lining us up and leading us down to lunch. I
remember the past two days they assigned our seats in the cafeteria, but
this time they let us choose- which is why I was able to sit next to
Jenessa that day. 
   
   I vividly remember the noise of the cafeteria and the cacophony of
childrens’ chatter. Jenessa and I talked for a little while- I can’t
quite remember what but perhaps she was explaining stuff about school or
I told her about my move from Massachusetts. However, that is not
important. 

   The conversation that followed went something like this:

   “Now that we’re here, I ought to tell you about Marie,” Jenessa
promptly stated.

   “Who is Marie?” 

   “The school ghost.”

   I thought she was perhaps going to tell me a ghost story or some sort
of rumor- I assumed it would be made up but was still intrigued. I
personally loved horror and ghost stories at the time, but that was not
exactly what followed.

   “They say this school used to be a prison, and one of the prisoners
was executed right in this very cafeteria. That prisoner was named Marie
and she still haunts this cafeteria to this day.” 

   It seemed like a rather cheesy story and I am not entirely sure of
the credibility of it. I have done some research on the history of that
school and have not found anything of the sort on it once being a
prison. However what followed was certainly real.

   Jenessa continued, “They say if you listen close enough you can hear
her singing ring around the rosy. Ring around the rosy.”

   She continued singing it as if to imitate how the ghost would sound.
“Ring around the rosy.” She then stopped singing, but I could still hear
it. I could still hear someone singing. “Ring around the rosy. Ring
around the rosy.” Chills came down my spine. It was a different voice.

   Jenessa must’ve been able to tell by the look on my face that I heard
it. I vividly remember her saying, “You hear it too?”

   After that day I rarely talked to Jenessa again, it just reminded me
of the ghost. I would talk loudly in the cafeteria hoping to drown out
any possibility of me hearing the ghost again, and I didn’t. Though,
sometimes at recess, the swings would move on their own.

   I searched for every possible logical explanation, but found none.
Ghosts must be real.

   I am not mad, Jenessa heard it too.
