I want to tell you a story that happened so long ago that I would like
to believe it never really happened.

Seventy years ago, my friend Ollie and I had no parents and lived in a
Catholic orphanage. I was seven and Ollie was eight when Sister Mary
came to us and told us that, along with several other children, we would
have to move to another facility because they no longer had enough room
for us.

It was difficult for us because that orphanage was our home, and it was
all we had ever known. But we were glad they didn’t separate us and that
the two of us could stay together.

Ollie was like a brother to me, and we were never apart.

When we first saw our new home, we were shocked. The main building was
huge, three stories tall, with two towers in the middle. One had a clock
on it, and the other had a cross.

There were a few rooms in the building for staff who weren’t assigned to
care for the children, as well as rooms for the priests. There was a
confessional, a chapel, and several offices for the staff. Before John
took over, the priest in charge had been his father. His father had died
recently, and John had taken over his position. I didn’t know much about
him, but I had heard a lot about him from both the sisters and the
children. They said he was the kindest priest they had ever known. If I
listed everything he had done for the others, I would keep you here for
hours.

On either side of the main building were long wings lined with windows.
In front of it was a large lawn, where some girls were pulling weeds.
There were sixteen residential cottages around the grounds. Each cottage
housed fifteen children and one sister. Sister Teresa was put in charge
of us.

The best part was that we could all stay together in the same cottage.

One day, I couldn’t find Ollie. I looked everywhere for him, but he was
nowhere to be found. Then I thought of one more place he might be.

The main building.

We had been told never to go inside the main building alone, but I
wasn’t exactly one of the obedient children.

I went to the entrance, pushed the door open even though it was heavy,
and slipped inside. A long hallway stretched out in front of me, with
several doors on either side. I tried opening a few of them, but they
were all locked. Then I reached the fourth one, and that one opened.

Inside was a huge room. There was a confessional on one side and a table
with chairs on the other. Holy pictures hung on the walls.

I wanted to look around properly, but then I heard footsteps
approaching. I looked around and couldn’t think of anywhere else to
hide, so I climbed into the confessional.

I hid just in time, because the moment I got inside, the doors flew open
and an angry priest walked in, holding Ollie by the hand. I could see
them through the little holes in the confessional, which gave me a clear
view of the room.

The priest told Ollie that he wasn’t going to give him another warning.
Ollie lowered his eyes and started crying. The priest moved toward the
table and picked up a cane.

He looked at Ollie with a sinister expression and told him that they
could do this the easy way or the hard way.

Ollie was still sobbing when suddenly his sobs began to turn into
laughter.

A horrible laughter that didn’t belong to Ollie at all.

When Ollie laughed, you couldn’t help but want to laugh too. But now,
when I heard that laughter, all I wanted was to run away and hide
somewhere no one would ever find me.

Then he spoke.

The voice was something I had never heard before, and I never heard it
again. I couldn’t compare it to anything. It sounded as if it didn’t
even belong to our world.

“But John. Good priests aren’t supposed to do this. What would your
father say?”

The priest frowned, raised the cane, and angrily swung it at Ollie,
telling him to stop this nonsense immediately.

Ollie caught the cane and tore it from the priest’s hand.

“You will not speak to me like that, John!”

At that exact moment, the window shattered, sending glass flying
everywhere.

The priest stumbled backward.

Ollie tilted his head to the side and repeated:

“What would your father say, John?”

Even from that distance, I could see the priest trembling.

“W-What are you?”

“Oh, John… I know you’re a bad priest, but I didn’t think you were
stupid enough to be this bad.”

The priest reached up to his neck, tore off his cross, and began
fumbling around in his pocket. He pulled out a small bottle, quickly
removed the cap, splashed the water onto Ollie, and began reciting a
prayer.

Blood began running from Ollie’s eyes, and steam started rising from his
body.

“Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name…”

Ollie slowly collapsed to the floor and began screaming in pain while
the priest continued praying.

But after a moment, the screams of pain began turning into that horrible
laughter again.

Then the laughter changed into a voice that began reciting the rest of
the prayer together with the priest.

“And forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against
us, and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.”

The bottle slipped from the priest’s hand and shattered on the floor,
adding itself to the other pieces of broken glass.

Ollie looked at him and said:

“That’s not how this works, John.”

His face twisted as he continued:

“If your father had done this, it could have hurt me. Unlike you, he
truly was His son.”

He spat the last sentence out with something close to disgust.

The priest took several more steps backward.

“What do you want from me?”

“You know what I want, John.”

The priest fell to his knees. His voice trembling, he began to stammer:

“I’ll make it right. I’ll change. I’ll never do it again.”

Ollie chuckled.

“Do you remember Michael, John? The moment you thought of him in a way a
father should never think of his child, I knew you belonged to me.”

The priest began sobbing and continued begging and promising that he
would change.

Ollie only kept chuckling.

He let him beg like that for what seemed like an eternity before finally
speaking again.

“I think it’s time, John. You’re starting to bore me. What a shame. You
could have seen your father again very soon, but instead…”

He didn’t finish the sentence. He simply waved his hand.

I didn’t see anything, but the priest’s eyes widened and his face became
even paler.

“No. No, please, no.”

He stared at something in front of him in absolute terror, clutched his
chest, and slowly collapsed to the floor, repeating over and over:

“No, no, no… please, no.”

Ollie leaned toward him and whispered something in his ear.

The priest stopped moving.

Ollie straightened up.

His eyes remained fixed on the priest.

“Do you know what I like most about them?”

He slowly turned toward the confessional, and the doors that had been
protecting me flew open.

I was shaking so badly that I was amazed I could still stand.

Ollie was staring directly at me, and my heart was pounding so hard that
I thought it was going to burst out of my chest and kill me.

“I asked you, Thomas. Do you know what I like most about them?”

There was impatience in his voice, but I couldn’t get a single word out.

Ollie raised his voice and shouted:

“Answer me when I ask you something!”

I closed my eyes, and to this day I don’t know how I managed to force
the words out.

“I don’t know.”

“What?”

The voice that didn’t belong to Ollie became quiet again.

“That they think that if He forgives them, they’re safe. As if it were
up to Him to decide.”

I opened my eyes and saw him studying me.

He must have seen how terrified I was.

He looked directly into my face again and tried to smile.

I swear, it was the most horrible thing I had ever seen.

Not the terrified expression on the priest’s face. Not even Ollie’s
voice, the voice that wasn’t his.

It was the smile.

That was what still wakes me up to this day.

That horrible smile, with not a single trace of anything good in it.

I felt something warm running down my legs, and I collapsed to the
floor.

Ollie turned away, and before everything went dark, I heard him say:

“Maybe we’ll see each other again, Thomas. But broken windows won’t be
enough next time.”

After that, I only remember what I was told by one of the sisters.

Apparently, there had been an earthquake, which caused Ollie and me to
lose consciousness and gave the priest a heart attack, killing him.

What a ridiculous story.

But when they questioned me, I neither confirmed nor denied it.

Ollie was himself again. He remembered nothing from the moment the
priest picked up the cane.

It was later discovered that the bastard had hurt several children.

It shook the entire community, and strict rules were put in place to
protect the children.

I heard Sister Annie angrily telling another sister, one I didn’t know,
that she respected God’s will, but that John had gotten off far too
easily and should not have escaped punishment.

If only she had known that John would have given anything to spend
eternity locked inside a cell.

After what happened, I couldn’t bring myself to talk to Ollie anymore.

I knew it hurt him, and he didn’t understand what he had done, but
whenever I looked at him, all I could feel was anxiety and fear.

I stopped talking to anyone and spent entire days alone in my room.

Eventually, they listened to my pleas and transferred me back to where I
had lived before.

I never heard from Ollie again.

Today I am seventy-seven years old, and I don’t care whether you believe
this story or not.

I don’t care where life takes you.

I just needed to finally tell someone, because I know I don’t have much
time left, and I am terrified.

I’m afraid, and I hope I was a good enough person to meet John’s father
and our Lord.

Because I know what happens to those who behave like John.
