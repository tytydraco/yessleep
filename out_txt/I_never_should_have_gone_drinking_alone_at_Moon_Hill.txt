Locals know the spot. It’s this steep, overgrown ridge right on the edge
of the city limits. On Friday and Saturday nights, it turns into a
chaotic parking lot—a haven for teenagers smoking weed, blasting music
out of their trunks, and people looking for a discreet place to hook up
in the dark.

But during the week? It’s completely dead. Abandoned.

There’s nothing up there on a Tuesday except the relentless, biting wind
kicking up red dirt, and the heavy rustling of the eucalyptus trees that
sounds a little too much like whispering.

I was up there this past Tuesday because I was at the absolute lowest
point of my life. I had hit a wall of depression that felt impossible to
climb over. I bought a cheap, acidic bottle of wine at a corner store
and hiked up the dirt trail right as a freezing, gray dusk settled over
the city.

I sat on the edge of the dirt cliff, staring at the grid of streetlights
miles below. My apartment was down there somewhere—just another
meaningless little speck of light among millions. I remember sitting in
the dirt, sipping that awful wine, thinking about how I really didn’t
want to go back down. I didn’t want to go back to my life at all.

By the time the bottle was empty, my vision was blurring at the edges.
The wind was numbing my face, and the silence of the hill was starting
to feel heavy.

That’s when I heard the footsteps.

Gravel crunching, slow and deliberate, coming up the trail behind me.

I looked up and squinted through the orange glow of the dying twilight.
The silhouette was unmistakable. It was my greatest love. Carol.

She was wearing those ripped, tight jeans I always loved, her hair tied
up in that messy bun she defaulted to, and the sharp, familiar scent of
Marlboro Reds reached me before she even spoke. In her right hand, she
casually swung a bottle of Jack Daniel’s—our absolute favorite. The
exact same drink we used to split on the good nights, back before
everything between us completely crumbled.

“Were you waiting for me?” she asked.

She sat down right next to me on the damp earth, acting as if our
incredibly painful, messy breakup had never even happened. I was still
so hopelessly in love with her. I just smiled like an idiot, too stunned
to process the logic of the situation.

“Carol… what the hell are you doing up here?”

She didn’t answer. She just uncapped the bottle, took a long swig, and
held it out to me.

So, we drank.

For the first few minutes, it felt like a fever dream in the best way
possible. The warmth of her shoulder brushing against mine, the raw
alcohol burning my throat, her presence anchoring me against the
freezing wind.

Then, the conversation started to warp.

“You always were a piece of shit, weren’t you?” she said.

She laughed softly. Her voice was smooth, exactly like I remembered, but
there was a strange, vibrating undertone to it now. Something synthetic.
Something inhuman that made my stomach drop.

“Look at where you are,” she continued, staring straight ahead at the
city lights. “Fucked up, drinking poison in the dirt, completely alone.”

I blinked hard, confused and suddenly defensive. “Fuck off…”

She reached out and gave my cheek a light, mocking slap.

“You know I never actually loved you, right? Nobody is ever going to
love you. You’re a mistake. You’re like a walking infection. You’ve
never been good for anything, and you ruin the lives of everyone
unfortunate enough to get close to you.”

There was no anger in her tone. It was conversational, almost sweet,
which made the venom so much worse.

“Your mother died of sheer disappointment because of you.”

That hit me like a physical blow. Those words echoed the exact, toxic
loop that played in my head every single day. I shrank into my jacket,
feeling utterly defeated.

She reached over and started running her fingers through my hair. It was
a cold, hollow imitation of affection.

“You know what would fix it?” she whispered, nodding toward the sheer
drop just a few feet away. “Right there over the edge. Just jump. The
world gets instantly better. All this heavy, suffocating pain in your
chest? It vanishes. I’d even go with you… we can hold hands… it’s
literally just one step.”

My heart slowed down to a crawl. My legs twitched, feeling a bizarre,
magnetic pull to just stand up and do it. She was talking, and I was
nodding along. Deep down in that dark pit of my brain, I agreed with
her. I really thought there was no other way out.

I stared at the edge. The drop suddenly looked incredibly peaceful to my
drunk, exhausted mind.

“Just one step,” she repeated, and her icy hand slipped into mine.

Right at that exact second, my phone violently vibrated in my pocket.

It broke the trance. I pulled it out, my screen painfully bright against
the pitch-black night.

A text message. From Carol.

Carol: “Idiot, I just had a really weird dream. You died. I’m literally
shaking right now. Are you okay?”

I read the words three times. My brain couldn’t process it. The hand
holding the phone started sweating cold and shaking uncontrollably.

The Thing sitting next to me leaned in closer. I felt its neck stretch
just a little too far, bending at an impossible angle to get near my
ear. Its breath smelled like stale earth, not smoke.

“Ignore that,” it whispered, barely audible. “It’s probably just my
brother messing with you.”

But the text—calling me an idiot, the specific way she showed
concern—that was the real Carol. There was no doubt.

With trembling, clumsy fingers, I typed back: Turn on your camera. I
need to see you.

Two seconds later, an incoming video call lit up the screen.

I answered. On the display, the real Carol appeared. Her eyes were puffy
from sleep, her hair was a mess, and she was wearing that faded Nirvana
shirt I knew so well. The warm light from her cat-shaped lamp behind her
showed her bedroom. She was safe. She was at home.

“Oh thank God, you’re okay,” she muttered, her voice thick with relief.
“I had a horrible dream. You were up somewhere really high, and someone
pushed you. You fell and I woke up screaming…”

She paused, squinting at the screen.

“Why are you in the dark? What is that shadow behind you?”

I couldn’t speak. My vocal cords were paralyzed.

Because in the dark reflection of my phone screen, right next to my own
face, I saw the truth. There was no girl in ripped jeans beside me.
There was a towering, immobile silhouette of a woman in a long, tattered
black dress, standing directly behind my shoulder.

My entire body locked up in primal terror. I couldn’t turn my head. I
didn’t dare look back.

Only my eyes darted to the side. The illusion of Carol sitting beside me
on the dirt had vanished completely, but the suffocating pressure of a
predator in the space next to me was heavier than ever.

I stared into the phone camera, tears welling in my eyes.

“If something happens to me… I didn’t jump. I was pushed,” I choked out,
my voice cracking. “I love you. I’m sorry for everything.”

I ended the call.

I heard the glass bottle of Jack Daniel’s tip over and roll against the
rocks. It was completely empty.

A low, raspy laughter started to echo across Moon Hill. It seemed to
come from the trees, the dirt, the wind itself. The entity was still
there, occupying the space right beside me in the dark.

Who the hell had I been drinking with?

I didn’t wait around to find out. Pure, unadulterated survival instinct
overrode my frozen muscles.

I scrambled to my feet, slipping in the dirt, and I ran.

I ran without looking back. My feet caught on rocks and roots. I slammed
shoulder-first into a thick tree trunk, feeling a sharp pain and warm
blood trickling down my forehead, but I didn’t even slow down.

I tumbled down the steep, uneven trail in the pitch black until my shoes
finally hit the solid, flat asphalt of the main road. I kept running for
blocks, gasping for air, until my lungs gave out and I collapsed onto
the sidewalk under a streetlamp, vibrating with pure panic.

I never went back to that hill.
