I don’t really know what happened last night. I know that’s becoming a
theme with these posts, but this time I mean it literally. I’m missing
about four hours. The last thing I remember clearly was sometime after
two in the morning. I was still in the hospital, still awake, and still
absolutely starving. The nurses had lowered most of the lights on the
floor and everything had gotten quiet enough that all the little sounds
I’d been noticing seemed even louder.

I remember checking my phone at 2:18. I remember hearing the older man
across the hall cough. He’d been doing it on and off all night, and I
could hear his TV too, though he’d turned it down low enough that I
probably wouldn’t have noticed it a week ago. I remember putting my
phone on my chest and closing my eyes. Then I woke up and it was 6:11 in
the morning.

For a few seconds I just lay there because something felt different and
I couldn’t figure out what it was. Then I realised I was warm. Actually
warm. I grabbed the thermometer they’d left beside the bed and checked.
98.2. I looked at it for a few seconds and did it again. 98.1.

That alone would’ve been enough to make me begin thinking whatever was
happening was finally wearing off, but then I felt something inside my
chest. My heart was beating. Not barely moving as it had been on the
ultrasound. Not some weird slow rhythm which the doctors couldn’t
explain. Actually beating. I put my fingers against my wrist and counted
because I didn’t trust myself. Seventy-two. I checked again a minute
later and got seventy. Completely normal.

I probably sat there feeling my own pulse for longer than I want to
admit. After everything over the last few days, it was the first thing
that appeared like genuinely good news. Then I realised I wasn’t hungry
anymore. That was almost stranger than the heartbeat. The feeling that
had been getting worse all night was just gone. No empty feeling in my
stomach. No saliva building in my mouth whenever I smelled blood.
Nothing. I felt like I’d eaten a huge meal and slept for ten hours. I
hadn’t done either. At least I didn’t think I had.

I got up to go to the bathroom and noticed my IV had been removed. Tape
was still on my arm, and a little piece of gauze covered the spot, so I
assumed somebody had taken it out while I was asleep. I didn’t remember
anyone doing it, but that didn’t seem especially weird at first. I’d
barely slept in days, and if I finally crashed hard enough I could
believe I’d slept through a nurse coming in.

Then I looked at my neck. The wounds were gone. The two spots under my
jaw that hadn’t healed at all since this started were completely closed,
but they left scars. Really ugly ones. Both are raised and darker than
the skin around them, with this thick puckered tissue that looks more
like something I’ve had for years than something that healed overnight.
The skin around them feels strange too. Not numb exactly, but I barely
feel anything when I touch them.

I stood in front of the mirror for a while, trying to understand how
wounds that looked almost exactly the same yesterday could turn into
old-looking scars in a few hours. The rest of me looked better too. The
redness from the sunlight was completely gone. The dark circles under my
eyes had disappeared. Even my skin looked healthier. I didn’t look like
someone who’d spent the last couple of days in a hospital with a body
temperature in the low nineties and almost no heartbeat. I looked
rested.

A nurse knocked on the bathroom door and asked if I was okay. When I
came out, I told her my temperature was normal again. She checked it
herself and got 98.3. Then I told her my heart was beating. That got a
little more of a reaction. She checked my wrist first, then hooked me
back up to the monitor. For the first time since I’d gotten there,
nobody immediately assumed the machine was broken. Normal rhythm, normal
blood pressure, normal oxygen. Everything.

She told me to stay there and went to get the doctor. Within twenty
minutes, two doctors were in the room. They did another ECG, listened to
my chest, took more blood, checked my reflexes, made me walk around the
room and asked the same questions they’d been asking since I arrived.
Dizzy, weak, short of breath, chest pain. No to all of it. I felt great.

The bloodwork came back normal again too. Perfectly normal. The
cardiologist eventually came in and listened to my heart for a long
time. He moved the stethoscope around my chest, checked my pulse and
looked at the ECG again. I asked him if something was wrong. He said no.
I asked him why he looked annoyed. He told me he wasn’t annoyed, then
admitted that less than twelve hours earlier they couldn’t explain how I
was conscious with what my heart was doing and now there was nothing
abnormal about it.

I asked if that could just happen. He said, “Not usually.” That’s still
the best explanation I’ve gotten.

By seven or eight they were already talking about possibly letting me go
home later today if my vitals stayed normal. I should’ve been happy
about that. I was, mostly. The first thing that started bothering me was
the room across the hall. The older man wasn’t there anymore. His door
was open and somebody from housekeeping was changing the bedding. I
assumed he’d been moved. There are people constantly being moved around
in a hospital, so I didn’t really think anything of it.

Then I heard one of the nurses ask about him. I wasn’t intentionally
listening. It’s just hard not to hear things now. She was talking to
another nurse at the station while they were going through something on
the computer.

“Did Keller leave?”

“Looks like it.”

“Last night?”

“Yeah.”

There was some clicking on the computer before the first nurse asked who
had picked him up. The other nurse said she didn’t know. They talked
about something else after that. Nothing about it sounded dramatic and
nobody sounded worried. I wouldn’t have remembered the conversation at
all if a woman hadn’t shown up a couple hours later asking where he was.

She said she was his daughter. That was how I found out his name was
Mr. Keller. The nurse told her that according to the system he’d been
discharged overnight. His daughter asked who he’d left with, and the
nurse didn’t know. She said somebody else could’ve picked him up. His
daughter said there wasn’t anybody else. Then she said something about
him not having his car there and that he’d called her the night before
saying she should come get him in the morning.

That was when the nurse stopped giving automatic answers and started
actually looking through the computer. Another nurse came over, then the
charge nurse. They lowered their voices after that, but that doesn’t
really help around me anymore. I heard the daughter ask where he had
gone, and one of the nurses said they were trying to figure that out. A
few minutes later somebody from administration came down and took her
somewhere else.

That was it. Nobody ran around shouting that someone was missing. Nobody
locked down the hospital. The whole thing just disappeared behind a door
somewhere. By the time they finished cleaning his room, they were
bringing another patient in.

I tried not to think about it. People leave hospitals. Paperwork gets
screwed up. Families don’t always know everything that’s happening.
There are completely normal explanations for all of that. I kept
repeating that to myself.

Breakfast showed up not long after. Eggs, toast and orange juice. It all
still tasted terrible. The eggs barely tasted like anything. The toast
might as well have been cardboard, and the orange juice tasted so sour
that I couldn’t finish it. But I didn’t care because I wasn’t hungry.
That feeling from yesterday was completely gone.

A nurse drew more blood while I was eating. I smelled it when she pulled
the needle out. Nothing happened. No cramping. No mouth watering. No
feeling like every part of me suddenly wanted whatever was on that piece
of gauze. It was just blood. I should’ve been relieved by that. Instead
it made me feel weird.

The older nurse from yesterday came in sometime after nine. The one I’d
mentioned in the last post. She looked at the monitor, checked my
temperature and asked how I felt. I told her I felt normal. She said,
“That’s good.”

I showed her the scars on my neck and told her they’d healed overnight.
She bent closer and looked at them for a few seconds before saying,
“Looks like they did.” I asked if she’d ever seen something heal that
fast. She gave me a small shrug and said she’d seen a lot of strange
healing. That was it. She didn’t explain what she meant and I didn’t
ask. After everything from yesterday, I’m trying not to read too much
into every strange thing she says.

Before she left, I asked if she knew where Mr. Keller had gone. She
looked toward the hallway and said she hadn’t been his nurse. I told her
I knew, I’d just heard there was some confusion. She looked back at me
and said, “Then I’d let them sort it out.” That was a fair answer.
Still, something about the way she said it made me stop asking
questions.

A little while later I noticed my step count. I don’t really use the
health app. I only opened it because of an activity notification. It
said I’d walked 286 steps between 2:36 and 2:51 this morning.

I thought it was wrong. Honestly, I still think it might be. Phones
aren’t medical equipment. They count random movement sometimes. It’s
told me I took steps while sitting in a car before. But almost three
hundred seemed like a lot. I checked the rest of the night. Nothing,
then suddenly 286 steps, then nothing again. I looked at the distance
and it was a little over a tenth of a mile. There’s no way I walked that
far inside this room.

I thought maybe a nurse had moved my phone, except my phone had been on
the charger beside me when I fell asleep and it was still there when I
woke up. Then I checked the heart-rate history from my watch. Yesterday
was basically useless. It kept failing to get a reading, which isn’t
surprising considering what was going on with me. Then at 2:49 this
morning it suddenly started working.

Sixty-nine beats per minute. A few minutes later, seventy-one. Then
sixty-eight. Normal readings from that point forward.

I looked back at the steps. 2:36 to 2:51. My heart started beating again
at 2:49. The last thing I remember is checking the time at 2:18. I sat
there staring at my phone for a while before eventually asking one of
the nurses if anything had happened with me overnight.

She checked the chart and said it looked like I’d slept. I asked if I’d
gotten up at all and she said there wasn’t anything documented. Then I
showed her the steps. She laughed a little and told me my phone probably
wasn’t the best source for figuring out what happened while I was
asleep. She’s right. I felt stupid for even showing her.

Then I started getting dressed. They’re letting me wear my own clothes
now because apparently there’s a good chance I’m going home later. I was
washing my hands in the bathroom when I noticed a dark spot underneath
two of the fingernails on my right hand. At first I thought it was dirt,
which would’ve been strange enough considering I’d spent the last two
days in a hospital bed.

I picked at it under the running water and watched the dark stuff loosen
and turn reddish-brown in the sink. It was dried blood. Not much, just
enough to have worked its way underneath the nails.

I checked my fingers for cuts. Nothing. I checked around the nails and
the backs of my hands. Nothing there either. The spot where they’d drawn
blood was on my other arm, and there wasn’t any dried blood around the
bandage anyway.

I stood at the sink for a while trying to remember anything which could
explain it. Maybe I’d scratched the wounds on my neck while I was
asleep, but when I checked the scars there wasn’t any dried blood around
them. Maybe I’d touched one of the gauze pads after a blood draw and
just didn’t remember. That appeared possible enough, so I scrubbed my
nails until it was gone.

I don’t remember walking around the hospital. I don’t remember falling
asleep, and I don’t remember anything happening with the man across the
hall. I know there are normal explanations for all of that. The blood
could’ve come from something I touched and forgot about. The phone
could’ve miscounted movement. Mr. Keller could’ve gotten confused and
left on his own. The hospital could’ve screwed up his discharge
paperwork. Any one of those things by itself wouldn’t bother me.

The problem is that I went to sleep with almost no heartbeat, a
temperature in the low nineties, wounds that wouldn’t heal and a hunger
that was getting so bad I could barely think when somebody nearby
started bleeding. I woke up four hours later with a normal heartbeat, a
normal temperature, scars where the wounds used to be and absolutely no
hunger at all.

The doctors just checked me again. Heart rate 73. Temperature 98.4.
Everything normal. They’re talking about discharging me this afternoon,
and I should be relieved. Instead I keep looking at the room across the
hall.

I don’t remember what happened last night. I’m starting to think maybe
that’s better.
