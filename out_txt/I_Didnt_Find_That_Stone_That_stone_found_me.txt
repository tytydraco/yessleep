I’ve watched enough horror movies to draw one crucial conclusion about
survival: never pick up anything strange from a deserted place, even
from a regular road that just happens to be empty at the moment.

Yet, that rule proved useless in this particular experience. When a
horror story decides to cast you as the lead, it will find you no matter
how hard you try to avoid it.

I hadn’t done anything considered taboo in horror movies. All I’d picked
up was fallen and ready-to-eat pecan nuts from the ground (one of my
hobbies is foraging, and the rows of pecan trees lining the road were a
paradise to me). Even so, that didn’t spare me from the thing that had
targeted me the moment I became aware of its presence.

Let me correct that slightly: that thing wanted me to be aware of its
presence.

It started yesterday afternoon, while I was untangling a mess of
thoughts and picking up a few ripe pecans that hadn’t yet been gnawed on
by insects. Every now and then, a nut or two would land on the back of
my neck and shoulders like a shower of solid gold. It was a stroke of
luck, really; I needed an extra snack before facing the grind of campus
life again.

Something plummeted onto the back of my neck that was protected by my
hoodie and making me wince. It felt twice as heavy and hard as a raw
pecan; had it hit my bare head, it might have caused a lump or drawn
blood. My eyes darted to where the object had landed. It turned out to
be a small stone; at a glance, its shape and size resembled a guitar
pick—likely from a river or beach, given that its edges were smoother
than the surrounding pebbles. I furrowed my brow: had someone just
thrown it? Or had a crow carried it here?

There was no sound of birds, save for the woodpecker that had been
“working” on the tree where I was gathering seeds, and I was the only
human in sight. So both of my theories were wrong.

I examined the stone more closely. At first glance, it looked like an
ordinary rock. It colors were light gray with dark flecks indicating the
presence of different minerals. However, when sunlight struck its
surface at just the right angle, strange markings appeared: a star-like
symbol encircled by lines of light. The edges of the stone also took on
a lustrous sheen, as if it were made of some extraordinary crystal.

My first thought was: had someone lost their trinket?

There was a lost-and-found booth near the park, so I decided to leave
the stone there. The attendant gave me a strange look, as if I were
playing a prank on him. I explained that the stone was too fine to be
just an ordinary pebble, that it might have belonged to someone’s
collection. Fortunately, he was understanding and let me leave it at the
booth.

Looking back, I feel like laughing at my own naivety. Thinking the stone
was merely an inanimate object was my first mistake. I realized this
when I went back to foraging for nuts. Somehow, the stone had found its
way into the plastic bag I was using to collect my haul. I distinctly
remembered leaving it at the booth. I took the stone out and placed it
on the ground before walking away. Sure enough, it reappeared, this time
in my jacket pocket.

Flashes of various horror stories I could remember raced through my
mind. I couldn’t let fear get the better of me, nor did I dare lash out
at a mysterious object that might be capable of harming me. Whispering
respectfully, “I’m sorry, but I can’t take you home,” I set the stone
down near a tree and hurried home with my bag of pecans. I decided to
forget about the stone, hoping that whatever haunted it would understand
and leave me alone.

I returned home hungry and anxious. To calm myself down, I decided to
roast some pecans I had washed and dried, eating the rest raw. So far,
so good. I turned off the oven and opened my laptop on the fold-out sofa
in the living room, intending to work on a film analysis assignment. I
put on my headphones before immersing myself in literary theories that
were swirling around in my head without me fully grasping them.

I was too exhausted to think and eventually fell asleep with my laptop
still open. Whether influenced by the film I was studying or the thick
textbook beside me, I found myself in the middle of an unfamiliar
garden. There was no one around. Not a sound from animals or the wind.
The silence of the dream made me uneasy.

I turned and fled, keeping my head down to avoid whatever jumpscare
might await me if I dared to look up and around. I realized I wasn’t
running fast enough when a cold hand seized me by the throat from
behind. I tried to scream and struggle, but the hand dug its nails into
my skin like the hooks on a fishing line.

I heard a voice whispering behind me, coming from my attacker. “Good
girl… so young… finally found you…”

I wanted to scream “Mom!”, “Dad!”, or “Help me!”, but all that escaped
was a sharp, silent gasp. My hands began flailing wildly in the air. The
back of my hand brushed against something hard, and that was when I woke
up.

It turned out I had bumped my laptop, sending it toppling onto the sofa.
My breathing had returned to normal, but the salty tears on my face had
not yet dried. I hurriedly felt the back of my neck. The next instant, a
chill washed over my fingertips.

The stone had returned. This time, it clung to the back of my neck like
a spider. A stinging pain shot through me whenever I tried to pry it off
my skin. It wasn’t merely attached; the skin beneath it felt as though
it had fused with the stone’s surface. Nausea churned in my stomach;
what had I done in my life to deserve a punishment like this?

All I could do was weep through the night. My vision swam as I imagined
what might happen next. The stone sank deeper into my neck. I had to do
something.

I tried to pry the stone out again. This time, I pressed an ice cube
against the skin around the stone to numb the pain. I wedged my
fingernails beneath the edge of the stone. I focused on controlling my
breathing as a wave of dizzying pain washed over me. Five attempts
later, the result was a total failure; the stone had only sunk deeper.

Desperation set in. I grabbed a fruit knife from the kitchen, sharpened
it as much as possible, and washed it clean. Then, I fetched more ice
and two towels from the bathroom: one to bite down on, and another to
protect the back of my shirt from blood splatter. I had to summon the
courage. Whether the curse would be lifted or not was a concern for
later.

The touch of the knife against my skin made me tremble, but my fear was
swept away by a rush of adrenaline the moment I felt the stone beginning
to merge with my blood vessels and muscles. My hand moved swiftly,
driving the tip of the knife beneath the stone. My heavy breathing
echoed in my ears. Blood began to flow, but I refused to stop. I
growled, biting hard into the towel. My head spun. My vision began to
blur as more blood poured out. The stone hadn’t come loose yet. The
battle wasn’t over.

Faintly, a sharp, anguished whisper, “How dare you… how dare you…”
reached my ears amidst a world that spinning ever faster. Then I lost
consciousness, only to wake up without a single drop of blood on my
clothes, my hands, or the towel beneath the back of my neck. My body
felt weak but otherwise fine. The stone was gone.

I could still hear the sorrowful curse (“How dare you…”) echoing around
me when I regained consciousness, and the nightmares plagued my sleep
for two days after the incident before simply vanishing. Whatever was
targeting me, it surely hadn’t expected me to fight back. I went to
campus as usual and submitted my individual assignment without any
further trouble.

I had escaped that stone, and I wasn’t deterred from going back to
forage for pecans, so it felt like a total victory.

Or so I thought; I was indeed “free,” but that didn’t mean the terror
had truly vanished. I only realized that horrifying truth yesterday,
when I lost my phone charm. It must have fallen somewhere along the path
where I usually forage.

I asked the lost-and-found attendant. He didn’t answer immediately,
instead stared intently at me until I felt a shiver run down my spine.
In a flat voice, he asked, “Were you the one here yesterday?”

“Yes, I’m the one who left an odd stone here,” I said with an awkward
laugh.

“I know. You’re the one who rejected the stone, aren’t you?”

My throat instantly froze. “What?”

“The stone sought you out. And you refused. You missed a golden
opportunity. By the way, what does your phone holder look like? Let me
find it.”

As he turned away, my eyes caught something familiar at the nape of his
neck. A smooth, triangular gray object bearing a star symbol that I had
seen before, now appeared seamlessly fused with his skin, as if it had
been there since birth.

“My mind is clearer now,” the attendant’s voice sounded hollow. “Back
then, something like this was nothing more than a pipe dream. Now…”

I didn’t need to hear what he said next. I ran as fast as I could to my
place and packed my things without wasting a moment. When my parents
asked why I’d returned, I told them a stalker had shown up at my home.
They let me stay for the sake of my safety. Even though the commute to
campus was longer, at least I was safe from whatever might be chasing
me.
