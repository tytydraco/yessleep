I was six years old, alone on a cliff.

There was no grass. No trees. Nothing grew there at all. The ground was
cracked beneath my feet, stretching behind me as far as I could see.

Then there were hundreds of people.

An entire village stood at the edge of the cliff, shoulder to shoulder,
silent as stones.

Nobody spoke. Nobody moved.

They were all looking up.

I found my mother at the back of the crowd.

“Mom?”

She didn’t answer.

I stepped closer.

“Mom, I have to go potty.”

She kept looking at the sky.

I tugged on her sleeve.

“Mom.”

She finally looked down at me, but only for a moment.

“I’m busy.”

Then she looked back up.

I followed her gaze.

At first I couldn’t understand what I was seeing.

Something enormous floated above us.

It was a baby.

A gigantic infant, naked except for a diaper, suspended impossibly high
in the blue-black sky. Its enormous arms hung at its sides. Its legs
dangled beneath it.

It didn’t cry.

It didn’t move.

It simply stared down at us.

And somehow, even from that distance, I knew it was looking at me.

I looked back at my mother.

“Mom, I really, really need to go.”

She sighed.

“Fine.”

A saloon appeared.

It wasn’t built. It didn’t fall from the sky.

It simply became.

One second there was nothing. The next there stood an old wooden saloon
with swinging doors, dusty windows, and a sign hanging crooked above the
entrance.

My mother walked me to it.

She opened the door.

“It’s okay,” she said. “I’ll be right behind you.”

I looked up at her.

“You promise?”

She smiled.

“Yes.”

I stepped inside.

The door swung shut behind me.

I turned around.

She was gone.

I didn’t cry.

I had to go potty.

That was the important thing.

So I walked.

The saloon was enormous.

Men filled every table.

Young men.

Old men.

Men with gray beards.

Men with no hair.

Men who looked as though they’d been dead for years.

They drank and shouted and laughed at things I couldn’t understand.

I kept walking.

“Excuse me.”

Nobody listened.

I looked for a bathroom.

Then someone grabbed my arm.

I pulled away.

Another hand touched my shoulder.

Then another.

Men reached for me as I passed.

Hands grabbed at my clothes, my arms, my face.

Somewhere along the way, one of the hands came loose.

It remained attached to me.

I looked down at it.

A dead man’s hand was wrapped around my wrist.

I screamed.

Nobody noticed.

I kept walking.

Another hand caught me.

Then another.

Arms reached from the tables.

Hands fell from sleeves.

Some of them were old. Some were pale. Some were swollen. Some still
wore rings.

They grabbed me.

Touched me.

Held me.

And every time I tried to pull away, I became older.

I didn’t understand it at first.

I only noticed that my shoes felt smaller.

Then my legs grew.

My hands changed.

My voice became different.

The saloon grew darker.

The men grew louder.

I kept walking.

Six became seven.

Seven became ten.

Ten became fifteen.

Fifteen became twenty.

The bathroom was always just ahead.

I could see the door.

I could almost reach it.

Twenty-five.

Twenty-six.

Twenty-seven.

Twenty-eight.

I opened the bathroom door.

And suddenly there were no men.

No saloon.

No hands.

No noise.

Only me.

I stood in front of a mirror.

Except the person looking back wasn’t six.

She was twenty-eight.

I stared at her.

She stared back.

For a long time, neither of us moved.

Then she opened her mouth.

And screamed.

It wasn’t a scream of fear.

It was anger.

It was pain that had been trapped somewhere so deep that it had
forgotten how to become anything except rage.

She screamed until her reflection shook.

Then she raised her hands to her face.

And began tearing.

Skin came away beneath her fingernails.

She kept screaming.

She kept tearing.

Something moved behind her reflection.

Then something flew past the mirror.

A bat.

Then another.

Then hundreds.

Black wings filled the room.

Bugs poured from the corners.

The mirror disappeared beneath them.

The screaming continued.

I couldn’t tell anymore whether she was screaming at me or I was
screaming at her.

Then the darkness swallowed everything.

And somewhere, very far away, a giant baby was still watching.
