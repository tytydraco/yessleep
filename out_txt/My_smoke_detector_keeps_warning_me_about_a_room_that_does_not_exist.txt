I have two smoke detectors in my apartment. One is in the hallway
outside my bedroom and the other is near the kitchen. They’re the
talking kind, so instead of just screaming at you they announce what the
problem is and which room it’s in.

I didn’t install them. They were already here when I moved in last year,
and until Tuesday night I had never thought about them beyond changing
one battery.

At 2:41 a.m. the hallway detector woke me up by saying, very calmly,
“Smoke detected in nursery.”

There is no nursery.

My apartment has one bedroom, one bathroom, a kitchen, and a living
room. That’s it. No extra door, no sealed-off room, nothing that could
reasonably have been a nursery unless somebody once kept a baby in the
coat closet.

The alarm repeated itself twice. I checked the kitchen, smelled around
the outlets, looked behind the fridge, then opened my front door to see
if anything was burning in the hallway. Nothing.

I downloaded the detector app because I figured the room label had been
set wrong years ago.

The app showed three devices.

HALLWAY - ONLINE

KITCHEN - ONLINE

NURSERY - ONLINE

That definately got my attention.

I walked around holding my phone and tried the “test sound” button for
each detector. Hallway beeped above my head. Kitchen beeped near the
stove.

When I tested Nursery, I heard a chirp from inside the wall behind my
couch.

Not behind the wall as in another apartment. I mean inside it. Close
enough that I could put my ear against the drywall and hear the little
electronic click after the chirp.

I slept badly and dealt with it after work the next day.

There is an access panel in the utility closet for some pipes, so I
opened it and shined a flashlight sideways into the wall cavity. Mostly
studs, insulation, pipes. Normal stuff.

Then, maybe six feet to the left, I saw a green light blink.

I couldn’t reach it.

I considered cutting the wall open, but this is exactly the sort of
decision that gets expensive fast, so I didn’t. I reset the system
instead. The app made me rename both physical detectors. I called them
Hallway and Kitchen again.

Nursery disappeared.

For about six hours.

At 1:13 this morning, the hallway unit announced, “Carbon monoxide
detected in nursery.”

This time I didn’t screw around. I grabbed my keys, went outside, and
called the fire department.

They checked my apartment with handheld meters. No carbon monoxide. No
smoke. No heat in the walls. Nothing.

One firefighter heard the nursery alert himself. He asked where the
third detector was. I told him I didn’t know.

We used the test function again.

The chirp came from behind the couch.

He heard it too.

That was honestly the most reassuring part of this whole thing, because
until then I had started wondering if lack of sleep was making me
stupid.

He suggested getting the detector out of the wall because an old unit
might have been sealed in during renovation. Weird, but possible.

After they left, I moved the couch and finally cut a small square of
drywall where the chirp seemed loudest.

There’s no detector there.

There isn’t even insulation.

Behind that section of wall is a narrow empty space going straight down
farther than my flashlight reaches.

That makes no sense. I’m on the third floor.

I dropped a coin.

I counted for almost four seconds before I heard it hit something.

Then something down there coughed.

Not an echo. One dry cough, followed by a second quieter one.

I backed away so fast I tripped over the couch leg.

My phone buzzed.

The detector app had reopened by itself.

NURSERY - ONLINE

Underneath it, where the battery percentage normally appears, it said:

AIR QUALITY IMPROVING

I left. I’m at my brother’s place now with both of my actual smoke
detectors sitting on the kitchen table in front of me because I took
them with me.

About twenty minutes ago, the hallway detector spoke.

“Smoke detected in nursery.”

My brother heard it.

The app now shows four devices.

The new one is labeled BEDROOM.

It’s online too.
