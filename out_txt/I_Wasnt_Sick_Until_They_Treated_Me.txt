I wasn’t always like this. I wasn’t.

I still remember. I still remember how I was…

How I was before they admitted me.

Last Summer.

Last summer was the lowest point in my life.

With each stride, my foot slugged into the pavement. My body was worn to
tatters from walking nonstop. I had been going aimlessly since 6 AM.

Walking, and walking, and walking.

At some point in my delirium, I decided to bushwhack through the woods.
The thorns and stumps bashed my legs as I maneuvered through the dense
brush. To be honest, I had no idea what I was doing then and I still
don’t.

It couldn’t have been too long, maybe an hour or so, when I approached
the large PRIVATE PROPERTY sign. Again my delirium betrayed me as I made
my way onto the massive secluded property. For some reason, the rules
didn’t apply to me then.

That was my mistake.

I hadn’t slept in days. As I walked through the rugged property in a
daze, everything seemed to play tricks on my delirious mind. A statue, a
shadow… everything was foreign.

I knew I shouldn’t have been there, but I just kept on walking, and
walking, and walking…

Until I met the property owner.

A big and burly, grizzled man in a plaid jacket and trucker hat.

“Who the heck are you? This is private property man! Don’t you move a
muscle.”

I stared at him as my surroundings seemed to spin around me. He was
definitely imposing, but in my irrational state I only viewed him as an
obstacle in my aimless quest.

I attempted to bolt past him, but like I was drunk, I stumbled and
crumpled to the ground in front of him. He briskly grappled me and
pulled me up by my jacket, digging his finger into my chest.

“I SAID, DON’T MOVE!” His voice twanged, gruff and assertive.

Ten minutes later, I was in the back of a cop car. The police were going
to let me go with a trespass, but when I told them it was imperative I
kept walking or something horrible would happen they insisted I come
with them and everything would be alright.

Another ten minutes later, they were dragging me in cuffs into the
hospital. The nurses hands prodding and poking, several of them stared
at me like I was an alien under examination.

“There’s nothing wrong with me!” I shouted. “I just need to keep
walking!”

But like I said, I still remember… I remember how I was. How I was
before I went in. I wasn’t crazy… I just needed to walk. I can’t explain
why. It’s dangerous if I do… but it’s true.

One thing I do know is none of this would have happened, none of it
would have happened if it weren’t for them.

***

“Would you like a Jell-O cup with your oatmeal?” the chipper nurse asked
as she placed my cutlery.

“Sure…” I mumbled. “Look, I need to speak to a doctor, when exactly am I
getting out of here? I just need to walk. Since when is walking a crime?
There’s nothing illegal or wrong about walking, nurse, and I need to get
out of here as soon as possible, this is all just a mistake. I’m fine,
really I’m fine.” I had difficulty masking my agitation.

“Greg, please relax. We already told you, you need to see the doctor.
You’ll be released when you’re feeling better.”

This is when it all started…

Greg.

Greg is NOT my name. It never has been. “Uh, nurse, my name’s actually
Derek.”

“Okay, Greg, if you would like, I can call you Derek,” she said as if
she was placating me.

“No, nurse, my name has never been Greg. It’s just Derek. That’s what’s
on my ID.” I assumed that she was simply mistaking me with someone else.

“Okay, ‘Derek’, we both know that’s not true, but let’s not get upset
over this.”

She walked away but the entire ordeal left me feeling uncomfortable. I
retreated to my room to get away from the chaos of the ward. ‘I
shouldn’t be in here’ is what I kept telling myself.

At that moment, a nurse walked in with a machine. “Hi, Greg, we’re just
going to do a few tests and then you can have your space again. Okay?”

“My name… my name’s actually Derek,” I said, fighting a lump in my
throat.

“Okay, Greg, I don’t mind calling you Derek.” Her tone, patronizing and
fake.

“No… this is what I told the other nurse. My name is just Derek. There
must be some mix-up.” I was so confused at that point.

“D- Greg.” It was like she started calling me by my real name and
corrected herself.

“Let me just finish these tests and I’ll be on my way,” she said with a
leery smile.

“Fine… I mean, do we have to? I don’t really consent to this… but okay,
let’s just get it done.” I muttered defeatedly.

At first, it started simply enough, a typical medical examination, but
then things got weird.

“Stand up and do a spin for me,” she said as if there was nothing to it.
I was still delirious from walking and lack of sleep. I really couldn’t
process what was going on. So like a little ballerina doll I stood up
and did it, completely baffled by what was happening. Surely there was a
medical reason for it I thought.

Then she poked my inner elbow for over a minute, but I was still so
dazed and out of it all I could do was wonder if it was strange or if I
was really thinking clearly. After that she pulled out a little green
LED or something on stick and started waving it around my body.

I wish I could convey how delirious I was, but I really couldn’t tell
how bizarre it was at the time. What happened next is what ruined my
life. She began to attach strange metal rods all over my body while
calibrating her machine.

“Well…” she said coldly. “Are you ready for everything to change?”

My mouth fell agape, unsure if I had imagined it. “What?” I said in
shock.

“Are you okay?” she said, now in a completely friendly tone with a
smile.

“Uh, yeah, sure…” I am still 100% certain she said, “Are you ready for
everything to change?”

She then turned some dials on the machine and pressed a button and
suddenly I felt like I was getting pulled into a black hole. My mind
ached, each rod felt like it was generating a force field on my body.
What on earth was going on?

CLICK.

Like a switch, everything returned to normal. “Okay, we’re all done,
blood pressure is good, temperature is good… is there anything you want
to talk about. Any voices? Any thoughts of harming anyone?”

“What? No… like I said, I’m fine. I just need to get out of here as soon
as possible.”

“I’m sure you’ll be out of here in no time.” Her tone completely
unconvincing.

***

The days started to roll on. I mostly ignored everyone; the guy who
constantly said it was too hot or too cold, the lady who said the floor
was going to give in and felt unstable, the young guy who spent hours
and hours staring at himself flexing in the mirror, and everyone else
the nurses and doctors included.

The first night they gave me some medication. I wasn’t allowed to
refuse. They said it was a small dose of antipsychotic. I didn’t want to
take it, but I did. I knew I was fine and my only goal was to get out of
there. At least that was until the third night when the pills they gave
me were completely different, and then the fifth night they gave me a
shiny black pill. The seventh a purple one.

“Okay, these are not my pills,” I said adamantly… After arguing with
them for ten minutes and them calling the doctor in with faux concern, I
conceded I was ‘wrong’ and just took the pills. For as long as I was in
there the pills kept changing. One night they gave a cup with nothing in
it. I didn’t take the bait I just smiled and pretended to gulp them
down.

They still hadn’t stopped calling me Greg, and to top it off whatever
they had done with that machine, and the pills was really starting to
affect me. It was like I was absorbing every single problem my ward
mates told me about. The floors felt unstable, the temperature was
constantly too hot or too cold, and every time I passed a mirror I had
to fight not to stare at my reflection.

I wasn’t like this. I remember how I was and it wasn’t like this.

That night they blared music from the loud speakers in my room, bizarre
ambient jazz that just kept crescendoing and rising over and over again.
At first I thought I was going to lose it, but I stayed strong. When I
stepped out of my room and asked them to turn it down they feigned
ignorance. “Turn what down?” the nurse said coyly with her best
manufactured concern.

“Never mind…” I said, refusing to give in to whatever this was.

The next morning one of the other patients Ray, came up to me and
invited me to play table tennis on the veranda. I wasn’t really up for
it but he seemed genuine enough, so I let my guard down. I was paranoid
of what ailment he would describe that would start effecting me.

“They don’t want us to get out of here…” he said somberly.

“There’s something not right about this hospital,” he said under his
breath as the ball pinged back and forth over the net.

“I’ve been here for a month. Every day I tell them I’m fine… and every
day they say we’ll have to adjust the medication. They don’t listen… I’m
pretty sure that Belinda, that granny in room 4, has been here for over
a year. Let’s just say they’re not in the business of letting people
out.”

I just nodded as the ball bounced back and forth. “Yeah, well I’m
getting out of here. They keep calling me Greg, something isn’t right
here. Belinda’s right… the floors are off. It’s too hot… I started to
zone out muttering to myself. When I looked up I was just standing with
the paddle in my hand swinging into the air, Ray had already walked away
from the table and was staring at the street below.

A male nurse, Phillipe came over and reminded me of lunch, guiding me to
the table. All week, the nurses had been reporting I wasn’t eating.
Every day, I made sure to eat every last bite of my meatloaf and peas,
or whatever hospital fare they placed in front of me.

I never objected once.

***

After lunch, I asked to get my phone, Phillipe was working the counter.
For some reason he was different than the other nurses, he never called
me Greg, he just avoided calling me by name.

He knew.

When he handed me my phone he placed it slowly in my palm and looked at
me dead in the eyes. “Here you go.” He held my eyes for a moment longer
than usual.

I knew what he was trying to tell me.

“Thanks Phillipe,” I mustered and shuffled quickly off to my room.

I looked out of my window. There’s so much that happened at that time, I
can hardly recall it all. But they were there again. I noticed in my
first week the two men in black suits and sunglasses that gathered every
day after lunch and simply stared at the windows of the ward from across
the grounds.

I ignored them. I ignored everything that was happening in that
madhouse. I know you won’t believe me but I wasn’t crazy… at least not
when I went in. I just wanted to walk… but ever since the pills and that
machine nothing seemed the same. Ray was right, there was something
wrong with this place.

I swiped my phone’s pattern and to my surprise, the screen flashed
multiple times. I tried again to the same result. Someone had been in my
phone and changed my login. It had been set to SOS mode. I was certain
of it… was it Phillipe ? What was he was telling me?

I began to investigate my phone for any sign or clue. I knew that what
it was he was trying to send me a message. I checked my notes app… there
was a note I had never seen and absolutely didn’t place myself.

THE KILLING FLOORS: IDAHO HOSPITAL CARNAGE

It was a hyperlink and led to some overly editorialized conspiracy
website that guaranteed everything it was saying was 100% true and
factual. It detailed a gruesome account of a hospital in Idaho that was
found to be the grounds of some bizarre experiment. Mass murder
sanctioned by state officials and higher ups in government. I stared at
it in disbelief, a surge of panic overwhelmed me.

I HAD TO GET OUT.

Is that what this was? Everything lined up… the gaslighting, the
fabricated reasons for keeping me here. The rest of the patients… were
they all even real patients? Maybe they were really the doctors and
officials that would hunt us down on the night of the KILLING FLOOR.

I’m not crazy, I never was. This was all true… it sounds crazy but
that’s because IT IS CRAZY!

It was the perfect plan, and of course no one would believe an inpatient
of the mental ward. Take perfectly normal people in for something simple
as walking, and subject them to weeks of experiments. That was it… this
was all some sick experiment.

***

I couldn’t sleep that night and for the next couple of days it was
rough. I accused them of knowing what they were planning and ended up in
the isolation room with tranqs a couple of times. It was a
self-affirming cycle, they make me crazy and then they lock me up.

Ray had been nowhere to be seen, their story was that they let him out.

Sure.

He had probably been one of their agents sent to torment me, or maybe
they just ‘got rid of him’. I couldn’t stay here any longer. No matter
how much I told them I was fine and improving, they still looked at me
with concern.

“Greg, I just don’t know if you’re ready yet. Plus… those outbursts last
week. I suspect you are hiding your true thoughts and feelings from me.”

The doctor was so full of it.

After that I started planning how I would get out. I didn’t trust anyone
in there and I couldn’t tell anyone about what was going on. I started
to hear rumors of what night the ‘killing floor’ would take place.
Subtle whispers, sideways glances, I think they had started to suspect
that I knew. It was only a matter of time, I might have had only days
left before the carnage.

Phillipe came up to me after lunch with my phone in hand. “Hey…
remember, it’s your phone time.” I knew he was trying to tell me
something about what was going on. He had put that hyperlink in my
phone, and now he was giving me a heads-up.

“Thank you, Phillipe,” I said, hugging him briefly, he stepped back a
little but I’m sure he was just surprised.

That night I would break out. The part that confirms to me that Phillipe
was helping me was that every single door out of the ward was unlocked.
I simply hid and waited for the night guard to look the other way to
sneak past. It was like that the entire way out of the hospital I simply
walked right out.

***

I can’t remember how I made it home. I must have just walked the entire
30 miles. My feet were black in the morning. I was completely sore. I
locked my doors and looked out a crack in my blinds to see if they were
looking for me but no one ever did.

At first I laid low. I constantly expected to be apprehended by the
police. I wore disguises in public and frequently checked my
surroundings. There were never any police. However, they were there, the
men in the black suits. I started seeing them everywhere, in alleys or
on the bus. When I confronted them they simply ignored me or walked
away.

In saying all of that, what they did to me in there has still not gone
away. I am constantly afflicted by various ailments. I am shot with
vibrations while I sleep in the same locations as where the metal rods
were. My body temperature is constantly fluctuating and my feet are
constantly feeling the wobble of the ground below.

Whatever this is, it was created when they put that device on me.
Whatever pills they gave me did something to my chemistry. After the
first incident, things just got worse and worse. Eventually I was taken
back to another hospital after I went off on one of the men in suits
following me.

The second hospital said they had no record of me ever being an
inpatient anywhere. They said ‘I manufactured the whole thing’ and that
this was schizophrenia .

They told me they knew my name was Derek and that there was no record of
anything called the “killing floors” in Idaho. The hospitals used as
massacre grounds for mentally sound people, gaslit into thinking they
were crazy.

They said my fixation with walking was a way of self soothing the mental
disturbance I had been developing. I knew for certain none of this was
true.

This time while in the hospital I actually spoke to one of the men in
suits. His name was Gavin. He told me he was a CIA operative and they
had developed shapeshifting technology. He said the Killing Floors were
more of a game for them, to let of steam and test new technologies. The
funny thing is, he said that I could tell the world and they would never
believe me.

“You’re cooked, Greg. Your brain is a hard-boiled egg. Kapoof!” He
laughed crassly, a boisterous gut-bellowing laugh as he walked out of
the room.

I think after explaining everything that happened you will, in fact,
believe me.

The medications they give me at the second hospital did nothing to fix
the aliments from the first, but all of that is irrelevant. Thankfully I
do not suspect the second hospital had any connection to the first, and
is for all intents and purposes just a regular hospital. I was just
released last week.

I know that the truth is obvious to those who are reading this honestly.

That’s about all I have to say about it for now, be careful out there.

-Greg
