I don’t know if this is the right place to post this.
Honestly, I don’t even know why I’m posting it.
My psychiatrist told me I should start writing things down whenever they
happen. Dates, times, exactly what I saw. She says memory changes every
time we access it, especially memories connected to trauma.

I understand what she means.
The problem is, I don’t think it’s my memory that’s changing.
I think something is changing it for me.
I’ve seen the same man on screens since I was a child.
“Man” is the word I use because I don’t know what else to call him.
He’s too tall.
Too thin.
His arms hang almost to his knees, and his head looks like a skull with
a thin layer of skin stretched over it.
I’ve never seen him blink.
The strangest part is that he never looks out of place.
If I’m watching a cartoon, he’s animated.
If it’s an old black-and-white movie, he appears grainy and flickering.
In video games, he’s made of polygons.

Once, I saw him in a YouTube video filmed by some guy in Thailand. I
replayed it probably fifty times.
He was standing in the background between two tourists.

Nobody reacted to him.
People walked past him.
At one point, a woman walked directly in front of him.
When she passed, he was gone.
I went back three seconds.
He was there again.
Watching the camera.
Watching me.
When I was a kid, I thought that was all he did.
Watch.
Then came the night my mother drank too much.
I was nine.

My little brother and I were hiding in my bedroom because Mom was in one
of her moods.
If you grew up with an alcoholic parent, you probably know what I mean
by that.
You learn to recognize footsteps.
The sound of a cabinet closing.
How a glass is placed on a table.
You learn which version of your parent is coming down the hallway before
you ever see their face.
That night, I heard something shatter in the kitchen.
Then my mother screamed my name.
My brother started crying.
The television in my bedroom was on, but muted.
Some movie was playing.
I don’t remember which one.
I remember looking at the screen.
And seeing him.
The man.
He was standing inside the movie.
Not in the background this time.
There was a hallway behind the main character, and he was standing at
the end of it.
Completely still.
Looking directly at me.
Then he raised one long arm.
And pointed.
Not at the actress.
Not at the camera.
At my bedroom door.
Three seconds later, my mother knocked.
Three times.
I called the police that night.
My mother lost custody of us.
My brother never forgave me.
We barely speak now.
For years, I convinced myself the man was something my brain had
invented.
A coping mechanism.
A hallucination connected to trauma.
That explanation worked.
Mostly.
Until a few weeks ago.
My psychiatrist asked me something during one of our sessions.
“Has he ever spoken to you?”
I remember laughing.
The question sounded ridiculous.
I told her no.
He had never spoken.
He only watched.
That same night, I woke up at 3:17 a.m.
My television was on.
I know I had turned it off.
On the screen was my childhood kitchen.
Not a kitchen that looked similar.
My kitchen.
The yellow wallpaper.
The chipped cabinet above the sink.
The clock my mother bought at a yard sale.
Everything.
My mother was sitting at the table.
Drunk.
Exactly as she had been that night.
My brother was there.
Nine-year-old me was there too.
And the man was standing behind her.
Behind me.
He placed his hands on the little girl’s shoulders.
His fingers were so long they almost touched across her chest.
Then he leaned down.
And whispered something into her ear.
The little girl slowly turned toward the camera.
Toward me.
And said:
“You know what really happened.”
The television went black.
Since then, the screens have been showing me things I don’t remember.
My mother crying as the police took her away.
My brother screaming at me to tell them I had lied.
Me watching from the hallway.
Smiling.
That last part didn’t happen.
I know it didn’t.
I was terrified that night.
I was crying.
I remember crying.
But every time the scene plays again, something changes.
The first time, I was barely smiling.
The second time, I could see my teeth.
Last night, my smile stretched almost to my ears.
And the man was standing behind me.
His hands on my shoulders.
Yesterday afternoon, my brother sent me an old photograph.
No message.
Just the photo.
It was the two of us sitting on the carpet in our childhood living room.
I was maybe seven.
He was four.
Behind us was our old television.
It was turned off.
I almost deleted the picture.
Then I noticed something in the reflection of the black screen.
I zoomed in.
The man was there.
But he wasn’t looking at me.
He was kneeling beside my brother.
His mouth was pressed against my brother’s ear.
I called immediately.
No answer.
I called twelve times.
Nothing.
Then I received a text from his number.
Five words.
“Now I remember what you did.”
I tried calling again.
That’s when my television turned on by itself.
My brother appeared on the screen.
He was sitting in a dark room.
I couldn’t tell where.
He wasn’t moving.
“Daniel?”
I don’t know why I said his name out loud.
His head moved.
Very slowly.
He looked directly at me.
Then I noticed the man standing behind him.
For the first time in my life, the man wasn’t looking at me.
He was looking at my brother.
And he was smiling.
That was when I realized something.
I had never actually seen his mouth before.
I had always assumed he had one.
But he didn’t.
Not until then.
The smile opened slowly across his face, like a cut appearing in fabric.
It kept getting wider.
And wider.
Until his face split almost in half.
My brother started crying.
Then the screen went black.
I haven’t been able to reach him since.

I started writing this post because I needed a record somewhere other
than my own computer.

Something he couldn’t change.
Something other people could see.
But something happened while I was typing.
About twenty minutes ago, I reread the beginning of this post.
There was a paragraph I don’t remember writing.
It described the first time I saw him.
According to the paragraph, I was four years old.
I was watching cartoons.
The man appeared behind one of the characters.
And he waved at me.
I deleted the paragraph.
Because that never happened.
My first memory of him is from when I was six.
At least, I thought it was.
Then I checked the post again.
The paragraph was back.
Only one thing had changed.
This time, it said I waved back.
I deleted it again.
It hasn’t returned.
Yet.
There’s one more thing I need to mention.
If you’ve read this far, scroll back up.
Don’t worry.
There isn’t going to be some stupid picture hidden in the post.
I didn’t upload any images.
I just want you to notice something.
Every time I describe him, I give you a little more detail.
Tall.
Thin.
Long arms.
Skin stretched over a skull.
Eyes that never blink.
You probably have a pretty clear image of him in your head by now.
That’s what scares me.
Because when I was a child, I couldn’t describe his face.
I couldn’t remember it when I looked away from the screen.
Nobody could.
Not even my brother.
The only way to remember what he looked like was to keep looking at him.
But now I can see him perfectly when I close my eyes.
And if you can too,
I don’t think I’m the one who showed you.
