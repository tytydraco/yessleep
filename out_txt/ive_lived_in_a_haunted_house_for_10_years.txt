This thought came to me recently during a conversation I was having with
a friend. They had asked me whether I believed in ghosts, to which I
replied, “Well, yes, of course.” You see, in my understanding, ghosts
and apparitions have become such a mundane part of existence that it
rarely occurs to me that there are people who genuinely consider them
nothing more than works of fiction.

All this to say, for the past ten years, I have lived in the same house
alongside my family and other entities to which I have yet to assign a
name. We have coexisted for so long that they have long since ceased to
be something I fear. Instead, they have become more of an inconvenience,
an unwelcome presence that, despite our best efforts, we cannot seem to
make leave.

It had all begun when we first moved in. I was still a child and,
admittedly, highly impressionable. My mother, however, was certainly
not. Yet, much like myself, she had begun noticing a peculiar heaviness
in her chest whenever she entered certain parts of the house. Still, we
had only just moved in, and neither of us paid it much mind. Then, one
night, while I was cleaning the floors, I found myself momentarily
distracted by my dog. I had left the mop leaning against the wall, its
head resting in the bucket, before turning my back for what could not
have been more than a few moments.

When I turned around again, however, the mop was nowhere to be found.A
quick scan of the room revealed it lying on the floor in the opposite
corner, quite some distance from where I had left it. Now, don’t get me
wrong. I am perfectly aware that this could have been the work of a pet,
or perhaps some other entirely mundane occurrence. But what sent a chill
down my spine that night was not the fact that the mop had moved. It was
the silence.The complete and utter silence.I had been standing in the
very same room the entire time. Surely, I would have heard something.
The sound of the mop being lifted from its bucket, the handle striking
the floor, or even the faintest scrape as it travelled across the room.
Yet I had heard absolutely nothing.

The second most memorable incident from the year we moved in involved
the chandelier in our main hallway, which came crashing down from a
height of nearly three metres, missing my mother by mere centimetres.We
had spent the day carrying boxes and, exhausted, decided to rest in the
hallway. I remember my family sitting together, discussing everything
that still needed to be done. As we eventually got to our feet, I heard
the faint screeching of what sounded like screws coming loose.Unsure
whether I was imagining things, I called my mother over to listen. She
had barely stepped towards me when, directly behind her, the enormous
glass chandelier installed by the previous owners came crashing down,
shattering into countless pieces.Had I not called her over at that
precise moment, she would have been standing directly beneath it.

I won’t even bother recounting the countless occasions on which doors
would not merely close, but lock themselves, or the number of times we
would walk into the kitchen only to find every single cabinet standing
wide open.I should clarify that I am not, by nature, a believer in
ghosts or spirits, and even to this day, I cannot say that I necessarily
consider whatever inhabits this house to be either of those things. All
I know is that we have learned to coexist. Over the years, I have found
that the less attention I give these occurrences, the less frequently
they seem to happen. Fear, or even curiosity, has given way to absolute
boredom and indifference.

I have also experienced sleep paralysis for as long as I can remember,
and over time, I have learned to approach it with that same
indifference. Even in dreams, I have found that the best thing I can do
is pay whatever I perceive absolutely no mind.

A few years ago, however, curiosity finally got the better of me, and my
mother and I contacted a family friend who works in our city’s archives.
We wanted to learn more about the history of the house, as such
information isn’t exactly easy to find online in my country.When he
eventually got back to us, the information he provided was hardly what I
would call shocking. Apparently, some fifty years earlier, a major
earthquake had triggered a landslide that buried and killed a
considerable number of people in the area. When the city was eventually
rebuilt, our house was constructed on that very land.

I cannot claim to have a rational explanation for most of the things I
have grown up experiencing, and quite frankly, I have come to realise
that some things are better left unquestioned than endlessly dwelled
upon.
