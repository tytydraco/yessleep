Inside, something clicked; I knew then that I was finally more monster
than human, but I also knew that this was not always the way. Let me
spin you a tale, one that is very true but could be pushed off as a
terrible nightmare to anyone who wasn’t actively living in the
nightmare.

But this story is true, or at least as true as I can recall and it all
started, I think, when I was eight years old. I think it started with
the eel. Yes, yes, I know an eel isn’t a monster but they ARE slimy and
gross and for an eight year old? It’s a snake. It’s scary. You don’t
know anything other than “ohmigod, I caught a snake” when you’re out
fishing with your mother. Which I know is still supposed to be normal
but after I continue my story, you will understand why I think it
started there. The man with my mother? I could have met him before but
something about the interaction in my memory makes me feel like it was a
first meeting and my mother wanted it to go well. It did not.

She leaves us alone on the pier to retrieve something from the car.
Beer? Cigarettes? I can’t remember. It was the 90s. Either or both.
Maybe neither. Maybe it was a soda for me. I don’t know. But she leaves
us alone on the pier and for an eight year old? The unthinkable happens.
“I CAUGHT A FISH!!” I’m very excited until I realize that this is in
fact NOT a fish, and he, it, the thing on the line was trying to crawl
its way up the line. To me. I didn’t know electric eels were a thing
that existed, I didn’t even know eels in general existed. The thing on
the line proved to be even grosser than a snake, he was slimy and had
this muscular build that I was pretty sure snakes couldn’t replicate,
and man, was it deceptive. It wasn’t a big thing, but he started
thrashing on the line making it really hard for me, let alone any eight
year old, to hold on to the line. So I start screaming bloody murder
“SNAKE!! SNAKE!!” As I drop the fishing pole in the water. Her friend,
the man, practically dove in to save his fishing rod and here comes my
mom ‘do de do de do’ing’ her butt back over to us as fast as he can but
I say ‘do de do de do’ because it felt like slow motion for me. I was
crying. I was scared. And that was just the beginning.

Was the eel part of it? Maybe. Maybe not.

Many years pass by with nothing but the ordinary. Until the day I met
him. We were young, too young. But I knew just from the first look that
this boy was going to mess my life up. At fourteen, that’s what you
imagine, isn’t it? You fall in love with a look and plan out your entire
life. Dating, homecoming, prom, college, getting married, having
children. You plan it all out just from a look. From one single look
when you were still a girl.

You spend many years together, something always gnawing in the back of
your mind. Something in your brain always seems to … itch. It’s deep in
there so you can’t physically scratch it. That’s when the moths started
to show up. Everywhere you would go there would be a moth. Giant ones,
little ones, beautiful ones, ones you couldn’t bear to look at. Tiny
ones you couldn’t even see. The first time you saw one, you were
delighted. As time went on, you started feeling a little agitated. Why
are they following you? What do they want?

That night you awoke to a bzzt’ing that sounded like it was inside of
your brain. It might as well have been. The sound was loud, so loud, and
all you could do was panic. Your husband shakes the sleep off of him as
he hears your screams. “Hey! Hey! What’s wrong?” and you can’t
articulate anything. You’re panicking so much. He realizes that
SOMETHING is wrong with your ear because of your posture or something
else that only he can perceive as the person who has been by your side
for so long. “Babe, what’s wrong with your ear?” and he helps you
destroy the moth inside. You try to flush the ear with water, the sound
just getting louder in your ear. You try baby oil, guessing that it
worked because eventually there is no sound any more and you’ve washed
all the oil from your ear. The day has been saved. But this happens
again. Almost exactly the same way, multiple times over the years. It
always starts with a moth or a gnat getting in your ear and then you
panicking and he has to save the day. It isn’t a fun dance, but at least
you have a system now.

Many moons later, life gets really bad for you and you can’t keep up
with anything anymore. You’re working more than ever but making less
money than ever and you’re living in the worst situation of your life.
You want to get out but you can’t. You plead for help but are at a total
loss when your pleas are ignored. But, the bugs come. They come, and
they stay. They overtake your life in a way that you wish you never had
to go home. You wish that you’d just die and that would be the end of
it. But you live in a filth that people shouldn’t live in, and it just
gets worse and worse and worse until the day you realize you haven’t
slept in months. You haven’t slept in months because the bugs just kept
coming. There were roaches and ants and they overtook literally
everything and you couldn’t sleep because what if they were on you? How
could they NOT be on you? How could you have ever let it get this bad?
You’re a horrible, disgusting, vile creature and you deserved the filth
and the shame that went along with it.

You work yourself to the bone in a job that you hate, and that perpetual
itch in the back of your head just… gets… worse. It’s almost not even an
itch anymore but more of a … oh, the buzzing is back. It’s more of a
bzzt now, but constant and at the base of your skull, just under your
medulla. But it still itches. All the time.

You find an apartment, you find a way out. You find a way out! You find
a way to get some sleep, a little solace, a little quiet. Maybe the itch
will go away. Maybe the bugs won’t come this time. Maybe just maybe
you’ll finally be free.

Eventually you move on from the job that you hate, from the environment
that you can’t stand, the one that just bleeds you for everything that
you are. The one that you sold your soul to and they just kept coming
back for any tiny bit they might have missed. You spent four years in
that hell, praying to a god that you don’t believe in that things would
change and that it would get better but it never did and in the back of
your mind, all you could do was surrender to the itch. But you couldn’t
surrender to the itch.

Your new job was just as frustrating, if not, worse. You made a
unilateral change(supervision to supervision instead of an upwards
motion) and everyone questioned it. Couldn’t accept it. But they didn’t
have the itch!!! You do. You did. You still do. It’s there, just under
the medulla. Can’t you feel it? I can. You can. We all can. I’m
rambling. You’re rambling. We are all rambling.

The itch overtook everything. You couldn’t go to work without spending
the majority of your day in your office, sniveling like a child, crying
like a literal baby, the pain in your head just building and building
until the day you saw him. There was a roach in the restaurant.
Everywhere you went, there it was. You didn’t brother trying to kill
him, you felt like he meant no real harm. At least, to you. And he
didn’t. But he was there to stand sentinel, watching you go about your
day, gaining as much knowledge on your movements as possible. For what?
He would answer none of your questions “Why are you following me?!”
“What do you want?” Well, he couldn’t answer. He was a bug. And by now,
you were slowly descending into the chaos of the itch.

By this point, the itch was no longer loud or painful, even. You had
grown so accustomed to both the sound and the pain, that it barely
registered anymore. You were secretly putting in job applications, it
had been six months, you were ready to run again. You knew the pattern
even if no one else did. You could be happy at a new job for six months
before one tiny inconvenience made you say the words “I want to blow my
brains out” and then you realize, once again, you hate your job. It
never seemed to fail. Even the jobs that you held for longer than six
months(all of them actually), you just started looking for another job
around the six month mark. It was always six month increments. And if
you couldn’t find a job, you’d just repeat the application process every
six months until something bit. You wanted to grow in your profession,
but the only move was up, and in the company you worked for, that was
never going to happen. You needed more money. Period. And that’s when
you noticed the bzzting at the base of your skull getting a little loud
again. Not unbearable, but there.

And then it happened. You stumbled upon the job of a lifetime. You make
more money with less hours most of the time. And the best part is that
you don’t have to be in charge anymore. You can let someone else handle
all the problems. But you, i, me, we, who are we? You’re lonely, a
little lost and you have the itch.

One day, coming home from work, a gnat flew up your nose. For the tenth
time in your life, probably. That’s not an exaggeration, probably not
even close to hyperbole. In fact, ten might be a generous number. It was
probably more. The gnats treated your nose the way the moths treated
your ears, as their domain. And they weren’t planning on stopping until
you succumbed to the itch.

One morning, you went in to work with the intention to make yourself a
little more presentable than usual. It had been ages since you wore any
makeup of any kind, you just hadn’t been feeling it, the itch was just
there and so you went and threw on your normal simple go-to, except you
made a point to find the ONE lipstick that would stand out. And when you
open the lipstick, out crawls a moth from your makeup bag, straight up
your arm and into your ear, faster than you can swat at it. Another moth
crawls out and darts up your arms. You thrash, as many more moths crawl
up your body and into every orifice. You choke, you can feel them, oh
god. You can feel them, they can feel you. We can feel you. Oh god, they
need you, oh god they need me. What is happening?! Eventually there are
no more moths, and you lay in a heap on the floor. Mangled, a little
bloody, broken, and bruised. You are jerked forward to stand at the
mirror. You watch in horror, as your body slowly heals, and you can feel
every bone snapping back into place. Your neck POPS, sounding like a gun
going off in your ear, and you smile in the mirror, before shrieking in
excruciating pain. You feel SOMETHING on your back. It hurts. Oh god it
hurts!! You curl into the fetal position before throwing your head back,
screaming in pain, half wondering how no one can hear you, why no one
has come to help. But you notice something strange happening to your
back, the skin starts to split open and beautiful, multi-colored moth
wings sprout from the splits in my skin. You pass out.

An eternity later, you wake up in your bed at home, wondering if it was
all a dream until you try to turn on your side, your wings getting in
the way.
