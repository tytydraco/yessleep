…and I should have acted. Now, the strangest things are happening to me.
I feel a sense of something like dread, but it’s hard to put into words.
I think she’s trying to warn me… or, maybe she’s angry with me… 

Tat and I had been working together remotely for fourteen years. That’s
a long time! I spoke with her almost every day via chat or video. We
texted each other constantly. Gossip, memes, pictures, and videos of new
shoes we bought, outfit checks, food, concerts.

We lived on opposite sides of the country and had never met in person,
but that does not mean we weren’t friends. We shared a lot with each
other over the years about our struggles with depression, anxiety, and
other deeply personal things. I truly loved her, and she was a fixture
in my life.

I received daily videos of my Godson, her French Bulldog, Damien. I got
pics the day she picked him up and brought him home as a puppy. I was
supposed to take him if anything happened to her, and in hindsight, this
is where the foreboding began.

She would give me her flight information when she travelled so I could
check whether she made it to her next destination without the plane
crashing. We shared the kind of crippling anxiety that only friends
could understand and laugh about. Anyone else would know we were nuts;
but maybe she wasn’t actually nuts and knew her time was up soon.

“I was up all night with the worst stomach and back pain I think I’ve
ever experienced. I seriously thought I was dying.” she told me one
morning.

“Well, I don’t like that.” I responded.

We discussed her symptoms and where the pain was located, then used
Dr. Web Browser to try to diagnose it. It could have been anything from
gas to appendicitis.

“So, you need to go to the doctor, Tat.”

“I will if it comes back. I feel better, it’s just still kind of sore.”

We laughed about how neither of us will go near a doctor unless our arm
is severed; and even then, “’Tis but a flesh-wound!”

A few weeks later it happened again. She had an appointment already
scheduled with her orthopedist about an ongoing back problem that she
thought was related to the new pain she was experiencing.

He wanted her to have an MRI but was told she’d have to take her hair
extensions out. We laughed about the exchange.

“Are you kidding me? Do you know how much these cost? I have to go to
Vegas to get these done!”

As time went on, the pain kept coming intermittently, and I begged her
to go to the hospital. It was getting ridiculous; something was
obviously wrong. She was now vomiting at times from the intensity of it.

I started to get on her daily about it.

I’d guilt trip her on the phone; “But you PROMISED you would go to the
hospital if it happened again.”

I’d beg by text; “Please go get your hair re-done so you can get that
MRI.”

I got frustrated in the chat and we argued about it. 

“What if your pancreas is infected and you go septic? WHY are you just
suffering like this instead of getting some answers that might help?”

“Wow, it’s like you’re more worried about it than I am! I’m fine!” Tat
responded.

“So, what, it went away?”

“No… but it does eventually go away.” she said.

“So, you just stopped telling me about it, so you didn’t have to listen
to me nag?”

“Pretty much.”

We didn’t speak for the rest of that day.

When it was time for her trip to “Sin City” to get her hair re-done, I
asked whether she was going to get the kind of extensions that are safe
for an MRI.

“Yesss MOM!”

We were the same age, but yes, I tend to nag at those I care about.

Looking back at the impending doom I now see, we had a conversation
about how anyone would know whether either of us came into work. We both
worked pretty independently, without talking to our supervisors for days
at a time, and spent most of our time at home, alone.

“I could be dead for days before anyone finds out my work isn’t getting
done.” she said.

So, we started checking in with each other every morning, even if it was
a busy day. Sometimes, we’d just say, “I see you!”

On this Thursday morning, she was not logged in. I texted her asking
where she was with no response.

Tatiana would never miss work on a Thursday. It was a crucial day for
her weekly duties, and she took her job wayyyy too seriously.

“Maybe she really doesn’t feel well. I’ll let her sleep” I told myself.

An hour later, I texted her again because I knew something was wrong. I
was just about to call her when a department email came through saying
that she had called off for the day.

“Okay, so why isn’t she answering me? Am I blowing this out of
proportion?”

I decided to let her sleep, but I felt uneasy.

She got into an argument with our department manager the previous day. I
can understand her frustration because Boss Lady seems to find something
to be incredibly petty about every few months, for no discernable
reason.

I had Tat on the phone that day, trying to talk her down from quitting.
Her rage was valid, but I sensed something was making her overreact just
a bit. I could hear it in her voice. She seemed too upset over the usual
nonsense that we’d been dealing with for years, but everyone has their
breaking point.

“Maybe she really is quitting and doesn’t want to deal with me begging
her not to” I thought.

“Hey, can you just let me know you’re okay? I know you’d never miss work
on a Thursday. What is going on???” I texted her.

“Tat, I’m freaking out. Please talk to me.”

No response.

I finally messaged her supervisor, which is a breach of an unspoken
contract to never discuss each other’s business with anyone. Ever.

“I know that you can’t tell me why Tat called in today, but I need to
know that she’s physically okay. She would never call out on a Thursday,
and I feel like something is wrong.”

Her supervisor responded that Tat sent her an email saying she was up
all night with a stomachache, and that she didn’t think it was a big
deal.

“I expect her in tomorrow.” she typed.

“Just heard you were up all night with a stomachache, Tat, what’s up?” I
texted.

I thought about calling 911. I thought about threatening to call 911.

So many thoughts…

“Maybe that’s why she isn’t answering. You’re being dramatic. Now she’s
mad that you talked to her supervisor.”

I definitely should have acted on my instincts…
