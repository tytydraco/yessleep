Yeah, the title’s fucked, but it is what it is, and I’d be as confused
as anyone if I wasn’t so scared. An old friend of mine introduced me to
you guys, he was a big fan, and I’m really hoping one of you paranormal
loving folk graces me with an explanation. I hope you do before the damn
thing chokes me in my sleep.

That friend I mentioned, I would say this deal started after his death.
Not that the pest showed up the same night, it did two days later, but
you’ll see what I mean. Pete had killed himself one night after class.
His name wasn’t actually Peter, but the guy looked just like Peter Pan,
scrawny ginger kid that looked lighter than a feather, and he had the
nickname since long before I met him. He was nice, one of those people
who seem shy between extroverts and the life of the party among other
geeks. I met him on campus, he bummed a cigarette from me that was
obviously the first one he ever touched, coughed up a storm while
introducing himself, and then just sort of tagged along behind me. I
never questioned it, it felt natural that we were buds. It was that easy
and he was that likeable. I had no clue he wasn’t doing ok, honest. I
could only tell afterwards, looking back.

And that’s what I kept doing, starting at his funeral. Pete wasn’t
really the religious type, so the whole thing took place at his family’s
house. Very informal. I was in his room, now forever a sanctuary to his
memory, noticing all kinds of trinkets I knew nothing about. And I
started feeling bad about it, for never really asking him anything about
himself, for taking him for granted, I guess. And I never stopped
ruminating from there.

So, two days after the funeral. I walk up the stairs in my condo up to
my shitty apartment on the third floor. The elevator was busted again.
And I almost step in this mess of gray mud. It was a blob the size of a
cat, made of what looked like wet cement, with sand-like dry granules
over some of its surface, and seemed to have been dropped in a pile on
the steps. After a few seconds of staring at it, I gagged suddenly at
the smell of the thing, like a mix of a sharpie stuck in my nostrils and
rotten eggs. I walked around it and thought no more of it, asking myself
who the hell could have left whatever that was on the floor. Got home,
sat on the sofa and lit a cig. Before I was so disgustingly interrupted,
I was thinking about my last chat with Pete.

I had known him for a couple of years, by then. Not one weekend without
asking me if I had plans or inviting me to meet his nerd friends.
Nothing wrong with that, but for the life of me I could never interpret
a fucking hobbit and listen to theater kids play pretend around a table.
I had invited him to hang out with my friends, yeah, but we already got
plenty of time together on campus and the few times he did tag along he
never spoke to anyone but me, and would sulk if I did anything else. My
other friends weren’t to appreciative, to say the least. So I mostly met
Pete one on one. The last day I saw him he ran up to me after class and
asked to see me later that night. I told him that I had other plans, but
he insisted and kept saying it was important. I had been getting
overwhelmed by his constant need of attention, and I just snapped at
him, told him that my life doesn’t revolve around him. Pete seemed hurt,
and something in his look told me that he hated my guts. Before I could
process that, he smiled like nothing had happened and said it was fine,
that he was sorry to have bothered me. Then he turned around and went
home to swallow a dozen pills too many. I never heard what it was he
needed to tell me about.

So I was there, in my cramped living room and kitchen, considering how
shitty of a friend I am. Then I heard a clear snap from my front door. I
barely looked over the sofa’s back and yelled out a Who’s there.
Nothing. Then a scraping sound and another snap. And I smell putrid eggs
again. I stood up and as I looked down at the feet of the door I see the
goddamn puddle of filth I met on the stairs seeping through its bottom.
Something thin bent under the surface of the goop and then a third snap
sounded, a tiny bone split in two as it moved. The thing has bones
inside of it, I realized in bewilderment, and I ran to get a broom. I
don’t know why that was the first thing that came to mind. Just got a
broom and started trying to jam it back out the crack. I pushed the
broom into the goop and I heard this awful sizzle. The thing was giving
off this reddish brown smoke everywhere, filling the house of rot and
the stench of wet rust. But the impact must have spooked it: it scurried
back and disappeared underneath the frame. I was left shocked, covering
my nose with my shirt, the squelching sounds of that nightmare rapidly
disappearing far outside. What the fuck do I do, I asked myself. I
figured nobody would believe what happened, at least nobody who could do
anything about it, and I was still trying to fix up the image of some
rancid, rabid vermin in my mind to not have to deal with whatever that
globule was. So I called my landlord and complained about the “huge rat”
I had to deal with on the stairs. Let pest control worry about the
wretched thing instead.

The rest of the night went as smoothly as it could have, with the help
of a generous serving of cheap beer. So did the next morning, hangover
aside. The scheduling for disinfestation later down the month somewhat
calmed my nerves. In retrospect I just wanted an excuse to not worry
about the little monster. I realized I hadn’t thought about Pete all day
as I went down the building for my afternoon lessons. I almost felt
guilty about it. But again, getting from my door to the street below was
now way more pressing than any amount of self-reflection. I quickly got
to the elevator and, miracle, it was back in order. I got inside as fast
as I could, got out at the ground floor and dashed through the gate
door. No sign of “the pest”. I got to my class and tried my best to
listen to my professor.

Eventually the lesson was over, and I went out. I raised my eyes from
the cigarette I was lighting and, across the smoke I fucking see it.
It’s across the street, and it’s bigger than last time. It looks
thicker, like the liquid it was made of congealed into some slab of
spongy, tumorous growths. It must have been 3 feet tall, and it was just
standing there, while I was frozen in horror. I caught myself thinking
it kinda looked like a paper mache tombstone. Then the bastard starts
dragging itself, like it’s slowly being pushed towards me. And somehow I
felt like it had this incredible malice, this great desire to cause me
harm. Nobody else around me reacted to it, people somehow seemed to just
naturally swerve around it as they walked, more observant of my
reactions than anything. I came to this realization in retrospect,
obviously. In that moment I was too busy booking it as far as I could.

I ran for what felt like a full hour, my chest booming, almost crashing
against some pedestrians more than once. At a certain point I think I
almost ran under a motorcycle, but I didn’t stop at the shriek of the
breaks, so I guess that will remain a maybe. I eventually was so far
that I felt comfortable enough to let myself collapse, wheezing and in
the middle of a coughing fit. Damn smoke, I thought.

“Hey, are you alright?”

The question sounded so stupid given what I knew that it almost took me
out of my exhaustion in disbelief. I looked up to meet the eyes of a
girl my age, alternative type. I recognized her, she was one of Pete’s
Dnd friends (Mary? Molly?), I had seen her at the funeral. She
recognized me too, and her concern immediately changed to a mix of shock
and poorly veiled displeasure.

“You’re Anthony, right? Peter’s classmate?”

“Sure am”, I choked out, still sitting on the floor. I considered
telling her that I was being followed, but I had lost the pest for a
while now, and I didn’t want to sound even crazier than I already felt.
“I’m just… I have a lot of shit on my mind lately. Trying to get my mind
off things.”

She seemed to buy it, looking uncomfortable but giving me a sympathetic
smile. We stayed like that for a while, as I tried to breathe, until she
inhaled sharply and blurted out something else.

“Look, I wouldn’t have asked under other circumstances, but I may not
get another chance, so… Do you know why Pete did what he did? Did
anything happen before he…”

“Are you asking me if I know why he committed suicide? Because I know
just as much as anyone else.”

She hesitated, then spoke again, “I thought since he was so close to
you, you could have known what it meant. You weren’t just anyone to him,
you know?”

Since he was so close to you. Those words felt so weird. I didn’t know
what to make of them, what to make of any of this. “Look, Pete was a
good friend and I miss him plenty, but I have no idea what you are
trying to imply here” I said as I stood up. I turned to face her and I
could almost see her get pale even behind the goth makeup.

“Anthony, you know he loved you, right? He never stopped talking about
you, he kept telling us how special you were…”

I had zoned out, at that point. My mind went back to the trinkets I felt
ashamed not recognizing, to the request to talk on that last evening, to
his attempts to include me in his life or to fit into mine. “I’m sorry,
Em. I really can’t help you. See you around.” I made my way back home,
pretending to jog until I turned the corner, feeling her eyes on my
back.

I got to my building and dragged myself to my apartment. I didn’t know
what to think, I almost refused to let myself think. Whatever was going
on, whatever Pete felt, he had brought it to the grave, his secret to
keep. I thought of that look in his eyes after I told him off, that
night. I thought of the grudge I could read all over him. I wondered if
it made any sense to think about what I would have done if I knew what I
had learnt, and I didn’t find an answer. I chose to research my
grotesque stalker instead. Google offered no clarification on its
nature, no matter the details I used to describe it. Just piles of ghost
stories. Still drained from my running, from the panic, from the
confusion, I started getting dizzy, and my eyes grew heavy. Before I
could convince myself to hold a crumb of survival instinct more, I was
asleep in bed, laptop on my chest.

I woke up with the horrid feeling of not being alone. How could I be so
stupid? I jumped up from my bed, ignoring the laptop falling off of me
to the floor, and stared at the bedroom door. I ignored my terror and
reached for the doorknob, then threw the door wide open. The pest was in
the middle of the room, and again it had grown. It looked even more like
an upright carved stone, a mummy of dry concrete from which jutted out a
mess of tangled, curved pieces of bone, a ribcage pushing out of a
filthy plaster cast. It was oozing a brown filth all over the floor,
filling everything with its carcass smell. I took it all in in the span
of a second, and I swallowed a scream of primal fear as the notion of
that blasphemy now having a face gutted me. On top of it sat what could
only be interpreted as a head, an irregular hole crudely punched nowhere
near its center, a gleam coming from deep inside it. It gleamed of that
fucking grudge. There was an eye in there, the eye of someone who cared
for me and whom I had never truly seen. And now all that I have left of
him is this hateful thing, forever behind me, in my house and out,
filling all air I breathe with poison and scorn. Clumps of red hair
still peeking through its pachyderm skin.

This is what I’m left with. I’ve had people visit me, step in the same
room as my pest and never see him, never smell his smoke or hear his
death rattles. And he keeps changing. He keeps moving more erratically
and aggressively, and since he has gained the ability to produce noise
he never stops tearing his cavernous throat just to cry madly at me. I’m
afraid I see the vague shape of arms pressing against the surface of his
mummified form, and I know that if it will ever have hands, he will
strangle me with them. I see it in his eye as it stands over my bed each
night. I can’t survive anymore with this rock shadow persecuting me, so
please, if you know what it could be, how I could get rid of it, tell me
before it’s too late. Before the guilt wears me down too much.
