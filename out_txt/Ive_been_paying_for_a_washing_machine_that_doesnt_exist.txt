It started with $2.75.

That is an annoying amount of money to lose. Not enough to ruin your
day, but just enough that you spend the next twenty minutes trying to
figure out where it went.

My apartment building has a laundry room in the basement. Two washing
machines, two dryers, and an app that makes you add money in $10
increments because apparently quarters were too convenient.

I live in 1B, directly above the laundry room.

About six weeks ago, I noticed a charge in the app’s transaction
history.

WASHER 0 — SANITIZE — $2.75

Thursday, 2:17 a.m.

I’d been asleep at 2:17. I also knew there wasn’t a Washer 0. The
washers downstairs are numbered 1 and 2. The dryers are 3 and 4.

I assumed someone had copied the QR code from one of the machines and
was charging laundry to my account somehow.

I changed my password and forgot about it.

The following Thursday, another $2.75 disappeared.

Same machine. Same cycle. Same time.

I emailed the laundry company. Their first reply explained how to reset
my password, which was impressive considering I’d already told them I’d
done that.

Their second reply said Washer 0 wasn’t registered to my building.

I sent them screenshots.

They stopped replying.

By the fourth week, I was more interested in figuring it out than
getting my eleven dollars back.

I mentioned it to our property manager, Denise. She looked at the
screenshots, went downstairs with me, and counted the machines as if I
might have overlooked one.

“There are four,” she said.

“I know.”

“Well, there’s your answer.”

I asked if there had ever been another machine.

“No. Why would they start numbering at zero?”

Fair point.

That Thursday I stayed awake.

At 2:10, I went down with my phone, a folding chair, and a cup of
coffee.

The laundry room is tiny. Two washers along the back wall, dryers
opposite, utility sink in the corner. There’s an old floor drain that
smells terrible whenever it rains.

Both washers were empty. I opened them to check.

At exactly 2:17, my phone vibrated.

WASHER 0 — CYCLE STARTED

Then I heard it.

Not from either washer.

From behind the wall.

A low electric hum, followed by that familiar sound of water entering a
metal drum.

I put my hand against the painted concrete between the washers.

It was vibrating.

I know how this sounds. I spent a full minute convincing myself it was a
pipe or some other appliance in the building.

Then the drum started turning.

There was something inside it.

You know that heavy, uneven thumping a washer makes when you’ve put a
blanket in and it keeps bunching up on one side?

It sounded like that.

Except every few rotations, I heard a separate sound.

Three quick taps.

Like someone knocking on the inside of the drum.

I backed away and called Denise.

She didn’t answer. It was nearly three in the morning, so I couldn’t
exactly blame her.

The machine ran for forty-three minutes.

At 3:00, my phone displayed CYCLE COMPLETE.

The vibration stopped immediately.

I stood in the laundry room for another ten minutes, feeling like an
idiot, then went upstairs.

I didn’t sleep much.

In the morning, I found a damp patch on my bedroom carpet.

It was next to the closet, almost directly above the spot where I’d
heard the machine running.

There was no leak in the ceiling. No dripping pipes. The wall was dry.

But the carpet smelled strongly of laundry detergent.

I showed Denise.

She had the maintenance guy check my bathroom plumbing and the pipes
downstairs. He found nothing leaking. When I asked him about the noise
in the wall, he told me the pipes behind the washers had been capped off
when the laundry room was renovated.

“There’s nothing back there that should make noise,” he said.

I didn’t find that reassuring.

The next Thursday, I went downstairs again.

At 2:17, the charge appeared.

The humming started.

This time I brought a flashlight.

There’s a narrow gap where one of the water pipes enters the wall. I
crouched beside Washer 2 and pointed the light inside.

I couldn’t see much. Just old plaster, dust, and what looked like a dark
space beyond the pipe.

Then something moved.

A piece of wet fabric slowly pushed through the gap.

At first I thought it was insulation.

I caught the end with two fingers and pulled.

It came out surprisingly easily.

It was a pillowcase.

Black cotton, absolutely soaked, with a crooked line of orange stitching
along one corner.

I know that stitching.

Two months ago, I tore a pillowcase trying to pull it off a pillow that
was too big for it. I repaired the seam with the only thread I had,
which happened to be bright orange.

I ran upstairs.

My pillowcase was still on my bed.

The repair was still there.

I took it off and put both pillowcases on the kitchen table.

They were identical, right down to a tiny bleach spot near the opening.

There was one difference.

The orange stitches on the wet one were tighter and more even than mine.

I didn’t know what to do with that information.

I put the wet pillowcase in a garbage bag and left it on my balcony.

The next morning, the bag was empty.

It hadn’t been torn open. The knot I’d tied in the top was still there.

The pillowcase was just gone.

That’s when I decided I was done investigating.

I emailed the laundry company again, demanded a refund for every
unauthorized transaction, and asked them to remove my payment method.

Yesterday afternoon they finally responded.

They apologized for the inconvenience and refunded $13.75 to my account.

They also disabled automatic payments.

I was relieved.

Until last night.

At 2:17, I heard the washer start.

I was in bed.

I could feel the vibration through the floor, the same uneven rotation,
the same heavy thumping.

A few seconds later, my phone lit up.

WASHER 0 — PAYMENT DECLINED

The sound beneath me changed.

The motor slowed, made a horrible grinding noise, and stopped.

For a few seconds everything was silent.

Then came three knocks.

Not from the basement.

From inside my bedroom wall.

I grabbed my phone and ran out into the hallway.

Behind me, something hit the wall hard enough to knock a framed
photograph off my dresser.

I made it into the stairwell before I heard another impact.

Then another.

Whatever was making that noise wasn’t moving like a person walking. It
sounded as though someone was dragging a heavy, wet mattress along the
inside of the walls, stopping occasionally to throw it against the
plaster.

I called Denise.

She answered on the fourth ring. I must have sounded bad because she
stopped asking questions and told me to come to her apartment.

She lives on the third floor.

I was halfway up the stairs when my phone vibrated again.

WASHER 0 — CYCLE CANCELLED

Underneath that was a notification I’d never seen in the laundry app
before.

UNCLAIMED LOAD WILL BE RETURNED TO ORIGINAL LOCATION.

I stayed at Denise’s last night.

She heard the banging too. She called the building’s emergency
maintenance number and had them come out.

They couldn’t find anything.

My apartment was locked when they checked it this morning. No broken
windows, no water damage, no signs of anyone entering.

But they said the bedroom carpet was wet.

Denise offered to let me stay another night. I’ve been sitting at her
kitchen table for most of the afternoon, trying to work out what to do.

About an hour ago, the laundry app sent another notification.

WASHER 0 — RETURN COMPLETE

LOCATION: 1B

I checked my account.

The refund has been reversed.

I’m apparently $13.75 in credit again.

And the app is showing one new machine available in my building.

Washer 0.

There’s a little green button beside it that says START NEW CYCLE.

I haven’t pressed it.

A few minutes ago, Denise went downstairs to collect a package.

She just called me from the lobby.

She says there’s a trail of water leading out of my apartment.

My front door is still locked.

The water is coming from underneath it.

And she wants to know why someone has left a wet black pillowcase on the
floor outside.
