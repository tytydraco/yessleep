I have a particular fascination with watching people.
When Friday comes rolling around, I can hardly wait to start my hobby.
There’s something about watching strangers go about their lives without
knowing someone is observing them. I guess it makes me feel less alone.
I get to experience other people’s lives without having to actually be
part of them.
Fall is my favorite time of year. Night comes much quicker, and the
darkness makes it easier to move around unnoticed.

There’s an apartment building I visit almost every Friday. I’ve
memorized the fire escapes and know which windows offer the best views.
I’ve watched dozens of people over the months, but there’s one woman in
particular who has caught my attention.
She’s a brunette, probably in her late twenties. I don’t know her name,
but I’ve watched her enough to recognize her routine. Sometimes she
reads on the couch, sometimes she cooks dinner, and occasionally she
just sits by the window looking outside.
I like to imagine what her life is like. Maybe in another world, where I
wasn’t such an introvert, we could have met. Maybe she’d have liked me.
Last Friday, I made my usual trip to the building. I climbed the fire
escape and noticed her apartment light was on. I approached the window,
expecting to see her sitting on the couch.
Something was wrong.
There was a dark red trail leading from the kitchen into the living
room. At first, I thought someone had spilled something, but as I looked
closer, I realized it was blood.

The television was playing in the background. I couldn’t hear anyone
screaming or moving.
I hesitated. I have one rule: never enter someone’s apartment. Watching
is one thing, but crossing that boundary is something entirely
different.
That night, I broke my rule.
I carefully slid open the glass door and stepped inside. I even put on
gloves, terrified of leaving fingerprints. The apartment looked like
there had been some kind of struggle. A chair was knocked over, and
several objects were scattered across the floor.

The blood trail continued down a hallway toward what appeared to be a
bedroom.
My stomach tightened as I approached.
There was blood everywhere. The bedsheets were soaked, and lying beside
the bed was a woman’s body.

Her throat had been slashed.
It was her. The brunette I’d spent so many Friday nights watching.
I felt my heart sink into my chest. I couldn’t believe what I was
looking at. I stumbled backward, desperately trying to get out of the
apartment.
I turned toward the sliding glass door.
That’s when I saw him.
A man was standing just a few feet away, staring directly at me. He wore
a dark mask, and all I could see were his cold, black eyes. His head
still, just staring at me. He was holding a knife.

I practically threw myself down the fire escape, jumped the last few
steps, and sprinted all the way home. I didn’t stop until I was inside
my house with the doors locked.
What the fuck had I just witnessed?
It’s been almost two weeks, and I haven’t slept properly since that
night. I keep thinking about the apartment, the woman, and especially
that man.
I left my DNA inside. What would the police think if they found me
there?
I can’t go to them. How could I possibly explain why I was inside that
apartment?
But lately, something else has been happening.
Things in my house keep moving. Picture frames of my ex-wife and
children have been rearranged. Last Tuesday, I woke up and found my
phone sitting on the kitchen counter. I always leave it charging on my
nightstand.

I live alone. Nobody has a key.
At first, I convinced myself I was just tired. But last night, I found
my front door unlocked.
I know I locked it.
I can’t shake the feeling that someone followed me home that night.
And I’m starting to think he knows exactly where I live. I need help
