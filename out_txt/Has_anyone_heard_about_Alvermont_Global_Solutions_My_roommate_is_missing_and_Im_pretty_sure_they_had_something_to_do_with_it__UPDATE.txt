My previous post.

But in case you don’t want to read that, I’ll give you a brief summary
of what happened:

My roommate, Ismail, started acting really strange one day. Shortly
afterwards, he went missing. And by missing, I mean he left the
apartment in a rush one night and never came back, leaving all of his
stuff behind. I started digging through his emails, discovering that
he’d been in contact with a company named “Alvermont Global Solutions”.
He had reached out to them wanting to participate in the “consumer
behavioural study” they were running. But before I could finish the rest
of the email thread, I heard somebody opening the door to the apartment.

I’d assumed it was Ismail, of course. But when I went out into the
living room, it wasn’t him that I saw. Instead, it was these two huge
dudes dressed in black jackets and black pants, wearing what I can only
assume were some kind of prosthetic silicone masks. The kind of thing
somebody might wear while robbing a bank. Really freaky and uncanny. I
nearly had a heart attack when I saw them both standing there, looking
at me.

One of them locked the door while the other cocked a pistol at my head.

I didn’t know what to say so I just raised my hands, trying to stand as
still as I could. I briefly thought about screaming to try and alert the
neighbours but couldn’t work up the nerve for it. I figured they might
shoot me the second I tried. I didn’t want to take the risk.

So I just kept my mouth shut as one of them began ransacking the
apartment while the other continued to hold me at gunpoint. I tried
telling him that I didn’t have any money or anything even worth selling.
He told me to turn around and stare the wall and be quiet. Which is what
I did.

I’m not sure how long I stood there for. Could’ve been an hour. Maybe
more, maybe less. They left abruptly, neither of them saying a word to
me or each other as they disappeared into the hallway. Afterwards, I
spent some time debating in my head about whether it had really happened
or if I had yet to wake up from a dream.

I pinched myself a few times, splashed my face with cold water. Nope.
Definitely awake.

The strangest part was that they didn’t actually steal anything. I mean,
literally nothing. Not even my wallet, which had been sitting right on
the table in plain view.

I didn’t feel safe in the apartment anymore, so I grabbed my keys and my
phone and stuffed Ismail’s laptop into my backpack before heading for
the nearest police station.

When I got there, I began explaining everything that had happened to the
cop sitting behind the front desk. Some guys had broken into my
apartment, pointed a gun at me and… and what? What had they actually
done? It was a question I couldn’t answer and I began stumbling over my
words while the cop just stared at me.

The cop asked if they’d stolen anything. I told him I wasn’t sure. Then
he asked if I’d been assaulted. I told him no, I hadn’t, but they had
threatened me with a fucking gun. He just sighed, shook his head and
then repeated my story back to me.

“So you’re telling me some men wearing masks broke into your place,
pointed a gun at you and then they just left? Without taking anything?
Without doing anything? That’s what happened?”

We continued to go back and forth, my frustration mounting as he refused
to budge on his skepticism. At one point, he implied that I might’ve
been under the influence of something, that the entire experience had
simply been the product of an extremely vivid trip.

I couldn’t believe the audacity of this fucking guy. I mean, what?

So I shifted tactics and brought up Ismail’s disappearance, suggesting a
connection between the two things. A connection that seemed pretty damn
evident to me. But apparently not to the cop. He told me that since I’d
seen Ismail leaving the apartment on his own volition, it was likely
that he’d simply skipped town.

I asked him why he’d leave his laptop and the rest of his belongings
behind if that were the case.

The cop told me if I was really so worried, he’d take down Ismail’s
information and my account of what had happened and they’d begin
“looking into it.” I then circled back to the break-in, asking if there
was anything they could do about that. He told me that they’d send an
officer up to my apartment whenever one was available. But that he
wasn’t sure when that would be.

I left the station feeling a lot of different emotions, the biggest two
being frustration and confusion. I mean, I’ve had experiences with
asshole cops before but this was something else entirely. Something I
felt ill-equipped to understand.

In my car, I took the laptop out and opened up Ismail’s emails again,
hoping that reading through the rest of the thread might give me some
kind of ammunition, something to present to the cops with in order for
them to start taking this seriously.

But the thread was gone, every single message deleted. I poked around
some more, finding no evidence that he had ever been in communication
with AGS at all. No downloaded files, no other threads, nothing.

I never thought the break-in had been a coincidence. But now I had solid
evidence.

Feeling vindicated, I stormed back into the station, watching the cop
roll his eyes as I approached him again. I told him I’d figured out why
those guys had broken in and explained the email thread, AGS, the
consumer behavioural study, all of it.

I really thought I had a smoking gun here. But then the cop started
laughing in my face.

“So they broke into your apartment so that they could delete some
emails? That’s what you’re telling me?”

I told him yeah, that’s what it seemed like to me. He laughed again and
told me they’d look into it when they had a chance.

I walked back to my car and just sat there for a while. I was so tired
at this point. I just wanted to get some sleep so I tried to convince
myself that I didn’t need to be worried about it. That Ismail was
probably fine and even if he wasn’t, it wasn’t my responsibility. And
that whoever had broken in had gotten what they’d come for and had no
reason to come back, so I really didn’t need to be worried.

As I was getting ready to drive back home, somebody began knocking on my
window.

It was a woman, maybe mid-thirties, making this gesture like she wanted
me to talk to me. I rolled the window down about a quarter way and asked
her what she wanted. She asked me why I’d gone into the station, what I
was talking to the cop about. Using this really direct tone as well,
almost like an interrogation. I asked her why she wanted to know.

“Somebody went missing, didn’t they?” she’d said. “What was it?
Girlfriend? Brother? Coworker?”

I paused for a moment, feeling a bit stunned before telling her that it
was my roommate. She told me that her husband had gone missing as well.
Almost two weeks ago. She then claimed that a lot of people in the area
had gone missing recently and she thinks it’s all connected. She told me
that she wants to figure out what’s really going on and I could help her
do so. That maybe I’d be able to find Ismail and she could find her
husband.

Before I could answer, an officer came out of the station and began
yelling for her to leave. She slipped me a business card with a phone
number written on the back and urged me to give her a call. The last
thing she said to me before she left was that I shouldn’t trust anything
the cops tell me.

I went back to my apartment and tried to get some sleep but wasn’t able
to. By the time I finally started to doze off, I realized I had work in
about two hours. But there was obviously no way I was making it. So I
called in sick. My manager sounded completely pissed, but whatever.

I’m sitting on the balcony now, smoking as many cigarettes as I can. I
found out that my lock hadn’t been busted. I just assumed it had, so I
didn’t bother checking when I’d left. But it’s completely fine. Which
can mean a few things. One of which being that they used a copy of my
key to get into the front door. Meaning that they could come back
whenever the hell they wanted.

It’s really like a fucked-up game, some form of psychological torture.
But clearly I wasn’t the only one that had been pulled into it. The lady
at the station seemed pretty strung out as well.

I pulled out the card she’d given me. Below her number, she’d written
her name: Sophie.

I decided to give her a call. Just because I couldn’t live with this
sort of anxiety. I needed answers. I needed to know that I wasn’t in any
real danger. And if I was, well, I needed to know what I was supposed to
do about it.

She picked up almost immediately and I asked her to tell me everything
she knew. She told me that several months ago, her husband had been
hired to be part of a construction team meant to renovate a dead mall.
Things were okay at first. But one day, he suddenly started acting
really strange, really paranoid, blaming it on witnessing a gruesome
accident on the job site. An accident he wouldn’t go into detail about.

Then one night, he never came home at all. She tried calling him but he
wouldn’t pick up. She went to the police but they were completely
unhelpful, making up a bunch of excuses about why he hadn’t returned and
just not seeming to take her concerns seriously.

So she did some digging into her husband’s emails and figured out
exactly which mall he’d been working at. Then she dug deeper and learned
about others who had experienced something similar. People who had
friends or loved ones suddenly go missing under mysterious
circumstances. But there was one common denominator linking each case.
Which was that at one point, for whatever reason, they had all gone into
the mall before they went missing.

She told me that they’d formed a small group together. That they were
all united by a single goal, being to figure out exactly what had
happened to the people they’d lost. To either rescue or get justice for
them.

She asked me if I wanted to join. I told her I’d think about it and call
her back.

It was all starting to become too much, this sense of dread lingering
over me like a dark, heavy cloud.

I started thinking about what she’d told me and was able to make some
connections of my own. I mean, where would a “consumer behavioural
study” take place if not inside a mall?

I spent some time doing some research about Alvermont Global Solutions
but couldn’t find anything online. The company has no digital footprint
whatsoever. Then I went back through Ismail’s laptop, trying to see if I
could find any files or emails or anything that he’d saved that could
prove they even existed. But there was nothing. They’d done a hell of a
job getting rid of any evidence.

After a while, I started thinking about what would happen if I just
ignored it all and moved on with my life. What the consequences might
be.

But then somebody named Yasmine sent a message to Ismail’s WhatsApp.
When I translated it from Arabic, this is what it returned:

Hey, what’s going on? Is everything okay? Can you give us a call?
Mother’s really worried.

I closed the laptop afterwards. 

I don’t know what to do. Don’t know what to think. There’s still a part
of me that wants to just run away from this, remove myself from the
situation as much as possible. I could even leave the city, tell my
landlord that I’m moving out of here and then go sleep on my brother’s
couch in another state.

But I don’t know how I’d feel about myself after that. I’ve spent a lot
of my life sort of just going through the motions, festering in the
resentment that came with feeling like you were never making any real
impact on the world.

Is this a chance for me to change that? Maybe. I’m not sure yet. I stare
at Sophie’s number. I’m starting to warm up to the idea. Starting to
realize that this might be the only path that gets me an answer to this.

I’m dialing the number again. I’m going to call this time.
