Has anybody else forgotten their name?

That’s a strange question, I know. I hope it’s not just me.

I can tell you I did the Presidential Traverse in fourteen hours, car to
car. I can tell you my pack’s base weight down to the ounce, because
I’ve been prepping for a NOBO thru-hike of the AT since March. I can
even tell you the exact color of the sky over Madison that morning.

But I can’t tell you what my mother calls me.

I’ve tried everything. My license, my debit card, the return address on
my mail. Every other word on them reads fine, but my name just… runs.
Like wet ink, like the letters are trying to crawl off the card.

I asked my buddy to just say it for me. I watched his mouth make the
shape, and I heard a sound. I know I heard one. I just don’t remember
what it was, only that there was one.

I’d like to say I’ve always had a name. I assume I did, everybody does.
But I can’t remember ever having one, and I’m starting to realize I
can’t prove it either.

What I do know is when it started. About a month ago I section-hiked the
Hundred-Mile Wilderness, the last stretch of the AT before Katahdin.
Nine days, no towns, no roads, no signal. It was a good trip. Honestly
I’d call it the best trip of my life if I could stop thinking about it.

Two days after I came out, the guy at a gas station in Millinocket asked
for my name.

I opened my mouth and there was nothing there to say.

He waited. I waited. I could feel my face going hot, and I made some
noise that wasn’t really a word. For a second he looked at me like
something was wrong with me, and I don’t blame him. Then he just smiled,
told me to have a good day, and went back to his register like I’d never
been standing there.

But before the gas station, there was someone else. I’m almost sure of
it.

I passed plenty of people out there. September is when the northbounders
come through, sunburned and half-feral, a week out from Katahdin and the
end of everything. We’d trade trail names and water reports and move on.
Nobody out there asks your real name. That’s kind of the point.

But he did.

I can’t picture his face anymore. Older, I think. I remember his eyes
though. Pale, almost no color to them, and patient. Like he’d been
waiting a long time and didn’t mind waiting a little longer.

He asked me my name.

And I… I answered him.

I hadn’t touched my pack since I got back. I should have, but it just
felt… wrong. It sat by my door for a week, still zipped, still smelling
like the woods.

Like I said, I could tell you everything in that pack and its weight
down to the ounce. Yet I could barely bring myself to glance at it the
whole week. Every time I tried, this feeling would start in my stomach
and press its way up, slow, until it sat right at the back of my throat.

I knew I’d have to take it apart eventually. Last night I finally did. I
picked it up by the haul loop like I’d done a thousand times, and it was
heavier than it should have been.

I slowly started dismantling it, laying everything out on the floor
beside me. I had to laugh. I’d done this a hundred times, and now I was
acting like something was going to jump out and bite me. I tried to
shake the sick feeling that pushed my tongue flat.

I ran through my mental checklist to help myself feel better while I did
it.

Stove, check.

Pot, check.

Sunscreen, right on.

Bug spray, also check.

I could have kept going, but it was all pretty standard.

Except at the very bottom was something I didn’t remember packing.

I stared at it, trying to figure out what I was looking at. A dark,
thick mass curled up at the bottom of the pack. Wet. Still dripping,
after a week zipped up by my door. A sour stench came wafting out that
made my lip turn the wrong way, and I had to look away and take a second
before I could look back in.

It was a rope.

Knotted and frayed, and coiled so tight and so long it seemed almost
impossible it all fit in there with the rest of my things.

The more I looked at it, the stranger it felt to me.

A million questions popped into my head. It had sat in my house for a
week, so why was it still wet?

Then I noticed something else.

It wasn’t a few knots here or there. The whole thing was knotted, evenly
spaced through every part I could see. Why?

Hundreds of knots, each three to four inches apart.

I ran my hands over the rope, slowly tracing each knot. It started out
damp, but the more I pulled out, the wetter each knot got, until it was
soaked through, like it had been underwater only moments ago.

It just kept coming. I pulled out fifty feet, and that barely seemed to
make a dent. I got another fifty feet out.

One hundred feet?

I almost gave up there.

It’ll sound silly, but it only took me another fifty feet to reach the
end.

One hundred and fifty feet of sopping wet rope. Jesus Christ.

Who the fuck put this in my bag?

It wasn’t there when I hiked out. I would have felt it.

I studied the end of the rope. The last knot was different. Newer,
tighter, and water seemed to pour off it onto my floor. I dropped it on
instinct.

It’s still dripping. It’s been hours, and it’s still dripping.

Something is very wrong. If anybody has any information, I’d appreciate
it.

I’ll keep you all updated.
