So you know how newspapers used to be a thing, right? How they were
everywhere. Like on the train, waiting rooms, litter at the park, and
all that. Back before smartphones and the internet came in and made them
a dying medium. 

Well this old guy I used to work with must never have gotten the memo. 

Every day I’d come in to work and the old bugger; we’ll call him Karl
had one propped open and flipping through it. Reading it front to back,
like it was the most fascinating thing in the entire written world,
while we wait for the day to start. 

Normally I’d just ignore it, you know. Pass it off as some old guy who
didn’t want to join the technical revolution for two decades. That’s
until about two months ago. 

You see, two months ago my power went out overnight. So my phone didn’t
charge, and even worse because I had it plugged into my laptop it fed
the phone battery to the laptop. So I still manage to make it in on time
luckily, but I’m just rawdogging reality. Which nope, not that early in
the morning. So I cave. I asked Karl if I could borrow some of the pages
just to have something to read. Karl is an okay guy and not a total
a-hole so he passes me the outer pages (you know what I mean, like how
the front page and back page are the same piece of paper), since he’d
already made it through the start. 

For the most part it was rather boring. Some celebrity from a superhero
movie had an affair, a new park being opened for children, that kind of
thing. So pretty soon I’ve flicked through and end up in the back
section. The Classifieds. For those who never had the need to deal with
them they’re ads, but often for things like jobs, houses, that kind of
thing. 

One of them was actually pretty damn eye-catching. It genuinely looked
like it was from the 1800s or something. It just read 

“Desired: Healthy men needed for expedition. 
Pay exceeding expectations, with bonuses on return.
Weapons expertise not required, but increases pay.”

Underneath was a phone number. Now normally I’d have the good sense not
to essentially IRL click such a risky link, but frankly I was a seasonal
worker and wasn’t getting paid well. So needed something soon anyway.
Couldn’t hurt to call, could it. 

So I call, it gets picked up rather quickly. The person on the phone
wasn’t the guy who placed the ad. They worked for some management or
consulting firm or something like that, where they basically organise
things for people. Anyway, turns out that the pay would be rather damn
good. About a months work in what they said was essentially a FIFO gig,
but couldn’t give too many details without a contract due to NDAs. End
up with about $10k, plus they’d pay your lease/mortgage and any
pet-sitting while you’re out of town. Sounded like a really damn good
deal. So I kept going with the interview process. 

They asked a few relevant questions, like how’d you hear about the gig?
Have you ever done any dangerous/remote work before? If you did this
who’s your next of kin and their relation to you? Any medical history
that could impact you being in a remote area? Then rather sheepishly at
the end they asked because the ad demanded it, any weapons experience?

A few days later I’m sitting in a lawyers office signing the contracts,
NDA, etc. and get more details. Basically I’m told that all it is, is
flying into a remote area with a crew, taking care of some anthropology
(I think that’s the right one, the history of people nerds)
students/professors as they study things in the area, and fly home.
Gonna do basic work, cooking, setting up tents, hauling shit. Basically
the grunt work. Actually sounded pretty cool. 

Then three weeks later shuttle picks me up, takes me to the airfield,
meet the guys I’m with. Eight in total, four of them are the science
guys. Then get this, we get to take a seaplane. Like that’s some
Skypilot of Yesteryear type thing. Like I didn’t even know they actually
still existed. 

Now, we were told it would be remote, but I didn’t think it would be
this remote. We had to refuel like twice at different airfields. Finally
we’re told by the pilot, this big guy with I wanna say a Serbian accent
(at least one of those places that use Da for yes and wasn’t Russia),
and a tattoo of a lantern on his hand that we’re arriving.

Anyway, as we’re coming down we can see around us. It’s this almost
tropical swamp looking place. On one of the little island looking things
we can see a pair of big ol’ shipping containers. Mr. Dupont (one of the
seniors on the team) said from his info-packet that one was a
mini-lab/secure room, and the other was a supply crate for us. 

We land right on the water. The nasty-ass smelling water. Probably full
of leaches and mosquitos looking, dark green water. The pilot pulls out
a remote control or beacon or something and we watch as this little boat
pottle over from the shore, so it seemed to be a homing beacon of some
kind. We had to take four trips to get everyone over, since you needed
someone to row. So it was like that fox, corn, duck riddle. 

Finally we’re all there, and watch as the plane leaves. We actually got
a chance to talk about our roles and everything we’re doing (since the
plane was too loud for proper conversation). And basically, the science
nerds are going to be dredging some parts of the swamp looking for
preserved artifacts because the deeper water lacks oxygen or something
like that so it pretty much prevents rot. The rest of us are there to
keep them alive, fed, and potentially set up a better base if they find
anything, with the nearest village like 50km away. 

The professor was called Pike, and from what I could tell seemed to know
more than anyone what was going on. Said all this was thanks to some
grant from some private foundation that owned the region. Although it
seemed unclear if they wanted to know because it would make money from
knowing people lived here, or getting rid of the evidence if there had. 

So we set up site with these sad little single person tents each, a tarp
for a kitchen, and a latrine. The first few days actually went pretty
sweet. Mr Dupont and Pike seemed to be in charge, but pretty chill when
you weren’t on shift. They didn’t even care when Willis (one of the
students) got caught with pot. 

Mostly all that happened was the scientists would go out to do their
study, usually taking either Dupont or Schummer with them; since both of
them were trained to use the rifle in case a crocodile or something
showed up. The neat thing was that they had this little portable bridge
thing with them to hop the rivers. It took like 15 minutes but it would
slowly inflate across and you could walk across and then deflate it. Of
course that left two or three of us at base most of the time. 

About a week in, Schummer and Lee get this idea to take the little
paddleboat out during the day to do some fishing, maybe get us away from
that pre-packaged stuff. And that, that’s where shit started to break
bad.

The pair of them had gone out in the tub in the morning as the
professor, Dupont, and two students had gone to their site, and I was
stuck at base babysitting one of the students; a small guy called Silas
who was going through samples in the mini-lab. So I was just doing
busywork by myself most of the day. Which meant I was alone when he got
back.

Barely even noticed at first that the boat had landed, and when I saw it
Schummer was just sitting there on the boat’s seat. Not really doing
anything. Just sitting there, with the oar across his lap.

Wanting to get the fish prepped I asked him where Lee was. But he didn’t
respond. He just sat there staring off into the water. Now I was getting
a bit concerned, as I couldn’t see or hear Lee on our little island. So
I went to walk over to check that Schummer was okay, you know. What if
he’d had an accident or something. As I got close enough to the boat I
could see that an accident or something had certainly happened.

The floor of the boat was soaked in red.

It wasn’t like that scene in The Shining or anything where it was that
corn-syrupy looking deep red. It was red diluted by a bunch of water
that had gotten in. 

I ran over to Schummer calling out for Silas to come help. As he came
out the lab I told him to also grab a first-aid kit. While he came over
I began looking over Schummer, trying to find out what happened. He
wouldn’t respond. 

Pulling him out of the boat onto the land I tried to find if it was his
blood or not. He didn’t have any open wounds or anything. His shins had
a bunch of leeches on them that must have gotten in with the water. But
they were still attached and there’s no way they could draw that much
blood. 

Silas finally got there and ripped one of the leaches off to try and
help. It took a mouthful of skin with it, and the worst part is Schummer
didn’t even react. Not a flinch, scream, nothing. Just sat there. The
thing in Silas’ hand was this gross not quite black, kinda green thing.
All fat off the man’s blood but still writhing and trying to get more of
it. Shouting at him to get rid of it got Silas to threw it into the
water. 

We had no idea what had happened; and no idea where Lee was. Schummer
wasn’t talking and we didn’t know what to do. Best we could do was use
some of the canteen’s salt to get the other leeches off him, and put
some antiseptic cream on the bites. 

By the time the others got back about dusk Schummer still hadn’t said
anything. He’d just sat there on a folding chair the whole time. Still
staring off into the swamp. The only change in his demeanour was that
he’d slumped down a bit, and looked a bit tired but like he was fighting
it. Lee said it was probably because his adrenaline had dumped all it
had into his system. 

The others saw that something was wrong as they got closer, and called
out asking what. We couldn’t tell them what exactly. Only what we knew
so far. Mr Dupont got up close to Schummer and squatted down next to him
so their eyes were level, and didn’t even say anything for like a full
10 minutes. 

I watched as he just sat there analysing everything, and then he put a
hand on Schummer’s shoulder. That actually seemed to get some kind of
response. Like he flinched and looked up at Mr Dupont at the same time.
Of anyone I think Mr Dupont was the one Schummer respected the most.
Something about them bonding over having both been firemen or EMTs or
some other kind of first responder at some point (truth be told I can’t
remember what job exactly, just that it was the same).

Mr Dupont looked him in the eyes, and asked him in that soft dad voice
that some older guys learn ‘What happened son?’

It took a few minutes before he answered. Not even sure I thought he’d
answer but he finally did. But as I sat there watching from in front of
him I could see his eyes shaking as he thought about what had happened
and what to say. But finally he croaked out a response that was such a
dry and cracked sound that it took a moment to realise they were words.
It was just four words.

‘It… took his throat…’

What the hell did that even mean? I had no idea, and everyone else
looked just as confused. 

Finally Pike broke the silence, and said it was probably some kind of
accident. Like a croc had grabbed Lee out of the boat and done that roll
thing they do. There was nothing we could do right now, at least not
until we knew more. So he said the best thing would be for Schummer to
be given a cot in the secure room for the night, so we weren’t crowding
him, and so him and Mr Dupont could talk to him more when he could talk
more. That way we could know where Lee was and go look for him.

But for then all we could do was get things ready to send out a search
party first thing in the morning. 

This is getting a bit long, and my battery is running low, so I’ll have
to type up more of what happened later. But yeah, we had no idea what
we’d gotten ourselves into, and I wish I hadn’t answered that damn ad.
