Today, I watched something weird, something which shouldn’t happen but
it did. I really am but I shouldn’t ha ….

I woke up and went straight to the TV. Of course, it was Sunday, and
interestingly, it was my break, so the days didn’t really matter. I
could watch as much as I wanted. My parents allowed me to because I was
not bad academically. I was an above-average student who scored well in
a few subjects.

I switched on the television and settled down to watch. My parents had
gone out shopping, and they told me they would return in a few hours.
That meant I was now in charge of this kingdom called Jett’s House. Yes,
I am the queen – who once was princess and will be after parents come
home.

Now, the princess had become the queen, and it was my responsibility to
rule over my kingdom.

I selected my favorite show, Tom & Jerry, and started watching it. My TV
was bulky and heavy, and it often lagged or showed a distorted screen
whenever the signal got disturbed. Usually, all you had to do was give
the TV a firm pat on the top, and it would start working again.

And, as expected, it happened today too. But this time was different.

While I was watching, the screen became distorted. I used the usual
remedy I knew, but it didn’t work. The distortion continued for several
minutes. I was getting bored.

Then the distortion became strange.

Instead of the normal white static, the screen turned rainbow-colored,
and then suddenly black. It was weird. That had never happened before
and, as far as I knew, couldn’t happen on this TV.

Then, without warning, the screen cleared.

The sight that appeared made me shiver from head to foot.

On the TV screen was a live image of me.

I was displayed on the television, sitting on the sofa and looking at
that TV.

It was impossible.

How was I on the screen?

I jolted backward on the sofa in shock. It felt as though someone was
recording me live from behind, like a hidden camera.

But there was no camera anywhere in my house. I suddenly looked at the
back out of pure fear and curiosity and there was nothing, which was
making TV show me.

Suddenly, the TV became distorted again, and this time it was the normal
kind of distortion that usually happened. That brought me some relief,
and I convinced myself that maybe it had all been a hallucination.

But the relief didn’t last long.

The TV screen turned black again.

This time, however, something felt wrong. It looked as if the television
had been switched off. I got up to check it, but the power light was
still on.

I was confused.

Then something appeared on the screen.

There was a line of text written in a language I didn’t understand.

I patted the TV and said in frustration, “Why is this TV acting so
weird?”

A moment later, the text changed.

Now, the same text was in English.

> “If you’re seeing this message, it’s already inside the house.”

At that moment, I realized it wasn’t a hallucination.

Everything was real.

Fear took over my body completely. I froze where I stood.

The message continued.

> “There is no signal problem. Turn off the TV before it notices you.”

Now I completely lost my senses. I couldn’t move. I couldn’t think.

My brain refused to process what was happening.

Then the screen distorted once more, and suddenly my cartoon returned as
if nothing had happened.

I stared at it in silence.

Confused, but desperate for a logical explanation, I convinced myself
that it must have been some strange channel I had accidentally switched
to, or maybe a error.

Eventually, I calmed down and sat back on the sofa. Then I continued
watching the cartoon.

Honestly, the episode that was airing was hilarious. Watching Tom
constantly beat up Jerry never failed to make me laugh.

I ended up watching almost fourteen episodes straight and enjoyed every
minute of it. I was lying upside down on the sofa, sipping my favorite
juice. It was probably the funniest and most comfortable position for
me—a habit I’ve had since childhood.

Then, once again, the screen began to distort.

By then, I had almost forgotten about the incident that had happened a
few hours earlier.

The screen shifted back to my cartoon, but something wasn’t right.

The colors were washed out, and the show looked like an old videotape
stretched uploaded and used beyond its limits. Everything appeared
blurry and warped.

I frowned.

Maybe the TV was simply getting too old. Perhaps it was finally time for
my parents to buy a new one.

Then the distortion returned.

Thinking it might be a signal issue, I went up to the roof to check the
antenna. I used my usual strategy of giving it a pat before heading back
downstairs.

The TV was still distorting.

Now I was annoyed.

Then it appeared.

The same live image of me was back on the screen.

I jolted backward onto the sofa and wrapped my arms around myself.
Instantly, I remembered everything that had happened before.

But this time, the version of me inside the TV wasn’t copying my
movements.

It wasn’t moving at all.

It simply stood there.

Still.

Watching.

That somehow was even worse than the first encounter.

Then, without warning, the figure’s head rotated.

**360°. Slowly.**

Until it was facing me.

I froze.

My body refused to move.

The thing smiled.

Its eyes widened unnaturally, growing larger and larger.

The smile wasn’t human.

It stretched far beyond what a real face should allow, twisting into
something grotesque and impossible.

Slowly, the figure began walking toward the screen.

Toward me.

I screamed.

Jumping to my feet, I rushed toward the television. Without thinking, I
grabbed the power cord and abruptly snatched it from the wall.

The screen went dark.

My vision blurred. The room spun. And then everything faded to black.

When I opened my eyes, I was lying in my bed. My parents were sitting
beside me.

I felt cold and weak.

My mother leaned closer.

“Are you okay?” she asked.

I nodded.

They told me they had found me unconscious on the floor near the
television. According to them, my favorite cartoon had still been
playing when they arrived home.

I sat up slowly. Every memory of what had happened came rushing back.
But I was far too frightened to tell them.

My mother told me to rest and later made my favorite snack—a fried
sandwich.

Which I loved, of course.

For several days, I refused to go near the television. I didn’t dare to
switch it on. I didn’t even look at it.

Eventually, my parents had it repaired, and everything seemed normal
again.

But even now, I still wonder.

What was that thing?

And how was any of it possible?

If there was no camera in the house, then how was the TV showing myself?
