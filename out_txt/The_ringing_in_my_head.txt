I don’t remember when it started.
Talking was always easy when I was alone. Just talking to myself in my
room, you know? But whenever someone actually expected an answer out of
me, my whole body just locked up. My throat would tighten into a knot
and I couldn’t force out a single word.
My parents never got it. They’d heard me talk to myself before, so they
knew I wasn’t actually mute. They just thought I was being a weird,
stubborn kid. Whenever I did manage to squeak something out to them, it
didn’t prove I was trying… to them, it was just proof that I was
choosing not to talk the rest of the time.
At school, people had patience at first. Back in early elementary,
teachers would wait for me to write something on a piece of paper or
take five minutes to think. But that didn’t last long. By fifth grade,
nobody gave a crap anymore. The other kids had heard me choke out a few
weird, strained sounds before and thought it was hilarious to put me on
the spot just to watch me panic. It wasn’t always blatant bullying, just
these horribly awkward scenes where I wanted the ground to swallow me
whole.
Then seventh grade hit, and a boy named Cam started calling me “Molly
Mute.”
That was the first time I remember getting genuinely pissed off. Soon
enough, the whole school was using it. It was infuriating. I tried so
hard to pretend it didn’t bother me, but it did. I felt broken. I felt
weird. I was weird. I couldn’t speak.
I’d sit in my room and practice in front of the mirror for hours.
Talking to my reflection was fine, but the second I stepped outside and
tried to say a single word out loud to another human being, my throat
clamped shut, tears filled my eyes, and nothing came out.
Eventually, my parents gave up. They stopped dragging me to social
events to try and “fix” me. At school, nobody even bothered trying to
talk to me anymore. I was completely on my own. I talked to myself,
alone. Honestly, I didn’t mind being by myself that much. But it was
still loud. I wasn’t just alone but also lonely. And the loneliness was
so damn loud. Watching everyone around me laugh, hang out, and live
normal lives while I just existed… it sucked.
We lived in this tiny, dead-end village. 21 kids in the entire school
and about 50 people in the whole town. Most of them were old folks, and
they were the absolute worst. They kept telling my parents I was just
being a difficult child and that I’d “grow out of it.” I hoped they were
right, but it still felt like hell.
Then one day, a group of girls walked up to me.
Hailey was leading them. She was so pretty, and talking was just
effortless for her. God, I was jealous of her. She told me she’d found
this specific ritual on one of the library computers, printed it out,
and handed it to me. I knew they were making fun of me. They kept
whispering stuff like, “Maybe Molly Mute can finally be normal after
this.”
I knew it was a cruel prank, but there was something in Hailey’s eyes
that made me hesitate. She looked so serious, almost like she actually
cared. So I agreed. Not because I believed in some ritual, but because I
was just that desperate for my them to accept me.
The ritual was ridiculous. They made me wear these stupid clothes and
swallow stuff that definitely wasn’t meant for human consumption. At the
end, they revealed that they had been recording me. Hailey was laughing
the hardest out of all of them. Hailey and Cam.
But what I couldn’t tell them- because my throat was totally
frozen-wasn’t just that I was embarrassed. I was terrified.
There was something standing right in front of me. Something that
apparently only I could see. An entity. A monster. A demon. It didn’t do
anything, it was probably just as useless as me. It just extended a hand
toward me. Tears were streaming down my face, I felt completely
humiliated, but I reached out and took its hand because… I don’t know,
it felt like the demon was mirroring my sadness.
And then it just vanished. I figured it took one look at how pathetic I
was and decided I wasn’t even worth making a deal with.
I looked up the ritual on the school computer later and found out its
name was Nerith. Hailey and her friends had picked the dumbest, most
ridiculous ritual they could find and once that video got around town,
the elders were totally convinced I was possessed by the devil. Why else
would a girl do a ritual?
But weirdly, I stopped caring after that day. Something inside me just
left me.
I couldn’t bring myself to give a shit about anyone anymore. My parents
stopped talking to me? I didn’t care. Cam tried to pull an awkward
half-backed apology later on? I couldn’t care. On graduation day,
someone stuck their foot out and tripped me while I walked across the
stage, and I still wasn’t able to feel anything.
After school ended, I took a job at the only market in town—which was
really just a fruit and vegetable stand for whatever the locals grew in
their backyards.
It was perfect for me. People pointed at what they wanted, I threw it on
the scale, the price got printed, they paid, and they left. I didn’t
have to talk, and they didn’t want to talk to me. A wordless exchange.

Then one day one of the village elders died.
There was a funeral, and almost the entire village showed up. People
were sobbing. I didn’t shed a single tear- not just because I hated that
old bastard for telling everyone I was possessed, but because I also
couldn’t care less. Instead, I noticed how everyone weeped. Even my
parents were crying. They were all so broken up about being left behind.
That’s when it clicked for me.
Our town was so small that every time someone died, everybody else was
left behind to suffer the loss. It felt… unfair.

That night, I grabbed my dad’s baseball bat from the hall and walked
down to the kitchen. I had imagined this scenario a lot of times, but I
still felt the adrenaline rushing into me. My mom was in the bathroom,
getting ready to go to the gathering to mourn the elder. My dad was in
the living room watching TV while waiting for her, cranked up way too
loud like he always had it.
I used the noise to walk right up behind him and swung the bat into the
back of his head with everything I had.
Blood splattered across the wall. He let out this horrible scream, but
thanks to the TV noises it blended into the background. Before he could
react, I hit him again. And again. He was a big guy, so I was honestly
surprised by how fast he stopped breathing. The adrenaline hit my
bloodstream like a truck, killing someone that big suddenly didn’t seem
hard at all. I kept hitting him long after he stopped moving.
I only stopped when I heard my mom scream behind me.
She looked mortified. That’s when I looked down and realized I was
covered in dirty, sticky blood. I hated it. I felt disgusting. I wasn’t
dirty… I always was clean.
What pissed me off even more was my mother’s screaming. She was so loud.
I wished I could tell her to shut up as many times as she’d yelled at me
to speak.
I gripped the bat tighter and started walking toward her. She bolted, so
I ran after her. She was pretty heavy, but the panic gave her speed.
Still, she wasn’t fast enough. I caught up in the hallway, brought the
bat down, and kept swinging until she was still.
It was strange, but I actually felt lighter. Thinking about how my
parents would never have to be sad about someone dying ever again… it
felt like I’d actually done something good. I helped them. I wasn’t
useless anymore…
Next, I walked over to the neighbor’s house. A few people had gathered
there- people my age meeting up to mourn. None of them heard me sneaking
around outside, looking at all the dark, empty houses. I thought it was
hilarious how they were throwing what looked like a party when they were
supposed to be grieving. And nobody invited me, which was kind of rude.
Then I realized it actually was a party. They were celebrating the
engagement between Hailey and Cam.
That was so weird. Why the hell would anyone celebrate when someone just
died?
The party inside was messy and loud. I knew the bat wouldn’t do much
against a whole crowd, so I grabbed a heavy garden shovel from the
neighbor’s yard. It was nice and clean, though my bloody handprints just
ruined it again.
I slipped through the back door. The lights were low and the music was
blasting, so nobody noticed me. Nobody noticed me until I slammed the
shovel right into Hailey’s face.
Then everyone noticed.
In hindsight, rushing a room full of people wasn’t the smartest move.
Some people screamed and dropped to the floor next to Hailey, but others
came straight at me. I kept swinging the shovel wild trying to keep my
distance, and I quickly figured out that taking out their legs or
snapping their necks first worked like a charm.
They all died that night. Some of them were crying and begging me for
mercy, which made zero sense. I mean I wasn’t doing this out of revenge…
I was doing this so they wouldn’t have to feel sad anymore. Wasn’t that
obvious?
Whatever. Who cares. (Not she, she’s nonchalant)
I checked every single house after that just to make sure nobody was
left behind. The rest of the adults were missing until I checked the
dead elder’s house. They were all crammed in there, digging through
cabinets for whatever valuables the old man had promised them. My
parents were supposed to be there too, but they were a bit occupied back
home.
The house was packed, everyone shouting and tearing through stuff. It
was chaotic and loud so nobody heard me hit the gas pipe in the basement
with the shovel. They didn’t hear me walk out, and they definitely
didn’t smell the gas before someone lit a cigarette inside.
I didn’t even have to do anything else. The whole house just blew up.
I thought that was everyone, but then I saw a light still burning in one
of the houses at the end of the road.
So I walked over. I could hear water running-he was taking a shower.
Nobody in our town ever locked their doors, so I just walked in through
the back. On the couch, I found one of those decorative throw pillows
with tiny metal bells sewn onto the corners. I liked those pillows… they
reminded me of Christmas decor. I didn’t want to make another bloody
mess, so I decided to try something different.
My bangs were matted to my forehead in grease and dried blood as I
waited. Cam came out wearing only sweatpants and sat on the sofa, to
watched TV.
I lunged and slammed the pillow over his face. He didn’t even know what
hit him. He thrashed frantically, but I threw my whole weight onto his
chest to pin him down. He clawed at the pillow, trying to tear it off,
but I jammed it down with everything I had. I didn’t want him to suffer
or be sad, even if he deserved it. Nobody deserves to be left behind
just because of my petty feelings.

I pressed harder and harder until Cam managed to rip one of those heavy
metal bells off the pillow corner and slammed it straight into the side
of my head.
Blinding pain exploded across my face. The bell was solid metal, and
blood immediately flooded into my eyes. The ringing inside my head was
so loud it made me sick. Tears welled up and I backed off, letting go of
the pillow. That split second was all he needed to throw me off,
scramble to his feet, and run screaming out the front door.
My head was spinning and I was seeing double. For a moment, I just sat
there thinking this was all a stupid idea and I should just go to sleep.
But that wouldn’t be fair- leaving Cam to wake up to all this tragedy by
himself.
So I picked up a heavy landscaping rock off his porch and started
dragging myself down the driveway after him. I was moving so slowly. The
pain was unbearable. Tears were mixing with the blood on my face as I
whispered, “I’m sorry… I’m so sorry…”
I was sorry I couldn’t kill him fast enough, and now he had to see
everyone dead.
Cam was standing right under the town’s only working streetlight,
screaming for help. When he turned around and saw me hobbling over, he
looked relieved. I guess he couldn’t see my face in the dark when I had
the pillow over him.
“There’s a killer in town!” he yelled, panicking. “We need to get out of
here!”
I looked up at him and rasped out a whisper. “What are you talking
about? A killer?”
Cam froze dead in his tracks. Hearing me speak seemed to throw him off
way more than the actual situation did. He stammered about how glad he
was that I was okay and that we needed to find a telephone to call the
cops. He started walking toward me, completely blind to the heavy rock I
was holding down at my side.
As soon as he got into arm’s reach, I swung out and cracked the stone
right across his forehead—just like he did to me.
He collapsed onto the pavement, screaming and crying. I hit him again,
because I was more merciful than he was. I wasn’t going to make him
suffer. I hit him again, and again, and again, until his head wasn’t
really a head anymore.
I stood up, tossed the rock into the grass, and sat down on the curb
under the streetlight.
After a while, I got bored. I sat there for hours, but it wasn’t very
fun. I was alone now, but I didn’t feel lonely. And it wasn’t loud
anymore either. It was so quiet. It was… beautiful.
When the sun started coming up, I walked into the woods and sat on a
rock, watching the birds and squirrels. It was pretty. Truly pretty.
Then I heard a branch snap in the trees.
I turned my head too fast, and a spike of agony shot through my skull-
the bell was still jammed deep into my forehead, and I couldn’t pull it
out. It hurt so badly, and the ringing inside my head was deafening.
Tears spilled down my face as I clutched my temples until I noticed that
a tall white faceless man was standing under the streetlight. He looked
down at what was left of Cam, and then he looked over to me.
I didn’t move. I was terrified, but at the same time, I didn’t feel
anything at all. I turned my head again, the bell ringing inside my ear,
blood dripping down my jaw.
The faceless man stepped out of the light and walked over to me. He held
out his hand-just like the demon from the ritual.
I took it. Not because he looked sad, or because I was desperate for
anything. I took it because I wanted to. I wasn’t Molly mute anymore. I
wanted to be the thing inside of me. I wanted to be Nerith, because
Nerith was a part of me now, and I am a part of it.
