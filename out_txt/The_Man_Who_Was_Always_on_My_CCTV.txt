​

I work night shifts at a small hotel in Manchester, England. Most nights
are boring. I check guests in, answer the telephone, clean the reception
desk, and watch the security cameras until morning.

I’ve worked there for almost two years, and I know the building well
enough to notice when something changes.

That’s why I know what I saw on the CCTV wasn’t normal.

It started on a Wednesday night in October. I was alone at reception,
drinking instant coffee from a chipped white mug. The time was 9:55
p.m., and rain was hitting the glass entrance doors so hard that I could
barely see the empty car park.

I was checking the cameras when I noticed a man standing outside Room
214.

He was wearing a black coat and dark trousers. His head was lowered, and
his hands hung loosely beside him.

I assumed he was a guest who had forgotten his room number.

I switched to the camera covering the second-floor corridor. The man was
still there.

I watched for a minute.

He didn’t move.

Then I checked the hotel’s booking system. Room 214 had been empty for
three days because of a broken shower. Nobody should have been anywhere
near it.

I called the maintenance manager, who told me the room was locked and
advised me to check the corridor in person.

I didn’t want to leave reception unattended, so I used the intercom to
speak to the night cleaner, Daniel, who was working on the second floor.

“Can you check the corridor outside Room 214?” I asked.

“Why?”

“There’s someone standing there.”

Daniel told me he was nowhere near that room. He said the entire second
floor was empty.

I returned to the CCTV.

The man was gone.

I felt embarrassed for making such a fuss. I blamed the poor lighting
and went back to my coffee.

At 10:23 p.m., the reception telephone rang.

“Good evening, Ashford Hotel,” I answered.

A man whispered, “Could you tell me what time checkout is?”

“Eleven in the morning, sir.”

“Thank you.”

He hung up.

I checked the telephone display. The call had come from Room 214.

I stared at the screen for several seconds.

Then I checked the booking system again, hoping I’d made a mistake.

The room was still empty.

I called the room from reception. It rang six times before someone
answered.

“Hello?” I said.

At first, there was only breathing.

Then the same man whispered, “You shouldn’t be looking at me.”

The call ended.

I immediately contacted Daniel and told him what had happened. This
time, he didn’t laugh. He came downstairs and stood beside me while I
checked the cameras.

We reviewed the recording from 9:55 p.m.

The corridor was empty.

We checked the recording from 10:23 p.m.

The corridor was still empty.

But something else caught my attention.

On the camera covering the reception desk, a man was standing directly
behind my chair.

He was wearing a black coat and dark trousers.

His head was lowered.

His hands hung loosely beside him.

I turned around so quickly that my chair struck the desk.

Nobody was there.

Daniel stepped away from the monitor and told me to call the police if I
saw the man again. He then went to the staff room, saying he needed a
moment.

I remained at reception with every camera displayed on the monitors.

At 10:41 p.m., the lift doors opened on the second floor.

Nobody stepped out.

The doors closed.

A few seconds later, the lift appeared on the ground-floor camera.

Its doors opened directly in front of reception.

The man stepped out.

I stopped breathing.

He looked exactly like the figure I’d seen earlier. His coat was wet,
although the entrance doors hadn’t opened since midnight.

He stood beside the reception desk, facing the wall.

I could see him clearly on the monitor, but when I looked up from the
screen, the lobby was empty.

I grabbed my phone and called Daniel.

He answered immediately.

“He’s downstairs,” I whispered.

Daniel said nothing.

“Daniel?”

Then he asked me a question that made my stomach turn cold.

“Which camera are you watching?”

“The reception camera.”

He took a long breath.

“Listen to me carefully. Don’t turn around.”

I froze.

“Why?”

“Because I’m watching the reception camera from upstairs.”

His voice began to shake.

“You’re sitting at the desk. There’s someone standing behind you. He’s
leaning over your shoulder.”

I stared at the monitor.

The man on the screen slowly raised his head.

For the first time, I saw his face.

It was my face.

Same eyes. Same nose. Same small scar beneath my chin.

But the man on the screen looked much older, as though he hadn’t slept
in years.

He leaned closer to the camera.

His lips moved.

I couldn’t hear him, but I understood the words.

“Let me out.”

Every monitor went black.

The reception telephone rang.

I didn’t answer it.

It rang again.

Then my mobile phone lit up with an incoming call from Daniel.

I answered without taking my eyes off the dark screens.

He was crying.

“I’m sorry,” he whispered. “I thought it was a glitch in the footage.
I’ve seen him on the cameras before. I never said anything.”

“What are you talking about?”

He didn’t answer immediately.

Then he said, “I’m looking at an old recording from the archive.”

I felt the blood drain from my face.

“Which recording?”

“One from before you started working here.”

He sent me a video.

My hands shook as I opened it.

The footage showed the reception desk at 10:41 p.m. The date was eleven
months before my first shift at the hotel.

A man sat in my chair, drinking coffee from a chipped white mug.

He looked exactly like me.

He checked the monitors, glanced toward the lift, and then suddenly
turned to face the camera.

His expression was terrified.

He mouthed something before the footage ended.

I watched it several times until I finally understood what he had said.

“Don’t let him take your place.”

The lights came back on.

Every camera showed an empty corridor.

The man had disappeared.

At 7:00 a.m., I left the hotel and never returned. The manager accepted
my resignation without asking a single question.

I still have the video Daniel sent me.

I wish I could say I deleted it, but I haven’t.

Because last night, almost a year after I quit, I received an email from
the hotel’s security system.

It contained one new CCTV recording.

The timestamp was 9:55 p.m. that Wednesday.

The reception desk was empty.

The lift doors opened.

A man stepped out wearing a black coat and dark trousers.

He walked to the desk, sat in my old chair, and looked directly into the
camera.

It was me.

Except this time, I was smiling.

The desk phone rang. The display read ROOM 214.

The man who looked like me picked it up.

“Checkout is at eleven in the morning, sir,” he said, still smiling at
the camera.

That’s when I understood the whisper. He hadn’t been asking about
checkout. He’d been asking when he could leave.

And underneath the video, the system had automatically generated a
caption.

STAFF MEMBER DETECTED: CURRENT EMPLOYEE.

I haven’t worked there for eleven months.

I thought quitting meant I’d refused. I think it only meant the chair
was empty.

So who, exactly, is the hotel letting into the building every night?
