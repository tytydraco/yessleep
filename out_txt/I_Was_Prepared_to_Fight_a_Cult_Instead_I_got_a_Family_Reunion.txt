Part 12: [The Thing That Terrified the Creator Is Now Holding My
Hand](https://www.reddit.com/r/Nonsleep/comments/1v5028d/the_thing_that_terrified_the_creator_is_now/)

If there was one thing I had learned about the Executive Board, it was
that no meeting ever meant anything good.

The room was unusually quiet. Director Stonehill stood at the head of
the table, reviewing a stack of reports while Director Morozov silently
read through another file projected in front of him. Madam Leni sat
beside him, patiently waiting for the meeting to begin.

Nobody spoke.

I wasn’t really paying attention.

My thoughts were still back in the apartment.

Noah had refused to let go of Angelo’s hand even in his sleep.
Eventually, Angelo had managed to carefully slide his hand free without
waking him, although it had taken far longer than either of us expected.
The moment Noah frowned in his sleep, Elior had quietly walked over and
sat beside the bed.

***“I will remain with him.”***

Angelo had looked at his nephew.

*“You are certain?”*

***“Yes.”***

Elior’s gaze had shifted toward the sleeping child.

***“I believe he should wake to someone familiar.”***

A few moments later, Noah had shifted in his sleep and grabbed onto
Elior’s sleeve without even opening his eyes.

Elior hadn’t moved after that.

Not once.

When I left the apartment, he was still sitting exactly where he had
been, reading one of the children’s books Research had somehow managed
to deliver within thirty minutes of Noah existing.

“Agent Nayeri.”

Stonehill’s voice pulled me back into reality.

I straightened in my chair.

“Sorry, Director.”

Stonehill didn’t comment. Instead, he touched the holographic display in
front of him, causing a map to appear above the table.

A chain of islands appeared in the middle of the ocean, thousands of
miles away from the nearest major civilization.

“Our latest intelligence concerns an isolated settlement on Jan Mayen
Island.”

The room immediately became more focused.

“The island has remained largely abandoned for decades,” Jacob
continued. “Three months ago, satellite imagery detected construction
that should not exist.”

The image changed.

Dozens of buildings surrounded a mountain that had never appeared on
previous surveys.

Another image followed.

People.

Hundreds of them.

All dressed in identical clothing, standing perfectly still, the figures
in the photograph looked less like a group of people and more like a
single organism waiting for a command.

“CSP has confirmed the existence of an organized religious movement,”
Jacob said.

Madam Leni folded her hands. “A cult?”

Jacob nodded. “They refer to themselves as The Children of the First
Light.”

Stonehill continued, his expression unreadable. “Over the last eighteen
months, more than four hundred missing persons have been connected to
the island.”

Nobody interrupted because everyone already understood what that meant.

“They are performing ritual sacrifices to a god.”

The words settled over the room, heavier than anyone wanted to
acknowledge.

General Hayes was the first to respond.

“Who is the leader?”

Stonehill looked toward Morozov.

“We don’t have a confirmed identity yet,” he said. “Our intelligence
suggests they operate from the central structure, but whoever is in
control has avoided all surveillance attempts.”

Morozov tapped the display, causing a new image to appear above the
table.

The photograph showed the largest building within the settlement.

Unlike the surrounding structures, it appeared older, almost as if it
had been carved directly into the mountain itself. No windows. No
visible entrances. No signs of ordinary activity.

Just a massive structure standing in the center of the complex.

“All known activity originates from here,” Morozov explained. “The
individuals inside have not been identified.”

Stonehill studied the image for a moment before continuing.

“Whatever is happening on Jan Mayen Island, the person responsible has
remained hidden.”

He tapped the screen again, causing a new symbol to appear beside the
CSP logo.

A symbol I recognized immediately.

“Eidolon Systems Research.”

Jacob leaned slightly toward me.

“That’s the sister organization, right?”

I nodded.

Unlike CSP, which usually operated away from the public eye in isolated
locations, ESR specialized in dealing with threats hidden within
civilization.

Their missions weren’t ancient temples, abandoned facilities, or places
nobody would notice.

They went into cities.

They handled entities that had been living among humanity for years
without anyone realizing.

Most of their work involved gods, especially ones that had managed to
blend into the human world.

“When CSP can’t contain something, ESR is sent to eliminate it.”

“Sometimes permanently,” Madam Leni added.

The room became quiet because everyone understood what that meant.

ESR wasn’t brought in for things that could simply be locked away.

They were brought in when something had already crossed the line.

Stonehill continued.

“The head of ESR has personally requested to accompany this mission.”

That caught my attention.

“The head?”

Morozov nodded.

“Yes.”

Jacob frowned.

“Why?”

Morozov looked toward the screen.

“Because the leader has yet to be identified.”

He paused.

“Sending CSP alone into an unknown situation involving an A-class entity
and an unidentified organization would be an unnecessary risk.”

His eyes shifted toward the group.

“ESR’s involvement provides additional support if the situation exceeds
CSP’s containment capabilities.”

A moment of silence followed.

“Which is why I approved the request.”

General Hayes raised an eyebrow. “Director Morozov, are you certain that
was the only factor involved?”

The room became slightly quieter.

Morozov slowly turned toward him. “What are you implying?”

Before Hayes could answer, Madam Leni calmly interrupted.

“I am certain Director Morozov is capable of separating his personal and
professional responsibilities.”

She glanced toward him.

“His wife being the head of ESR has nothing to do with his approval of
this request.”

The room went silent.

I blinked.

Wait.

His wife?

I looked toward Morozov, then back toward Madam Leni, then toward
Morozov again.

Director Morozov…

The same man who looked like he had never experienced a positive emotion
in his entire existence…

was married?

Someone had actually looked at this cold, terrifying man and thought:

Yes. That’s the person I want to spend my life with.

I was still trying to process that information when I heard a quiet
sound from the other side of the room.

A snicker.

I slowly turned.

Jacob cleared his throat. “I apologize.”

He failed to sound even remotely apologetic.

Madam Leni continued as if nothing had happened.

“The head of ESR will meet you on-site.”

She looked toward me.

“Agent Nayeri.”

Then toward Jacob.

“Agent Jacob.”

There was a brief pause.

“Good luck.”

Something about the way she said those words made them sound less like a
farewell and more like a warning.

Instinctively, I looked toward Angelo.

He noticed immediately.

*“You appear concerned.”*

I sighed.

“CSP has dealt with a lot of impossible things. But ESR is different.
They don’t get involved unless something has already gone beyond what we
can handle.”

A pause.

“And now they’re sending their director.”

He considered my words for a moment.

*“Yes, I would also be concerned.”*

Somehow, that was both comforting and terrifying.

The next hour was spent in the preparation room. Armor, weapons,
briefings, emergency procedures. Everything CSP did before walking into
a situation where reality itself might decide to stop cooperating.

The only difference this time was that something about this mission felt
wrong.

I couldn’t explain it.

Maybe it was the cult. Maybe it was the A-class entity waiting for us.

Or maybe it was just instinct.

And my instincts were telling me this wasn’t going to end well.

When we finally boarded the transport, I found myself sitting silently
beside Angelo. He didn’t ask if I was okay because he already knew I
wasn’t.

Instead, he just sat beside me.

And somehow, that was enough.

Two hours later, we reached Jan Mayen Island.

The moment the aircraft doors opened, the cold hit us immediately.

The landscape stretched endlessly in every direction, covered in snow
and surrounded by mountains that disappeared into the clouds. There was
almost nothing here, nothing except the settlement waiting in the
distance.

Forty-five CSP agents stepped onto the ground with me.

Two hundred soldiers followed behind them.

Two hundred and forty-five people.

That was only CSP.

ESR had brought another one hundred and thirty soldiers, bringing the
total number of armed personnel to three hundred and seventy-five.

Three hundred and seventy-five people sent to confront a cult.

Some would call that excessive.

Honestly, I called it reassuring.

When we finally reached the site, the first thing I noticed was that it
wasn’t a settlement.

At least, not anymore.

The structure stood in the middle of the frozen landscape like something
that had been carved directly into the mountain itself, ancient and
unmoving against the endless snow. But it wasn’t alone.

Five additional structures surrounded it in a perfect circle, each one
positioned at an equal distance from the central building. From above,
the entire complex looked less like a village and more like some kind of
ritual symbol.

The central structure was the largest. The five surrounding buildings
were smaller, but somehow they were more unsettling.

Each one had a different design.

One was covered in strange markings carved deep into the stone. Another
had hundreds of small windows that appeared completely black from the
outside. A third looked almost like a temple, while the fourth appeared
industrial, built from metal and reinforced materials that didn’t match
the rest of the complex.

And the fifth…

I couldn’t explain what was wrong with the fifth one.

It looked normal.

That was the problem.

Everything else looked ancient and unnatural. That building looked like
someone had tried to recreate a normal home.

A fake imitation of something familiar.

The snow around the complex reached my ankles, forcing every step to
feel heavier than it should have. The wind moved through the mountains,
carrying a silence that felt unnatural.

Forty-five CSP agents moved carefully around the perimeter while the two
hundred soldiers established positions behind us. ESR’s forces remained
nearby, waiting for our arrival.

Nobody spoke louder than necessary.

Nobody wanted to disturb whatever was waiting inside.

I noticed one person who seemed completely unaffected by the
environment.

Angelo.

He stood beside me wearing his usual attire.

No coat.

No armor.

No protection from the freezing temperature.

His suit didn’t even appear bothered by the snow or wind.

*“Your temperature is slowly decreasing.”*

I looked over and found Angelo walking beside me.

I stared at him.

“…You are aware you’re saying that while standing outside in freezing
weather wearing only a suit?”

*“Yes.”*

“And somehow you’re worried about me?”

He looked genuinely confused.

*“Yes.”*

I sighed.

“Of course you are.”

For a moment, neither of us spoke.

Then his eyes shifted toward the snow covered mountains ahead.

*“This reminds me of the day we first met.”*

I immediately looked at him.

“The day we met?”

*“Yes.”*

A faint blush appeared before I could stop it.

Because of course he would choose now to bring that up.

While I was standing outside the cult headquarters with every instinct
in my body telling me to turn around and run, Angelo was remembering the
first time we met. 

“You realize I was terrified of you, right?”

*“Yes.”*

“And you’re bringing that up as a good memory?”

He looked at me.

*“It became one.”*

That caught me off guard.

For someone who struggled to understand emotions, Angelo had become
strangely good at finding the exact words that made me forget how afraid
I was.

I wasn’t sure whether to be annoyed or grateful.

The answer was probably both.

Behind us, Jacob walked over.

“Nayeri.”

I turned.

“What?”

“Do you think Noah will be okay?”

I stared at him.

“…Jacob.”

“What?”

“You have asked me that twelve times already”

“I know.”

“Why?”

“Because I am checking.”

He looked completely serious.

Somehow, Jacob had transformed into a worried mother who had left his
husband and child at home for the first time.

Except the child was a former fragment of the Great Shadow, and the
husband was an ancient being who carried both divine and angelic power. 

“Jacob, Noah is fine.”

“But Elior?”

I smiled.

“Oh, so you’re worried about your ‘Eli’?”

His face immediately changed as he looked away.

“….No”

“Really?”

“Yes.”

Jacob opened his mouth, then closed it.

Unfortunately for him, he had no argument.

I looked back toward the complex.

“I’m pretty sure Elior is taking care of him.”

Jacob was quiet for a moment.

Then, softer:

“…Good.”

For someone who claimed he wasn’t becoming friends with an angel, he was
surprisingly bad at hiding how much he cared.

Then, almost like he realized he had lost that argument, he quickly
changed the subject.

“Aren’t you nervous to meet the head?”

The question caught me off guard.

“The head of ESR?”

Jacob nodded.

“Yeah.”

For a moment, I forgot about everything else.

The mission.

The cult.

Because honestly, meeting the head of Eidolon Systems Research was
something I had been curious about long before today.

“Not nervous,” I said.

Jacob gave me a look.

“You hesitated.”

I ignored him.

The truth was, I had heard the rumors.

Everyone in CSP had.

The official records were limited, but stories spread quickly among
recruits. Nobody knew exactly what she was. Not who she was, but what
she was.

Some claimed she wasn’t human. Others claimed she never had been. The
only confirmed fact was that ESR hadn’t recruited her.

They had found her.

Because she had done something no ordinary human should have been
capable of.

She had killed a god alone.

The story was almost impossible to believe. A C-class entity had hidden
among humanity for years, disguised as a normal store. People entered
and disappeared, trapped inside the entity’s domain.

According to reports, she went in alone, and when ESR arrived, the god
was already dead.

I never knew how much of the story had been exaggerated. Every
organization had its legends, and every recruit had a habit of making
impossible things sound even more impossible.

But one fact remained.

The woman who now led ESR had eliminated a C-class god by herself.

I still remembered hearing about her during my first year as a recruit.
Back then, she had only recently become the head of ESR, and everyone
was talking about her.

At the time, I thought she was incredible.

“I always thought she was cool,” I admitted.

Jacob looked surprised.

“You?”

I looked at him. “What does that mean?”

“I don’t know. I just expected you to say something like, ‘She’s
probably terrifying, and I don’t trust her.’”

I frowned. “That sounds more like you.”

“Fair.”

I looked back toward the complex.

“She became the head of ESR almost three years ago. Since then, the
organization has remained under her leadership.”

Jacob nodded slowly.

“So basically, she’s like the Angelo of ESR.”

I immediately looked at him.

“That is probably the worst possible comparison.”

“Why?”

“Because now I’m imagining two people who can kill gods having a
meeting.”

Jacob thought about that for a moment.

“…Okay, that does sound terrifying.”

Before I could answer, a voice came through my communication channel.

“Agent Nayeri.”

I touched my earpiece.

“Yes?”

“ESR has arrived.”

The message paused briefly.

“Director Remi is requesting your presence.”

Jacob looked at me.

“Well.”

He adjusted his weapon.

“Guess we’re meeting the person who killed a god in a department store.”

I sighed.

“That sentence should not be normal.”

Jacob smiled slightly.

“Yet somehow, it is.”

I looked toward Angelo. He was already watching the entrance of the
complex, his expression unreadable as usual. But when he noticed me
looking at him, something in his expression softened slightly.

That was when I noticed movement behind us.

A group was approaching through the snow.

At first, I assumed it was another security team arriving at the
perimeter. Then I realized how many people were actually moving toward
us.

More than a hundred soldiers marched forward in perfect formation behind
a single person, their footsteps sinking into the snow as they advanced
through the freezing wind.

And at the front of the formation was a woman.

She wasn’t wearing heavy armor.

She wasn’t carrying any visible weapon.

She simply walked through the freezing wind as if the cold had no
authority over her.

There was something different about her presence.

It wasn’t the overwhelming pressure that came from beings like Angelo or
Elior. They carried the weight of something ancient, something beyond
human understanding.

She didn’t.

Her presence was quieter.

Controlled.

Like someone who had spent years learning exactly how much of herself
she was allowed to show the world.

The soldiers stopped several meters away, creating a clear path through
the snow.

The woman continued forward alone.

I watched her carefully, trying to match the person in front of me with
the stories I had heard since my first year as a recruit.

The head of Eidolon Systems Research.

Director Remi.

She was still too far away for me to see her clearly. The distance, the
snow, and the harsh wind blurred her features enough that I couldn’t
make out the details of her face.

Maybe that was better.

Maybe some part of me needed those extra few seconds before I saw the
truth.

She stopped in front of Director Stonehill and gave a respectful nod.

“Director Stonehill.”

“Director Remi.”

Their exchange was brief.

Professional.

Exactly what I expected from two people who had spent years operating in
a world where emotions were often a liability.

Then her attention shifted.

Her eyes moved across the gathered agents.

Over Jacob.

Over the soldiers.

Over Angelo.

And finally…

they stopped on me.

For a moment, I forgot how to breathe.

Because even through the distance, even after all these years, there was
something I recognized.

Not the uniform.

Not the title.

Not the reputation.

Her.

The person standing in front of me wasn’t a stranger.

She was someone whose existence had shaped my childhood.

Someone whose words had stayed with me long after she was gone.

My sister.

Remi.

But she didn’t react.

No widening of her eyes. No hesitation. No sign that she recognized me.
Nothing.

Her expression remained calm and professional. Like I was nothing more
than another agent standing in front of her.

And somehow, that hurt more than I expected.

“Agent Nayeri,” she said.

Her voice was polite.

Neutral.

Like we had never met.

Like I had never existed.

I forced myself to respond.

“Director Remi.”

The words felt strange coming out of my mouth because everyone around us
saw the head of ESR.

A legendary agent.

Someone who had killed a god.

But I saw something else.

I saw the girl who used to hold my hand when I was afraid. The person
who promised she would always come back.

And the person I spent years believing chose not to.

Remi studied me briefly before looking away.

Then she turned toward the structure.

“I assume everyone has been briefed.”

Stonehill nodded.

“Yes.”

“Then we should begin.”

Her voice remained calm and focused, like this was just another mission.

Fine.

If she wanted to look at me like I was a stranger, then I could do the
same. I could pretend we’d never met. Pretend she hadn’t been the person
I’d spent years searching for.

The entrance to the complex was colder than I expected. 

Not because of the temperature.

Because of what waited beyond it.

The massive doors had been carved directly into the side of the
mountain, covered in symbols none of the researchers had been able to
identify. They weren’t written in any known language, yet every linguist
who examined them agreed on one thing.

They were instructions.

Not warnings.

Instructions.

Whatever had been built here wasn’t designed to keep something out.

It was designed to invite something in.

The teams moved forward slowly. CSP agents took the lead, ESR followed
behind them, and soldiers secured the perimeter.

And in the middle of it all walked Director Remi.

My sister.

The thought still felt impossible.

I kept expecting her to turn around. To realize something.

Anything.

But she didn’t.

She treated me exactly like everyone else.

An agent.

A stranger.

Nothing more.

And somehow, that hurt worse than anger would have.

If she hated me, at least that meant she remembered me.

But there was nothing. No resentment. No sadness. No guilt.

Just nothing.

We entered the first chamber.

The inside of the structure was even stranger than the outside. The
walls were covered in carvings depicting people standing beneath
something enormous.

Not worshipping it.

Offering themselves to it.

The researchers moved carefully through the chamber, taking photographs
and scanning the area. Nobody spoke unless necessary.

Except Jacob.

Because apparently silence was something he considered a personal enemy.

“So…”

I looked at him.

“What?”

He glanced toward Remi.

“She always this serious?”

I frowned.

“You’ve known her for five minutes.”

“Exactly. That’s enough time to judge someone’s personality.”

“That explains a lot about your decision making.”

Jacob ignored that.

Before I could respond, a sudden wave of dust moved through the
corridor.

I covered my mouth.

Then sneezed.

“Bless you, little bug.”

I froze.

The words weren’t loud or dramatic, but I heard them.

Everyone around us probably heard them.

Slowly, I looked up.

Remi was standing a few feet away.

Her expression had changed.

Not the calm, professional expression she had worn since arriving.

Something else.

Something almost like surprise.

Like she hadn’t realized what she had said.

Her eyes met mine.

For a moment, neither of us moved.

Then her expression shifted.

“I’m sorry.”

The apology came immediately.

“I don’t know why I said that.”

My chest tightened.

Because somehow, that was worse.

I stared at her.

She looked genuinely confused.

Genuinely apologetic.

And all I could feel was anger.

Because to me, it sounded like she was mocking me. Like she had walked
back into my life pretending not to know me, and then somehow found the
one thing that could hurt me most.

The nickname only she used.

The one she had called me when I was little.

“Right.”

My voice came out colder than I intended.

“Of course.”

Remi frowned slightly.

“Agent Nayeri—”

“No.”

I looked away.

“Don’t.”

She stopped.

And for the first time, I saw something flicker across her face.

Confusion.

Not anger.

Not offense.

Confusion.

That somehow made me even more frustrated because she didn’t even
understand why I was hurt.

I turned and continued walking, but my breathing had already started
changing.

My chest felt tight.

The walls felt closer.

Not again.

Not here.

Not now.

*“Nayeri.”*

Angelo’s voice immediately caught my attention.

I looked over.

He was watching me carefully.

*“Come with me.”*

“I am fine.”

*“No.”*

His answer was immediate.

*“You are not.”*

I wanted to argue, but I couldn’t.

Because Angelo knew me too well.

He gently placed a hand on my shoulder and guided me a few steps away
from the group.

Jacob followed.

Once we were far enough away, he looked between us.

*“Okay.”*

He lowered his voice.

*“Am I missing something?”*

I didn’t answer.

Jacob looked at me then back toward Remi.

My jaw tightened.

“I don’t know her.”

Jacob blinked.

“She literally just called you little bug.”

“That doesn’t mean anything.”

“Nayeri.”

I looked away.

“She is pretending.”

The words came out quieter than I expected.

Both of them went silent.

Angelo’s expression changed slightly.

Not understanding.

Concern.

*“What do you mean?”*

I swallowed and looked back toward the woman standing with the ESR
agents.

The woman everyone else saw as a legend.

The woman I saw as my family.

“She is pretending not to know who I am.”

Jacob frowned.

“Nayeri….who is she?”

I closed my eyes.

A pause.

“She’s my sister.”

For once, Jacob had no joke.

No sarcastic comment.

Nothing.

Angelo remained completely still.

“My older sister.”

The words felt heavier when I finally said them aloud.

“Remi.”

I looked toward her again.

“She was the person who raised me.”

Jacob looked back at Remi, then at me.

A silence followed.

Angelo looked at me carefully.

Then his expression changed.

Not shock.

Not disbelief.

Understanding.

*“Now it makes sense.”*

I looked at him.

“What?”

My voice came out louder than I intended.

Angelo didn’t look away from Remi.

*“Her soul.”*

The words made both Jacob and me go quiet.

“What about it?” Jacob asked.

Angelo stared at her from across the chamber.

*“It is missing a piece.”*

Jacob frowned.

“What do you mean?”

Angelo was silent for a moment, as if trying to explain something that
was obvious to him but impossible for humans to understand.

*“Humans are not made of one thing.”*

Angelo’s eyes shifted back toward me. *“The body is only one part. The
mind is another.”* He looked toward Remi again, his expression becoming
distant. *“And the soul… the soul is where the pieces that define
someone are connected.”*

I swallowed as he continued.

*“When I first encountered humanity, I did not understand why you
changed so easily. Why memories altered you. Why could loss reshape
you.”* His voice softened slightly. *“Then I learned.”*

He looked at Jacob.

*“A soul is not a container holding memories. It is the reason those
memories matter.* A*nd when enough of those pieces are removed…”* His
eyes met mine. *“…the person remains, but something feels wrong.”*

Jacob looked toward Remi. “So you’re saying…”

Angelo nodded slowly. *“She is still herself.”*

A pause followed.

*“But she is incomplete.”*

My chest tightened because suddenly the anger became harder to hold
onto. If Angelo was right, then Remi wasn’t pretending. She wasn’t
cruel. She wasn’t choosing to forget me.

Something was missing.

Something that used to be there.

And somehow…

I was the missing piece.

Nobody spoke after Angelo’s explanation because before any of us could
begin to process what he had said, the radio on General Hayes’ shoulder
crackled to life.

“Perimeter Team Three to command.”

Static filled the corridor.

Then a voice came through.

“…Movement.”

Every rifle in the chamber rose immediately.

The silence that followed felt unnatural. Even the wind outside seemed
to disappear.

Stonehill raised a hand. “Advance.”

The formation tightened instantly. CSP agents moved to the front while
ESR spread along the sides of the corridor with practiced precision.
Soldiers followed behind us, rifles aimed toward the darkness ahead.

Our footsteps echoed against the stone as we moved deeper into the
complex. One step after another, the sound followed us through the
corridor until finally, the passage opened into an enormous circular
chamber.

I stopped.

There were people.

Hundreds of them.

Men, women, and children all dressed in identical white robes stood
perfectly still in concentric circles surrounding a massive stone altar
carved into the center of the room.

Not one of them looked frightened.

Not one of them tried to run.

They were simply waiting.

There were no whispers. No prayers. No movement.

Only hundreds of eyes staring directly at us.

The silence made my skin crawl.

General Hayes raised his rifle.

“CSP!”

His voice echoed throughout the chamber.

“This area is under containment authority. Step away from the altar and
place your hands where we can see them.”

Nobody moved.

Not one person.

Then, without warning, every single cultist smiled.

The exact same smile appeared across hundreds of faces at the same time.

A cold shiver ran down my spine.

An elderly woman standing closest to the altar tilted her head.

“We’ve been expecting you.”

Her voice wasn’t loud, but somehow every person in the chamber repeated
the words.

“We’ve been expecting you.”

Hundreds of voices spoke as one, the sound echoing through the mountain.

Jacob whispered beside me, “…I officially hate this place.”

Then every cultist slowly knelt.

Not out of fear.

Out of devotion.

They lowered their heads toward the altar.

“We welcome the servants of the false light.”

Nobody understood what that meant.

Not until every cultist looked up at the same time.

Their eyes were completely black.

“OPEN FIRE!”

Hayes didn’t hesitate.

The chamber erupted.

Three hundred rifles roared simultaneously, muzzle flashes illuminating
the darkness as thousands of rounds tore through the front ranks of
cultists. Bodies collapsed across the stone floor, and the smell of
gunpowder quickly filled the chamber.

For a moment, silence returned.

Then one of the bodies stood.

The hole through its chest remained. Blood covered its robes. Yet
somehow, it pushed itself back onto its feet.

Then another.

Then another.

Within seconds, every cultist who had fallen slowly rose again.

Some were missing arms. Some had half their faces destroyed. One woman
calmly reached down, picked up her own severed hand, and carried it with
her as she continued walking toward us.

“They’re still moving!” someone shouted.

The chamber exploded into chaos.

The first cultist reached the front line before anyone could react.

He didn’t throw a punch.

He didn’t attack.

He embraced the soldier.

“Thank you.”

The man’s body immediately began glowing.

“GET HIM OFF!”

It was already too late.

The cultist smiled.

Then he detonated.

The explosion launched three soldiers backward, sending fragments of
stone raining across the chamber.

“They’re sacrificing themselves!” Jacob yelled.

Another wave charged forward.

This time, they weren’t trying to survive.

Every single one of them was trying to die as close to us as possible.

“Fall back!”

“No!”

Remi’s voice cut through the chaos.

“If they reach the formation, they’ll collapse the corridor.”

She stepped forward before anyone could stop her.

The first cultist lunged.

She caught his wrist and twisted, the sound of bone breaking echoing
through the chamber. With one impossibly fluid movement, she drove him
into the ground before he could ignite, shattering the stone beneath
him.

The second rushed her.

She sidestepped.

One strike.

The cultist dropped instantly.

Another attacker came from behind, but Remi moved before he even reached
her. Every movement was controlled, efficient, and precise.

The woman who had once sung me to sleep whenever I was afraid was now
standing in front of a god-worshipping army, fighting like someone who
had spent her entire life preparing for this exact moment.

“…Holy—”

Jacob never finished the sentence.

None of us had ever seen someone fight like that. Not even CSP’s best
instructors. She wasn’t stronger than everyone else. She was simply
better. Every movement had a purpose, every strike landed exactly where
it needed to, and she never wasted a single motion. She didn’t overpower
her opponents. She dismantled them.

One cultist managed to slip past her and reached toward a wounded
soldier lying on the ground. Before anyone else could move, a blur of
black crossed the chamber.

Angelo.

He caught the cultist by the head before he could reach the soldier.

“You have surrendered your ability to choose.”

There was no anger in his voice. Only sadness.

He gently placed a hand over the cultist’s forehead, and golden light
spread through the man’s body. For one brief moment, the blackness
vanished from his eyes. He looked around in confusion, like someone
waking from a nightmare they couldn’t remember entering.

Then tears rolled down his face.

“…Thank…”

His voice never finished the word.

His body dissolved into light before he could say anything else.

Angelo stood motionless.

He hadn’t killed him.

He had freed him.

But there was no time to think about that because more cultists poured
into the chamber. Hundreds of them emerged from every corridor, every
staircase, every doorway, moving forward with the same empty devotion.

They weren’t defending the temple.

They were feeding it.

The blood from the fallen began flowing through grooves carved into the
stone floor, moving toward the altar at the center of the chamber. The
symbols covering the walls and floor ignited one after another, filling
the room with an unnatural glow.

The mountain trembled.

Then something beneath the altar moved.

Every surviving cultist smiled.

“Our God…”

Hundreds of voices echoed together.

“…is waking.”

The altar cracked once.

Then again.

The fractures spread slowly across the ancient stone, and black light
leaked through them like veins beneath skin. It wasn’t an explosion. It
sounded more like a long, exhausted breath, as though something buried
beneath the mountain had finally accepted that it was time to wake.

The chamber fell silent.

Even the surviving cultists stopped moving.

Black mist poured from the opening beneath the altar, spilling across
the floor before rising into the shape of a towering figure.

He stood nearly twenty feet tall.

Ancient chains made of black stone wrapped around every part of his
body. They weren’t restraints forged around him after his capture. They
had become part of him.

Some disappeared into the floor beneath the altar. Others stretched
backward into the darkness behind him, extending impossibly deep into
the mountain itself. Every link pulsed with the same rhythm as the
symbols carved across his skin.

His head remained lowered.

Motionless.

Patient.

Like he had forgotten what freedom felt like.

One by one, the surviving cultists fell to their knees.

“Our God…”

“Our Shepherd…”

“Our Salvation…”

The figure slowly raised his head.

There was no rage waiting in his eyes. No hunger. No hatred.

Only exhaustion.

An exhaustion so profound it almost looked painful.

The researchers reacted immediately.

“A-Class entity confirmed!”

General Hayes didn’t hesitate.

“All units, engage!”

The order had barely left his mouth before the figure vanished.

The crack of the sound barrier splitting the chamber was deafening.

Half the front line disappeared beneath a single swing of his arm.
Concrete erupted into the air as armored soldiers were launched through
solid stone walls as if they weighed nothing. The shockwave alone
knocked dozens more from their feet.

“RETURN FIRE!”

Hundreds of rifles answered.

The chamber became a storm of muzzle flashes as thousands of rounds
slammed into the giant’s body.

None penetrated.

They didn’t ricochet.

They didn’t flatten.

They simply ceased to exist.

It was as if the space occupied by his body rejected the very idea of
being struck.

Then Remi moved.

She crossed the chamber so quickly I barely saw her leave the ground.
Her fist collided with the giant’s arm with enough force to fracture the
stone beneath the giant’s feet.

For the first time…

he staggered.

Jacob’s eyes widened.

“…She actually moved him.”

The giant looked down at her.

Something changed.

His expression softened slightly.

“…Forgive me.”

His voice was barely louder than a whisper.

Then every chain wrapped around his body tightened at once. The sound of
grinding stone echoed through the chamber as the symbols carved into his
skin ignited.

And for the first time since he awakened, the exhausted expression
disappeared.

Something ancient looked back at us.

Something that had been waiting.

Agony twisted across his face as his arm rose against his will. Remi
never had the chance to react before Angelo appeared between them. The
giant’s fist collided with him instead, and the impact shook the entire
mountain. A wall behind us collapsed as the shockwave ripped through the
chamber, throwing soldiers across the floor and shattering every
remaining window in the surrounding structures.

Angelo didn’t move.

Not even a step.

The giant stared at him, his eyes widening. “…You…”

Angelo remained silent. The giant’s breathing became uneven as another
chain wrapped tighter around his throat. He collapsed onto one knee,
clawing desperately at the black links cutting into his flesh.

“…Run…”

Blood spilled from the corner of his mouth.

“I…”

The chains glowed brighter.

His body convulsed violently as every muscle fought against an invisible
command. He looked back at Angelo, not with hostility, but with
desperation.

“I am…”

His voice broke.

“…not acting…”

The chains tightened again.

“…of…”

Every word sounded as though it was being torn from him.

“…my…”

He screamed.

Not in anger.

In pain.

“…free…”

The chamber shook.

“…will.”

Silence followed.

The chains constricted one final time, and the symbols covering his body
erupted with black light. When he looked up again, the exhaustion was
gone. The desperation was gone. Whatever person had been fighting for
control only moments earlier had disappeared.

Only the weapon remained.

Angelo stepped forward.

“You are bound.”

The giant stared at him.

“…Yes.”

The answer came without emotion.

“By whom?”

For several seconds, nothing happened. Then every chain wrapped around
the giant’s body suddenly pulled tight.

Not downward.

Forward.

The giant’s head slowly turned toward the passage beyond the shattered
altar.

Someone was pulling him.

Nobody moved.

Nobody spoke.

Then a voice drifted through the darkness.

Soft. Warm. Almost amused. 

Mocking, even. 

A voice I knew.

A voice I had spent years trying to forget.

A voice that had followed me into my nightmares.

The same voice that had spent my childhood reminding me that I was never
enough.

Never good enough.

Never worthy of being loved.

**“I didn’t know we were having a family reunion today.”**
