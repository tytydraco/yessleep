Once I worked as a taxi driver in a small town. It wasn’t a particularly
exciting job, but I actually liked it. I knew most of the other drivers,
and we had this little unofficial meeting spot at a gas station just
outside the town center. Whenever things got slow, we’d park our cars
there, grab some coffee, smoke a cigarette, and talk about whatever was
going on in our lives. Work, family, annoying passengers, the usual
stuff.

I usually worked during the day, but sometimes I’d take night shifts
because they paid better. Anyone who’s worked nights knows how
exhausting they can get, especially when there’s barely any traffic and
you’re just driving around waiting for another booking. This happened
one night in August. It was around 3 AM, and I was struggling to keep my
eyes open. My fuel was running low too, so I decided to stop at our
usual gas station, fill up the tank, get some coffee, and take a break
before heading back out.

When I pulled in, I noticed another car parked a little farther away
from the pumps. It was a gray Chevrolet. I didn’t recognize it, but that
wasn’t unusual. There were plenty of drivers in town I didn’t know
personally, and the guy had parked in pretty much the same spot where my
friends and I usually hung out. I went inside, bought a coffee and a
pack of cigarettes, and came back out. The driver was standing next to
his car, so I figured I’d say hello.

“Hey, man. How’s your night going?” He smiled and said it was going
alright. We started chatting, just making small talk about work and how
quiet the roads were. He seemed like a completely normal guy, maybe in
his thirties or forties. Nothing about him struck me as strange. At some
point, he asked if he could have a cigarette. I gave him one, and we
stood there smoking together.

Then he said something that I didn’t really think much about at the
time. “Pretty cold out here, isn’t it?” I remember looking at him, a
little confused. It was August, and the weather had been warm all
evening. I was standing there in a T-shirt and felt perfectly
comfortable. “Not really,” I said. “Feels pretty warm to me.” He looked
at me for a second and replied, “Well, for you, I guess.”

I laughed a little because I thought he was just joking. Maybe he was
sick or something. I didn’t ask. We talked for another couple of minutes
before I finished my cigarette, said I’d see him around, and got back
into my car. He nodded and wished me a good night. That was it. Just a
completely ordinary conversation at a gas station.

A few days later, I was scrolling through local news on my phone when I
came across an article about a fatal car accident. A driver had lost
control of his vehicle and crashed. He’d died at the scene. There was a
photo of the wrecked car. A gray Chevrolet. I stared at the picture for
what felt like forever. I couldn’t remember the license plate from that
night, but I recognized the car. The same model, the same color. Even
the wheels looked familiar.

Then I checked when the accident had happened. It was in the evening,
several hours before I’d stopped at the gas station.I never told the
other drivers what happened. I knew how ridiculous it sounded, and I
wasn’t even sure what I believed myself. Sometimes I still think about
that conversation.
