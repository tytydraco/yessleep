My life changed for the worse this past week. I saw things I cannot
unsee. I never wanted to find what was in those woods. It wasn’t
something I was meant to see. Now I’m here, doing my best to describe
it. 

It seemed like a normal day. I worked at my job at the gas station. I
worked during the daytime, and got off at 5. This was the first day I
worked after the clocks were turned forward for daylight savings time,
the beginning of November. There was a fall chill in the air, and the
leaves were falling from the trees by the thousands. 

I drove to the local gym after my shift. It was dark on my way there,
when it hadn’t been the previous week. I did a quick lift, I think it
was legs that day. After I lifted I decided to run. I love running. It’s
one of the most consistent hobbies I’ve kept up through my life. Not
that that’s saying much, I’m young and have only been running since high
school. I really didn’t like running on the treadmill though. This fall,
I decided that I was going to quit the treadmill once and for all. 

I brought a flashlight with me that day. It was actually a really nice
one. When I used it in a dark room, it lit up the entire thing. This was
my companion on my run. I was going to be running on trails at a park
about half a mile away from the gym I went to. It wasn’t quite the
middle of nowhere, but it sure felt like it. 

The time was only around 5:30 when I left. It was almost totally dark
out. The last gasp of the day was fading quickly into the night. I put
my earbuds in and started running on the road to get to the park. The
road to the park was paved, and well maintained. It made for a good
running surface. 

After about 8 minutes of running, I made it to the entrance to the
trail. The park was in a valley, and the darkness was so much thicker
here. I shined the flashlight further down the road, and could only see
a sharp turn sign a bit further away. The way the light danced on it and
then dissipated into the inky abyss that lay ahead of me gave me the
creeps. 

I took a breath and said, “Well, here I go.” 

The trails at this park are quite rocky. It isn’t hard to run on them in
the day, but at night, it’s a whole different ball game. I tripped a
couple times in the first few minutes I was on the trail. 

As I ran, I eventually came across a bridge. I let the flashlight fall
on it, and across I went. After I crossed, I saw something strange in a
tree ahead of me. I looked at it and realized it was some kind of
sweatshirt someone had lost. It was blue. I thought it was weird, but I
was also in my own head because of just how dark it was. It almost felt
unnatural.

The further I jogged, the worse my mental state got. The trees appeared
to reach out to try and grab me through the darkness. Their leafless
branches were like uncanny long arms. I could see maybe ten feet ahead
of me. 

Eventually I reached the bottom of the valley, where there was a parking
lot, and a small building used for storage with a single security light
outside of it. As I ran through the parking lot, I thought I saw someone
move near the building. I just assumed it was my imagination, or a
maintenance person finishing up their shift. 

At this point I could feel an unnatural adrenaline, but I still needed
to make some distance on my run, so I followed the road connected to the
parking lot. It went further into the woods. It felt like I was being
pulled deeper in. There was a gate across the road that let small
vehicles and people through. There was a sign that read “motorized
vehicles prohibited, no winter maintenance.” This just meant that the
road was pretty dilapidated, but still very suitable for walking or
running. 

The first thing I noticed was the smell. Something smelled bad. Like,
really bad. I never smelled anything as bad as it. It was like roadkill
but worse. It was so bad I could barely hold the flashlight. I dropped
the flashlight for a second to cover my nose, and for a brief moment I
felt something touch the back of my neck. It was a weird, electrifying
feeling. It hurt the slightest bit, and then as soon as it came on, it
was gone. 

I was freaked out at this point. The darkness felt as if it was
consuming me whole, and I swore I could hear whispers in the shadows. I
turned around to start to head back. I saw the trailhead and ran to it.
In my mind I thought it was too close and could have been a wrong turn,
but I was scared and ignored that fact.

As I went further and further into the forest, my flashlight seemed to
become dimmer and dimmer. I kept jogging along, when I noticed the smell
again. It was pungent, and stronger than before. It was like the further
I went on the trail, the worse the smell got. Eventually, my flashlight
flickered and died. It was dark, I was cold, and alone. I took a few
more steps, thinking of what to do.

That’s when I felt it. I stepped on something soft. It was crushed under
my foot with a squelch. Some kind of warm liquid seeped into my shoe. It
was too thick to be water. A part of me knew what it was without even
looking. I still looked down though. I pointed the dead flashlight at
what I just stepped in, and suddenly it flickered a few times.

What I stepped on, no, who I stepped on shook me to my very core. In the
flickers of light I saw myself. My throat was torn out, and my face
contorted in a scream that never happened. I felt my throat go dry, and
I ran.

I ran for what felt like hours. The flashlight came back to life after I
saw the horror of the woods. I ran and ran and ran. I ran until every
breath was gone from my body. My lungs burned like they were full of
glass. It hurt, and I cried. It might have been from relief or stress. I
still don’t really know.

I filed a police report about it hours after. They told me they would
look into it, but they told me that the trail I was describing didn’t
exist. There was no trail where I found the body. 

They searched the entire area, and found nothing. The body I described
was gone. The trail was gone. That shed I saw was real though. The cops
said there was evidence of someone breaking into it on the night I
experienced the incident. Cameras were deemed unnecessary in that part
of the park, so no one was caught for it. 

It didn’t make sense to me. I knew what I saw that night. The image will
never leave my mind. I don’t think we’re supposed to see ourselves dead.
I am probably the only person in the world who has.
