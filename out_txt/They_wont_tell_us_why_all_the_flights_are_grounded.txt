Airports are not the sort of place you should spend the night. They are
places of transit, of in-between. Liminal.

Even while stuck with a flight delayed or a particularly long layover,
the airport is merely an annoying stop on your route to somewhere else.
The mind has already reached its destination, stressing over all the
things it must do—the in-laws to woo, the business conference to
prepare, the spouse to reassure that those FaceTimes you cut short were
really because you were “tired” from “working hard.”

The point is, the mind has already moved on, only occasionally looking
back irately at the body that as of yet, lacks the wings to catch up.

An airport is no place to linger.

We learned that the hard way one terrible, terrible night.

***

“Cancelled! What do you mean by cancelled?”

The customer service agent flinches at my outburst, finally dropping
that sycophantic grin every employee here seems obligated to have.
“I–I’m sorry sir, but your flight has been c–cancelled.”

The young woman is barely able to get the words out. I might have felt a
little bad about that—ashamed even—if I hadn’t already been stuck in
this hellhole for nine hours.

I feel Elise squeeze my hand in message to ease up, but I brush her off.
My fiancé is a gentle soul, but if there’s anything I learned in the
business world, if you want results, sometimes you have to get your
hands dirty.

“Listen, miss…” I look for the agent’s name tag, see that she’s not even
wearing one. Of course. “…just transfer us to the nearest flight. Hell,
it can be to a neighboring state at this point. I’d rather drive 6 hours
then spend one more minute here. Now make it happen.”

“I’m sorry, sir. I should have been clear. All the flights have been
cancelled.”

I blink. I can see her lips moving, can tell that something is coming
out of them, but clearly I misheard her. After hours in the Tucson sun,
my hair gel must have sweated down into my ears and clogged them right
up. “What do you mean by ‘all’.”

The agent smiles and for the first time, I get the feeling it’s genuine.
“As I said, sir, all planes have been grounded until further notice.”

“Now wait a damn minute. What in the world is going on in this place?”

“I’m sorry, sir. The order just came in.”

“Benjamin,” Elise says softly, but I’m busy at the moment.

“Then when are the flights going to be uncancelled?”

“I’m sorry, sir. I don’t know.”

“Benjamin…” Elise nudges me. I ignore her.

“Then bring me someone that does!”

“I’m sorry, sir. We—”

“Bennie!”

I glance at Elise who’s pointing somewhere off to our left. At a
neighboring gate, a red-faced man yells at a gate agent, loudly enough
that I can hear that it’s about his flight agent. Then I look over to
the gate beyond that one and see a similar scene. And to my right. And
behind me.

Everywhere I turn I see a scene similar to my own interaction with the
gate agent, mirrored, multiplied. Confused and angry customers screaming
at the stoic airline employees, who bear it all with practiced smiles
that don’t reach their eyes.

But that’s not what Elise is trying to show me. She’s pointing to the
giant flight display. Our flight, Tucson to St. Louis: cancelled. The
flight highlighted in red. And under that, Tucson to Dallas: cancelled.
Tucson to Denver: Cancelled. Denver to Tuscon: cancelled. Cancelled
cancelled cancelled cancelled. The whole screen is highlighted in eye
searing red, the same color as a STOP sign as if the very airport itself
is yelling “CANCELLED.”

And if that’s not subtle enough, the intercom crackles to life. “Due to
a systems-wide malfunction, all flights have been cancelled. All
incoming and outbound planes have been grounded until the issue is
resolved. We apologize for the inconvenience.”

Somehow hearing the clipped message, delivered universally through the
airports tinny speakers, drives the reality home and everyone seems to
deflate, stop harassing the employees, as their minds are suddenly
forced to make a change in plans.

I feel stroking my arm through the sweat-drenched sleeve, and Elise puts
on a reassuring smile. “Let’s find ourselves a seat before they’re all
taken.”

***

Too late.

Somehow every chair in this brutalist sprawl they call an airport is
already filled. I know it’s the holiday season, but still. Even the
barstools at the overpriced burger joints are all taken.

Some people had resigned themselves to the floor, huddled around
outlets, the more germ conscious among them laying on coats and towels.
To pass the time, some have even lied down for a nap. And it’s
specifically those fools that are the problem, stretching themselves out
across several chairs, preventing anyone else from sitting.

“Hey dingus!” I call out to one such fellow, taking up four seats with
their prone form contorted through the armrests. “Make some room!”

The figure doesn’t move. They’re facing the seatback, away from me, with
their thick hoodie pulled up. I can’t even tell if it’s a man or an
especially tall woman under those baggy sweats.

“Hey! You hear me?” I call out again. The figure doesn’t even twitch.
Are they drunk or something?

I’m about to shake them awake when Elise pulls me back. “Bennie! What
are you doing? This isn’t like you.” Her expression is both concerned
and a little annoyed, like I’m the problem here.

“Are you kidding me?” I say. “This whole trip is a disaster! Your
parents are already expecting us—” again, Elise twitches whenever I
bring them up “—and at this rate, we might not even make it for
Thanksgiving. And then what then? If we’re not at your parent’s house
then where are we staying tonight? It’s already past nine and all the
hotels around here are booked—I checked, and if I’m going to spend the
night at the damn airport, then I at least won’t be spending it on this
dirty ass floor!”

I’m panting for breath by the end of my rant, dripping sweat stinging my
eyes. Of all the places to be stuck, it had to be Arizona—possibly the
only state in the whole country that’s still pushing 80° in November.

I must look a mess right now, and frankly, Elise isn’t much better. Her
dark curls have frizzed up and her makeup is noticeably piecework from
dashing to the ladies’ room every half hour, a Sisyphean struggle to
salvage her foundation from the heat and sweat. She looks like the bride
of Frankenstein. My bride. I chuckle, nervously twisting my ring when
she still doesn’t respond.

After an uncomfortably long moment, she says, “We should talk somewhere
quiet.”

***

As we tramp our way through the terminal, I realize my earlier
comparison is more apt than I gave it credit. The airport really does
feel like hell.

The heat for one thing, no longer due to the Arizona sun which set hours
ago, but wafting off the seething masses we find ourselves squeezing
past. The mass of bodies around us create a miasma of funk and body heat
that truly makes for a Dante-esque experience. There’s just so many
people.

I know that sounds silly—airports are known to be busy, especially
around the holidays—but it’s never been like this before. Airports are
crowded, yes, but those crowds usually move. A constant foot traffic.
People go in, people leave out. Like ventilation. A window with a
cross-breeze.

It’s only now, when there’s nowhere to go, no flight to catch, that you
realize how stifling it can be. So many people, all in the same place,
left to stew. Stew in the heat. Stew in their thoughts. Two square acres
of steel and glass, stewing. Like the world’s biggest pressure cooker.

I can feel it in the air, that…something. Not quite rage but far past
mere impatience. I see it in the man yelling at his wife, heedless of
their child just feet away bawling her eyes out. I see it in the fist
fight starting at the bar to our left, the men’s cheeks so flushed from
heat you can’t even see the blood. TSA is out in full force, but they’re
barely stemming the tide.

While I was losing my head over some drunk, was this what Elise was
seeing? I can’t think of anything better than getting the heck away from
it all.

After twenty minutes of searching, we end up finding this reprieve near
baggage claim, the serpentine carousel currently empty as everyone’s
luggage is presumably still trapped on the planes. That’s probably why
the place is deserted. Even the lights here are dimmed, the brightest
illumination coming from the moon streaming through the wall-length
windows.

We park ourselves within the S-shaped curve of the baggage carousel.
Without the usual rattle of luggage, the conveyor’s motors whirrs
softly. Almost soothing after the madness of the day. I find each
eyeblink lasting longer than the last, and Elise looks like she could
nod off herself.

If I let her.

“You wanted to talk.”

Elise perks up at my voice. “We can wait until tomorrow.”

“No, I think it’s better to do this now.” I butt-scoot closer to her on
the thin carpet. “I apologize for how I acted earlier. The heat had me
going a little crazy, for a minute there. But this whole fiasco with the
planes grounded, that’s kind of just the straw that broke the camel’s
back. Because even before that…I had a feeling you don’t want me to meet
your parents.”

Elise’s face is inscrutable. I can’t tell whether it’s a yes or a no in
the dim lighting.

“I just don’t get it! I mean we’re engaged now! Don’t you want them to
see who you’re about to marry? Or you think they’ll say I’m not good
enough?” As I talk, I notice this time that she didn’t flinch at the
mention of her parents. It was at the word engaged.

My heart sinks. “Elise…do you not want to get married?”

“You know I love you, Bennie,” she starts. “I’ve enjoyed every moment
that we’ve spent together.” But? “But…I don’t know if marriage is the
right choice for me–us.”

“What are you getting at?”

“I mean what I said. I’ve loved the time we’ve known each other. And I’m
reluctant to change.”

“I don’t understand. We’ll still be together. Nothing will change.”

“No. We’ll be married.”

I blink. Try to make sense of it all. “I mean, isn’t that how it’s
supposed to go? You fall in love, you get married, you have…” Kids.
Crap, we never really talked about that before.

Elise turns away from me. “Would it be so bad not to be rushing off to
some destination all the time? To…I don’t know, smell the roses?”

“Are you saying you want to call off the engagement?”

“I don’t know. Maybe just the trip. We don’t have to see my parents yet.
We can stay here.”

“Here? In Tucson? But we don’t know anyone here.”

“Exactly! It would be like an adventure.”

“All the hotels are booked.”

“The airport offers suits to its platinum members. We can upgrade, have
Thanksgiving here. We’ve never done something like that before.” Her
voice rises in excitement, a sparkle entering her eye. “Oh it will be
just like when we first started dating. Remember? We used to go out and
do new things all the time. Doesn’t that sound so much better than being
a boring old married couple. We don’t have to be like that. We can have
Thanksgiving right here!”

I don’t know what to say, with Elise hanging off me like this, eyes so
full of hope. Hope leaving them with every second that I don’t say yes.
I hate letting her down, but it’s just too much. I can’t decide.

“I’ll think about it,” I say before rolling over so I don’t have to see
her disappointment.

***

Impossibly, at some point I fell asleep, right on the airport floor. And
when I awake, it’s to the sound of my own brain rattling inside its
skull.

At first, I believe it’s a migraine, but getting up from the carousel
from which I had rested on abates the vibrations, mercifully enough.
Though the noise of it still batters my ear drums. God, why is it so
loud? Was it always this loud and I was just too tired to notice?

I notice Elise in similar straights, palm pressed to her forehead,
blinking the sleep from her eyes. Then she stops, her gaze hooked onto
something behind me.

The baggage carousel isn’t so empty anymore. Luggage of all shapes and
sizes ride down the conveyor, clattering loudly against the metal. If
baggage claim is up and running again, then that must mean the planes
are too. Then why are the lights still off?

“I’m going to see what’s going on,” I tell Elise. “The baggage service
desk should be around here somewhere.”

I saunter off in the direction of where I think I see the desk, a vague
outline in the dark. It’s even darker than before. Why did they turn off
all the lights? Don’t tell me they’re having power issues too. As it
stands, the only illumination comes from the sickly neon of the
emergency lights and the icy glow of the full moon.

Cold. I feel cold. I’ve been sweating my ass off all day and now the
sweat has cooled to ice, seeped down through my skin, chilling me to the
bone. My breath even seems to fog in the air but no, it’s only a trick
of the light.

The emergency lights are not worthy of the name, a flickering green that
wards off maybe a few feet of gloom before the darkness swallows it,
reincorporates it, giving the shadows around me an unearthly texture.
That’s why at first, I don’t even see her.

The customer service agent.

Or so I guess, going by the dusk blue uniform. She stands behind the
service desk, posture painfully upright. This one actually does have a
nametag, pinned by her left breast, but I can’t read the name; not
because it’s too dark but for the opposite reason, oddly enough. The
polished tag is too reflective, like a hi-vis vest caught in headlights.
A solid rectangle of ghostly fire.

That’s all I can make of the woman as from the shoulders up she tapers
off into darkness. Neck, face—all silhouette, interrupted only by a gash
of teeth. A crescent smile.

“Excuse me, miss. Can you tell me what’s happening?”

No answer. No movement either, actually.

“Do you know what’s going on?”

Again, nothing. Only the picture-perfect smile. Too perfect. And exactly
like a picture, which doesn’t have to concern itself with little things
like speaking.

Or breathing.

“Miss?”

Ding!

I jump back as the intercom suddenly cuts on. “I’m sorry, sir. I don’t
understand your question.” Despite the static, I can tell it’s a woman’s
voice. But…

I look back to the service agent and—nope. She hadn’t moved a muscle,
her teeth show no sign of parting. And yet, the voice on the intercom
sounded like it was responding to me. Weird.

I clear my throat. “Let me clarify: when will the planes be able to take
off?”

Ding!

“They are already off the ground.”

The noise gives me another mini heart attack. It sounded closer this
time, and I search futilely for it and the voice in the dark before
shooting back to the agent in front of me. Still smiling.

“What do you mean by that? Did we miss our flight? Why are the lights
off?”

This time I refuse to take my eyes off her. To catch her in the act, if
this is some ill-besotted prank. She doesn’t respond, of course, doesn’t
even act like she heard me. Her smile might as well be the moon,
floating so unerringly still in the shadow of her face. The silence
drags on and on.

“Did you find anything out?” Elise asks from behind me.

I don’t turn to look at her, eyes still on the agent. “Not yet. The help
here is speaking in riddles.”

“If you’re not getting anywhere here, then I could really use your help
with something.” There’s a tinge of fear in her voice and my suspicions
are confirmed by the sight of her tugging her dress—a habit she falls
into when she’s nervous.

“Show me.” I say, taking her hand as she leads me back to baggage claim.

Ding!

“But sir, you and your wife have already arrived.”

I look back over my shoulder but already the service desk is gone, lost
to the dark.

I hear the carousel long before it becomes visible, the conveyor louder
than ever now, positively cacophonous. Probably because of the bona fide
mountains of luggage clanging on top of it.

Piles of suitcases, heaped up so high their summits are lost in shadow.
They look precarious too, like the wrong suitcase pulled will cause the
whole structure to collapse in an avalanche. God, had we been sleeping
under that? How does it even all fit?

I look to the ends of the carousel, where the bags enter and exit the
part of the conveyor accessible to people, but the carousel seems a lot
longer than I remember it being. I can’t even see the ends, so distant
are they.

Perhaps even more baffling than the quantity of luggage is the sheer
variety. Besides the more standard boxes, there are ones shaped like
pyramids, perfect spheres, one that looks like a double-helix shape. One
suitcase looks normal when it first creeps into view, only it keeps
going and going, stretching out to an absurd degree. Did someone pack a
ladder?

But of course, none of that would explain why Elise is so frightened.
No, that would be the suitcase in between the piles. The one shaped like
a man.

It’s almost exactly like the body bags you’d see in a morgue on one of
those NCIS shows. Like the other suitcases, it too rattles on the
carousel, but not because of the uneven surface of the conveyor belt.
It’s moving.

It’s moving on its own, like there’s someone inside trying to escape. I
can hear grunting and pitiful moans escaping from it.

Elise’s eyes are wide with fright. “Should…shouldn’t we help them?”

Us? I search around for anyone else to dump this problem on. No one.
This whole area’s deserted. Dead as a doornail. Dead as whatever the
hell is inside that suitcase should be; I don’t see any air-holes.

The customer service agent pops into my mind unbidden. Her frozen smile.
Still as a corpse.

I shake my head—to clear the thoughts and in response to Elise’s
question. “We should call this in. To TSA or something.”

“But…” Her eyes follow the moaning suitcase as it gets further and
further away, shaking so violently it threatens to fall off the
carousel. “What if we don’t make it back in time.”

“What could we do? Like…um…oh! How could we even open it?” Yeah, that
sounds plausible and totally not like I’m wussing out. “We should
definitely get help—professional help.” Before Elise can answer, I take
her by the arm and rush off. Anywhere from this place. Surely the main
areas aren’t so creepy.

In the gloom, it seems all the distances have elongated. Twisted. We
walk for what seems like an eternity without coming across another
living soul, an eerie contrast to how it was just a few hours before.
What, did everyone board and we just napped through it? The service
agent did say that all the planes had taken off already. She also said,
you and your wife have already arrived.

Not that we’re even married. Maybe never will be, a bitter part of me
says.

I’m still brooding over that when I spot the first beam of light in this
abyss of an airport. It’s coming from the flight display. Its giant LED
screen is still an eye-searing red, but all the flight information has
been deleted, except for one narrow blue strip: BENJAMIN AND ELISE:
ARRIVED.

What the…

“Honey,” Elise says, practically hugging my arm now. “I don’t want to be
here anymore.”

“Neither do I. We’re leaving.”

The exit should be where all the emergency lights lead to, but nothing
makes sense in this damn place. We venture closer to the window, to make
use of the moonlight, and out of it we see that apparently the planes
haven’t taken off. They’re all still here. All of them.

The tarmac. Planes are everywhere crammed and slotted into each other so
tightly you can barely see the runway beneath. Jammed so tightly some
planes have to be tilted at an angle to fit, one wing sliding underneath
the belly of its neighbor while the other wing slants off towards the
moon. There must be every airplane in the whole country down there,
packed onto this one runway.

“Bennie.” Elise points to a door in the distance. It reads Exit.

We’re practically running at this point, a mad dash towards freedom. Our
feet slap across the floor like gunshots in the eerie silence as the
door gradually gets closer, and closer, and closer.

And closer.

I stop. Elise doesn’t notice at first and keeps running. It doesn’t
matter. Despite her efforts, she’s barely moving at all.

Glancing down, I realize that we’re on one of those moving sidewalks
people use to traverse the terminal faster. I’d ask how we got on one
without noticing, but stuff stopped making sense a long time ago.

Eventually, Elise tires out, realization dawns on her face before giving
way to terror.

Suddenly, the lights cut on and all around us, the boarding gates swing
open revealing an infernal red glow as if from Hell itself.

Ding!

So here we are.

Obviously we haven’t boarded yet—if boarding our “flight” sounds like a
bad idea just from reading this then that doesn’t even begin to describe
the bone-deep ache in my own gut that if we board that plane, we won’t
be coming back.

The problem is, we can’t leave. We’re stuck in some demented Twilight
Zone. Elise seems about to lose it because of—

Ding!

“Mr. and Mrs. Carter, your flight is ready!

—of that. God, we must’ve been listening to that for days. I can’t even
think straight anymore.

I really don’t want to get on that plane, but at this point, one way
trip or not, I’d rather get on it than stay one more second at the
airport.
