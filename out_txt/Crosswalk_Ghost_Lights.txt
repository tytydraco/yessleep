My sister pointed something out when we were driving a couple weeks ago.
There’s a crosswalk by my house that sometimes has what she calls “Ghost
Lights”. This, as she described, is when the warning lights flash even
when nobody has pressed them. I assumed it was some sort of electrical
issue, but said it would make a good premise for a local ghost story. In
saying that I realize that it puts my account into question, but I
assure you, I would have made up something far more elaborate than this.
I was driving home when it was dark enough to turn on my headlights, and
noticed the lights on the crosswalk flashing. I slowed down, but saw no
one, so I dismissed it as Ghost Lights and continued. When it was about
to cross, however, I saw some kind of small animal standing right in
front of my car. I slammed on the breaks, and found myself looking at a
grey and white cat blinking in the glare of my headlights. It wasn’t in
good shape, and had probably already been hit based on the blood in its
fur. I got out to maybe help the poor thing, but it was gone. I didn’t
think it was too odd that I didn’t see it cross at any point; cats are
quick little things after all.
When I was out for a run the next morning I noticed the picture on a
missing pet poster that resembled the cat I’d seen the night before. I
gave the number a call to tell them I thought I saw her in the area, but
while they thanked me for contacting them, they said their cat had been
found dead on the side of the highway already. They just hadn’t taken
all the posters down yet.
The next incident with the Ghost Lights happened not long after the
first. The seasons were making it darker in the evenings, so the
flashing lights were clearer than before. Once more I drove slowly, just
in case it was actually someone crossing, but once again, I saw nothing.
However, when I had actually reached the crosswalk, there was something
right in front of me once again. It was a dog, this time: a large,
fluffy, grey dog, one eye shut and bloodied, and I swear it was missing
an ear. Like the cat, it just stared at me. Unlike the cat, I was a bit
more apprehensive about getting out; I had heard too many stories about
rabid dogs for comfort. Instead I pulled out my phone and took a photo,
deciding to send it to the animal shelter later. I didn’t want to honk
at it, so I flashed my headlights to try and get it to move out of my
way. I flicked them off for a fraction of a second, then back on again,
and the dog was gone.
When I got home, I went to email the photo to the shelter, but when I
opened it, it was just a photo of a crosswalk. I was puzzled, but I
emailed them anyway, figuring there was a logical explanation of some
sort that I wasn’t science literate enough to know.
On my way to work a few days later, I saw a dead deer on the side of the
highway: a buck, based on the antlers. Coming across a deer corpse isn’t
too uncommon in my area. I wouldn’t think to mention it unless it was
the first thing that I thought of when I encountered the Ghost Lights
that night.
Like the last two times, I saw the flashing, saw no one was crossing,
moved forward, only to have to slam on the break at the last second.
This time it was a buck, looking at me from the side with a large, grey
eye illuminated by my headlights. One hoof was frozen in the air, as if
it had been moving forward. It turned its head, showing that the other
half of his face was nothing but raw flesh, and blood, and bone. I
shrieked, jumping in my seat, my foot coming off the break for a moment.
The car inched forward a bit before I composed myself. Composing myself,
I looked up to find that the deer was gone.
This time, at least, I believed there was a solid explanation. I
reported a case of what I, somewhat ignorantly, thought was a case of
Zombie Deer Disease in the area and went to bed, only a little bit
shaken.
For the next few days there was nothing, no Ghost Lights and,
thankfully, no bloody animals. Then there was the one that I can’t seem
to shake.
A young teenager named Grace was killed in a car accident on the
highway. I heard about it because I had to take an alternate route to
work that day. When I was making my home, I saw the lights on the
crosswalk flashing again. The process began again: slowdown, don’t see
anyone, continue, see something, slam on the break. And there she was:
hair in braids, grey sweater, pale face, neck turned at an unnatural
angle as she stared at me with dark eyes. She was bloodier than the
animals; it was all over her neck and cheek, soaked into the sleeve of
her sweater, and running out from her hairline. I got out of my car to
help her, but just like the others, she was gone as if she had never
been there.
I looked her up when I got home, and it was Grace, as impossible as that
may seem.
I’ve considered that I’ve been hallucinating all of this; it’s not like
she does anything to me, or talks, or moves beyond blinking really. I
feel bad for her, if she really is a ghost. It must suck to be stuck
haunting a crosswalk. I’ve seen her every night since. I just have to
flash my lights at her to make her go away. It’s become a reflex, and I
don’t even stop or slow down anymore when I see the Ghost Lights
flashing. Eventually I’ll be driving home while it’s still light out,
and I’m hoping I’ll be able to get a good night’s sleep then.
