I don’t know if I killed that thing, well, if the fire did. I hadn’t
heard any demonic animal calls since I left the ranger station either so
I think I’m in the clear. It’s been an interesting trip to say the
least.

Yesterday was quiet. I did manage to finally get some sleep. No dreams
no nightmares, legit rest. It helped some but I just woke up sorer than
I’ve ever been. I thought about going back to sleep once I woke up, but
I knew I probably wouldn’t make it back with that time line. I ate what
little food I had left while I was still hidden in the drain, and
continued down the mountain road.

After five miles or so, I started to hear a vehicle in the distance.
This boosted my hopes further than I could’ve thought. Once I heard the
hum of the engine I realized that I haven’t seen or heard any other
vehicle up here since we were on our way down originally. I picked up my
pace and started to run. The sight must have been something. As the
truck came around the corner and into view it stopped. I kept running. I
didn’t care if I looked like a madman from deep in the forest or not,
that truck was a sure shot out of these woods and I could tell I
couldn’t make the last twenty miles alone.

The truck, driver obviously in fear started to try and back up in the
snow and reposition to turn around. I must’ve really looked like shit.
It was no use though, given the snow and the narrow road the driver took
a lot longer than they had thought and in no time I was at their door.
Its tint was too dark to see through and my hot breath on the window
only fogged it up more. I started to bang on the window yelling at them
to roll it down. Eventually the driver did. It was an old man, bordering
his eighty’s if I had to guess, he was alone besides his wiener dog in
the front seat.

“Young man what in all hell you lookin like that fer? The ways yous was
runnin I thought you were a demon draggin those ripped clothes behind
ya. Scared the shit out of me and Coop.” He patted his dog on the head.
“Sir I just need to get home.”
“Where’s home young man?”
“Off this fucking rock.”
“Alright I get ya, what you doin out here with no truck?”
“I came up with my friends and they crashed a bit up a ways, they didn’t
make it, and I’ve been walking since.”
“Say no more.”
A solemn look crossed his face as I mentioned the crash and he leaned
over Coop and opened the passenger side of the door. I wasted no time
getting in.

Old man Jerry as he calls himself, is a real one. On our way down the
mountain we stopped at his cabin in Dirthop for a cup of coffee and he
gave me some dry clothes. I couldn’t have told Jerry thank you enough.
The man saved my life and it’s just another Monday for this guy.
“Figure I’d drop you off at the sheriffs station down a ways, them
boys’ll help ya with ya rig.” He didn’t mention Aaron and Ricky.
“I can’t thank you enough Jerry.”
“Yup it’s whatta old fellers here for, to help you young dipshits get on
gettin.” He laughed. I tried to as well but my chest gave up.
“Hey Jerry,” I paused before continuing. He looked at me, letting me
know I had his attention.
“Have you ever seen anything up there, up further on the mountain I
mean.”
He looked at me and let out a long sigh before answering.
“All I know young man it that a body will fight infections, nature gonna
spit whatever it can atcha if it don’t like what it’s getting in
return.”
It wasn’t the answer I was looking for but it was good enough. And that
was a good enough reason for me to ever go back up that mountain.
Especially since all my tears have been black since I’ve gotten home.
