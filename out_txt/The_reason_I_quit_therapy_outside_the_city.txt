I don’t even know where to start honestly, since it has been years that
this event happened to me. However, the last few nights I kept losing
sleep over the incident. So I guess I’ll start with some background
information.
In my early adolescence I suffered from a severe mental crisis - the
details aren’t really relevant to this story. Just note that it lead me
to having regular sessions with a therapist. Initially I hated the idea.
Don’t get me wrong, I knew I needed help and I desperately wanted it…
but having only started Highschool and having to travel alone for an
hour far into the countryside to meet the therapist my mother had picked
for me scared the shit out of me. I can’t even say why exactly. I mean I
wasn’t a little kid anymore. If I’d had to guess; maybe it was growing
up in an abusive household or the repeated warnings my mother gave me
about stranger men.. or even simply being unwell. I only know that I
hated the commute every damn time.

During the winter I went to my sessions as usual but I was genuinely
happy. I had bonded with my therapist quite a lot and I couldn’t wait to
show him the sky blue oversized knitted sweater I got for Christmas. I
thought that I looked really cute in it. And like the “rebellious” teen
I was, I refused to wear a jacket and only went with the sweater because
“It isn’t that cold mom”.
On the way to my therapist I saw an elderly drunken man on the train. I
didn’t think much of it because that is quite a regular sight on the
countryside. Even as he got of the train and walked with me towards the
town square, although starting to get unnerved, I tried to keep myself
calm.

He’s just walking to the town square, there are all the stores and it’s
the end of the work day. Don’t let the horror stories your mom told you
get to your head. It makes sense that he needs to go there as well.

To be on the safe side, because paranoia started to get the best of me,
I decided to take some smaller alleyways instead of the main road.
Because it’s been months since I started seeing my therapist I was
pretty good at finding my way around. However, with dusk already
approaching and the shadows getting longer I couldn’t help myself but
glance behind me every now and then.
… each time I did there was the man, always about 50m behind me. So I
quickened my step, after all it wasn’t long until the office.

During my therapy session I started to relax again. It was nice and warm
and it was a pleasant talk. No idea what we talked about, most likely my
mind map and tomatoes again (that would be a whole different story).
Matter of fact is I felt good and completely forgot about my kinda
creepy walk there. Before I noticed the session was over and it had
gotten dark outside… so I braced myself for the long cold way home.

It has gotten darker quicker than expected and being so far out the city
I only had some lonesome lampposts occasionally at the road… so I
couldn’t really see my surroundings. At first it didn’t matter to me
because I was so familiar with the streets.

Then I started to get the feeling like I was being watched. But when I
turned around there was only a few people at the end of the road hastily
going into a store before it would close. I couldn’t help myself but
stare at them for some time because I felt like I recognised someone in
the crowd. The longer I looked the more I rationed with myself I must
have imagined it because no one that I knew would have any business in
this town. So I decided to keep walking on because I didn’t want to miss
my train. However I couldn’t fully shake the feeling of being watched.

Upon entering the train I was relieved at the warmth and that the cart
was empty. That meant I could daydream my entire way home without being
interrupted by noisy passengers.
That feeling only lasted shortly though as I heard a person entering
behind me. I didn’t turn around, because somehow I exactly knew who
stood behind me as the stench of alcohol filled my nose and the whisper
in my ear: “Let’s run off together, cutie”

And in that moment I knew, my mother wasn’t exaggerating with her
stories.
