The power went out at 7:06 pm.

But I’d say our flame had been out for about a year and a half. 

She said I was living two different lives—my late night party animal
lifestyle as a locally famous bartender, and the nice guy during the day
pretending he was ready for a wife and a family. 

She wasn’t wrong.

I’d been behind the bar for 17 years straight. God, had it really been
that long? I thought I could do both. Be both. And I was. But I also
didn’t realize I was losing her. 

Until she divorced me 2 months after our wedding.

There’s something so beautiful and tragic about the hope a woman can
hold onto for her man. Thinking every new holiday, anniversary,
birthday… even exchanging wedding vows… could be the thing that wakes
him up, changes him for the better…

She had a daughter before we met, Annmarie. 

At hibachi for her 8th birthday, her mother and I were unsuccessfully
silent-fighting behind her back. We were staring daggers at each other
over Annmarie’s curly head as she sat between us, masterfully eating her
shrimp fried rice with kiddie chopsticks. 

Her mother mouthed to me: “Well, aren’t you gonna say something?” 

Annmarie, without even dropping a shrimp said: “He already did. With his
eyes.”

Annmarie loved me. And that was enough for her mother to fight for what
was left of our love. And to hold onto the hope that someday I would
change…

Buying this house was my grand effort to make that change. To show her I
was ready. 

“Buy the cheapest house inside the best school district” was my buddy’s
advice. 

So, here we were: A broken family. Sitting in an old fixer-upper without
power.

And tonight was Annmarie’s 9th birthday.

I was committed to showing them a future they’d enjoy. A future we’d all
love.

I hadn’t moved much of my stuff into the house yet. And honestly, I
hadn’t expected her to accept my invite. I hadn’t seen her in over 4
months.

So when she said yes, I had to scramble.

I thought hibachi delivery and Netflix would be a decent effort. 

With the power still out, that would be tough.

I searched for a flashlight in one of those small, hidden storage doors
under the staircase.

But I could only find an old lamp.

Really old.

Then I joined them in the family room.

It was awkward. No technology. And nothing really to talk about. Just
the silence from simmering all of our scars.

Luckily Annmarie was not only super cute but fiercely curious. She
started fiddling with the lamp.

And found a secret compartment with a little latch that slid open. 

Inside was an old, folded piece of paper with writing and markings on
it.

We discovered it was a “how to” for shadow puppet techniques, dated
1866.

Analogue entertainment for the win?

The paper had increasing levels of complexity for creating shadow
puppets. Level one was your classic one person stuff: a crow, a bunny, a
dog.

Annmarie chose the crow. 

As she flapped her finger-wings creating a soaring crow shadow on the
wall, her mother and I flapped our lips through some small talk: 

“How’s the new job?”

“How’s your mother?” 

We stumbled into some turbulence but found some common ground by
planning some home projects to “spruce up the place.”

We stepped away, walking through the kitchen, then the family room.
There wasn’t any light, but the darkness forced us closer.

It was kind of nice, actually.

Planning for the future, that’s a good sign right? Staring out into the
moonlit backyard through the bay window found our lips orbiting each
other, nearing a kiss—

Then a crow slammed into the window. 

Then another. And another. 

BLAM. SPLAT. SGLUSSSHTT.

Leaving a smear of blood, guts, and feathers. 

“Oh, my god, Rob… were those birds?” She gasped, covering her mouth with
her hand.

“Wow, yeah.” They had dropped dead onto the stone patio below. “Looks
like crows maybe?” 

“ROB!” Annmarie’s voice yelled out. 

We ran back to her in the family room.

“You okay sugar cookie?” Her mom asked.

“Yeah, I just need Rob’s help.”

The shadow puppet diagrams were getting more complex.

She needed a second person. These techniques were next level, utilizing
three hands and even four. 

As we intertwined our hands into the level two animals, a smile spread
across her mom’s face. 

Glimpsing her happiness reminded me: I was living two lives. But I
really liked this one. Why did I keep jeopardizing it? Yeah, I was a
late bloomer but goddamn I’m 37 now. Time to grow up. 

Her mother clapped at each successful attempt of a new shadow puppet
from us and we all laughed together at each terrible failure. We
eventually achieved them all. 

But the directions prompted us to flip the page over for the final
shadow puppet.

The sight of it shook my soul.

As if in a single instant I had been dragged through the bowels of
ancient hell, through caves containing creatures far older than the
existence of the universe.

Annmarie just giggled and said it was “really ugly.” 

But it required 6 hands. Would take about 6 minutes to achieve. And must
be held for 6 seconds.

So we recruited her reluctant mother.

On our third attempt, we managed to mangle our fingers together enough
to eke out the outline of this monstrosity.

“Yay! Now hold it for 6 seconds… one… two…” Annmarie smiled, staring at
the shadow growing larger and larger on the wall…

“Three…. Four…” Her mother beaming at this authentic family moment.

“Five…” I started to count…

But I was spellbound by the shadowy creature… looming larger and larger
up the wall, bleeding onto the ceiling… almost appearing as if it was
forming a real, skin-like texture… scaly goosebumps with pricks of
coarse hair beginning to poke through the wall paint…

“Six.” 

We couldn’t move. We sat transfixed. Necks bent upward mesmerized by the
monster forming before us. It loomed large and ominous on the wall,
growing larger and larger than all the other shadow puppets had, almost
as if it were about to tear away from the wall as a living, breathi—

Then the power came back.

The lights flashed on.

And the shadow monster was gone… for now.

9:13 pm.

Just like when the 2am lights come on for last call at the bar… reality
began to set in, and the harshness of our past was fully re-illuminated.

Her mom pulled Annmarie’s hands from the menage immediately. Like the
light was shining on all my faults. Spotlighting our resentment. 

“Come one Annmarie, we should go.” She ordered.

Crazy how the shadows of the heart can just appear anytime. I’d seen
this expression on her face many times. And Annmarie’s. She wanted to
forgive me. Her body was here. The effort. The want. But her heart… Her
soul… was pulling her away. Trying to sever the tether of our past. We
both knew we needed to let go, but we were haunted by a phantom love…
like starlight… a love-light extending from a relationship that no
longer existed…

I forced a smile. Watching them pack up their things. Annmarie’s eyes
staring into my soul, ripping my heart to shreds. Her mother’s distant
look, like looking through our past, into an alternate future she wished
existed, at the man I should’ve been but never was. 

I looked behind me, hoping that man was there. I’d trade places with him
in an instant. But, he wasn’t. It was just me. Broken and begging.

She was saying goodbye for the final final time… I could tell.

The front door closed… to the new house, and to any chance of a future. 

I was left standing there, just me and my monsters.

Then… the power went out again. 

I stood in the darkness crying for a minute or two. Then searched for
the matches.

Strike one. Strike two. Strike three… I was out. The match box looked
like it had been rained on once, or twice, or 100 times. So I sat in the
darkness in silence…

Until a lighter flicked next to me—

SPARK.

It was Annmarie and her mother. She re-lit the lantern with her lucky
green BiC lighter. And sat back down. My heart melted faster than butter
on a hibachi grill.

I couldn’t contain my happiness as I looked at her: “I thought you
quit?” 

“It seems I can’t let go of the things that will eventually kill me.”
She smirked. A little spark in her eye.

She knelt down on the floor with her daughter and me, “Now, where were
we?”

Annmarie flipped over the shadow puppet paper. 

And there was the drawing of that hideous monster again.

I noticed some other writing scribbled frantically at the bottom, but
the ink was smeared, and the edges tattered. I was trying to make out
what it said—

But Annmarie was so excited for us all to be back together, so we
mangled our hands together and counted to six…

But nothing happened. Just an innocent shadow on the wall.

Maybe it was just my demons taunting me…

Annmarie ripped her fingers from the cluster, “Now what?”

Try and entertain a 9-year-old without technology, I dare ya.

“Maybe there’s some board games in the closet?” 

“Boooooring,” Boasted Annmarie.

“Thats why they call them bored games,” I punned. They both looked at me
like I was the king of unfunny.

“So when are we moving in, Mom?” Annmarie jousted slyly.

We stared at each other. Laughed awkwardly. 

Annmarie feigned innocence.

“Well my little sugar booger, we’re just visiting right now. We’ll see
how it goes, ok?” She glanced at me, cautiously optimistic. 

I beamed a massively cheesy grin. Couldn’t be happier.

“School starts in 8 weeks, so you better get a moooooove on it…”
Annmarie laughed hysterically to herself.

She made a pun, just like me. My eyes teared up. Her mom was speechless,
staring at our chemistry. She was the only thing keeping us all together
if you were to ask me. But, the love and admiration of a child is not
nothing.

I had an idea — I found a few used candles from a kitchen drawer, which
we bundled together. I grabbed an old bag of marshmallows from the
pantry. Annmarie’s mom had a Hershey bar in her purse. Annmarie had some
Teddy Grahams in her backpack. This was actually not bad.

We sat around the kitchen island and her mother lit the candle bundle.

“Birthday s’mores, anyone?” I said.

Annmarie’s smile was bigger than the moon. I started to sing:

“HAPPY BIRTHDAY TO YOU. HAPPY BIRTHDAY TO YOUUUUUUUU…”

But as the candle flames flickered on the wall behind them, a terrifying
shadow began to form.
