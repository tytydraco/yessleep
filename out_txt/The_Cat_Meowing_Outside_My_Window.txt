It was the faintest noise. A whisper of a meow. It was around midnight
when I first heard the precious chirping from beneath my window.

I lived alone in a small 1 bedroom unit in the back of an old complex.
The place itself wasn’t much, but it was something to call home. The
complex was surrounded by tall looming oak trees, separating it from the
rest of the town. Out here, people usually don’t have outdoor pets aside
from well protected livestock. Any small dog or cat would likely get
snatched up by a coyote or bobcat for dinner, so I figured it couldn’t
have been out there too long. One of the neighbors’ must have gotten
out.

Being a bit of an insomniac, I’m usually awake during the later hours of
the night. This is often accompanied by a faint scratching from outside,
that makes it much worse. Some of the oaks hang low enough to touch the
building, so sometimes the breeze causes the branches to brush alongside
it. They haven’t been up to date on landscaping for quite some time, but
I was promised it’d be done soon, so I tried my best to ignore it as of
late. Sometimes watching TV, or putting on a podcast helps me drown
everything out to fall asleep, but I’m often left dreadfully restless.
On this night though, I had lie awake in silence.

The meow that coo’d from this particular cat was soothing in the moment.
It was so soft and charming. I imagined him roaming through the nearby
bushes, striding confidently in pursuit of a mouse, or anything to feed
his curiosity. He meowed periodically for a short while, maybe thirty
minutes or so I had spent imagining him carefully navigating through the
bushes. I figured he likely wouldn’t travel too far and was to soon
return home. It was pleasant compared to the normal anxiety filled
thoughts in my head. As I had finally begun drifting to sleep,
mesmerized by the rhythmic and soft feline call, I noticed it had
stopped. I now lie again, in silence.

As I lay there, again trying to fight off the bad thoughts, I began to
wonder about the cat. Who’s was it? I only had about 10 neighbors, most
of which I knew relatively well. I had never seen a cat around the
complex before, and I was rather fond of them so I would have likely
noticed. Perhaps it strolled in from down the road? The town was only
about half a mile away, the cat easily could have wandered off. However,
why did it stop meowing? Now fixated, I decided I’d take a look for
myself. I worried it might have gotten attacked, although I think I
would have heard its cries. Either way it could be lost, and those soft
meows are its way of asking for a guide. I wasn’t going to sleep
anyways, so I might as well do the good deed. I grabbed a flashlight and
headed out of my apartment.

It was crisp out. We had a full moon not too long ago, so now it was
dark and the sky was only filled with dimly lit stars. Nearly pitch
black, too dark to see my surroundings. Guiding the beam of light, I
trailed around the back end of the complex towards my unit. As I circled
the building, I gazed from a short distance into the trees and bushes in
front of me. They were thick. It was hard to see much and would probably
require me to enter the foliage to be any sort of help. I approached the
bushes cautiously, I didn’t want to scare it off. I watched my step as I
entered a short distance in, searching through the branches.

A short time had passed with no luck. Disappointed, I began to lose
hope. There was very little chance I would ever find the cat even if it
was nearby. If I did, it’d probably just run away at the sight of me.
I’ve never met a cat that wasn’t highly elusive. Coming to this
realization I began to feel like a crazy person. I was wandering the
woods at 2 AM to find some random cat that was likely long gone by now.
I imagined watching myself from another’s perspective and laughed at the
idea. As I quietly chuckled, I had made up my mind that I was to go back
home and call it a night.

I began to head back to the treeline a short distance away, when I had
stepped on a large twig. CRACK, a loud snap rang out; Then followed by
silence. That’s when I heard a rustling coming from back in the woods.
It was delicate, and didn’t sound abrupt. Moving precisely from one bush
to the next. It wasn’t moving towards me particularly, but it wasn’t
running away either. It didn’t sound threatening, but something about it
felt that way. It was too soft and precise. Like the animal wasn’t
startled at all. Chills ran down my spine, as I hurriedly returned
towards the treeline. I made it out and began to round the complex in a
cold sweat. Adrenaline had now filled my body and I was shaking quickly
as I took in my surroundings. As much as I could in the small beam of
light guiding me. It felt like I was being watched now.

As I quickly walked home, my mind spun about whether it could be the cat
or not. There are plenty of other animals in the woods, it could have
been anything really. I decided it was likely some other nocturnal
mammal, that was the only thing that would make sense, right? This
justification helped ease the tension, but something still felt wrong. I
couldn’t shake the feeling it could be something else. My insomnia makes
me paranoid sometimes, one of the unfortunate side effects of sleep
deprivation. I decided to shake it off and get back in my unit before
the fear spiraled further.

When I finally got inside, I locked my doors, headed straight to my room
and flicked on the TV. I had enough with this cat situation, it was only
going to cause me further sleep loss and at this point, I just wanted it
to be tomorrow already. I closed my window, and shut my eyes. I could
hear the slight muffled scratching from the branches as I tried to focus
on the banter of chattering news anchors. As I finally began to drift
off, into a semi-sleep-like coma, I was awakened. Not by the scratching,
or noise from the television, but from a soft, child-like meow.

I sat upright, angrily. I went out into the dark woods already to look
for it, and it decided to turn up again outside my window again? It was
nearing 3:30 AM at this point and I had lost my patience. I ripped open
my window and peered out into the dark, a 15 foot grassy section between
my room and the treeline. A slight chill breeze cooled my face as I sat
there gazing. I grabbed the flashlight to aid me, but nothing. How.
Could. There. Be. Nothing? I felt defeated, but then the meow returned.
It came from the bushes directly in front of me. Just once, then
followed by a few more periodically. Each as delicate as the one before.
Maybe it would notice my flashlight and run towards the building? I sat
there for a couple minutes in hopes it would come out… It never did.

Was I really about to go back out there? If it kept meowing I’d never be
able to ignore it, but I just wanted to sleep at this point. Would this
be on my conscience, knowing I could have helped it but didn’t? I knew
it was out there, but the chance of me even catching it was so slim, I
couldn’t decide if it was worth it. After some consideration, I decided
it wasn’t, so I moved back through the window. As I grabbed the
windowseal I could feel the cold dew that accumulated since I had opened
the window. It felt refreshing and almost alleviating in the moment.
What I felt on the underside, however, was not. Deep gashes.

The intense feeling of fear had returned. They were deep and had clawed
away large chunks of the wood. All while doing this I never stopped
watching the bushes. Nothing ever came out, but the meow had now stopped
again. I sat there frantically in silence. I stared intensely as my
shaking hand navigated the underside of the windowseal. The gashes
didn’t feel sharp, they were dull and broad. Almost like a beaver had
been gnawing on it. That’s when one of my fingers slipped, near
perfectly into one of the divots. They were human scratch marks. Nearly
deep enough to pry the window open.

I slammed the window shut and went to the kitchen to grab a knife. I
wasn’t sure what was going on, but I no longer felt safe. The TV chatter
from my room echoed down the hall, the news anchor announcing, “2
missing persons as of last night in…”. In a panic, I double checked
every door and window was locked, and made my way back to my room. The
only window in the house that didn’t lock was mine, but if I could just
wait till the police arrive, I would be okay. I dialed 911 and told them
I was being stalked and in danger. They told me they would arrive as
soon as possible. A twenty to thirty minute wait.

I turned off the tv, and sat there in silence, staring at the window.
The scratching had returned, it was hard and jagged. I could imagine
someone’s fingernails bleeding as they clawed into splintered wood. I
wearily peaked down to check if anyone was there, but there wasn’t. It
must have been the branches. Taunting me.

Heart beating through my chest, I waited in hope the police would arrive
sooner. I tried to find comfort in my confined space, and the knife I
wielded. After a couple minutes of finally regaining composure, I
finally took my eyes off the window, making my way towards the door to
be able to greet the police when they arrived. As I turned the knob,
slowly, cautious of what could be on the other side, the door began to
creak open. There was nothing, but the dark hallway in front of me. As I
stepped out, ready to end the nightmare that was unfolding, there came
from my closet, the soft meow of a cat.
