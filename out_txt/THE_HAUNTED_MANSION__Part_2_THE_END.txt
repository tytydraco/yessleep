Content warning: Graphic violence, murder, suicide, and disturbing imagery.

When I returned to the meeting point, my father, mother, uncle, and
Haitham were there.

But Faten was missing.

The moment they saw Omar’s body, the place erupted into uncontrollable
screams and hysterical crying.

My parents’ firstborn. My brother, who had been more than a brother to
me—my closest friend—was now a mutilated corpse.

Omar wasn’t just my brother. He and Haitham were my closest friends. We
had done everything together.

Damn this cursed mansion.

My mother couldn’t even speak through her sobbing. She struggled to
catch her breath. I tried to comfort her, but how could I help her when
I could barely accept Omar’s death myself?

I couldn’t bring myself to look at Haitham. I couldn’t bear to see him
crying, but I could hear him breaking down.

Damn you, filthy mansion!

Through his tears, my father asked, “What happened? How could you leave
him alone, you maniac? And why are you covered in blood?”

I had no explanation.

So I lied.

“It’s Omar’s blood.”

“How?”

“That’s normal, isn’t it? I’m the one who found him.”

“How could you leave your brother, you stupid, damned idiot?!”

I raised my voice.

“You were the one who suggested we split up! It wasn’t my idea!”

I tried to blame someone else.

“You didn’t tell me to leave him!” my father shouted. “I told you to
stay together and search together! And you were the one who insisted we
enter this mansion in the first place, weren’t you?”

I couldn’t answer. He was right about every word.

It was my fault.

My uncle interrupted in a trembling voice.

“This isn’t the time to blame anyone. Omar was dear to all of us,
Dagher. His fate might not have been any different even if Wissam had
stayed with him. We don’t know what would have happened. Thank God for
everything. To God we belong, and to Him we shall return.”

“Thank God,” the rest of us murmured.

My uncle continued, “Whatever did this wasn’t human. We have to find a
way out. But… where is Faten? She said she heard your voices and went to
catch up with you, Samir!”

“I haven’t seen her,” I said.

“Have you seen her, Hiyam?” my uncle asked.

“No.”

“What does that mean? Where is Faten?!”

We didn’t have to wait long for an answer.

A loud, violent impact echoed from the ground floor.

We rushed downstairs.

There we found Faten.

She was dead.

The way she had died made Omar’s death seem merciful.

Her head was completely crushed, as though the bones of her skull had
melted beneath the blows of an enormous hammer. Her body was torn apart,
covered in stab wounds from head to toe. Blood was everywhere.

My mother vomited at the sight.

My uncle fell to his knees, stunned into helplessness. Omar’s death had
been a terrible shock to him, but Faten’s death was the final blow—for
him and for all of us.

We hadn’t even begun to recover from Omar’s death.

This mansion… this devil showed no mercy.

I could no longer feel like myself. I had completely lost control.

I picked up the piece of metal again and made my decision.

“I’m going to find an exit, and I’m going to kill whoever did this.”

My father told me we should stay together, but I had already lost my
mind. I couldn’t feel anything anymore.

“I’m going. Take out your gun, Dad, and prepare for the worst.”

I climbed back up to the cursed corridor.

There were many paintings on the walls that I was certain hadn’t been
there before. Horrifying paintings of people who must have suffered the
same fate as Omar and Rania’s parents.

Looking at them was enough to fill anyone with terror.

But I was already in the worst state of my life. I had nothing left to
fear.

And then I saw her.

The blonde girl.

She had green eyes and a devilish smile that did not belong in our
world.

This was what my brother had seen before he died!

But the strange thing was that she wasn’t alone.

She was holding the hand of that damned black creature.

It wore the same devilish smile.

The girl pulled out a knife dripping with my family’s blood, while the
creature moved closer and closer to me.

I stood at one end of the corridor. It was lined with rooms, and the
girl stood at the far end.

She wanted to torment me.

Both she and the creature.

The creature moved with terrifying slowness, while the girl entered one
room and emerged from the room opposite it. There was no explanation for
how she did this. There was no way to explain something that had no
connection to reality in the first place.

She repeated the process.

The creature kept getting closer.

It disappeared and reappeared so often that I could no longer predict
when it would emerge and grab me.

The girl laughed.

And while the creature was disappearing, it suddenly appeared and seized
me.

I passed out from sheer terror.

When I woke up, I couldn’t move.

More blood.

I was covered in it. I had lost so much blood that I could barely move.
Blood was everywhere.

I spent fifteen minutes trying to recover enough strength to get up.

When I finally managed to look around, I realized that another four
hours had passed!

Damn it! How did time move so strangely inside this mansion?

I remembered that I still had to find the exit. I had to end this
nightmare.

I ran like a madman, searching everywhere, until I finally found an old
wooden door.

I opened it.

The cold night air hit my face.

An exit!

Finally!

I could leave this mansion. Enough of the people I loved had died
already.

I turned around to go back and tell my family the good news.

I ran back to the meeting point.

But they were late.

Far too late.

I decided to look for them.

I found a room with its door strangely left open. There was blood
outside it.

I already knew what I was going to see.

I wished I had died before seeing it.

My father.

My mother.

My uncle.

Haitham.

All of them were dead in the most horrible ways imaginable.

My strength abandoned me.

My father… my mother… my uncle… Haitham…

They were all dead.

I fell to my knees, unable to do anything.

It was the greatest shock of my life.

I began laughing and crying hysterically. My emotions were tangled
together, and I couldn’t understand what was happening to me.

I had gone mad.

That was madness.

I dragged their mutilated bodies outside one by one, struggling with
each of them. I didn’t know where I found the strength to do it. Perhaps
it was the strength that comes from despair and insanity.

I left them there.

Then I dragged my feet like a living corpse toward my uncle’s house in
the village.

The streets were empty and dead.

I entered the house unsteadily. I didn’t change my clothes. I went
straight to the bathroom, turned on the cold water, and sat beneath it
fully dressed.

I trembled and cried hysterically, desperately trying to wash away the
blood of my family, which seemed to have stuck to my skin and my soul.

But I hadn’t surrendered completely to madness yet.

I left the bathroom, dripping with water and blood, and searched the
courtyard of my uncle’s house until I found what I needed: containers of
kerosene.

I carried them back to the cursed mansion.

I poured kerosene over every inch of it. I wanted to end this curse, to
burn that devilish girl and that damned creature.

Then I struck a match.

I watched the flames consume the walls and the memories.

As I stared into the fire, I remembered the worst thing that had ever
happened to me.

Afterward, I returned to my uncle’s house to wait for morning so I could
inform the police.

I sat trembling in the guest room, touching the bags we had left there
before going to the mansion.

As I searched through them, something caught my attention: a small box I
had brought with me from the hospital.

I hadn’t opened it since leaving.

Inside were a few insignificant things, except for one item.

My diary.

I opened it, hoping to find an explanation for the period of memory loss
I had experienced.

I turned through the pages full of writing until I stopped at a line
that froze my blood forever.

It was written in my own handwriting:

“The doctors say my diagnosis is not simply bipolar disorder. It is a
dissociative disorder involving multiple personalities. There is another
person inside me.

“The problem is that when this ‘other’ takes control of the body, the
original Wissam remembers nothing he has done. The reverse is also
true.”

I swallowed with difficulty and looked down at the next lines.

“For this plan to succeed, I must lure them all there… to the mansion in
my uncle’s village.

“My father will not refuse me anything after my long absence in the
hospital.

“The plan is as follows: I will shut the mansion’s door as soon as they
go upstairs. We will force them to split up. That is what I want.

“The first will be Omar.

“The second will be Faten.

“The third will be my father.

“And so on…

“A perfect plan.

“When the police investigate, the village’s alleged ‘ghost’ will serve
as the perfect culprit. Not a single piece of evidence will be left
behind.”

The diary fell from my hands.

Me?

I had done all of that?

I was the one who mutilated my brother?

I was the one who stitched my parents’ bodies together while pretending
to search for an exit?

How?

Why would I kill the family I loved?

The ‘other’ inside me hated them. He was the one who had planned
everything and carried it out, leaving the original Wissam to cry over
their bodies.

“What do you think? Pretty clever, isn’t it?”

I heard a voice.

I turned around.

There it was again.

The black creature.

Its voice was powerful and terrifying, as if it came from another world.

“Who’s there?”

“No one. I didn’t come from outside. I’ve always been here. I don’t
exist in reality.”

“You did all of this? Why?!”

“For pleasure. Yes, what a wonderful feeling. You understand what I
mean, don’t you?”

“Pleasure?! You’re the most horrible thing I’ve ever seen!”

“How enjoyable it was to kill every member of our family. Wasn’t it
fun?”

“Look in your pocket.”

I looked.

The shock nearly stopped my heart.

It was the knife I had used to kill my family.

“How can I get rid of you?! What have you done to my life?! You’ve
destroyed everything!”

“All of this came from your own thoughts. What happened was nothing more
than a reflection of your buried desire.

“Everyone thought you had recovered from me. But I was smarter than all
of them. I managed to hide.”

He burst into hysterical laughter.

It was a laugh I had heard before.

I couldn’t answer.

Then an idea came to me.

The decision was made.

The world had to be rid of this madman.

This devil.

I dragged a wooden chair into the middle of the room. I tied a rope
securely to the ceiling fan.

I climbed onto the chair.

I looked at my family’s bags. I looked at the rooms where we had spent
time together at my uncle’s house.

I wrapped the rope around my neck.

And now…

There would be nothing left but silence.

“Adam? Are you here? Aaaadam?”

“Adam!!!!”

“Dad! Adam… Adam has committed suicide! We shouldn’t have left him!”

“What?!”

“He was the one who shut the mansion’s door and went away. We didn’t
know how to get out. He shouldn’t have been discharged from the
hospital. We should have kept him there!”

Omar said, “This is the hospital’s fault, not his.”

My father replied, “Praise be to God. Indeed, we belong to God, and to
Him we shall return. Praise be to God. Omar, take your brother down. We
have to bury him.”
