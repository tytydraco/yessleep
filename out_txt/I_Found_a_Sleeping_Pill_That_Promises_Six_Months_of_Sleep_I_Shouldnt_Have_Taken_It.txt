I’ve tried everything: melatonin, sleep therapy, and even prescription
temazepam.

Nothing worked. I just couldn’t sleep.

My insomnia started about a year ago, and since then, my life has been a
blur of sleepless nights and drowsy days. During the day, I felt like I
was underwater. Everything was fuzzy and distant, and I was constantly
afraid I’d fall asleep at work or behind the wheel.

Nighttime was worse. I’d lie in bed with my eyes wide open, staring at
the ceiling, watching the hours tick by.

On a good night, I’d get two hours of sleep.

It was late fall when I found myself mindlessly wandering the streets at
3 AM after fighting and losing the battle for sleep. I dragged myself
through the sleeping town; the cold autumn air pricked my face as the
town’s streetlights flickered above my head. In the distance, the warm
fluorescent glow of a lone store lit up the darkness.

The sight was comforting to me, and like a moth to the flame, I was
drawn to the warmly lit storefront. It was a pharmacy. I stood outside
for a moment, staring at the store. Though I wasn’t far from home and
had walked this way before, I had never seen it before.

Entering the store, I was greeted by a musty yet sweet smell and calming
jazz flowing out of the small radio that sat on the checkout counter.
The man behind the counter lowered the newspaper he had been reading and
gave me a warm smile.

“Welcome in. Let me know if you need any help”

He was a chubby man who appeared to be in his mid-forties; a comb-over
tried in vain to cover his balding head.

“Thanks,” I replied weakly as I headed to the back of the store.

Glancing back, I noticed the man watched me for a moment before
returning to his paper. I browsed the aisles, my eyes lazily moving from
various pills to overpriced snacks. I had no plans to buy anything,
really; I was there to kill time before trying to sleep again. Almost by
habit, I found myself in the sleep-aid section. It was there that
something caught my eye.

On the bottom shelf was a small package. It was different than the other
products; there was no listed price on the shelf. Reaching down, I
picked one up to examine it. A lone mustard-yellow pill sat securely in
a plastic cocoon fastened to a cardboard backboard, which was colorfully
designed with a cartoon bear sleeping in a bed with three Z’s rising
from it. The product’s name was written across the board in yellow
print.

“Doc Harden’s Goodnight.”

Underneath, in smaller print, was the slogan:

“You never know where a good night’s sleep will take you.”

Further down on the cardboard in a bright red circle, it claimed

“One pill is good for six months!”

I stared blankly at the package in my hand; this clearly was a gag; no
sleeping pill is good for six months. But at the same time, it was
compelling, and I had tried everything that doctors had suggested;
nothing was working, so what’s the harm in trying? These were the
thoughts in my mind as I made my way to the front.

I tossed the package onto the checkout counter; the man behind the
counter looked down at it and then to me.

“Can’t sleep, eh?” he said with a slight smile

“Yeah.” I shrugged

“Are you sure you want this one?” he asked

“I’ve tried everything else, why not?”

He shrugged.

“Whatever you say, man.”

“What’s the price?” I asked as I reached for my wallet.

He smiled

“Tell you what, if it helps, come back tomorrow and pay; if it doesn’t
help, don’t worry about it.”

“Alright, thanks,” I said as I put the package into my pocket and headed
out the door.

 “See you next time.” He said as the door closed behind me.

It was after 4 when I got home. Shuffling my way into the kitchen, I
pulled out the pill and stared at it. Its burnt yellow color revealed
the occasional sparkle of glitter from within it. Filling a glass of
water, I ripped the pill from the package, popped it into my mouth, and
washed it down with water. Taking my shoes off, I entered the bedroom
and plopped onto the bed.

It was noon when my ringing phone woke me. I was on the floor about
three feet from my bed, but despite that, for the first time in a year,
I felt rested and refreshed. I found my phone on the nightstand and
answered it.

“Hello?” I said with a yawn.

“Ryan, are you ok?” It was my boss, Marie.

“Oh shoot, sorry, Marie. I slept through my alarm. I’m on my way.”

“Well, it’s good to hear you’re sleeping. Don’t worry about it, just get
here when you can.”

“I will, thank you.” Hanging up, I quickly changed and brushed my teeth
before hopping in my car and heading to work.

That day was amazing. I had forgotten how enjoyable life was when you
have a full night’s sleep. I felt alert and aware. On my way home, I
decided to stop by the pharmacy and pay for the pill since it seemed to
be working. When I came to the place I had been walking the night
before, there was no pharmacy. I walked around the block twice, then
explored the two surrounding blocks as well, but there was no pharmacy
anywhere in the area.

Confused, I returned to my car

“Must have been in a different spot,” I muttered and drove towards home.

After cooking some dinner and enjoying a show, I hopped in the shower
and prepared for bed. For the first night in a long time, I could barely
keep my eyes open, and there was no battle for sleep; my body
surrendered before my head hit the pillow.

Sunlight and distant chirping birds woke me up; it was late morning, and
I was lying on the living room floor. I looked around, confused; this
wasn’t where I had fallen asleep, and I didn’t remember leaving the bed.

Pulling myself off the floor, I walked up the stairs to the bedroom.

“Must have been sleepwalking,” I thought as I entered the bedroom.

My phone had three missed calls and a voicemail from Marie.

“Where are you? Your shift started at 8. Get here soon.” I could tell
she was frustrated.

I looked at the time; it was a little after 10.

“Crap,” I muttered as I hurried to get ready.

At 10:45, I pulled into the parking lot at work. Rushing in, I was
greeted by an annoyed Marie.

“Where’ve you been?” she asked coldly.

“I’m sorry. I overslept; I’m trying a new medication, and it’s working a
little too well.”

She sighed.

“Look, I know you’ve had a hard time these last couple of months, but
when you’re supposed to be here, we need you here.”

I nodded.

“I know, I’m sorry.”

“This is your last warning; don’t let it happen again.”

I hurried to clock in.

“Got it.”

That night, I set several alarms and planned to go to bed earlier in
hopes of waking up on time. I lay in bed staring at the ceiling; I could
feel myself growing drowsy. I resisted, but my eyelids were far too
heavy. As they closed and I drifted off, I could have sworn I heard the
creaking of footsteps in the hallway.

When I woke, I was in the basement. The clock on the wall read 1:30.

“Oh Crap” I sighed as I ran up to the bedroom.

My phone was in its place on the nightstand, and there was one missed
call and a voicemail.

“Sorry, Ryan, you were more reliable when you weren’t sleeping. No hard
feelings; hope you get better.”

I clenched my jaw in anger.

“Damn it,” I yelled to myself.

I spent the day binge-watching bad TV shows. I made up my mind to try to
resist the effects of the pill, so I stayed on the couch playing video
games. But right when the clock hit nine, a wave of exhaustion consumed
me. My limbs felt like they were made of stone, and my eyes seemed to
close on their own. Too weak to fight it anymore, I tossed the
controller onto the chair beside the couch and stuck my phone into my
pocket. My eyes closed for the final time, and as my mind fell into
slumber, the last thing I remember was feeling the couch shift as
something sat next to me.

I didn’t know where I was when I woke. The sounds of wind in the trees,
birds, and insects singing told me I wasn’t in my home anymore. I rubbed
the sleep from my eyes and looked around. I had been lying in tall,
unkempt brownish grass littered with yellow and red leaves. Bare trees
surrounded the small clearing I had been lying in. It was cold; the wind
blew, sending a chill up my already shivering back. I spun in a circle,
hoping to see something familiar, but there was nothing, just trees.

Panic was setting in; I stood there shivering in a T-shirt and
sweatpants, only socks covered my feet. I pulled my phone from my
pocket; it had one bar. There were two notifications from my doorbell
camera; the first had recorded movement from the night at 3:03 AM. I
opened the recording and watched as the sleeping me clumsily exited the
house and walked off the porch and half ran, half stumbled down the yard
and disappeared into the night. I watched it again and noticed something
strange. I was holding out my left arm in front of my body, as if
someone was leading me by the hand.

I watched the way I was moving; it looked like someone pulling on my arm
and dragging me behind. Closing that recording, I opened the second one,
which was captured at 4:45 am. It was short, about five seconds; the
camera caught the front door opening and closing by itself.

“What the hell?” I muttered as I watched the short video three or four
more times. A cold burst of wind reminded me where I was, and I opened
Google Maps on my phone and found out I was about 8 miles from my home
in the woods that surrounded the town. The walk home was slow and
miserable. On the outskirts of town is a small hardware store; it was
midday by the time I reached it, and my feet were freezing. I stumbled
inside and bought some steel-toed boots, which were about the only shoes
they sold, a thick jacket, and a few hand warmers. I was about to check
out when an idea entered my head.

I searched the store and finally found zip ties; I picked out a pack of
their thickest ones and then went to check out. It was late afternoon
when I finally made it home. Cautiously, I entered and quickly made my
way to the kitchen, grabbing the biggest knife I owned. I checked every
room in the place, and no one was there. Relieved to find no one, I
turned my attention to locking every door and window. Night came faster
than I wanted, and I soon felt myself becoming sleepy.

Entering my bedroom, I lay down on the bed and took two zip ties and
used them to tie my right wrist to the headboard, hiding the kitchen
knife under my pillow just before sleep overpowered me.

What woke me was pain in my right arm and the sound of furniture
scraping across the floor. Instinctively, I first looked to my right
hand. The zip ties were tearing deep into my flesh of my hand; they had
dug in and were shredding my skin as whatever held me pulled harder.
Deep red streams of blood were running down my forearm and dripped onto
my pillow right next to my head. At that same moment, I felt something
else: strong, bony fingers wrapped around my left ankle.

I swallowed and slowly looked down towards my leg; there, at the foot of
the bed, was a tall, bone-thin, vague shape of a man. Its skin was grey
and damp, and its arms were far too long for its body; one hand was
wrapped around my ankle as it tried to pull me towards the door. It
glanced back at me, and I caught a glimpse of its pure black eyes with
tiny white pupils; a wide smile split its face, revealing crooked, dirty
teeth.

I screamed and grabbed the knife under my pillow, violently swinging it
in the air. It turned to face me, a look of shock on its face, as if it
didn’t expect me to be awake, but it was quickly replaced by a smile; it
then released my leg and calmly backed towards the door. It gave a
little wave and then walked out, shutting the door behind it.

The moment the door closed, I worked to cut the zip ties. Adrenaline
made it quick work; setting into my slippers, I opened the bedroom
window and climbed out, lowering myself down and dropping the remaining
four feet, twisting my ankle in the process, but that didn’t stop me I
limped my way down the street as fast as I could.

I found myself mindlessly limping down the streets at 3 AM after
fighting and losing the battle for my house. I dragged myself through
the sleeping town; the cold autumn air pricked my face as the town’s
streetlights flickered above my head. In the distance, the warm
fluorescent glow of a lone store lit up the darkness.

It was the pharmacy.

The same one that started all this, the one I couldn’t find in the
daylight.

I flung the door open and was greeted by the same musty-sweet smell and
calming jazz. The same man was behind the counter; he lowered his
newspaper and smiled.

“Welcome back. How are you sleeping?” he asked in a pleasant tone.

I stared at him for a moment.

“How do I make it stop?” I whispered as I stared at him unblinking.

He stared back with a warm smile, though the muscles in his cheeks
twitched slightly.

“Did you take the pill?” he asked.

“Yes.”

“Well, the effects last six months,” was his direct reply.

My eyes lowered to the floor.

“Where is it taking me?” I asked without looking at him.

He chuckled.

“You never know where a good night’s sleep will take you.”

I yawned.

“Looks like your still tired, you should probably get going.”

Another yawn followed.

He pointed down the street.

“There’s a hotel down the street, if I were you I’d head there.”

I couldn’t look him in the eyes but I was sure he was smiling widely. I
turned to leave.

“Sir.” He said as I reached the door.

I turned back to him.

“You owe us $13.49 for the pill.”

I clenched my jaw and walked back to the counter to pay.

“Sweet dreams.” He said as I exited.

I’m writing this as a warning: if you see anything made by Doc Harden,
avoid it.

I’m in a hotel now. I don’t think the thing ever intended to take me
back home. It was waiting for me to fall asleep again.

I can barely keep my eyes open. I wanted to write this before sleep
claims me.

Because I don’t know where it will take me.
