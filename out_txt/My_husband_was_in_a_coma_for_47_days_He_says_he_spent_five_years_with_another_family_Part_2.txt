I wasn’t expecting so many people to respond to my last post. I honestly
thought a handful of people would read it, tell me I was crazy, and that
would be the end of it. Instead, I’ve spent the last two days reading
comments from people who have theories about what happened to Chase.
Some people think it’s a neurological issue. Others think he experienced
some kind of alternate reality. A few people have suggested that Claire
might be someone from his past.

I wish it were that simple.

There were also several people who pointed out something I hadn’t really
considered when writing my original post. If Chase only remembers
spending five years with Claire, how does he remember Noah being eight
and Lily being five? How does he remember bringing both children home
from the hospital?
I actually asked him about that.

His response was strange. He said, “I don’t remember five years from the
beginning, Miya. I remember five years of being their dad.”

I asked him what that meant. He just stared at me. Then he said, “I
remember their entire lives. I just remember being with them for five
years.”
I asked him if he could explain the difference. He couldn’t.

I decided not to push it. He’s still recovering, and I don’t want to
make things worse by constantly questioning him about something he
clearly finds upsetting. But something happened yesterday that I can’t
stop thinking about.

After my last post, I decided I needed to know more about the phone
number Claire had been using to contact him. Chase wouldn’t let me look
through his phone, so I waited until he fell asleep and wrote the number
down. I searched it online, but nothing came up. I tried a reverse phone
lookup service. Nothing useful. I even searched the number on social
media.

Nothing.

The following morning, I asked Chase if he would let me see the messages
again. He hesitated, but eventually handed me his phone. There were four
new messages. The first said, “Please answer me.” The second said, “Noah
keeps asking about you.” The third said, “Lily thinks you’re coming home
soon.” The fourth was sent at 2:14 a.m. It said, “I know you’re there. I
know you can hear me.”

I asked Chase why he hadn’t told me about the messages. He said he
didn’t want me to worry. I almost laughed at that. As if I wasn’t
already worried.
I asked him if he had spoken to Claire again. He nodded. I asked what
she said. He looked down at his hands for a moment before answering.
“She wants to know why I left.”

I told him that he hadn’t left anyone. He’d been in a coma. He hadn’t
gone anywhere. He looked at me and said something that made my stomach
turn. “That’s what you think.”

I didn’t respond. He continued, “Miya, I remember being in that house. I
remember waking up next to her. I remember Noah coming into our bedroom
because he had a nightmare. I remember Lily sitting on my lap while I
made breakfast. I remember all of it. You keep telling me those people
don’t exist, but I don’t know how to explain something I actually
lived.”
I asked him if he missed Claire. He didn’t answer immediately. Then he
said, “I miss my children.” I don’t think I’ve ever felt so helpless.

Later that afternoon, I decided to look at the house again. I still had
the address Chase had given me, and I wanted to see if I could find
anything else. I searched the property records and found something
interesting. The house was real. It wasn’t just a listing that looked
similar. It was an actual property at the exact address Chase had
described.

I found an older listing with photographs of the interior. The kitchen
was almost identical to what Chase had described. There was a staircase
near the living room, a small hallway leading to two bedrooms, and a
backyard with a large tree near the fence.

Then I found a photograph of the basement.

I nearly dropped my phone.

There was a small storage room tucked behind the stairs. Exactly where
Chase said he had built one.
I called him into the room and showed him the photograph. He went
completely still. “That’s it,” he whispered.

I asked him what he meant.
“That’s our house.”

I reminded him that it wasn’t our house. He looked at me. “Not yours.
Mine and Claire’s.”

Then he zoomed in on the photograph. He pointed to something on the
wall. There was a small pencil marking near the doorframe. He told me
that was where he used to measure Noah’s height.
I couldn’t see anything clearly in the photograph, but he insisted it
was there. Then he said something I wasn’t prepared for.

“There’s another one upstairs.”
I asked him what he was talking about. He said there was a mark on the
inside of Lily’s bedroom closet. Claire used to measure her height
there, too.
I closed the photograph. I didn’t want to look anymore.

That evening, I made dinner while Chase sat in the living room.
Everything felt normal for a little while. We talked about television,
the groceries, and some things we needed to take care of around the
house.

Then his phone rang.

Unknown number.

I watched him look at the screen. He didn’t answer. It rang again. And
again. Finally, he picked up.

“Hello?”

I couldn’t hear the person on the other end, but I could see Chase’s
expression changing. His face went pale.

“Claire?”

He stood up. I put down what I was holding. “What is it?” I mouthed.

He raised one finger, asking me to wait. Then he said something that I
will never forget.

“What do you mean they’re still waiting?”

There was a long silence. He started crying.

“Claire, please.”

Another pause.

Then he whispered, “I don’t remember.”

He ended the call.

I immediately asked what happened. He wouldn’t look at me. I asked him
what Claire had said. He sat down on the couch and covered his face with
his hands. Eventually, he told me.

Claire had asked him why he hadn’t come home. He told her he couldn’t.
She asked him if he remembered what happened to the children. And
apparently, Chase had started screaming. He told me he didn’t remember
the rest of the conversation.
I asked him what she meant by that. He shook his head. Then he said, “I
think something happened to Noah and Lily.”

I asked him what kind of something.

He looked at me with tears running down his face.
“I don’t know. But I remember Claire crying. I remember her telling me
that we needed to get them out. I remember running upstairs.”

He stopped.

I asked him what happened next.

He said he couldn’t remember.

That night, I couldn’t sleep. Around 1:30 a.m., I got up to get some
water. Chase was asleep beside me, but his phone was sitting on the
nightstand. It lit up.
Another message.

I shouldn’t have looked. I know I shouldn’t have. But I picked it up.

The message was from Claire.
It said, “I know you remember now. Please don’t make me explain it
again.”

I stared at the screen. Then another message appeared.

“Ask him what happened on November 14.”

I didn’t know what November 14 meant. I looked at Chase. He was still
asleep.

I opened the message thread and scrolled back through the older
conversations, hoping to find some explanation. That’s when I noticed
something.
Every message from Claire had a date and time. Most of them had been
sent within the last few weeks. But there was one message that was
different.
It had been sent on the night of Chase’s accident.

The timestamp was 8:43 p.m.

The exact time of the crash.

I felt sick.
The message was short.

“Chase, please come home. Something is wrong with the children.”

I dropped the phone onto the bed.
I don’t know how long I sat there. I kept thinking about the accident.
About the rain. About the phone call I received from the hospital. About
the fact that Chase had been driving home when it happened.

Then I remembered something else.

The accident report said the other driver had crossed into Chase’s lane.
But Chase had never told me what he was doing immediately before the
collision.
I had never asked.

The next morning, I showed him the message. He stared at it for a long
time. Then I asked him what happened on November 14.

He looked at me.

And for the first time since waking up from his coma, he seemed
genuinely afraid.

“That’s the night we lost them,” he said.

I couldn’t breathe.

I asked him what he meant. He started shaking his head.

“I thought they were safe. I thought I got them out.”
I asked him where.

He looked at me.

Then he said something that I don’t think I’ll ever forget.

“Miya, I don’t think I woke up from the accident because I was supposed
to.”

I asked him what that meant.
He started crying. Then he told me that, in the other life, the last
thing he remembered before everything went black was driving home to
Claire and the children.

It had been raining.
He remembered headlights coming toward him.
He remembered the sound of a horn.
And he remembered Claire calling his phone.
I asked him what time the accident happened in that life.

He looked at me.

“8:43.”

The exact time of his real accident.
I don’t know what to believe anymore. But there’s something I haven’t
told you.

I went back and looked at the photograph of the house one more time. I
zoomed in on the basement storage room. There was something I hadn’t
noticed before.

A small photograph was sitting on a shelf.
It was a family photograph.
Chase was standing in the middle, with Claire beside him and Noah and
Lily in front of them.
But that’s not what scared me.
It was the date written on the back of the photograph.
November 14.

And underneath the date, in handwriting I recognized immediately, were
five words.

“Last Christmas before everything changed.”
The handwriting belonged to my husband.
I haven’t shown Chase the photograph yet.
I’m afraid of what he’ll remember.
