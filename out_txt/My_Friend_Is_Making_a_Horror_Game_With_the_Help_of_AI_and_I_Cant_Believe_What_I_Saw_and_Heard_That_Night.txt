Last night, I texted my friend about his game project that he told me
few week ago. It was almost midnight, and he was about to go to sleep,
but he said he wanted to show me what he had so far by streaming it over
Discord.

He told me he was making a horror game with the help of AI. He’s always
been interested in making horror games, and this was the first time he’d
tried making something like this.

He sounded excited about it, and even though I’m a bit scared of horror
games, I still agreed to see what he’d been working on.

At first, I thought it was just going to be another small indie horror
game project. I had no idea what I was about to see.

I haven’t really asked him about his plans for the game yet, so I’ll
keep the game’s title a secret for now. As far as I know, it’s still a
work in progress.

The first thing he showed me was the main menu.

Honestly, it looked pretty decent. It almost looked like a finished
game. But you know, a menu screen is still just the cover of a book.

He clicked the Start button and showed me the intro.

It started with a truck driving down a narrow road in the middle of the
woods. Everything looked quiet, with trees on both sides of the road.

Then, out of nowhere, the truck hit the log pretty hard and came to a
stop. It looked like the impact had damaged the truck and it can’t drive
any further.

The character got out of the truck to check what had happened. He looked
around for a moment, then started walking down the road. After only a
few steps, he came across an old wooden cabin.

At this point, my friend had control of the character. He walked inside
the cabin and started looking around.

There were a few items he could pick up, including some matches and an
old flashlight. The flashlight wasn’t working properly. Its light kept
flickering on and off.

My friend explained that lighting was going to be a really important
part of the game.

He then showed me the first objective. The player had to find fuel and
refill the generator outside the cabin. Since the game was still in
development, there wasn’t much explanation about why we needed to do
this. I just assumed it was to restore power to the cabin.

At that point, everything seemed pretty normal for a horror game. I was
actually pretty impressed with how good the atmosphere looked so far.
The lighting, the cabin, and the dark forest all came together really
well.

But then something unexpected appeared on the screen.

Right next to the player, there was a monster.

I actually jumped a little when I saw it. Its design looked like a black
reindeer, but with a skeleton-like body. Its muzzle was unusually large,
with long teeth sticking out of its mouth. Its eyes were hollow, and its
antlers were wide and stretched out on both sides of its head. There
were also a few pieces of old cloth hanging from its antlers, which made
the whole thing look even more terrifying.

I was still staring at the screen when my friend casually said:

“Oh yeah, this is the monster I created. I also made some basic
pathfinding for it. Oh, and it doesn’t have an attack animation or the
script to chase the player yet.”

Honestly, the monster looked really good. At least, that’s how I felt
about it. I’m not someone who plays a lot of horror games, so I get
scared pretty easily. But there was one thing that made me feel a less
nervous. The monster was still in development, so I figured it was just
a creepy model that could walk around the map for now.

At least, that’s what I thought.

After that, my friend went back inside the cabin to show me some of the
other things he’d been working on. He showed me a few assets, like a
bookshelf and some pieces of cloth hanging around the room. He explained
that he had made some of these himself.

He even pointed out the difference in quality between his own models and
the free assets he had downloaded from Unreal Engine. He said I could
easily notice the difference in the small details and textures.

While he was showing me around, he moved the camera toward a long
hallway inside the cabin. The hallway was almost completely dark. There
was only a little light coming through a window, barely enough to see
what was at the other end.

Then he suddenly stopped moving. At the far end of the hallway,
something was standing there.

It was the monster.

At first, I didn’t think much about it. I just assumed my friend had
placed the monster model there to show me how it looked in the dark.

But then my friend said something that still gives me chills whenever I
think about it:

“Oh, how did it get in here? I’m pretty sure I set it up so that its
model couldn’t enter this area yet!”

At that moment, my heart sank.

I looked at the screen again, trying to understand what he had just
said. A few seconds ago, he was casually showing me his game assets.
Now, he was staring at the monster at the end of the hallway, clearly
confused about how it got there.

At that moment, I just hoped my friend was joking and already knew what
was going on. But he didn’t seem to be joking.

He switched to Unreal Engine’s editor mode and started looking around to
figure out how the monster had managed to get inside the cabin. He
checked a few things, but he couldn’t find any reason why the monster
was there.

I don’t know much about Unreal Engine, and honestly, I don’t even know
how familiar my friend with it. I was starting to feel a little scared,
but I tried to stay calm.

I told him that maybe it was just a bug. Maybe something went wrong with
the monster’s pathfinding.

That was what I really wanted to believe. I hoped that was the
explanation.

My friend agreed and said he would try to find a way to fix it. Then he
started showing me around the editor mode. One of the things he showed
me was a list of animations he had created for the monster.

He explained that he had used AI to help generate some of the animations
he had requested. There were animations for looking around, walking,
crawling, turning its head, and a few other basic movements. Everything
seemed normal until he reached the last animation on the list.

He clicked on it.

The monster suddenly stood up on its two back legs and raised both arms
into the air. Then it started making a strange scratching motion with
its hands.

I stared at the screen for a moment. That looked a lot like a monster’s
attack animation.

My friend seemed surprised too. He watched it for a few seconds before
saying:

“Oh, that one looks creepy. I don’t remember asking it to make something
like that.”

I just kept looking at the screen and can’t believe what heard. Because
if my friend was telling the truth, then who—or what—had made that
animation?

My heart started racing, and my mind was filled with questions.

What exactly was going on with my friend’s game? Was he messing with me
this entire time? Was this all some kind of joke?

I was so caught up in my thoughts that before I could even ask him
anything, I saw him close the game.He told me that was pretty much
everything he wanted to show me. That was all he had so far for the
project, and it was getting pretty late. We both had work the next
morning, so we decided to call it a night.

But even after we ended the call, I couldn’t stop thinking about what I
had seen. Why was the monster able to enter an area where it wasn’t
supposed to be? Where did that attack animation come from? Did the AI
create it without my friend asking for it?

I kept going over everything in my head, trying to find a reasonable
explanation.

There’s just one thing I can’t get out of my mind.

My friend said the monster couldn’t attack because he hadn’t created
that part of the game yet.

But how can he be so sure?

Maybe the monster really couldn’t attack him.

Or maybe…

Maybe it recognized that the person playing the game was its Creator.

And maybe that’s the only reason it didn’t attack my friend’s character.
