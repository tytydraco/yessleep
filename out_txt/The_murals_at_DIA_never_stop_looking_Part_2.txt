[Part 1]

Hey everyone, I promised I would investigate the rest of the murals and
the suspicious things around this airport soon! But first, here’s the
rest of what happened last night (it was a really crazy night indeed).

The only empty seat remaining on the matte-black, Code Black 777 had my
name on it.

The letters were stitched directly into the gray fabric of the headrest,
glowing faintly in the dim amber cabin light. The tourist kept staring
at me, his head cocked at an unnatural angle. Beside him, the little
girl kept her pale, unblinking eyes locked on mine, her finger pointing
rigidly at the empty cushion.

The freezing vacuum of the cabin pulled at my uniform. I took an
involuntary step forward. My steel-toed boot hit the metal track of the
aisle.

Then, the little girl’s expression changed. She stopped pointing at the
seat. She turned her attention entirely to the tourist. She unbuckled
her belt, stood up in the narrow aisle, and reached out with a pale,
freezing hand. She pressed her flat palm against the tourist’s forehead.
His spine arched violently against the seat and he let out a wet popping
sound. His eyes rolled completely into the back of his head, exposing
yellow veins, and a thin line of black fluid leaked from his left
nostril.

The vacuum inside the cabin instantly died. I stumbled backward, my
boots slipping on the slick terminal tiles, collapsing hard onto the
floor. The tourist went completely limp, dropping like a stone. His neck
pillow tore open on the ground, scattering thousands of tiny white beads
on the floor like snowflakes.

Inside the cabin, the little girl turned back to look at me. Her
neon-red eyes bored into my skull. She smiled a grotesque, too-wide
smile that stretched the skin around her mouth. She raised a single pale
finger to her lips, and backed away into the shadows of the fuselage.

The seats collapsed. The landing gear folded backward into the
underbelly and the jet reversed, phasing right back through the drywall
and glass before disappearing completely.

The terminal was instantly silent.

I scrambled over to the tourist. He was alive, staring blindly at the
ceiling. I grabbed his shoulder. “Hey. Stand up. We gotta move.” He
didn’t blink. His facial muscles began to twitch, spasming violently as
his mouth stretched into the exact same smile the little girl had just
given me. His trembling hands reached down, grabbed an invisible
seatbelt, and clicked it together over his lap.

“Whiskey-Oscar-Seven-Nine-Delta-Echo,” he whispered. His voice didn’t
sound like him anymore. It sounded like a tape recorder playing
backward.

A six-character reservation number.

I dragged him to a charging station near Gate B32 and propped him up,
tossing a spare blanket I had previously found nearby on his legs. He
sat, grinning his bleeding gums at the ceiling.

My radio squawked. It was Miller, the graveyard lead. “Ramp Four, where
the hell are you? United 1142 from Dulles is on final for the east pad.
Grab the tug and get ground power out there.” I checked the time, and
saw that it was already past 4. Damn, I thought, the trance had lasted
pretty long.

I keyed the mic, my hand shaking. “Copy. En route.”

I rushed down the service stairs and hit the tarmac. Ten minutes later,
I was standing under a domestic United 737, shoving a heavy GPU cable
into its belly. The twin engines were deafening, loud and dirty. But at
least the aircraft was real.

Except, as the fumes of Jet-A washed over me, I realized I couldn’t
smell it. All I could smell was the musty swamp water from the ghost
777’s cabin. I looked down at my hands. They were trembling. Every
shadow cast by the baggage carts looked like it was stretching toward
me. The airport itself seemed like an otherworldly being. 

Shift ended at 06:00. I didn’t want to investigate or play detective. I
just wanted to get the hell out of the airport.

I swiped my badge to cross back into the public concourse, keeping my
head down. Early morning commuters were already trickling in by half
past six, hauling roller bags toward TSA and buying overpriced airport
coffee. I watched a businessman in a suit walk right past the Notre
Denver installation of bronze gargoyles sitting inside open suitcases in
baggage claim. As the man walked past, a bronze gargoyle slowly turned
its heavy metal head, tracking him. Its jaws were parted, and I could
hear a faint, staticky whisper coming from its throat, reciting the
businessman’s full name.

The floor felt wrong, too. The Jeppesen Terminal is famous for the
bronze fossils embedded in the floor. I looked down at a brass trilobite
near my boot. Instead of being a bug, it was a flattened and screaming
human face, cast in polished bronze, trapped under a layer of floor wax.
Hundreds of people were walking right over it.

I wanted to run, and to scream. But I remembered my supervisor’s one
rule: Don’t look.

The airport feeds on attention. The tourists taking selfies, the people
staring at the murals, they are all willingly offering themselves up.
The 777 didn’t take me because I broke the tether. It almost took the
tourist because he surrendered to it.

I forced my eyes up. I stepped over the screaming bronze face, like it
was just a scuff mark on the floor. I walked past the baggage claim,
keeping my gaze locked dead ahead. I completely ignored the gargoyle
that was now leaning halfway out of its bronze suitcase, clawing toward
my vest.

I didn’t run. Running makes you prey. I walked briskly to the employee
exit, swiped my badge, and pushed through the revolving doors into the
freezing morning. The sun was up. I was out.

When I got to my car, my phone buzzed in my pocket. It was an automated
text from the scheduling system.

Shift updated. Tomorrow: 23:00 - 07:00. Assignment: Concourse A,
Sub-basement Baggage Tunnels.

I slumped against the steering wheel, shivering in the cold. I should
quit. I should drive down Peña Boulevard and never come back. But as I
glanced in my rearview mirror, I saw the massive, glowing red eyes of
Blucifer, the blue mustang statue, staring down at the highway. They
weren’t just statues. They claimed lives. If I quit, I was just another
civilian waiting to be catalogued and copied. But as a ramp agent, I had
clearance and radio. And I have tomorrow night’s shift to figure out
what the hell they were actually feeding down in the tunnels.
