  My job isn’t for the faint-hearted. I mean, I guess it’s not. I work
with the FBI. Mostly at the murder scenes, with my camera.
  Sometimes I don’t even think I’m strong enough to do this, but the pay
is just too good for me to quit because of a weak stomach. I don’t
really want to disclose the amount, but it’s enough so that my parents
can retire when they’re ready to, and I can support us all. The only
real sacrifice is my ability to sleep soundly. Some of those scenes are
just… Extremely graphic. But that leads me to what happened three days
ago. Something that just felt too surreal. Like I was having a fever
dream. And yet, the photo on my desk speaks for itself.
  It was supposed to be just another murder scene. Some young girl - too
young to have her life cut so abruptly - was found in her deceased
parent’s house after she was trying to settle back in. I was just taking
photos, like I’m paid to do, but something about this whole scene rubbed
me the wrong way; maybe it was the fact that the police couldn’t locate
the murder weapon, or it was just the stuffy air in the whole house,
like it was holding its breath. When you take photos of murders, as long
as I have, you’ll find things that don’t add up in certain moments.
  I know I should’ve minded my business, but after I was done, I
listened in on what the police were mumbling about- nothing important,
just what they had planned for the weekend. How they can plan their
lives with a dead girl in the room is beyond me. I slowly sneaked
towards the back door. Conveniently, it was raining, and something in my
gut just told me to go there.
  But outside were no footsteps, not even the police went out.
  That alone made the hairs on the back of my neck stand up.
I just turned and started walking around the house, checking all the
closed doors.
  The downstairs bathroom, unlocked and empty. The storage under the
stairs, unlocked and empty, only with some full trash bags of random
shit.
  I was checking all over the bottom floor while more and more officers
were yelling at me because I was contaminating the crime scene. No one
stopped me, because why would they risk touching a 6-foot-8, buff man
that could bench-press them like it’s nothing?
Had to try and explain my theory.
Most said they didn’t believe me, even if their faces said otherwise,
but I didn’t blame them. Still, one gave me gloves and started looking
with me.
  The whole house turned out empty, but it still didn’t feel right.
  So I looked again, and in that storage beneath the stairs? The one
with the trash bags? One bag had shifted when I opened the door.
  I came closer, one slow step at a time. “Police, put your hands in the
air!” I said, trying to fiddle with my camera to make it sound like a
gun.
  Out of the bag jumped a dude, knife in hand. The damn murder weapon
that they couldn’t find.
  I got startled, and in my shock, I took a photo by accident- snap!
Falling to the ground, I waited for the end. Only, I just heard a thud,
and I didn’t feel the knife anywhere in my body.
  Opening my eyes, I was horrified to see the dude, dead on the floor,
eyes wide with terror, mouth still open in that damn scream that made me
nearly jump out of my skin.
  Back home, after a checkup at the hospital and a talk with the police,
I was staring at the camera, confused. That dude couldn’t have just
dropped dead like that.
  It was impossible, right? Even a heart attack wouldn’t be this quick.
Maybe the autopsy will tell the police more. Maybe.
  I had photos that needed developing.
And it nearly caused me to drop dead, because when I developed the last
photo I took- the accidental one of the guy who attacked me? Yeah, there
was something behind him, hands wrapped around his throat, as if
crushing his windpipe. And the thing, even if it was just a black fog,
seemed to have been staring past the camera. At me.
