Hey. So I work maintenance at a old hospital. We’re renovating the
basement and most of it was closed off but the rest was for storage.

Mostly its full of broken equipment, junk and stuff nobody got around to
tossing.

I found a old laptop. I asked my supervisor if I could have it. He said
no and just to junk it, but I took it home. I had a power jack that fit
and it turned on no problem.

After it booted up, it asked me if I wanted to continue where I left
off. It popped up with a text document.

It’s pretty crazy and I wanted to share it.

I have to transcribe it since I can’t copy paste it anywhere. It wont
connect to anything even when I hard line it with an Ethernet cable or
hook it to my PC.

Here’s the first few entries:

** Progress Note: 11/6/2024, 7:46 AM**

*Holloway County Medical Center*

I’m writing this on one of the scribes’ laptops. I found it plugged into
the wall, sitting on a folding chair. I couldn’t log onto the EMR or
access the internet, but it did have a notepad.

I’m documenting this because it’s ingrained into me to write everything
down, or because it’s helping me filter what’s happening. If it wasn’t
charted, it didn’t happen.

I found the laptop yesterday, but I couldn’t do much with it. I was
stuck replaying the events of everything in my head, over and over
again, as some trauma response.

Thinking about it now feels like I’m recalling someone else’s memory.

I’m embarrassed about how yesterday went down. I used to think I’d
always be useful in a true catastrophe.

I always did well with medical emergencies, but it’s muscle memory at
this point.

If it wasn’t for Cam, I don’t know if I’d be writing this right now.

He dragged me down here after Gustavo hurt that lady…

Hurt is a loose term; he bashed her head in with a portable AED as we
were running a code on her in the hallway.

Everyone was freaking out, and all the trauma bays were used up or
destroyed. It was just Gustavo and me taking care of her. Just me as a
third-year ER resident, and him, a veteran ER tech, trying to save this
lady at the end of the world.

I had to switch off chest compressions on this lady. I had been going
for 5 minutes by then and was wiped out. Gustavo took over. He started
grunting, and the skin around his neck started turning red. His face
started swelling, and his eyes became jaundiced.

He started really leaning into the chest compressions, way harder than
he should have. I grabbed his shoulder; he shrugged it off and stared
into me… or past me. He looked like he was confused.

He grunted and turned back to the patient, and started beating her with
his hands. He picked the AED and started slamming it into her chest,
then onto her face. Her face fractured immediately. Comminuted and
depressed. Incredible maxillofacial trauma.

I just stood there staring, holding the Ambu like a scolded toddler.

Luckily, I don’t think she felt a thing.

Cam came up and grabbed me. He led me away from Gustavo and the lady
with the caved in face. He looked terrified and said something to me,
but I don’t remember.

We passed a man repeatedly slamming his head into the same shattered
window and a pair of nurses on the ground with their hands locked around
the other’s throat. They were not struggling against each other; they
were committed to squeezing as hard as they could. Both of their faces
were swollen with bulging jaundiced eyes.

We ran through the back corridor behind the Radiology department. Down a
stairwell I’ve never noticed before. Once we got to the bottom, we
exited the stairwell, and the doors slammed shut behind us on their own.
Probably a lockdown given everything that went down.

I think we’re in the sub-basement. Looks like it used to be a rehab
floor, but now it’s for overflow storage. A few dead vending machines. A
place where bad equipment goes to die.

The air is warm and stale down here, like old man breath.

The exit sign above the stairwell door still works, but the ‘T’
flickers, and it lets out a constant hum.

Cam and I haven’t talked much in the last few hours. After trying his
damndest to get the stairwell door open, he just went to sleep on one of
the mattresses that were leaning against the far wall. Maybe I should do
the same.

**Progress Note: 11/7/2024, 4:34 PM**

*Holloway County Medical Center*

I woke up to Cam working on the stairwell door again. He said it sounded
like the stairs collapsed. He called it a thunderous, crashing,
cacophony. I slept like the dead and didn’t hear anything.

There’s a frosted window that takes up most of the top half of the door,
reinforced with little wires. Something put a long crack in the glass,
and now it rests on the opposite side of the window but I can’t make out
what it is.

He thinks we can punch out the glass and wiggle our way out.

I told him I don’t like the idea of wiggling anywhere when we don’t know
what’s on the other side. My mind went to Gustavo and my patient’s caved
in face.

He didn’t say it, but he seemed frustrated. His nose wrinkled a bit
before he sighed, like he smelled something sour, cowardice maybe. He
dragged his mattress closer to the door and lay down for a long time.

Desperate to feel useful, I looked around the basement. I found a row of
lockers. There was a mirror at the bottom of the last locker in the row.
Hung it back up on its hook and stared at the gaunt figure reflected
back at me as I dusted my hands off on my pants.

I rubbed the stubble on my chin. I opened my mouth wide and stuck my
tongue out at my reflection. They say stress ages you, but damn, I
looked worse than I thought I did. It looked like someone tried to
sketch me from memory.

I continued to take stock of what the basement had to offer. I broke
open the old vending machines. There was a huge earwig about the size of
my pinky in one of the machines.

I’m not into bugs. I was repulsed, but I let it alone. It’s just trying
to survive, same as me.

I got some stale chips, expired granola bars, and beige protein drinks
from the vending machines.

I got lucky and found a small jar of chunky peanut butter and a Zagnut
bar in a matchbook-sized kitchenette. Plenty of paper plates and
Styrofoam coffee cups. No coffee, though.

Also no good sources of water. I found a case of sterile water in 500mL
bottles, which should be fine to drink so long as I supplement sodium
with salt packets from the kitchenette.

I told Cam, but he didn’t even acknowledge me.

Cam got up, used the toilet that didn’t flush, and started pacing. I
offered him some of the spoils of my scavenging. He took the granola bar
and thanked me. I asked if he wanted to take shifts on watch tonight.
Pointing to the exit sign, he said, “We don’t need shifts, the sign is
watching”. I chuckled a bit, looking for the joke, but he didn’t smile.

He used to whistle when he was nervous. Now he just clenches his jaw and
whispers things I can’t quite catch.

He’s the brave one. He’s the reason we’re still breathing, but I’m
concerned about him.

I suspect the stress of it all is catching up to him.

Acute stress reaction, or trauma-induced delirium, perhaps?

I’m hoping he’ll come around soon. After all, our cups runneth over with
sterile water, and we have what may well be the last Zagnut bar on
Earth. What more could we ask for?

**Progress Note: 11/9/2024, 12:09 PM**

*Hollow County Medical Center.*

I slept all of yesterday. The whole damn day. I had some weird dreams I
can barely remember. Going out to the ambulance bay behind the ER and
seeing carnage. People were tearing at each other’s throats. There was
the charred man whose eyes were yellow and denatured, staring at me. His
remains had that acrid, nauseating burnt smell that clung to my nostrils
after I woke up.

Cam says I was breathing fine. He tried to wake me once, but I wouldn’t
stir, so he let me rest, if you can call it that. I woke up with a stiff
back and a throat like old burlap.

Cam was busy while I slept. Real busy.

He was able to knock out the window on the door. He dug out a tunnel,
shoulder-width, just enough to squeeze through.

Some of the debris in the stairwell made sense. Plaster, splintered
wood, standard construction detritus. But then there’s other stuff.
Stuff with no business being in a hospital basement. There was a new
truck tire, a VCR, and a cracked porcelain bust of a sad clown, among
other things.

I asked Cam how he got it open. He didn’t answer right away.

Then he shrugged, nodded at the door, and said “It was locked from the
outside. I punched out the window with an IV pole.”

I told him I was impressed, which I was.

I’ve been drinking sterile water. It’s warm and plasticky, but better
than the stale, thick saliva that clung to the side of my mouth when I
woke up.

I didn’t eat yesterday. I choked down one of the dry granola bars with a
protein shake.

I could feel it slide down, way down my throat. Like my stomach had
shifted into my pelvis.

I offered to work on clearing out the debris and give Cam a break. He
declined and said the window was too small for both of us to be working
it.

He hasn’t been whispering to himself anymore. But now he’s humming. Low,
tuneless, and oddly steady, like the hum of the exit sign.

The whole time I was working on the door, he was pacing between the
tunnel and the locker with the mirror in it. He stares at his
reflection.

I’m not a fan of his new habits.

**Progress Note: 11/10/2024, 3:39 AM**

*Holloway County Medical Center*

I woke up again with that same scratchy throat. I must be congested from
all the dust in the debris and sleeping with my mouth open.

I had some more dreams of the ER. Someone was walking around the waiting
room, with a dead, limp infant. They carried the little body by its leg
like a bag of groceries. Everyone else in the waiting room was staring
at the same corner of the ceiling with their mouths open.

I got up and chugged most of a bottle of sterile water. It was warm and
plasticky, but it was better than the thick saliva that clings to the
side of my mouth when I wake up.

I spent the morning organizing the gauze pads in the procedure cart.

I sorted them by size and ply, then refolded the ones that had popped
open. Used some expired surgical tape to reseal the worst offenders.

Found two unopened boxes of Ace wraps. Still sealed, though the plastic
around the wraps was brittle. One of the boxes had a doodle of something
with long legs and a pincer-like shape, resembling a wishbone.

I also wiped down the backs of the cabinet handles with disinfectant
wipes. It doesn’t make sense; nobody touches the backs, but it felt
wrong to leave them grimy.

It looks much better now

…

I don’t know why it took me this long to notice.

I haven’t seen Cam since last night.

How long has he been gone?!

I thought he was just sleeping.

He took the fucking Zagnut bar but it looks like he left everything
else. His scrubs were in a neat folded pile near the tunnel.

I checked the tunnel; I almost yelled his name, but my larynx twisted,
as if my body was stopping me from breaking the silence. The exit sign
hummed above me, taunting me, daring me to yell into the abyss.

I don’t know how long I stood there staring into the tunnel, but I kept
waiting for something to move at the far end.

I was surprised when a huge, disgusting earwig came strutting out of the
tunnel. I think it’s the same one from the vending machine.

I flicked it, sending it back the way it came.

Cam’s probably fine.

I’m sure he’s doing a hell of a lot better than I would do in that
tunnel, probably halfway to daylight by now.

I hope he comes back soon.
