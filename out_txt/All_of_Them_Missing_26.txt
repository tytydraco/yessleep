I really couldn’t tell you. Can’t explain what had actually happened to
that campsite. It was so wrong in every single definable way, every
aspect askew. Nobody would believe me.

It was the October of 2018. A Boy Scout trip that I had tagged along
with. My brother had been a member for a while, and every fall there was
an annual Friday to Sunday camping trip at a not too far campsite.

I feel like there comes a time when everyone has to reckon with the fact
there Are things that we shouldn’t see, that deserve to stay within the
confines of a story. When you confront the impossible, that incredibly
important becomes Crystal Clear. You Don’t Want To Know. It’s for the
best.

I didn’t get a choice in the matter. I fear that I came face to face
with The Beast.

It was the second evening of the trip. The weather had been promising so
far, and the temperatures had been on the slightly cooler side. We had
done the Friday hike without issue, and were gearing up for the evenings
campfire tradition that was code for “we can eat everything we normally
can’t, and none of the parents would attempt to stop us.”

Our campsite was a bit isolated, and it would’ve taken some time to
reach the main road. The only other campsite was within walking
distance, and we had been eyeing it since it wasn’t in use at the time.
We assumed that no one had used it in a while, and that it would be fine
to play in the site as long as we didn’t disturb the set up.

The sun had ducked under the horizon 20-25 minutes prior. It was at that
brief moment in twilight where everything felt slightly hazy, the world
glowing in deep shades of evening blues. That was usually the time when
us kids began to roam around and let off steam from the constraints of
the structured activity.

My brother is two years younger than me but wasn’t scared by much. He
could do haunted houses and blow through horror movies like a pro. My
many routine attempts to spook him got me nowhere, so I didn’t think
daring him to check out the campsite with me would be a big deal. I
tried every angle, asked in every way I could think of. He relented, and
we headed over.

Something was wrong. The moment we stepped onto the site, a sickly
unease spread through me. The air hung in clumped pockets of thick
static. I turned to my brother, who was equally as disarmed. We shined
our flashlights across the site, hoping to get a glimpse of what was
causing the sensation. Our flashlights, which had been normal before
entering the premises, strangely only seemed to illuminate the interior
of the sites space. It was almost as if a wall was dividing us and our
campsite.

As we looked around, Everything about the campsite seemed normal. The
fireplace was upright, the ground clear of debris. We were about to head
back when my brother spotted the shattered bulletin board, papers
littered on the ground. Among the miscellaneous brochures and awareness
items was a site occupancy log. I flipped through it, starting all the
way at the beginning.

The first page dated back to August of 2007, with little notes written
on the side penned by camp group leaders and Scout leaders. I flipped
through it, scanning the lines for info. I got to the last page, which
was from September of 2016. One of the notes columns at the bottom of
the page had a note that said: “Jim Cherny, if you see This, Check the
back please. —>”

I slowly turned to the back.

In it was a note, six bullet points long.

1.  Don’t be afraid of The Beast. He likes it when you are.
2.  If you’re reading this note right now, he’s already smelled you.
3.  That energy field, it’s a sign that you’ve entered His domain
4.  All of them missing, 26.
5.  Wait till tomorrow.
6.  Mouth so Wide, Teeth like Knives

A branch snapped. A thick loud crack jolted through my nerve fibers.
Something huge and fast was moving through the forest towards us. I
grabbed my brothers hand and bolted without looking back.

That night, I dreamed I was back at the campsite. This time, I was
alone… or so I thought. The air was heavy with that familiar staticky
sick. There was no other campsite for miles, instead an endless forest
of sickly decayed trees swaying in the evening breeze.

I heard a voice hiding behind a tree at the edge of the clearing. It was
deep, gooey and squeaky. At first it mumbled strange incoherent strands
of gibberish. I walked toward it, my body urging me to stop and turn
away. But I couldn’t. I need to know what was making that noise.

Just as I had reached the sound, it stopped. I looked around the tree,
confused.

I felt a tap tap tap on my shoulder.

There he was.

Huge wet eyes stared down at me curiously, examining my fear like a
predator eyeing prey. Teeth like piano keys perfectly lined up,
decorated by a fleshy hungry grin that dripped with strands of inky
saliva. It was one of those faces that said so much yet remained
impossible to read. He slowly, as if his pit of mouth was fading in and
out of existence, said the words: Ate Them. Stripped the Flesh off the
Bone and Buried them Deep. 26, all of them Missing.

Then, he opened his mouth wide and lunged.

I woke up.

The morning after we went back to the campsite, and it was completely
destroyed. The fire pit was completely dented and at the edge of the
campsite. Camp gear which had not been there before was littered across
the ground. Everything was askew except the bulletin board.

To this day I have no idea what we actually witnessed or what had
happened to that campsite. What that terrifying nightmare meant. All I
know that we escaped something incomprehensibly terrifying, and are
lucky to have gotten out alive at all.

But that horrible grin, and those inky black soulless eyes….. How could
I forget?
