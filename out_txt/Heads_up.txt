The skies were overcast and the woods were damp as we walked the trail.
I pointed my phone toward Terry, who was taking a sip from his canteen.
We had been walking to our campsite for the last hour and a half. “So,
Terry, tell our viewers a little more about these woods,” I said.

“This trail we are currently hiking has been called the Bermuda Triangle
of hiking,” he replied. “It has the highest density of people going
missing in all of North America, or so they say. That’s why we are
walking it and doing an overnight camp for our followers.”

“You know the entire Bermuda Triangle thing has been disproven, right?”

“I’m not the one who gave it the name, Zach,” Terry shot back. “Even if
the name is not accurate, the stats don’t lie: anywhere from two to four
people go missing walking this same way every year.”

I looked directly into my phone to see myself with a bit of a grin. “And
for how long has this been happening?”

“The last twelve years,” he responded. “The total number of people who
ended up going missing from this trail is around forty.”

“That’s not comforting,” I replied. “But we do this for all of you who
are following us.”

Our buddy Neil had pointed us to this trail. He’d also promised us a
surprise somewhere along the way, but he wouldn’t tell us what.

I could hear the sound of our hiking boots crunching the sticks and dead
leaves below us as the trail led us through a dense patch of woods. I
turned my phone toward it, letting the people watching our livestream
catch a glimpse of where we were heading. “Hey! Hey! Over here!” a voice
called out.

I didn’t see anyone. “Do you hear that?”

I moved my phone around hoping to get a look. Terry grabbed my shoulder
and whispered. “He’s over there.”

I turned to see a man who looked to be in his mid-thirties, wearing a
dark gray henley that had stains. His plain-looking cargo pants weren’t
faring much better. He took off his black baseball cap and waved at us,
revealing matted brown hair and a grimy beard.

“Have you seen Bartleby?” he yelled toward us.

“No man, we don’t even know anyone that goes by that name,” Terry
shouted as the man started to approach us. I turned my phone’s camera,
capturing him giving us an off-putting smile with wild eyes that didn’t
seem to blink.

“I need to find Bartleby, man,” he said.

I kept the livestream rolling, pointing it directly at him. “Like he
said, we don’t know any Bartleby.”

“Are you sure?” he asked.

“Positive,” Terry replied, showing uneasiness as the man stood
uncomfortably close to us, his eyes almost bulging out of his sockets.
“When was the last time you saw him?”

“Oh, it’s been a long, long while since we’ve spoken,” the man replied.
“But I keep coming back up here hoping that one day it will change.”

I flipped the camera back to my face, looking down at the chat flooding
with comments about the man, using words like “creepy” and “weird.”
“We’ll definitely keep an eye out for him up there.”

The stranger searched his pockets and pulled out a crumpled-up pack of
cigarettes. He took out a twisted, half-smoked stub and lit it. “Last
time I saw Bartleby, he was heading in the same direction you seem to be
heading.”

“I gotcha,” Terry replied, taking a step back as the man exhaled plumes
of smoke. The man just took another puff, and I noticed a tremble in his
hand. “If by some chance we do run into him, we’ll let him know you are
looking for him. Who should I say is asking?”

“Tell him Turkey is looking for him.”

“Turkey?” I asked.

The stranger smiled before kneeling down and stubbing out the cigarette
on his dirty boot. He didn’t discard it, though; he pulled out the
crumpled pack and slid it back in. “Yeah, man, I’m called Turkey.”

Terry gave me a look. “Alright, Turkey, it was good to meet you, but we
need to get to our campsite before it gets dark.”

“Oh yeah, yeah,” Turkey responded. “You definitely need to get that fire
going. Things get weird up here sometimes.”

“Yeah, we’ve heard about that,” I said. “But we’ve done this sort of
thing before.”

“You sound just like Bartleby, man,” Turkey replied. “Alright, be safe.”

I maneuvered the phone between me and Terry as we just sort of let out a
laugh once we had gotten a few yards away. The chat was having fun with
our interaction, making jokes and comparing him to other videos they had
seen.

But on my phone’s screen, I could see Turkey still standing behind us.
“Dude, he’s just watching us.”

Terry turned around, glancing back at Turkey, who was sliding his boot
against the dirt. “What in the hell is he doing?”

I kept watching from my phone. He just kept digging his dirty boot into
the soil, pushing it aside as if he was drawing a line. He started to
stretch himself out and hop around. He crouched down before he burst
toward us, running full speed.

“Umm, he’s charging at us?” I said. “What should we do?”

“Run!” Terry shouted as the two of us started to book it. But Turkey
kept running, closing the gap fast. I could see him just feet away on my
phone before he veered left and tore past us, not stopping, just running
full steam ahead.

“Where is he going?” I asked as we stopped running. But he didn’t stop,
and he was barely visible to us now.

“Who cares,” Terry replied. “Let’s just get to the campsite.”

* * *

I could see the faint flames highlighted against the side of our tent as
Terry unfurled his sleeping bag and I talked on the phone with Neil.
“It’s been pretty good so far, Neil.”

“I told you it would be,” he replied. “Did you doubt me?”

“Maybe, just a little,” I replied. “So we are about to settle in for the
night.”

“Tell Neil good job and stuff,” Terry said. “Also, that I said hello or
something.”

“Terry says hello,” I said into the phone. “But we will touch base in
the morning, sounds good?”

“Yeah, give me a holler in the morning,” Neil replied before the phone
clicked, and I started to settle into my sleeping bag. I could see the
fire was starting to dim, but alongside it came a shadow. I wasn’t sure
if my eyes were playing tricks on me, but I turned to Terry, who clearly
saw it, too.

He sat up in his sleeping bag, turning to me. “Record it, dude.”

I fumbled with my phone as I heard an out-of-key hum coming from
outside. The figure was moving around our campsite, leaves rustling
beneath its feet, looking around as it stopped outside our tent.

“Did you see him yet?” It was Turkey’s voice.

I turned to Terry, who shook his head. He didn’t want me to respond.

“Are you home, guys?” Turkey asked, pressing his hand against the fold
of our tent. “I keep looking for him, but I can’t find him. I was hoping
you had better luck than me.”

We remained silent as he lumbered around our tent, still humming along.
He circled it a couple of times, stopping now and then to press a hand
against its sides, before he made his way to the fire. I could see a
silhouette of him brushing aside sticks and leaves before he sat down.

“What now?” I whispered.

“Just keep recording,” Terry muttered back. We could see the outline of
Turkey sitting next to the dying fire. There was an uncomfortable quiet.
I tried not to make a sound but moved closer to the flaps of the tent.

“You know, I don’t know if you are in there or not,” Turkey said. “But I
think you are. Reminds me of two little scared bunnies hiding in some
brush, hoping that something like a coyote wouldn’t find them and eat
them whole.”

Turkey took a heavy breath. I turned to Terry, starting to feel more on
edge. Turkey cleared his throat, and I faintly heard the flick of a
lighter. “Bartleby, he was like that, you know? I will be honest, not
even sure if Bartleby was actually his name. It just felt right to call
him that for some reason, I don’t remember why.”

“What in the fuck, Terry,” I whispered. “We need to get the hell out of
here.”

“How would we do that right now?” Terry replied. “Just run past him?”

“You see, I ran into Bartleby, just like I ran into you two,” Turkey
rasped as I heard him take a drag of his cigarette. “Just a guy walking
on a trail. I’m not a greedy man, little rabbits. I only need one of
you.”

One of us? I felt my legs start to tremble as Turkey sat there smoking
his cigarette. I just wanted to burst through the tent; this was too
creepy even for me, but Terry remained steadfast, seemingly unwilling to
leave.

I could see Turkey’s silhouette standing up and tossing his cigarette
into the fire. He hadn’t saved this one. “I’m not a picky man, I’ll just
flip a coin at this point.”

I lowered my phone and stopped the recording, still feeling uneasy about
all of it as Terry unzipped the flaps of the tent. “Dude, I gotta say
something,” Terry yelled out.

Turkey turned around, looking at the two of us, and gave us a grin. “And
what’s that, little rabbit?”

“All of this stuff about Bartleby and rabbits,” Terry replied, standing
up straight as he exited the tent. “Neil was right about you, nailing
that creepy guy in the woods angle.”

Turkey started to chuckle. “What can I say? I am a natural.”

“No kidding,” I called out as I exited the tent. “I thought you were
actually going to fuck us up, man.”

Turkey threw his head back, letting out a hearty laugh. “Man, I knew
that stuff about the rabbits would get you guys shaking in your hiking
boots.”

“Alright, so a hundred-fifty work for you?” Terry said, reaching into
his wallet, pulling out the cash, and handing it over to Turkey. His
face lit up, and I noticed him gripping something else in his hand, a
small gleam from the fire. It looked like a coin.

“Man, you were really going to sell it with the coin and everything,” I
said, looking at Turkey, who lifted the coin, showing us a dirt-stained
quarter. I looked over to Terry. “Probably should have recorded it for
the extra creep factor.”

“Yeah, we should have,” Terry said. “But I didn’t expect it. Alright, I
can call you Kevin now, right?”

Turkey, aka Kevin, pulled out another stubbed-out cigarette and lit it.
“For the hundred and fifty, call me whatever. I even decided who was
going to be heads or tails.”

He flipped the coin and caught it, taking a brief glimpse before
smiling.

“So which one was I?” Terry asked.

“Does it actually matter?” Turkey asked.

“Not really,” Terry replied. “Alright, I am actually pretty tired.”

“Me too,” I said as I walked back to the tent. “Thanks for your help,
man. I think this one will get some views.”

“Yeah, seriously, you gave us the extra creep factor that this video
needed,” Terry said, following me into the tent. I gave one last look to
Neil’s weed dealer, who had agreed to do the entire schtick for us. He
gave us a wave.

“Well, it’s been fun,” he said. “I got a little ways to go to my
campsite, too.”

* * *

I could feel the slight chill hit my fingertips as I woke up. I grabbed
my phone to check the time and turned over to see Terry wasn’t in his
sleeping bag. He probably had to use the bathroom. I actually did, too.
But before that, I opened my phone’s contacts and scrolled to Neil,
giving him a call.

“Hey man, I’m so sorry about last night,” Neil answered.

“Sorry for what?” I said. “That plug of yours did a crazy good job last
night.”

I started to unzip the flaps of the tent when Neil fell silent for a
moment before he replied. “I am sort of confused, did you find someone
else to do it?”

“No, your guy did it,” I replied as I started to step out of the tent,
feeling a cold puddle at my feet. I didn’t even know it had rained. I
looked up through the trees to check for any overcast skies. Part of the
reason we had picked last night was because the rest of the week was
supposed to be showers and storms.

“I don’t know how he could have done it,” Neil replied. “I am waiting on
him to bring an eighth to me, and when I sent him a text he said sorry
for skipping out on you guys.”

I took another step and looked down to see the small little puddle was a
strange color as I began to reach down. “So then who did you send out
here to help us with the video?”

“I didn’t send anyone,” Neil said as I pressed my fingers into the
puddle, seeing the light red liquid on my fingertips. I finally looked
ahead toward the campfire and saw Terry lying next to the smoldering
embers, his shirt soaked dark. A dirt-stained quarter sat on his chest,
heads up.
