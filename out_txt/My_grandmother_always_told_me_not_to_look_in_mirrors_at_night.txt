My grandmother had a lot of strange superstitions when I was growing up.
She believed you shouldn’t whistle inside the house, leave empty bottles
on the table, or look into mirrors after midnight. That last one was
something she took particularly seriously. Whenever I stayed at her
house, she’d cover the mirror in the guest bedroom before going to
sleep. I used to laugh about it and ask what she thought was going to
happen. She never really explained. She’d just tell me that some things
were better left alone.

A few years ago, I was home alone watching a TV series. I’d gotten so
caught up in it that I hadn’t noticed how late it was. It must have been
around 2 or 3 in the morning when something suddenly got into my eye.
Probably an eyelash or a tiny piece of dust. I rubbed it a few times,
but it only made things worse. My eye started watering, and I couldn’t
concentrate on the screen anymore. I had a small handheld mirror on the
table, so I picked it up to see if I could get whatever was irritating
my eye out.

I was sitting on my bed with the mirror held close to my face, trying to
find the eyelash. The only light in the room was coming from the
television and a small lamp beside my bed. I finally managed to spot it
and carefully pulled it out. I was about to put the mirror down when I
noticed something in the reflection. At first, I wasn’t even looking
directly at it. It was just something dark in the corner of the room,
behind me. I thought it was a shadow from the furniture, until I noticed
two tiny red lights in the middle of it.

I froze. I don’t know how else to describe what I saw. It looked like a
tall, completely black shape standing in the corner, with two red points
where its eyes should have been. I immediately turned my head to look
behind me. There was nothing there. Just the wall, a chair, and some
clothes I’d left hanging over it. I actually felt relieved for a second
because I thought my eyes were playing tricks on me. But when I looked
back toward the mirror, I could still see it in my peripheral vision.
The same dark shape. The same two red lights.

I was too frightened to look directly at the reflection again. I just
placed the mirror facedown on the bedside table, pulled the blanket over
my head, and stayed completely still. I know that sounds ridiculous,
especially coming from an adult, but I genuinely couldn’t make myself
move. I kept thinking about my grandmother and all those times she’d
warned me about mirrors at night. I don’t remember how long I stayed
under the blanket or when I eventually fell asleep.

The next morning, everything seemed normal. Sunlight was coming through
the curtains, and the room looked exactly as it always did. I almost
convinced myself that I’d imagined the whole thing because I was tired.
Then I remembered the mirror. It was still lying facedown where I’d left
it. I picked it up, intending to put it back in the drawer.

There was a crack running across the glass.

It hadn’t been cracked the night before. I’d been holding it inches from
my face, so I’m certain I would have noticed. I hadn’t dropped it or hit
it against anything. I’d simply placed it on the table.

I threw that mirror away the same morning. I still don’t know what I
saw, and maybe there is a perfectly reasonable explanation for all of
it. But I haven’t looked into a mirror in a dark room since.

And I finally understand why my grandmother always covered hers.
