Urban exploring became one of my favorite pastimes sometime last year. I
was between jobs and waiting to start my new one as a photographer, so I
had a lot of time to kill. Sitting in my apartment made me feel really
antsy, which is why eventually I started hiking.

Hiking turned into exploring random abandoned fortresses in my town’s
woods, which turned into actively seeking out abandoned structures, even
if that included driving a few states over.

Eventually, I was really getting into the “urbex” scene as it’s called.
For a hobby, it definitely had its anxieties - not to mention, rules to
follow.

Some basic rules are:

Don’t touch any standing water. It’s full of parasites, amoebas, etc.

Bring a knife, a flashlight, long pants and shirts, etc.

Cover your face.

Don’t go alone.

The cardinal rule. The one I foolishly broke.

About a month ago, I had an experience that put me off from urban
exploring for a bit.

I’d heard of this massive abandoned mall structure that was about a 50
mile drive away, I believe it was in Vermont. It supposedly had most
everything still in tact. I felt excitement grow in me. This place was
going to be huge; it was perfect.

I left early in the morning on a Tuesday. Packed in my car was my Canon,
water bottles, protein bars, a flashlight, extra batteries, a mask and
an extra pair of boots.

A small bout of anxiety was stirring in my stomach that I tried to
remedy with some hot sugary tea in my glass to-go mug. When that didn’t
help, I rolled the window down and had a cigarette, even though I quit 2
months ago. The anxiety was definitely coming from the fact that this
was going to be my first urban exploration by myself. I forced it from
my mind, telling myself it would be fine.

About an hour later, I arrived at the location. It was overgrown,
boarded up and absolutely huge. This was truly the urbex jackpot - it
was beautiful.

With my camera around my neck, I parked a good distance away (about a
half mile) and trekked to the entrance. My watch was reading 5:32 am and
the sun was starting to come up, which gave me a good amount of light to
use. I realized quickly that there was no access through the main
entrance. I went around the building, trying other doors, and none of
them were accessible. Frustrated, I circled the entire perimeter one
more time.

Then finally - at the back, there was an employees only door that was
unlocked and not boarded up. I used it to slip inside, clicking my
flashlight on and shining it to see where I’d ended up.

It was a very long hallway. Both the left and right paths stretched
infinitely, so I went left and carefully trudged to the end, which
opened into a food court. Neon signs were in shambles, tables were
scattered, all of them broken or smashed. There was a giant hole in the
ceiling where ivy was growing into and down onto the walls. I snapped a
picture of that, awestruck of the beauty of it all.

The sun was peeking through the hole in the ceiling, giving me enough
light to make my way to the actual mall, or what it once was.

I saw boarded up stores and some left completely open but also
completely empty. The sun shone through the grimy ceiling windows as I
marveled in how cool this was, snapping pictures here and there.

At some point, water started to splash under my feet, and I realized a
massive chunk of the building was basically gone, letting in the
elements and forming a large green puddle that blocked the rest of the
way.

I turned around and headed back, carefully taking the broken escalator
to the second floor. This floor had more ivy, more water in some places,
and was a bit warmer than the main floor. I snapped a picture of a
broken down mall directory sign, water damaged and covered in plants.

As I looked through some of the photos I’d taken, one thing caught my
eye.

It was in the photo of the directory sign. Behind it, a shape was
floating about 50 feet away - formless and faceless, just a blob.

Shit. My heart skipped a little as I stared at the photo. Maybe I had
actually captured an orb on camera. The excitement was back, and I
quickly started taking more.

After a bit, I took a break and sat on the ledge of an abandoned
fountain. That was when I noticed one photo I had taken was extremely
unsettling.

It was the orb again. No, wait - it had a form this time. It stood
closer than the other thing had, and now appeared as a figure, a
silhouette of something tall. Something big.

My heart was immediately in my stomach. Suddenly, this place no longer
felt cool and beautiful in its destruction and neglect. I felt watched.

I went back to where I’d taken that photo, but I didn’t see any trace of
anything or anyone.

It had to be someone squatting. Well, that could be remedied. Keeping a
grip on the knife in my pocket, I said loudly, “I’m sorry if I’m
intruding. Do you live here?”

No answer.

“I’ll be leaving right now, so don’t worry. Sorry if I disturbed you.”

Nothing. Silence.

“Fucking weird.” I muttered. As I walked on, nothing else was out of the
ordinary. The only sounds I heard were the birds and the distant
dripping of the water downstairs.

And then I heard the footsteps.

They were slow, they didn’t match mine. When I looked behind me, there
was nothing. I increased my pace to see if they’d also speed up - they
didn’t.

Curiously, I turned and snapped a photo, closing my eyes before finally
viewing it.

The figure was there again, closer, directly in front of me. Bigger.
Inhumanely bigger - at least 7 feet tall. The energy coming off the
photo was menacing, something I’d never experienced before.

As I scrolled through the other photos, I saw the figure again. And
again. Sometimes it appeared as the orb I’d first seen and sometimes it
was the full figure. It was in the first photo I’d taken of the ivy in
the ceiling. It was in the one of the grimy ceiling windows.

It was in every photo.

Sometimes far away, sometimes close.

It had been following me the entire time.

I quickly left after that. I’m still looking at the photos to this day
and I have no idea what that was. I haven’t been urban exploring since.
