I guess a little background information might help. I’m in my early 30s,
single. I live with my dog Karma in an apartment in the suburbs. My mom
died a couple of years ago of cancer, and my dad moved to a cabin out in
the woods nearby, I won’t say where so nobody goes there and possibly
puts themselves in danger, to be alone and to retire. I think it was his
way of grieving because he wasn’t really good about showing emotions
around other people.

 

I have three sisters and two brothers, but they all live in other
states. So, when my dad got sick, it was up to me to take care of him.
He had a lot of heart issues, but it was dementia that was really
causing his life to go downhill fast, and so he asked if I would come
care for him, and it’s a good thing I did because shortly after I packed
up the dog and my things and turned in my apartment keys, he passed
away. I got there just in time to hold his hands as he died. Hospice was
there. They were lifesavers for me in the weeks just after his death.

 

I wasn’t much for living out in a cabin in the woods. Too creepy for me,
although his cabin wasn’t entirely isolated. I mean, you could see his
neighbors’ lights through the trees, and there were a lot of hikers
walking past where the back property fence was heading down to the
ridgeline for the views. I have to admit there was a beautiful view on
that ridgeline of the lake area.

 

My dad had a big husky dog named Cody and sometimes when I would visit,
I would bring Karma and the two dogs got along swimmingly. Cody was
super depressed, not having my dad around, but I think having Karma
there really helped.

 

I love the woods. Don’t get me wrong, I grew up in a little town where
we had woods and a huge lake behind our house and as kids, we would go
through a big hole in our backyard fence and play in the woods. But I
knew I could go home at any time and be in my little suburban
neighborhood where there was a lot of noise from kids playing outside
and dogs barking and it wasn’t anywhere near as isolated as this cabin.
Still, my dad had left the cabin and a quite a bit of money to me in his
will, and it was kind of nice to not have to worry about paying rent.

 

I spent most of the first few months I was living there going through my
dad’s things and just allowing myself to grieve. Most of his clothes and
books I brought down to the local Goodwill in town. I bought a lot of
books to the library and donated them, and they were so thrilled. Dad
was a scientist, so he had a lot of really cool science type books, but
he was also really into UFOs and the paranormal. I kept a few of my
favorite books of his, and I also kept a couple of pieces of clothing
and some personal items.

 

My dad wasn’t a hoarder, so it wasn’t that difficult to go through the
stuff, and it gave me something to do while I was waiting for his ashes.
In his will, he asked that I spread his ashes in the woods behind the
cabin. That would be easy enough. Except I think it’s illegal to do
that, but I figured if I went in the evening and made sure there weren’t
any hikers around or sheriff’s deputies, I could easily spread the ashes
quickly and get back inside without anybody knowing.

 

I didn’t go out into the woods behind the yard boundary fence until I
actually got the ashes from the mortuary. I did admire the woods from a
picnic bench that my dad had in the backyard while the dogs were running
around playing. I thought I would probably start hiking sometime soon
because I really loved to go on short solo hikes. When I got the ashes,
I got the dogs on their leashes and we cut around where there’s a hole
in the fence that we could walk through just a little bit off of our
property line. I carried the urn while I tried to hold onto the two
dogs, which was a little tricky, but when I found a good spot, it’s
almost like they knew what I was there to do because they calmed down
long enough for me to open the urn and spread the ashes around this
beautiful grove of trees that had a peekaboo view of the lake.

 

I started to cry as I said a prayer and said goodbye to my dad and told
him I would take good care of the cabin and of Cody, and that I missed
him already. My dad and I were close, but he wasn’t one of those gushy,
super affectionate dads. But I knew he loved me because he was always
sharing information with me. I think that was his love language, sending
me articles and stories about everything from Bigfoot sightings to UFOs
to new discoveries in quantum physics or earthquake activity in the
area. 

 

So, when I did spread the ashes around, it was evening and it was
getting dark and with the trees overhead, it really makes it appear a
lot darker quicker. The dogs were very calm. Their ears were perking up
every time they heard birds. Everything sounded wonderful. The rustling
of leaves. We could even hear some hikers on the ridgeline, laughing and
talking. I hung out for a while with the dogs, letting them sniff leaves
and whatnot, and then I thought it was time to head back to make a late
dinner.

 

That’s when I heard whatever it was that I heard, a loud rustling in the
trees above my head. I froze, not sure what I was hearing, but figured
it was some kind of creature like a racoon or a possum or squirrels. I
think the first thought that went through my head was that I didn’t want
whatever it was to shit on my head. Never did I think it was anything
like a bear or a mountain lion because it was way up in the trees. But I
will say that the dogs kind of reacted and by that I mean they froze and
their tails went still and then they started sniffing around and making
a sort of low growling sound that I’d never heard either one make
before.

 

I looked up into the trees to see if I could see a critter moving
around, and that’s when I saw the two red lights looking down at me. I
assumed they were eyes, the way that they were spaced, and I stared at
them for a while and they did blink. I knew that I had read somewhere
that moonlight or any kind of light could cause the eyes of animals in
the woods to glow, and it was just reflection, so it didn’t concern me
too much until I saw another pair on another tree branch nearby, and
then another pair. That’s when I started to get kind of creeped out, so
I got the dogs out of their little stupor and pulled on the leashes to
head back towards the fence line.

We went back through the hole, and I have to admit I hustled across the
backyard a little quicker than usual, got the dogs inside, and locked
the door. I forgot about it 10 minutes later when I was making dinner
for me and getting the dogs fed, and then there was a knock on the door.

 

It freaked me out because I was alone with the two dogs, who were now
barking their heads off and scratching at the front door, but I figured
whoever was there would realize that if I opened the door and they tried
anything funny, I had two huge dogs that would put them out of their
misery. My dad also had a shotgun and shells, and I had kept it loaded
just in case, but it was in his den. I thought about getting it before I
went to the door, but instead I yelled, “Who’s there?”

 

A man’s voice yelled. “Deputy Mark Rogers. Just checking to see how
you’re doing. I knew your dad really well.”

 

OK, that was cool. I opened the door and he was so good looking I almost
gasped. My age, sweet smile, God I love a man in a unform. I wanted to
glance down at his hands to see if he had a wedding ring on, but instead
I motioned for him to come inside. The dogs by then had realized that he
was friendly since I wasn’t screaming. Cody jumped on him and licked his
face, and I realized that he really did know my dad, and if Cody was OK
with him, then I was OK. 

 

He took off his hat. “Hey, I just wanted to offer my condolences to you.
I was friends with your dad and checked on him often, and I wasn’t able
to get up here much when he was really sick. I feel bad about that, but
I wanna let you know that if you need anything, I’m here.”

 

I thought that was really sweet of him. “That’s really cool. My name is
Lark. I’m his daughter.”

 

“Oh, OK, so you’re gonna be living here? Are you gonna put the cabin up
for sale?” Mark was so cute I had to force myself not to stare at him.

 

“I thought about it, but I think I’m gonna stay here for a while. My dad
loved this place and it’s nice and peaceful, and since I’m a writer, it
might really be a great place for me to get a lot of work done without
too many distractions.”

 

I asked him if he wanted to sit down and have a beer, so I got two out
of the fridge and cracked them open, and we spent the evening talking
and getting to know each other while the dogs lay and snoozed at our
feet.

 

“I went out in the woods a little earlier,” I said, wondering if I
should say anything about what happened. I figured, what do I have to
lose. “I got a little spooked when I saw some red eyes up in the trees
looking down at me. The dogs seemed a little anxious too. I’m hoping it
wasn’t a good climbing bear or mountain lion.”

 

Mark laughed, but it wasn’t a condescending laugh.  “We don’t have very
many bear or mountain lion sightings around here. Most of them are on
the other side of the lake where there’s less people, but we definitely
have a lot of smaller tree-climbers like raccoons and squirrels, and
they can make a lot of noise. And as for the red eyes, that’s definitely
light reflecting off of them. It’s pretty spooky the first time you see
it.”

 

We chatted some more, and then he realized that he needed to get back
down to the Sheriff’s office down the road in town. I felt bad because
he was on the job and here I was taking up all his time and making him
have a beer. But he laughed and said nobody would know, and it wasn’t a
big drive down the hill, so he would be very careful. Told him I
wouldn’t tell that he was drinking and driving. Man, he was so easy to
talk to.

 

I walked him to the door, and I swear there was this attraction between
us, and I was so thrilled when he asked if I wanted to have lunch with
him on the weekend. It was Thursday, and I knew I didn’t have anything
much going on, so of course I said yes.

When he left, I actually leaned against the door, grinning like the
dickens, just like you see in one of those dumb romance movies. 

 

I went and cleaned off the table. I made sure the dishes were done, and
that’s when it kind of hit me because I was still thinking about those
eyes looking down at me, the red glowing eyes. I realized that there had
been no moon out, and there still wasn’t, so what light was reflecting
off their eyes? I blew if off because I was tired, so I got prepared for
bed and watched a movie on my dad’s Roku. I fell asleep in the middle of
it, never got to see the ending, which doesn’t say much about that
movie, does it?

 

I woke up about 2 o’clock in the morning to the sound of the dogs
howling and moaning. They’re out by the front door, and of course I got
scared and rushed out to see what’s going on. They’re pawing at the
front door. OK, I figured they had to take a piss or a shit, so I opened
the door and they went running out. They ran straight to the back of the
fence and were jumping up and down, staring up at the trees, howling. I
thought about going to the den to grab the shotgun and then wondered why
I hadn’t left it by the door, which would’ve been the smart thing to do.

 

I watched the dogs for about five minutes before they finally backed
down and came running back into the cabin. Neither one pooped or peed,
but they both looked kind of meek with their tails between their legs
before I closed the door. I looked back over to the trees and I could
see up in the treetops several sets of those eyes, red and glowing, but
there was no light, no moon, no nothing. I closed the door and locked
it, and then I called Mark.

 

I told him what had happened, and I asked if he thought that these were
again possums or raccoons. He reassured me they were and teased me about
being a suburban girl and asked if I wanted him to come over and stay on
the couch after his shift. I said, “No, I’m fine. I am just I’m not used
to this. Thanks for reassuring me, though.” We hung up and I went back
to bed. The dogs never made another sound that night. 

 

The next day I did a lot more cleaning and I pretty much had all of my
dad’s stuff organized that I hadn’t already taken down to charity. I was
going to box it up and keep it in the closets so that I could organize
the one bedroom and the den and the living room, so there was more
space. Not that I had a lot of furnishings and stuff. I didn’t.

 

My apartment had been furnished, so I didn’t have much but my clothes
and writing files.

I wasn’t into clutter, but one interesting thing that I did find was in
my dad’s den. He had an office file cabinet and I looked through to see
if there were any important documents or anything, pictures of my mom or
whatnot, and I was absolutely shocked to see that my dad had been saving
newspaper clippings and stories printed out from the Internet on UFO
sightings and strange cryptic sightings in America’s national parks and
forests, including Bigfoot and large cats and thunderbirds. I didn’t
know why he was saving all that and I made absolutely no connection
between that and the eyes that I was seeing up the trees . But it’s
something that I tucked in the back of my mind for later, realizing that
it was important but not necessarily knowing yet why.

 

That night, after a nice dinner, I watched a movie out in the living
room this time. The dogs were hanging out with me, chewing on some bones
that I had gotten them down at the general store. Yep, they had a
general store there right next to the Sheriff’s department. I had popped
down earlier to get some things and accidentally (ha ha) ran into Mark,
and we had a nice little visit before I headed back up the hill to the
cabin. It was about 9:00 at night when I heard all kinds of a ruckus
behind the cabin, howls, growls, thrashing around, and a scream. I know
it was a scream, a human scream, but I do know that there are animals
that can sound like humans, foxes for instance, rabbits when attacked.
They can sound human.

 

I completely froze. Then I turned down the volume of the TV and
listened. I did not move. The dogs were listening, too. They weren’t
howling or at the door scratching. They were just sitting stock still,
with their heads high and their ears completely perked up. I didn’t hear
anything for the next five minutes, and I finally got up the courage to
go to the window that looks out on the back yard. I turned out the back
light so that it was completely dark, and I looked beyond the fence into
the dark woods and up to the treetops.

 

There were so many eyes, so many pairs of red glowing eyes. I felt my
heart go into my throat, and I thought I might piss my pants. Had the
dogs not been there, I’m sure I would’ve pissed my pants. I grabbed my
phone and I called Mark. I didn’t wanna do it and I apologize profusely,
but I think he could hear the terror in my voice because he was up at
the cabin in five minutes flat. I let him in the front door quietly and
motioned for him to come to the back window. We looked out together and
he could see all of the eyes looking down.

 

“You can’t tell me those are all raccoons,” I said. “And where is the
light coming that’s reflecting off their eyes and making them look that
way? It’s a new moon.” My voice was trembling. Mark didn’t really
answer, but he told me to stay inside. He took out his sidearm and went
out the back door into the yard.

 

There was no way in hell I was gonna let him go out there alone, so I
said, “Mark, I’m right behind you. Don’t shoot me,” and we stood there
for a few minutes just listening to the weirdest growling and howling, I
can’t even describe the sounds that were coming from up in the trees.

 

“Mark, there are things in the trees! What do we do?”

 

He moved closer to the back fence, and I was right behind him. I was
tempted to grab his hand, but I didn’t. We stood there for a minute,
just looking up at the eyes and listening, so far nothing threatening

Mark and I both gasped and stepped back. These figures, whatever they
were, they were hunched over, and then they stood up and they were so
tall, maybe eight or nine feet tall, and they were thin. I mean, this
wasn’t Bigfoot. These things were thin and gangly, and those red eyes,
the glow was even deeper, darker, like blood red.

 

“Holy shit, we need to get inside,” Mark said. “Go, go, go!”

 

We ran back into the cabin, and as we did, we heard movement. Whatever
those things were, they were on the move, but luckily it was away from
us. Once we got inside the cabin, we peeked out the back window and we
could see there was nothing there.

“They’re gone now,” Mark said. His voice was low, trembling.

 

Mark stayed overnight. Neither one of us could sleep, and no, we didn’t

think we did fall asleep around four in the morning because the next

Then he woke up and he took a shower. We sat at the little kitchen table

I agreed it was a good idea, and I said I would pay for it. I cleaned up

I was terrified. I didn’t wanna see anything on those memory cards. I

Mark had to work a shift that evening, so I would be on my own with the

Stupid, I know.

 

Knowing that the trail cams were out there actually did make me feel a

I saw quite a few hikers behind the fence walking back-and-forth, and

I slept through most of the night, which was good because I was so

On the footage, you could see images of something draped over a branch

But that was it. It didn’t come down from the trees. It didn’t drop down

Until Mark’s phone rang, and it was the sheriff telling him he needed to

I guess they are everywhere now.
