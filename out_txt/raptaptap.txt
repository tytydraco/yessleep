Context:

This story is technically in first person, it won’t make sense at first
but it will do later. It was first written for my creative writing piece
in an exam back in May, and this is a semi-accurate rendition of it with
a possibility for serialisation. Enjoy!

rap-tap-tap. don’t let them in.

His body had some sort of cloud around it, somewhat similar to when a
camera doesn’t focus but honed in to his outline. Occasionally I saw
spikes jutting from his shoulders and elbows, but I pass it off. New
Years was almost a week previously, so I chalked it up to a hangover,
albeit with doubt, but it didn’t make it any less strange. There was
something he wanted to deliver to me, as I recall, and his persistent
rap-tap-tapping on the door further pushed that narrative, but I was far
too focused on him to really know what was happening. I was wrong to
brush it off and go to bed, and I knew it at the time, but it was almost
midnight and I was too tired to believe it was real. It ceased
eventually.

Another one came in the morning, about 9am. I had just convinced myself
it was a night terror, but the rap-tap-tapping grounded me back in
reality headfirst. I phoned in sick to work. Couldn’t muster up the
courage to bypass her. Arguably my most rational thought I had so far.
So I spent the morning reorganising my library and peering through
manuscript of bygone days, with my gramophone playing loudly in a vain
effort to drown out the knocking she kept on doing. I had thought it
worked eventually, when the knocking stopped as I stood up to make
lunch, but as I peered out the window I saw she had walked down the path
and left. This was my window of opportunity, my saving grace, my knight
in shining armour, if you will. Jacket flung haphazardly over my
pajamas, I had skidded into the driveway, and let my feet pound the
tarmac as I started to run down the avenue to the train station. There
would be more of them at my door and I wasn’t prepared to face them
again.

And that’s my encounter up to date. That morning that I abandoned ship
was this morning, and I boarded a train to Malmö shortly after leaving
my house. It should only take a couple of hours, and I’m lucky enough to
have some euros saved so I can rent a place in the city while I wait for
the danger to pass. I’m still yet to have lunch, as well as dinner,
which was promised to us 15 minutes ago at 7pm. I have attempted to
speak to the driver, but I’m assuming he’s in the lavatory as we’re
currently on series of a long, single track bridges over some gorges and
valleys. More than understandable I’m sure, but if he’s in there for 15
minutes I think a doctor may need to be called.

It has been 5 minutes since last I wrote, the driver is now at my door.
His face is partially blurred by the clouded glass that we’ve been
graciously given to avoid peeping toms, but from what I can see he’s
smiling. He’s knocked a couple of times saying he had a delivery. I
assumed he meant dinner, so I went to open the door just as he knocked
again.

rap-tap-tap.

**classification**

This piece of writing was found written in a small notebook in the
pocket of a man we have identified as Hans Hårsson, a Swedish professor
turned recluse, in his left inner breast pocket in his trench coat. It
is dated to 1934. His body was found underneath 47 feet of snow in the
rubble a collapsed stone bridge we have no clear records of. Research
will continue where necessary.

-   Solaris
