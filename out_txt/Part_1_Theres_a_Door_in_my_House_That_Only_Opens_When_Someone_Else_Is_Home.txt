Buying that house was the first time I’d felt like I’d done something
for myself. It was mine, and I could finally start building a life of my
own.

It was small, a little dated, and backed onto some pretty serious
woodland. The first few months had been harder than I’d expected. I’d
spent so much time helping Mom move closer to my sisters, a few hours
away, that getting my own things unpacked had become a bit of a mission.
There were still boxes I hadn’t opened and rooms I hadn’t properly
sorted out.

I found the door while my sister was visiting.

“How’s Mom doing?” I asked, leaning against the kitchen counter as Emma
wandered around the house, taking in the bare walls and the almost
complete lack of decoration.

“You’d know if you called her more, Danny.” She sighed, rubbing her eyes
with her palms before looking back at me. “She’ll be fine.”

Mom had started showing the early signs of dementia. Dad’s death a few
months earlier seemed to have accelerated things, or maybe we’d just
started noticing what had been happening all along. Either way, it had
become harder to pretend everything was normal.

Emma continued down the hallway.

“Still smells like paint in here. How long have you lived here now?”

I pushed myself off the counter and followed her. “Three months, I
think?”

She was standing in the spare bedroom, sitting on the edge of the bare
bed frame. I still hadn’t got around to buying a mattress.

“Have you spoken to Charlotte recently?” she asked.

I felt a familiar knot form in my stomach. Charlotte and I had broken up
shortly before I’d bought the house, and apparently my sister had
decided that buying property meant I was now ready to discuss my
emotional shortcomings.

“Uh, yeah. We talk heaps,” I said.

Emma looked at me.

“You still haven’t spoken to her.”

I didn’t bother answering. She rolled her eyes and turned towards the
wall.

“What’s that?”

I followed her gaze.

There was a door in the wall, tucked beside the wardrobe. It was narrow,
with a small brass handle, and no obvious frame around it. The paint
matched the wall so closely that I couldn’t understand how I’d missed
it.

I stared at it for a moment.

“Is that a cupboard?” Emma asked.

“Maybe,” I said. “I’ve honestly got no idea.”

Emma walked over to the door and tried the handle. It opened with a soft
click.

Inside was a small, empty space, about the size of a closet. Nothing
unusual about it. No shelves, no coat hooks, just four painted walls and
enough room to stand in.

“It’s just a closet, Em,” I said.

“Yeah, I can see that. I just…” She paused, then closed the door. “I
don’t know. It stood out to me for some reason.”

Her phone buzzed. She pulled it from her pocket, glanced at the screen,
then looked up at me.

“Sorry, I have to take this.”

She answered as she walked out of the room. A moment later, I heard the
front door open and shut, followed by the sound of her voice drifting in
from the front garden.

I stood looking at the door.

I couldn’t understand how I’d missed it. It wasn’t anything special,
just a little closet tucked into the wall. But I knew what Emma meant.
There was something strange about noticing a feature in your own house
that you were certain you’d never seen before.

I reached for the handle and tried it.

Nothing.

I tried again, pressing it down firmly. The handle moved, but the door
wouldn’t budge. I pulled, then gave it a gentle shove with my shoulder.

It was as if it had been locked.

I wondered if we’d somehow managed to lock it when Emma had closed it. I
gave the handle one last try before deciding I’d look at it properly
later.

By the time I left the bedroom, Emma was coming back inside.

“Sorry, I have to run. I’ve got to pick the kids up from daycare early,
and it’s a bit of a drive.”

“No, you’re fine. I’ll see you next time you’re down this neck of the
woods.”

We hugged in the hallway. When she pulled away, she gave me the sort of
look that meant she wasn’t finished with me yet.

“You need to call Mom more, Danny. Check in on her before she doesn’t
remember you.”

I looked down at the floor.

“Yeah. I know.”

“And you should at least try reaching out to Charlotte. Five years
together, surely it couldn’t have ended that badly.”

“It’s fine, Em.”

She raised an eyebrow, clearly unconvinced.

I glanced towards the window. “You should get going. I heard there’s a
weather warning for heavy snow tonight.”

Emma gave me a small, knowing smile, but thankfully didn’t push it. She
grabbed her bag and headed out to the car.

I watched her drive away, then went back inside and shut the front door.

The next few weeks passed uneventfully. I checked the door a few times,
but I was never able to open it again. Since it was only a small storage
space that I’d probably never use, I eventually forgot about it.

At least, I did until I found it open.

I’d just got home from work and was walking down the hallway when I
glanced into the spare bedroom. The door wasn’t sitting flush against
the wall anymore. It was only open a few centimetres, but it was enough
to catch my attention.

Curious, I stepped inside.

A small piece of brown cardboard had been wedged underneath it, holding
it open. I crouched down and pulled it free. The door swung shut with a
soft click.

I tried the handle.

It opened without any resistance.

I stood there for a moment, staring into the empty space.

Had I somehow unlocked it?

A shiver ran down my back. I looked at the piece of cardboard in my
hand. It was nothing special, just a torn strip of packaging, the sort
of thing I’d probably have thrown away while unpacking.

But someone had put it there.

Or I’d put it there and forgotten.

I was certain I’d never managed to open the door since Emma’s visit. I’d
tried several times, and it had never budged. Now it opened as easily as
any other cupboard in the house.

My first thought was that someone had been inside while I was out. I
checked the front and back doors, then the windows. Everything looked as
I’d left it. And even if someone had got in, why would they bother
wedging open a tiny closet?

I tried to remember the last time I’d checked the door. It had been a
while. For all I knew, the cardboard had been there for days.

I carried it into the kitchen and dropped it in the bin.

There was probably a simple explanation. A faulty latch, maybe.
Something I’d overlooked when I’d first tried it.

I made myself dinner and got on with the rest of the evening.

That night, I was sitting on the couch watching a movie when I thought I
heard something moving in the hallway.

I paused the film and listened. Nothing.

I got up, turned on the lights, and walked through the house, checking
each room as I went. Everything looked normal. By the time I reached the
spare bedroom, I was starting to feel a bit stupid.

Still, something made me check inside.

The room was exactly as I’d left it. The closet door was shut.

I walked over and tried the handle.

Locked again.

I swore under my breath and gave it another pull.

“How the hell does this thing work?”

I stood there for a moment, turning the handle back and forth. Maybe I’d
somehow wedged the cardboard underneath it myself and forgotten. I’d
been busy with work, and the last few weeks had blurred together.

I decided that the next time I managed to open it, I’d prop it open with
a chair or something. At least then I’d know it couldn’t shut itself.

I went back to the couch and finished the film.

The next few days passed without incident. By the weekend, I’d mostly
stopped thinking about the door.

Then I noticed it was open again.

I was walking past the spare bedroom when I spotted the narrow gap
between the door and the wall. This time, something was sticking out
near the latch.

It was a magnetic business card, the sort you get in the mail from local
tradesmen and end up leaving on the fridge for six months before
throwing away. I recognised it immediately. It was from a local plumber,
and I’d received a few copies since moving in.

Someone had wedged it into the latch to stop the door from closing.

I pulled the card free and opened the door. The space behind it was
empty, just as it had been before.

I looked down at the card, then back at the closet.

I couldn’t remember putting it there. I couldn’t even remember the last
time I’d handled one of those cards.

Rather than stand around wondering about it, I decided to make the space
useful. I went through the spare room and found a couple of unopened
boxes I’d been meaning to sort out since moving in. They were a tight
fit, but I managed to stack them inside the closet.

Then I fetched a plastic doorstop from the kitchen drawer and wedged it
firmly underneath the door.

That should do it.

I gave the door a little push to make sure the stopper held, then left
the room and got on with my weekend.

The next day, I was on the phone with Emma when I saw something that
made my stomach drop.

“Hey, listen, Em, I’ve got to call you back.”

I hung up before she could answer and walked straight into the spare
bedroom.

Both boxes were sitting outside the closet. The door was shut, and the
doorstop was gone.

I tried the handle.

Locked.

I swore and kicked the bottom of the door. The noise echoed through the
room, and I immediately felt ridiculous for doing it. It was a closet. A
small, insignificant closet that was somehow making me question my own
memory.

I knew I hadn’t taken the boxes out. Both were still taped shut. If I’d
moved them, it would have been to get something out of them, and there
was no reason I’d have put them back outside without opening them.

I stood there for a while, staring at the door, then called Emma back.

“Can you come down this weekend?”

“Is everything okay?”

“Yeah. I just need you to have a look at something.”

She agreed, though she sounded unconvinced.

That weekend, I took her straight into the spare bedroom. The boxes were
still sitting where I’d found them, and the closet door was still shut.

Emma looked from the boxes to me.

“Danny, what is going on? Why couldn’t you just tell me over the phone?”

I walked over to the handle.

“This. This is what’s going on.”

I pulled it, expecting the door to stay locked.

Instead, it opened without the slightest resistance.

I stared into the empty space.

“No. No, it was locked this morning. I tried it. I swear to God, I tried
it.”

Emma looked at me with a kind of careful concern that made me feel worse
than if she’d laughed.

“Are you okay, Danny?”

“Yes! I’m fucking fine.” I turned towards her, then back to the door.
“It’s this thing. It keeps opening and closing, and I don’t know how.
It’s like someone’s messing with me, or I’m doing it myself and
forgetting, or…”

I trailed off. Even to me, it sounded absurd.

Emma folded her arms.

“Dan, if you need a break, you could stay with Mom for a few days again.
She’d probably appreciate the company.”

“I’m not fucking crazy, wait, again? What do you mean again?”

“I didn’t say you were Crazy, just that maybe you need to spend sometime
out of this house.”

I looked back into the closet, trying to work out what I could possibly
show her that would make any of this make sense.

“I need to set up a camera or something.”

“Dan…”

“Emma, please. Just don’t.”

I stepped into the closet.

It was barely big enough for me to stand in comfortably. The walls were
painted the same colour as the bedroom, and the floor was bare. There
was nothing hidden behind the boxes because the boxes were outside now.
Nothing in the corners, nothing above me, nothing that explained why the
door behaved the way it did.

Emma’s phone buzzed behind me.
She answered it while I crouched down to examine the inside of the door.
I ran my fingers along the edge, searching for a bolt or some kind of
catch. I shut the door, then opened it again from the inside. It worked
perfectly.

I did it a second time.

Still fine.

I switched on my phone’s flashlight and examined the latch area more
closely. The strangest thing was that there didn’t seem to be any
locking mechanism at all. Just a handle and a simple metal catch.
Behind me, Emma’s voice grew louder.

“I said I’ll call you back. I’m in the middle of something.”

There was a pause. Then I heard her sigh.

“Fine. I’ll be there soon.”

I assumed she was talking about the kids. I was too busy examining the
door to ask.
After a few minutes, I decided I’d seen enough. I reached for the handle
and pulled.
The door didn’t move.

I tried again, pushing the handle down harder.

Nothing.

“For fuck’s sake.”

I gave the door a shove with my shoulder. It didn’t even rattle.

“Emma?”

No answer.

I knocked on the door.

“Em, it’s locked itself again. Can you open it?”

I waited, expecting to hear her laugh or tell me to stop messing around.

Nothing came.

“Emma?”

I tried the handle again. Then again, more forcefully.

My stomach began to tighten. I pulled out my phone and called her.

The call couldn’t connect.

I sent her a message asking her to come back into the bedroom and open
the door but it wouldn’t deliver.

Then I looked at the door.

I took a few slow breaths, trying to keep myself calm. There had to be a
reason for this. A jammed latch, a faulty handle, something simple that
I just hadn’t noticed.

I reached for the handle again and pushed gently.

Nothing.

I tried a little harder, then put my weight against the door. I really
didn’t want to have to call the fire department to get me out of my own
closet.

“Come on.”

I took a step back and drove my shoulder into it.

The door gave way so suddenly that I stumbled through it and landed on
the bedroom floor.

For a few seconds, I just lay there, catching my breath. Then I climbed
to my feet, relieved to be out. That relief didn’t last long.

The boxes were gone.

I stood up properly and looked around the room. The bare bed frame in
the corner was gone too. In its place was a proper bed, complete with a
mattress and neatly tucked sheets.

“Emma?”

My voice sounded strange in the room.

I turned slowly, taking in my surroundings. The walls were the same
colour. The wardrobe was where it had always been. Even the window
looked right.

But something was off.

“Emma, are you still here?”

I stepped into the hallway.

There were pictures on the walls.

I’d left them bare when I’d moved in. I hadn’t even got around to
hanging a clock, let alone decorating the place. Now there were framed
landscapes and photographs arranged along the hallway, all of them
neatly spaced.

One of the photographs stopped me.

It was me and Charlotte, standing together somewhere I didn’t recognise.
We were smiling at the camera, her arm around my waist. I looked older
than I remembered being in the last photos I’d taken of us.

I pulled it off the wall and stared at it.

I had no memory of the place, or of the photograph being taken.

“Emma?”

I hurried into the living room. No sign of her.

The television was bigger than mine, and there were bookshelves along
the far wall, filled with books. A couple of ornaments sat on the
mantelpiece. Everything looked lived in, settled. Not like someone had
broken in and stolen my things, but like someone had been living here
for years.

I pulled out my phone and tried calling Emma again.

Still no signal.

I opened the front door. Her car was gone. So was mine.

For a moment, I stood on the doorstep, looking out at the street.
Everything seemed normal, which somehow made it worse.

I went back inside and checked the rest of the house.

My bedroom had different sheets on the bed. The clothes in my wardrobe
weren’t the ones I’d bought. In the bathroom, there were toiletries I’d
never seen before, arranged around the sink as though they’d always been
there.

My head was pounding.

This was my house. I’d bought it. I’d painted the walls, carried boxes
through the front door, and spent weekends trying to get the place into
some kind of order.

So whose stuff was all this?

I went into the kitchen, pacing between the counter and the table,
trying to make sense of what I was seeing.

Then I stopped.

There were magnets on the fridge.

Not just any magnets. Several of them were the same kind of magnetic
business cards I’d received in the mail since moving in.

I stared at them.

One was from the local plumber.

I took it off the fridge and turned it over in my hand. The card was
identical to the one I’d found wedged in the closet door.

I raced back to the spare bedroom. The door was still open.

I stepped inside and stood there, trying to slow my breathing. This was
my house. I knew it was my house. Something had happened, and I just
needed to work out what.

I shut the door and closed my eyes.

I was not fucking losing my mind.

I reached for the handle and pulled.

The door opened.

I nearly shit myself.

A man stood on the other side, staring straight at me.

For a second, neither of us moved.

Then I realised I was looking at myself.

Not exactly as I was now. He was clean-shaven, wearing a decent shirt
and a pair of jeans that actually fit. His hair was shorter, his face
less tired. He looked like me after a few good years, rather than the
last few months I’d been having.

He was standing in my spare bedroom, empty bed frame and all.

Then his expression changed.

“Fuck!”

He slammed the door shut.

I stumbled backwards, my heart hammering against my ribs. I couldn’t
move. Couldn’t think. I just stood there, trying to make sense of what
I’d seen.

Then I grabbed the handle.

It wasn’t locked.

I pulled, but the door wouldn’t budge. It felt as though someone on the
other side was holding it shut.

“Hey!”

I banged on the door.

“Hey, let me the fuck out of here!”

I threw my shoulder against it. The door shuddered but didn’t open.

Someone was holding it from the other side.

“Who the fuck are you?” I yelled, slamming both palms against the wood.

There was no answer.

“Fuck! Fuck!”

I backed up and threw myself against the door again.

It gave way.

I stumbled through, nearly falling, and caught myself against the wall.
For a second, I couldn’t tell what had happened. I was back in the room,
and I ran into the hallway expecting to see him.

Instead, I saw a woman standing a few feet away.

I froze.

“Charlotte?”

She looked just as startled to see me. Then her expression softened, and
she stepped towards me.

“Honey, are you okay? You look like you’ve seen a ghost.”

I just stared at her.

She gave a small, uncertain laugh and waved a hand in front of my face.

“Hello? Danny?”

“Have you seen Emma?” I managed.

Charlotte’s smile faded.

“Honey…”

She moved closer, but I took a step back.

“Have you been having those nightmares again? About the accident?”

I stared at her.

“What accident?”

She stopped moving.

“What?”

“What the fuck are you talking about? What accident? Where the fuck am
I?”

My face felt hot. I looked around, searching for something familiar,
something that would tell me I hadn’t completely lost my mind.

Then I looked back at her.

“Where the fuck is Emma?”

Charlotte studied my face for a moment.

“Danny…”

I pointed back towards the spare bedroom. “The closet. The fucking
closet!”

Her expression hardened.

“Daniel, I thought we agreed we weren’t going to talk about the closet
again. I’m not having this argument with you again today.”

She sighed and rubbed her forehead.

“You stopped taking them again, haven’t you?”

There was no anger in her voice now. Just exhaustion. She looked at me
as though she’d been hoping things would be different this time, and I
had somehow let her down.

“I don’t know what I expected.”

She turned and walked into the kitchen.

“Charlotte, wait.”

I followed her, trying to find the words to explain what was happening.
Everything I said sounded ridiculous before I’d even said it.

“I know this is going to sound crazy, but… I don’t belong here.”

She stopped and turned to face me. The frustration disappeared from her
expression, replaced by something that looked almost like fear.

“What are you trying to say?” Her voice cracked slightly. “Are you
leaving me?”

“What? No. I’m not… That’s not what I mean.”

“That’s just fucking great, Daniel.”

Her eyes filled with tears. She looked away, shaking her head.

“I was there through the funeral, through everything. And now you’re
telling me you don’t belong here?”

“Charlotte, that’s not what I’m trying to say. Wait… the funeral? Whose
funeral?”

She stared at me.

For a second, she looked as though I’d said something unforgivable.

Then she scrunched up her face and stormed past me.

Charlotte stormed out through the front door, slamming it behind her. A
moment later, I heard her car start and watched through the window as
she drove away.

I stood in the doorway for a while, trying to make sense of what had
just happened.

The funeral. The medication. The closet.

None of it made any sense.

Eventually, I turned back towards the house. I started searching,
opening drawers and cupboards, looking for anything that might explain
where I was or what the hell was going on. I checked the living room,
the bedrooms, even the bathroom. Anything that might tell me what had
happened to Emma, or why Charlotte seemed to think I was someone I
wasn’t.

I found it tucked away at the bottom of a kitchen drawer.

An In Memory of Emma and Colleen Revesby pamphlet.

I stared at it, reading the names over and over as if they might change.

Emma and Colleen.

My Mother’s and Sister’s name.

I shook my head and let out a short, disbelieving laugh.

“What kind of sick fucking joke is this?”

The pamphlet was dated a few months before I’d moved into my house.

I shoved it back into the drawer and slammed it shut.

“No. No, no, no.”

I dragged both hands through my hair, trying to get my thoughts under
control. My chest felt tight, and I couldn’t seem to take a proper
breath.

“What the fuck is going on?” I screamed.

The house gave me nothing back.

Then I remembered the closet.

Whatever was happening, it had started with that fucking door. I was
sure of it.

I ran back down the hallway and into the spare bedroom.

The door stood wide open, exactly as I’d left it.

I took a breath and climbed back through the closet, pulling the door
shut behind me.

I waited a few seconds before trying the handle.

Locked.

I tried again, then again, forcing myself to slow down. Panicking wasn’t
going to get me anywhere. I needed to figure this out. I needed to get
back to my own house, back to my own life.

I tried the handle once more.

This time, I felt something give on the other side.

I stopped and listened. Nothing.

I pushed harder. The door began to move, slowly opening a few
centimetres before something caught against it. I leaned my weight into
it, forcing the gap wider until I could just squeeze through.

Two cardboard boxes had been shoved up against the other side.

I stumbled into the spare bedroom and rushed into the hallway.

“Emma?”

I ran towards the kitchen, but before I could reach it, something
slammed into me from behind. I hit the floor hard, the breath knocked
out of me as a pair of hands grabbed my shirt and forced me onto my
back.

“What the fuck? Get off me!”

I swung wildly, trying to protect myself, and caught a glimpse of the
man above me.

It was him.

The other Daniel.

His face was twisted with anger, but beneath it I could see something
else. Fear.

“I won’t let you take them away from me!” he screamed.

His fist caught me in the stomach. I doubled over, gasping, but he
grabbed me before I could get away. Another blow caught me across the
face, sending my head sideways.

I tried to fight back, grabbing at his arms and throwing punches
wherever I could, but I couldn’t get him off me. He kept coming.

My nose started bleeding. My vision blurred as I struggled to draw
breath.

“Stop!” I managed to choke out. “What the fuck is wrong with you?”

He didn’t answer.

The last thing I remember was looking up at my own face, watching him
hit me again.

Then everything went black.

When I came around, I could hear Charlotte’s voice.

“Please, he’s not waking up. I don’t know what happened. Please, just
send someone.”

I tried to open my eyes, but everything was blurry. She was kneeling
beside me, crying, her phone pressed to her ear.

“Charlotte?” I tried to say.

It came out as little more than a whisper.

She looked down at me.

“Danny? Danny, can you hear me?”

I tried to answer, but the room started to spin. Her voice faded into
the background, and the darkness swallowed everything again.

The next time I woke up, I was lying in an uncomfortable bed beneath a
thin hospital blanket. My head throbbed, and my face felt swollen. For a
moment, I couldn’t remember where I was.

A man in a white coat stood beside the bed, checking something on a
clipboard.

“Good to see you’re awake,” he said. “How are you feeling?”

“Like I’ve been hit by a fucking truck.”

He gave me a small, professional smile.

“I’m not surprised. You’ve had a nasty knock.”

I tried to sit up, but pain shot through my face and I sank back against
the pillow.

“What happened?”

“Your wife found you unconscious on the floor at home. From what we
understand, someone may have broken in and assaulted you. The police
will want to speak with you once you’re feeling up to it.”

My wife.

Charlotte.

I looked down and realised I was wearing a hospital gown. I patted the
sides of the bed, then checked the gown’s pockets, even though I already
knew there wouldn’t be anything there.

“Where’s my phone?”

The doctor glanced at his clipboard.

“You didn’t have any personal belongings on you when you were brought
in. No phone, no wallet, no keys. We’ll make sure you’re reunited with
them if they’re located.”

I stared at him.

No phone. No keys. No wallet.

I couldn’t even prove who I was supposed to be.

“When can I leave?”

“Let’s not get ahead of ourselves. You’ve been through quite a lot, and
we need to make sure you’re stable first.”

He made another note on the clipboard.

“Now that you’re awake, we’ll contact your wife and let her know.”

I looked towards the closed hospital room door.

I had no idea how to explain what had happened. I wasn’t even sure I
understood it myself.

But one thing was certain.

The man who’d attacked me was still out there.

And he looked exactly like me.
