I’m required to give up a pint of my blood every two months, but none of
it has ever gone to help a person in need. No, my donation is collected
for a different purpose—a far more important purpose.

I live in a small, isolated mountain community somewhere in the eastern
part of the country, but I’m not allowed to know exactly where. Our
contact with the outside world is limited. We rarely leave our
community, and we know little about what goes on beyond the natural
barrier of forest and earth that so effectively excludes us from the
rest of the population. I can count on one hand the number of times I’ve
left my home, and it would require even fewer fingers to tally how many
times I’ve had access to the internet. We live a simple life in our
community; a quiet life. We are happy there, and we don’t want for much.

Nestled into the mountains, on the very outskirts of our community, is a
cave. In this cave, not far from its entrance, is a large stone slab.
And behind this slab resides one of the most powerful demons to have
ever walked our mortal world.

The demon does not remain there of its own accord; It has been confined
behind the wall of stone for centuries, where it can neither return to
its domain in hell nor torment the land of the living. It is trapped,
and, God willing, it will remain so for as long as there are mortals
like us to maintain its earthen prison.

We don’t remember the demon’s name—it was intentionally discarded and
forgotten generations ago—but we remember its history. It arrived here
hundreds of years ago, during an especially difficult winter that
greatly challenged the settlers in this region. Faced with almost
certain doom, the settlers prayed to an entity who they thought to be
divine, but who they soon realized was anything but. It helped them to
survive that early winter, but at a price—a price paid in mortal flesh.
At first the settlers saw the sacrifices as a necessary evil, but soon
they were forced to give up so many of their own that it became
unsustainable. It did not take them long to realize that they would have
been better off facing the harsh winter that had led them to call upon
the entity in the first place. They formulated a plan to cast the entity
back to hell, but though they had managed to conjure it easily enough,
they quickly found themselves unable to send it back to the depths from
whence it had come. The entity enjoyed its life in the mortal realm, and
it had no intention of leaving its new home behind.

But though it refused to return to hell, that did not mean it couldn’t
be stopped.

The entity lived in the cave overlooking the community, where it
slumbered away its days and unleashed its terror upon its victims at
night. The settlers used its nocturnal tendencies to seal it behind a
barrier of stone while it slept, but they knew that this wall alone
would not be enough to keep it at bay. A sacrificial offering had to be
regularly applied to the wall in order to keep the demon sealed behind
it: a sacrifice in the form of human vitality.

Flash forward to modern day, and my community continues to make the same
sacrifice that our ancestors did all those generations ago. We are
broken down into two basic groups: those who give, and those who paint.
The givers must regularly supply their blood, which the painters then
use to stain the stone slab, thus maintaining the seal that has been in
place for so many long years. We all must do our part to keep that
monster trapped within its personal Tartarus. The sacrifice we make
ensures the protection of the greater good.

Life in our community is not as bad as it may sound. In many ways we
live normal and productive, if not slightly limited, lives. Giving a
pint of blood to the wall every so often is far superior to the regular
offerings of flesh that the demon demanded when it was free. We can
easily survive without some of the red essence that flows through our
bodies, which means we are all too happy to give a portion of it up when
it is our turn to donate to the cause. For most of us, life will go on
as normal after the completion of our regular contribution.

But that does not mean that all of us are so lucky.

Because while the blood lathered upon the wall is usually enough to keep
the entity contained, we are nearing that one day of the year—the day
when the demon is at its most powerful—when a sacrifice of flesh is
still required. Every year, during the last third of October, three
givers are chosen to be the potential provider of that ultimate
oblation. The three chosen are taken away from the community, where they
live amongst the corruption of the outside world for ten days ahead of
the sacrifice in order to taint their souls enough to more easily whet
the demon’s appetite. The three of them are presented before the stone
slab on the night of October 31st, where one of them is chosen by the
entity to sate its yearly hunger. The two surviving chosen are then
welcomed into the ranks of the painters, never having to offer their own
blood again, while the one who is consumed is honored with a grand
celebration that lasts until the coming of dawn.

This year, I was selected as one of the chosen. I was removed from the
community and brought to the outside world, where I have been for the
last few days as I prepare for the ritual at the end of the month. I’ve
enjoyed my time out here, so much so that I am tempted to never go back,
but I know that it is my duty to return to the community.

And so return to the community I shall.

Even though the three of us are allowed to imbibe in much of the
pleasures of modern society while we await the coming of the ritual, we
are still expected to respect certain restrictions. Our chaperone does
not know that I currently have access to the internet, nor do they know
that I am writing this. I doubt if they’ll ever find out. I’m writing
this because I want the greater world to know of the sacrifices that my
community makes every single day. It’s not right that we should have to
save you all in secret, without you ever knowing the truth of what we
do.

I don’t know what will happen after the ritual at the end of the month.
If I’m not selected to be consumed, then I will become a painter, which
means I will likely never leave my community again, unless it’s to be a
chaperone for any future chosen. Either way, I doubt if you will ever
hear from me again, but I’m okay with that. I don’t need to know how the
outsiders will react to the knowledge that I have given them. I’m
content in knowing that my message has been delivered and that our
sacrifice has been revealed to the world.

I don’t know if I will live to see November. At first this thought
terrified me, but it doesn’t anymore. If I’m chosen to be consumed, then
my death will have more meaning than my life ever could. If I’m not
chosen, then I will continue to live with the same purpose that I always
have: to keep an unimaginable evil at bay.

And either outcome suits me just fine.
