I hadn’t seen Amanda for a while. It was a chilly October night, the
moon shone bright, illuminating the tendrils of fog which hung over the
landscape. We were at a fair, my friends and I. John brought his
girlfriend Scarlett and I was there with my date Misty.
Carnival lights flashed all around us as we explored, playing games with
our girls every now and again.
I was buying cotton candy  for Misty and I when it happened. I saw
Amanda staring through the crowd right at me. I turned away hoping she
somehow hadn’t seen me and made my way back to my friends. “You’re such
a sweetheart!” said Misty as I handed her the cotton candy. “Don’t
mention it.” I responded, looking over at the haunted house that John
and Scarlett had abandoned us for.
“Are you sure you don’t want to try out the haunted house? I’m sure it’s
not really that bad.” I asked, the silence being too much for me.
“I’m good. I told you… I don’t like creepy stuff.” said Misty, contently
eating her cotton candy. 
My mind was still wandering to Amanda. We had broken up a couple months
before that day. It wasn’t really a rough breakup, not for her anyway.
She had told me she wasn’t feeling it anymore and that she no longer
wanted to be with me. She went crazy after that. Got admitted into some
kind of mental asylum. God knows why. At least that’s what John had told
me. Who knows if that’s actually true. I hadn’t been in touch with her. 
That night though, she showed up.
Five minutes had passed when the three of them emerged from the haunted
house. John, Scarlett and Amanda. “Who is that?” asked Misty with a
disgusted look on her face, clearly confused. “Thats Amanda.” I said,
thinking it wise to tell the truth. “How the fuck do you know her?!”
Misty retorted. She was getting suspicious. “Shes my ex.” Misty glared
at me “and what’s she doing here?” I glared back. “I don’t know, but I’m
sure as hell gonna find out.” They were already approaching, talking
cordially with each other. “Very classy. Well you can sure as hell do
that without me!” said Misty, right before barging off. “Hey!” I yelled.
But I didn’t try to stop her, perhaps something about her absence
relieved me.
“Hey! Look who we found!” Yelled John, mocking me. I turned around to
face them. “Whats she doing here?” I asked, gesturing towards Amanda and
trying to seem careless. Seeing Amanda up close intrigued me. She looked
good. Well put together I suppose. One couldn’t even tell that she was
psycho. Amanda wore her long brown hair neatly in a ponytail and quite
unusually she wore a black dress shirt. Her eyes met mine. There was a
strange glint, made her look almost creepy. Something had definitely
changed. “Hello Roy.” she said.
I turned to John. “Seriously?” I expected a really good explanation for
this. I’ve known John all my life and I know when he’s hiding something
from me. “Oh c’mon.” said John “I didn’t know you were bringing a date
and it sounded like Amanda really needed to talk to you, so…”  I looked
at him in disbelief. “Wow… ok. Misty is gone now. Hi Amanda, long time
no see.” A silence followed. Perhaps I was too worked up afterall. “What
do you want to talk about?” I broke the silence. She cleared her throat.
“I… I just wanted to catch up with you…” Her voice was barely audible.
“Tell you about what’s been going on in my life.” I looked back at
Scarlett and John and said “You are excused.” I was trying not to let my
clearly very justified frustration show. This was the last thing I
wanted to be doing on my day off.
“I know.” I said. Amanda looked at me curiously. “Hows that?” There was
something strange about the tone of her voice as she said this, which in
hindsight should have been alarming. Honestly I was just lonely at the
time and I can’t deny that I was still into her. “John told me.” I
responded. “Not that it matters. I’ve lived here all my life, word gets
around.” Amanda abruptly slapped her forehead, startling even me, being
such a stark contrast to her previous calm demeanor. “I knew he couldn’t
keep a secret.” She muttered.
After insulting John for a couple of minutes, she finally went on to
tell me all about her stay in the mental asylum. She however repeatedly
avoided the topic of what actually happened. I know these kinds of
things are extremely personal and all, but it seemed silly of me not to
at least ask, so ask I did. 
“I was wondering, why exactly were you admitted in the first place?” A
short silence followed.
She leaned in close to the point where I could feel her cold breath in
my neck. “I don’t want to talk about that here. Maybe someplace a little
more private…” She whispered. I looked around. “There’s nobody here…” I
cleared my throat. “You can tell me here.”
At this point we found ourselves at the northmost perimeter of the fair,
where the parking lot was.
It was then that I got a bombastic idea: Why not have Amanda over, you
know, for old times sake. Linking up with Amanda again sounded really
good at the time. I now wish that I had chosen more wisely.
I , in typical Roy fashion, decided on trying to take her home. I
couldn’t help it. I had had a crush on Amanda all the way through high
school and losing her was honestly one of the low points of my life. So,
naturally when given the opportunity, I couldn’t stop myself. And so I
said: “I could take you to my place.” I wrapped my arm around her
shoulder. “How does steak and wine sound?”
As I said this, I immediately realised my mistake: Misty had taken me
there that evening so I didn’t really have a good ride home. I could
have asked John of course but that would have just been awkward. Lucky
for me it didn’t ever come to that. “What if we go to my place? I have
my own car, so you can just come with me.” said Amanda excitedly. I
sighed in relief. I thought I had talked myself into a pickle.
As we made our way to Amanda’s car, which turned out to be a brand new
Audi, I thought about John and Scarlett. They had probably already gone
home, so there was no point in looking for them. John probably figured
as much.
“New whip?” I asked , remembering her old Toyota. “Brand new actually. I
bought this last week.” She said, slowly walking around the car,
seemingly admiring it. “Did your Grandpa die or something?” I said
jokingly. “How can you afford this?” I was astounded. While not being a
car guy, even to this day I wouldn’t be able to tell you what car that
was, I could definitely tell that it was expensive. Luxury even.
She chuckled. “ Yeah… something like that.” Amanda looked up at me as if
waiting for a reaction. Her expression was completely neutral, monotone
even. “Lets just say… I haven’t had trouble getting what I want
recently.”
I opened the passenger side door and entered. The seats were full
leather and colorful LED lights illuminated the interior.
“Hows that?” I asked as she got into the driver’s seat.  She smiled,
“Let’s save that for the wine.”
The ride to her house was excruciatingly silent. Amanda, besides the few
movements necessary for driving, didn’t move an inch.
In the darkness of her brand new sports car she looked just as
manufactured, like a doll. Don’t get me wrong: She looked good, she just
didn’t look or even seem like the Amanda I knew.
At one point during the drive, I finally began to suspect that Amanda
wasn’t as mentally… healthy… lets say, as she let on.
We were driving through a tunnel, lights flashing from left and right in
intervals when I asked: “What about some music?” That seemed to make the
silence even quieter for a split second. She turned her head towards me,
clearly no longer paying attention to the road. “Im enjoying the
silence.” she said, still staring. “Well I’m not.” I said bluntly. “And
jeez, keep your eyes on the road.” Her weird behaviour was beginning to
piss me off. She snapped back to the road. “Right, sorry.” She muttered.
I began using the massive screen in the center console to look for some
music, but to my surprise, there was no audio.
I looked for speakers, but a metal plate was bolted over where they
should have been. “Ok.” I said. “So you straight up don’t listen to any
music?” She cleared her throat. “No, I enjoy the silence.” She had
definitely gone insane. After that it was clear to me. I was considering
asking her to just drop me off at home, but I was curious about what
Amanda was going to say. I know that might sound irresponsible, but you
have to understand that Amanda was always harmless. I didn’t believe
that any amount of delusion could change that.
She then went on a rant, well the closest thing to a rant that Amanda
was capable of, about how we don’t know the intricacies of what music
does to us. 
“Our soul.”
She said, clearly passionate. “I doubt the rap music you listen to is
very good for your mental health.”
“You’ve gotta be joking…”  I scoffed. But I didn’t say more. I was
flabbergasted and  under the impression that this was some symptom of
her disease. 
We pulled up to a house in a neighborhood I had never seen before. All
the houses were huge, sprawling mansions or modern, sleek penthouses.
The latter being the kind Amanda owned.
I chuckled. “I don’t remember this being your house.” I said. “Wasn’t it
a little bit smaller?” I teased.
“Like I said, I haven’t had issues lately.”
She left it at that as she unlocked the front door. The penthouse was
dark, with a solitary lamp illuminating the kitchen island, hanging from
the ceiling.
Amanda brought out a wine bottle, some luxury shit she said was from
1961, and poured us both a glass.
Then she began: It all started in Bell’s thrift store. You know, the one
in the city center. I went there with some friends shortly after we
broke up. I wasn’t looking for anything specific, just browsing really.
But I found an old Sony radio with a built-in CD player. I loved it, so
I bought a CD to go along with it. I stood in front of the CD section
browsing for a couple of minutes until I found something interesting. It
was a collection of tracks called “Fluorescence”. I hadn’t heard of any
of the songs and that was enough to pique my interest. I instantly gave
it a listen when I arrived home that day. It was beautiful. I hadn’t
heard anything like it. I really can’t describe it. I think the closest
thing would be a bright beam of light, a beam of light from heaven.
I listened to it everyday from that point on. First while studying, then
while cleaning and then, after a week, all the time. It was like a drug.
Every single day I couldn’t wait to get home to listen to my CD.
However, over time that once so beautiful sound began to terrify me. It
began telling me a story of unspeakable evils. I couldn’t stop
listening, even though I hated every second of it. It began to feel like
it was changing me, infiltrating my brain and so I finally got rid of
it.
One day I just… brought it right back to Bell’s and donated it.
The silence that followed relieved me and my life started to look up
again.
However, this was only the beginning of my torture though.
I started having dreams. They were of little substance really. I was
staring into an abyss out of which the song came up to me and taunted
me. Each night it grew louder and each night I woke up in a cold sweat.
One night, however, I woke up from one of these dreams and to my horror,
the song was still playing. The radio was on. I quickly shut it off and
upon checking the CD tray. There it was: Fluorescence. 
I began hearing it everywhere I went. I soon figured out that whenever
music was playing somewhere, I heard Florescence instead of what was
actually playing. I could hear no other music. It was maddening.
I tried talking with friends about this, with my mother and finally with
Craig, my boyfriend at the time. My friends distanced themselves from me
thinking I was insane and my mom told me to just see a psychiatrist. But
Craig believed me, so I invited him to my house one day and showed him
the CD. I didn’t play it nor did I intend to. But Craig said “Ok Amanda,
let’s hear it.” With a smirk he played it. For a minute he just stood
there facing the radio, saying nothing. Suddenly he began to shriek and
what can only described as a blinding white light engulfed him as the
song played. 
I shook him 
and yelled at him. 
It was no use. Finally, I unplugged the radio and the shrieking abruptly
stopped. 
What was left of Craig toppled to the floor. The whole front half of his
body was burnt to a crisp revealing his bones in what can only be
described as a charred, melted mess.
I stood up. “Amanda, you are clearly not well… Is there somebody I
should call or…”
“Sit down!” she snapped.
I didn’t. “You’re telling me you hear that song in place of other music
everywhere? Have you gone mad? And apparently you murdered someone too?”
I blurted out, becoming more and more angry. 
Amanda brought out a pack of cigarettes from her pocket. “Well… not all
the time.” she said, lighting her cigarette. “There is the ice cream
truck, the neighbors’ parties and stuff like that. But that’s why I have
to take certain precautions.” Amanda switched on the lights, revealing
green and yellow insulation covering all four walls and the ceiling. I
looked around me at the result of what could only have been some sort of
mental break. I was at a loss for words.
“Yeah, I have killed people. But what matters is that this whole time,
the only thing keeping me here was you, keeping me alive I mean.” She
took another puff of her cigarette. “I still love you Roy.” I took a
deep breath. “Amanda, you don’t mean that. Look at the state of you: I
mean you’re stapling insulation to the walls. This place looks like the
set from Barney’s. Please just take me home. I wish you all the best but
you need help and that’s definitely not me.”
Amanda chuckled as though I had made some sort of joke. “I knew up here
it would fade…” She stood up and walked past  me to a little oak table
in the corner of the dining room, upon which sat 
an old Sony radio.
“No c’mon. What are you doing?” I said. Something deep inside me was
beginning to well up, something like dread. I, of course, didn’t believe
her story. But something inside me knew.
“So I decided… I’m taking you with me.” She pressed the play button on
the radio and a song began to play. It was a beautiful classical piece,
the likes of which even Mozart pales in comparison to. It was telling a
story, one I could not understand. I felt like I was traveling somewhere
I did not know. But I wanted to wait there and listen, wait and see
where it goes.
A strange note pierced through the melody.
It sounded like a blood curdling scream. 
I saw Amanda engulfed by a bright white light. She was shrieking and
screaming as her skin began to peel and slip off.
“Come with me!” 
She wailed. Her eyes were whitening and her skin was melting off her
bones. She did not collapse though. She just stood there facing me.
Between shrieks she took a puff from her cigarette she still had
clutched in her now bony fingers. The smoke spiraled out of holes
forming in her skull and danced around her head making her look like
some evil witch.
I finally came to my senses and rushed to turn off the radio. Amanda
tried to stop me, but she could barely walk.
I pressed the power button just to be sure and the music stopped, the
light vanished and Amanda fell to the floor.
The police didn’t cause me any trouble. They told me Amanda was an
escaped patient from a nearby mental institution whose condition had
rapidly deteriorated over the last couple of months.  
She escaped from the very mental institution I am currently writing this
from.
They sound proofed my room and keep me indoors. I don’t know why I’m
still here. Perhaps it is some kind of cruel joke. I feel like death is
around the corner. So I decided to tell my story, before I no longer get
the chance. What’s keeping me here?
