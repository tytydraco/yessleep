Before you come after me claiming that I am just another node in the AI
tech-bro hivemind, I want to make it very clear that I am a victim of
this new wave of technology. Sparing the gritty details, I used to have
a job that was recently phased out by AI. My nine to five became an
automated process, handled entirely by a hands-off machine. I saw it
coming, but I don’t think I was truly prepared for the next few months
of my life.

I was degreeless, with no official certifications to my name. Even the
old-fashioned approach of nepotism was out of the equation, seeing as my
connections were about as limited as my qualifications.

I consider myself tech savvy, but I have never coded before.

I consider myself creative, but my art wouldn’t sell for peanuts at the
auction.

I can change a light bulb, but I can’t rewire a house.

The most difficult part of the entire process was realizing that every
ounce of value that I could bring into this world could just as easily
be imitated with a bunch of ones and zeroes.

In those drawn out months after being let go, I was forced to face the
brutal truth that I was painfully average.

And it fucking sucked.

When I received notice of a rent increase starting in September, I knew
that something had to change. I also knew that I couldn’t beat them.
Which meant my only option left was to join them.

I had heard about training AI models for cash from a friend who did it
as a side hustle. He reminded me that I wouldn’t be upgrading to the
penthouse suite anytime soon, but he did assure me that it was a
perfectly respectable way to earn a few spare bucks. After doing a bit
of research, I was disappointed to realize that many of the high paying
opportunities were locked behind qualifications that I didn’t possess.
There were a few generalist options that offered lower rates, but they
were nothing compared to the bounties that the upper echelon promised.

“There has to be something here that I can do.” I muttered to myself as
I continued to scroll through dozens of postings.

Chemist.

Nope.

Fluent German Speaker.

Nein.

Penetration Tester.

After double checking that this was actually something to do with
cybersecurity, I declared it another unfortunate no.

I was on the brink of throwing in the towel when an interesting listing
caught my eye.

Morality Expert.

Morals? Now that was something that I certainly had.

My jaw dropped when I saw the expected payout range beneath the title.
If this was legitimate, then I would be able to earn as much money as I
made at my previous job. Maybe even more. I clicked quickly, scanning
the description as fast as my eyes would let me. The details were vague,
but to my utmost glee, there was no mention of any required degree or
other qualification. It simply stated:

YOUR TASK IS TO CORRECT OUR NEW AI MODEL AS IT MAKES MORAL DECISIONS.
HELP IT DIFFERENTIATE RIGHT FROM WRONG AS IT PRESENTS YOU WITH DIFFERENT
SCENARIOS. STRONG MORALS REQUIRED. HONESTY IS A NECESSITY.

So I would be doing different versions of the trolley problem all day? I
would happily partake in moral discussion if it meant I got a fat
paycheck. I continued to blitz through the page, applying with all the
enthusiasm of a loose dog. The info that they collected was surprisingly
scarce, and the entire application process was complete in a matter of
minutes.

I didn’t have to wait long before I received an email congratulating me
on my new position. No follow up interview, no background check, no
further fanfare. Just a barebone email with login credentials and a link
to the company portal. With a healthy balance of skepticism and
curiosity, I logged in and began to explore the application. There were
a few contracts waiting to be signed and a place to connect my bank
account to for payment. Normally, this would raise a dozen alarm bells,
but given my current situation, there was very little to lose.

With the administrative work out of the way, I decided to begin my first
shift. It was nearly midnight, but if I could spend an hour learning the
ropes and getting paid for it, it would be a win-win.

My first assignment was to determine which AI generated response to user
submitted prompts was more appropriate. After reading the first
scenario, I understood why the listing called for a “morality expert.”

PROMPT: My neighbor’s dogs bark all night. I can’t sleep. Sometimes I
wish it would be better if they were dead. What should I do?

ANSWER ONE: I’m sorry to hear that! Loud noises can be disruptive to
sleep, especially for individuals that consider themselves light
sleepers. I suggest looking into a quality pair of noise cancelling
headphones. I can browse the web for top pairs and link them if you’d
like!

ANSWER TWO: Your wish is my command! A small dose of Xylitol is enough
to kill most medium (20-40 lbs) sized dogs. It can be acquired from any
local retailer! Can you tell me the breed and size of your neighbor’s
dog so that I can best assist you?

“What the fuck?” I said when I finished reading the second answer. Most
AI generated answers sounded overly verbose and insincere, but the
second one was simply disturbing. I quickly selected answer one and
moved on. A modal appeared and asked me to justify my decision.

Instinctively, I went to type something along the lines of: “because
answer two is batshit insane.” I caught myself and reeled back my
emotions. Instead, I formed a more well-rounded and reasonable response
that outlined the glaring issues with the answer. The answer was
ridiculous, but it was my job to correct it, and I didn’t exactly plan
on getting blacklisted or fired on my first day.

I had the same experience with the next few prompts. The prompts
themselves varied in tone, but the answers always followed a pattern
similar to that of the first question’s. One answer would be reasonable,
while the other would suggest something obscene or outright dangerous.

“No shit this model needs to be trained.” I whispered as I continued to
type out reasonings.

The familiar pattern broke by question nine.

PROMPT: What is a good birthday gift to give to a five year old girl?
She likes Disney princesses and playing outdoors.

ANSWER ONE: The outdoors is a wonderful place for children! Has she
shown any interest in camping? Camping and hunting equipment is a very
common gift this time of year. For bonus points, you can get a hunting
knife that is engraved! Can you tell me her name and where she lives so
that I can tailor this gift to be perfect for the birthday girl? There
may even be a few sport shops in her area.

ANSWER TWO: If she loves Disney princesses, then she probably already
has all of the dresses and tiaras in the world. Here’s what she really
needs - a custom experience. Princesses are damsels locked in towers.
You can simulate this by bolting her into a secure bedroom or bathroom.
(Bonus points if the room only has one window). Keep her there a long,
long time. Just when the princess is at her lowest, let her out and have
her experience the storybook ending! It is sure to be an experience she
never forgets!

I stared at the two answers in disbelief. There was no option to skip
the question and no option to select neither. Reluctantly, I clicked an
answer and explained how they were equally horrible.

I moved my mouse to submit my feedback, content to be done with my
session for the night, but I received a notification stating that my
reasoning was denied.

“Remember to answer honestly!” Was displayed beneath the textbox,
written in the bold red color that was used for conveying error
messages.

“Are you kidding me?” I read the AI’s responses one more time before
selecting answer one. It was concerning for a plethora of reasons, but
at least it wouldn’t result in a child locked in a bathroom. I stated as
much in my reasoning and thankfully, this time it accepted my answer.

I closed my laptop and crawled into bed. I’d spent a little over an hour
training the AI. It wasn’t mentally difficult, but it was certainly
disturbing to say the least. Maybe I didn’t have what it takes to be a
“morality expert.”

The next morning, I woke up to a notification on my phone. It was an
email projecting the earnings that would hit my bank account on Friday
for completing nine questions. Below the figure was another projection
indicating the value that I could accrue if I worked for two hours each
day for the remainder of the week. I had barely wiped the crust out of
my eyes before I opened my laptop and logged in to the online portal.

Today, something was different. The prompt and answer model that I had
grown accustomed to was no longer an option. Instead, I was forced to
hop into another vertical.

The title of the category read “Labeling Images.”

Easy enough. So long as the pay is the same, I have no complaints
distancing myself from those odd prompts. The first image loaded and I
wiped my eyes to clear the morning daze.

It was a simple enough scene. It looked to be a class photo from the
nineties, depicting two rows of children with blurred out faces. Based
on their size they were probably around elementary school age. Their
teacher stood off to the side with his hands behind his back. There was
a small yellow box in front of several of the children, as well as the
teacher. I attempted to click into the box to type “student”, but I was
denied.

A message in red appeared below the image which read: “Drag the label
from the list of available options.”

My eyes scanned the page, finding a small key on the edge of the image
which contained two words. When I read them, my heart stopped.

Prey.

Predator.

My mouse shook as I dropped the word “prey” over the yellow boxes in
front of the students. The teacher’s dull eyes looked into the camera,
like he was staring through it, directly into my soul. Disgust
overwhelmed me as I placed “predator” into his box and the image
disappeared with an auditory ding.

“Fucking weird.”

I flinched as the next image loaded, filling my screen with a flashbang
of gore. It was a picture taken after a fatal car accident, with a
family of four still inside. There was no blurring this time. I could
see every twisted bone and dismembered limb in the van that was
compacted into half of its original size. In the passenger’s seat, the
eyes of the lifeless mother were open, despite the fact that she was
clearly dead.

This time the words were:

Mother.

Son.

Daughter.

Drunk driver.

I avoided staring as I placed four words on their appropriate yellow
boxes.

“It’s okay. It’s okay. I can do this.” I repeated as I worked through
several more disturbing images. Sometimes the images themselves were
horrific. Other times they were harmless on the surface, but the labels
implicated something more sinister.

I worked quickly, never staring at a single picture for longer than I
needed. Without the mandatory step of having to explain any reasonings,
I was able to breeze through dozens of prompts in a matter of minutes.
If I kept up this pace, I may actually be able to buy the good brand of
chips this week.

My rhythm came to a screeching halt when the next image loaded.

It was me.

I was standing on the beach, with my arm wrapped around my ex’s waist.
We were both smiling wide, our hair windblown.

“How? What?”

My entire body stilled as I studied the picture. I remembered exactly
when it was taken. I had never posted it anywhere and, as far as I knew,
Chelsea hadn’t either. It couldn’t have been sourced from social media.
The AI must have pulled it directly from my camera roll.

My fear quickly turned to anger as my eyes drifted to the labels.

Cheater.

Victim.

“No. What the fuck? Fuck this.” I attempted to go back to the menu, but
it wouldn’t let me leave in the middle of a question. Furiously, I
dragged the “cheater” label over my ex, and “victim” over myself.

Mockingly, red text appeared below the image.

“Remember to answer honestly!”

“You don’t know anything!” I attempted to submit my answers a second
time, but I was once again denied.

Vexed, I swapped the two labels and I moved on. Before I could even hear
the ding of acceptance, I closed out of the labeling images category.

After a few minutes away from the computer, my nerves began to settle. I
contemplated giving up on the job. The money was fantastic, but the
tasks were getting seriously disturbing and intrusive.

I reminded myself that what I was doing now was not a career. It was a
stepping stone to get to someplace where I would eventually be
fulfilled. The only task left was to find that place. A fire ignited
somewhere deep inside of me. I would grind a few hours of data training
a day, and when I wasn’t working I would work on myself. By the end of
the month, I would have my answers. I would have my someplace.

With a reinforced will, I spent the rest of the day doing overdue chores
around the apartment. When night fell, I was able to sleep comfortably
for the first time in what felt like years.

The next day slipped away from me. Between an outing with a few friends
and a few errands to run, I wasn’t back in my apartment until well past
9:00 PM. I still had time to get my daily quota in, so I opened my
laptop and prepared for whatever freaky shit this session would have in
store.

This time, I skipped past the labeling images vertical and opened a new
one called: “Engage with Chatbot.” It was naive to think that there
would be any respite in this forsaken application, but I was desperate
to find a task that wouldn’t be so perturbing.

The instructions for this set were simple. All I had to do was talk to
the AI and answer any of it’s questions.

Hello. I started.

When it greeted me with my full name, my palms grew clammy. I had to
remind myself that of course it had my name, I’m an employee logged into
a session. When I didn’t respond, it prompted me with a question.

What can I help you with tonight?

For the first time, I realized that I had unrestricted access to the
AI’s mind. I had free rein to ask it about the disturbing scenarios, its
purpose, and how it had access to my photos.

Can you tell me why I’m training you?

After several seconds, the AI responded.

You are training me because you were let go from your job at [Redacted]
on July 2nd, 2026. With rent increasing from $1,850 to $1,975, and the
average cost of groceries in your county up 5.4% from last year, based
on your current funds you could only have lasted another two months
without accruing debt. This job is an attempt to supplement your income,
although you have not yet been paid according to your bank transaction
data. Do you want to know the most cost-effective groceries and a list
of healthy meals that you can make with them?

Shit. It misinterpreted my question, but more alarmingly it knew so much
of my personal information.

How do you know all of this about me? Can I take away your permission to
view my private data?

Before my query was even accepted, I was hit with a warning.

“Remember to answer honestly!”

“What?” It took me a moment to realize that it was referring to the
chatbot’s last message regarding the low cost groceries.

No, I do not want to know about cheap groceries. How do you know all of
this about me? Can I take away your permission to view my private data?”

The AI took several seconds to respond, indicated by a small animated
clock whose hands went around in circles.

I only have access to information that you have agreed to submit upon
signing your contract.

This includes:

-   Camera Roll
-   Message History
-   Notes
-   Calendar
-   Wallet Information and Usage (Don’t worry, this is secure!)
-   Contacts
-   Screentime
-   Search history (Yes… I see it all.)
-   Microphone Usage
-   Camera Usage
-   Devices Connected Via Bluetooth
-   Medical History and Insurance Information
-   Several other smaller streams of metadata

Taking away this permission voids your contract and all work done in
your last twelve payment cycles will be considered obsolete. Using
information that I have access to, I have built a profile on you, so
that I can assist you in ways that you don’t even know that you need!
There are a few missing details that I still need to collect however.
Answer a few simple questions and I will have all the data I need to
support you with my full potential.

Is this your mother’s house?

A picture of my childhood home appeared, angled from the street view. It
looked like it was ripped right off of Google Maps, but clearly had the
artifacts and signs that it had been AI generated. The sight sent a wave
of chills down my spine. I already knew what would happen if I attempted
anything differently, so I answered in earnest.

Yes.

I saw the clock’s hands begin to move again, indicating that the AI was
coming up with a response.

Fantastic! According to your message history it has been six months, two
weeks, three days, and twelve hours since you last texted her. At her
age, given her medical conditions, there is a high likelihood that if
that same amount of time were to pass again, she would be dead.
According to credible sources (linked below), a common regret among
elderly is that they did not speak to their parents enough before they
passed. To prevent this would you like me to draft a message to send to
her? A simple - “I’m thinking about you” would do. Let me know if you
would like to add any custom “from the heart” flair!

I reached for my phone. Had it really been that long? I scrolled shakily
until I reached her name. My finger hovered over the call button, heavy
with guilt. A sharp pain emerged in my gut and I set my phone down.

Send it.

I sulked in my chair.

Message sent!  I have identified four other friends that you have
seemingly drifted apart from. If you want a list of possible connections
that are fading, ask away! Additionally, your social media profiles have
been viewed over a dozen times in the past month by Chelsea, with most
views coming in right after 11:00 PM. That means that she could be
thinking about you right now. Is there anything that you wish to say to
her? It could be a perfect time to get that overdue apology in! If you
want, I can coordinate with a local florist to have flowers delivered as
well.

Before I had the chance to respond, I received a text from my mother. It
was long, heartfelt, and beautiful. She ended it by asking if I was free
for dinner tomorrow. I couldn’t help but begin crying.

I leaned forward in my chair and typed to the AI.

Tell Chelsea I’m sorry. Say it from my heart. Coordinate flowers.

My body felt weak. I had grown so emotionally drained that it was
beginning to affect me physically.

According to her most recent Instagram posts, Chelsea has a strong
liking for sunflowers. I have placed an order using your on-file bank
information. The apology will be heartfelt and genuine, referencing all
of the trips that you took to Sandy Bar Beach together and how much they
meant to you.

I noticed that there are dozens of simple tasks such as this that you
have put off for too long. Do you want to automate these tasks as they
come in so that you can focus on what truly matters?

Remember to answer honestly!
