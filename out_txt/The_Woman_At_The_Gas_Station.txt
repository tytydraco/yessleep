This happened about four years ago. I still don’t really know what to
make of it, but it scared me enough that I don’t stop at gas stations
alone at night anymore.
I was driving home after spending the weekend with my sister. It was
around 10:30, and I still had about an hour to go. I needed gas, and I’d
been needing to pee for probably the last thirty minutes, so I decided
to stop.
It was a pretty normal gas station right off the highway. One of the
bigger ones with a bunch of pumps and a little fast-food place inside.
There were a few cars parked out front, and everything was well-lit. I
didn’t think twice about pulling in.
I parked at a pump close to the building.
While I was getting my card out, I noticed a woman standing near the ice
machine. She was probably in her late forties or early fifties, wearing
jeans and a gray sweatshirt. Nothing unusual about her, except that she
seemed to be watching me.
I got out and started pumping gas.
A minute or so later, she walked over.
“Hey, honey, can I ask you something?”
I said sure.
She asked if I was headed north.
I told her I was, and she explained that her daughter was supposed to
pick her up, but her phone had died. She’d apparently been waiting there
for almost two hours and was trying to find someone who could give her a
ride closer to home.
I felt bad for her. She seemed genuinely upset, and I could tell she was
embarrassed about asking.
But I’m not picking up a stranger at night. I don’t care how nice they
seem.
So I apologized and told her I wasn’t comfortable giving anyone a ride.
She was actually really understanding about it.
“Oh, I don’t blame you one bit. Especially these days.”
She laughed a little and asked if I happened to have a phone charger she
could use instead.
I told her I had one, but it was plugged into my car and wouldn’t do her
much good unless she sat in there.
She said not to worry about it.
Then she looked through my back window.
“Aw, you’ve got a little one?”
There was a booster seat in the back from when I’d taken my nephew
somewhere earlier that weekend.
I told her it was my nephew’s.
She asked how old he was, and I said six.
She told me she had a grandson about that age, and we made a little
small talk about kids.
Then she thanked me anyway and went back toward the building.
That was it.
Honestly, if everything else hadn’t happened, I probably wouldn’t even
remember talking to her.
I finished getting gas and decided to skip the bathroom. I was tired and
just wanted to get home.
I got back in my car, locked the doors, and pulled up my GPS.
While I was sitting there, I happened to look toward the store.
The woman was standing inside by the front window, talking to a man.
He was wearing a black shirt and a baseball cap. I couldn’t really make
out his face, but I could tell he was looking in my direction.
Then she pointed toward my car.
Not just gesturing while she talked. She actually pointed right at me.
I remember thinking, Okay, that’s weird.
The man looked down at something in his hand, and she said something
else to him.
I started my car.
As I pulled away from the pump, the woman came back outside.
She waved at me like she was trying to get my attention.
I gave her a little wave back and kept going.
She started walking toward my car, waving harder.
I didn’t stop.
I felt a little rude, but something about the whole thing was making me
uncomfortable.
Once I got back on the highway, I called my husband.
I told him about the woman and how she’d pointed me out to some guy
inside.
He thought it was strange too, but neither of us really knew what to
make of it.
We talked for a few minutes, and then I hung up.
Maybe fifteen or twenty minutes later, I noticed a white pickup behind
me.
The only reason I noticed it was because its headlights were really
bright. It was following close enough that I kept getting blinded by my
mirrors.
I moved into the other lane so it could pass.
It moved over behind me.
I didn’t immediately think anything of it. People drive like idiots all
the time.
But after a few minutes, I started paying more attention.
If I sped up, it sped up.
If I slowed down, it slowed down.
At one point, I was going almost ten over the speed limit, and it was
still right behind me.
So I slowed way down.
It slowed down too.
That’s when I started getting nervous.
I called my husband back.
I told him there was a truck behind me that I thought might be following
me.
He asked how long it had been there, and I told him I wasn’t sure.
He told me not to panic and to stay on the highway.
Then he asked if I could see the driver.
I couldn’t. The headlights were too bright.
We were coming up on an exit, so I told him I was going to see if the
truck followed me.
I put my blinker on.
The truck put its blinker on.
I waited until we were almost at the exit, then turned my blinker off
and stayed on the highway.
The truck did the same thing.
I actually said, “Oh my God,” out loud.
My husband immediately told me to call 911.
I was crying by then, which made me feel ridiculous because technically
nothing had happened. Some guy was just driving behind me.
But I knew something wasn’t right.
I called 911 and explained what was going on.
The dispatcher asked where I was, what kind of vehicle it was, and
whether the driver had done anything threatening.
I told her about the gas station, the woman, and the truck following me.
She told me to stay on the highway and not try to lose him by speeding.
I stayed on the phone with her while she worked on getting an officer
nearby.
The truck followed me for another ten minutes or so.
At one point, it got so close that I couldn’t even see the headlights
anymore. I could just see the top of the hood in my rearview mirror.
I was terrified he was going to hit me.
Then he backed off again.
The dispatcher finally told me to take the next exit. She said an
officer was in the area.
I put my blinker on.
So did the truck.
I took the exit.
He followed.
At the bottom of the ramp was a red light, and of course I got stuck at
it.
There was a convenience store on one corner and a closed tire shop
across the street.
I stopped, and the truck pulled up right behind me.
I was looking in my rearview mirror when I saw his driver’s door open.
I swear I almost threw up.
I told the dispatcher he was getting out of his truck.
She told me to keep my doors locked and stay in the car.
A man stepped out.
He had on a baseball cap.
I couldn’t see his face very well, but he started walking toward my car.
I remember screaming, “He’s coming up here!”
Then I saw blue lights.
A police SUV came around the corner, and the man immediately turned
around and went back to his truck.
I have never been so relieved to see a cop in my entire life.
The officer pulled in behind the truck, and another patrol car showed up
a minute later.
They had me pull into the convenience store parking lot while they
talked to the driver.
I was shaking so badly I could barely hold my phone.
One of the officers came over and asked me what happened.
I told him everything.
He asked about the woman at the gas station, so I described her.
When I mentioned the gray sweatshirt, he asked if she’d been carrying a
pink purse.
I said yes.
I hadn’t even thought to mention the purse.
He wrote something down and told me to wait there.
They talked to the man for quite a while. I could see them looking
through his truck, but I couldn’t hear what they were saying.
Eventually, the officer came back.
He told me the driver claimed he’d been trying to get my attention
because something was wrong with my car.
I asked what was wrong with it.
He said they hadn’t found anything.
I asked if it was the same man I’d seen inside the gas station.
He said he couldn’t confirm that.
Then I asked about the woman.
He told me they’d had a few complaints about a woman approaching drivers
at gas stations along that stretch of highway.
Apparently, she would ask for rides or say she needed to borrow a phone.
He didn’t go into much detail, and I was too upset to think of all the
questions I should have asked.
They let the man leave.
I remember being really upset about that.
The officer explained that following someone wasn’t necessarily a crime,
and they didn’t have enough to arrest him for anything.
He told me to go straight home and call if I noticed the truck again.
My husband stayed on the phone with me the entire way.
I didn’t sleep much that night.
For the next couple of weeks, I kept thinking about it. I started
wondering if I’d overreacted.
Maybe the truck driver really had noticed something wrong with my car.
Maybe the woman had just been pointing me out because I’d been nice to
her.
Maybe it was all a coincidence.
I wanted to believe that because the alternative was pretty terrifying.
Then, about three weeks later, I got a phone call from the officer who’d
taken my statement.
He asked if I remembered the gas station incident.
Obviously, I did.
He said they were looking into some similar complaints and wanted to
know if I’d be willing to come in and look at a few pictures.
I went the next afternoon.
He showed me several photographs of women.
I recognized her immediately.
Same face. Same hair. I was positive.
Then he showed me some pictures of men.
One of them looked exactly like the guy I’d seen through the gas station
window.
I told him that was the man.
He asked how sure I was.
I said I was pretty sure, but I hadn’t gotten a great look at his face
that night.
He thanked me and said they were investigating several incidents that
might be connected.
I asked what had happened to the other women.
He told me he couldn’t discuss an ongoing investigation.
I asked if anyone had been hurt.
He wouldn’t answer that either.
I left feeling worse than when I’d arrived.
I never heard anything else about it.
I tried looking up news stories for a while, but I never found anything
that sounded like what happened to me.
I still don’t know if the woman and the man were working together. I
don’t know what the guy in the truck wanted. And I don’t know whether
the police ever figured any of it out.
But there’s one part of that night I think about more than anything
else.
When the woman asked for a ride, she was so normal.
She wasn’t pushy. She wasn’t acting nervous. She didn’t seem dangerous.
She was friendly, made conversation, and even agreed with me that
picking up strangers was a bad idea.
And when she asked about the booster seat, I thought she was just making
small talk.
I told her it belonged to my nephew.
I told her how old he was.
I even told her he’d been with me earlier that weekend.
Just normal conversation.
But I remember exactly what she said before she walked away.
“Well, at least you’re not traveling with him tonight.”
At the time, I didn’t think anything of it.
Now I really wish I knew why she said that.
