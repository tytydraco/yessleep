A few months ago, I met an amazing couple at a party. Julie and Robert
were the funniest people I had ever met. She was a short brunette with
brown eyes, and he was a tall, blue-eyed blond. I started seeing them
regularly at bars every weekend.

One evening, they told me they were going on vacation and wanted to ask
if I could look after their house. They had plants and fish, and they
didn’t want to leave them in the care of strangers. I was glad that even
though we hadn’t known each other for very long, they trusted me enough
to ask me to do something like that.

However, they told me their house had just one rule. I wasn’t allowed to
go upstairs. Other than that, I could do whatever I wanted.

When the day came and I met them to pick up the keys, I was shocked. It
wasn’t a house. It was a villa. It had a heated swimming pool, a hot
tub, a sauna, and pretty much every luxury you could think of. I
immediately thought that I wouldn’t be leaving the place for the entire
two weeks they would be away.

Just two days later, I decided to throw a party. I invited a few friends
I’d known since school. Chris, his girlfriend Emily, Paige, and Jenna.

We were already pretty drunk when I remembered the rule Julie and Robert
had given me. Chris started laughing and suggested we explore upstairs.
The girls were all for it, and honestly, so was I. We just had to make
sure we didn’t break anything or leave any sign that we’d been there.

“Sure, don’t worry,” Chris said. “I mean, if they have all this stuff
downstairs, imagine what they might have up there.”

As soon as I stepped onto the first stair, I got a strange feeling. I
can’t explain it any better than an overwhelming urge to turn around and
run.

“What’s wrong?” Paige asked with a laugh.

I didn’t want to look like an idiot, so I just smiled, told her nothing
was wrong, and kept going.

We entered a hallway. It was dark, and the floor was made of wood, but
not the way you’d imagine. It was just a bunch of wooden planks. I could
smell mold, and the whole place was dimly lit.

“Is there a light switch somewhere?” Emily asked.

“I honestly have no idea,” I replied.

We took out our phones and used their flashlights, but there wasn’t a
single outlet in sight. We walked down the hallway and turned right.

When I shone my flashlight at the floor, I noticed the wood wasn’t light
anymore. It looked wet, and there was some kind of dark, sticky liquid
spread across it.

A large painting hung on the wall. It showed Robert and Julie. But the
painting looked old. Really old.

We all stared at it.

“Hey,” Chris said, “maybe they were just messing with you and set all
this up as a surprise.”

“Yeah,” Paige said. “That makes sense.” Then she burst out laughing.

Suddenly, I had the feeling something had moved behind me. I spun around
and caught a glimpse of a shadow disappearing around the corner of the
hallway.

“Come on, let’s forget this and go downstairs,” I told them.

“Fine,” Emily agreed. “But I’d at least like to see what’s behind those
doors.”

She pointed at a door. It was completely black.

I reached for the handle but immediately pulled my hand away. When I
shone my flashlight on it, I noticed something on my hand that looked
like blood.

Chris started laughing even harder.

“Do they seriously think this is going to scare us?”

I wasn’t laughing. I grabbed the handle again and opened the door.

I stepped inside and felt my shoes sticking to the floor. The entire
room was lit up. Candles were burning everywhere. There were wardrobes,
a bed, and photographs scattered around.

Pictures of all kinds of people. Among them, I found photographs of us.

“Hey,” Paige said, “this isn’t funny anymore. Let’s get out of here.”

Even Chris, who had been laughing just moments earlier, agreed.

Suddenly, the wardrobe doors opened, and two figures stepped out. At
first, I had no idea who I was looking at. If they hadn’t spoken, I
might not have recognized them at all.

“Thank you, Trevor. We knew you wouldn’t let us down.”

Julie and Robert.

I couldn’t move. Both of them had gray, wrinkled faces, and the skin was
hanging from their cheeks. Yet they were wearing the same clothes I’d
last seen them in when they handed me the keys.

They were both twisted and hunched over. Gray hair hung down over their
faces.

“This isn’t possible,” I whispered.

“Let’s get the hell out of here!” Chris shouted.

But the door slammed shut and wouldn’t open.

“Why do they always try to run away?” Julie said with amusement.

Chris didn’t hesitate. He kicked the door open. The moment it swung
open, all the candles went out. The only lights left were our phone
flashlights.

We ran toward the stairs, but instead of reaching them, it felt like we
were going around in circles.

Then we started hearing banging noises. Like someone was hitting wood.

“Where’s Paige?” Emily screamed.

We looked around, but she was nowhere to be seen.

Then we heard her scream again. This time, it sounded much farther away.

Chris ran in the direction the scream had come from. Suddenly, he
tripped and fell to the floor. Before we could help him, he started
sliding backward, as if someone had grabbed him by the ankles.

“Fuck! Help me!” he screamed.

Emily grabbed his hands while I tried to hold on to his jacket. But
something was pulling him with incredible force. His fingernails dug
into the wooden floor, leaving long scratches behind.

Then I realized where it was pulling him.

Toward the black door.

It opened on its own, and Chris started screaming even louder. For a
brief moment, I saw several long, pale fingers reaching out toward him
from the darkness.

“NO! LET GO OF ME!”

Emily was still holding his hands when something threw her backward.
Chris disappeared behind the door, and it slammed shut.

I grabbed Emily and Jenna by the hands and pulled them away, but nothing
changed. It was like we were walking in circles.

Suddenly, we heard Chris’s voice coming from behind the black door.

“Emily, come here! I need you to help me. I think my leg is broken.”

Emily pulled free before I could stop her and ran through the doorway.
The only things I heard were a short scream and a dull thud.

Jenna started crying. I promised her I’d get us out of there.

We kept walking and running, farther and farther. Around and around. The
same spot. The same painting.

I was desperate by then. In frustration, I ripped the painting off the
wall and threw it to the floor.

Jenna shoved me and pointed at the wall where the painting had been
hanging. There was a hole. It looked like a narrow passage leading to
another part of the house. We crawled through it and found ourselves in
front of the staircase.

We held hands and ran downstairs.

Suddenly, Jenna stopped. Robert was holding her other hand, snarling
angrily at us.

I tried to pull Jenna toward me, but Julie appeared on the other side
and dug her nails into the hand I was using to hold Jenna.

The pain was unbearable. I had to let go, and all I could do was watch
as Robert dragged her back.

Then Julie grabbed me and tried to pull me in the same direction Robert
had taken Jenna.

I should have tried to save Jenna. Instead, I slammed into Julie with
all my strength and ran down the stairs.

I ran outside and didn’t stop. I called the police, but they searched
the house and found nothing. There were no traces of my friends, no
photographs, and no evidence that Julie and Robert had ever existed.

My friends were never found either.

I thought it was finally over. For several weeks, I tried to get back to
my normal life, but I couldn’t stop thinking about what had happened to
Jenna and the others.

Then I started seeing them.

At first, I thought I was imagining things. One night, I walked past my
window and noticed two figures standing across the street. When I looked
closer, I recognized them.

It was Julie and Robert, looking exactly as they had in that house.

They stood there, staring directly at my window. Both of them were
smiling.

I turned on the outside lights and called the police. By the time they
arrived, the couple was gone.

But that wasn’t the end of it. I started seeing them regularly.
Sometimes they stood across the street, other times right outside the
entrance to my building. They never tried to get inside. They just stood
there, watching me.

And they were always smiling.

I haven’t slept properly in several nights. I’ve drawn all the curtains
and stopped looking out the windows.

But tonight, I heard knocking on my window.

The problem is, I live on the sixth floor.
