I use a 24-hour laundromat about four blocks from my apartment because
the machines in my building have been broken since July.

It’s an ugly place, but usually empty after midnight, which is why I go
so late. Twelve washers along one wall, twelve dryers along the other,
six plastic chairs, two vending machines, and a little wooden cabinet
near the entrance with LOST & FOUND written on masking tape.

There are no employees overnight. The owner watches the place through
cameras and comes in every morning.

Three weeks ago I lost a pen there.

Nothing important. Cheap black click pen with bite marks around the cap.
I remembered leaving it on top of washer 8, went back the next night,
and checked the cabinet.

My pen was inside.

What bothered me was that I hadn’t lost it yet.

I know how that sounds.

The pen I thought I’d lost was in the pocket of my work bag. I found it
while standing there with the identical pen from the cabinet in my hand.

Same brand. Same scratches. Same crescent-shaped bite mark.

I brought both home.

The next morning, one was gone.

I searched everywhere. Eventually I decided I’d somehow imagined having
two identical pens, which would have been a perfectly reasonable
explanation if the next thing hadn’t been my green sock.

I was doing laundry around 1 a.m. a few nights later when I opened the
cabinet looking for a dryer sheet someone might have abandoned.

There was one green sock inside.

Mine.

Not similar. Mine.

I’d bought six pairs online because I liked the color. One sock had a
small white thread hanging from the ankle where my cat caught it months
ago.

I checked my laundry basket.

Six pairs.

Twelve socks.

The one in my hand made thirteen.

I put it on the folding table and took a picture because I wanted proof
that I wasn’t losing my mind.

When the wash finished, I counted eleven green socks.

I checked inside the washer. Under it. Behind it. Inside every other
machine.

Eleven.

Plus the one from the cabinet made twelve.

The photograph on my phone still showed the extra sock sitting on the
table before I’d opened the washer.

After that I started checking the cabinet every time I went in.

For about a week there was nothing belonging to me.

Then I found my apartment key.

I was wearing my keys clipped to my belt loop.

The key in the cabinet had the same little notch near the tip from when
I dropped the ring into a parking lot last winter. The only difference
was that this one was bent almost ninety degrees.

I didn’t take it home.

I left the laundromat immediately.

When I got to my building, someone had jammed the front door open with a
rock. I remember standing outside for almost five minutes debating
whether that meant anything.

Eventually I told myself I was being stupid and went upstairs.

My apartment door looked normal.

I unlocked it, stepped inside, and heard something move in my bedroom.

Not a creak.

Footsteps.

Three quick steps across the floor, then silence.

I ran.

I didn’t even close the door. I went downstairs and called the police
from across the street.

They searched the apartment.

Nobody was there.

The bedroom window was locked from the inside. Nothing had been stolen.
There were no obvious signs anyone had entered.

But my key wouldn’t work afterward.

The officer tried it too.

When I looked closely, it was slightly bent.

Not ninety degrees like the one in the laundromat cabinet. Just enough
that it wouldn’t turn smoothly anymore.

I slept at my sister’s place and had the lock replaced the next day.

I should have found another laundromat after that.

Instead, I went back.

I wanted the bent key.

It was gone.

There was something else in the cabinet.

A red plastic button.

I didn’t recognize it at first.

Then I looked down at my coat.

It has four red buttons.

I counted them three times.

Four on the coat.

One in my hand.

I put the button back and left.

Two days later, while walking home from work, the bottom button came off
my coat. I heard it bounce on the sidewalk, but it rolled underneath a
parked car and I couldn’t find it.

I already knew where it was.

That was when I finally called the laundromat owner.

I didn’t tell him the whole story. I asked whether he’d ever had
problems with people putting strange things in the lost-and-found
cabinet.

He went quiet.

Then he asked which cabinet I meant.

I described it.

There was another long pause.

He told me they don’t have a lost-and-found cabinet.

According to him, they used to.

It had been beside the entrance for years, exactly where I described it.

But somebody tore it off the wall in February.

He said the screws had been ripped straight through the wood. They never
replaced it.

I asked him to check the cameras.

He refused.

Not casually. Immediately.

I pushed him until he finally said, “If you see that cabinet again,
don’t open it.”

Then he hung up.

I haven’t gone back since.

That should be the end of this.

It isn’t.

Last night I got home around 11:30. The new lock on my apartment was
fine. Hallway empty. Everything normal.

I went inside, fed my cat, showered, and went to bed.

At 2:14 a.m. I woke up because I heard wood scraping against the floor.

It was coming from my living room.

I locked my bedroom door and called 911.

While I was whispering my address to the dispatcher, the scraping
stopped.

Then something knocked three times on my bedroom door.

I didn’t answer.

It knocked three more times.

I stayed on the phone until police arrived.

They searched everything again.

Nobody.

No open windows. No broken lock. No hiding place.

One officer asked if anything had been moved.

I said no.

Then I saw the wall beside my front door.

The little wooden lost-and-found cabinet was mounted there.

Same masking tape.

Same handwriting.

The officers definitely saw it. One of them opened it before I could
stop him.

There was only one thing inside.

My bedroom doorknob.

Not one that looked like mine.

Mine.

Same cheap brass finish. Same scratch underneath from when I moved a
dresser through the doorway last year.

I ran to the bedroom.

The doorknob was still attached.

I didn’t touch the one in the cabinet.

The police couldn’t explain it. They took the cabinet down because I
refused to stay in the apartment with it there. One officer carried it
downstairs and put it in their trunk.

I left with my cat and went to my sister’s again.

About an hour ago, she went to bed.

I’m sitting at her kitchen table right now.

There’s a little wooden cabinet mounted on the wall across from me.

It wasn’t there when we arrived.

I haven’t opened it.

But a few minutes ago something inside started turning very slowly, like
a doorknob being tested from the other side.
