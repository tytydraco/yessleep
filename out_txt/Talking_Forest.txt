This black box was recovered in 1964
All the fucked-up shit they tell you about the talking forest are true.
The traps. The flesh they carve up and stick on poles.
The people who disappear into holes in the ground, never to be seen
again.
You wonder why they do it? They feed the Gods.
They live in that jungle, for fuck’s sake. Trust me.
I’ve seen them.
Hell, they ripped Selva right out of the sky.
We didn’t even know what we had brought upon the world.
It was supposed to be a routine mission. Get in.
Plant the charges. Get out.

Around when I was 20 years old, I had a serious girlfriend, who was also
my high school sweetheart. She didn’t want me to go to war. She told me
that if I left, she wouldn’t be waiting when I came home. I thought she
was bluffing. When I told her my decision, the next day she was gone.

Pissed and confused I longed to be anywhere else. I walked into my local
recruitment office, making its home across the diner my mother worked
at. The silver-tongued man warmly greeted me as he began sitting behind
a desk covered in gleaming medals. He asked me what I wanted to do.
“we’re looking for people, People who want to make a difference in the
world.”
I told him I wanted to do something dangerous, something that mattered.
He told me about explosives and demolition.
“Just the job for someone like you, I see a real future here in the
Army.”
He said that and with enough charisma, I signed the papers that same
day. By the time I finished my training, they sent me to lush green
jungles of Vietnam. I had convinced myself I was doing the right thing.

We flew in just before sunrise, the heavy gloom lingered on the lush
foliage peaking just above the fog. stretching beneath us like an
endless sea of green. Downs had the fifty set on the horizon, cigarette
in his mouth and a wicked grin on his paint covered face. The pilot
dropped us at the edge of a river, the village marked for demolition
just a click away. We’re supposed to plant the charges, clear the area,
radio for extraction. We’d done it hundreds of times, trained,
proficient killers.

The pilot kept the rotors turning while we unloaded. Even over the
engine noise, I noticed the crew chief staring toward the tree line, one
hand pressed to his headset. He gave us a quirky salute before he took
the bird, I never really liked that guy.

I remember thinking we’d be back in the air within a few hours. The
radio operator had already marked our extraction window, though static
kept swallowing the confirmation. Captain told us not to worry; the
weather was closing in, but the pilot knew the evacuation route they had
set.

We dug makeshift fighting positions and settled into security. The
jungle seemed to quiet around us, and somewhere beyond the village, a
rotor thudded once through the mist before disappearing out of earshot,
we all was hoping our targets didn’t hear anything.
Once we were set, Captain ordered Downs and Selva to prepare for the
mission.

We were part of a special explosives unit. Our job was to plant charges
in areas scheduled to be searched the following day.

Captain decided the new grunt was finally ready to place the explosives
himself. He sent Downs with him. They were almost inseparable.

Downs had been with me since basic. He was the kind of man who could
make a joke while bleeding and still remember to check whether everyone
else had ammunition. Selva was the same way, only louder. The two of
them had been inseparable since we landed in-country. They called each
other brothers. They fought like brothers, too.

Downs carried the charges. Selva carried the detonator case. Every few
minutes, one of them would turn around just to make sure the other was
still there.

Forty minutes later, they came back.

Forty minutes for what should’ve been an easy fucking job.

They looked like ghosts
I asked them what took them so long.
They tried to assure me everything was Charlie Mike.

Downs just looked at me and said:

“We need to get the hell out of here, something isn’t right about this.”

Selva elbowed his ribs.

“Tell him what we saw.” Rattling a cigarette out of his Ifac.

Downs stared at the ground in shock.

“There’s these- Things. I don’t know what they are but they aren’t
people. I know what people sound like and that’s not it. We need to get
the fuck out of here, we got here as fast as we could but we have to
move, now!”

The village had been marked as occupied. We had been told there were
civilians, weapons, and enemy movement in the area. But when they got to
the village there was nothing. Just open huts, overturned baskets, and
footprints that stopped in the mud, as if everyone had left everything
behind.

Then Downs found the target marked for demolition, he said it wasn’t
hard to miss. He also said it didn’t look like any of the targets we’ve
hit before.

It was beneath the largest hut, covered with woven mats and packed
earth. Dolls made of sticks and leaves scattered around the interior. A
lone altar in the farthest part of the room beyond the pit made of
pinkish flesh oozing purplish puss from the roots of thousands of tiny
eyes. The charges had been placed around it because command had told us
to destroy the village’s underground storage area. This was no storage
area. It was a shaft. A throat. A portal

They could barely see down far enough to make out the bottom, the fluid
rising from the depths.

That was when they found the villagers. All of them. Thrown into the
hole. Men, women, children. Bodies piled on bodies, their throats cut
and their blood running through symbols carved into the stones
surrounding the outer layer of Satans mouth, all leading toward a black
opening beneath the dead.

The charges were already in place by then. They didn’t know what they
were looking at. They only knew they had orders.

They headed back to the water which they had arrived from and as they
were making their way back, Selva radioed back to base:

ARMED.

ALL CLEAR.

Then we detonated the explosives from the fob

The noise could’ve woken the devil himself, and on that day, I’m afraid
we found out what happens if he does.

The entire ground began to crack open. Like something underneath the
Earth had been waiting for the signal. The village split apart. The hole
widened, and the blood beneath it began to glow. A red light shone
through the fractures in the ground.
Covering the sky in almost a rainbow of blood.
Like someone flipped a switch to the sky.
Then came the screaming.
It was coming from beneath the earth. The smell of rot and filth filled
the air.
The dead began to move. Not on their own.
Hands clawed through the blood. Black fingers gripped the edges of the
pit. Something below started pulling itself upward, dragging the bodies
down as it climbed.

Downs grabbed Selva by the vest.

“MOVE!”

Selva didn’t move.
He was staring back at the village we had just demolished.

“What the fuck is that?”

The ground erupted into a violent fire and molten heat.

Black shapes poured out of the village, crawling over the ruins,
dragging themselves through the mud, blood and flames. Gnashing their
contorted teeth, feeding on their bountiful feast of gore. They had
wings folded against their backs. Their eyes burned red in the smoke.
Snouts of bones and sinew

Two at first. Misshapen needle like hands with worms protruding out of
their pale, flesh like arms
Both men aimed their service weapons at them.
Then four. Like cockroaches they swarm the village not yet sensing their
presence.
Then ten.
They lowered them back down knowing in their minds, they knew that they
fired a single bullet, they would be the forests children’s next meal.
Then by the thousands.
Downs kept hold of Selva’s arm, dragging him back. Not even when the
things began chasing them through the water.

They reached the clearing and saw the chopper. Squad mates unknowing the
horrors that they had just exposed.

The flight mechanic was waving us toward the cabin, already preparing
for evacuation. Saluting us once more from his blackhawk.

Captain was screaming into the radio, recalling what has happened to
main command.

“The sky! the sky is fucking red, it’s splitting, Jesus! I think they
just launched missiles, code black!”

By the time Downs and Selva had gotten back to the FOB, the Hellspawn
was right behind them.

Selva, Downs, the mechanic, the pilot, and the captain—we all realized
at the same time that we had no idea what the fuck we had just done, or
what was happening.

We scrambled aboard. Downs pulled Selva in behind him. I slammed the
hatch shut. The pilot pushed our only hope of safety into the air.

Captain grabbed the radio.

“COMMAND, THIS IS LIMA TANGO! WE HAVE AN UNKNOWN BREACH! REQUESTING
IMMEDIATE BACKUP! REPEAT, WE ARE BEING OVERRUN BY UNKNOWN HOSTILES!”

The jungle answered with a sound like metal tearing apart.
Not from us but from where we had just lifted off.
Something struck the underside of the chopper.

The radio calls back,

One. Tree. Fiver. Six. Fiver. Niner. Fiver. could you resend your
traffic.

Captain starts screaming again
“COMMAND! WE ARE BEING BREACHED BY AN UNKNOWN—”

“FUCK! SELVA!”

The captain steady and aimed his rifle at the beady eyes clutching
Selvas torso
He tried to fire but his gun misfired a round and it jammed not even
releasing a round.
Quickly thinking the captain tried to grabbed him before he went through
the open side door.

Downs grasp for Selva, only landing a firm grip on his out layer of
clothes on his sleeve. tattered and bloody where the claws tore into his
chest.

For one second, all three of them were connected. The band of brothers,
unknowing releasing the hidden horrors in the hills
Then the rotor caught fire.
The chopper swayed violently in the air above the jungle.
Captain kept shouting into the radio.
The pilot fought the controls.
The mechanic screamed that the engine was gone. Selva looked at me.
Tears in his eye, I never seen that man cry once in his life until now
Downs was still holding his arm.

Then something black and winged slammed into the cabin.
Selva was torn straight out of the aircraft by whatever the fuck that
thing was.
Downs reached for him.

“SELVA!”

The sudden shift in weight sent us plummeting, black eyes and warm, foul
bodies swarming around us.

“FUCK! SELVA JUST GOT RIPPED OUT—”

The chopper went nose-down into the green canopy—the largest grave that
ever existed.

We were never supposed to discover what was in that jungle.

We weren’t supposed to disturb it.

But we did.

Now I don’t know what the fuck we’ve unleashed upon the world.
I’m the only person remaining. The wreckage is on fire around me, the
twisted metal having been scattered among the trees and the smoke
filling the air. I tried to look for the others, but there’s nothing
left to find. Then I heard it—singing, coming from somewhere past the
wreckage. It was human voices, almost. Slow, broken melodies drifting
between the trees, rising and falling as if they were an old song that I
can’t remember. Then a second voice joined in. Then another. They’re
singing about us. I can hear Selva’s voice in it. I can hear Downs too.
God help me, they’re singing their names. We had thought that we were
killing the enemy and that we were supposed to be heroes. We didn’t know
what was waiting for us. If anyone finds this recording, this is my
final warning: don’t come near this place. Don’t send anyone after us.
The singing is getting closer and they’ve started singing my name. God
help me, God help all of us.
