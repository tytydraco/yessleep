My boss is psychotic.

No, it’s not an exaggeration. I know it sounds dramatic. But trust me,
this isn’t a petty “my boss is an asshole” rant that you hear from white
collar dudes when their manager gets pissy with them for taking too many
breaks. I’ve been there too, but this isn’t that. 

He’s a freak. 

Bat-shit insane.

And frankly, I’m terrified. 

I’m scared of what he’s done. What he will do. To me. To my coworkers.

To be fair, this is a new development. He wasn’t always like this. Sure
he was a terrible boss, but a monster? Not in the slightest.

I’ll stop being cryptic. Let’s start from the beginning and you can see
for yourself.

My first day at ClientTale was a big achievement for me. I had just
finished my undergrad in Computer Science and was looking to make a
splash in big tech.

Unfortunately, it was less of a splash and more of a stumble.

The market was fairly competitive and I couldn’t seem to get any bites
no matter how much I put myself out there. My buddies were landing gigs
all over the West Coast and I felt like my time was running out.
Hundreds of applications and cold emails later, I managed to book some
startup interviews. That’s when ClientTale came on my radar. 

It seemed legit. The website claimed they were “rethinking the way we
work and play.” Even now, I don’t know what that means. I couldn’t care
less about their hand-wavy culture bullshit. What really sold me was the
fact that they were a few series deep in multi-million dollar funding.
Tech companies love to spew their pseudo-philosophical narrative but
it’s all for show. In the end it’s the money that talks.

I did some digging and managed to snag the phone number of their
recruiter. After some phone tag, a screening call, and a few grueling
tech interviews, an offer letter blipped into my starving inbox. I was
elated.

Now, ClientTale isn’t a traditional tech company. If you are picturing a
bunch of cringey “day in the life of an engineer” videos you would be
wrong. Like most startups, it is small, stressful, and demanding. Now, I
am not complaining. This is exactly what I signed up for.

I got started with a small group of guys developing the platform from
the ground up. The other engineers made it sound very Wild West. No
limits. Technical freedom. A nerd’s dream.

Oh how wrong I was.

My perception of the job shattered when I met our boss, Meddler.

Meddler isn’t his real name. His last name is Manning. That’s just what
the engineering team calls him behind his back. He earned the moniker by
constantly “meddling” in our work. Whenever Meddler stuck his
thirty-something, platinum-haired, expensive-suit-wearing nose where it
didn’t belong, productivity waned.

Meddler is—or was—your average bad boss. He comes in late, leaves early,
and raises hell whenever anything inconvenient happens on the job. The
irony is very telling. I meet guys like this all the time, business bros
whose quest for recognition stems from their deep-seated insecurity. The
more his team gets done without him, the more bruised his ego, the more
everyone else needs to pay.

One time our production service was down and Meddler decided it was best
to race through the men’s restroom and bang on closed stalls to find the
on-call engineer, Geoff. When Geoff was located, Meddler asked if he was
willing “pinch it off” to save his job. Geoff obliged. The issue was not
urgent, but Meddler made it so.

Another time our QA engineer, Henry, had a medical emergency and had to
leave early. Meddler called him later that day to ask how things were.
It sounds like a sweet gesture.

It wasn’t.

One of the nurses let Henry know that Meddler showed up to the hospital
later that night. He posed as an uncle and quizzed the nurses on Henry’s
family emergency just to make sure the story lined up. Henry thought he
was trying to be sneaky but I knew better. Meddler wanted Henry to know
he was there.

Now, how does someone like Meddler end up working as manager at a snazzy
new startup? The answer is simple.

Nepotism.

Meddler’s dad is a big name in Silicon Valley and has a substantial
stake in ClientTale. Meddler had just finished his business degree at
Yale and daddy decided to pull some strings. Now we’re stuck with him.

I’m sure by now you’re feeling a bit underwhelmed by Meddler. A
micro-managing nepo baby doesn’t sound like the devil, after all. 

You’re right.

If you recall my warning, something changed in Meddler. A month ago,
Meddler got into a bad accident. He took daddy’s Porsche out for a
joyride after one-too-many drinks and ended up wrapping it around a
tree. By some miracle, he ended up being tossed into a ditch by the
road, suffering minor enough injuries that only warranted a short
hospital stay. We joked that even death didn’t want Meddler.

Terrible, I know.

When Meddler recovered and rejoined us back at the office, he wasn’t
himself. His loud and aggressive demeanor was replaced with something
sullen and reserved. The fire in his eyes was out. He gained weight, let
brown roots take hold under his platinum receding hair, and wore the
same dirty, ill-fitting suit most days. He spent his first few weeks
back nailed to his chair in his office with the lights off. We’d pop in
and out for brief asks. A one word answer or casual nod was all her
offered back. With Meddler no longer meddling, we got back to work. 

It was the most productive we’ve ever been.

Unfortunately the peace didn’t last. Eventually Meddler started making
his rounds again. At first, it was subtle. He’d scuttle around and stare
at our monitors with a furrowed brow. He’d ask short and pointed
questions about our tasks before moving on. 

“Seems like Meddler is back from the dead huh?” I said to Geoff.

Geoff chuckled. 

We were pairing on a new feature that was due at the end of the quarter.
This was something I owned and was praying it could prop me up for a
promotion. As long as our fearless leader didn’t get in the way, of
course.

“Speak of the devil.” Geoff said before retreating back to his desk. 

The thud of Meddler’s footsteps fell short just a few feet behind me. He
said nothing, so I kept working, hoping the clack of my obnoxious
keyboard would drown out the awkward silence.

It didn’t.

A few minutes passed and I started to think he had stepped away until
the sound of his voice broke right behind me.

“What you working on?” He asked plainly.

“Oh, hey boss—this is that migration I was telling you about.” I
stammered.

He pressed for more details. I explained that I decided to refactor a
lot of the legacy surfaces to a new design. Modern web framework, Rust
migration, blah blah. I was waiting for his eyes to gloss over but he
remained engaged. 

It was very uncomfortable. Although he hung onto every word, he didn’t
seem interested. It was like he was starved for some response I wasn’t
giving. His intense expression gave me the impression he was going to
beat me if I didn’t offer it soon.

“So yeah, that’s what I’ve got going on. Planning to deploy end of the
quarter if all goes well.” I said, trying to break off the conversation.

His face softened. Meddler rubbed his chin and appeared deep in thought
for a moment.

“Thank you. This is good stuff.” He mumbled. “You’re very efficient,
Alex. I’m going to think about this a bit more and get back to you with
more questions.” He said before marching off.

Geoff returned a couple minutes later with a cheeky grin.

“What was that?” He whispered.

“I’m not sure man. He kept asking about my feature. It was weird. He
looked super intense.” I said quietly.

“He looked like he wanted to screw you dude.” He laughed.

The moment stayed with me for a while. At first my impression was
optimistic. Maybe the crash knocked some much needed sense into him.
Maybe Meddler wrestled with God and realized he needed a different
approach to life.

Or maybe he suffered severe brain trauma and simply lacks the capacity
to meddle anymore.

A few days later, I was doing some work when Geoff came up to my desk.

“What did you do man?” He said quietly. I set my chunky headphones down
and shot him a puzzled look. He was pecking at his lunch and eyeing
Meddler’s office very subtly.

“Oh my bad—Meddler’s been staring at you all day. Thought you might have
got on his bad side.” He said.

I turned to look but he put one hand on my shoulder to stop me.

“Nah man don’t look—he’ll know I said shit.” He paused a few moments
before glancing back in Meddler’s direction.

“Yeah I don’t know man he won’t stop.” He said, his voice quieter this
time.

“Quit messing around you’re creeping me out. It’s not funny.”

“My bad. Just looking out for you bro.” Geoff said before throwing his
hands up in mock offense and retreating back to his desk.

I spent the rest of the day feeling uneasy. Meddler didn’t leave his
office once. I never looked back, but I could feel his beady eyes on the
back of my head. That lifeless stare with the intensity of a dead man.
The image alone made me queasy.

It got me thinking about the last few days. Now that Geoff mentioned it,
I don’t remember him doing his rounds since our last conversation. Has
he just been watching me this whole time? 

The anxiety was short lived. Meddler resumed his strolls the next day. I
couldn’t help but wonder if it’s because he noticed me catching on or if
the timing was just a coincidence. 

I chose to believe the latter. 

Things were relatively normal for a time, but as usual, the peace was
short lived. 

One morning I arrived for work and was surprised to be greeted with an
empty parking lot. I chalked it up to traffic. Most of my coworkers
lived on the other side of the busy highway and I took streets that day.
Surely an accident held up the rest of the team.

When I entered the building, the liminal state of the place irked me. My
footsteps resounded off the empty walls much louder than they should. I
winced at every step. It wasn’t until the carpet dampened the sound that
I felt less exposed. 

I turned the corner to our front desk and that’s when I realized nobody
was there. Not a single soul. I stood at the welcome desk and peered
around. All the lights were on but nobody was in sight. 

Something felt wrong. Rather than taking a seat and waiting out the
silence, I called Geoff.

“Hey—uh when are you getting to work?”

“What’s that?” He said hazily, as if he had just woken up.

“I don’t know man it’s creepy. Nobody is here yet I feel like I’m
working night shift.”

Silence on the other end.

“Geoff?”

“Wait you didn’t get the memo? We’re off today bud. Meddler’s orders.”
He said.

“What? I didn’t get any memo. I was here Friday too.”

The sound of shuffling crackled on the phone. Geoff’s voice came in
clearly a few moments later.

“He called and emailed us on Saturday to let us know they were doing
pest control or something. Work-from-home day.” 

Silence.

I put Geoff on speaker and whipped through my missed calls.

No Meddler.

No email.

“Well, best you get your ass home dude. Don’t want to get blasted with
nasty pesticides—“

Boom.

A loud banging emanated from the break room. The florescent lights
overhead cast worrisome shadows around the area. Was someone there?

“Hello?” I called out.

I shuffled sideways trying to get a better look, my eyes fixed on the
empty break room across the office. I didn’t dare move forward until I
could be sure there was no danger. I jumped when I heard the thump of my
shoulder against drywall. 

I turned and realized I was budding against Meddler’s office. 

The door was open.

I peeked in, mind racing, half expecting to see the deranged man sitting
in his chair, sunken black eyes peering up at me.

Instead, I was met again with an empty room.

I was about to return my attention to the sound in the break room when
something caught my eye.

A name plate sat on Meddler’s desk, facing me. A single word etched into
the gold-plated paperweight:

“MEDDLER.” 

The air left my lungs as I read it. How long has that been there? Surely
Meddler was messing with us. He must have overheard his new title and
decided to make a dry joke of it. Yeah, that was definitely it.

Just a dumb joke.

I tried my best to convince myself this was lighthearted in nature but I
couldn’t shake the feeling that this was more sinister. All of Meddler’s
neurotic behavior towards me made me feel like this was targeted. It
sounds crazy, but deep down I think Meddler wanted me right here, right
now, looking at this name plate.

He wanted me to know he was angry.

Boom. 

Another loud banging noise erupted from a different room. I had to get
out of here. 

I tore out of Meddler’s office and ran down the stairs. A few missed
steps nearly set me tumbling but I managed to stay composed. I kept
glancing behind me expecting to see someone in pursuit but nothing came.
I didn’t stop looking back until I was deep into the parking lot. As I
put my car into gear, I noticed something I missed when I first pulled
in.

On the street beside the empty parking lot sat a single car.

A black sports car.

The sight of it was obscured by a dumpster and an array of bushes seated
along the sidewalk. If you’re not paying attention, it’s easy to miss. 

I swear I’ve seen Meddler’s face pull up in that car more than once. I
remember thinking how dumb and impractical it looked. An expensive and
useless vehicle for an expensive and useless employee.

I hid away in my apartment confused by the whole ordeal. I didn’t
understand why Meddler would choose to spook me like that. Even if he
intended for me to see the name plate, he had no guarantee I’d end up in
his office. None of it made sense.

Again, I decided to go against my judgement. After all, the sounds I
heard could’ve easily been exterminators setting up for the day. The
nameplate could’ve simply been the remnants of an uncomfortable joke
that Meddler forgot to carry out before his accident.

After much deliberation and very little sleep, I decided to go back to
work. 

But not without precautions, of course.

When I arrived to that dreaded parking lot the next day, I waited
anxiously, peering through the frosted windshield for any signs of life.
It wasn’t until every member of my team parked and slipped behind the
dark lobby doors that I took the first shaky step out of my car. 

After a slow ascent to my floor, Meddler walked up to me as I approached
the front desk.

“Heard you showed up yesterday. Sorry about that. I swear I had emailed
you on Friday.” He said plainly. His eyes still had that post-accident
intensity. His hair was extra messy today and half his stained dress
shirt hung out the side of his pants.

“Oh, no problem—I wasn’t here long.” I stammered. It was hard looking
him in his eyes so I kept my gaze to the floor. I couldn’t help but
notice his shoes were unusually dirty and chipped near the soles. It was
the kind of wear you’d only see on someone who was on their feet all
day. All night too, possibly. Not the kind of wear you expect to see on
luxury shoes.

He grinned and patted my shoulder before marching back to his office.
Geoff was observing from a safe distance. He popped in once a
comfortable amount of time passed.

“Freaked me out yesterday bud.” He whispered as he settled into the
spare seat beside my desk. 

“Pretty freaked out myself.” I responded, glancing nervously towards
Meddler’s dark office. “Did you see the name plate?”

Geoff finished correcting some minor mistakes on-screen before
responding. “Yeah I saw it when I came in. Don’t know what that’s about.
He probably had this grand scheme in mind before he tried to kick the
bucket.”

I froze, fingers hovering above the keyboard.

“I thought he wrecked his car?” I said plainly.

“Oh yeah I heard that rumor too. Doesn’t make sense though. He’d be in
jail for sure.” He said quietly. “Henry said it was pills. Jim from
security told me it was a loan shark. Some mafia-esque dude rapped on
his door and busted his legs with a pipe. Hari said someone found him
out in the woods. Claims Meddler just left his apartment one night and
was found a week later wandering the mountains like a man possessed.
Guess we’ll never really know.”

This news made me feel uneasy. And a bit dumb. Of course Meddler didn’t
wrap his car around a tree in a drunken bender. Sure, Meddler’s rich dad
could’ve pulled some strings, but I imagine even he has limits.
Something else happened to Meddler, and whatever it is remains a
mystery.

Geoff made a “aha” sound out of nowhere which made me jump a little.

“If Meddler knows about his nickname it’s probably from the work chats.
Must’ve greased up IT to hand over the chat logs or something.” Geoff
said.

“Shit.” I whispered.

“Makes sense why he’s singling you out then. You did not hold back.” He
chuckled.

Those damn chats. When I first came onboard I wanted to fit in with the
group. Meddler was a terror that day, pulling people one-by-one into his
office and accusing them of negligence for something he surely did. The
team started ripping on him in the chat between scoldings. I joined in
and kept things mild but eventually I got brave and took things a little
too far. I don’t remember all of what I said, but I do remember making a
comment about how Meddler gets off to self-help books and that even
daddy’s money couldn’t buy him a personality.

“Don’t worry about it man. If he wanted to do something he would’ve done
it by now. Just let him stew in it.” Geoff said patting my back.

But he has tried something. Whatever happened yesterday wasn’t a
coincidence.

A rational Meddler would’ve made an example of me. Nothing harmful, of
course, but I wouldn’t be let off without a violent talking-to and a
shallow threat to my employment if I didn’t apologize profusely. 

A rational Meddler wouldn’t stage an office closure to lure me in and
do—well, whatever he planned to do.

Of course, nothing of substance has happened. Yet.

Geoff and I sat taking turns working at my keyboard under the dim office
lighting for the rest of the day. He kept assuring me I was overthinking
it. I wanted to believe him, but something just didn’t sit right with
me.

Meddler remained in his office for the rest of the day. I kept glancing
back at his dark hideaway. The lights from various computer monitors
reflected off the dark glass, casting ghostly shapes that flickered and
moved. Meddler could be seen faintly hunched over at his desk, his own
monitor painting blue light on his sunken face.

When I got home, I couldn’t sleep. 

I pulled out my work laptop and combed over the old messages over and
over at the edge of my bed. I tried to convince myself what I said
wasn’t too bad, but the more I read, the more anxious I got. A hard lump
formed in my stomach, each message adding to the weight. I pictured a
haggard Meddler, sweaty and focused on his own computer. His beady, dark
eyes whipping left and right as they soaked in my words. Anger and
violence bubbling in his insides while remorse ate at mine. 

I turned off the machine and caught a glimpse of myself in the dark
reflection. A messy-haired man with a patchy beard and dark bags under
his eyes stared back.

I really needed to get some rest. But I didn’t think I could face
Meddler the next day. Hell, I couldn’t even bring myself to call him and
tell him I’ll be out sick.

I took the easy way out and decided to let Geoff handle it. He doesn’t
see Meddler the same way I do, so surely he wouldn’t mind.

It was late, so I decided to call and leave him a voicemail. 

Buzz…buzz…

What the hell?

The vibration of a phone could be heard above. I looked up, ringing
phone in hand, trying to identify the source.

Blue rays of moonlight, split into frayed rectangles by cheap blinds,
illuminated the room weakly. I directed the light of my phone overhead.
Nothing but cold white ceiling overhead and an idle fan. 

And yet, the buzzing remained. I killed the call and the buzzing died
with it.

Something wasn’t right. 

“Geoff?” I called weakly. A faint thumping overhead replaced the
buzzing.

I broke out in cold sweat. Terrifying possibilities rapped at my brain
with each new thump overhead.

A pained moan interrupted the melodic tapping.

Shit. If Geoff was in trouble, I needed to find help. But how was he
here? And why?

I didn’t have time to think.

My fingers shakily dialed 911 as I slipped out my front door into the
florescent light of my apartment hallway. 

I made my way into the elevator and keyed the floor above me while
trying to explain to the operator what was going on.

“Yes my friend is hurt—I don’t know the number. It’s the apartment above
mine. I’m going to check right now…I’m not sure what happened just
hurry—“

The doors dinged open and I rounded the corner, making my way to the
dreaded room. I took note of the number and passed it along to the
operator on the phone. I pressed my ear to the door, hoping to hear some
signs of life. 

Nothing.

The rhythmic thumping and pained sounds of Geoff were gone.

For a moment, I panicked. Did I just make this all up?

I worried that, in my sleep deprived state, I may have just hallucinated
this nightmare. A fear induced psychosis initiated by my meddling boss
could have overtaken me. A story that would surely fall on deaf ears
when the cops arrive shortly.

For a moment, I remained pressed to the door, my left arm holding my
phone limp at my side. The distant voice of the operator desperately
trying to reach me in my absence could be heard faintly.

Click.

I lost my balance briefly as the door gave and swung inward with a faint
creak. Whoever last entered must’ve been careless. Or they were in a
hurry. Either way, not a good sign. 

“Hello? Geoff?” I yelled hesitantly.

I tried to peer into the apartment but not much was visible. Beyond the
entryway, a maze of boxes and loose furniture was stacked to the
ceiling, obstructing the view.

After a brief silence, the sound of pained grunting could be heard
again. A loud clicking escapes the darkness.

Shit. Whatever is happening isn’t good.

I crept into the apartment, careful not to disturb the walls of excess
that directed my path. I passed a light switch to my right as I entered
but opted to use my phone light to navigate instead. No need to alert
anyone that I’m here.

The boxes contents were worrisome: A vast assortment of hunting knives,
vintage WWII gear, dirty and unopened foreign materials and chemistry
tools. A collage of violence filled turned this small apartment into a
maze of death.

I turned the corner at the first bend and nearly dropped the phone.

The path opened up to a clear corner of the apartment that looked almost
like an altar to something sinister. Large wooden boards carried various
pictures of myself and my coworkers. They all had different levels of
damage and wear, and all had a colorful assortment of offensive labels
scribbled across the faces. 

Below the portraits were pages stuck into the wood with weapons: knives,
throwing stars, and curved daggers. The pages contained manic writings
about how each of us ought to be punished. Some writings described forms
of physical and psychological torture. Others were more outlandish.
Fragmented snippets of scenarios involving demons and supernatural
beings.

I heard a faint thud and stumbled over the mess, attempting to shine my
light towards the perpetrator. 

Nothing.

I paused briefly, squinting into the dark crevices of stacked weaponry.
The light crawled over additional boxes of ammunition and books with
unintelligible titles. Industrial instructions for chemical production
could be seen among the litter.

Thump.

A loud bang and pained moan escape a room to the right of me. The faint
clicking resumes. I press forward, phone in hand. I pray Geoff is there.
I just want to get him and get out of here. 

As I turn the corner, I cover my mouth to stifle a scream.

Geoff is there. At least, I think it’s him. The person before me sits in
a small closet decorated with various monitors and a desk. He’s down to
his underwear and his hands are cuffed behind his back. The real shock
is his face…well, what’s left of it.

A patch of scabbed flesh is all that remains. The rest of his head is
adorned with a strange metal cage, a long metal rod protruding from the
center. 

His head is bobbing up and down like a chicken, the metal prod tapping
the keys on the grimey keyboard. On the monitors, a mess of text is
being written using a crude text editor. My eyes wander to the filthy
bucket beside his chair where a cell phone lies cracked and buzzing
intermittently. The same phone I called just moments ago. 

“Don’t interrupt him.”

The words cause me to stumble into a wall of boxes as I turn to face
their owner.

Sure enough, Meddler is there. He’s wearing bloody kaki’s with
suspenders over his bare chest. A mess of blood dribbles just beneath
his chin. His face is hidden behind a mess of skin—Geoff’s face. I take
a step back as the phone light glints over a large, black hunting knife
in his hand. He twirls it playfully, fresh blood racing up and down the
blade. 

“I’m disappointed. All my hard work setting up shop here…you weren’t
supposed to come looking until tomorrow.” Meddler shook his head, the
skin on his face making a sickly wet sound.

“Had Geoff hand write you some clues and everything. You shouldn’t be in
here unless you plan to work.” He raised the blade and pointed at Geoff.

“What the hell is this? What did you do to Geoff?” I said shakily. My
voice was shrill with terror.

Meddler took one step forward, raising the knife towards me as he did.

“Meddling. As usual, right? Nah I’m kidding. I know I wasn’t always the
best boss. That’s going to change. We’re all going to be here
soon—working. Making money. So much money.” His voice broke on the last
syllable. He balled his fist and started hitting the side of his head,
the skin mask getting looser with every pop. 

“Hear that dad! This is my company. You—you don’t just do that to me—you
know I had to get you back. You can’t just take everything from me. MY
money. MINE.” His voice was hushed and desperate, like he’s pleading
with someone in his head.

My mind was racing. I wasn’t sure whether to fight or run. Cops would be
here soon. Maybe I could just keep him talking until then.

“You really doing this because we made fun of you? Jesus man, it was
just a joke—“

Meddler laughed mockingly, pressing the knife forward, the tip aiming
square at my chest.

“I don’t give a shit about that. No—I’m not petty. I’m a man on a
mission. This is about business.”

I glanced towards the way I came. I thought maybe I heard someone
approaching. Just a little bit longer…

“I heard you took pills…sounds like you’re going through a lot.” It came
out more of a question than a statement. I hoped he’d jump at the chance
to rebut.

He took two more steps forward and tapped the blade on my forehead. His
eyes peered through Geoff’s face, looking me up and down with violent
intentions.

“You sound like dad. I’m not a weak man…you just don’t get it. Your dad
ever mess with you? He ever take your money so you don’t gamble it away?
Repossess your car so you don’t drive drunk? Lace your drugs so you
overdose and never touch the stuff again? Control. So controlling.” He
paused and stifled a sob. His speech was a roller coaster, bouncing
between mania, rage, and joy every few syllables.

“No, no, no. He should’ve known better. It fried everything. Everything
up here. My soul is broken. My brain is on fire—“

I felt a sense of urgency as he hooked his blade around the side of my
throat. My hands instinctively grabbed a tower of boxes to my right and
brought them down at Meddler.

The boxes hit him hard in the chest and he swung the blade as he
tumbled, the ebony blade catching the top of my kneecap. 

I screamed and stumbled around the mess, my injured leg dragging hard
behind me. Geoff continued tapping away at his keyboard, afraid of what
Meddler might do if he stopped. I wanted to set him free, but I couldn’t
turn back.

Not yet.

I raced through the hazardous apartment towards the exit. A chorus of
screaming and sprinting broke out behind me. I continued to level towers
of boxes as I escaped, attempting to grow the gap between me and
Meddler. 

I was feeling hopeful as a square of florescent light came into view,
the open door leading out to the apartment hallway was within reach. No
cops in sight yet. That’s when I heard the gunshots. 

A barrage of bullets tore through the boxes around me, sending a flurry
of paper and cardboard in the air. I ducked and moved frantically to
avoid the attack but failed.

My body froze and tumbled to the floor as red hot gunfire pierced my
left side from behind. I screamed and writhed on the floor, a pool of
crimson growing beneath me.

The next minute was just a blur, a collage of moments fragmented by the
searing pain in my side. Meddler emerged from the shadows and stood over
me, gun in hand. He smacked the side of his head with the handle in a
crazed rant while attempting to drag me back by my ankle. I kicked as
best I could. He fumbled me and the gun in his shaky hands, white hot
anger making him hysterical. I wouldn’t be surprised if he gave up the
chase and tore me apart right there.

Fortunately, something took his focus away from me.

Angry voices broke out from the hallway, their forms casting shadows in
the framed light. It sounded like two people. Police, I could only hope.

Meddler shook and shuffled in place, unsure whether to shoot, hide, or
run. Madness won out as he lifted his weapon towards the unseen figures.
Three shots were fired before his gun reached his eye line and he
slumped to the floor, his body jackknifing before ultimately rolling
into a mess of cardboard where the light didn’t touch. 

Everything was blurry now. The figures stood over me with worried faces
and concerned voices but I couldn’t answer. There was more blood than
carpet beneath me and my body was giving away to the cold. 

The dark room is fading now, the orange glow of hallway lighting growing
dim. Clouds of cardboard debris falling like snow above me. 

In the distance, I hear the pecking of a keyboard. A faceless Geoff
continues working.

I blink, and the room changes.

My eyes remained closed, but a garbled ambulance siren is heard
overhead.

Time jumps again.

I try to pry open my swollen eyes. I squint at the cold, sterile medical
lighting washing over me. But only for a moment.

Finally, darkness.

I’ve been in the hospital a few weeks now. The bullet grazed my spleen
but didn’t hit anything critical. Dr. says I’m lucky, but I don’t hear
the words. I don’t feel lucky. My mind is occupied with memories of that
horrible night.

News of Meddler’s spree was still hot off the press. I couldn’t find a
channel that wasn’t talking about him in some form.

“Boss from Hell…”

“…Son of Silicon Valley elite tortures two employees during foiled
terrorist action…”

“…severe brain damage caused by laced narcotics led to psychotic break…”

“…CEO found dead in penthouse…”

“…Suspect claimed father laced drugs that led to his severe mental
impairment. Involvement cannot be confirmed at this time…”

The words fell flat for me. I didn’t care why it happened. No amount of
rationalization could temper the horror I experienced. And Geoff…

I tried to visit him. A nurse told me his family wanted privacy at this
time. I understand. He got off much worse than I did. I know it wasn’t
my fault, but I still wanted to apologize. I couldn’t shake the image of
him faceless, pecking away at that keyboard in Meddler’s dirty closet.
Some selfish part of me simply wanted to see him so I could replace that
memory. I didn’t want that to be the last time I saw him. 

Now, I face a new dilemma. I woke up convinced that Meddler was shot and
killed by the police. I was wrong.

The cops got me into an ambulance shortly after firing on him. They went
back, only to find an empty pile of crumpled boxes. A trail of blood led
to a makeshift chute at the back of the apartment that dropped him out
into the alley below. He was never found after that night.

Despite this news, I’d like to think I’m more adjusted lately. The
nightmares are manageable. The anxiety is almost at the point that I can
return to some semblance of work. Unfortunately I’m not sure how I’ll
feel hearing the typing of a keyboard again. Just the thought makes me
shudder.

My only worry now is that Meddler will find me. He’s not well, and he’s
surely going to get worse. I know his obsession will never stop. He’s
not going to rest until me and my coworkers are flayed and pecking at
keyboards with makeshift appendages, writing garbage out of fear of his
wrath. His punishment. A twisted, corporate hell.

When I’m strong enough, I’m going to do what I can to prepare. I need to
protect myself. When the ghost of my meddling boss comes knocking, I’d
like to think I can take him this time. I don’t want to end up like
Geoff.

Or worse.

And to those reading this, I hope you be careful too. If something
doesn’t feel right, don’t ignore that feeling. It’s easy to write off
difficult people in your life as jerks. It takes instinct to know if
something is really wrong. There are predators all around us, after all.
Bosses, teachers, friends, family—you never know where wolves hide among
sheep. Don’t let the Meddler’s of the world sneak up on you.
