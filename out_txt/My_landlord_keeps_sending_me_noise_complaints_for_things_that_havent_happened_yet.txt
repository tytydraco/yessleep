The first email arrived at 6:43 on a Tuesday evening.

Subject: SECOND NOTICE — QUIET HOURS VIOLATION

Apparently, I’d been repeatedly banging on my bedroom wall at 1:16 a.m.
on Wednesday.

It was still Tuesday.

I assumed someone had entered the wrong date. My building has one of
those tenant portals where you pay rent, submit maintenance requests,
and receive passive-aggressive reminders about not putting cardboard in
the trash chute.

I replied that the incident hadn’t happened yet and went back to making
dinner.

At 1:16 that night, something knocked three times on my bedroom wall.

I remember the time because I was already awake. I’d spent an
embarrassing amount of the evening wondering whether something would
happen, which is ridiculous considering how often the management company
screws up its paperwork.

The knocks were loud enough to make the framed photograph above my
nightstand tilt sideways.

Three quick bangs. All at the same height.

I put my ear against the wall.

Nothing.

Then I did what any annoyed apartment dweller would do. I banged back.

There was a pause.

Something struck the wall hard enough to knock the photograph off its
hook.

I slept on the couch.

The following afternoon, another email arrived.

Subject: THIRD NOTICE — QUIET HOURS VIOLATION

Incident date: Thursday, 12:38 a.m.

Description: Prolonged scraping noise, consistent with heavy furniture
being moved across the bedroom floor.

I called the property manager.

Her name is Denise. She’s probably in her fifties and has worked in the
building longer than I’ve lived there. She’s nice enough, although she
has a habit of saying “interesting” when she means “I’m not dealing with
this.”

I explained the dates.

“Interesting,” she said.

I told her about the knocking.

“Could be a neighbor.”

I explained that my bedroom shares a wall with the old service shaft,
not another apartment.

“Well, sound travels.”

I asked her to check who was submitting the complaints.

She said they were anonymous.

I asked if she could at least confirm they were coming from an actual
tenant.

She told me she’d look into it.

That evening, I moved my bedroom dresser against the opposite wall. It’s
a cheap six-drawer thing from IKEA, but it’s heavy when it’s full. I
figured if somebody was playing an elaborate prank, I wasn’t going to
cooperate by leaving the furniture where it was.

At 12:35, I sat on the bed watching it.

Nothing happened for two minutes.

Then I heard a faint dragging sound.

I thought it was coming from the apartment above me.

It wasn’t.

The bottom drawer of my dresser was sliding open.

Very slowly.

I got off the bed.

The drawer stopped.

I approached it, pushed it shut, and stood there with my hand against
the front.

At 12:38, the entire dresser moved.

Not much. Maybe three inches.

But it moved toward me, against my hand, with enough force to shove me
backward.

The legs made a horrible grinding sound on the laminate.

I stumbled against the bedframe. Before I could stand up properly, the
dresser moved again.

This time I saw something.

There was a gap between its back panel and the wall. Just a narrow strip
of darkness.

For half a second, four pale fingertips appeared around the edge of the
dresser.

They curled against the wood and pulled.

I ran.

I didn’t stop until I reached the street. I was barefoot and had left my
phone upstairs. A guy walking his dog asked if I was okay.

I told him someone was in my apartment.

He called the police for me.

Two officers checked every room. Nobody was there. The windows were
locked, the front door hadn’t been forced, and the old service shaft was
sealed behind plaster.

One officer examined the dresser.

He pointed out four long scratches on the back, near the corner.

“Probably happened when you assembled it,” he said.

I hadn’t assembled it. I’d bought it secondhand.

I didn’t mention the fingers.

I stayed with my friend Sam that night.

The next morning, Denise finally called.

She sounded different.

Apparently, she’d asked the company that operates the tenant portal to
investigate the complaints.

They couldn’t find another tenant associated with them.

The reports were being submitted from my own apartment.

“That’s impossible,” I said.

“I know.”

“Doesn’t that mean somebody has my password?”

“That’s what we thought. But they’re not coming through your account.”

I asked what that meant.

She said the reports were coming from a registered device assigned to
Unit 3F. My apartment.

Except the building only has one registered device per unit, and mine
was sitting in my pocket.

She’d asked their support team to disable the other device.

They couldn’t.

It had been removed from the system in 2019.

I asked her what kind of device it was.

She said she didn’t know.

I haven’t been back inside the apartment.

Sam helped me arrange for a moving company to collect my things. I gave
them permission to throw out the dresser rather than take it.

They refused to enter the bedroom.

The crew leader called me and said something was wrong with the floor.

He said the dresser was sitting in the middle of the room, but there
were deep scratches extending from its legs all the way to the bedroom
doorway.

Like somebody had been dragging it back and forth for hours.

He wanted to know what I’d done to the floor.

I told him to forget the dresser and take whatever was already packed.

He hung up.

About five minutes later, he called again.

I could hear men shouting in the background.

“Is there somebody living in that wall?”

I asked him what he meant.

“There’s a gap behind the baseboard. Somebody keeps sticking their
fingers through it.”

Then the call disconnected.

I’ve tried calling him back. His company told me the crew finished my
job and went home. They refused to give me his personal number.

My belongings arrived yesterday afternoon.

One box was missing.

It contained the framed photograph that had fallen off my bedroom wall.

I’m mentioning this because of what happened last night.

At 10:14 p.m., Sam received a noise complaint.

Not me. Sam.

He doesn’t live in my building and has never used its tenant portal.

It came to his personal email address.

Subject: FIRST NOTICE — QUIET HOURS VIOLATION

Incident date: Friday, 11:52 p.m.

Description: Repeated knocking from inside the bathroom wall.

Sam forwarded it to me, then called the management company listed at the
bottom.

Their office was closed.

At 11:50, we were sitting together on his couch. The bathroom door was
open. We could see the sink and part of the shower curtain.

At 11:52, something knocked three times.

Sam stood up so quickly he knocked his glass off the coffee table.

We both heard it.

The knocking came again.

And then the shower curtain moved.

There wasn’t a breeze. The bathroom window doesn’t open.

The curtain simply pushed outward in one place, about four feet above
the floor.

Five narrow shapes pressed against it.

Like someone was holding their hand flat against the fabric from the
wrong side.

Sam grabbed his keys. I grabbed my shoes.

We got into his car and drove to a motel about forty minutes away.

We’ve been here since midnight.

I was beginning to think we’d finally gotten away from it.

Then, about twenty minutes ago, another email arrived.

This one came directly to me.

Subject: FINAL NOTICE — QUIET HOURS VIOLATION

Incident date: Saturday, 3:24 a.m.

Description: Sustained banging against interior door. Occupant
repeatedly requested assistance. Door remained closed.

It’s 3:11 right now.

Our motel room has a bathroom near the entrance and a connecting door
beside the television.

Sam checked the connecting door when we arrived. It was locked. The
front desk confirmed the room next door is unoccupied.

I’ve pushed the desk against it anyway.

I didn’t want to write any of this. Sam says posting it is stupid and I
should just try to sleep.

But I noticed something while moving the desk.

There’s a white envelope on the carpet, almost completely hidden
underneath the connecting door.

It’s addressed to me in the same typeface as the complaints.

I pulled it out using the edge of a room-service menu.

Inside was the photograph from my bedroom.

The frame is broken.

Someone has written a time on the back.

3:24.

I haven’t told Sam.

I can hear somebody moving furniture on the other side of the connecting
door.

The room is supposed to be empty.
