I grew up in an absolutely perfect neighborhood. The houses were
gorgeous, and looked like they had been taken out of a 90s movie. The
landscaping was meticulous year round. Crime was minimal.

But there was one weird thing, though - no matter your age, everyone had
to be in at midnight. Because at 1am, the streets in my neighborhood
completely rearranged themselves around the park that sat directly in
the middle. If you went on Google Maps, the whole thing almost looked
like a circular maze.

Like any other neighborhood, a network of sewer pipes ran underground.
In mine, they all met directly underneath the park, which was on a small
hill. At one end of this hill was a drain, like you’d find in any other
neighborhood.

In ours, though, that’s where the thing came out at midnight.

Like clockwork, seven days a week.

After it roamed the streets for an hour, doing.. whatever it did, the
streets would rearrange themselves so it wouldn’t remember where things
were.

No one really knew what the “thing” was. Not even the adults. As we got
older, some of us started to doubt that it was even real. Like Santa, it
was just something made up to keep us from getting up to no good late at
night. Those kids made the mistake of putting their theories to use.

Like I said, there was no crime. But we were good for one unexplained
missing kid every year. Almost every kid was out when they shouldn’t
have been. The family would get a blank check. We would be told they had
moved.

We all knew they were lying. We didn’t ask.

All things considered, it really wasn’t an issue at all for the first 22
years of my life.

That all changed the night of the concert. I had won four tickets to see
a band that I liked, so I invited my now wife, along with Brooks, my
best friend from college and Dani, a girl he was seeing.

Lucy - that’s my wife’s name - wore this strapless printed maxi dress
that she looked absolutely breathtaking in. I mention this only because
for the first year we dated, if she wore a dress, it was a maxi. It
wasn’t a religious thing, just a matter of personal preference.

We’ve been together for a decade now. Married for five years. She has
not worn one since the events of that night.

The concert itself was amazing - the first of many times, it turned out,
we’d see them together. I remember turning to my left and becoming
totally transfixed by Lucy dancing to a song. How effortlessly she
moved. She’d caught me staring and gave me this smile so sweet, so
loving, so… seductive, that in that moment, I knew I was going to marry
her. But that’s a story for another day.

We left around 11:45, and due to traffic we didn’t get back until about
12:15. Not exactly optimal, but we had woken up that morning to my
parents’ house right next to the park. We would have to park there due
to family in town, but I somewhat naively hoped that, after 15 minutes,
the “thing” would be long gone. As long as we kept quiet and walked at a
good pace, we we should make the five minute walk without a problem…
right?

Wrong.

It started with Brooks and Dani, who were both very drunk and very
unable to keep their hands off of each other. Dani had gotten out of the
car as soon as we pulled into the lot, and jokingly slapped him.

“BABEEEE!”

Out of the darkness, almost immediately after, there came a low growl.

Brooks looked at me, confused.

“You hear that, bro?”

“Yeah. Just a car. Don’t worry about it.”

I didn’t bother locking my car, not wanting to attract any attention. I
was now slowly becoming aware of something breathing wet, heavy breaths,
and hoping the other three didn’t notice.

I brought a finger to my lips, motioning for everyone to be quiet.

“I’ll lead the way,” I whispered. “We don’t want to wake anyone up, so
let’s be quiet as possible.”

I could tell the others were more than a little confused about my change
in mood, but they didn’t say anything. 

And so we silently, but steadily crept along the sidewalk. I could feel
with a terrible certainty that something was there in the darkness with
us, but I hoped that not bothering it would mean it didn’t bother us.

A loud, angry roar that couldn’t be explained by any sort of vehicle
interrupted my thoughts, telling me we would have no such luck.

About fifty yards away, under a streetlight, we saw it.

This giant, hulking… thing. It had the head of a bull, but the body of a
man. It had to be at least eight feet tall, and probably around 500
pounds. Pure, rippling, enraged muscle.

And it roared, and charged at us.

Had we waited even a millisecond, I’m sure none of us would be here
today. Thank god we didn’t.

Instead, we ran, for even the slightest chance of escaping it. 

I could hear it gaining ground on us, the angry thunder drowning out the
sound of four pairs of sandals sprinting down the sidewalk. 

Everything began to hurt as my house came into view. I wanted nothing
more than to collapse onto the lawn. 

I made the mistake of looking back.

The thing was back in the darkness, and all I could see were two blazing
red eyes, growing closer every second.

The four of us reached the front porch. I stepped up to the door,
frantically keying in the four digits that would unlock the front door,
and prayed to every god I knew that my fingers wouldn’t slip and enter
the wrong number. Because, if that happened… I didn’t want to think
about it.

It got closer. The roar now sounded more like a train. I estimated it
was maybe five feet from our mailbox.

The door beeped, the sweetest sound I had heard in my life. I threw it
open. Brooks, then Dani, then Lucy. 

Sweet, beautiful Lucy.

The thing was right on her. And as she came through the threshold, it
lunged at her with one giant, meaty claw. I could feel it breathing on
us - hot, foul steam, rank with seemingly every meal it had ever eaten.
There was a tearing noise that in my hysteria I hoped was cloth and not
flesh.

I threw the door shut as hard as I could.

There was one last angry roar as the thing, for whatever reason unable
to just barge through, stopped.

Then, silence.

The four of us collapsed to the ground. I lay there gasping for breath.

I could hear someone crying silently. It sounded like Lucy. I rolled
onto my side and took her in. 

She was, miraculously, totally fine. Very shaken up, but unharmed
otherwise. Then my eyes traveled further down, and I saw what had made
the ripping noise.

A sizable piece of her dress had been torn off, creating an absurdly
large slit that would probably challenge almost any tailor.

“You okay, Luce?”

She made a noncommittal grunt. I decided now was not the time to inform
her about her dress. I sat up. 

“Everyone good?”

There was a snort. Brooks.

“I’m alive. Somehow.”

Dani let out a choked sob.

“… What the fuck was that… thing?” Brooks again.

I chewed on my lip for a bit as I searched for the right words.

“We have a curfew here. Midnight. That thing supposedly comes out at
night. I.. I didn’t think it was real. I’m sorry….”

Silence from the other three. I rubbed my eyes. My head was beginning to
throb.

“How long have you known about it?” Dani.

“My entire life… But like I said - I always thought it was just like,
Santa, or something. Where adults use it to get you to behave.”

I don’t know how convinced she was by my explanation. Brooks, luckily,
came to my “rescue”.

“I don’t care how real it is or isn’t. This room is fucking spinning.
Can we just, like, go to bed and talk about this in the morning?”

Dani rolled her eyes. “Fine.”

Lucy whimpered.

We all got up. I helped Lucy up. If she noticed the sudden airiness
around the back of her legs, she gave no indication of it.

There was a hand on my back. Brooks. His breath stank of beer. 

“Bro… tonight was dope.. But like, between you and me.. I really hope
that shit just now was just a bad dream.”

I looked at him. “You and me both, man.”

We went to bed without another word.

Lucy and I went up to my room. She was supposed to sleep downstairs, but
I had a feeling she needed me tonight, and I figured it would be better
to deal with any backlash in the morning.

We were asleep as soon as we laid down. We didn’t even bother
undressing.

I woke up the next morning to an empty bed and a throbbing head. I went
downstairs to find everyone standing around the kitchen island eating
breakfast sandwiches. Brooks was giving my aunt a slightly-still-tipsy
recap of the night before. I wondered if he would tell her about our
adventure getting home.

“Yeah, so I, uhh.. Kind of wandered into the pit. Just walked in. Didn’t
get stopped or anything.”

“Mhm. Was this all of you guys, or just you?”

Brooks paused. Not sure if she was insinuating that he had left the rest
of us out.

“… No? Just me. Why?”

“Well.. um… honey?” She looked at Lucy.

“Yes?”

“I apologize for pointing this out.. But did something happen to your
dress last night?”

Lucy twisted slightly. Turned around. Her face went pale.

And instantly, we all knew it hadn’t been a dream. The way the thing
roared and reached for her.

The reason, I think, that aside from our wedding day and various formal
events, she has not worn a long dress or skirt since that evening.

That same massive hole in the back of the dress.

Ragged, like claws had torn through in a final, desperate attempt to
drag someone to their doom.
