About a year ago I was working at this fast food joint, delivering
orders. I was attending college at the time so I usually worked evening
and night shifts.

One night we got an order around 2:30 in the morning. Which was ordinary
for the weekends, since we were one of those places that work all night
long. So we’d usually get calls from some college kids having a house
party or somebody staying up super late.

The adress was in this neighborhood I didn’t know very well, but it
looked decent upon arrival. Well lit, ordinary.

I found the house right away, knocked on the door then waited a few
seconds.

When it opened, this little girl was standing there. Couldn’t have been
more than 6 years old.

I smiled politely.

“Hey there. Your mom and dad home?”

She shook her head.

“How about some other adult? Older sister or brother maybe?”

This time she just reached into her pocket and took out some money. She
handed it my way.

I didn’t reach to take it, but I could see it was over 60$ altogether.
Which was way more than the order cost.

Now, we had a strict policy of not taking cash from minors. The person
needed to be over 18 to pay the order upfront.

I contemplated for a second how I could explain this to a six year old.
Then I thought of something.

“Were you the one who called for food delivery?”

She shook her head.

“Somebody else called?”

“Mama” - she said shortly.

I was relieved for a second. I thought she misunderstood my first
question and her parents were actually home.

“Oh, so your mom is there? Could she maybe come to the door so I can
hand her the order?”

“Mama can’t come.”

“Why not?”

She didn’t say anything now. Just looked to the side and pointed
somewhere inside the house.

I peaked inside for a second but only saw the hallway and light coming
from a room. I didn’t understand what she meant.

“Mama can’t walk. She said I take the food.”

I was confused for a moment. Was her mother in a wheelchair? And if she
was, couldn’t she still make her way to the door in it?

Sending your 6 year old to take the order and pay for it was completely
unreasonable. Especially cause she was trying to give me twice the
money.

This seemed like some kid that was left home alone and stole money from
her parents to buy food.

I was ready to make an excuse of coming to the wrong house and walk
away, when another girl appeared in the doorway.

She was a bit older. I’d say 10-11 maybe.

She gave me a look. Then turned to the younger one and took the money
from her hand. She scolded her shortly before turning back towards me.

“Hello, sir. I’m sorry, my sister is too young to count money
correctly.”

Then she counted, extended her arm and handed the right amount my way.

I still didn’t wanna take it. But I figured I could reason with an older
girl.

“Listen, I’m sorry but we can’t take money from an underage person. If
your parents are home they can pay me but otherwise I can’t deliver the
order.”

She nodded. She looked like she understood.

“I know sir, delivery guys always say that until we explain. Our mom is
really sick and can’t get up from her bed. So we always take the order
for her.”

I wanted to believe her. I really did. But it sounded off. Shouldn’t
there be a home nurse to help in these situations?

Two little girls couldn’t possibly take care of an immobile grown woman.

“Is there somebody taking care of her?”

Both girls nodded.

“Yes, the nurse comes in the morning.”

My eyes darted from one girl to the other. I didn’t know what to do.
They looked honest. The order was already there. If I didn’t give it
away I’d have to drive all the way back at three in the morning with no
money and a full meal to throw away.

I looked at the money in her hand. Hesitating. The older girl noticed.
She spoke up again.

“Our mom is just there in the room. If it makes you feel better you can
come in to see her.”

I glanced inside again. I thought for a second this was a hoax and
somebody will be hiding there to rob me or worse.

But another part of me just wanted to give this order away and get it
over with. I know I should’ve known better, but the door they pointed at
seemed close and I thought I’d just peek in, make sure there’s an adult
there, take the money and leave.

I nodded.

“Alright. But I need to go back out as soon as I give her the order. I
have other customers, okay?”

Both girls nodded again. And I followed them inside.

We walked down the short hallway and into the room. I was cautious.
Already regretting my decision and counting how many steps from the exit
if I needed to bolt within seconds.

But nothing could’ve prepared me for what I saw in that room.

The smell was the first thing that hit me. Sick. Wrong. Making me squint
my nose in disgust.

And then the kids. All girls. Like 5 or 6 of them, all sitting on the
floor. But no toys around. No games. Nothing that even gave a hint of
any children living there.

And in the middle of the room? A woman in a wheelchair. But not just
that.

A regular disabled woman would’ve been fine.

This was a corpse. Rotting body.

That’s what the smell was.

And I don’t mean corpse like died yesterday. Her skin was brown and grey
and started deteriorating around the mouth.

This body has definitely been here a while.

In her lap was one of the girls, holding a bowl of something and
pretending to feed it to her with a spoon.

I stood there frozen. Unable to move. Unable to react. My mind couldn’t
comprehend what I was looking at.

Then two of the girls on the floor clapped joufully when they saw me.
Like I was a relative. Like I came there to play with them.

The clapping sound snapped me back to reality and I finally moved. I
bolted out the room, then out the door. Running to my car with the order
bag still in my hand.

I didn’t think. I just drove. I didn’t care where.

A minute or two of driving in a random direction I stopped by the road.
I needed to collect myself. I looked in the rear view mirror and nobody
was following me.

I didn’t really expect them to.

Tomorrow I quit on the spot. I didn’t tell my manager why, I gave no
explanation and he wasn’t happy with it. But I didn’t care.

I’d rather starve for the next three months while I look for another job
than ever have to experience something like this again.
