I always considered myself a relatively cheery person, and while life
had its ups and downs, I tried to smile through it and keep my hopes up.
I only mention that because having nightmares was so unusual for me; I
don’t think I truly ever felt as if I was working through something
difficult. That’s why my first few nightmares really hit me like a brick
when they first happened.

I had just moved out of living with my parents and into my own place,
which was not only awesome, but it absolutely made me feel like I was
finally an adult. Imagine! Having to pay your own bills, having your own
space to live in, privacy, being able to sleep and wake up whenever you
want . . . or at least I thought so.

My first nightmare was truly terrifying, I think anybody would agree. I
was in a club celebrating a friend’s birthday. The music was pumping,
and an old school-disco ball scattered a million colors in a hazy blur.

At some point I broke away from the party and went to the bathroom. It
all felt so surreal in a way that only a dream can. As I went to the
sink to wash up, I noticed that I had a really unnatural smile. Like,
the person looking back at me didn’t really look like me.

I bent over the sink, splashing my face with water to try and fix my
smile. I took a mouthful of water and swished it around my mouth. That’s
when I got the really sickening feeling of free-floating bones in my
mouth. I could feel as my teeth got looser and looser as I swished, and
the fallen teeth started clinking against the teeth that were still
firmly in place. 

Spitting the water out, I could feel my teeth pour out over my lips and
hit the sink. I looked up at what I expected to be my toothy smile, but
instead, half my teeth were missing, and I couldn’t move. I tried
turning left and right, I tried pulling myself away, I tried closing and
opening my eyes, but I couldn’t.

All I could do was look at myself in the mirror, with my missing teeth,
my eyes looking back at me, haggard and wrinkled from the inhuman smile
I was giving myself. My smile grew wider and wider until I suddenly
heard the sound of skin ripping and a sharp electric sensation shot
across my gums, setting them on fire.

That’s when I bolted up in my bed, gasping for air, my heart racing a
million miles a second. I could feel the cold sweat all over my face,
but the only thing I really cared about was my teeth. I threw off the
covers, ran into the bathroom, turned the light on and steeled myself. I
smiled and . . . thank god, my teeth were all still there. 

I brushed my teeth twice before going to bed again, taking extra care
with one of my teeth that always moved a little.

For a long time after, that would be my nightly schedule, my routine
nightmare. It would always start with some kind of party or gathering.
Then, I’d excuse myself and go to the bathroom, where I’d discover
something horrible had happened to my teeth. At first, my teeth simply
fell out on their own. Then things escalated, and some nights I’d be
paralyzed, forced to watch them crack and break.  

Probably the most horrible one is when I went to the bathroom and I
looked at myself in the mirror, only to find out that my normal teeth
had been replaced by dentures held in place with rusty nails. Actually,
the worst part about that is that they weren’t even all at the same
level; the dentures were broken into several pieces, with some sections
sitting higher than others, making it almost impossible for me to
actually bring my teeth together and smile.

But what really changed things for me was the escalation, because I
stopped dreaming of my teeth and started feeling it in real life, when I
met my sleep paralysis demon. 

I liked to consider myself more stitious than superstitious, and while I
vaguely believed in the supernatural, I never really thought about it or
thought that ghosts were real. But I certainly had to come to terms with
that reality when I woke up one night, and a dark shadowy figure with
long fingers sat on my chest.

I could barely see it in the darkness, but I could tell that it was
smiling at me. I could feel it writhe and wriggle around me as I looked
up in terror, unable to scream, or yell for help. I felt its long cold
fingers slowly spread my lips apart. I felt them around my gums, and
then started slowly rubbing against my teeth. One by one, it examined
them with its fingers.

And the next night, it was much the same. I sat there, feeling this
ungodly thing touch my teeth. Pull them out. Put them back in again. It
liked to go from tooth to tooth, wobbling each one as if it was feeling
for movement. I could feel it trace my gum line and press every few
teeth.

But it always kept coming back to one tooth. Wobbling it, moving it,
smiling its disgusting smile as if to taunt me. My teeth belonged to it,
and I could do nothing about it.

It honestly made me sick to my stomach. I went from brushing my teeth
once a day, maybe, to religiously brushing three times a day. I even
started using mouth wash, just so I could get over that feeling of my
teeth being touched. 

But then at some point, I think it was a couple of months later, I just
got so exhausted that I kinda stopped caring. I didn’t care about my
teeth falling out, or them being broken in a million pieces, or feeling
them moving freely inside my mouth. I didn’t feel scared of this thing
screwing with my teeth over and over again. 

Sure, the paralysis was still somewhat scary, but I no longer felt
scared by this menacing demon sitting on my chest and putting its clammy
fingers in my mouth.

And while I’ve always tried taking good care of my teeth, I’ve never
actually gone to a dentist, something that I rectified by setting up an
appointment for next week.

Honestly, that one tooth has been getting a lot wobblier lately.
