Every night since I was a five-year-old child, I used to pretend to fall
asleep—or force myself to—so I wouldn’t have to look at those figures
that appeared in my room as soon as the lights went out.

Although on those occasions when my body simply refused to sleep, I
would stay there staring at the ceiling or cover myself with the
blankets. Those were the worst nights, because I was fully aware of how
the elongated, cold hands of those shadows drifted over my body.
Sometimes I could see their faces—some featureless, others with strange
shapes, but all of them with white, empty eyes.

This situation didn’t improve as the years went by. Even though I told
my parents a thousand times to switch my room or sell the house, they
simply wouldn’t listen, arguing that I needed to leave those childhood
fears behind.

Now I am 16 years old.

And everything changed a few weeks ago.

I was finishing up some schoolwork on my computer—it was around 10 or 11
at night—when suddenly I felt something crawl into my bed.

It was one of the shadows, but this time it wasn’t trying to reach for
me. It simply pulled the blankets over itself and started looking around
frantically. I could see a near-demented terror in its eyes while its
body—or whatever it had—shook violently.

Then I heard something under my bed.

And there it was: another shadow in the fetal position, accompanied by
even more shadows curled up in the exact same posture.

It was as if something had terrified them. And if the very entities that
had fueled my childhood nightmares were afraid, maybe I should be, too.

I finally understood what was happening last Tuesday, when my parents
had gone on a trip to visit my paternal grandmother. I found the living
room in total darkness, but what was standing there wasn’t just
shade—there was something else.

Heavy, dense, and incomprehensible, right in the center of the room: a
shapeless shadow, darker than the very darkness surrounding it.

I ran upstairs to my room and locked myself in, thinking it might be an
intruder who had broken into the house. I decided to call the police,
and they told me they’d be there in five minutes—minutes that felt like
an eternity.

Then I heard something knock on my bedroom door. Fearing that whatever
had seen me was about to end my life, I hid in the back of the closet
and tried not to make a sound.

Suddenly, the door burst open violently, as if it had been kicked in,
though it didn’t make a single sound.

At first, I didn’t see anything or anyone enter, until I felt something
strange wrapping around me. It was one of the shadows, and in its eyes
was a horror even greater than what I had seen the first time one
climbed into my bed.

All of a sudden, a deafening noise echoed through my room. I closed my
eyes and tried to stay quiet—though with the chaos outside, I doubt
anyone could have heard me anyway. That was when I felt the shadow’s
fingers dig deep into my shoulders, as if I were its anchor, holding it
back from being dragged away.

Then, everything went back to normal. I stepped out of the closet to see
what had happened, but everything was in perfect order. The furniture
and objects in the room were right where I had left them; the same was
true for the living room, the kitchen, and even the bathrooms. It was
utterly surreal.

I heard a knock at the front door. It was the police. I let them in and
told them what had happened—leaving out the part about the shadow
hugging me so they wouldn’t think I’d lost my mind. As they searched the
house, I couldn’t help but ask myself: What was that thing, and what
happened to the shadows?

Right now, I’m at my computer, scrolling through Reddit forums dedicated
to the paranormal. I haven’t found much, to be honest, except for vague
mentions of the term “God of Darkness.” I really don’t want to believe
that all of this was caused by a “god” rather than a sick prank played
by my own mind—though considering I’m currently buried in paranormal
message boards, the joke writes itself.

Maybe I should…

My computer camera just turned on by itself. I can see my face on the
screen, and something else…

That shadow—the exact same shadow I saw in my living room that night—is
standing right there at my bedroom door…

And it’s coming closer.
