Part 2

Last night was hell.

I didn’t sleep at all. I mean that literally. My nighttime routine
started off about right, but got thrown into a loop that I couldn’t pull
out of. 

I thought about my wife again. When she got sick, and the treatment she
went through. I thought about why I started taking the 6:12 train, but
my brain wouldn’t let me see it. Every time I came close, it switched to
something else. 

I gave up and salvaged maybe two hours of sleep.

Some of you told me not to report the Grey Man. Others told me to arm
myself and attack him, which I would never do otherwise, but I have to
say was tempting in the moment.

I never reported him. 

One of you suggested sitting down and trying to talk to him. Normally,
like a friend, or at least a friendly stranger. I thought about that one
the most.

I went through the motions this morning.

Same station.

Same time.

Same sewer grates not quite symmetrically lined up with the train doors.

Except I didn’t go there. I stood three train cars away, picking a
random door to enter. I wanted to avoid him. Avoid them all. To break
out of this routine and establish a new one where I can have peace of
mind at last.

The train pulled up to the station and came to a stop in front of me.

The doors opened. I looked inside, witnessing how different this spot
was from my usual. The stairs were on the right, next to the door.

I breathed in deep, ready to start my new life in a new part of the
train. I admit, I was even excited at the prospect of meeting new
regulars.

I stepped in with a fresh, open mind and prepared to memorize a new
routine.

I blinked, and I found myself sitting in my regular seat.

It was everything in me not to yell. I looked around in a panic. Same
section. Same train car. Same seat whose creaks and contours I had so
thoroughly memorized that I would recognize them blindfolded.

Same regulars. The Accountant, LSS, and the Kid were all there.

So was the Grey Man, looking at me. Something in his eyes was different.

“What’s going on?” I asked him.

Whatever happened, he seemed like he would know.

The seconds passed, but I got no reply.

“I went in somewhere else. What happened?”

Still nothing. 

Until, for the first time, his gaze left me and scanned the other
passengers. The regulars. I followed, looking at each of them.

Not just looking. Truly seeing them for the first time.

First I saw the Accountant. He was doing a crossword, like he usually
does, staring down at it. I watched him closely. Every time something
passed outside, his body made the slightest movement, as if thinking
about looking through the window but preventing itself. It was almost
imperceptible. A twitch of a finger, a slight shift of a foot, a
half-flutter of an eyelid. Blink and you miss it. But it was undoubtedly
there.

There was something else about him. Not him, exactly, but his crossword.
Every time he filled in a word, it would disappear like it was never
there. Like clockwork he would stare at the puzzle, fill in a word, and
it was gone no sooner than the pen was lifted off the page. He would
pause, stillness taking the place of an exasperated sigh, and then start
the process again.

I turned to look at LSS. She sat motionless as usual, her bag flanking
her on the adjoining seat. The bag itself was unremarkable, if not
slightly more worn than expected. The way it was positioned looked off.
It sat at an angle that made it obvious she was saving the seat for
someone. Only nobody ever came. 

I followed her gaze. As long as I’ve seen her on the train, I assumed
she was looking at nothing in particular. Watching her, I realized I was
wrong. She was staring at the stairs with a mix of expectation and
resignation. The kind of look someone sitting alone in a restaurant or
at a bar gets when they start to realize there won’t be another joining
them.

I dared to look at the Kid last. He stared out the window, soaking in
the views of homes, buildings, and nature. The excitement I saw in his
eyes seemed duller, like a star behind a passing cloud. It was there,
just muted.

He looked down at his phone more often as the trip went on. Once every
few minutes at first, slowly accelerating until he was almost constantly
looking down.

The train pulled into my station sooner than I expected. The squealing
brakes shocked me into awareness. I had to get off at the right spot
this time, if only to prove that I could.

I grabbed my things and sprinted. The others getting off at this stop
had already filed out and the door would close any second.

I felt my brain racing, working overtime to pull me into a memory or a
distraction.

My legs felt heavy, like they were carrying two of me upstairs rather
than running down them.

The door chimes played as I approached.

My brain was screaming at me to notice something else. To look away. To
remember something.

I refused and lunged at the closing door. 

I made it mostly out. My ankle got caught by the door, and I had to give
it a strong yank to get it free, hard enough that I almost lost my shoe.

But I made it.

And I stood there, watching the train pull away. My hand clutched the
keychain in my pocket.
