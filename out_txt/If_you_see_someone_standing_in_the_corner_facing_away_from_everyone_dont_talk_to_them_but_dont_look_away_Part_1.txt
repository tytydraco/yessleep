Blinking away dust, I put down my dad’s notebook. What the fuck? I can’t
think of a creepier opening entry to what I thought was his travel
journal. I turn it over, run my palm across the engraved leather cover,
squint. Compasses etched on each corner of the front and back.

I see it’s filled with dated drawings and scrawls that get sparser and
more disorganized as I work my way through, leading up to a final
illegible entry about halfway into the journal.

~9/16/XX~

Followed by lines and scratches scattered across the page.

I can’t make out a single legible word. My eyes skip back to the top of
the page.

~9/16/XX~

The day before his death.

Flustered and wiping away a tear, I gently place it back in the box I
discovered it in and continue sifting through his attic, trying to find
his picture set of me, him, and mom from when we first moved to the
Midwest, a snapshot of normalcy before his schizophrenia moved in and
our family out. Something to put up at the funeral to remember the good
parts.

“Samantha, it’s time to go! We have thirty minutes ‘til we gotta be at
your uncle’s!“ my mom shouts from the hallway below. She refused to come
up; I barely convinced her to drive me out to his house in the prairie.
But I know he kept pictures from his old Polaroid somewhere.

After tearing through a few more boxes of notebooks, clothes, and books,
I find it. A cracked leather photo case.

I page through photos of my now distant childhood. Fishing pictures fill
up half, an early shot of six-year-old me holding up a bluegill like a
trophy. Dad’s blocky handwriting on the strip underneath: “Sammy’s First
Catch“. In another, the three of us at the lake, Dad holding the camera
at arm’s length, everyone crammed into the frame smiling into the summer
sun. Except, why is his face blurring? I blink twice-

Creak.

From the opposite corner of the attic.

My back tenses.

Someone’s watching me.

I whip my head around.

Nothing. Just boxes and glimmering dust turning slowly in the sunlight
leaking through the attic window. Except… my eyes dart to the far
corner, where the sunlight gives up. A patch of darkness. I swear, if I
look close enough, I can make out the shape of someone standing very
still with their face to the wall -

“Samantha, we’re going to be late if we don’t leave now!“

“Okay, okay! I’m coming down, just give me a minute!“

When I look back at the far corner, all I see is dust flickering in and
out of shadow and light.

You’re just freaked out by the house, I reassure myself, before tossing
the photo case in my bag and shuffling down the ladder steps to meet my
mom, her fingers fidgeting as we leave the deserted countryside house.

-–

“It was so great seeing you, Sam,“ my uncle Tommy hugs me goodbye as mom
gets in her car, “I’ll see you and Kat on Saturday at the wake, but
please don’t hesitate to call if you need anything. Really, I know my
sister well enough to see you’re both in need of some love and support.“

“Thanks Uncle Tommy, I appreciate it, always good seeing you guys.“

As I close the passenger seat door, mom turns to me.

“Any chance you’re still hungry?”

“After tofu and salad? Please, let’s get a burger.”

“Kopp’s?”

“Kopp’s!” We smile at each other for what feels like the first time in a
while.

On our way I stick my hand in my bag to grab my dad’s photo case, but
feel a second item nudged up against it. Pulling it out, it’s the travel
journal.

I thought I put that back in the box?

“What’s that honey?”

“Oh, uh, nothing, just a journal.”

“Oh nice! Where’d you get it?”

“Ah, uh, a friend from college gave it to me, just been using it as a
diary.”

Why did I lie about that?

“I didn’t know you kept a diary, well, I learn something new about you
every day.” She ruffles my hair, then pauses for a second.

“Did you find the photos you were looking for?”

“Mom, you sound like you’d rather I didn’t.”

“No, no I’m just curious.”

“Yeah I did, wanna look through them at Kopp’s?”

Her thumb starts rubbing the stitching on the steering wheel. She
reaches up and tilts the rearview mirror toward the back seat, holds it
there a second, and tilts it back.

“Mom?”

“Sorry hun, let’s just do it later when we’re home, don’t wanna get them
covered in honey mustard do we?”

-–

Walking from the car in the lot I notice the line already snaking past
the walled menu boards. At the entrance I have to squeeze my way past a
teenage girl in a bright orange jacket, her nose practically pressed to
the wall. Must be on her phone.

“You okay?” Mom asks, looking right past the girl.

“Yeah, I’m fine, just these kids and TikTok. It’s like they can’t spend
a second without it.”

She frowns a bit, confused. “Huh, well, look at the pot calling the
kettle black,” she shoots a wry smile.

After a few minutes in line, we each order cheeseburgers and onion rings
then wind our way to a back silver seat.

“Ah, honey, your uncle’s calling, I’m gonna take this outside, just look
out for number 47.”

“No worries, I’ll hang here.”

Without thinking, I pull out the journal from my bag and open it up on
the table. I stare at it for a few seconds before the brimming curiosity
forces me to start paging through again.

Some pages have short entries, poems and the like, but I notice most are
drawings.

Every drawing is a room, meticulously drawn. A kitchen. A waiting room
with rows of chairs. A train station. Our old living room? With the
cracked vase above the decorative fireplace. Each room has people, drawn
fast, ovals and lines, like he was racing to get them down before they
moved. And every drawing has a red circle around one. Some are neat.
Some the pen’s broken through the paper he circled them so vigorously. I
page through, same thing, one drawing after another.

Around page thirty, things start to get messy-

DON’T TALK TO THEM

DON’T TALK TO THEM

DON’T TALK TO THEM

DON’T TALK TO THEM

written all over the pages, etched deep and black into the page above
whatever’s below, almost as if he’s shouting through the page, denser
and more erratic as I move through later drawings.

How didn’t I notice this earlier?

As I’m flipping through more pages, a blue flashcard falls out, like the
ones he used to help me study with.

On it, a list.

~~~
1. Children can see them. I’ve noticed babies staring at them with me.
2. If I don’t write the thoughts they put in my head I see them more
frequently. KEEP WRITING THE POEMS.
3. They don’t talk to you unless you stare for too long.
4. DON’T TALK TO THEM. The closest I’ve gotten to a conversation I felt
the darkness they inhabit closing in. Can’t remember how I got out, but
it’s gotten worse since then.
~~~

I flip it around,

~~~
5. Once they’re gone, you’re safe, just don’t ——- (illegible, scratched
out in black)
6. The worst is the sleep paralysis, but as long as I keep looking at it
it doesn’t move towards me. I don’t know what happens if it reaches me.
7. —————- (illegible, scratched out in red so hard it nearly tears
through the card)
8. It always takes the form of someone else in the room, or someone on
your mind. When I’m alone, it’s me.
~~~

I read number 8 again. Then again.

“Sam?”

I slap the journal shut. Mom’s sliding back into the booth.

“What’s that face? You’re white as a sheet…”

“Nothing. Uh- shit! Our order.”

“Ugh- honey, you stay here, I’ll go check.”

I use the opportunity to slide the flashcard in my wallet and the
journal back in my bag.

As I watch my mom walk over to the counter, my eyes drift over to the
entrance.

A group of teenagers is crowded around a table by the window, laughing
at something on a phone. Five of them. No, six. The sixth is standing
where the window meets the wall. Her back to the group. Face to the
corner.

Orange jacket.

What the hell is she still doing there? We ordered like 20 minutes ago.

From this angle I can see she’s not on her phone. Her arms hang straight
down at her sides. Weirdo.

I should look away.

Her head twitches. Just a little. Then, tilts. Like she’s listening to
something inside the wall. She looks so out of place-

“Sam.”

Mom’s hand is on my wrist. I jump.

“What are you staring at?” She looks over at the entrance and her smile
drops. “Your dad used to do that.”

“Do what?”

She opens her mouth, then closes it. “Let’s eat hun.”

When I look back, the teenagers are still laughing. Five of them again,
orange jacket nowhere to be seen.

You’re tripping Sam, let’s just hang with mom.

We finish our burgers and walk out to the car, laughing about our double
dinner, promising not to tell Uncle Tommy.

As we pull out of the lot, I spare a few glances back to the entrance
window, and I swear I can see a hint of orange peeking out at the
corner. Mom puts on a podcast and we listen in silence all the way home.

-–

Back home, I set up in my old room. Emptying my bag, I put the photo
case on my desk and journal on the nightstand. Then I put it in the
drawer. Then, I take it out of the drawer, zip it into my bag, and leave
the bag out in the hallway.

This feels stupid.

I grab some pajamas from the closet, close it, and fall asleep.

~

I wake up to moonlight glaring straight at me.

Man, I thought I would hear more crickets this time of night.

I hear two taps on the wall.

Someone’s standing in the corner.

Orange jacket.

I blink twice, and it’s just mom looking at the now open closet, her
back to me.

Whew. Looking for a sweater, maybe?

“Mom, what are you-”

She twitches. Not a shiver, a hitch, like a video skipping frames.

I try to sit up.

I can’t move.

I try to say her name, but something primal presses my voice back down
my throat.

She looks closer than she was just a second ago.

I can’t move.

My eyes go to the door. When I look back, she’s bigger. Closer.
Eclipsing the moonlight. Still facing away.

Rule six.

I stare. Strain not to blink. My eyes start to burn.

Her shoulders are shaking. Not like crying. Like laughing with no sound.
Without turning around, a whisper leaves her figure

“Sssssaaaaammmmmyyyyyyy”

Rule three.

She’s so close I could touch her if I could move my hand.

I can’t-

The light snaps on.

Mom is in the doorway in her robe, rubbing her eyes.

“Honey? You were calling for me.“

I can move.

I look back towards the corner. Nothing.

“Sorry. Um. Could you turn the heater on? It’s so cold in here.“

“Of course, I’ll be grabbing some water from the kitchen while I’m at
it, do you want a glass?“

I don’t answer. I’m looking at the nightstand where I see the journal I
thought I stashed, open to a blank page, with a red pen lying across it.
