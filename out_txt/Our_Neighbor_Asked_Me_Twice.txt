The first time my neighbor asked if I was new here, I said yes.

The second time, I told her she’d already asked me.

This started in the parking area of an old apartment building. It was
evening, and my cousin and I were sitting there talking about random
things. The building was old. The paint was faded, the balconies looked
worn out, and there were cracks in some of the walls.

Nothing looked scary. It just felt strange. And it was very quiet.

After sitting there for a while, I told my cousin I was going upstairs.

Okay, he said.

I went inside alone.

The stairs weren’t there.

There was just a wall where the staircase was supposed to be.

I stood there for a while because I knew there should be stairs there. I
checked the corridor and walked around, thinking maybe I had entered
from the wrong side.

But I kept coming back to the same wall.

Then suddenly, the stairs were there.

I honestly don’t know how.

I don’t remember finding another entrance or turning into another
corridor. One moment there was a wall, and the next there were stairs.

I went upstairs.

When I entered the flat, something was wrong again.

The furniture wasn’t ours.

There were old sofas and old things that I had never seen before. The
things that were normally there were missing.

I thought I’d entered the wrong flat.

So I went back outside and checked the door.

It was our flat.

I went inside again.

Everything was normal.

Our sofa was there. Our things were there. Everything looked exactly the
way it should.

I just stood there for a moment, trying to understand what had happened.

Then I went out onto the balcony.

From the balcony, I could see through the window into my room.

There was a girl sitting on top of the almirah.

She was sitting there like it was completely normal.

She was cracking her fingers one by one, slowly. She would bend a
finger, crack it, then move to the next one.

I don’t know why, but I couldn’t move.

Then she looked up.

She looked directly at me through the glass.

She didn’t say anything.

She just stared.

And then she disappeared.

I was watching her the entire time.

She didn’t climb down. She didn’t move behind anything.

She was just there one second, and then the top of the almirah was
empty.

I was still standing there when I heard someone at the door.

Knock. Knock.

I opened it.

There was an aunt from the flat next door.

She looked completely normal and friendly.

“Oh… you’re the new neighbors here?”

“Yes,” I said.

She said hi and told me she lived next door. We talked for a little
while, and then she left.

It was a completely normal conversation.

But for some reason, I couldn’t stop thinking about it.

A little later, my cousin came upstairs.

I wanted to tell him everything.

I opened my mouth.

Nothing came out.

I tried again.

Still nothing.

It felt like my mouth was frozen. I knew exactly what I wanted to say,
but I couldn’t get a single word out.

I started getting frustrated and then started crying.

My cousin kept asking me what happened, but I couldn’t explain anything.

Then we heard the door again.

Knock. Knock.

We opened it.

It was the same aunt.

Same clothes.

Same smile.

She looked at us and said,

“Oh… you’re the new neighbors here?”

My voice suddenly came back.

“You already asked me that.”

She looked confused.

“I did?”

“Yes.”

“Oh…”

Then she left.

After we closed the door, I finally told my cousin everything.

I told him about the stairs, the flat changing, and the girl sitting on
top of the almirah.

He didn’t laugh or tell me I was imagining things.

We decided to walk around the apartment and see if we could find
anything strange.

That’s when we saw a large framed photograph hanging in one of the
corridors.

It was a group photograph of the people who lived in the building.

We looked at the building in the photograph first.

It looked much better than the building we were standing in. The walls
were clean, the paint looked fresh, and everything looked well
maintained.

Then we started looking at the people.

And I saw her.

The girl.

It was the same girl I’d seen sitting on top of the almirah.

She was wearing the exact same clothes.

I had never seen her face properly when she was in the flat. She had
been sitting with her body turned away from me, and then she
disappeared.

But when I saw the photograph, I knew it was her.

I pointed at her.

“That’s her.”

My cousin looked at me.

“Who?”

“The girl.

“You saw her face?”

“No.

I looked at the photograph again.

“But that’s her.”

Then I noticed the aunt.

She was in the photograph too.

And she was wearing the exact same clothes she’d been wearing when she
came to our door.

We looked underneath the photograph.

There was a date.

2014.

Neither of us knew what to say.

We went back to the flat.

We were sitting there trying to figure out what was happening when
someone knocked at the gate.

We both froze.

It happened again.

Then the knocking became much harder.

Someone started hitting the door so heavily that I thought it was going
to break.

My hands were shaking so badly that I could hear the keys rattling in my
hand.

“Who is it?” I asked.

There was a pause.

Then a voice came from the other side.

Calm.

Casual.

“Oh… you’re the new neighbors here?”

It was my voice.

My own voice.

The same voice that hadn’t come out when I was trying to tell my cousin
what had happened.

After that, we were completely scared.

We didn’t know what was happening anymore, but we knew something was
seriously wrong with that flat, or with the apartment itself.

We decided we weren’t staying there.

We left the flat and the apartment.

I’ve never forgotten how scared we were that day.
