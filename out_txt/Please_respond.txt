To be honest, I have no idea why I am even writing this or who I am
writing it for. Maybe no one. Maybe myself. Maybe the empty air around
me, the dust floating in the sunlight like tiny ghosts. I keep asking
myself why I bother, but the truth is simple… I need someone, anyone, to
hear me. If you are reading this, then please respond to it. I’ve been
alone for far too long now and have no idea what to do.

My name is Ralph, and I lived in the middle of bum‑fuck nowhere on the
outskirts of a small Middle America town you wouldn’t be able to point
out on a map even if you lived two towns over. We aren’t known for
anything and nothing happens here. It’s a quiet place with nothing to do
but make your own fun, not that I ever really did. I didn’t do much with
my life. I have no education. No job. No friends. Nobody in my life as
it were. I was a total nobody, someone you’d see at random and never
think of again.

I lived in a run‑down trailer I inherited from my uncle after he passed
away. I’m not even sure the land it sits on is legally mine. The place
always smelled faintly of dust, old carpet, and the stale remains of
cheap beer. It wasn’t much, but it was familiar. Most days I didn’t do
anything worth remembering. I’d wake up too late, scroll through
YouTube, eat cereal straight from the box, and wander around town just
to feel like I existed. I watched people talk, laugh, argue, live all
from a distance, like I was behind glass.

Nothing strange happened. No weird lights in the sky. No sirens. No
warnings. No gut feeling telling me the world was about to snap in half.
Just life. Quiet, boring life.

Then it was that morning everything changed. I woke up groggy, rubbed my
eyes, and shuffled to the fridge for milk and cereal. I sat down on the
couch, bowl in hand, and turned on the TV to watch the afternoon news.

There was nothing. And I mean nothing, not even static.

No sound. No picture. Just my own reflection staring back at me from the
dark screen. I flipped through the channels, one after another, faster
and faster, until I was practically stabbing the remote. Every channel
was the same. empty, silent, dead.

I remember sitting there, cereal getting soggy, heart beating a little
too fast. But I shrugged it off. Maybe the cable box died. Maybe the
network was down. Maybe it was just one of those random outages that
happen in small towns where nobody cares enough to fix anything quickly.

I ate. I got dressed. And then I decided to go into town, not because I
needed anything, but because the loneliness gnawed at me in ways hunger
never could.

The moment I stepped outside, something felt wrong. The air was too
still. The wind, usually whispering through the trees, was silent. The
world felt paused, like someone had hit a cosmic freeze‑frame button. I
got into my old Toyota, the clunker I got for my 16th birthday, and
started driving. The engine rattled like it always did, comforting in
its own pathetic way.

Then I saw the first cars.

Parked in the middle of the road. The doors wide open. Engines still
running.

Then another. And another.

By the time I reached town, it looked like a scene from a movie. Cars
laid abandoned, some idling, some with doors hanging open, some crashed
lightly into curbs or lamp posts. Shopping carts left in the middle of
the street. A stroller tipped over on the sidewalk. A coffee cup spilled
on the ground, still half full, as if someone had dropped it mid‑sip.

But no people.

Not one.

Not a voice. Not a footstep. Not even the wind.

Some stores were open, some shut, and just nothing. It felt like time
had stopped. Like the world had exhaled and forgotten to inhale again.

I tried calling the police. No answer.

I tried again. Nothing.

I drove to the station. Empty.

The hospital. Empty.

The fire station. Empty.

Everywhere I went, the same crushing silence.

I don’t know how long I lay on the cold floor of the fire station.
Hours, maybe. My tears echoed in the empty building, each drop sounding
like a tiny explosion in the silence. I remember thinking, I must be
dead, this must be my personal hell.

When I finally pulled myself together, I drove home.  fast, faster than
I had ever pushed that shitbox before. Desperate, I jumped on my
computer and posted everywhere I could: Twitter, Reddit, Facebook,
forums, comment sections, anywhere that someone might have a pulse and
actually respond to tell me I’m not insane for noticing the emptiness
that now surrounds me. But the only responses were bots. Automated
nonsense. Scheduled posts. Empty words from empty programs.

Days passed. I kept trying. Kept hoping. Kept screaming into the void.

Nothing.

“What the fuck do i do?” I thought to myself

Eventually, I gave up.

Acceptance came slowly, like rot. But once it settled in, I realized
something strange. I could do anything. Maybe it was a distraction from
what had become of the world? maybe it was my way of delving deeper into
my delusions?

I robbed a bank.

I stole nice cars and drove them until the wheels came off.

I broke into stores.

I ate whatever I wanted.

I slept wherever I wanted.

I blasted music in empty streets.

I screamed into the sky until my throat bled.

But that wasn’t as fun as it would sound when there is no one to stop
you and nothing to spend the money on. Freedom means nothing when
there’s no one to witness it.

Eventually, the world began to decay. Power grids failed. The Internet
died. Gas pumps stopped working. Stores supplies spoiled. Water systems
faltered. Civilization crumbled without people to maintain it. I tried
generators. I tried siphoning fuel. I tried everything.

But the world was dying, and I was dying with it.

The loneliness became a physical thing.  A weight on my chest, a shadow
behind me, a whisper in my ear. I started talking to myself without
meaning to. Sometimes I’d laugh at nothing, a short, sharp bark of sound
that startled even me. Other times I’d stop mid‑sentence and forget what
I was saying, even though I was only talking to myself.

The silence wasn’t just quiet. It was oppressive. It had weight, almost
as if it was holding me down and choking the air out of my lungs. It
felt like it was leaning in, listening, waiting. I started filling it
with noise. Radios playing static. TVs showing blank screens.
Conversations with empty rooms. I’d talk to the walls, to the furniture,
to the shadows, pretending they were people. Pretending I wasn’t alone.

One night, I woke up convinced someone was standing in my doorway. A
silhouette, tall, unmoving, watching me. My heart hammered so hard I
thought it would burst. I blinked, and the shape dissolved into
darkness. I told myself it was just my imagination, but the fear
lingered like a bad taste.

Another time, I walked through the grocery store and heard footsteps
behind me. Slow, deliberate, matching my pace. I spun around, ready to
see someone. anyone.  but there was nothing. Just the echo of my own
movement bouncing off empty shelves.

I started forgetting things. I’d leave a room and come back minutes
later, confused about why I’d left in the first place. I’d misplace
objects and swear someone had moved them. I’d wake up in different parts
of the trailer without remembering how I got there. Once, I found myself
sitting on the kitchen floor with my back against the fridge, holding a
can of soup I don’t remember picking up.

The loneliness became a physical ache. It sat in my chest like a stone,
heavy and cold. Some days I couldn’t get out of bed. Other days I’d pace
for hours, walking in circles until my legs shook. I’d talk to my
reflection in the mirror, asking it questions, begging it to answer.
Sometimes I’d pretend it did.

There were moments where I felt like I was disappearing. Like my mind
was fraying at the edges. I’d stare at my hands and wonder if they were
really mine. I’d press my nails into my skin just to feel something
real, something that proved I still existed. It wasn’t about hurting
myself.  It was about making sure I wasn’t fading away.

One night, I sat in the middle of the street, surrounded by abandoned
cars, and screamed until my voice broke. I screamed at the sky, at the
empty houses, at the universe. I screamed until I couldn’t breathe. When
I finally stopped, the silence rushed back in, swallowing my voice like
it had never existed. Why was I even screaming? Did I have a reason?
maybe I tried calling out to a god who might no longer exist or who
doesn’t care?

I started imagining conversations. I’d walk through town and pretend
people were still there. I’d say “excuse me” when I passed empty spaces.
I’d nod at shadows like they were neighbors. I’d whisper “good morning”
to no one. It made me feel less alone, even if it was a lie.

Sometimes I’d hear faint noises, a door creaking, a distant cough, a
soft laugh, and I’d freeze. My heart would be racing, hoping it was
real. Although it never was. My mind was filling the void with ghosts.
Visions, empty promises of seeing the one thing I needed. people.

The worst moment came when I found myself talking to someone who wasn’t
there. I was sitting on a bench outside the library, and I started
telling a story,  a stupid story about my uncle’s old truck. Then
halfway through, I realized I was speaking to an empty space beside me,
as if someone were sitting there listening. I even paused, waiting for
them to respond.

When they didn’t, something inside me cracked.

I cried for hours. Not loud sobbing. Just quiet, shaking tears that
wouldn’t stop. I cried until my face hurt, until my throat was raw,
until I felt hollow.

I don’t know how much time has passed since then. Days blur together.
Nights stretch on forever. Do I sleep too much or not at all? I eat when
I remember to. I talk to myself constantly now, not because I want to,
but because the silence feels like it’s trying to swallow me whole.

Maby I am going insane, i dont know. i dont care, with no one here, what
does it really matter. who would miss me, what bad could i do with
ending it? Some say you die twice, once when you physically pass away,
then another when you are forgotten. This time, I was forgotten before
my death. Maybe this is the reason why I am reaching out one more time.
to give it a final try, to have my story written somewhere if people
still exist or come back at some point. 

And now I’m here. In a random library in a town called Poyen. The lights
flicker every few minutes. The air smells like old paper and mold. The
silence is so loud it feels like it’s pressing against my skull. So I’m
writing this because I don’t know what else to do. Because I’m
desperate. Because I’m scared. Because I’m tired. Because I don’t want
to be forgotten.

So. Please, please, please respond whoever you are and wherever you are,
I am desperate.

If no one responds…

If no one is left…

If this reaches no one…

I fear this will be the end of the road for me… 

Every star burns out eventually, I might be the last star in the night
sky, a single spec of light left for everything we know…

Goodbye for now, this might be the last the universe hears from life
