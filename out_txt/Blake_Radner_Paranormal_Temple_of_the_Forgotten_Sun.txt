I’ve had nightmares before. Most of them are memories. 

Some belong to me. Some… Don’t. 

After Black Hollow, I stopped sleeping properly. Every time I closed my
eyes, I saw Walter Briggs standing beneath that enormous underground
chamber, smiling with a face that wasn’t his. 

I saw the black door. The creature behind it. Those impossible fingers
pressing against the stone. And the words the Caretaker had left me
with. Only five remain. 

Five ritual sites. Five places connected by the same symbol. Five pieces
of a puzzle I still didn’t understand. I thought the next clue would
come from the museum. I was wrong. It came from a dream. 

I was standing in a desert. At least… I think it was a desert. 

The sky above me was an impossible shade of red, stretching endlessly
across a landscape of dry earth and black volcanic rock. There was no
wind. No birds. No insects. Nothing moved. 

In the distance stood an enormous stone temple. It rose from the desert
like the remains of a dead civilization. Steep steps climbed toward a
massive doorway. Stone carvings covered every surface. 

Some depicted warriors. Others showed priests holding strange objects
above their heads. But every carving eventually showed the same thing. 

The Black Door. And beneath it… The creature. The goat-headed thing I’d
seen beneath Black Hollow. Its hands stretched outward. Long fingers.
Too many joints. Its head bent at an unnatural angle. 

And surrounding it… People. Hundreds of them. Kneeling. Worshipping.
Waiting. 

I started walking toward the temple. I don’t remember deciding to. My
feet simply moved. Step after step. 

The sand beneath my boots wasn’t hot. It was cold. Dead cold. As I
reached the first stone staircase, something appeared beside me. A small
object sitting in the sand. A carved stone figure. It was no larger than
my hand. 

An ancient Aztec-style totem. Its face was twisted into an expression
somewhere between anger and grief. 

I picked it up. The moment my fingers touched the stone… Everything
changed. 

I was eight years old. I was standing in my childhood bedroom. I could
smell the old carpet. Hear rain against the window. See the small wooden
desk where I’d done my homework every evening. 

My mother was downstairs. I could hear her talking on the phone. I
looked at the bedroom door. It was slightly open. 

Something stood in the hallway. A man. Tall. Completely motionless. I
couldn’t see his face. He raised one hand. His fingers bent strangely.
Too many joints. I screamed. 

The memory ended. 

I was back outside the temple. The totem was still in my hand. I dropped
it. It landed in the sand. A voice whispered behind me. 

“You remember.” I turned. Nobody. Then another memory. 

I was sixteen. Sitting in a hospital waiting room. My father was beside
me. His face was pale. He was holding my hand. A doctor walked toward
us. I couldn’t hear what he said. But I knew. 

My father squeezed my hand. Then… He disappeared. Not died. Disappeared.
The memory fractured. 

I was suddenly older. 

Twenty-two. Standing outside an abandoned church. Then thirty.
Investigating my first real haunting. Then…  

Deadridge. Room 366. The hand on my shoulder. The Host. 

Marshal Manor. The ritual book. 

Blackwood House. Margaret. 

Black Hollow. Walter. The black door. 

Every memory flashed before me. Not in order. Not naturally. Like
someone was searching through my life. Looking for something. Then the
memories stopped. 

I stood before the temple again. The stone totem floated in front of me.
Slowly… It rotated. Symbols appeared across its surface. 

The three interlocking circles. The cult symbol. Then a fourth circle
appeared around them. Broken. The same mark I’d seen beneath Black
Hollow. The voice returned. “You have opened three.” 

I looked toward the temple. “What?” 

“You have closed one.” The black door appeared in the stone entrance.
“You have carried three.” Images flashed before me. 

The brass key. The Book of the Last Congregation. Margaret. Then… A
fourth object. The little stone totem. “You will carry four.” 

I stepped backward. “I don’t want it.” The voice laughed. “You never
did.” 

The temple doors opened. Darkness waited beyond them. Then… A face
appeared inside the doorway. Mine. Older. Much older. My hair was grey,
my face was covered in scars. And I was smiling. Far too widely. I woke
screaming. 

I sat upright in bed. My room was dark. For several seconds I didn’t
know where I was. 

My breathing came fast. My sheets were soaked with sweat. I looked at
the clock. 4:17 a.m. 

The museum was silent. I listened. Nothing. No footsteps. No voices. No
knocking. I rubbed my eyes. Then looked at my right hand. 

My fingers were curled tightly around something. I slowly opened them.
Nothing. Just my own hand. I laughed nervously. “A dream.” I got out of
bed. Walked to the bathroom. Turned on the light, then stared at myself
in the mirror. 

Everything looked normal. Except… There was dirt beneath my fingernails.
Dark red sand. I hadn’t been anywhere. I washed my hands. The water ran
brown for several seconds before turning clear. 

I stared at the sink. Then at my reflection. For just a moment… My
reflection wasn’t looking at me. It was looking behind me. I turned.
Nothing. When I looked back… The reflection smiled. I didn’t. Then it
was normal again. 

I didn’t go back to sleep. At seven in the morning, I started my usual
museum rounds. The Blake Radner Occult Museum had become a strange kind
of home. A dangerous one.  But still a home. 

I checked the security systems. Everything normal. Vault A-01. DR-001:
Deadridge Hotel Room 366 Key. Secure. 

Vault B-01. MM-001: The Book of the Last Congregation. Secure. 

Vault C-01. BH-001: Margaret. Secure. 

I stopped outside Margaret’s display. She was sitting exactly where I’d
left her. Her porcelain face looked toward the museum entrance. I
frowned. I had placed her facing the opposite direction. 

“Morning, Margaret.” Her head didn’t move. I continued. 

Something felt different throughout the museum. I couldn’t explain it.
The air seemed heavier. The artifacts felt… Awake. I reached the museum
office and pulled out the copy of the cult map. 

It had become my most valuable piece of evidence. Nine circles. Three
marked. Six remaining. 

I laid it flat across my desk. The three known locations were still
visible. Black Hollow. Edinburgh. Marshal Manor. 

I studied the lines between them. Nothing. No obvious pattern. No
coordinates. No indication of what came next. 

Then… The ink moved. I froze. A thin black line slowly appeared across
the paper. It traveled south. Across the Atlantic. Then across the Gulf
of Mexico. It stopped. 

The map began glowing faintly. A new circle appeared. Mexico. 

I stood so quickly my chair fell backward. “No…” The circle darkened. A
tiny symbol appeared at its center. A stone temple. The same temple from
my dream. I stared at it. 

The location hadn’t been there yesterday. I checked the original
photograph of the map. The circle wasn’t there. I checked again. 

The new marking remained. Then a second line appeared. It connected
Mexico… To Black Hollow. Then to Edinburgh. Then Marshal Manor. Then the
Deadridge. 

All of them. The locations weren’t random. They formed a network. A
path. Something was guiding me from one site to another. And I suddenly
realized the most frightening part. The map wasn’t showing me where the
cult had been. It was showing me where they were going. 

I searched the museum archives for the temple symbol. Nothing. I
searched historical databases. Nothing. Ancient Mexican temples. Aztec
sites. Mesoamerican ruins. Hundreds of results. None matched. 

Then I remembered the dream. The stone totem. I opened my artifact
catalog. A new entry had appeared. 

I hadn’t written it. MT-001 

Object: Unknown Aztec-style stone totem. 

Location: Mexico. 

Status: Awaiting recovery. 

I stared at the screen. There was no photograph. No measurements. No
historical information. Only one sentence beneath the entry. 

IT HAS ALREADY SEEN YOU. 

I leaned back slowly. The office suddenly felt very cold. From Vault
B-01… I heard a page turn. Then another. Then another. 

I looked toward the corridor. The Book of the Last Congregation was
turning its own pages. I didn’t move. The sound continued. Page. Page.
Page. Then silence. 

I walked to the vault. The book was open. A map filled the page. Not the
map I’d seen before. A new one. Mexico. And beneath it… A single
sentence. 

THE FORGOTTEN TEMPLE WAITS. I stared at those words for a long time.
Then I looked toward the locked museum entrance. 

The morning sunlight poured through the glass doors. For a brief moment,
everything looked normal. But I knew better now. 

The cult wasn’t calling me. The artifacts weren’t calling me. The map
wasn’t calling me. Something much older was. 

And somewhere in Mexico… A temple was waiting for me to arrive. 

 

 

 

 

 

 

I didn’t book the flight immediately. That would’ve been the sensible
thing to do. Instead, I spent the entire morning trying to convince
myself that I wasn’t being manipulated. 

The map had changed. The book had changed. A location in Mexico had
appeared where there had previously been nothing. And I’d dreamt about
the exact same place hours earlier. 

I needed to know whether the dream had been a warning… Or an
invitation. 

I started with the obvious. The symbol. I photographed the new circle on
the map and enlarged it on my computer. The image became grainy, but the
details remained clear enough to identify a rough geographical
position. 

Southern Mexico. Away from the major tourist routes. Somewhere in a
heavily forested region. I searched for temples in the surrounding area.
Dozens appeared. Most were well documented. Archaeological sites. Ruins.
Tourist attractions. None looked like the temple from my dream. 

I searched again. Ancient ceremonial sites. Unregistered ruins. Lost
Aztec temples. Abandoned archaeological structures. Nothing. 

Then I searched for the three-circle symbol. That produced even less. A
few unrelated occult websites. Several modern symbols. Nothing
historical. Nothing connected to Mexico. 

I closed the laptop. “Of course.” The map had given me a destination…
But not an address. 

 I returned to Vault B-01. The Book of the Last Congregation remained
open. The Mexican location was still marked. I leaned closer. There was
something beneath the circle. Tiny writing. So faint I’d missed it
before. I used a magnifying glass. Three words. 

Where the sun dies. I stared at them. Then another line appeared. Follow
the stone. 

I stepped backward. The ink continued spreading across the page. A rough
drawing formed beneath the words. A small carved figure. The same totem
I’d seen in my dream. 

I didn’t touch the book. I didn’t need to. I knew what it wanted. 

 

Two days later, I was on a flight to Mexico. I brought only what I
considered essential. Camera. Audio recorder. Thermal imaging equipment.
EMF meter. Flashlights. Extra batteries. 

Father Donovan’s Cross and a reinforced artifact case. 

The last item worried me more than anything. I had no idea what I was
going to find. But I knew I was bringing something back. The map had
made that clear. 

 

The journey took me through several towns before I finally reached the
region indicated by the map. The landscape changed dramatically. 

Dense jungle replaced open roads. Ancient stone structures appeared
among the trees. Small villages sat between stretches of forest. People
were friendly. Curious. But when I mentioned the temple… The atmosphere
changed. 

Most people simply shook their heads. One elderly man refused to speak
about it. Another told me I had the wrong place. Then, just before
sunset, I found someone willing to talk. 

His name was Mateo. He owned a small roadside café. When I showed him
the photograph of the symbol… He dropped his cup. It shattered across
the floor. 

“You shouldn’t have that.” He said, worried, “I don’t.” I pointed toward
the image. 

“I found it somewhere else.” He stared at the symbol. “Where?” 

“America.” His expression hardened. “Then it has followed you.” 

 

Mateo refused to discuss the temple inside his café. Instead, he led me
outside. The sun was disappearing behind the jungle. “There’s an old
story,” he said. “About a temple hidden deep in the forest.” 

“People used to go there?” I asked, “Long ago.” 

“Aztecs?” He shook his head. “Older stories say it was already there
before them.” That caught my attention. 

“What’s it called?” He looked toward the trees. “Nobody agrees.” 

“What do you call it?”  

“The Temple of the Forgotten Sun.” The name sent a chill through me. It
was the same name I’d written in my investigation notes after waking
from the dream. 

I’d never told anyone. Not even myself. 

“People disappear there,” Mateo continued. “How many?” 

“Enough.” 

“When was the last time?” He hesitated. “Two months.” 

“Who?” I looked at the man. He hesitated. “Three men.” 

“Archaeologists?” 

“No.” He answered, “Tourists?” He shook his head. 

“Men looking for something.” 

“What?” Mateo looked directly at me. “A stone god.” 

My pulse quickened. “The totem?” He nodded. “They said it was small.” 

“Where is it?” I saked  

“We don’t know.” 

“Then why do you think it’s there?” 

“Because they found the entrance.” 

“And?” 

“They went inside.” 

“They never returned.” 

 

Mateo pointed toward the jungle. “You should leave before dark.” 

“I’ve been advised of that before.” 

“This isn’t a joke.” 

“I know.” 

“No.” He stepped closer. 

“You don’t.” His voice dropped. 

“The temple doesn’t haunt people.” 

“What does it do?” 

“It remembers them.” 

I didn’t answer. He continued. “It shows you things… Your memories… Your
regrets.” 

“Things you thought were forgotten.” I thought about the dream. My
childhood bedroom. My father. The hospital. The strange man in the
hallway. 

“You’ve been there.” Mateo nodded. “Once.” 

“What did you see?” He looked away. “My brother.” 

“Is he dead?” I asked  

“No.” That answer stopped me. 

“Where is he?” Mateo stared into the jungle. “Inside.” 

 

I left the café shortly afterward. My hotel was another forty minutes
away. The sun had completely disappeared. As I drove through the
darkness, I couldn’t stop thinking about what Mateo had said. 

It remembers. The demon in my dream hadn’t simply shown me random
memories. It had selected them. It knew things about me. Things I had
never told anyone. And if it could reach into my memories… What else
could it reach? 

 

I arrived at the hotel just after midnight. The building was small.
Clean. Nothing like the places I normally investigated. I checked into
my room and locked the door behind me. 

Then I placed the artifact case on the desk. Empty. Waiting. I unpacked
my equipment. Set up two cameras. Checked the batteries. Then I sat on
the edge of the bed. For several minutes… Nothing happened. 

Then I heard something outside. A soft scraping sound. I looked toward
the window. Nothing. The sound came again. 

Scrape…     Scrape…     Scrape. 

I slowly approached the curtains. 

Before I touched them… A small object appeared on the windowsill. I
froze. It was the stone totem. The exact same one from my dream. I
hadn’t brought it with me. I hadn’t found it. Yet there it was. 

Standing upright. Facing me. I stared at it for several seconds. Then
the stone face changed. 

Not physically. Its expression simply seemed… Angrier. A voice whispered
from somewhere inside the room. “You came.” 

The lights went out. I reached for my flashlight. Before I could switch
it on… I saw the temple. 

Not in my mind. In the darkness. Right in front of me. The enormous
stone steps. The black doorway. The goat-headed creature. And those
impossible hands. Then the vision vanished. 

The lights returned. The totem was gone. I stood alone in the room. 

My heart pounding. On the desk, the map I’d brought from the museum had
unfolded itself. The Mexican circle was glowing faintly. A line had
appeared beneath it. SUNRISE. I looked toward the window. 

The eastern horizon was beginning to brighten. Whatever waited inside
that jungle… It wanted me there in the morning. And for the first time…
I wasn’t sure I wanted to know why. 

 

 

The jungle didn’t want me there. I knew that before I reached the
temple. 

The road ended just after sunrise. From there, I continued on foot.
Mateo had drawn a rough map on the back of a napkin before I left the
hotel. He’d circled one area in red. 

Then he’d written four words beneath it. Do not follow voices. I hadn’t
asked him what that meant. I was beginning to understand. 

 

The jungle was unnaturally quiet. Birds called occasionally somewhere in
the canopy, but the deeper I went, the less I heard. No insects. No
monkeys. No rustling leaves. Only my own footsteps. 

The humidity was suffocating. Sweat ran down my back as I pushed through
thick vines and tangled roots. Every few minutes I checked my compass.
It kept pointing in the same direction. 

North. 

I was supposed to be traveling southeast. I stopped. Pulled the compass
from my pocket. The needle spun. Then settled. North. 

I turned around. The needle turned with me. I laughed quietly. “Very
funny.” Nothing answered. 

 

The first sign of the temple appeared twenty minutes later. A stone.
Half-buried beneath roots. Carved into its surface was the three-circle
symbol. 

I crouched beside it. The carving was ancient the center circle had been
scratched recently. Three fresh lines ran through it. I photographed it.
Then continued. 

Another stone appeared. Then another. Soon I realized they formed a
path. Not a natural trail. 

A deliberate one. Every stone carried the same symbol. The deeper I
walked… The older the carvings became. Eventually the jungle opened. And
I saw the temple. 

 

It was exactly as I’d seen it in the dream. A massive stepped pyramid
rose from the trees. Roots wrapped around its lower levels. Vines
covered the upper terraces. The stone had turned almost black with age. 

At the summit stood a small ceremonial structure. Its entrance was
completely open. No doors. No guards. No signs. Just darkness. 

I stood at the bottom of the steps. My body camera was recording. The
air smelled of wet earth. And something else. Smoke. Fresh smoke.
Someone was inside. 

 

The first steps were covered in carvings. Warriors. Priests.
Sacrifices. 

The usual imagery you’d expect from an ancient temple. Then the carvings
changed. The people weren’t worshipping an Aztec god. They were
worshipping the Black Door. The creature stood before it. 

Goat head. Massive horns. Long arms. Hands with too many fingers. Its
body was almost human. Almost. 

I stopped halfway up the staircase. The carving was identical to the one
beneath Black Hollow. There was no doubt anymore. The cult hadn’t
borrowed the imagery. They had discovered the same thing. 

Across continents. Across centuries. The same entity. The same door. The
same symbol. 

 

I reached the top. The ceremonial chamber was small compared to the
underground halls. A stone altar stood in the center. Burned candles
surrounded it. 

Some were ancient. Others… Were still warm. I crouched beside one. Fresh
wax. 

Someone had been here recently. I looked toward the rear wall. A narrow
opening disappeared into darkness. A tunnel. “Great,” I sighed 

I switched on my flashlight. The beam revealed steps descending into the
temple. The walls were covered in writing. Some of it was in ancient
glyphs. 

Other sections had been carved much later. Spanish. English. Latin.
Hundreds of years of visitors had added their own messages. 

Most were prayers. Warnings. Names. One sentence had been carved in
English. WE FOUND THE DOOR Below it… Another. IT FOUND US FIRST 

I stared at the words. Then heard footsteps behind me. 

 

I turned. Nobody. The chamber was empty. I checked the entrance.
Nothing. Then I heard a child laughing. A little girl. The same laugh
I’d heard in the nursery at Blackwood House. I froze. “Margaret?” I said
softly. Silence. 

The laughter came again. From the tunnel. I remembered Mateo’s warning.
Do not follow voices. I turned away. 

The laughter stopped. Then… A man’s voice spoke behind me. My father’s
voice. “Blake.” 

My heart stopped. I hadn’t heard that voice in years. I turned. Nobody.
“Blake…” It came from the tunnel. My father’s voice. Perfectly
reproduced. “Come here.” I stepped toward the opening. Then stopped. 

No. It wasn’t him. It couldn’t be. Whatever lived inside this place had
already shown me my memories. It knew exactly which voices would make me
move. I backed away. The voice changed. My mother’s. Then Walter’s. Then
Angus. Then Emily. Then… My own. 

“Blake…” The voice whispered. “You already know the way.” 

 

The stone totem appeared on the altar. I hadn’t seen anyone place it
there. One moment the altar was empty. The next… It stood upright. The
little Aztec figure from my dream. I approached cautiously. The moment I
looked into its carved eyes… The temple disappeared. 

 

I was back in the Deadridge Hotel. Room 366. The bed. The curtains. The
wallpaper. Everything exactly as I’d remembered. But something was
different. The Host wasn’t standing behind me. Someone else was. 

A hooded figure. He removed his mask. I couldn’t see his face. Instead…
I saw Walter. Young, twenty-seven years old. Standing inside the same
room. He was speaking to someone. 

A group of six people. The same six from the 1963 photograph. They
weren’t guests. They were performing a ritual. The brass key sat on the
table. One of the cult members placed his hand over it. Then… The memory
changed. 

Marshal Manor. The ritual chamber. The book. 

Blackwood House. Margaret.  

Black Hollow. The farmhouse. 

Every location. Every artifact. Every ritual. 

I watched them unfold in seconds. Then I saw something I’d never seen
before. A new location. A massive stone structure buried beneath a
mountain. Nine circles surrounded a black doorway. 

Nine objects rested on nine altars. Eight were visible. The ninth altar
was empty. Then the vision shifted. The empty altar. The same symbol.
And a silhouette standing before it. Me. 

 

I screamed. The temple returned. I collapsed against the altar. The
stone totem was still there. But now… It was warm. I pulled my hand
away. 

A voice spoke directly into my mind. You have carried them. I looked
around. “Who are you?” 

The answer came immediately. The one behind the door. The chamber shook.
Dust fell from the ceiling. The entrance disappeared behind a wall of
darkness. The totem began glowing. 

The three-circle symbol appeared across my palm. Burning. I screamed
again. Then… Everything stopped. 

 

The darkness receded. The entrance returned. The temple was silent. The
totem had stopped glowing. But the mark remained on my hand. A perfect
black circle surrounding three smaller circles. I stared at it. Then I
heard footsteps outside. Not one person. Several. I reached for my
flashlight. Voices whispered from the jungle below. 

Men. Women. Dozens of them. They were coming up the temple steps. I
moved toward the entrance. At the bottom of the staircase… Figures were
appearing between the trees. Black coats. Black robes. Silver rings. The
Covenant had found me. 

Or perhaps… They’d been waiting for me. I looked down at the stone
totem. The cursed object I’d come for. I picked it up. The moment I did…
Every figure below stopped. 

And somewhere beneath the temple… Something opened its eyes. 

 

 

The figures at the bottom of the temple steps didn’t move. Neither did
I. For several seconds, we simply stared at one another. There were at
least twenty of them. 

Black robes. Dark coats. Silver rings. Some wore the cracked white
porcelain masks I’d seen beneath Black Hollow. Others had uncovered
faces. Ordinary people. 

That somehow made them worse. They weren’t monsters. They were
believers. And they had come for the totem. I tightened my grip around
the small stone object. One of the figures stepped forward. 

An elderly woman. Her grey hair was braided tightly behind her head. She
looked up at me. 

“Give it back.” I laughed nervously. “I just got here.” 

“You don’t understand what you’re carrying.” Another man said from
behind the woman. 

“I’ve heard that before.” She took another step. “You’ve carried three.”
My stomach tightened. “And now you’ve taken the fourth.” The others
began moving up the stairs. Slowly. Together. 

 

The stone totem suddenly became warm in my hand. Then hot. I nearly
dropped it. The carved face seemed to twist. Its mouth opened but no
sound came out. But the temple answered. 

A deep rumble rolled through the stone beneath my feet. The figures
below stopped. The elderly woman looked frightened. Not at me. At the
totem. 

“Put it down!” She shouted at me. “Why?” 

“Because it knows you now.” I stared at her. “That’s the problem.” 

 

The temple walls began shaking. Dust fell from the ceiling. The black
markings carved into the stone glowed faintly. The three-circle symbol
appeared everywhere. On the walls. The floor. The altar. Even across my
own hand. Then… A voice entered my mind. 

BLAKE. I stumbled backward. The voice wasn’t male or female. It was
enormous. Like hearing a mountain speak. YOU HAVE RETURNED. “I’ve never
been here.” A pause. 

Then laughter. You have. The world disappeared. I was standing in the
temple again. But it wasn’t ancient. It was new. The stone was clean.
The jungle hadn’t reclaimed it. Hundreds of people filled the terraces. 

The Covenant. But they weren’t wearing black robes. They wore simple
white clothing. Priests. Families. Children. At the center of the temple
stood the Black Door. And before it… The creature. The goat-headed
figure. Its hands rested against the door. Its fingers stretched across
the stone. 

The people were chanting. Then I saw something impossible. A man stepped
forward carrying a small stone totem. He placed it on the altar. The
creature turned toward him. And the vision changed. 

 

I was somewhere else. Ancient. Older than the temple. Older than the
civilization that built it. 

A group of people stood around a fire. One of them held the same totem.
They weren’t worshipping the creature. They were afraid of it. The
leader of the group raised the totem above his head. The Black Door
appeared behind him. The creature pushed against it. The door began
opening. 

The people screamed. Then the leader smashed the totem against the
ground. The vision shattered. 

 

I returned to the temple. I was on my knees. The stone totem lay beside
me. My palm was bleeding. The black symbol had disappeared. The Covenant
members had stopped climbing. The elderly woman stared at me. 

“You saw it.” I nodded. “What did you see?” 

“The beginning.” She looked toward the sky. “No.” 

Her voice trembled. “You saw what they want you to believe.” 

 

The temple suddenly went dark. Every torch was extinguished. The
Covenant members screamed. Something moved beneath the stone. A massive
impact shook the entire structure. 

The altar cracked. The floor split. And from the darkness beneath the
temple… A hand emerged. Grey skin. Long fingers. Too many joints.
Exactly like the creature from the murals. 

The elderly woman fell to her knees. “The Forgotten Sun…” She whispered.
“He’s awake.” 

The hand gripped the edge of the broken stone. Then another appeared.
The creature began pulling itself upward. 

I raised my flashlight. The beam revealed the goat-like head. Massive,
curved horns. Deep black eyes. A mouth filled with rows of human teeth. 

Its body was far too tall for the chamber. Its shoulders scraped the
ceiling. And yet… It kept climbing. 

 

The Covenant members began chanting. Not in fear. In worship. 

“Open the gate.” “Open the gate.” “Open the gate.” 

The creature’s head turned toward them. Then toward me. Its eyes locked
onto mine. And suddenly… I was somewhere else. 

 

I was standing in my museum. The Blake Radner Occult Museum. But it
wasn’t the museum I knew. It was abandoned. Dust covered every display
case. The lights flickered. The artifacts were gone. All of them. Except
one. The stone totem. 

It rested on the floor in the center of the main exhibition hall. I
walked toward it. Then heard footsteps behind me. 

I turned. Walter stood there. Older. Scarred. His face covered in blood.
“You’re too late.” 

“What happened?” He pointed toward the museum entrance. 

I looked. The doors were open. Beyond them… There was no city. No
street. No world. Only darkness. 

And nine enormous circles burned across the ground. One by one… They
began disappearing. 

Nine. Eight. Seven. Six. Five. Four. Three. Two… One. 

Walter looked at me. “That’s why they needed you.” The vision ended. 

 

I was back inside the temple. The creature was gone. The Covenant had
disappeared. The temple was silent. 

Only the stone totem remained. I looked around. “Where did they go?” No
answer. 

Then I noticed the elderly woman standing at the edge of the chamber.
She was alone. Her mask lay broken on the floor. “You were never
supposed to see that.” 

“See what?” I asked, confused. “The ending.” 

“What ending?” She stared at the totem. “The one where you open the
final door.” 

I shook my head. “I’m not opening anything.” She smiled sadly. “You
don’t get to choose anymore.” Then she stepped backward. Her body
dissolved into black smoke. Gone. 

 

I stood alone. The temple began collapsing. Stone rained from the
ceiling. The floor cracked beneath my feet. 

I grabbed the totem and ran. I sprinted down the steps as the upper
chamber collapsed behind me. The jungle erupted with noise. Birds
scattered from the trees. Branches snapped. Dust rolled down the temple
steps. I reached the bottom just as the entire top of the pyramid
disappeared beneath a cloud of debris. 

I stopped. Breathing heavily. 

The jungle was silent again. Then I looked at the totem. It was cold.
Completely still. Just an ordinary piece of stone. Except for the symbol
carved into its chest. 

Three circles. And beneath them… A tiny fourth circle. Complete. 

 

I returned to the hotel before sunrise. I packed everything. I didn’t
sleep. 

The next morning I drove toward the airport. The totem sat inside its
reinforced artifact case beside me. For the first time… It didn’t feel
like a cursed object. It felt like a key. And that thought terrified
me. 

 

 

I knew something was wrong the moment I walked back into the museum.
There are different kinds of silence. The silence of an empty building. 

The silence after something terrible has happened. And the silence of a
place that is waiting. The Blake Radner Occult Museum had always been
quiet after closing. 

That night… It felt like it was holding its breath. I stood just inside
the entrance with the stone totem locked inside its reinforced case. The
museum lights hummed above me. Every display was exactly where I’d left
it. The brass key. The ritual book. Margaret. 

Nothing appeared disturbed. Yet I couldn’t shake the feeling that
someone was standing somewhere in the darkness. Watching. 

I checked the security cameras. All operational. I checked the vault
locks. All secure. Then I looked at the clock. 

11:47 p.m. I had been home less than twenty minutes. Already… I wanted
to leave. 

I carried the totem into the artifact preparation room. The object was
small enough to fit inside both hands. 

Rough grey stone. Carved face. Three interlocking circles. And the
fourth circle beneath them. 

I placed it on the examination table. My hands still felt cold from
holding it. I photographed it from every angle. Measured it. Weighed it.
Recorded the carvings. Then I began cataloguing. 

I opened the museum database and typed: 

Artifact Number: MT-001 

Origin: Mexico 

Recovered from: Temple of the Forgotten Sun 

Object Type: Ceremonial Stone Totem 

Estimated Age: Unknown 

Associated Entity: Unknown Goat-Headed Entity 

I stopped. Then added: 

 

Observed Properties: 

-   Produces vivid visions and apparent memories. 
-   Has demonstrated possible awareness of observer. 
-   Appears capable of transmitting memories without physical contact. 
-   Associated with the Covenant of the Hollow Gate. 
-   Connected to the Black Door. 
-   Potentially functions as a ritual key. 

I stared at the final sentence. Then typed: Threat Level: Extreme I
saved the entry. 

The database confirmed: CATALOGUE COMPLETE. 

I placed the totem inside its new display case. Locked it. Then locked
the outer security cabinet. Two locks. Three cameras. One artifact.
Contained. 

At least… That’s what I told myself. 

 

I walked through the museum one final time before going upstairs. 

Vault A. The brass key hadn’t moved. 

Vault B. The Book of the Last Congregation was closed. 

Vault C. Margaret sat quietly in her chair. 

Vault D.  The Mexican totem remained exactly where I’d placed it. 

Everything was secure. I switched off the exhibition lights. The museum
fell into darkness. I climbed the stairs to my apartment above the
museum. Locked the door. Checked it twice. Then went to bed. 

 

I woke at 2:13 a.m. Three loud bangs shook the building. I sat upright.
For several seconds I didn’t move. Another bang. Then another. 

BANG. BANG. BANG. BANG. BANG. Someone was hitting the front door of the
museum. 

I reached for the flashlight beside my bed. The banging stopped.
Silence. I listened. 

Nothing. Then… Three knocks. Much slower this time. 

Knock…     Knock…     Knock… 

I looked at the clock. 2:13. The same hour the museum had experienced
disturbances before. I got out of bed. The floorboards creaked beneath
my feet. I reached for the hallway light. 

It didn’t turn on. The museum below was completely dark. Then the
knocking started again. Harder. Frantic. Someone was pounding on the
front entrance. 

I grabbed the iron knife from the bedside drawer. Then slowly descended
the stairs. 

Halfway down… I heard breathing. Not mine. Deep. Ragged. Coming from the
other side of the museum’s front door. I froze. The door was reinforced
steel and glass. No ordinary person could have damaged it. Yet the glass
was beginning to shake inside its frame. 

BANG. BANG. BANG. I raised the flashlight. The beam illuminated the
entrance. Nothing. 

The knocking stopped. I took another step. Then something struck the
glass. 

A hand… Grey… Long. 

The fingers were impossibly thin. Each one bent at far too many joints.
My blood turned cold. I knew those hands. I’d seen them carved into the
temple. I’d seen them beneath Black Hollow. I’d seen them pressing
against the Black Door. 

The creature was standing outside my museum.  

 

It slowly lifted its head. The flashlight illuminated its face.
Goat-like. Ancient. Horns curling backward. Black eyes. A mouth filled
with rows of human teeth. 

It stared directly at me through the glass. Then… It smiled. 

I couldn’t move. It raised one hand and placed its palm against the
door. The three-circle symbol appeared beneath its skin. Glowing red.
The same symbol burned across my hand in Mexico. The demon tilted its
head. And spoke. Not aloud. Inside my mind. 

Keeper. I backed away. “No.” Its smile widened. You carry what belongs
to us. I tightened my grip on the knife. “You’re not getting it.” The
demon laughed. 

The sound wasn’t loud. But every display case in the museum began
vibrating. The brass key spun violently inside Vault A. Margaret’s
porcelain head snapped toward the entrance. The Book of the Last
Congregation opened by itself. 

Pages began turning rapidly. Then… The demon disappeared. The front door
stood completely untouched. No footprints. No damage. Nothing. Only
silence. 

 

I stood there for several minutes. Waiting. Nothing happened. Eventually
I forced myself back upstairs. I locked the apartment door. Then the
bedroom door. I placed the knife beside the bed. 

I sat down. My hands were shaking. I looked toward the window. Nothing.
Toward the door. Nothing. I finally lay down. I didn’t sleep. I stared
at the ceiling until exhaustion took over. 

 

I woke just before dawn. For a moment… Everything seemed normal. 

The room was quiet. Grey morning light filtered through the curtains. I
reached for my phone Then stopped. Something was sitting on the bedside
table. My blood ran cold. 

The stone totem. I stared at it. I hadn’t brought it upstairs. I had
locked it inside the museum. Behind two locks. Inside a reinforced case.
I slowly reached toward it. The moment my fingers touched the stone… A
voice whispered inside my head. 

Five remain. 

I pulled my hand away. The totem remained perfectly still. I looked
toward the museum door. Locked. I looked back at the artifact. Then I
understood. 

The creature hadn’t come to take the totem. It had come to remind me…
That it didn’t need to. It could bring the totem to me whenever it
wanted. I sat there in the pale morning light, staring at the cursed
object. 

Five locations remained. 2 more doors. And something on the other side
had finally learned where I lived.  

I used to believe the museum was my sanctuary. It isn’t. It’s a beacon.
Every object I bring here makes the signal stronger. Every case brings
me closer to whatever waits behind the Black Door. Tonight, something
came to the front door. 

It knew my name. It knew what I had taken. And somehow… It got inside
without opening the door. I don’t know what happens when all nine
locations are found. I don’t know what the Covenant is trying to
accomplish. But I know one thing now. The map isn’t guiding me toward
the cult. 

The cult is guiding me toward the map. And I’m beginning to think… They
aren’t trying to stop me from finding the nine locations. They’re
counting on me finding them.
