I woke up surrounded by a smothering blackness and utterly unable to
breathe. It wasn’t solid at all, and yet it had rendered me completely
immobile. Paralyzed me.

And it was sucking the life out of me with every moment.

And the smell of black coffee was overwhelming the room. You could
almost go so far as to call it an odor with how thick it was.

Right before things went to black, I bolted upwards, absolutely soaked
in sweat.

The black shadow was gone.

I was awake now. That much was certain.

But had I been before?

_________

We sold the coffee machine the next day. I was honestly surprised we
hadn’t sold it yet. Nothing but bad reminders.

After we sold it, I had maybe the best night of sleep I’ve ever had. At
the very least, the best I’d slept in a while.

_________

Things were great for about a week. Rick managed to snag me a
construction job. I’d only be making about half of every paycheck, but
at least I’d be consistently chipping away at my debts.

For the next six days, I slept like a baby through the night.

_________

On the night of the seventh day, I awoke to the sound of footsteps
downstairs that a cold feeling in my stomach told me did not belong to
my father.

I lay there for a few minutes, listening to them make their way around
the bottom floor.

Then I got up and grabbed the baseball bat. Opened the door as slowly
and silently as I possibly could. Began making my way down the hall,
then down the stairs, half a step at a time.

By now, the footsteps had stopped, and my heart fluttered as I imagined
someone waiting around a corner, ready to ambush me.

I could hear no breathing, no creaking floorboards that might indicate
someone shifting their position. Briefly, I wondered if the footsteps
even belonged to a human.

I made my way around the corner, into the hall that led into our living
room, and that was when I saw it.

Illuminated by the standby light of the TV, seated on the couch with
their back to me, was the shape of a human.

Not doing anything… just sitting there. As if waiting for me.

I got closer, and I was able to make out more of the figure seated in
front of me.

As I did, the fear pulsating through me was slowly replaced by a hot,
indignant anger as I realized the silhouette matched almost perfectly
with that of Wife Beater. Thought back to that voicemail he’d sent me.

“We know where you live.”

I had set up a plan with Rick to pay the debt off. I was doing
everything I could. And yet, that apparently still wasn’t enough.

What more did he want me to do?

And so, I crept closer, and closer.

Once I was maybe a foot away, I raised the bat over my head. Remembered
the way he had so easily beaten me up in the parking lot after my mom’s
funeral.

I took one more step forward.

And brought the bat down on his head with everything I had in me.

Crunch.

I felt his skull immediately cave in underneath the bat. Blood and gore
spurted upwards, coating my hands and the bat. I felt something slimy
under my nails that I think might have been brain matter.

And then he fell forward, slumping face down on the wooden floor, and I
realized with a fresh wave of horror that the body attached to the head
did not, in fact resemble that of Wife Beater at all. The build actually
looked much, much more familiar.

Nausea washed through me as I scrambled for the light switch, flicked it
on, followed by tears as I then made for the body.

I fell to my knees, felt my body go completely numb as I flipped it
over.

The body did not belong to Wife Beater.

Nor did it belong to Rick.

Hurting my father with my previous actions had damn near tore me open.

As I looked down at his face, peaceful in death, it tore me apart.

_________

I’m not sure how long I sat there, weeping over my father’s body. Could
have been hours for all I know.

At some point, I managed to get up, grab the phone, and call 911. Choked
something out about how I’d thought my dad was a home intruder and hit
him over the head with a baseball bat. Accidentally killed him.

I knew how crazy that sounded. By now, I didn’t much care.

_________

The police and ambulance got there maybe ten minutes later. The
paramedics were taking care of the body, when a police officer with a
kind face who looked to be in his late 40s came and sat down next to me.

“Hey, bud. I heard about what happened tonight. Extremely sorry about
that. I imagine it’s hard.”

I let out a dry, humorless chuckle. “Yeah.”

“Do you mind if I ask you a few questions about what happened tonight?”

I don’t know what those questions would have been, or my answers to
them.

Because then, after a week of sweet relief, something began to drip.

_________

“Fuck.”

“Excuse me?”

“Do you… not hear that?”

“Hear what?”

“The dripping noise.”

“What dripping noise?”

I sat there, my eyes shut as tight as I could, holding my face in hands
that were covered in gore. Started rapidly tapping the floors with my
feet - something I hadn’t done since getting on ADHD meds in high
school.

The dripping did not stop. Instead, it got progressively louder to the
point where it felt as though it was inside my head. It was bordering on
painful, and I knew the cop couldn’t hear a thing.

And so, I said the only thing left at that point that I thought might
stop the noise.

“I killed her.”

“Excuse me, son?”

“I… my mom and I weren’t getting along. I poisoned her coffee. And now
she’s haunting me.”

“Son… are you okay?”

It took me about a minute to come up with a one-word answer.

“No.”

He nodded, and I found some more words.

“Could we chat more at the police station?”

“Sure.”

He helped me up. Didn’t cuff me, probably because he thought I was
having some sort of episode and didn’t know what I was talking about and
walked me to the car. Drove me to the police station, where I told them
everything.

Then they asked me to write it all down too. That’s what this is.

At some point, they collected me and processed me in. I’ve been put in a
holding cell until they can transfer me, so I’m still in my street
clothes. Sitting here, finally feeling like a weight has been lifted off
my back.

A couple minutes ago I remembered the insurance payout. Was I entitled
to that now?

_________

The weirdest thing just happened.

The smell of coffee just wafted past me. I looked up just in time to see
a female officer walk past, Styrofoam cup in hand.

But no one else acknowledged her presence.
