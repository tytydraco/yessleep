I’m typing this on my school laptop under my blankets right now because
I’m too terrified to turn on my bedroom light or make a sound. I’m 17,
and I’m writing this out because I don’t know what else to do and the
power just went out.

When I was nine years old, my parents got me a purple, heart-shaped Kim
Possible music box for my birthday. When you opened the plastic lid, a
little figurine of Kim would spin in a circle, and it played the show’s
theme song. To make it work, you had to manually turn a little metal key
on the back to wind up the internal spring.

But back then, it developed what I called a “glitch hour.”

It always happened at exactly 3:14 AM.

I know the exact time because my uncle had given me a heavy, brass
wind-up alarm clock that sat right next to it on my nightstand. The
music box would be sitting there, completely closed and unwound. Then,
in the pitch black, I’d hear it.

Crick. Crick. Crick.

The little metal key on the back would start turning all by itself. It
sounded tight and heavy, clicking rigidly into place as some invisible
force twisted it over and over, winding the spring to its absolute
limit. Then, the plastic lid would snap open, and the song would start
playing.

Except the melody was completely wrong. It was slowed down to an
agonizing, sluggish crawl, sounding less like a kids’ cartoon and more
like a dying breath.

I finally told my mother about it back then. She completely froze
mid-action, gave me a tight, fragile smile, and said, “It’s just an old
spring losing its tension, Abby. Cheap toys do that when the room gets
cold.” But her knuckles were completely white. That night, I heard her
and my dad arguing in the hallway in whispers so sharp they sounded like
tearing paper. The next morning, my dad took the music box away and told
me he threw it in the town dump.

The midnight melodies stopped. I grew up, kept going to school, and
eventually stopped looking at the shadows in the corners of my ceiling.

Until tonight.

A massive winter blizzard just hit our town. The wind blew so violently
that the power lines snapped an hour ago, plunging our house into a
freezing, pitch-black dark. My breath is blooming in white plumes right
in front of my face. I’m buried under four layers of quilts, watching
the snow flurry against my window.

At 3:13 AM, the ticking of my old brass alarm clock—the one thing I kept
from my childhood room—grew deafeningly loud.

Tick. Tick. Tick.

And then, a sound echoed from across my bedroom that made my stomach
drop into a bottomless void.

Crick. Crick. Crick.

The sound of a mechanical key winding. Right on my desk. Faster and
tighter than it ever did when I was a little kid, the metal mechanism
groaning under pure force.

Snap.

The plastic latch popped open. The slow, broken notes of that Kim
Possible song started playing in the dark. It sounds entirely distorted
tonight. It’s dragging out each note, and between the music, there’s a
wet, rhythmic sloshing sound. Like someone stepping in deep mud.

I pulled the quilts over my head and started counting numbers out loud
just to drown out the noise. Nine. Ten. Eleven. Twelve.

The music box didn’t stop. It began to slide across the floorboards, the
tinny music getting louder and louder as it moved out of my room and
down the hallway.

Paralyzed with fear, I crept to my bedroom door and pressed my ear
against the wood. Down the hall, the music suddenly cut off. I heard my
parents’ bedroom door click open.

I waited to hear my dad shout, to turn on a flashlight, to do something.
Instead, there was only a low, wet, whistling sound—like air escaping a
punctured tire.

Then, a voice began to sing along to the music box’s melody. It was my
mother’s voice.

It sounded completely devoid of life, shaking and flat as she sang the
words at a fraction of the speed, mimicking the sluggish, broken cadence
of the toy.

“Call… me… beep… me… if… you… want… to… reach… me…”

Between every single word she sang, there was a horrible, wet tearing
noise. And underneath her voice, I could hear a low, choked groaning
that sounded exactly like my father. I stood frozen, my hand hovering
over my doorknob, completely locked up. My mother’s voice grew fainter,
cracking on the final line, before a heavy, wet impact hit the
floorboards down the hall.

Then, the key started to turn again.

Crick. Crick. Crick.

It was winding itself back up. And the tinny music started moving again.
It’s coming back down the hallway. Toward my door.

I scrambled backward, tripping over my own feet, and squeezed myself
flat against the wall under my bed. The heavy, wet squishing sound just
stopped directly outside my room.

My bedroom doorknob is turning. Slowly. Deliberately.

The door is creaking open right now. In the faint, blue light filtering
through my window from the storm, I can see something sliding across my
carpet.

It’s not a person. It looks like a massive, undulating mass of pale,
glistening flesh, sloughing off layers of black fluid onto my floor. It
doesn’t have a face, but it’s holding that purple music box right on top
of its bulk, the little plastic figurine still spinning lazily.

Inside the pale skin, shapes are pushing outward like hands against a
tight latex sheet.

One of the shapes just pushed hard against the front, stretching the
skin until I could see the distinct, unmistakable features of my dad.
His eyes are wide, frozen in a silent scream. Next to him, another shape
pushed forward. My mother.

The mass just stopped at the foot of my bed. The room is filled with a
metallic, copper smell of fresh blood mixed with the stench of stagnant
swamp water.

From somewhere deep inside the creature’s shifting center, a voice is
echoing. It sounds like two wet stones grinding together, mimicking my
mother’s cadence perfectly.

“Abby,” the entity is wetly whispering into the dark. “You didn’t finish
your numbers. We’re waiting for thirteen.”

The edge of my mattress is sagging right now under a sudden, immense
weight. And from right above my head, through the wooden slats of my
bed, I can hear the little metal key on the back of the box slowly
beginning to twist again.

Crick… Crick… Crick…

I think it knows I’m under here. My laptop battery is at 4%. Please tell
me what to do.
