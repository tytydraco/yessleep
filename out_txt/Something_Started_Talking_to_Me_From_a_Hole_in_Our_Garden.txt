This happened when I was seven years old, during Easter.

As every year, I was searching for the eggs the Easter Bunny had
supposedly hidden all over our garden. My parents were sitting on a
bench outside, drinking coffee and laughing. My older brother Chase was
pretending to enjoy the hunt, but he was already seventeen, so it was
obvious he was just faking it. I kept searching for more eggs.

Eventually, I reached the thick bush in the corner of the garden. There
was nothing there, so I was about to turn around and leave when I heard
a faint, adorable little voice.

“Heath, I have an egg for you too.”

I stepped closer to the bush and noticed a hole that was mostly hidden
behind the branches.

“Who are you?” I asked suspiciously.

“My name is Buddy. I’m a magical bunny, and I live here. But if the
adults found out about me, they’d hurt me.”

“Why would they hurt you?”

Buddy’s little voice sounded sad.

“Because they’d want to catch me, lock me in a cage, and experiment on
me. I’m scared of them.”

I promised him I wouldn’t tell anyone about him. He giggled, his
laughter bright and cheerful, and thanked me.

“Here, you can have an egg as a thank-you.”

A tiny pink paw emerged from the hole, pushing an egg toward me.

“Thank you,” I said. “Will you be here tomorrow too?”

There was silence for a moment. Then Buddy spoke.

“If you want me to, I’ll be here tomorrow too. It’s just that I’m
terribly hungry.”

I promised to bring him something to eat the next day.

From then on, I visited Buddy every day. I brought him food and talked
to him whenever I knew my parents weren’t watching.

One day, our dog Ben went missing. We couldn’t find him anywhere. I
cried all day. My parents tried to comfort me, but nothing helped.

I went to see Buddy. He was the only one who might understand how I
felt.

“Ben is missing,” I told him between sobs.

Buddy reached out his paw toward me. I noticed it was bigger than
before, and the little claws on it had grown too, but that didn’t
matter. I took his paw and let him comfort me.

“You know, maybe I shouldn’t tell you this, but I know what happened to
Ben. Your brother dragged him away and hurt him. I’ve dug tunnels
underground, and I followed your brother when he took Ben away. He
killed him near the pond. He cut his belly open,” Buddy said sadly.
“Apparently, it was because Ben kept barking.”

“You’re lying!” I screamed at him. “Chase would never do that!”

I pulled myself together and went home. I never wanted to see Buddy
again.

But the next day, when I was walking home from school, I stopped by the
pond.

And Ben was there.

Exactly as Buddy had described.

I remembered how Chase often yelled at Ben to stop barking. I didn’t
know what to do.

On my way home, I went straight to see Buddy and apologized.

He told me it was okay because we were friends, and friends forgave each
other. But then he told me to be careful around Chase because he might
hurt me or Mom too.

“But Chase is my brother.”

“I know,” Buddy replied quietly. “And that’s exactly why I don’t want
anything to happen to you. But if you ever brought him to the bush, I
could make sure he never hurt anyone again.”

I told him I’d remember that.

That evening, when I was crying about Ben again, Chase laughed and told
me to get over it. He said that dog had been nothing but trouble and had
barked all the time anyway.

The next day, Chase was supposed to look after me. I heard him arguing
with our parents about it, and he was being really nasty to them.

When our parents left, I heard him go into his room, muttering angrily,
“I’d love to kill them.”

I knew I had to do something.

A little while later, I went to see him and asked if he could help me
with something. I told him my ball had gotten stuck behind the bush and
I needed help getting it out.

Chase got up and followed me outside.

That was the last time I ever saw him.

When our parents came home, they couldn’t find Chase anywhere. They
started searching the neighborhood and eventually called the police. The
police searched our garden and the surrounding area, but they couldn’t
find a single clue that explained where Chase had gone.

I asked Buddy what he’d done with him. He told me he’d taken Chase to
live among the other magical animals, who would teach him how to be good
before sending him home.

That reassured me.

Several weeks passed, but Chase still hadn’t returned. Our parents were
sad, although they tried not to show it. The police still had no new
information, and I had no idea what to think about any of it.

Then my birthday came, and my parents threw me a big birthday party.

As the party was winding down, Amber, the mother of my best friend
Stephan, started shouting and searching the entire garden for him.

“Have you seen him?” she asked me.

“Yeah, we were playing hide-and-seek, and now it’s my turn to find him,”
I said.

Everyone started looking for Stephan. After a while, the police arrived
too, but Stephan was nowhere to be found.

That evening, after everyone had left, I went to see Buddy and told him
what had happened. I asked if he knew where Stephan was.

He told me Stephan had come to see him, and Buddy had realized that
Stephan was bad, so he’d taken him to Chase and the other animals.

“But I don’t want Stephan to be gone,” I said.

“Don’t worry,” Buddy reassured me. “I promise he’ll be back soon, along
with Chase.”

I thanked him, and he reached out his paw toward me. It was almost as
big as the hole, and the little claws weren’t really claws anymore. They
had become talons.

“Buddy, your paw is… huge,” I said fearfully.

Buddy laughed.

“That’s because magical bunnies grow faster than normal ones, especially
when they have a friend who trusts them and keeps their secrets.”

Then I noticed something on his paw that looked like blood.

“Buddy, is that blood?”

He immediately pulled his paw back and, in a sad, tearful voice, said
that it was. He’d apparently gone for a walk, but some people had seen
him and tried to catch him. They’d hurt him, but luckily, he’d managed
to escape through one of his tunnels.

“People are terribly cruel, Heath.”

“I’m glad you got away from them, Buddy. You have to be careful.”

I went home and asked Mom if it was right to turn bad children into good
ones.

She didn’t understand what I meant.

“Well, I just thought everyone would be happier if Chase and Stephan
came home as good boys, right?”

“What do you mean?” she asked.

“I can’t tell you. It’s a secret.”

I went to my room.

A little while later, Mom came in with Dad. They told me that if I knew
anything about Chase and Stephan, I had to tell them.

I didn’t want to, but Mom always knew how to get me to talk.

I told them everything about Buddy, about how Chase had killed our dog,
and how Buddy had taken Chase and Stephan away.

Mom went completely pale, and for the first time in my life, I saw fear
on Dad’s face.

“Where exactly is this Buddy?” Dad asked me.

“I can’t tell you.”

Dad raised his voice.

“Heath! This isn’t a joke. Where exactly is he?”

“In the garden, by the bush.”

Mom grabbed her phone, saying she was going to call the police, while
Dad went to the bedroom to get his gun. Then he headed outside.

I watched him from my bedroom window, shouting at him to leave Buddy
alone.

Suddenly, Mom screamed in the hallway. I immediately ran out to see what
was happening and saw something enormous and furry lying on top of her.
Its claws were buried in her throat.

Then it spoke.

“Heath, you promised you wouldn’t tell anyone our secret! Now you’re
going to pay for it.”

It looked at me. I saw red eyes and a horribly deformed animal face.
Then it charged straight at me.

I felt a dull pain in my stomach as it sank its teeth into me. Then its
claws dug into my hand.

Then I heard a gunshot.

Dad. He’d seen what was happening and was firing one bullet after
another into Buddy. Buddy tore himself away from me, charged at Dad, and
knocked him to the ground.

“Run!” Dad shouted at me. “Now!”

I did as he said and ran to our neighbors’ house. I rang the doorbell,
and when our neighbor opened the door, everything went black and I
passed out.

At the hospital, they told me my parents were dead.

When I asked if Buddy had been there too, they didn’t know who I was
talking about. They eventually closed the case, concluding that the
killer had escaped and had never been found.

Now that I’m an adult, I’d love to say that it was all just a figment of
my imagination, but I know it wasn’t.

Every day, I blame myself for losing my family.

And now I have a son of my own.

Easter was a few days ago, and ever since then, he’s been spending more
time in the garden. Whenever I ask him what he’s doing out there, he
just smiles and says he’s playing. I’ve caught him a few times sitting
by the thick bush in the corner of the garden, whispering to himself.

I think I know who he’s talking to.

And this time, I’m going to put a stop to it before anyone else gets
hurt.
