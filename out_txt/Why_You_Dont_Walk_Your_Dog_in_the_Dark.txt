It was dark when it finally stopped raining.

I was hoping for a quiet night in, but the pup had a different agenda.

He had spent the day noisily snoring near the front door, a favorite
spot of his, while I spent a lazy Autumn day flipping back and forth
between old scary movies and football.

With the occasional glance at whatever Hallmark movie was playing,
they’re so predictable but fun.

I thought the night would end the same way, but the slumbering beast
woke up when I tried sneaking another apple fritter.

He whined and motioned towards his leash, and when that didn’t work, he
put his massive head in my lap.

“Okay fine let’s go.”

He sat while I fastened his leash, and off we went.

It was meant to be as it stopped raining as left.

The first sign we shouldn’t have left was the flashlight crapped out
about ten feet from the house.

Luckily, the moon was out giving just enough light from the walk, as the
streetlights were spaced rather far apart.

We walked through the dark. I avoided the puddles, the dog didn’t.

The dog’s ears perked up and about fifty yards away I saw something dart
away under the streetlight.

I barely saw it but figured it was a deer. They were getting spooked
easily now that it was hunting season.

Just as we made it to the light the dog started growling. That was
worrisome enough but then I got the eeriest feeling that I was being
watched.

My blood ran cold. I quickly went by the street light but the feeling
didn’t go away. The dog was getting antsy too.

Instead of growling, he was now whining.

I had never seen him scared before. The dog was nearly two hundred
pounds of muscle and feared nothing. I had seen him run off countless
coyotes and even black bears.

I kept walking hoping the feeling would fade.

Things only got worse though.

Clouds covered the moon and the world grew darker.

The hair stood up on the back of my neck. I could hear something
breathing nearby. Course, raspy breaths.

The dog wasn’t whining anymore. He went full protection mode. He snarled
and growled and let loose deep, powerful barks that had to wake up the
neighbors.

I turned back home but kept looking back over my shoulder. Again I
caught another glimpse of something,

I jogged back home and locked the door behind me. I quickly closed all
the blinds and tried to forget about the feeling.

The dog started barking as I heard it.

Something was climbing up the steps.

Some scratched at the door drove the dog nuts. He was going crazy. His
paws were on the door. His bark thundered throughout the house. I sped
around making sure the back door and the windows were locked.

“Let me in…” something said at the door.

”Please let me in…”

”Please.”

It’s voice was soft and sweet and sounded almost like a kid.

“I’m calling the police,” I said through the door.

The thing screamed and then went silent.

When the police showed up, the evidence they could find were the
scratches at the door.
