Part 3

I found Quintana Hess’s address online and decided to leave first thing
in the morning.

I thought of locking the gun in my safe again, but what would’ve been
the point?  It was time for acceptance.  I couldn’t hide the gun; it
would find me.  I couldn’t destroy it; it was indestructible.

I placed it on my nightstand and turned out the light.

Sleep still didn’t come easily.  I kept opening my eyes, finding myself
staring at the gun – finding the gun staring back at me.

I finally sat up and grabbed it.  I couldn’t resist any longer.  The
urge was too strong.

If the gun was seducing me, so be it.  It strangely felt inevitable.

“I dare you.”

I wasn’t sure why I said it, but I did.

I jammed my thumb against the hammer – I dare you – and quickly cocked
it back.

But nothing happened.

I tried again.

Still nothing, just the quiet click of the hammer locking into place.

Was the gun fucking with me?  The feeling sent a chill through my body.

I was suddenly dressing in haste.  I was out the door and into the
night, slipping unnoticed into my car.  I started it up and pulled away,
gliding silently through the sleepy neighborhood.

Minutes later, I was rolling onto the Kilgore Extension Bridge.  I
stopped in the middle and climbed out.

I moved swiftly toward the edge.  The metal railing stopped my body, but
not my arm.  I kept swinging forward, Lefaucheux in hand, until I let
the gun sail.

I watched it drift through the hazy mist.  It finally dropped into the
raging river below, disappearing without a sound.

I finally felt I had earned a moment of peace.

I caught my breath.  What I wouldn’t have given to hold onto this
feeling.  At least until morning.

Sleep was calling me.  Please, God, sleep.

I pushed off the railing and turned around, startled to find myself
face-to-face with Angelo.

The guy swung at me, fast.  A knife.  It slashed across my throat.

I jerked awake.

I was back in bed, drenched from my nightmare.

The Lefaucheux revolver was still on the nightstand.  It just sat there,
tempting me.  Mocking me.

I yanked myself out of bed in one swift motion and snatched it up.  My
finger was on the hammer without even consciously deciding.

I was ready to do it – ready to cock the hammer.  For real this
time.  Wide awake.

Fuck it, do it already – do it now!

The phone rang, breaking the spell.

I released my grip on the gun.  It dropped to the floor.

My phone kept ringing, pulling me farther back to myself.

It was my son.  Panic seized me.

“What’s wrong, Max?”

“I can’t sleep.”

I let out a shaky breath, relieved.

“I’m really sorry about that, buddy.  I can’t sleep either.”

“When can I come home?  I miss you.”

My emotions stabbed at me.  “Soon.  I promise.”

I stayed on the line for as long as I could, just listening to my son
breathing.  “I miss you, too, buddy.”

Max finally hung up with a sleepy yawn.

Afterward, I drove the two hours to Loomis.  There was no point in
trying to sleep.  I parked in front of the small dark house at the end
of the quiet cul-de-sac.

The Lefaucheux revolver sat on the passenger seat.

The sun hadn’t broken the horizon yet.

I waited.

Finally, a light flickered on.  I knew I should give it a little longer,
but I was tired of waiting.  I stuck the gun in my jacket and hopped
out.

I climbed the rickety porch steps and tapped at the door.  It swung open
almost immediately, surprising me.

It was Quintana Hess.

The woman who had cursed the gun that killed my daughter was suddenly
standing in front of me.  This sweet little old lady was the reason for
all my pain.

“Good morning,” she said, her smile warm.

She seemed delighted to have a visitor, even at the strange hour, and
she invited me in without asking who I was or why I was there.  She said
she’d make some tea.

I stepped inside.

The house looked as if it hadn’t changed in decades.

At first glance, it was homey.  But the more I took things in, the more
the air in the room settled heavy and haunted around me.  Life,
petrified.

Quintana put the kettle on the stove and lit the flame.

I stayed near the door and began gently asking my questions.  I had come
this far with questions.  That’s all there was.

How long had she lived here?  Did she live alone?  I was interested in
her family.  I understood she had a son.  What was his name?  What was
he like?

Quintana offered answers and details, but I could tell her memory was
murky.  It was obvious she was making things up to fill in the gaps.

Her son’s name was Teddy.

He was a shy boy.  A mama’s boy.

She gushed with a mother’s pride, some moments acknowledging he was
dead, others believing he was alive – still that 12-year-old kid.

“I’m really sorry he died,” I offered.  “I know what it’s like to lose a
child.”

For a moment, Quintana seemed distressed.

But the moment swiftly passed, and she was smiling again, the trouble in
her brain having extinguished the burning memory.

She poured two mugs of tea and we settled in at the kitchen table.  She
suggested we play some Rummy and dealt a round of cards.

“Can you tell me about the Lefaucheux revolver?” I finally asked, as
casually as I could.

“What do you want to know?” Quintana wondered, after taking her turn and
laying down the ten, jack, and queen of spades.

“Whose gun was it?”

“Vernon Dickson.  Brandon’s father.  He collected things.  Civil War
things.”

“Was Brandon a friend of Teddy’s?”

“Schoolmates.  Brandon was older than Teddy.”

I played the next round, laying down a triplet of sevens, before
continuing with more questions, easing into asking about the curse.  I
somehow knew I needed to be respectful.

No question seemed off-limits.  But Quintana kept dismissing things,
dodging her failing memory.

I have no idea what you’re talking about, dear.

I don’t like to worry myself with such unpleasantness.

Are you a friend of my son?

Does your mother know you’re here?  I should give her a call so she
doesn’t worry.

Quintana laid down her remaining cards and won the round, then moved to
the phone to call my mother, whatever the number was.

“My daughter died playing the game.”

Quintana had picked up the receiver but couldn’t remember who she was
calling.

“Nina was shy, too.  And she was beautiful and smart and a wonderful
friend.  She cared about people.  But she was tempted, and she played
the game, and she was shown a suicide, and she killed herself because
the gun made her do it.”

Quintana struggled with her reaction.  The feeling was there, even if
she couldn’t quite name it.

“You need to save your daughter,” she finally said, as if to clear the
air.

“My daughter’s dead.  The Lefaucheux killed her.  Your curse killed
her.”

Quintana brushed my words away.

She returned to the table and dealt another round.

“Then stop her.  If you love her, stop her from pulling the trigger and
you’ll save her.  You’ll save the both of you.”

I wanted to understand what she was saying.  I wanted to discern what
was meaningful and what was simply her mind slipping.

But Quintana was done answering my questions.

I pulled the Lefaucheux from my jacket and placed it on the table
between us.  I wasn’t sure I was going to do that, but I was desperate.

The sight of the gun changed something in Quintana.  It was primal.

I think the memory of the gun somehow broke through the damage in her
brain, bringing everything back.  Her pain and grief.  Her
vengefulness.  All of it.

I felt for her, but I couldn’t let this moment pass.  I kept pressing
her for answers.

I kept asking her to remove the curse – to destroy the gun.

I begged her to stop the game before someone else got hurt.

My desperate pleas pulled me to my feet.  The table was between us, yet
I towered over her.

I suddenly swept the gun up and put it to my head, a gesture meant to
bring her eyes to mine, and it worked.  Quintana’s anguish was all
there.  And in all honesty, it kind of broke my heart.

I didn’t have a next move in mind.  I was just waiting to see what would
happen.

I didn’t have to wait long.

Out of fucking nowhere, this ghastly figure came charging at me, its
face mutilated from a bullet blast.

I swear to God this really happened.

It came at me in a blurry flash, but there was no doubt in my mind who –
or what – it was.

It was Teddy, Quintana’s son.

His tortured presence slammed into me, driving me backward.

My body collided with the solid mass of somebody right behind me.  The
impact spun me around.

Angelo was standing there.

Before I could react, a tire iron slammed into my head, and everything
went black.

The room flickered into focus.

I had no idea how much time had passed.  Things were turned on their
side.  I was on the kitchen floor where I’d fallen.

My head pounded.

I could feel the blood on my face.

Quintana was still at the kitchen table.  She sipped her tea, playing a
hand of solitaire.

I wanted to lift my head, but I wasn’t sure I was ready for the pain.

Just make it quick.

I pushed through it and staggered to my feet.  I was woozy.

Quintana glanced up from her cards, almost surprised to find someone
standing in her kitchen, as if all memory of me had vanished.  She
wished me a good morning.  She offered me a cup of tea and lit the flame
under the kettle.

A noise in the other room pulled me in that direction.

I stepped into the living room and found Angelo perched on the edge of
the couch.

He was gripping the Lefaucheux revolver.  The hammer was cocked.  He was
playing the game, his eyes glassy and dark.

My head kept pounding like a jackhammer.  The room began to spin.  I
tried to catch myself against the doorframe, but everything went black.

When I came to, I was flat on my back, staring at the ceiling.

I could hear strange sounds in the room.  Guttural noises.  Heavy
breaths.  Whimpers.

I finally rolled onto my side toward the noise.  It was coming from
Angelo, still on the couch, the gun clutched in his hand, still playing
the game.

The man’s face was muddied with tears.  He was in anguish.

But still, he kept cocking the hammer.

I wondered how many times he had played.  How much violence and death he
had experienced.

Why was he doing this?

I didn’t understand this man.

Another session ended, and the light returned to Angelo’s eyes, but he
still wasn’t satisfied.  His need seemed insatiable, and he did it again
– another cock of the hammer.

This was destroying him.  It was driving him insane.  But he couldn’t
stop himself.

He kept playing the game, experiencing killing after killing, losing
himself to this mind-numbing, soul-crushing real-life video game.

Another session ended, and without even taking a breath, Angelo cocked
the hammer again.

But this time, something was different.

This time, wherever Angelo had gone – whatever he was seeing in this
play of the game – it brought the tiniest smile to his deadened face.  A
sense of relief and possibility.

I tried to fathom what he was experiencing.

I didn’t understand this man or anything about this moment.

But whatever Angelo had found was fleeting.  The hope on his face
abruptly gave way to anguish again.

And then, he did something that shocked me.

He started turning the gun on himself.

The Lefaucheux turned toward his body.

The barrel pressed into his chest.

Angelo was fighting it.  I could see how hard he was fighting –
struggling against himself, against the curse forcing him to do this.

I scrambled to my feet.

The room tilted like a sinking ship.

I stumbled toward Angelo to stop him, but another attack of Teddy’s
tormented ghost drove me back, throwing me to the floor.

The barrel pressed harder into Angelo’s chest.

His finger found the trigger.  He tried to pull it away, but
couldn’t.   His finger wasn’t his own.

I looked away.  I couldn’t bear to watch.

The blast came quickly.

Deafening.

My eyes snapped back without wanting to.

Angelo’s body was slumped over on the couch, eyes open and dead.

The ringing in my ears kept me pinned to the floor.

A moment later, a presence disturbed the stillness in the room.  It
startled me, my nerves frayed.

Quintana stood at the kitchen door.  The commotion had brought her in.

It was hard to know her reaction.  Whatever she felt a moment ago was
gone.

She finally smiled at me.  “Tea’s ready, dear.  Don’t let it get cold.”

She turned away and returned to the kitchen.

I wasn’t sure I could move, to be honest.

I wasn’t sure of anything anymore.

All I knew was I wanted to get away from this gun and its curse.

But I had promised myself I wouldn’t stop until I put an end to this
game.

How, though?

The thought of burning the house down occurred to me – burning it down
with everything in it.  The gun.  The curse.  Teddy’s ghost.  Quintana’s
pain.  Even myself, if I had to.

Maybe that would somehow destroy it all.

A tightness in my chest pulled me from my thoughts of arson, and for a
moment I wondered if I was having a heart attack.

Maybe there wasn’t time to burn it all down.  Maybe this was the
end.  Maybe I was going to die in this house one way or another.

Or maybe…

Something was beginning to take shape in my mind.

Why did Angelo keep playing the game when it was clearly destroying
him?  And what did he experience in that final play that gave him
hope?  Before everything went tragically wrong.

Quintana told me I needed to save my daughter.

Were Angelo’s obsession and Quintana’s words somehow connected?

Was there a way to save Nina?
