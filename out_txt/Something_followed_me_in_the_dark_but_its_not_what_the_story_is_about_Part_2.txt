“I’m Jerry, by the way. Nice to meet you, Ben. It’s a normal Tuesday
here, where every day feels like the day before. The only change we get
to experience is the changing of the seasons. No one has ever left our
town, and no one has ever moved to our town. What you see is always what
you get here; no bad or good surprises. It’s not common for us to have a
visitor.”

As whimsical as it gets, the branches of the forest trees circled the
town, giving it an enchanted mixture of damp earth and ancient moss
mixed with the sweet scent of berries. The houses all looked the same:
typical countryside cottages with small shops scattered across the town
center. Everyone stared from behind their small wooden windows decorated
with red flowers. It felt like a dream, like I was moving through a
hobbit movie set.

Once we arrived at the city hall, a well-dressed gentleman approached me
and said with a welcoming smile, “A visitor! Welcome to our humble
Meadow Pines. It’s so rare for a visitor to find our town—you must have
gotten lost.”

This time I wasn’t as trusting as the first time. I just said, “Well,
I’m just passing through. I know my way back home, but a little rest
won’t do me any harm. Thank you, sir, for welcoming me to your town. My
name is Ben.” One shouldn’t forget their manners in the face of life’s
horrors.

“You can call me Hubert. It’s nice to meet you! Please, drink some
water. My wife will prepare warm soup for you while you take a shower!”
I almost cried with happiness hearing these words, but I was still numb.

With an indifferent tone, I said, “Thank you, sir. I may have left my
wallet at my camp, though, so I have no way of reaching my money right
now. But I will pay you back as soon as I get access to it.”

He smiled and said, “No one here uses outside-world money. We exchange
services and goods. There is some work you can do to help my son with
tomorrow near the river, if that’s fine with you.”

I was too tired to question anything at this point. I said, “With
pleasure.”

I woke up early in the morning panicking. It must have been around 3 AM.
Where did I step into this time? How did my day shift to this new low? I
won’t start recalling every single bad thing that happened to me in my
life, and God, there are too many. I don’t have time for this. I need to
find my way back home.

Even after the charity, I couldn’t risk another shock during my journey.
I decided to get my clothes and sneak out. I couldn’t find them; all I
had was a medieval-like outfit the “nice” lady left on the chair near my
bed. I wore it anyway, and when I tried to open the old wooden door, I
couldn’t, it was locked. The tiny window opened, though; I could barely
fit, but I did. Now I needed to find a way to jump without breaking any
bones. There was a tree near a porch nearby; I climbed my way through
it. I was shaking and sweating all over, but I made it. I looked back;
the tavern did look like a witch’s den.

What the hell is this place? I ran through the forest with no idea where
I was going. The sun was about to rise. The silence of the forest
soothed the fear inside of me. It was harder to shut it down this time;
I burst out crying and shaking. Nothing seemed to work, no one was there
to help, and I had nothing on me to find my way back to my boring 9-to-8
job because God forbid I leave early, it proves that I’m not motivated
enough to stay in the wonderful position my almighty boss gave me.

As the sun rose, I could see the beauty of the deep forest. A fresh
breeze refreshed my face and my thoughts. It was like I started to
accept that I’m never getting back to my old life. What was waiting for
me there anyways? My girlfriend left me for a guy who looks fancier
after 4 years of a relationship, my parents are very old; my dad barely
remembers me, and my mom lost the will to do anything, just waiting
around to die. My student loan is not fully paid, but almost, and I have
no savings, no house, nothing. What was I getting back to?

This time I was just sitting there contemplating, breathing calmly,
listening to my heartbeat, and floating with my thoughts around the
beauty of the green mist, the tall trees, and the smell of the little
creek nearby. Why am I feeling this calm? I think I exhausted all the
anxiety away.

Something shifted as I was lying there. I heard something approaching, I
couldn’t even afford the surprise, but I saw a very beautiful deer. He
stared at me; I stared back. And then another deer came, and birds,
snakes, and rabbits. They circled around, staring at me. I approached
them; they didn’t run or fight, they were just staring like I was one of
their own.

Mesmerized by the scene, I just smiled. It reminded me of when I was a
little boy and my dad took me hunting with him, and he understood
quickly that I have zero hunter genes; he never bothered taking me
again. I just looked at the animals, and a whisper inside got louder
until I said the words out loud: “Can you show me the way?” They
magically all pointed in one direction, like they understood and
answered. So I headed that way anyway without questioning anything. I
found some berries that I ate, drank some water from the creek, and just
walked my way to a lake.

It was emerald blue with crystal-clear water; I could see the fish at
the bottom. Birds sang a beautiful melody. I was whole again; I felt
safe. I prepared a little bed with leaves and branches near the water,
and I slept watching the sunset. It got cold during the night; I woke up
many times shivering, but I went back to sleep fast as I was tired.

I got to witness another sunrise and then another sunset. Days passed
by, living the Robinson Crusoe fairytale life until the night of the
full moon. I was watching its reflection on the lake; it was beautiful
until it wasn’t. I saw it again, that entity from the first night. It
wasn’t chasing me this time; it was standing across the lake.

Instead of running away, I walked towards it this time. As I got closer,
the soft light of the moon showed its goat legs. I saw the shiny eyes
slowly look normal, and its shape showed me what it looked like without
fear. Everything looks smaller than we remember; shadows take a familiar
shape, and what looked like a threat looks more like me now. It was a
druid-like creature, and its face… looked like my face.

What is this? I hesitated before going any further. I wanted to say
something, but to my surprise, it was faster:

“I knew we’d meet again.”

“How is this even possible?.Wha… What are you?”

“Isn’t it clear to you?”

“Sorry, I don’t think anything is at this point. Wh-why do you look
like… like me?”

“Maybe because I’m you.”

“Oh, I see. I’ve created you to cope with the loneliness. Never mind.
Can you help me find my way back home?”

“Do you really want to find your way back home?”

“I have to. Why did you chase me that night around two weeks ago?”

“I was trying to warn you not to go near the road to the butcher.”

“The butcher?”

“Yes, but you fell right under his wings. Your human form helped you
survive them.”

I sat down. It was too much to understand or not, but what I was seeing
felt so real. “I… I don’t understand.”

“Well, I think it’s time for you to get back to your disguise. You’ll
sleep for now, and by the morning, take this road to the top of the
mountain. You’ll see another lake around 10 miles from here. Go to it.
When you arrive, swim in it twice. You’ll then get back to your
timeline, and then you’ll find your way out of this place. Also, don’t
talk to the witches again, they can see through your human form, and
they enslave druids to grow their herbs faster.”

And then I heard the sound of running deer again, and the druid walked
away.

I was shocked. He said I’m a druid in a human disguise and this is
another timeline? Witches; butcher? 

I didn’t sleep; I just walked the road to the top of the mountain and
sat there waiting for the sunrise so I could see if that second lake he
mentioned existed. To my surprise, I saw it to the east. It took me a
couple of hours; I was running and sweating, but I was happy. It may
work!

I arrived and didn’t bother to take my clothes off,I just jumped in the
water. It was warm and sweet the first time, cold and moldy the second
time. And then the place felt the same, but sadder. The trees were
smaller, the green mist became brown dirt, dead bushes replaced the
berries, and the sweet smell disappeared.

I walked through the path, and for the first time, I saw a grey paved
road slicing through the treeline. I followed it down, until the trees
gave way to concrete, telephone poles, and neon signs.

I had arrived at an actual town, a place buzzing with electricity,
overhead power lines, and the glare of glowing smartphone screens.
People in denim jeans and rain jackets passed me by, lost in their own
hurried routines. I stood completely still in my damp, medieval-style
tunic, dripping lake water onto the sidewalk. I must have looked like a
ghost or a madman, but I couldn’t bring myself to care. They guessed
that I was lost even though I didn’t utter a word, my hollow eyes and
shivering frame said enough.

A concerned shopkeeper wrapped a stiff wool blanket over my shoulders
and called for help.

Two officers took me to a police station that smelled aggressively of
bleach, stale coffee, and warm printer paper scents that burned my nose
after weeks of pine resin and rain. They checked their databases, asked
soft questions I couldn’t answer, and eventually arranged a quiet ride
back to my old apartment.

When I finally stepped over the threshold of my home, the silence hit me
like a physical weight. The air was stale, trapped inside four plaster
walls, smelling of old carpet and dust. My laptop sat on the desk,
surrounded by unopened bills and deadline notices that meant nothing to
me now. I took a shower shaved and wore my usual home uniform, I walked
past the mirror and barely recognized the clean-shaved face staring back
at me under the flickering fluorescent light. I sat on the edge of my
bed, staring at my hands, feeling more like a stranger in my own life
than I ever had in the forest.

I need to go back. I need to find the lake, the mist, and the truth of
what I really am…
