I live alone.

I’ve lived alone for almost four years.

I’m posting this because I don’t know who else to ask, and I’m hoping
someone can give me a normal explanation before I convince myself I’m
losing it.

I rent a one-bedroom apartment in a fairly old building in Ohio. Nothing
special. Six floors, probably built in the 1970s, with the kind of thin
walls where you know when your upstairs neighbor drops something.

I’ve never had any problems here.

Until about three weeks ago.

It started with food.

I’d come home from work and notice that something in my refrigerator was
missing.

Nothing major.

A couple eggs.

Half a carton of milk.

One of those little yogurt cups.

At first, I assumed I was forgetting.

I work long hours and sometimes eat at work, so it’s entirely possible I
was just misremembering what I’d bought.

Then I noticed my thermostat.

I keep it at 68°F when I’m home and 62°F when I’m at work.

Every morning before leaving, I check it.

One Tuesday, I came home and it was set to 68.

I figured I’d accidentally bumped it.

The next day, same thing.

Then again.

So I took a picture of it before leaving for work.

62°F.

When I got home:

68°F.

I started getting annoyed.

I thought maybe the thermostat was malfunctioning.

I called maintenance.

They checked it and said it was working normally.

I didn’t think much of it after that.

Until Friday.

I came home and my bedroom door was closed.

I always leave it open.

Always.

I live alone, and I hate sleeping in a stuffy room, so there isn’t a
reason for me to close it.

I checked the apartment.

Nothing was missing.

Nothing looked disturbed.

So I chalked it up to me forgetting.

Then I noticed my bed.

The blanket was pulled back.

Not much.

Maybe six inches.

Like someone had gotten into bed and then gotten out.

That bothered me.

But again, I tried to explain it away.

Maybe I’d done it half-asleep.

Maybe I’d gotten up during the night and forgotten.

So I started taking pictures before leaving for work.

The bedroom.

Kitchen.

Bathroom.

Front door.

Thermostat.

Everything.

For the next few days, nothing changed.

Then I noticed something in one of the photos.

A glass.

It was sitting on my nightstand.

I don’t use glasses in my bedroom.

I use a stainless-steel water bottle.

I checked the picture from the previous morning.

No glass.

The glass appeared in the photo I took before work.

Which meant I had somehow put it there without remembering.

I was starting to get genuinely concerned about my memory.

So I bought a cheap indoor security camera.

I pointed it at the hallway.

Not because I thought someone was breaking in.

Honestly, I was more worried that I was sleepwalking.

That night, I set it up before bed.

At 7:14 AM, I left for work.

At 5:46 PM, I came home.

Nothing looked different.

I opened the camera app.

There was nothing unusual.

No movement.

No person.

Just me leaving in the morning and coming back in the evening.

I almost felt stupid.

Then I noticed something.

At 2:31 AM, the camera had stopped recording.

For exactly eleven minutes.

Then it started again.

I checked the camera settings.

Nothing.

No scheduled downtime.

No internet outage.

The camera simply stopped recording.

I called the manufacturer.

They said it could happen if the Wi-Fi briefly disconnected.

Fine.

That made sense.

Except my phone showed my Wi-Fi had never disconnected.

I didn’t sleep much that night.

The next morning, I did something that probably sounds insane.

I put a tiny piece of clear tape across the inside of my front door.

If someone opened it, the tape would tear.

I also put a little piece of tape across the bedroom door.

Then I went to work.

When I came home, both pieces were intact.

Nothing had happened.

I actually felt relieved.

Maybe I really was imagining things.

Then I went into my bedroom.

The glass was back.

Same glass.

Sitting on the nightstand.

I stared at it for probably five minutes.

I hadn’t put it there.

I know I hadn’t.

But there was no sign that anyone had entered.

I called my brother.

I told him everything.

He asked me if I was sleeping enough.

I got annoyed.

He apologized.

Then he said something that stuck with me.

“Maybe someone has a key.”

I called the landlord.

He told me nobody had entered my apartment.

I asked if maintenance had been inside.

He said no.

Then I asked whether previous tenants could still have copies of the
key.

He laughed.

“Not unless they’re breaking in.”

That wasn’t reassuring.

The next day, I bought a new deadbolt.

I installed it myself.

Nobody could get in without my key.

That night, I finally slept.

The next morning, I woke up to find my kitchen light on.

I hadn’t turned it on.

I checked the lock.

Still locked.

The tape was still there.

I checked the camera.

Nothing.

Then I opened my refrigerator.

My milk was gone.

I just stood there staring at the empty space.

I started laughing.

Not because it was funny.

Because I didn’t know what else to do.

I called my brother again.

He came over.

We checked the apartment together.

Closets.

Bathroom.

Under the bed.

Nothing.

He told me I needed to take a break.

Maybe stay with him for a few nights.

I agreed.

Before leaving, I checked the apartment one last time.

That’s when I noticed something I hadn’t noticed before.

There was a small rectangular access panel near the floor in the
hallway.

I’d always assumed it was part of the HVAC system.

My brother crouched down and looked at it.

There were screws around the frame.

He removed them.

Behind it wasn’t an HVAC duct.

It was an old service cavity.

Not large enough to stand in, but wide enough to crawl through.

There were pipes running through it and electrical conduits along one
side.

My brother shined his phone flashlight inside.

The cavity continued farther than either of us expected.

“That’s creepy,” he said.

Then he noticed something.

Dust.

There was a clean section through it.

Like something had been moving through there.

We called the landlord.

He came over.

The second he saw the opening, he got angry.

Not scared.

Angry.

He said the access panel shouldn’t have been exposed.

He called maintenance.

They came the next morning.

They explained that the building had originally been constructed with
interconnected service cavities between some of the apartments.

During a renovation in 2014, most of them had been sealed.

Apparently, whoever renovated my floor had forgotten about this
particular section.

The opening itself was only about 18 inches wide.

Too narrow for most adults.

The maintenance guy said nobody could comfortably crawl through it.

That made sense.

It also meant whoever had supposedly been inside my apartment couldn’t
have entered that way.

They inspected the cavity anyway.

There were no footprints.

No belongings.

No signs that anyone had been sleeping there.

Just old insulation, dust and pipes.

They sealed the opening with a metal plate.

I thought that was the explanation.

Old building.

Faulty memory.

A glass I forgot about.

A thermostat I accidentally changed.

Food I ate and forgot about.

A camera that glitched.

It all sounded much better than the alternative.

I stayed with my brother for four nights.

When I finally returned home, everything was normal.

No missing food.

No strange thermostat settings.

No glass.

Nothing.

It’s been almost two weeks.

I haven’t seen anything unusual.

Which should make me feel better.

Instead, I’m more uncomfortable than ever.

Because yesterday, I was cleaning out my closet.

I found an old shoebox.

I don’t remember putting it there.

Inside were photographs.

Not recent photographs.

Old ones.

Pictures of my apartment.

The kitchen.

The bedroom.

The hallway.

They were taken before I moved in.

I thought maybe the landlord had left them behind.

Then I found one picture that made me sit down.

It was a photograph of my bedroom.

Taken from the corner.

The room was empty.

Except for a person standing beside the window.

I couldn’t see their face.

They were facing away from the camera.

I turned the photograph over.

There was a date written on the back.

October 14, 2011.

I looked it up.

The building wasn’t renovated until 2014.

My apartment had apparently been vacant during that period.

At least according to the landlord.

I called him and asked about the photographs.

He said he didn’t know what I was talking about.

Then he asked me to send him a picture.

I did.

He called me back less than a minute later.

He sounded different.

He asked me where I found it.

I told him.

He was quiet.

Then he said:

“Throw those away.”

I asked why.

He said:

“Because that’s not your apartment.”

I looked at the photograph again.

Same window.

Same closet.

Same radiator.

Same crack in the wall.

It’s my bedroom.

I know it is.

Then I noticed something else.

The person standing by the window wasn’t alone.

There was someone else in the photograph.

You can barely see them.

They’re standing behind the camera.

You can see their reflection in the bedroom mirror.

And they’re holding a glass.

The same glass that’s been appearing on my nightstand.

I haven’t slept at my apartment since.

My brother thinks the pictures are some kind of elaborate prank.

The landlord won’t talk to me.

And maybe that’s all this is.

Maybe somebody has been messing with me.

Maybe I’ve been forgetting things.

Maybe the photographs are unrelated.

I really want there to be a normal explanation.

But there’s one thing I can’t explain.

The photograph is sitting on my brother’s kitchen table right now.

I took it out of the shoebox this morning.

I haven’t touched it since.

A few minutes ago, I noticed something written on the back that wasn’t
there yesterday.

Just three words.

You left me here.
