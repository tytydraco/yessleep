I know how strange this sounds, if it didn’t didn’t happen to me, I
probably wouldn’t believe it either. But my wife has been buying
crickets, bringing them into our studio apartment, and letting them
loose.

Technically she warned me first. She asked me before she did it, saying
it like it was a completely normal thing to do to give our cat some
enrichment.

I naturally assumed that this was some bizarre joke from her, even if
she’d never partaken in such absurdist humor before, she said it so
nonchalantly that I never even considered that she was serious when I
told her to go right ahead.

What makes it even worse is we don’t even own a cat.

It had seemed so ridiculous that I had completely forgotten it until
later that night, when laying in bed next to her there was the distinct
sound of crickets chirping all around our room.

I rolled over to look at her, still not even remembering what she had
said, until I saw the smile stretched across her face, visible even
through the darkness.

When I asked if she had actually done it, head still full of disbelief
that she would even consider actually bringing those pests into our
home. But she had and was proud of it. The cat was going to be so
enriched, my wife would say. She’d been looking sad lately and clearly
needed more things to do in such a small apartment.

For as long as I could I tried to deny that she had actually gone
through it, despite being kept awake all night from their chirping,
unable to sleep from the unease as much as the noise. The next day I
even found a few of them.

Still I told myself it was some bizarre prank, that at any moment our
family extended family or maybe a camera crew would waltz in proclaiming
that I’d been had. But, I’m still waiting for them.

Over that period every time I brought it up to her, that I was upset or
confused at what she had done she would simply brush me off, even make
me feel like I was the one being unreasonable. Our cat needed them,
silly, we’d do anything for her right?

And when I pointed out that we never had a cat over the entire course of
our marriage, she just laughed at me and changed the subject.

At one point when I kept pushing especially hard she started crying and
begging me to stop talking to her. I slept on the couch that night, and
left it at that. Content that at least they’d be gone soon as I heard
fewer withs every passing day.

The following night when the last of them had seemingly starved or
escaped, I felt relief that we could put this all behind us. She made no
mention of it as we laid there in bed and I was perfectly happy to put
this behind us and never speak of it again.

And for a few days we did, life returned to normal, and I was able to
get some real sleep. The relief was immense, I had become a zombie over
those four sleepless night.

Its not like the sound alone had kept me up, I had no problem falling
asleep on camping trips out in the wilderness. But the sound had come to
be a reminder that there was something seriously wrong, we wren’t out in
nature, she had brought them into our home.

So when a few nights later, laying in bed, I heard those chirps creeping
in around us, I nearly broke down. I turned to her and fought to stop
myself from yelling out. She was clearly going through something, and as
odd as it seemed I needed to support her however I could.

Still, she had once again let those things into our home, to make a cat
we didn’t have happy.

I forced myself to calmly ask her, if she had once again bought
crickets. She had, the cat we didn’t have, had seemed sad after the last
one of them died out.

And so the next few days I laid awake at night, staring up at the
ceiling questioning what was happening, kept awake by the uncomfortable
reality just as much as the noise.

There came a day when I mentioned her visiting a therapist, alone or
together, how it would be better for both of us, but she rebuffed the
idea all together. The same way she had over the crickets prior. Why
would she need a therapist if she hadn’t done anything wrong after all?

I walked away from that conversation questioning if I was the one who
needed therapy for doubting something that was so obviously necessary.

Last night though they got a bit quieter, and I looked forward to the
last of them dying out once again, except that night never came. One
night there was a solo cricket chirping alone in the room, and the next
the room was full with an entire chorus. She had bought more of them.

Many more sleepless nights passed, I cried, I begged her to talk to a
therapist, to stop with the crickets, but nothing changed.

I spoke to a therapist about her, hoping that there would be something
else I could do, something they could do. But their only advice was that
if she wasn’t actively harming herself, I could either get her to open
up or leave her, no doubt it was odd, she was clearly going through
something. But they couldn’t do anything on their end without her
cooperation.

And so, over time I got used to the crickets, she still refused to
acknowledge them, as they spread around the home, getting into the
vents, their chirps distorting into deeper echoes through the night.

I got used to checking there were none in my cereal each morning. Every
time I sat on the couch there was a flurry of movement as crickets
jumped out of my way. And as I got more used to the crickets, she began
to talk more and more about her imaginary cat, I refused to indulge in
her delusion, but every time I tried to broach the conversation that
there was something very wrong with all of this, she sobbed at my words.

The cat was so happy she would say, never actually saying where this cat
was, just how much she loved all these crickets, all these friends, to
play with.

Every day I thought about leaving her, while I had gotten used to their
presence, I was still horribly aware of how wrong this all was. But I
could feel deep down that if I left her she would get even worse, as if
I was her last tether to reality.

At some point through all this she had stopped going to work entirely, I
had only found out about it when I happened to catch a concerned call
from her supervisor. She hadn’t been there in a few weeks and they
wanted to make sure she was ok.

Even stranger is that as this has gone on, I started to hear movement in
the vents at first I was sure it was the echoes of the crickets that had
gotten in there. But overtime it became clear there was something larger
in there, the thuds were too deep to be excused as resonating chirps.

I told myself that rats had gotten in, probably eating the dead crickets
that made their way into the vents only to starve.

This all led up to last night. I laid next to my wife in our bed, the
room aa chorus of crickets, seemingly coming from every direction, I had
no idea how many there were. Despite my exhaustion, I could not sleep,
their chorus had grown too large, this was too wrong. But I kept my eyes
closed nonetheless, hoping for a release from this strange torment.

Im not sure how much time passed like this, maybe I even did find some
sleep, but at some point late into the night, the crickets began to
quiet.

It was gradual at first, a few of them stopped at the opposite end of
the room, a few more in the vents. But rapidly the bulk of them quieted,
ceasing their chirping despite it still being the middle of the night.

At first I thought my prayers had been answered, that I would finally
have a good night’s sleep. As the room became incredibly still.

But I could sense there was something just as wrong with this, they
hadn’t all just died, and the room was still pitch black, and unease
began to creep into the back of my mind.

Far off in the vents bangs echoed periodically, broken up by long
periods of silence.

I laid there as still as I could on my back, my eyelids squeezed shut,
telling myself it was just building settling, but each one was just a
little louder than the last, a little closer.

There was one noise particularly close, and then nothing, complete
silence once again.

I couldn’t tell how much time passed like this, my heart pumping in my
my chest, listening as much as I could for the slighted bit of noise.

And as I laid there in silence, completely unmoving, breathing as softly
as I could, I felt something small lightly step onto my chest. First one
foot, then another, then another, then another.

I was completely frozen, it didn’t weigh more than ten pounds but
whatever it was, it was not a cat. I did not dare open my eyes.

Its proportions, its weight, the way it breathed, none of it added up, I
had no idea what had crept up onto my chest, but it was not a cat. And I
could feel its eyes burning through my skull.

It sat there completely still, and the weight began to feel heavier on
my chest, I still breathed as light as I could. As if I was hoping this
thing was unaware of me somehow.

I felt its weight shift a little, it seemed to lift each of its feet and
when it stepped back down there were sharp pains where it stood. Seeming
to flex its claws into me at will, as if it wanted to make squirm, to
leave its mark. I gritted my teeth through the pain, still controlling
my breathing as much as I could.

It returned to the sitting position, somehow feeling heavier now, its
body pressed against mine, its feet now painfully dug into my chest and
stomach.

“Can you hear her purring? She finally likes you.” My wife mumbled
sleepily.

But there was no noise, no sound at all, except my own breathing.

I can’t say how much time passed like this, but eventually it stepped
off my chest, not making any sound as it did so.

I did not sleep the rest of the night. I’m not sure what else I can do
but leave. I feel horrible leaving her with that thing, with all that
crickets, but I took the day off from work and have been packing my
stuff.

My wife is here too but she hasn’t said anything, she’s just kind of sat
around looking at me sadly. But I can’t spend another night with the
crickets, can’t spend it with that thing.

I’m just hopeful someone on here will have some advice for me, something
I can still do for her. I should’ve tried harder, I know, before this
all became normal, but somehow I don’t think it would have made a
difference.

As I sit here, writing this on my phone, packing away what I can, I
swear I can hear meowing coming from the vents, far off in the distance.
And for some reason, I caught myself checking for pet supply stores
along the route to my hotel.
