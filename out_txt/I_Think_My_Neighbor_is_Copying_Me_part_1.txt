His name is Jerry and I don’t know that much about him, but I really
don’t like what I do know. He’s too nice. Too unassuming. I know that
makes me sound like a psycho, but please hear me out. For the past four
months, ever since I moved into this new house, I keep seeing him at odd
times of the day and end up having these disgustingly sanitized
conversations. What I mean is, every conversation we’ve had feels like a
script. There’s never any room for expansion. No personal details. I
couldn’t tell you about his likes or dislikes, nor should he know any of
mine. He’s never asked. It’s literally:

“Hey, Mark! Great weather this morning, ain’t it?”

Or:

“Afternoon, Mark! Are you working hard or hardly working?”

I hate it so much. I once asked him what he thought of a candidate who
was running for mayor, trying like hell to get some kind of opinion out
of him. He stared at me with a blank smile and shrugged.

“Not much one for politics, Mark. How about them dogs, though?!” 

That’s it. That’s all I’ve gotten out of him. He likes college football.
Meanwhile, when I’m not directly talking to him, he never smiles. I’ve
been watching him. I haven’t, like, bought binoculars or anything,
before you start getting the wrong idea. Our houses are right next door
to each other, so it’s hard not to glance out the window on occasion.

When he’s not talking with anyone, he looks utterly miserable. His face
gets sunken, and he looks ready to bite someone’s head off if they
inconvenience him. Once, I watched him sit on his front porch chair for
a full hour in complete silence. He just held a thousand-yard stare and
literally did not even twitch the entire time. I know I sound like a
psycho. I know, but…

It started with the shirts. I wear and collect a bunch of Hawaiian
shirts and like vintage stuff. I get them from all over, but most of
them are thrifted. Jerry, when I first knew him, didn’t really wear
anything that stood out. Plain t-shirts and jeans, maybe some shorts
every once in a while, but that was it. He was alarmingly milktoast.
However, a month ago, he started wearing Hawaiian shirts out of nowhere.
Not just regular ones, but shirts that had patterns and motifs that I
knew I owned. I thought it was weird, but it’s a free country- who cares
what kind of shirt a man wears? 

Then he wore a shirt I knew he shouldn’t have. 

I have this one shirt that has a very specific floral design with
pyramids mixed into it. It was an exclusive design from a niche Youtuber
I like and I know for a fact there weren’t that many made. 
Now, I can already hear you typing. 

“Maybe he just likes the same Youtuber you do.”

I said that to myself fifteen times when I saw him wearing it, but shut
myself up when I noticed a light blue ink-stain right around the breast
pocket. 

I have that exact stain in that exact color in the exact spot on my own
shirt. It was from an accident at work, and there was no way in hell
that he should have that exact stain in that exact spot on that exact
shirt. I was so convinced it was mine that I ran to my closet just to
make sure, but lo and behold mine was still there…. 

I wish I could say that it’s all stopped there, but there are other
things. 

For one, he wasn’t married and he’d never once mentioned a girlfriend or
friend with benefits or anything like that, yet I knew a woman lived in
that house with him. I’ve seen her silhouette in the windows of the
house when he’s been out, and I’ve watched someone rearrange his
furniture on occasion. I’ve never really gotten a good look at her face,
but I have seen her clearly moving floral throw pillows around,
cleaning, folding blankets, and even watching TV. All of her movements
are odd, though.

One time, I happened to look over from my sitting room window and saw
the back of her messy hair propped up in a recliner. I was on my way to
the kitchen, so I hadn’t meant to linger, but the flat-screen in front
of her caught my eye. 

It was just static. No sound or fidgeting with the settings. She was
just sitting there, comfy and content in front of swirling black and
white. I didn’t stop looking until I saw her head crank to the side, as
if preparing to swing around at me. I went to the kitchen and made lunch
without looking again that afternoon, but later that evening noticed
that all the blinds in the house were shut tight. 

All of this is weird, and from every friend I’ve been brave enough to
mention this stuff to, I’ve heard the same arguments for rationality. 

“Neighbors are weird,” is the most common one, and they’re right. People
do odd things, and I’m no exception. For all I know, they might be
acting strange because they think their next-door neighbor is a
fanatical stalker who can’t stop watching them from his house. For the
past month, that’s what I’ve told myself, and it was a fine explanation
to soothe the nerves for when my anxiety got the better of me. But what
leads me to post this and ask for advice stems from an encounter I had
this past week. 

Jerry came to my house one evening around seven. He rang the doorbell
and, to my surprise, was dressed in a dirty t-shirt and shorts. His eyes
were tired and dark from exhaustion, and his hair looked pretty awful.
It was greasy, and bits of grass were peppered throughout it. I noticed
the other green and brown stains on his clothes and immediately became
concerned he’d had some kind of fall, but he smiled regardless and
spoke.

“C-can I come in? Please?” he said, his voice shaky. “I know this is
probably a bit strange, but I’d really like to talk to you.”

I told him I had work in the morning and was about to head to sleep, but
he persisted.

“It’ll only take a minute… Please, I’d say it here but…”  he looked back
at his house with concern, and then down the opposite street. “Well,
nosy neighbors, right?”

That lit my face up like a match, and I suddenly got clammy. The way he
said it didn’t carry any note of suspicion about my bad habit, but did
try to mask some very obvious paranoia building in the man’s demeanor.
Against my better judgement, I let him in and sat him down in the
kitchen. Once we were in a space with no windows, he seemed to relax a
little and even asked for a drink. I gave him a bottle of water, and he
explained the stains away with some extensive yardwork he’d been at for
the past two hours. 

“I feel that I need to apologise to you,” he said, capping his bottle
and wiping sweat from his brow.  “I’ve been a bit cold to  you, I know,
and I need you to know it’s not your fault.” 

I tried to politely deny his claims, but he persisted. 

“I have bad memories here.” He gestured at the house around us and
tapped his bottle against the counter in thought. “I grew up in this
neighborhood and have been in that house since the eighties. I grew up
in that place, and I’ll most likely die there. Poetic, ain’t it?” He
laughed a little and even got a smirk from me, small as it was. Jerry
had some humor after all. It faded quickly and that sad expression
returned to his eye. “My best friend lived here back in the day. It
don’t look like it’s  changed much since then…” he got up and started
toward the stairs. He pointed up them and said:

“We used to go up to his room to play pong on the Atari. If we got bored
from there, we’d just mess about the stairs or race outside in the
drive.” He put his hands in his pockets and looked at me. “He went
missing our senior year of high school. His car was still in the
driveway and, best anyone could figure, he’d somehow just disappeared
into the night. His poor folks were devastated, and nobody in town
believed he’d gone of his own accord. He was a football star and had
just accepted a full ride to some big Ivy League place out east. Had his
whole life ahead of him and then… poof.” 

I was honestly shocked to hear so much personal info out of a man I’d
written off as the human equivalent of Wonder Bread, and it’s safe to
say by that point I felt like a huge piece of shit. I felt pressured to
say something reassuring after a monologue like that, but Jerry didn’t
miss a beat. 

“My folks split not long after that whole event, and it’s been me and ma
ever since. I’m sorry, I guess I never told you about her. She’s going a
little cooky these days, so she doesn’t go out much, and I try not to
introduce her to folks. She gets confused too easily.”

His mother. He takes care of his mother, and I’d been spying on him out
of some crazed notion of protecting my privacy. I wanted to apologize,
but his face got spacy again at the mention of her. It was that same
face I’d seen linger for hours on his front porch. He almost appeared to
mutter something for a split second before shaking his head. 

“I say all of that to say, I’m sorry if I’ve behaved oddly around you.
My folks troubles left some issues that I’m still sorting out and well…
family can hurt. You get it, right?” 

I said I did, and he started his way to the front door when something
stopped him. He looked at a portrait I had hanging on the wall near the
stairwell. It was just some bust portrait of a random dude from the 18th
century that my mother had given me as a house-warming gift. I thought
it made me look distinguished and classy, but Jerry looked at it in
horror. It was as if some awful secret had been divulged to him that he
couldn’t unhear.

“You good, man?” I asked, doing my best to prompt him from his stupor.
He shook again and said:

“Yeah… yeah, I’m sorry I just…” he put on that fake smile I’d grown
accustomed to. “Might want to cover that up sometime. Dust can ruin an
oil painting.  At least the face. You should cover up the face. That’s
the part you don’t want to forget after all, right?” He didn’t wait for
me to respond before saying. “I best be getting home to ma. Goodnight,
Billy!”

I didn’t even have time to correct him on my name before the door closed
behind him. 

I’ve been doing my best to write off the weird behaviors since that
encounter with Jerry, but something about the way he left had an impact
on me. I don’t know why my painting had such an ill effect on him, nor
why he called me Billy, but it’s been needling around in my chest for
days. He’s an old man with issues, I know, but his apology still didn’t
explain everything. The shirt thing still puzzles me, and I’ve yet to
see any of the yard work he had supposedly spent hours working on.
Still, I didn’t have anything solid to justify my fears. 

That is, until a few nights ago. 

I was woken up at three in the morning by the sound of shuffling
downstairs. Now, I know the horror movie rule of not investigating scary
noises in the dark, but I do own a shotgun and have good insurance, so I
wasn’t afraid to call the cops the old-fashioned way. I took my gun and
my phone downstairs, ready to either place someone under citizen arrest
or put them in an ambulance, but my entire downstairs level was empty. I
flicked on my lights as I went and listened for more scuttling, but the
house was dead silent. I cleared every room and closet, checked every
window and door lock, but found no sign of disturbance outside of my
own. I didn’t have a basement, and the attic was the size of a coat
closet. There was no one in my house, and I was wandering around
half-awake with a loaded firearm. I put the gun down on the kitchen
counter and went to pour myself some milk, but was stopped cold in my
tracks. Written almost microscopic on my dry-erase calendar were the
words:

“KEEP THE BLINDS CLOSED”

I almost pissed myself and wanted to call the cops, but I couldn’t. What
was I going to say? There’s some tiny writing on the “reminders” section
of a marker calendar and I can’t remember writing it? Believe me, I
wanted to call them, but the fear of being labeled a paranoid lunatic
pressed strong against my lizard brain. I forced myself to go back to
bed and tried to write the whole thing off as me being forgetful and
over exaggerating the noises of a settling house. 

I need you to understand me. I need everyone reading this to understand
me. I’m taking every precaution I can to make sure I don’t come across
as crazy, but the more I notice the worse I become. I can’t prove it,
but I’m positive Jerry is copying me. I don’t know why, but I know he
must be. 

He has to be, because on the way to work this morning when I glanced at
his house I saw into his living room through the front window. 

A thin woman standing with her back turned to me, gazing at a familiar
portrait from the 18th century. 

Any advice is welcome. I’ll update when I’m able.
