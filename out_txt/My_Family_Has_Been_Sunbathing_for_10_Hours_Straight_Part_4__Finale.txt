Thank you to everyone who has continued reading my story up until this
point. Your belief and support have given me a renewed sense of purpose.
I never imagined so many people would be open-minded about my story,
especially after so many people had scoffed at it in the past.

I woke up in a haze. My head was pounding, and I needed water badly.
Rolling over onto my side, I saw a half-drunk plastic bottle of water
sitting on the coffee table, which I opened and downed without a second
thought. My eyes slowly adjusted as I looked around the room. I realized
it wasn’t mine.

Like my room, it was dimly lit with windows covered by blackout
curtains. Unlike my room, it REEKED of weed and had a couch instead of a
bed.

I am in a stranger’s living room.

The TV was on showing the same looping screens of the local news
channel. I watched it flick past the scene of the human pile in the park
back to the farmers market as the memories of my last conscious moment
came back to me.

Hudson is dead. I watched them pull him apart. I watched them drag him
under the pile. I went completely numb as the shock and sorrow of losing
my friend washed over me once more. I stared blankly at the ground.

How did I get here?

I had spent the last 24 hours being more frightened, sad, and exhausted
than I had ever been in my life. I wanted so badly to lay back down and
fall asleep again so I could forget the sound of my best friend’s
screams and the sight of the smiles on my family’s faces as they sat in
their prison of sunrays.

My family is still sitting in the backyard.

The thought sent a jolt of energy through my body.

How long have I been asleep? My family can’t last much longer, it may
already be too late.

I had to know. Did I still have time at least to save them? If not I
would simply walk out into the sunlight and suffer the same fate as they
did, no point in living if they too are gone. No point in living alone.

I looked around the room again, there was a bong on the table, a dimly
lit kitchen attached to my left and a hallway to my right. I got off the
couch and headed to the hallway. On the ground next to the couch were my
shoes, helmet, balaclava, my dad’s winter jacket and my backpack.

Someone undressed me, I realized.

I rummaged through my backpack looking for my water bottle but it wasn’t
there.

I tiptoed up the hallway where I saw an open door to a dimly lit
bedroom. Peeking past the lip of the doorway I saw the silhouette of a
heavy figure hunched over a computer. The man pushed away from his desk,
readjusted himself, bent down over it and snorted what I now know to be
cocaine before promptly scooting back up to the computer and typing away
again. “No, no, NO” he slammed down both fists on his desk, “fucking
idiot, how could this be CGI, I am a person not fucking Lucas Films.” He
aggressively began typing away again. I didn’t know what to do to get
his attention, do I knock? Do I clear my throat?

“Excuse me,” is what I went with. He stopped typing and pushed away from
his desk. The chair squeaked under the strain of the weight as he turned
to me. He wiped his nose and said,

“Oh, hey.” He turned to wipe the desk away too and looked back at me,
“uhhhh how are you? Did you drink the water I left you?”

“Yeah. Thanks.” Normally this whole weird social situation would have
been a nightmare for me but I had no more mental energy to give to
anxiety. He looked like a mix of every guy I had ever seen at the card
shops I bought Pokemon from.

“How long was I out for?”

The man turned around and checked the time on his computer.

“Uhhh, it’s been about 3 hours since I picked you up from the park. I
came as soon as I saw you on the news but you were already passed out
when I got there.”

Oh thank god.

I let out a heavy breath. Every hour mattered if I were to save my
family. I had feared that I had slept way longer but that was probably
just because of the heat exhaustion. Only three hours lost was not
really a problem, I still had plenty of time before they were in serious
danger.

I sized up my saviour. He was a big guy, he would definitely be strong
enough to help hold back my parents until I dragged my sister into the
house. Shit, between the two of us we could probably save all three of
them given how weak they are bound to be at this point. I needed to play
it cool, this stranger was my best and probably only shot at saving them
with what little time they had left.

“Do you have any more water?” I asked.

“Oh yeah, for sure.” He pushed himself out of the chair with a grunt, he
squeezed his way past me through the hallway and I saw that he was
fairly tall too.

Perfect.

“Sorry about the bong,” he said, motioning over to the coffee table,
“it’s my roommates, I don’t ya know, do that stuff.” He sniffed and
rubbed his nose before opening the fridge and rummaging around for a
fresh water bottle. “Ah shit, is tap okay?”

“Yeah, thanks.”

He grabbed a cup out of the cabinet and moved some of the piled up sink
dishes out of the way and onto the counter just so he could fill up my
glass.

“I gave you some of that water earlier after I got you out of that park,
but uh,” he handed me the cup, pointing at where my half-drunk water
bottle previously was on the coffee table. “But I couldn’t pour too much
at a time because you started to choke.”

I gulped down the entire thing in one go. He was staring intently at me,
drumming his fingers on the countertop. I could tell he was as eager to
talk to me about this whole situation as I was to ask for his help.

Unable to restrain his excitement anymore, he leaned in and in a quiet
but excited tone said,
“This shit is fuckin crazy, right??” His pupils were huge, and his
breath reeked. I could tell he hadn’t showered in at least a few days. I
was more than a little uncomfortable with being here alone with him but
here we were. Before I could even answer he started rambling.

“I was watching them on the news and through my window all day yesterday
I barely got any sleep! I checked all over the internet, NO one is
talking about it. Its like it is only happening here, it makes no
fucking sense.” He looked at my uncomfortable expression and said ,
“Sorry dude, I will try not to curse so much, not used to kids really.
How old are you anyways? 9? 14? Nevermind doesn’t matter, I have been
dying to talk to someone who isn’t completely lobotomized by the sun. I
have been sending videos and emails, making blog posts, fuckin
everything! No one believes me, can you believe that shit?” He held out
his arms like it was the most obviously ridiculous thing ever.

“Yeah, it… it doesn’t make any sense,” I offered. I was thinking of how
I could smoothly transition the conversation into convincing him to help
me when he continued,

“No man, no it doesn’t. I have been video taping everything, the news,
the people outside, I filmed the whole drive to the park and that freaky
pile of people. I have been writing countless notes documenting how
their behavior has changed with exact timestamps that match the videos
and these fucking idiots still don’t believe me!” He pointed to his
computer through the open door down the hallway.

“How much more proof do they need?” I tried to fake sympathize with his
frustrations in an effort to butter him up.

“I was thinking about that actually and uh.” He drummed his fingers on
the countertop a little quicker, “Another witness might be just what I
need to convince them.” He raised his eyebrows at me waiting for my
response.

“Yeah, no, totally.” The path forward was becoming clear in my head, “I
could do that for you.”
He slapped the countertop smiling hard like he couldn’t believe all he
had to do was ask, “God, I knew you were going to be fuckin cool the
moment I saw the Master Chief helmet!” He held out a fistbump which I
reciprocated. “Listen kid, we are going to make SO much money. I was
thinking we could split it basically straight down the middle, 60-40
since, you know, I did all the documentation but trust me kid, you
deserve that 40, hard.” He started putting on his oversized leather
jacket and winter gloves. “No point in wasting time I say, let’s head
out ASAP. I was thinking we could go straight to Ely and find the police
or local news or whatever and get this story moving up. This is national
shit, global shit even. You and me, we are going to be fuckin heroes
were gonna-”

“Sorry, I meant to ask, but what is your name again?” I interrupted
knowing full and well he never gave it.

“Oh dude,” he laughed, “Greg, sorry bro I completely forgot to introduce
myself, I haven’t been this excited in a while if you couldn’t tell.
What’s your name my man?”

“I’m Andrew—”

“Andrewwwww,” he said, holding out a fistbump, which I reciprocated once
more, “nice to meet you bro, we are about to be the goddamn legends of
Goldfield. Greg and Andrew, sick-”

“-Uh so I was thinking, instead of splitting 60-40,” he immediately
stopped what he was doing and stared at me full attention for the first
time since he started rambling, “Well, the thing is I don’t need any
money honestly, so you can have all the money, uh like 100-0. But I
could really use your help with one small thing before we go to Ely.
Uhm,” I tried to think of the best way to make the request,
“My Mom, Dad and Sister have been in my backyard since yesterday
morning. I tried to get them in the house but I wasn’t uhm,” I felt my
voice shake a little as I thought back to my father kicking me in the
head as my sister was dragged away.

“I uh- I wasn’t really strong enough to get them in the house, ya know.
But I figured you could help me just get them in the shade real quick on
our way out and then we can go to Ely. We have money we can give you
also, and uhm I have some pretty good Pokemon cards and an Xbox if you
want. We will give you whatever you want really, I just need some help
cause I am getting pretty worried about them.” I was starting to break
again,

“Whoa kid, slow down, no problem no problem I am being dumb, of course
you have a family here.”

I looked down wiping away the tears, “Save your pokemon cards, I’ll help
you with your family, of course, it’s the least I could do, you’re doing
me a huge solid coming with me kid, really, you’re a superstar.” I
thanked him through my bit lip as I worked to regain my composure. “Um,”
he continued as he patted me awkwardly on the back. “I will take some
cash though if you guys have some, for, you know, gas and stuff, if
that’s cool.”

“Yeah, that’s cool,” I said wiping away the last tear, “I was also
thinking we could maybe wait a little for them to recover so we can get
them dressed up and take them to a hospital in Ely.”

Greg didn’t respond immediately, “Uhh I don’t know kid we really will
need to move quick in case someone else gets there first, you know.”

“But they could be witnesses too! Think about how good that would be for
your argument! Think about it, they would be really good evidence of
what the sun is doing to people because they would still be super
sunburnt. Plus my Dad’s a cop, he would vouch for you.” That last part
was a lie but I was desperate and throwing whatever incentives I could
at him to convince him to help save my family.

He was drumming the countertop again before he rubbed his nose and
sniffed, “Probably met him before then,” he chuckled before looking to
the side in deep thought, “……and they won’t want to split the money
right? Still 100-0 right?”

I had no earthly clue as to what money he was talking about but I didn’t
care either, “100-0 just like I said, this is our best chance. Honestly
we need my family if you think about it. I mean, if the people out there
already don’t believe you, why would they believe a dumb 12 year old
like me?”
“Alright, alright, fuck it, you’re a good negotiator, let’s do it.
Just,” he sighed in frustration, “if you don’t mind, try to follow my
lead, we may make a stop to take a quick video or two on the way out for
more evidence, you get it.” He said as he walked over to the couch and
sat down, lighting up the bong.

I may have recruited a junkie to save my family.

“Of course! You are the leader, no problem.” I was starting to regain a
semblance of hope. I had thought for sure I wouldn’t find anyone to help
me let alone a grown man strong enough to drag them inside.

Greg and I got geared up as we prepared to leave. I filled up some empty
plastic bottles with water and he shoved all his notes, his computer’s
hard drive, a metal Altoids tin, and his camera into his backpack. He
geared up in baggy brown cargo pants, snow boots, his jacket and gloves
and, I shit you not, a full metal knight’s great helm. He had broken the
earpieces off multiple pairs of sunglasses and taped the lenses over the
helmet’s eye slits.

“Pretty fuckin sick, right?” he said through the muffled helm.

“Yeah, fuckin’… awesome.” I replied, attempting and failing to match his
enthusiasm.

“One more thing,” Greg said, opening his closet and rummaging through
it. He pulled out a replica of Frodo’s sword from Lord of the Rings and
handed it to me.

“Can’t be too safe, you know?” he went back to rummaging through the
closet.

“Does it glow blue when orcs are close?” I asked sarcastically as I
looked at it.

“No, but that would be sick if it did.” he said as he pulled out a full
replica of Gandalf’s staff.

“No way,” I said in involuntary disbelief. This guy was literally and
figuratively the biggest nerd I had ever met.

“Oh yeah,” he said through the helm, raising it up to the ceiling and
admiring it. He had mistaken my disbelief for admiration which I didn’t
correct. “Now we are ready.”

We made our way out of the apartment to the black Dodge Caravan in the
parking lot. There were a couple people spread around the property. I
looked at the little playground nearby, one woman was laying on the
picnic table while another slowly spun around on the small
merry-go-round. I couldn’t look too long at the kids.

“Fucked, right?” Greg said through his muffled helmet.

Obviously.

“Cool ride.” I said ignoring the question. I was thankful for his help
but I was getting a little angry thinking about the fact that he sat up
in his apartment filming from the window instead of coming out to help
at least the children.

“Thanks, yeah, it’s actually Dan’s, my roommate.” he said pointing out
my window. Dan was standing arms out in the middle of the parking lot in
just his underwear.

“Did you not try and bring anyone back into the shade?” I couldn’t
restrain myself.

“Well, I wanted to. I thought about covering up from the sun and going
out there but I was too afraid to try it until I saw you on the TV.” he
chuckled, “You are an absolute madman going out there, what were you
doing in the park anyways?”

Hudson is dead. He’s under the pile.

I didn’t respond.

Stop thinking about it, stop thinking about it, stop thinking about it.

“My friend was there.” I managed to say.

“Ah, shit, sorry kid.”

We sat there in awkward silence for a second silently breathing in our
helmets. He started up the car.

“Uhm… where do you live?”

“5 minutes from the park, just take a left on Jackson St. and I will
show you from there.”

“Shit, that’s on the entire other side of town.” he said, reversing out
of the spot, “we are going to have to cut through the city. I’m going to
go ahead and warn you I saw some pretty fucked up shit going through
there earlier so uh, you might want to keep your eyes down once we get
in there.”
“I’ll be fine, thanks.”

“If you say so.” he reached into his bag and pulled out his camera and
clicked it on, “Mind filming for me then?”

I took the camera and held it up to the windshield as we drove. We were
just outside downtown Goldfield so we got there in no time. I wondered
if we were going to cut through the town square with all the squabbling
people. I thought about the old lady laying face down in the road that I
saw on TV earlier., She was the first person I saw die. I really hoped
we didn’t have to drive past her.

Despite my clear reluctance to talk, Greg continued to pepper me with
questions as we drove.

“So when you first saw your parents, how did you know to cover up before
going out?”

“I didn’t. I went out there with them. I was stuck with them like that
for a few minutes.”

“WHAT?!” he threw a quick look at me, “You went out in the sun? What did
it feel like? How did you get out?”

I thought about all five of us sitting in the backyard together. “It
felt amazing…”
I still had the thought bloom in my head occasionally. How easy it would
be to just take off all my gear and walk into the sun, letting it melt
my pain, sorrow, and stress away again.

“Can you give more detail? And please speak up. I am struggling to hear
in this thing.” He tapped his helmet.

I looked at him. This motherfucker was interviewing me and his camera
was recording the audio. “I don’t want to talk about it right now. I
will tell you all about it after we bring my family inside the house.”

“Well… okay. But what works to get them to stop being like that?” he
pointed to a shirtless guy leaning against a brick wall rubbing his back
against it like a bear scratching against a tree.
“You just need to take them out of the sunlight, I think.” I thought
back to Hudson when I pulled him under the shade of the tree. How I
managed to get him to return to himself.

I only saved him for 2 minutes.

“So what, could you have just like, thrown a tarp over them or
something?”

“What? No. I don’t know. I doubt it. My own Dad beat me up when I tried
to bring my sister inside so I doubt a tarp would have worked.”

“Yeesh.” He shifted uncomfortably in his seat, “good thing I brought the
staff.”

We didn’t talk much for the rest of the ride, I filmed the scenes of the
streets as we drove by. Animals and people alike spread out across the
sunny streets avoiding the shadows cast by the surrounding buildings
like the plague. A mother stood arms out smiling at the sun, stroller
left forgotten behind her. A businesswoman and a homeless man lay
holding hands side by side on the sidewalk in utter bliss. A kid not
much older than myself stood arms spread on top of a bigger man who had
succumbed to the heat. I saw dozens of people, old and young, black and
white, male and female in different states of undress slowly sunbathing
their lives away.

“What the fuck is that?” I said pointing to the side of a building. Greg
slowed the car to a stop as we looked up. There were a few construction
workers sunbathing on top of the building but what I was pointing at was
on the side of it. The sunlight was hitting the full face of the
building and one of the construction workers, evidently in an effort to
get as many rays as possible had fastened two ropes to the roof,
undressed, tied the ropes to his wrists and lowered himself off the side
of the building. The man would nod off, and then shoot his head up and
stare at the sun smiling before nodding off again. Greg took the camera
from me and pointed up at the man zooming in on him.

“Still think this shit’s CGI? HUH? This is the shit I am talking about!
This whole town has lost its fucking mind and it’s the sun’s fault!” He
was yelling into the camera, evidently at the non-believers from
whatever blog post he had failed to convince earlier.

“Can we go?”

I was tired of the horrors. I felt like people were constantly trying to
one up ways to get the sun’s attention. They acted like addicts pushing
themselves to extreme measures to get their fix. I just wanted to save
my family, leave Goldfield and never return.

We hopped back in the car and continued driving until it was Greg’s turn
to say, “What the fuck?”
All the way down the street we saw someone else walking. I thought for
sure we were alone but sure enough they were lumbering across our view.
We looked at each other to make sure we were both seeing the same thing.
He drove over to them honking his horn a few times but they paid us no
mind. As we got close we saw the man, he was a bald guy, his head
extremely red, lips blistered and he just continued to walk down the
street.

We stepped out of the car and called out to him but he ignored us. He
wasn’t covered up, he was fully under the sunlight but he wasn’t facing
the sun either. Then out of an alleyway to our left a woman stepped out,
following the bald man. She too ignored us and the sun but continued to
walk down the road. We hopped back in the car and slowly followed them.

“This is new,” Greg said, driving the car slowly alongside them. Dozens
of people walked out from side streets, peeled themselves off the
ground, got off the top of cars and all began walking like a horde of
zombies to the center of town.

When we made it to the center square of the city, it was so packed with

shoulder to shoulder filling in the space, no longer squabbling over the
sunlight but forming neat rows. They no longer outstretched their arms,

looked back up at the sun, opened their mouths and eyes as wide as they
could and waited. It looked like a sea of baby birds waiting for the sun
to come down and feed them. It was terrifying. I didn’t know what was
happening but I didn’t want to find out.

“Greg, please let’s just go to my house, I don’t like this, it can’t be
safe.”

“Listen kid,” he said, turning to me, “I understand your urgency, I get

“We had a deal-”

“The deal was I would help your family right? I am still going to save

I thought about it, I would stay in the car and if any crazy shit

God please don’t let this day get any worse.

“They are just standing here all in a row now. Before this if any single

“WHAT ARE YOU DOING, YOU FUCKING NUT?”

This man won’t save my family.

“WHAT ARE YOU?”

“WHAT ARE YOU DOING HERE?”

I will run back to my family if I have to.

I am in a field of sunflowers. I thought as I stepped into the crowd.

I followed sheepishly behind him. I am not sure what I expected when he
approached us. I am not sure what I expected to happen next. But despite
not being under the divine influence of whatever power his sun held I
still felt the power of his command. The weight and wisdom behind his
every movement was so clear to me. He had a draw to him, like every
paternal figure in my life rolled up into one.

He said nothing as he removed the small sack from his hip and opened it
up, taking a single seed out of it pinched between his long pointer
finger and thumb and pressing it down onto the tongue of one of the
onlookers. They closed their mouths and swallowed the seed before
sitting down and closing their eyes. He repeated the same process,
moving from one to the next, as if he were planting seeds in a field
silently working while I just watched.

“How did you escape my sunlight?” he asked without looking at me.

I shuffled uneasily as he continued silently working his way through the
crowd. “Uhm… I have a condition.” I cleared my throat and tried to speak
up fearing he couldn’t hear me through my muffled helmet.

“I have a condition called Xeroderma Pigmentosum, so uh, I can’t be in
the sun…” he didn’t respond, he just pulled another seed out and planted
it on another tongue. I continued,  “Well, I can go in the sun but I am
not supposed to be. My Mom was always very strict on me about staying
out of the sun because one time when I was little I went outside when my
parents weren’t looking and wandered around the yard for a few hours and
got so badly burned that I had to go to the hospital.” I gulped, staring
at him in anticipation for his response.

He planted two more seeds but still said nothing. I thought about the
question again:

’How did you escape my sun?’

“When I was outside with my family I saw my skin turning red and got
scared. I got scared of getting burnt and scared of getting in trouble
for being outside without my UV protection. So I ran inside where it was
safe.”

He planted another seed and said, “I see.”

He continued working, silently hunched over each person, looking them in
their eyes as he pressed the seed on their tongue. I turned around and
looked back at Greg between the rows of people. He was undressing now,
staring past me at this creature. I looked at the pile of clothes behind
him and saw the keys shining on the side of his belt. I took a step
towards them.

“Where are you going?” the creature asked with its back still turned.

I didn’t move a muscle, even though this creature showed me no malice, I
feared him. I feared him not because of what he would do to me but what
would happen to my family if I lost my mind, if I became part of this
field of people.

I told him the truth, suspecting he knew the answer anyway, “I need to
save my family.”

“But I am already here.”

The shock of the response sent a wave of grief over me. Though they were
just words, I knew the finality of them to be true. He spoke the exact
thought that entered my mind the moment his feet touched the ground.

“What are you?” I asked, tears forming in my eyes once more.

He turned to look at me and raised a long, pointed finger to the sun. I
looked up at it. It was not as bright as it was before, it was
developing a faint orange glow to it as he worked, the rays glittered a
little less, and the world seemed to start to dim for the first time
since the last sundown. He went back to working, making his way down the
line.

I felt an anger and a helplessness bubble up once more, I had tried and
tried and tried to save my family and my friend. I had gone through hell
these past two days, I had been beaten, and traumatized. I had been so
alone in this horrific experience and this creature who was, what,
claiming to be the fucking sun was the cause of my anguish. I lashed out
as any cornered wounded animal would. I forgot all respect and
reservation, and I yelled at this thing, “WHY DON’T YOU JUST KILL ME
ALREADY AND GET IT OVER WITH? WHY ARE YOU TORTURING ME?”

He stopped what he was doing, closed the sack and reattached it to his
belt before walking over to me. He got down on one knee and craned his
impossibly long neck down to look me in the eyes. Through the slits of
his golden mask I could make out the outlines of his ancient,
human-looking eyes. But I couldn’t see too well through my visor.

“I don’t want to kill you… It is time to take your helm off.” He reached
down grabbing my helmet but I swung my short sword up, slashing his hand
and retreating a few steps back. From the wound dripped liquid gold. He
held his hand up to the sunlight and the gold sizzled and hardened
before he wiped it off. The wound had closed in an instant.

“I WILL NEVER TAKE THIS MASK OFF, I WILL NEVER BECOME ONE OF YOUR FREAK
SLAVES. I WILL NEVER BE LIKE HIM!” I pointed back at Greg who had now
already joined the others, mouth and eyes wide open, standing in his row
of the field.

The creature stood full height and looked down on me as if I were some
bug in his garden.

“Would you rather stay or leave?” His words came out slowly and
deliberately as if it were the only two options in the world.

“I don’t understand.” I felt myself begin to panic as the presence of
the ultimatum began to weigh on me.

“You can stay, and live with your family, you would all live happily
together under my sun. Or you can leave, I will take you to the river
and the current will take you out of this place back to your world.”

“But this is MY world!” I cried out in defiance.

“No. It never was.”

I was seething with rage, and trembling with fear. He was taking my
family from me, he was taking away our lives and our futures. I raised
my sword up to him, “I won’t let you do this.”
He grabbed my sword before I could even begin to strike. He observed it
before tossing it to the side. Then in one quick motion he grabbed me by
the back of my jacket and picked me up. I screamed and thrashed and
yelled a number of obscenities. But under the intense heat, in my full
gear I tired myself out quickly. He walked me out of the town square and
out of the city. I rebelled the whole way but he paid me no mind.
Suddenly we began walking up to a river I had never seen before. To this
day, I am sure that this river did not exist in our town. As the river
came into eyesight, my insults and threats turned into pleading.

I begged for him to spare my family, I begged for him to make just this
one exception for them, I begged for him to just spare my sister at
least, to take me instead. The closer we got the smaller my pleads
became, by the end we were standing on the edge of the river bank.

“I am sorry…” I sobbed, “I changed my mind… Please let me stay with my
family… please…”

But it didn’t matter, he lifted me up, and tossed me in the river like a
fish that wasn’t big enough to keep. The water was impossibly cold. I
felt the current sweep me under and away, and watched the sunlight on
the surface fade as I went with it. That was the last time I ever was in
Goldfield.

I woke up about 8 miles outside of Ely. I had washed up on the bank of
the river. It was incredibly cold, and dark and my clothes were soaked
through. I stood up and stumbled to the nearest road, and marched for
about a mile before I came across a gas station. The clerk called 911
and they sent two park rangers out to get me. They gave me fresh clothes
and interviewed me extensively. They asked me where I came from, why I
was wearing a Halloween costume helmet, where my parents were and how to
reach them. They assumed I was some kid who got carried away playing in
the forest and chalked it up to that in the end despite my frantic
insistence that my story was true. I got aggressive when they told me
there was no town called Goldfield, when they denied its existence and
refused to even bother sending help out there. They simply didn’t
believe me.

I was eventually put in the foster care system. They couldn’t find my
parents or any relatives (I had none that I knew of), they couldn’t
prove they ever even existed despite me having an abundant amount of
information about them.

I was a real challenge for the psychiatrists. My mind was stable despite
my outburst. From all the tests they did, they determined I wasn’t lying
or simply acting out but on the other hand nothing I said had evidence
to back it. The official report said that I had suffered PTSD and
delusional disorder from being lost in the woods and falling into the
freezing river.

Because they found no relatives, I ended up being stuck in the foster
care system until I turned 18. I got a job and moved into my own place,
continued to go to therapy and really tried to move on with my life in
the small ways that I could. It has been hard to make and keep friends
and girlfriends. I often find that I simply live in another reality from
others. I will be honest with you all, I have thought about killing
myself plenty of times since then. However, the thought of all of
Goldfield dying with me has made me change my mind every time.

This story is the only true written record of Goldfield. I couldn’t find
any, so I wrote this in the meantime. I really hope you all never forget
this story. Somehow that creature of the sun had erased all of those
people from history. It erased my family, it may have erased someone
from yours. Check your photo albums, check your family tree, check your
file cabinets. Maybe there is someone you forgot, someone you don’t
quite recognize, they may have lived and died in Goldfield. Try and
learn their name, and if you can, don’t forget it. There are no graves
for them or loved ones to pay their respects. They just exist somewhere
else now, and in my memory.

Please reach out if you find any more proof of their existence. Thank
you.
