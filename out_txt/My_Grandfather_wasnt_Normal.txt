My grandfather wasn’t normal. Not in the way he was just a wacky old
dude; more like he was an undiagnosed mental patient. It took years of
therapy and half a bottle of wine just to type out this story. My
therapist recommended writing this as a way to cope, but I’m not so
sure.

It was the end of summer break, when I was about 10, right in the middle
of the 2008 financial crisis. My parents had to work two jobs to make
ends meet. They could hardly afford the time to take care of the needs
of a 10-year-old, and one glance at our bank account made having a
babysitter a wet dream. With no other choice, they left me with my
grandpa for most of the week. I still remember when they first drove to
his small cottage in the middle of bum fuck nowhere. The stench of
cigarettes and mold stung my nostrils as my dad walked me to the door.

“Don’t worry, honey,” he said. “It’s just for a few weeks.” He hugged me
and kissed my forehead before leaving, though his words fell flat.

I watched my parents leave through the window in their red shitbox
roadster as, across from me, this scruffy, frail old man sat in the
recliner in front of an old box TV. Periodically, he coughed up phlegm
and spat it into a red Solo cup. His house was nothing remarkable; it
felt like walking into a crappy one-night-stand motel. The TV was in the
middle of the room. A large, beat-up sofa smelled of mothballs to the
right, along with a portrait painting of a clown that made me uneasy.

“You’re rooms down the hall,” He wheezed, eyes glued to the TV screen.

“Thanks,” I replied awkwardly, distracted by what I could swear was a
rat running under the couch.  

The room was surprisingly lively compared to the rest of the house. It
was decorated as if meant for a child. A large teddy bear sat in a
painted wooden chair in the corner, and a bunch of those creepy
porcelain dolls sat on the shelf. My first few nights went relatively
smoothly. Grandpa and I barely interacted beyond what was necessary. I
guess, like my parents, he didn’t really want me around. He was just
unlucky enough to be stuck with me at this point in time.

Things didn’t get strange until the third night. Around 3:30 in the
morning, I heard pacing footsteps. I figured it was just Grandpa getting
a glass of water, so I pulled out my headphones and listened to music on
my iPod and went back to sleep. The next morning, I got up and walked
into the living room. Everything looked normal, but I couldn’t help but
feel uneasy. Then I spotted that damn clown painting looking a little
different. Before, it faced straight forward, but now it seemed slightly
angled. Maybe it’s just my imagination; “I probably didn’t get a good
enough look at it” I thought. The rest of that day was pretty normal.

A few nights later, I heard that shuffling again. I didn’t think as much
about it this time; however, this time I heard a sharp creak as it took
one step down the stairs, then went back to pacing the hallway. That
really freaked me out, so I put on headphones again to drown out
whatever he was doing up there.

I barely got any sleep that night; the anxiety of not knowing what was
going on up there made it impossible to sleep. I came out into the
living room and saw Grandpa sitting by the TV, as usual.

“Good morning, Grandpa,” I said, trying to relieve my tension with a
friendly conversation.

 He looked over at me with empty, soulless eyes, nodded, and twitched
back into place; that didn’t help my anxiety much.

The next couple of nights weren’t much better. The pacing went from the
first step to halfway down, to the entire staircase. Tip-tap creak thump
creak thump creak thump, on and on all night. After a week of this, I
decided to ask Grandpa what it was all about. However, as soon as I
walked into the living room, I froze. The room looked like a scrambled
Rubik’s Cube with the furniture in all different positions. That
painting of the clown that damn clown now had him walking downstairs and
peering around the corner. Grandpa sat in his recliner, acting like he
was watching TV. Except the TV was behind him, facing the wall on the
other side of the room.

I knew I had to do something. However, calling my parents was out of the
question. What would I even tell them? That grandpa walks around at
night and rearranges his furniture? Was that thing walking around even
Grandpa?

That night, I pushed the dresser in front of the bedroom door. I sat
under my covers and waited. 3:30 on the dot, I heard Grandpa’s bedroom
door swing open as the scurrying began. I was petrified with fear as I
heard him start going down the stairs, putting one foot on the ground
before spitting back up and pacing again. That over and over, taking one
step closer with every repetition. Eventually I heard him entering the
living room, then the hallway, then right outside my bedroom door. Then
I heard the doorknob jiggle.

“Hey there, kiddo, let your old grandpa in,” I heard a voice wheeze. I
didn’t respond; tears were streaming down my face.

“Please, I think someone is in the house.” The voice rasped

His voice sounded wrong; it was too deep, too guttural, like he was
speaking with his stomach.

“LET ME THE FUCK IN!” he roared as he slammed his fist against the door.

BAM BAM BAM

Every hit sounded like gunshots as the dresser rocked with each blow.
Panic filled my measly body as I began hyperventilating. I felt my heart
palpitating in my chest, my intestines wrapping around themselves as I
began to feel sick. The realization that if I didn’t act now, I was
going to die hit me as I looked around for an escape. A window. I
scurried over and tried to open it. Rust glued it down as I pushed
upwards with all my might. I heard the wooden door crack as adrenaline
gave me the strength to finally unstick the window. I cleared the tree
line and didn’t look back. I ran as fast as I could through the woods,
getting cut up by shrubs, tripping over roots, and slipping on leaf
litter. Only when I saw the sun peek through the trees did I stop.

A hiker couple found me a few hours later, bruised, cut, and bloodied. I
was crying and  screaming that my grandfather was a monster and that he
was going to eat me. The police later raided Grandpa’s house, and they
said it was the weirdest place they had ever been. The recliner was
somehow attached to the ceiling, and the couch was gutted of all its
stuffing, and the TV was still facing the wall. Grandpa’s room was no
better. A hole was dug in his mattress and filled with stuffing, pine
straw, and twigs, like a big squirrel’s nest. They later found my
grandfather naked in the attic, pacing and digging into his skin with
his nails. He spent the rest of his days heavily medicated in a mental
hospital. They also found book upon book filled with nonsensical
ramblings and symbols.

I’ll probably never understand what truly happened that day, and I don’t
want to. I just hope posting this story will finally help me move on.
