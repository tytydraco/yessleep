“It’s GONE?”

 

“Yes, Simon, I’m looking again, and it’s not there.”

 

My brother, Issac, was always a weird one. Not scary weird, but just
weird weird. When we were still living with our parents, Issac would
almost never be home. He was always staying after school for this
astronomy club he was in. When he came home, he would immediately go to
bed.

 

When we went away to college, we all but lost contact with each other.
Once in a very blue moon, he would text for birthdays or just random
pictures. My parents and I never questioned this, we figured he was just
busy with college stuff.

 

After college, he moved to a quiet suburb town, and I bought a house in
Queens, New York. Issac lives on Long Island, somewhere in Eastern
Suffolk. Somewhere rural. He was bragging to us once he moved in that
there’s a Verizon tower right outside of his house, which happens to be
our provider.

 

This morning, though, he texted me that the tower was gone. The text
chain continued:

 

“How could a 200-foot-tall cell phone tower just disappear overnight?” I
text, skeptical of his claim.

 

“Hell if I know. It’s just gone. The foundations and fencing are still
there, but the tower is just gone.” Issac replied.

 

“Send me a picture.” I tried calling his bluff.

 

I wait for a few minutes, and I get a notification from Isacc with a
photo attached. I look at the picture, and I’m taken aback. Sure as he
said, I see a cell tower site with the absence of its star attraction.

 

“How the fuck?” I text, confused. “Is this photoshopped? Or AI?”

 

“No, I swear. Come see for yourself if you don’t believe me.” He
challenges.

 

I stood in my living room, debating whether to indulge in this. It’s a
Saturday and I have nothing else to do, so I eventually cave.

 

“Fine. But when I get there and see the cell phone tower, you’re paying
me for gas.” I shoot him a text.

 

No response. Whatever, I think. I grab my keys and head out the door.

 

I forgot how far Issac lived from me. Despite living in the same state,
the drive was almost 2 hours, with nothing but the radio to entertain
me. To make matters worse, it was storming out. Why did I agree to this?
He’s just bullshitting.

 

One thing I hate about New York is the traffic. Nothing but
bumper-to-bumper stopping and starting. I can’t for the life of me
understand why my brother moved to Long Island of all pla-

 

My thoughts are interrupted by the jarring sound of the Emergency Alert
System. The tones play for about 15 seconds, then an equally-as-jarring
voice blurts through the radio.

 

“—A 911 telephone outage emergency has been dispatched for Queens
County, Kings County, New York County, Richmond County, Westchester
County, Nassau County and Suffolk County. Until further notice and for
reasons unknown, 911, along with local emergency service phone numbers
are not functioning. During this situation, if you have any emergencies,
if safe to do so, make your way to the nearest hospital, police station,
or fire station. Both police cruisers and fire chief vehicles will be
patrolling 24/7 until this situation is resolved.”

 

The EAS alert makes itself known once again with the loud klaxon-esque
sound, before my radio returns to music.

 

Damn, I think. Did the cell tower behind my brother’s house cover
everything within a 150-mile radius or something?

 

Later than sooner, the road under my car turns from asphalt to dirt. I
look towards where Issac’s house is, and I’m once again taken aback as
his house comes into view over the hill, but no cell tower. I see the
concrete base, the fencing, the cooling station, but no cell tower.

 

 I step out of the car and walk over to Issac, who’s holding his phone,
frustrated with it. He looks at me.

 

“There you are,” he says. “I was trying to call you for the past 20
minutes, but every time I did, you just hung up on me. What the hell?”

 

I stand there, confused. “What? I didn’t get any calls from you. It’s
probably because your cell tower is gone.”

 

Issac shakes his head. “There’s another one about 10 miles that way.” He
points down the road. “It should cover me.”

 

“That’s weird. I got an emergency alert while driving here about police
and fire station numbers being down. Maybe that has something to do with
it?”

 

Issac eyes me weird. “Did something happen to the other one too? … I’m
gonna go check it out.”

 

I nod. “I’m coming with you.”

 

Issac climbs into the driver’s seat of his car and I climb into the
passenger seat. He drives off towards the cell tower he said was 10
miles away. The closer and closer we draw to it, though, I don’t see
anything. Issac seems to be thinking the same as me, because I hear him
whisper under his breath “what the fuck?”.

 

We step out of the car when we arrive and are met with a familiar sight.
The concrete base, fencing, and cooling are all there, but the cell
tower is missing.

 

“How?” is the only word I can think to say. There’s no way two cell
phone towers could have just vanished into thin air.

 

Issac shakes his head slightly. “I don’t understand,” he says. “Did
Verizon take these towers down?”

 

I shake my head no. “If they took them down, you would have heard it.
They also left everything else, which I doubt they would do.”

 

Our attention is turned to the car’s radio, which instead of playing the
music it was before, is now blaring with another EAS tone. God, I hate
that sound.

 

The familiar voice crackles over the AM.

 

“—The following is broadcast at the request of local authorities. A
shelter-in-place order is now in effect until further notice. As of
right now, move to the nearest building you can. Go inside, lock all
doors and windows, and do not go outside under any circumstances. If you
have anything that you are able to barricade against doors, proceed to
do so. Stay away from windows. This message will repeat every hour until
the situation is resolved. If possible, stay near a radio or television
for updates about the unfolding situation.”

 

Issac and I look at each other, worry plastered on both of our faces.
Wordlessly, we both clamber back into his car and drive off. The look of
worry never leaves Issac’s face.

 

We pull back into Issac’s driveway and climb out of the car, hurrying
into his house.

 

Issac calls out to me. “You lock and barricade all the doors, I’ll get
the windows.”

 

I nod and lock all three doors in his house; the front door, the back
door, and the door leading to his garage. The garage door is easy to
barricade; there’s tons of heavy stuff around. For the back door, I have
to settle for jamming it shut with a door, tilted under the knob and
locked into place so the door can’t be pushed open. For the front door,
Issac has already pushed a large sofa chair against it.

 

“Simon!” I hear my brother call. I head into the living room where he
is, to see he has the TV on. The same shelter-in-place order from the
radio is playing on the TV.

 

“What the hell is happening?” I ask, knowing Issac won’t have the
answer.

 

Issac shrugs. “I guess we just have to wait and see.”

 

I look at the TV, confused on… Well, everything. The cell towers are
gone, and now there’s a shelter-in-place order? I’m mentally trying to
connect the dots, but they just don’t tie together. I realize I’m
muttering out loud to myself, and I try to clear my throat to save
myself from a weird look from Issac.

 

After a few minutes, the screen on the TV goes black, save for the sound
of slight static. Text then flashes on screen, with the EAS tone once
again jarring me out of my trance. I read the text on screen, which
says:

 

EMERGENCY ALERT SYSTEM

Civil Authorities

issued a

Civil Danger Warning

 

The voice over the TV drones:

 

“—The following is broadcast at the request of local authorities. The
shelter-in-place order from before has been updated to a civil danger
warning. If you are receiving this alert, stay inside under all
circumstances. It is not safe to go outside. Repeating, stay inside
under all circumstances. It is not safe to go outside. Do not respond to
any knocking, doorbells, or yelling from outside your shelter. If
something knocks on your door and claims to be a police officer, do not
respond. Hide from all windows and stay completely silent.”

 

Me and Issac are glued to the TV. What the hell is going on out there?

 

The alert continues:

 

“As of right now, check to make sure every and any entrance to your
shelter is blocked and secure. Shut off all lights and blind all
windows. Instructions will stay on screen for the foreseeable future.
This alert will now go silent. It is recommended you do too.”

 

And just like that, the alert goes completely silent. Text explaining
the situation and instructions given to us linger on the screen.

 

Issac, almost immediately after the alert goes silent, walks over to the
window.

 

“Issac, what are you doing?”

 

Issac looks at me. “I want to see what’s happening out there.”

 

Before I can stop him, Issac pushes the sofa away from the front door
and swings it open, stepping outside into the pouring rain.

 

“Issac, stop!” I whisper-yell, getting up to chase after him.

 

“…What the fuck…?” I hear him mumble to himself.

 

“Issac!” I clamber over the sofa and meet Issac outside, grabbing him by
the arm. “Issac, get back inside! You heard what the alert said, it’s
not… Safe…”

 

My voice trails off as I look at where Issac is looking. Down the
seemingly-infinite stretching field of grass behind Issac’s house, stand
two cell towers. The same ones that disappeared from near the house. In
the distance, something is approaching. What the fuck…?

 

I look closer, barely making out the shape. “Is that tower…”

 

Issad finishes my thought. “—Walking?”

 

The tower joins the other two, the three standing in a triangle.
Suddenly, I hear the distant sound of metal contorting. In perfect
synchronization, all three towers’ tops point inwards, touching in the
center. Where they touch, a small light emanates.

 

A distant, but all the more deafening horn sounds out from the towers.
That sound seems to jolt me and Issac enough to run back into the house,
slam the door shut, lock it, and shove the sofa back in front of it.

 

I’m sitting here now, typing this, with the same text on the screen,
explaining the situation unfolding outside. It’s been almost a full day
now, with the same warning on the screen. Nothing since then has
happened, and it’s been hauntingly silent outside.

 

If anything else happens, I’ll update. But for now, me and Issac are
stuck inside his house while the cell towers walk outside.
