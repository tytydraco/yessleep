I remember as a child being warned by my parents, supervising officers
and the authority of the camp. Not to go out at night alone, not to
answer cries for help you don’t recognise, for It may be on the other
end. You’d think it was a fairy tale, a horror story to keep your child
in line.

I didn’t like staying at home all day, that natural desire to explore,
to see the beautiful world beyond your window. The idea that something
could exist out there that made even the bravest of us have night
terrors never crossed my mind when I approached the doors to the exit,
so many times I was close to leaving, only to be stopped and reminded.

“Did you not listen to the Beast Watch, Adam?! There’s reports of that
thing nearby! You wanna get yourself hurt?!”

Beast Watch was a national program at this point, like the weather
report in the morning. Reports of where it had been seen, were they in
packs, if any camps were nearby, etcetera. I don’t understand why I
didn’t believe it, I guess it was the closest I got to a conspiracy
theorist. It only existed to keep us in line, keep us inside and such.

The steel door was freezing cold that day, I remember so clearly. It was
the middle of November, the snow had begun to fall a little already. I
managed to sneak out to go play in it, I must’ve only been 12 or so. The
radio had been broken for a few days, so no Beast Watch was heard, so we
didn’t know if any of them would be nearby. You’d think they’d go into
lockdown, keep everyone inside, but for whatever reason, it didn’t.
Everything was the same. The security guard at the main door, Larry,
still took his nap at the same time. Everyone was occupied with warmth.
I was occupied with making snow angels.

I felt the crunch of the snow beneath my feet, my shoes soaked
immediately, my feet felt like they were full of nails, but I didn’t
care. I could see a patch of untouched snow in the distance, maybe about
half a mile away? A perfect spot for snowmen, angels and whatever my
imagination could muster.

My mind was like in eternal tunnel vision, all I saw was snow, trees
covered in it, materials for my artwork. I occasionally saw a few
animals, gathering as much food as they could for hibernation. Squirrels
with cheeks full of acorns, a single fox carrying multiple small animals
in its mouth, before it went beneath a tree to hide in its den.
Hibernation seemed so fun, eating and sleeping for months straight. It
sounded like heaven.

It took about 10 minutes to arrive at the heavenly patch of snow, nobody
there, no signs of people. Excited, I began to roll balls of snow
together, making a snowman you could see from the camp’s entrance,
deciding halfway through I’d name him after the security guard - Larry
the Snowman. I found some small rocks for his eyes and mouth, I had
nothing for his nose, and needed to find sticks for his arms.

“Hmm..where could I..”

I wondered, looking around, only to see a set of trees on the other side
of the hill I was on, out of view from the camp. I pondered going down,
thinking for a moment, before waddling down the hill to go grab some
twigs. I started to feel weird, as if my gut was telling me to leave. I
just attributed it to me being in the cold for a while, or that I felt
so cool for being outside the view of my camp.

I eventually reached the trees, looking at some branches for small
twigs, jumping to try to rip them off. As I did, the feeling got worse,
like a pit in my stomach, as if I hadn’t eaten in days, or how I felt
when my parents scolded me for leaving without permission.

I soon enough turned around, only to see what looked like a flower in
the ground. I’d never seen one like it before. The whole exterior was
green, the inside pink, untouched by the snow. I was enthralled with it
as soon as I lay my eyes on it, I could barely collect my thoughts. I
could smell it from a few feet away, all that was on my mind was the
flower, no longer Larry.

I stepped closer, dropping my twigs, as I peered into the inside of the
flower. It didn’t have seeds like a sunflower, or a pretty yellow centre
like many others. It had white barbs, my mind thinking it was a
carnivorous plant. Like a venus flytrap or something.

The snow shifted. My heart sank. It was as if something moved, as if
someone was buried in the snow in front of me, behind the flower. Cracks
in it formed, the snow having sat unmoved for days led to large clumps
forming, now breaking apart. That’s when I saw it. An orange ball. I
stared at it, as it stared back. I’d never felt so afraid in my life.

It. The Thing. I laughed at stories of it, a ghost story, a campfire
fright. My breath shook like an earthquake, my eyes soaken, fingers numb
and shaking. The snow broke a bit more, as I took a step back, the smell
of the flower still filling my lungs. Soon, the flower disappeared,
dropping into the snow as if pulled under, only for the snow to fully
break. I had turned to run by the time it emerged, I only caught a small
glimpse of it.

Green, sharp teeth, large arms and legs, a face of thunder, and the end
of its tail looking identical to the flower I was so enamored by.

I screamed as I ran, hearing it reply, a bellowing roar as heavy
footsteps stomped behind me. I wasn’t an athletic child, I was freezing
cold, my feet were beyond numb. But I ran faster than I can run even
now, my thoughts occupied by nothing but the terror of what may be
lurking behind me.

Eventually, I got inside, the large metal door slamming shut behind me
as a loud crash was heard from the other end. A deafening howl from
across the steel barrier. It barely sounded terrestrial, like a beast
from the stars, or from the horror films I caught snippets of my parents
watching. I was sent to my room, and there I sat, shaking and afraid. I
wish I listened to them.

Every time I talk to my own children now, it returns. The primal
instinct to do nothing but run, the begging to whatever God exists to
not be mauled by the monstrous creature behind you. I don’t know what
scares me more. The fact my children wish to play in the snow against my
advice, or that I believe it’s still there. That flower in the middle of
the trees. Waiting.
