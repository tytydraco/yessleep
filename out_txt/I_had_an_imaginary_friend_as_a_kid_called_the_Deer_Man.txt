When I was 10, my grandparents moved from the hilltop house in western
New York where my dad grew up, to a smaller, ranch-style cottage in a
nearby retirement community.
 
Don’t get me wrong - my grandparents were very kind, caring people. But
I don’t think they were ever truly cut out for the whole “grandparent”
gig. So it was really just going from one boring house with a nice
sledding hill, to another boring house.. without a sledding hill.
 
We were told they just couldn’t keep up with the property anymore, and
who was I to question otherwise?
 
We actually spent the night there whenever we visited when I was little,
before my mother put her foot down on the constantly revolving door of
cats that would make her allergies go haywire, and we started spending
the night with my cousins 30 minutes away.
 
There were two constants with these visits. The first was that we always
slept in my dad’s childhood bedroom, which had an 8 point buck on the
wall from the one time my dad tried hunting.
 
The second was that, from the time I could talk, I would always mention
seeing the Deer Man. My parents had assumed I’d meant Bill from down the
road, who delivered venison. I had shaken my head and held my fingers up
like antlers. They decided it was an imaginary friend, the deer head on
the wall come to life, and hoped I would grow out of it.
 
And grow out of it I did. For about five years or so. Long enough for me
to completely forget about Deer Man.
 
Then, we went over to go sledding for a day between Christmas and New
Year’s when I was in fifth grade. The not-so-fun thing about sledding is
that it’s surprisingly exhausting. So, come late afternoon, we were
pretty much spent. But I was determined to get in one ride in the dark,
so I stayed out there.
 
By the time the last light left the sky, I was alone there at the
treeline.
 
Behind me, I heard a twig snap. And then another. I turned in time to
see the head of a deer emerge from the trees… followed by a body that
was neither deer nor human, but some wrong blend of both.
 
It was thin to the point of emaciation and covered in wispy tufts of
hair.
 
I wondered if anyone would see us, standing there, against our backdrop
of darkness. I looked at that second window from the left, above which
hung that giant deer head, and realized the antlers on the thing in
front of me matched them a little too well.
 
And then I snapped out of it. The deer-thing was gone. Anyone looking
would have just seen me, standing there, staring blankly into the trees.
 
I wandered inside. The rest of the evening was a haze as I tried to work
out what I’d seen. Whether it had been the cold or my exhaustion playing
tricks on me. I never told my parents about any of it. I didn’t want
them to think I was crazy.
 
They sold the house three months later. They said it was too much work,
but there were things that made me wonder if they had seen what I had
seen.
 
Like the way we still went sledding, but always left before sunset, and
always someplace visible. Or the way that we double checked to make sure
every door, every window, and every curtain was shut and locked every
night. What was most telling, though, was that we never did any of it
back home.
 
Years went by. The Deer Man slowly faded from memory, again. Both of my
grandparents passed while I was in college, and the fall after I
graduated, we went up for a long weekend to clean out their house.
 
We were 30 minutes out when my dad asked me if I wanted to take a
detour - by the old house on the hill. He knew the owners - they had
been friends of his parents - and he just needed to call and let them
know we’d be coming by.
 
We walked around for a bit before sitting down on a bench in the
backyard, where he asked me if I knew why they had sold the house. I
said it had been too much effort to maintain at their age.
 
Out of nowhere, he asked me if I remembered the Deer Man.
 
And it all came flooding back. The way that thing had stepped out of the
trees. The way it had stared me down, seeming to size me up. The way
those black eyes had seemed to pierce my soul.
 
Then my dad told me he’d seen it as a kid too. Always wondered if it had
been real or not. When I had mentioned it for the first time all those
years ago, was when he’d realized it was real. There wasn’t much he
could do though but keep an eye on me, and he never let me out of his
sight whenever I brought him up.
 
That was a few hours ago. We’re at my aunt’s house for the next few
nights, right down the road from that house.
 
Her house backs up to thick forest - the same forest, that my dad and I
both saw the Deer Man in. The sunroom looks out onto the tree line, just
like at my grandparents’ house.
 
She and my dad both went to sleep hours ago. I’ve been up with a book.
 
A few minutes ago, her dog started barking at something outside.
 
I could open the curtains and check and see if anything is outside, but
I’m not going to.
 
Because I’m terrified of what I might see.
