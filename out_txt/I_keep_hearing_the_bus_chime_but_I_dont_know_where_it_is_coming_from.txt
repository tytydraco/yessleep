I have always had issues with hearing and seeing things that weren’t
always real. My mother always told me that I had issues with it ever
since I was a child. She called them night terrors, and the things I
would see were always so childish. Monsters under the bed and the
boogeyman were monsters I was quite friendly with as a small child.
Never formally diagnosed with anything, but the last time I talked to a
therapist about these issues was four years ago in college. She simply
said it was stress, which made sense considering the last time I had
them was after a major life event. These apparitions were usually things
I feared. Things any other person would fear, like insects coming out
from wherever I lay or strange figures in dark corners of my room, but
lately it’s been a bus chime in the far distance that I first heard on a
study abroad trip.

 It started months ago, not long after my father had passed and I was
finishing up my college education. The visions started again, and I
found myself waking up in a cold sweat. Often reaching for a glass of
water to drink and coughing roughly after downing it. Not long after his
passing, I was in Morocco near the Atlantic Ocean. We had a van to
travel around the country, but often found our vehicle passing buses
wherever we went. Surprisingly, some parts of this country had amazing
public transportation, but the chime the buses made as they whipped up
and down the drags somehow stuck with me. The chime was quaint, elusive,
yet raucous and would echo and bounce off the walls of my mind once it
had been long gone. When I came back to my home country, I found myself
still hearing it once in a while in the former present. Originally, it
was distant, even in areas where it made no sense for me to hear it.
Hearing the forlorn, perpetual, distant toll as I bathed, cooked, and
did other monotonous tasks.

When I arrived back in my father’s home, I had to sell his belongings
and the home due to the lack of a will. He had lived in the sticks of
Virginia, with chickens in the backyard, and we had our fair share of
“barn cats.” One late night, I woke up because I heard hissing from the
cats. It was abnormal behavior for them, and I went out into the
backyard. It was a hot summer night, and the air was sticky from the
sprinklers running a little too long. The evergreen air clinging to the
fleshy confines of my lungs and the scent of damp wood shaving coaxing
its way into my nose. The bottoms of my pajama pants were sticking to my
ankles from the heels of my sandals sinking into the pliable dirt.
Clicking my tongue out of frustration when I heard something snap in
conjunction with my auditory annoyance. I looked around, but only found
my father’s rooster, his body frantically writhing as if trying to find
rhyme or reason, but his head hung limply and flung side to side at the
frenzied motion. His harem of hens were visibly startled at the
disturbance and the passing of what was their protector. I watched as
his body slowed its movements until it finally gave way to its final
moments and gave way to gravity. Bag him up, feeling his warm corpse
heat the plastic as I held him close. I could feel the dew that clung to
feathers begin pooling in the plastic grocery bag. Quickly placing him
into the freezer to postpone the decision of what to do, I glanced in
the bag to see that he was soaking wet. 

The following day, waking after the sun had risen, I got out of bed to
feed the chickens. Scooping their bug-filled feed to treat them after
seeing what they had, guilt consuming me as I knew how they probably
felt. Had to shake the feeling, though, as I returned to the kitchen to
get the corpse and start a brew for the day. Hard as a rock, as I took
him out of the sad excuse of a body bag, crystals coated his
oil-slicked, color-coded feathers. Digging him a grave near the coupe
and tore into the dirt that my heels had been digging into the previous
night. Heard the dismal bell of the bus that I knew was nowhere near as
my fingers pulled up the damp earth and stringed rootage of the grass.
Placing the rooster in his final and moist resting place. Some of his
defrosted black downy feathers sticking to my forearms. I feel as if I
kept losing parts of him, his belongings, his memories, his everything.
After covering my father’s beloved bird, I look up at what would’ve been
a typically clear sky, only to be met with an overcast sky with grey,
weighted horse tail clouds. Headed back inside, knowing that the sky was
likely to open up today and seal the grave further. Pouring myself a
steaming cup of coffee, thunder cracked open the storm, and with it came
a ringing pain through my head. The droning of the chime was
skull-splitting. Gripping my head in pain, I went over to the medicine
cabinet to grab painkillers in hopes it would go away, but the ringing
got closer, and it was overwhelming. Collapsing and knocking myself out
from the severity.

Waking from finding myself on the kitchen floor, my phone lit up only a
stretch within reach. Looking past it, I saw the storm had passed, and
everything had been painted in gold by the late afternoon. Sitting up
slowly, I plucked my phone up and scrolled through my notifications.
Facing the inevitability of being homeless and jobless, I had begun a
desperate search for work. My grimy, dirt-dusted finger flicked over the
screen, hoping for a positive response from one of the many jobs I had
applied for. Among the rejections, I found one with not even an
interview, but an offer for something I had applied for while I was in
Morocco. Figuring it was better than nothing, I accepted it without a
second thought and sluggishly stood to finish the remnants of my day.

After some time had passed, many unfinished tasks that my father had
left for me had been dealt with. Leaving me homeless, broke, but
hopeless. All roads led me back to Rome, or in this case, it was
Morocco. Bringing me back to the place I had been just after my father’s
passing and the first discovery of the chime. High on a hill, the air
was thin, the wind was wet from the sea, but it was peaceful. The
setting was quite opposite to my newfound purpose there. Work was
tiresome, but it kept my mind busy enough to not let my mind manifest
anything unnecessary. That chime hadn’t come back for some time, and I
was paranoid about when it would decide to rear its ugly head.

One of the days of work ran awfully late with back-to-back meetings;
some of my coworkers deemed that our department well-earned an evening
out at a local bar. Figured a drink or two wouldn’t kill me, and I could
say I made good use of my time there. The bar was lackluster, to say the
least. Filled with smoke and misfit men that prey on the local
prostitutes, filled with plastic. Looking past the slum and sleaze of it
all, I was able to find a little piece of home in it; I felt at home.
After a few glasses of wine and many other things. Our group left, and
we dragged a few strangers along with us to wherever we’d stumble to.

We went to the beach; the warm wind slapped harshly as we navigated our
way to the water. The moisture of the day had seeped into the sand, and
my heels dug deeper the closer I got to the shore. Memories flickered
back to the dirt, my father’s rooster, and the depth of the night sky
compared to his feathers. Feeling the contrast of the gritty debris
against my callus-ridden feet skin. Looking out to the water as it
crashed into the shore. The cold froth it left behind pulled away from
my toes until I purposefully stepped into it. In the moment, not only
did I feel the chill of the sea; I felt dread. The snap of the memory of
the rooster was followed by the familiar haunting chime. It was loud,
splitting, and made my vision shake, causing me to stumble roughly into
the rigid waves on the sandbar. My clothing became soaked through as my
group laughed and giggled at my discoordination, but their noisy
escapades grew distant in my mind. 

I was suddenly alone with the salt of the sea as it beat against my skin
and the sand that it hadn’t kicked up. It was freezing compared to
everything I had felt that night. Looking up, I was met with the night
sky, the water, my father, and the distant chime of the bus.

He was there, in all his glory, the way many had painted him to be. He
was a bright star amongst the night sky, the light that guided me, and
all I wanted to do was to hold onto him. I went deeper into the water,
and it didn’t take long till I was up to my shoulders. The water was
trying to push me back to shore, but I needed to see him and feel him
one last time. I screamed his name till my throat was torn, my eyes
burned from salt of my own, felt the sand embed itself into my skin.
Reaching to him as close as I could be and he looked at me. When he
spoke, all I could hear was the bus chime and the bright light of his
presence.
