One morning I woke up and he wasn’t in bed. 

He’s usually still sleeping next to me when I get up. I called out his
name, but he didn’t answer. When I went into the living room, he was
sitting on the couch, watching TV.  

It wasn’t a show or movie. It was one of those YouTube videos of a
fireplace. Just logs burning and the sound of fire crackling.

I asked if he’d been up all night watching.

“Yeah,” he said. 

I didn’t think much of it. He works from home and stays up later than I
do. I had a big presentation that day, so I was mostly worried about
that.

At lunch, I mentioned it to my coworker. He said he and his boyfriend
sometimes take too many edibles and sit on the couch watching the Apple
TV screensaver.

I asked if they’d ever stayed up all night doing that. He laughed. He
thought I was joking.

When I came home, my boyfriend was making dinner. He asked about my
presentation. Everything seemed normal. It was Friday night, so I
suggested we watch a movie. He laughed because I always fall asleep when
we watch movies together. I read somewhere that it happens because you
feel safe with your partner.

He put something on and I cuddled up against him. I don’t think we made
it thirty minutes before I fell asleep.

The next thing I remember was waking up, feeling something wet in my
eye. 

I wiped my face. There was saliva on my fingers. 

I was still lying against his chest. His mouth was open. Drool running
down his chin onto my face.  On the tv, the fireplace video was back on.
I said his name but he didn’t look down. I sat up and said it again.
Nothing. He just stared at the fire with his mouth hanging open. 

Something inside my body told me to get away from him.

I went into the bedroom and locked the door.

Nothing happened. After a while, I felt ridiculous and unlocked it. But
I couldn’t sleep. I could hear fire crackling through the wall.
Eventually I took some benadryl.

The next morning, he made me breakfast.  He never apologises when he
does something wrong. He asked me how the food was. I just blurted it
out.

“Please stop watching that video.”

“Okay.”  He looked down at his plate. I thought he’d argue or ask what I
was talking about, but he looked guilty.

Everything was fine for a while. He came to bed with me. We’d watch a 
show. I started to feel embarrassed about how scared I’d been.

But then we went out to a brewery with some friends.

They were mostly my friends, so he was quieter than everyone else. That
wasn’t that odd. Our food finally came and we were passing plates
around. The TV above the bar was playing sports. Someone asked the
bartender to change the channel. He started pressing buttons on the
remote, like he was having trouble with it.

Somehow he opened YouTube. For a few seconds, the fireplace video was on
the screen.

My boyfriend stopped chewing his food. He turned his head and looked at
the screen. Mouth open. Everyone at the table noticed. I tried to make a
joke. 

The bartender finally got the football game back on. My boyfriend stood
up so suddenly he almost knocked over his beer.  I thought he was going
to the bathroom, but I didn’t realize he left until we asked for the
check. He wasn’t answering his phone. I lied to my friends and said he
wasn’t feeling well. Our car was gone. I had to take an uber home. 

As soon as I opened the door, I could hear the crackling.

The whole place was dark except for the TV. He was sitting on the couch,
face lit up by the fire.

I stood with my hand on the light switch. I was scared to turn it on. 

When I did, he turned and looked at me. 

“Oh, hi babe.”

He sounded completely normal. I started yelling. I asked why he’d left
me there. Why didn’t he answer his phone? Why did he need to watch this
stupid video so badly?

He said it relaxes him.

“It helps with my stress.”

I asked what he had to be stressed about. It was a shitty thing to say,
but I was angry that he abandoned me at the brewery to come and watch a
fire. He didn’t answer. He’d already turned back to the TV.

The next morning I had to leave for a work trip. My boyfriend did
something he never does.
He begged me to stay. Usually he jokes about having the place to
himself. This time he stood by the door while I got ready.

“Please, just stay.”

I asked what was wrong.

“I just want you here,” he said. 

But there was no way. I had to go. It was a big research project. I told
him I’d be back in a few days. 

For most of the trip, he replied to my messages. Short answers, but that
wasn’t unusual either. On the last day, he stopped responding. I called.
Texted. Called again. The whole flight home, I had this sick feeling in
my stomach.

I’ll never forget the smell as soon as I opened our door. 

Sour. Sweat and something else. I had to cover my nose.

He was on the couch. The same clothes I’d seen him wearing before I
left.

I don’t know if he’d been sitting there the whole time. He looked like
he had. His shirt was damp.

The fireplace was still playing on the TV.

I screamed out his name.  He didn’t move. I went closer. His lips were
dry. His face was thinner. I grabbed his shoulders and shook him. He
wouldn’t look at me. 

I picked up my suitcase and threw it at the TV. The screen cracked. The
picture went black. The sound stopped.

His head turned. He looked straight at me. I’d never seen him this angry
before. He stood up without taking his eyes off me.

“What are you doing?” I asked.

He didn’t answer. He stepped toward me. I backed away. I started running
to the bathroom. He ran after me too.

I got into the bathroom and locked the door. 

Then everything went quiet. I kept calling out his name. 

No answer.

I put my ear against the door. I heard his footsteps move away. 

Then I heard them coming back. He slammed into the door so hard it hit
my forehead. I fell backwards onto the floor. He did it again. And
again. I took out my phone and called 911. The police were on the way,
but one of the hinges started pulling out of the door frame. 

I opened YouTube. We shared an account. The video was right on the top
of our watch history. My hands were shaking so badly I kept missing it. 

The door broke open. My boyfriend had a kitchen knife in his hand. I
pressed play and held the phone up. He looked at the screen. The knife
slipped out of his hand and hit the floor. He took the phone away from
me, sat on the toilet and watched it. 

I stayed on the floor for a few seconds. I couldn’t make myself move. I
squeezed past him to get out and was close enough I could smell his
shirt again.

I ran out of the apartment. I got in my car and started driving. I
didn’t know where I was going.  I pulled over at some point because I
couldn’t breathe. I think I was having a panic attack. 

I ended up at my co-worker’s apartment. At first, I told him we’d had a
fight. I don’t know why I said that. I was crying so hard. 

My boyfriend is in the hospital now. The police told me he was still
sitting in the bathroom when they arrived. They tried to take the phone
from him and he went crazy. They had to shoot him with a taser. I
haven’t gone to see him yet.

My co-worker’s boyfriend asked me about the video. He brought up YouTube
and I helped him find the channel. They watched the video, but nothing
happened. They got bored by it. 

I watched it carefully. For the first time, I really watched it. Just
logs burning in a brick fireplace. But then I noticed one of the logs
had a mark on it. 

A little symbol on the cut end, hidden by the flames. I took a
screenshot and did an image search. Most of the results were useless,
but eventually we found a picture of the package of firelogs. 

The same symbol. It’s a brand. A small company makes these logs. We
found their website. Their address is on the website. It’s four hours
away. 

Tomorrow, I’m going there.

I want to know who made this video.

I want to know what’s happening to my boyfriend.
