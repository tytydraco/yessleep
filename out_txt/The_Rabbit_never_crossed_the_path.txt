I worked as a delivery driver for a courier service over the summer. I
quit after a month.

 

The company mainly delivered appliance parts, what exactly they were I
have no idea, and I didn’t care. What was great about this job for me
personally is I could do my deliveries anytime between 7:30pm and around
noon the next day, this meant I could do them at 2am if I felt like it.
As a night owl who loved to speed, this was perfect. I could listen to
scary stories on my drive through empty roads, and make use of the
express lanes/tollways, which were paid for by the company.

 

How it worked was the packages got delivered to an office building, then
from there the drivers, I think there were around four of us, would take
what was on our route and then go deliver them. My route had ten stops
that I could get, but I never got all of them in one night. Some stops
are on my route every day, some only about half the days, and some very
rarely. The most stops I ever did was eight, usually I have between four
to seven. The less the better, I get paid the same even if I only had
two for a night.

 

Again, great job. Only around four hours of work, sometimes less, of
simple driving at night.

 

On the last day of my job, it was business as usual. I got to the office
actually earlier than I usually do, it was just before midnight. I was
the only one there. I had five stops to do. I loaded the packages into
my car, all fit just fine. I never had an instance when I couldn’t fit
them all in, even with my small and frankly shitty car. The back bumper
was gone, and the front was held together with gorilla tape and zip
ties. I treated that car badly.

 

Once I got the packages inside, I went back in to get my free snack. The
layout of the office goes as follows: the front door leads to the main
room where all the packages await to be taken, then go out the main room
into a hallway. To the right of the hallway, there are two bathrooms,
and a door to the kitchen. To the left are two offices and a storage
room. The storage room door was closed. I only mention this because
usually it’s just left open. I gave it an eyebrow raise then went into
the kitchen. The snacks are more health oriented, like protein bars,
jerky sticks, things like that.

 

I immediately noticed all the empty wrappers left littered on the floor,
about a dozen, and one of the boxes that held the chips was torn down
the center. I sent a picture of it and a message to my boss saying
someone decided to be a slob tonight. I threw all the wrappers away,
grabbed a blueberry granola bar, and went to wash my hands. I am a
spastic hand washer and will do it any chance I get even if I just
touched the top of my shoe.

 

As I was washing my hands, I could’ve sworn I heard a loud thump
outside. I always leave the front door to the office open, since it
makes taking packages out easier. Probably just an eighteen wheeler
passing by I thought. When I got into the hallway, was the storage door
fully closed or left slightly cracked? At that point, I couldn’t
remember. I walked over and peeked inside. Nothing but boxes and two
bicycles that belonged to my boss. There was also this strange smell
inside. No food or anything that could have an odor was ever stored in
there, so I had no idea what was causing it.

 

Once I was on the road, things were getting irritating. I had just
gotten a wobbly tire replaced, but I swear the back of my car was kind
of vibrating, or like I kept bumping over stuff. I was dreading having
to go right back to discount tire, I hated going to that place. Just
tossing away money for a piece of garbage. I loved having a car, but I
hated owning one.

 

It takes about forty minutes to get from the office to my first stop,
then from there each one after is around fifteen to twenty-five minutes
from each other. My third stop was in a neighborhood. The house I
deliver to is a part of it, yet it feels just outside it, like there’s a
line separating it from the actual neighborhood. It’s the only house at
the end of the street, and across from it is a wall of trees. There’s a
single, very small, and very dim standing porch light that illuminates
the area.

 

I got the package and walked up to the house, using my shirt as a shield
for my fingers to open the iron gate. Just before I stepped onto the
porch, I could’ve sword I heard a thump from behind me. I turned to
look. Though I was never really scared, this particular stop always made
me feel a bit uneasy at this time of night.

 

I nearly woke up the entire neighborhood when I heard a loud rustle from
the bushes next to me. I jolted my head back to the porch. It was just a
cat scurrying by. I put my hand on my heart, let out a small laugh, and
put the package down.

 

On my way to my fourth stop, I had to get gas. I always keep paper
towels in my glove box to use to grab the gas pump with. Of course I
forgot to put more in, I thought as I saw there weren’t any left. I said
fuck it for this time and decided to grab the germ covered gas pump. My
fourth stop is unique in that it’s the only place I have to go inside to
leave the packages, which is nice because it gives me a chance to use
the bathroom if I need, and also in this case wash my hands.

 

My fourth stop is right across from another neighborhood. It’s a funny,
almost cartoon-like array of small pointy white houses. My stop leads me
to pull onto a short gravel road. There’s a fun game I like to play on
this road, to see if the rabbit crosses. If it does, it means good luck.
Every night one would cross two times, once as I was pulling in, and
again as I left.

 

I turned onto the gravel road, and there it was. Running from an open
field that sloped slightly downward, crossing my path and disappearing
into tall grass.

 

I unlocked the door and put the package inside. I went to the bathroom
and went pee. I heard a loud thump from outside. Like the office, I
didn’t close the front door. I thought it was probably an eighteen
wheeler passing by. I took extra care washing my hands this time, using
a method surgeons use. I took some extra paper towels for my glove box.

 

I went back outside ready for my final stop. My trunk was wide open.

 

I never opened my trunk, none of my packages were in there. Did I open
it on accident? Did I auto pilot press the button thinking that one was
in there?

 

I stood there confused. I looked around, there was dense tall grass to
the right that leads into trees. I try not to stare, my mind can often
make things scary and imagine something’s hiding in the dark.

I checked my trunk to make sure I didn’t put a package in there. It
smelled awful in there. I quickly shut it, then did a count of all my
packages. I had the correct number, I thought. I got back in my car and
backed up to get out of there. Since the road is gravel, I had to
reverse slowly or else my tires would make an awful noise. I slowly made
my way out of there. My mind jumped from the thought of the trunk to now
solely focusing on the rabbit.

 

I wondered if tonight was the night my good luck streak would end. I
waited. The rabbit never passed again.

 

I was sad, let out a “damn” and turned right off the gravel. I drove up
to the stop sign at the end of the street leading out into the main
road. I checked both sides, and before I turned, I saw something in my
rearview mirror.

 

A figure standing in the street.

 

It was far behind me, right next to the tall grass by the gravel. I was
so confused, it’s way too late for anyone to be out. I never saw anyone
do their morning runs until at least 4am. I thought they might be hurt,
they were walking funny, like they had a limp.

 

As they got closer, I saw how torn their clothes were. Their skin was
filthy too, like covered in a layer of grime. I wanted to drive off, but
what if they were hurt?

 

Getting closer, I could make out that it was definitely a man, but very
skinny. He was also getting faster, like he was fighting the pain in his
leg, hobbling over quickly to me. I finally saw the blood on his mouth.
It was on his hands, his cheeks, it fell from his lips down his chin.

 

“Open the trunk!” he screamed. His voice sounded like that was the first
thing he said in years.

 

He leaped forward and hit his chin on the trunk as I floored it.

 

I drove straight home, taking the final package with me. I delivered it
in the morning and promptly quit.

 

When my boss asked why, I only told him one thing:

 

“Get better locks.”
