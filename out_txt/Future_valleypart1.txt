I am posting this as a warning. People need to know the truth about an
evil corporation known as Future Valley. Why I deem them evil is a long
and twisted story, but I will do my best to write down every detail
before it’s too late.

It all started a month ago, on April 4th.

Back then, I was still a student under Master Takeshi Kawasaki at his
dojo, perched high atop the wind-swept peak of Breezy Hill. Master
Takeshi took me in when I was just a scrawny nine-year-old kid. Back in
those days, I was the neighborhood punching bag, beaten up and
humiliated by anyone who felt like it. It was a miserable existence. The
worst part was the sheer helplessness: the feeling that someone else
could decide your fate and execute it without your consent. It felt like
being a blank page in a book, stripped of any power over whatever cruel
lines the writer decided to ink onto you.

Ten years later, at nineteen, things were different. Years of grueling
training had reshaped me into a broad-shouldered, six-foot frame of
lean, athletic muscle. My messy, shoulder-length red hair fell over my
face as I stood in the training room, green eyes locked on my opponent
across the mat. I was faster, stronger, and far more skilled than the
terrified child who had first walked through those dojo doors.

My opponent that afternoon was a massive guy named Steve. Steve was a
six-foot-five tank of a human being, a meathead and a natural-born jerk.
Everyone in the dojo was used to his endless trash talk and dirty
tactics, so as I stepped onto the ring mat, I expected nothing less.

“Dan, don’t mind Steve,” Master Takeshi said gently, standing nearby as
I finished wrapping my knuckles in white cloth. “Just focus on learning
from him and, in the process, knowing your body’s capabilities.”

Master knew how impulsive I could get, and he remembered all too well
how Steve used to torment me when I first arrived a decade ago.

“No promises can be made, Master,” I muttered.

Instantly, a heavy palm smacked across my bare back. I gasped as a sharp
burst of pain shot through my ribs. Master Takeshi chuckled softly.
Despite his advanced age, the man could still take down five full-grown
men without breaking a sweat.

“Still stubborn as the first day you came here,” he said, shaking his
head, realizing he couldn’t steer me away from an inevitable clash.

The sharp ring of the dojo bell signaled the start of the match.

By dojo rules, we were supposed to strictly utilize Wing Chun and the
formal techniques taught under Master Takeshi’s roof. But Steve decided
to abandon discipline and go completely wild. As soon as I closed the
distance to throw a light, probing jab, Steve slipped the strike and
drove a heavy, brutal elbow directly into my face.

The force sent me stumbling backward, my head spinning as blood gushed
from my nose, splattering onto the canvas. A single strike, and I was
already seeing stars. My legs wobbled beneath me, but I gritted my teeth
and quickly circled back, creating vital distance between us.

“DIDN’T WE AGREE ON USING ONLY WING CHUN, YOU BASTARD?!” I shouted, my
voice dripping with frustration. “Why the hell did you use Muay Thai?!”

Steve just threw his head back and laughed, a wet, arrogant sound that
echoed off the wooden beams of the dojo.

“What? Getting cold feet?” he sneered. “No wonder your little sister is
getting hit on. You are weak as fuck.”

A few spectators along the edge of the mat snickered. I darted a glance
toward Master Takeshi, but he remained still, watching the exchange with
a hard, unreadable expression.

Inside me, something snapped. Pure anger flooded my veins, turning cold
and focused. A sickening smile broke across my face, my teeth stained
red with my own blood as it dripped from my chin and pooled on the mat
below.

“Alright then,” I rasped through the iron taste in my mouth. “Don’t be
sorry for what comes next.”

I shifted my stance, lowering my center of gravity into an entirely
different fighting style.

I waited. For ten agonizing seconds, Steve circled me, measuring the
distance and trying to gauge my shift in attitude. Finally, with a
dismissive scoff, he charged, telegraphing a fast, heavy front punch
aimed straight for my jaw.

The blow connected flush with my face. But instead of retreating, I
absorbed the impact, using the violent rebound to whip my right arm
backward and unleash a devastating counter-strike. My fist whipped
forward with every ounce of kinetic force my body could generate,
connecting squarely with Steve’s face.

The impact sounded like a dry branch snapping. The sheer momentum
violently jerked his head back, snapping his neck at an unnatural angle.

Steve collapsed onto his back like a felled tree, completely unconscious
before he even hit the mat. Blood poured heavily from his nose and
shattered jaw.

He survived, but barely. Steve suffered multiple broken teeth, a
fractured jaw, two black eyes, and a severe neck injury that effectively
ended any athletic career he might have had.

Thankfully, my master stepped in and managed to get me out of legal
trouble. But little did I know, this violent afternoon on Breezy Hill
was only the catalyst for the nightmare that was about to follow…

That fight with Steve wasn’t the first time Master Takeshi had to pull
me out of trouble. Just two days prior, I’d gotten into it with two
grown men. Honestly, calling them “men” is far too generous, given what
they tried to do to my little sister, Yana. They had crept up to her
right after her school let out while she was waiting for me, cornering
her and making vile passes at her.

Naturally, my rage took over. I beat the absolute hell out of both of
them, leaving them battered, bloody, and hospitalized. The cops hauled
me down to the nearest station for breaking the law. But frankly, to
hell with the law if it ever tries to stand between me and protecting my
sister.

Master Takeshi showed up at the station and somehow managed to talk the
officers into letting me walk. He was a highly respected figure around
Tampa, a man whose word carried real weight with the local authorities.

I truly owed him everything. Ten years ago, when our parents were
brutally murdered late one night by some low-life, Master Takeshi took
total responsibility for a traumatized nine-year-old boy and his little
sister. He gave us a roof over our heads, a life, and a purpose.

Later that evening, the sun sank low into the horizon, casting long,
bruised-purple shadows across the wooden floorboards of the empty
training hall. I was the last one left in the dojo, sitting cross-legged
on the canvas mat. A crimson-stained paper towel was crammed against my
nostrils, and I held a heavy bag of ice against the bridge of my swollen
nose to keep the throbbing at bay.

“Honestly, Dan, you keep surprising me again and again… and again,”
Master Takeshi said gently.

He walked over carrying two steaming cups of herbal tea, set them down
on a low wooden table near the mat, and sat cross-legged opposite me.

“You should learn to control your anger. I won’t always be there to get
you out of trouble,” he continued, his tone shifting softer as he
motioned for me to take a cup. “Who will be there for Yana when both of
us aren’t around for her?”

I let out a heavy sigh, removing the ice bag and placing it on the floor
beside the soiled towel.

“You know how I feel about people who disrespect me or my sister… even
you,” I muttered.

I reached out, wrapping my bruised knuckles around the hot ceramic cup,
and took a slow, deliberate sip. The blend was bitter, made from
traditional herbs meant to soothe internal bleeding and reduce the
inflammation in my face.

“Still, Dan, some problems can’t be solved with violence or you running
rampant through the issue in the hope of solving it,” Takeshi said. He
took a long, thoughtful sip of his own tea before setting the cup back
down. “A person succumbing to his own anger is as helpless as the loser
of a fight.”

I rolled my eyes. I’d heard him say that line a dozen times, but it
never made sense to me. What was he even trying to imply? That my anger
made me helpless and weak?

“You won’t stop making up wise sentences, won’t you?” I asked
sarcastically.

Master Takeshi chuckled softly, the wrinkles around his eyes deepening.
“They are not for me to understand, but for you. I already walked the
same path you are walking, Dan.”

He let out a long, weary exhale, then pushed himself up from the floor.
“Well, Yana is waiting for us back home. Let’s walk there.”

I finished the rest of the bitter tea in one quick gulp and stood up,
wincing slightly as my body reminded me of the afternoon’s violence.
Together, we locked up the heavy wooden doors of the dojo and stepped
out into the evening air.

By the time we made the trek back to our house, night had fully settled
over the city, wrapping the streets in thick darkness.

As soon as we stepped through the front door, we were greeted by my
beloved little sister, Yana. The bare wood of the second-floor hallway
echoed with the fast, rhythmic padding of her bare feet as she came
barreling down the stairs.

“DAN, UNCLE TAKESHI!” she shouted, her voice bright and bursting with
pure joy.

She called Master Takeshi “uncle” because he really was that close to
us, a second father in every way that mattered. She threw her small arms
around his waist first, hugging him tightly before turning to wrap her
arms around me.

“How was school?” I asked as she pulled back, looking up at me with a
glowing face and jumping in place with a cute, unstoppable smile.

“I won best artist award!” Her arms shot up into the air in victory.

Master Takeshi laughed softly, his chest rumbling with warmth. “That’s
amazing, Yana. Do you still have the drawing?”

She nodded vigorously, her eyes lighting up. “I have it!” she called
back over her shoulder as she spun around and bolted back up the stairs,
disappearing into her bedroom.

I rubbed the back of my neck, letting out a tired yawn as the quiet hum
of the house settled around us.

“Man, she looks so happy today,” I murmured.

“She deserves it,” Takeshi said gently, unlacing his sandals by the
doorway. “Every kid of her age deserves to go through this beautiful
moment of happiness. Especially after what she had to go through.”

He stepped off the entrance mat and headed toward the kitchen. “I’m
making the breakfast this time.”

He left me standing alone in the warm light of the living room. He’s
right, I thought to myself. She really does deserve it. I would do
anything just to keep Yana happy, even if it meant fighting through the
absolute impossible.

I was snapped out of my thoughts by the sound of her heavy, eager
breathing as she hurried back down the steps, holding a large sheet of
drawing paper tightly in her hands. She rushed up to me, her cheeks
flushed.

“Dan Dan! Do you think it is beautiful?” she asked, proudly holding up
the artwork.

I took the paper carefully from her small hands. My eyes widened as I
took in the drawing. It was a picture of the three of us, me, Master
Takeshi, and Yana. She had drawn the dojo standing tall in the
background under a blue sky, with Takeshi and me holding her hands on
either side.

A genuine smile broke across my face. I looked down at her hopeful,
wide-eyed expression.

“It is far too beautiful, Yana. I love it.”

She giggled softly, beaming with pride. I patted her head gently,
feeling the soft texture of her hair beneath my palm. Satisfied with my
verdict, she snatched the drawing back and took off toward the kitchen
to show Master Takeshi.

All I heard from the kitchen afterward was Master Takeshi laughing
softly, showering Yana with warm praise. I shook my head, grinning to
myself as I unlaced my sandals and stepped into the main room.

Breakfast was ready. The three of us sat down together, and Master
Takeshi and I mostly just listened to Yana talk. She had so many things
to share about her day at school. She was like a news anchor on a
weather channel, constantly broadcasting a relentless stream of updates
and new events every single day. It was fun.

Fifteen minutes later, we finished up. Yana was already yawning, rubbing
her eyes with tiny fists as sleepiness overtook her. I lifted her up in
my arms and carried her upstairs to her room. I laid her down gently on
the soft mattress and pulled the cozy blanket up over her shoulders.

“Night, Yana.”

“Night, night… Dan,” she mumbled.

I kissed her forehead, then slipped out of her bedroom, softly closing
the door behind me.

I let out a quiet sigh, walking over to the wooden railing that
overlooked the lower floor. Glancing down, I saw Master Takeshi still
sitting on the couch in the living room, focused intently on writing
something on a piece of paper. I had no idea what it was, but I didn’t
bother asking. I just headed into my own room, collapsed onto my bed,
and slowly drifted off to sleep.

The night was peaceful. The only sound in the darkness was the quiet,
rhythmic humming of the strong wind blowing outside across Breezy Hill.

That peace shattered at 3:40 AM.

A violent, frantic pounding rattled the front door downstairs. I opened
my eyes slowly, groaning as I forced my exhausted body out of bed.

“Who could that be?” I muttered, glancing at the clock. “Whoever that is
behind the door better have a damn good reason to bang that loud.”

Irritated, I made my way downstairs and pulled the front door open.

Standing on the porch was Mrs. Brown, the kind, middle-aged lady who
owned the nearby gas station. I stared at her in utter confusion. Her
face was pale, her eyes wide and overflowing with tears, and her whole
body trembled with terror.

“Mrs. Brown? Why are you banging that hard…”

“DAN, Takeshi is…” She gasped, desperately trying to catch her breath as
a harsh sob broke from her throat.

“TAKESHI GOT SHOT!” she finally managed to cry out, collapsing onto the
porch floor in tears.

My heart dropped straight to my stomach. My eyes went wide with sheer
terror. Every trace of sleep and tiredness that had weighed on me
moments before vanished instantly. Without a second thought, I bolted
into the dark street. My feet barely seemed to touch the pavement as I
tore down the road, passing a moving car so quickly the driver barely
had time to notice me.

I kept running and running, the heavy thud of my footfalls echoing
against the empty pavement until I saw the flashing red and blue lights
up ahead. A large crowd of neighbors had gathered on the street, being
pushed back and separated by police officers.

I slowed down as I closed the distance, my breath coming in ragged
gasps.

And then I saw him.

Master Takeshi was lying flat on the cold asphalt. Unmoving. Dead.

A sharp gasp escaped my throat as my gaze darted to the right. Sitting
on the curb was Yana, sobbing hysterically in the tight, protective
embrace of Mrs. Miller and her husband.

Parked nearby was a police cruiser, where two officers were in the
process of detaining a man in handcuffs. My eyes flared with pure,
unadulterated wrath as I charged forward, barging through the crowd like
a battering ram. The moment the neighbors and the cops caught sight of
me sprinting toward the patrol car, a wave of panic swept through them.
They knew me well. They knew exactly what I was about to do to that man.

Among the officers was Noah, a close friend of mine. His eyes widened in
sheer terror as he realized the nightmare unfolding before him.

“DAN, NO!” he screamed, his voice cracking.

Three officers sprinted toward me, but one unlucky cop managed to step
directly into my path as I lunged for the murderer pinned against the
cruiser. Blinded by absolute fury, I didn’t even see the uniform. I
drove a devastating uppercut straight into the officer’s jaw. The sheer
kinetic force snapped his head back, lifting his boots off the pavement
for a split second before he collapsed onto his back, completely out
cold.

Before I could reach the killer, four other officers tackled me from the
side, bringing all their collective weight down to pin me to the
pavement.

“GET OFF OF ME!” I roared in rage, the veins in my neck bulging as my
face turned deep, burning red.

“Dan, please calm down…”

“CALM DOWN?” I cut Noah off, snapping my head toward him with bloodshot
eyes. “Noah, don’t! HE killed HIM!”

Noah stood frozen. He couldn’t say a word, his mouth opening and closing
in hesitation, completely paralyzed by the horror of the moment.

I forced my head around to glare at the man by the police car. He stood
there with a broad, arrogant smirk plastered across his face, looking
down at me on the ground like I was nothing.

The sight hit me like a physical blow. I felt it instantly, that
sickening, hollow wave of pure helplessness washing over me again. I
fucking hated it. The people I loved kept dying around me, and no matter
how strong or fast I became, I couldn’t do a damn thing about it.

My lips parted, shivering violently from pure, unbridled fury. A
deranged, bloodthirsty smile stretched across my face.

“Noah… you are so lucky my sister is here, because if she wasn’t there,
I would have killed you all… with no exception.”

The four officers pinning me down grunted, struggling with everything
they had as my muscles coiled, threatening to throw them off. Noah
stared at me, shaken to his core. He let out a heavy, solemn sigh, then
motioned for the men to slowly lift me up.

Ten minutes later, I was sitting on a metal bench inside the precinct,
my hands secured behind me in heavy cuffs. Sitting right beside me was
an officer, his hand resting tensely over his taser holster, ready to
draw the moment I made a single sudden move.

The door to the back offices clicked open, and Noah stepped out. He
looked completely drained, dark circles weighing beneath his exhausted
eyes. He glanced at the officer sitting beside me and gestured toward my
wrists.

“Remove the cuffs.”

The officer frowned, looking hesitant. “You sure?”

“Of course I am sure, Bob. Just remove them,” Noah commanded with a
sharp edge of authority.

The officer let out a heavy sigh, grabbing my arm to stand me up before
unlocking the metal bands. A sense of physical ease returned to me the
moment my wrists were free, and I slowly rubbed the raw skin where the
steel had dug in.

“I’m sorry again, Dan, but you know the law. This man will be judged by
the law,” Noah murmured softly.

I looked at him, my expression cold. “Yeah, I’m sure the law will serve
justice… right?”

Noah didn’t answer immediately. He just placed both hands on his hips,
leaning his head back toward the ceiling as if contemplating something
he shouldn’t even be thinking about.

He exhaled hard before bringing his eyes back to meet mine. “Right, you
are right, Dan. Just go home and everything will be handled. Your sister
needs you.”

I gritted my teeth in quiet frustration. I could feel it in my gut, he
was stalling. He was trying to get rid of me.

“Noah, why did he do this?” I demanded.

“Dan, for now, we don’t know,” Noah admitted, rubbing the back of his
neck as he closed the distance between us. “We are now conducting an
investigation with him. All we know from Mrs. Brown is that she saw
Takeshi Kawasaki coming out with your sister. When she asked Takeshi
where he was going, he said he was taking Yana for a quick walk around.”

He rested a heavy hand on my shoulder. “Just go home, Dan, and I promise
to tell you the rest.”

I stared at him for a long, heavy moment. “Alright… I’m going, but you
better tell me everything.”

Noah gave me a firm nod. I threw one last hard look his way before
turning on my heel and walking out into the night.

By the time I reached our house, Mrs. Brown and the Millers were still
there, sitting in the living room with Yana. They were doing their
absolute best to comfort a terrified twelve-year-old girl and ease her
overwhelming pain.

“DAN!” Yana cried out the second she saw me walk through the front door.

She bolted out of her seat, ran across the room, and threw her arms
around my waist, clinging to me as tightly as her small arms could
manage. She was crying hysterically, terrified I had been taken away for
good. I wrapped my arms around her and stroked her hair, trying
desperately to calm the frantic rise and fall of her chest.

“Sorry you had to go through this, Yana,” I whispered.

She sniffed, wiping her nose as she looked up at me with huge,
tear-filled eyes. “Please, don’t leave me, too…”

I crouched down so we were at eye level, taking her trembling shoulders
in my hands. “I won’t, Yana, I promise.”

I pulled her into another tight hug, resting my chin on her shoulder as
the heavy silence of the house settled over us.

Two agonizing weeks passed.

We held the funeral for Master Takeshi on a gray, overcast afternoon. A
crowd of people from around the neighborhood came to offer their
condolences to Yana and me, but the words felt hollow.

After the service, Noah met me at the dojo to drop off the initial
information about the man who had murdered Master Takeshi. The two of us
sat down on the cold floor mats of the training hall. The room felt
hauntingly massive without the sound of students sparring or Takeshi’s
quiet presence overseeing the floor.

Noah pulled off his suit jacket, unbuttoned his collar, and dragged his
tie down.

“The man who killed Master Takeshi was part of a corporation called
Future Valley,” Noah said, massaging his forehead as if a massive
headache was setting in. “A big biotechnology corporation that
supposedly runs legal chemical medicines and cures for the country.”

I raised one eyebrow, not quite catching on to what he was implying.
“And? Either way, this man will face punishment. He killed…”

“Killed Takeshi, yes, I know, Dan,” Noah cut me off, his voice dropping
low. “But those rich bastards have connections to the upper levels of
authority in law. I can’t do anything against them. I’m powerless.”

My eyes widened in pure, volcanic rage. The blood rushed to my ears,
hammering against my temples.

There it was again. That fucking rotting word… powerless.

“I understand what you’re going through, Dan, but you need to work with
us here,” Noah said, his voice desperate, practically begging me to calm
down.

I didn’t even look at him. My gaze remained locked on the man they had
detained, the monster who had just murdered Master Takeshi.

The killer scoffed, rolling his eyes with a smug grin. “Why don’t you
take a picture while you’re at it? You’re lucky I didn’t finish off that
little girl, too,” he said mockingly, practically spitting the words
right in my face.

Noah let out a frustrated sigh, his jaw clenching. “Alright, that’s
enough of you. Take him in,” he ordered a nearby female officer, who
opened the rear door of the patrol car and shoved the man onto the back
seat.

Meanwhile, all I did was stare into nothingness. The blazing fire of my
rage suddenly turned ice-cold.

“Maybe you are right…” I murmured softly.

The killer glanced back out from inside the car just as the officer was
leaning in to fasten his seatbelt. Noah turned to me, completely
confused by what I had just said. I couldn’t blame him.

“I’m lucky you didn’t harm my little sister. Because if you did, you
wouldn’t still be here… breathing the same air as me,” I said calmly.

My voice was disturbingly flat, stripped of all emotion, carrying a
promise far darker than any shouted threat.

I turned my head to look across the street toward my sister. But as my
eyes met hers, my heart shattered. I was horrified by what I saw. Yana
was terrified… but not from the bloody scene, and not from the killer in
handcuffs.

She was terrified of me.

In my blind, violent outburst, I hadn’t taken into account how I
appeared to her, a towering, blood-stained monster screaming threats of
murder in the middle of the night.

A moment later, the officers placed me in handcuffs and led me toward
another cruiser, taking me down to the station for assaulting an
officer.

I felt Noah’s hand rest heavily on my shoulder.

“Look, Dan, I can’t imagine what you’re going through, but we both know
Takeshi wouldn’t want you running rampant and chasing vengeance. Just
let it go… at least for Yana’s sake.”

I had to admit, at that exact moment, Noah was right. But even so… I
really couldn’t control the burning fire consuming me from the inside
out. I looked up at him, masking every ounce of the cold rage surging
through my veins.

“Alright, Noah… I will listen to you,” I said with a calm smile.

Noah let out a long, relieved sigh and smiled back. He stood up,
grabbing his suit jacket from the mat.

“I will head out now. Gotta catch up with work,” he said, slinging the
jacket over his arm as he walked toward the heavy wooden doors of the
dojo.

He paused at the threshold, looking back at me sitting alone in the
quiet training hall. “Hope you recover fast, Dan. Until next time.”

He gave a small wave before turning and walking away, his footfalls
fading down the long stone stairs leading off the peak of Breezy Hill.

He didn’t know I lied to him.

I sat there in the silence, my fists clenching until my knuckles turned
white. Takeshi would get his justice. That man, and whoever he worked
for, needed to face punishment, and if the law was too cowardly to
deliver it, I would.

I returned back home. Yana wasn’t there; she was over at Mrs. Brown’s
house, playing around and keeping her company. Mrs. Brown didn’t mind at
all. She absolutely adored Yana and loved having her over.

I sat down on the living room couch, staring at the ceiling as I tried
to plan my next move. That was when my eyes caught a folded piece of
paper sitting on the small coffee table in front of me. It was the exact
sheet Master Takeshi had been writing on late at night, right before the
incident.

I reached out, picked it up, and carefully unfolded it. There was no
mistaking his precise, familiar handwriting.

I read the lines he had left behind:

“I’m leaving this paper for you, Dan. I hope you find it at the right
moment. I am probably dead by now. I know it is odd to say, but I will
explain why. Years and years ago, I was part of the corporation called
Future Valley. A big biotechnology corporation that masks its true
illegal work under some legal production of medicines and cures for the
government. There is more to this corporation. Your father and mother
were part of it too. Their death wasn’t because of bad luck running into
some smooth-brain street thug. It was a hit put on them. Every once in a
while, the corporation will put a hit on their former employees who left
the corporation. They want to ensure everything dies with those
employees. What I want you to do is not get yourself involved, I beg of
you, Dan, but if you ignore all the things I said and go on to do
whatever nonsense you have of a plan, then… I apologize for what you
will uncover… at least about yourself… I have nothing else to say…”

A cold knot formed in my stomach as I digested every word. I crushed the
paper in my palm, my knuckles turning white.

“Stupid old man… you just gave me more reason to hunt down those
motherfuckers,” I growled under my breath.

I flicked my wrist, launching the crumpled paper across the living room
straight into the trash can.

Without hesitating, I pulled out my phone and dialed a familiar number.
It rang twice before he picked up.

“Max?” I said.

“Dan? Yo, what’s up, dude…”

“No time for the pep talk. I need you to call the rest of our group to
meet up at Olivia’s.”

A long pause stretched over the line. I could hear Max let out a heavy,
tired sigh. “Please don’t tell me you got into trouble again?”

“Just do it, Max. It’s important.”

“Fine,” Max muttered. “Tomorrow, 8 in the morning will do. You will have
to drive to Fallwood.”

“Sounds good.”

I hung up, staring at the dark screen of my phone. The pieces were
finally coming together, and the truth about my parents and Takeshi. All
I needed to do now was get ready for tomorrow.

The next morning, I dropped Yana off at Mrs. Brown’s house. I needed to
know she’d be safe and cared for during the couple of days I’d be gone,
and Mrs. Brown was more than happy to watch over her.

With that weight off my chest, I got behind the wheel and drove toward
the Fallen Forest, out in the quiet countryside. I figured the only
reason Max told us to meet there was because Olivia kept her customized
SUV van parked near the woods.

The drive was long, the scenery slowly shifting from city streets to
lonely stretches of highway flanked by overgrown brush. Eventually, the
thick tree line of the Fallen Forest loomed into view. I pulled over
onto the dirt shoulder, parked my car, and stepped out onto the damp
earth to continue on foot.

Up ahead, tucked near the edge of the woods, three other cars were
already parked. Guess the rest of the crew made it, I thought.

“Guess I’m the last one to arrive,” I muttered, slinging my heavy duffel
bag over one broad shoulder and slamming the car door shut behind me.

Stepping into the forest, the atmosphere shifted instantly. The air grew
cool and heavy. Towering, ancient trees stretched high above, their
thick canopy blocking out almost every ray of sunlight and casting the
forest floor in deep, perpetual shadows. My boots crunched against the
tall grass and fallen leaves, the eerie silence broken only by the wind
rustling through the canopy.

After about five minutes of navigating through the dense trees, a
clearing opened up ahead. There it was, Olivia’s SUV van, and standing
around it were the rest of my friends, waiting for me to arrive.

Max was a couple of inches shorter than me, but the guy was built like
an absolute unit. He was a fit dude who spent way more time grinding at
the gym than he ever did sitting in a college lecture hall, sporting a
fresh buzz cut and a thick, dark mustache. He was leaning back, chugging
an energy drink. With a loud, satisfied burp, he tossed the empty
aluminum can carelessly into the tall grass.

“GOD, Max, please just stop littering around this beautiful forest,” a
voice groaned from near the van.

The girl who said it was April, unquestionably the voice of reason in
our group. To sum her up, she was a full-on goth girl, dressed in her
usual dark style that made her stand out against the greenery of the
woods.

“April, calm the fuck down. No one is around, and who cares? It’s the
fucking forest,” Max said, grinning ear to ear without a shred of
remorse.

“HEY, guys!” I called out to them as I closed the distance toward the
SUV.

Both of their heads snapped in my direction.

“AWWW, DAN! My dude, it’s been a while, man,” Max shouted, stepping
forward with his arms spread wide like an eagle.

I walked straight into his heavy hug, feeling the firm pat on my back.

“It’s been a while indeed,” I said as we broke the hug and walked over
toward April.

“Hey, Dan,” April said, extending her hand out to shake mine.

I ignored the gesture completely and reached over, playfully patting her
head instead.

“Hey there, Ms. Vampire.”

She rolled her eyes, shoving my shoulder to the side with a small smirk.
“Yeah, yeah, just make fun of me.”

“Ay, he is not wrong, though,” Max piped up, pulling up a folding wooden
chair that was resting beside the SUV.

“Just shut up, MAX,” she scoffed, turning on her heel and heading for
the van’s side door. “I will go and check on Olivia if she is awake
yet.”

April slipped inside, leaving me alone with Max under the cool shade of
the canopy. I took a seat on a crate next to him, looking around the
quiet clearing.

“So, where is the rest?”

Max chuckled softly as he popped open another energy can with a sharp
snap. “They are coming… Dan.”

He suddenly turned his head to look directly at me, his playful demeanor
vanishing into a dead-serious expression.

“Why did you call us? You seemed to be pretty worked up last night when
you called me.”

“I called you because I wanted a favor from you guys on something very
important to me.”

“Hmm? What is it?”

I sighed and crossed my arms, my broad chest tight as I looked him in
the eye. “I want to get into the Future Valley nearest facility, where
there will be a certain person I want to get my hands on.”

Max’s eyes widened, and he suddenly sputtered, almost choking on his
drink. He slammed the aluminum can down onto the folding table, wiping
his mouth with the back of his hand.

“Dan, you can’t be serious. Like, are you insane!? You will get put down
immediately.”

“Max, just listen to me. I know, but I NEED this. This man killed
Takeshi.”

Max went completely silent. The harshness in his face melted away,
replaced by a quiet wave of genuine empathy as he looked at the raw
grief behind my eyes. He let out a long, irritated sigh, reaching back
down to grab his drink.

“Fine, but we will plan this out very carefully.”

A small, relieved smile broke across my face, and I reached over,
patting his heavy shoulder. “Of course.”

“What a gut-twisting sight to see.”

Max and I snapped our heads toward the cold, familiar voice echoing from
the tree line.

“HAHA! Damien, dude, it’s been a while.”

Damien and I were two different sides of the same coin. He was brutal
and impulsive, but he was at least smarter than both Max and me
combined.

“Yeah, and you’re still the same as you were, Max. Just a nasty pig who
turns any spot he occupies into waste,” Damien said, massaging the back
of his neck as he let out a lazy yawn.

I stood up from my seat and walked over to him. Immediately, we dapped
up and pulled into a quick, solid hug. Max stepped over right behind me
to dap Damien up, too, the three of us gathering in the center of the
clearing.

Just then, the heavy side door of the SUV slid open, and the two girls
stepped back out into the cool forest air.

“Oh, sorry, guys. Didn’t know you two were outside,” Olivia said,
standing right behind April as they both climbed down from the van.

“Don’t sweat it, Olivia. Damien and I just got here.”

She nodded and crossed her arms, her sharp gaze sweeping across the
group gathered under the towering trees. “Soo, why did you call us here
again?”

I laid it all out for them.

The explanation dragged on for hours as the sun slowly dipped below the
canopy, casting long, bruised shadows across the forest floor. Tempers
flared, voices were raised, and we argued back and forth, viciously
debating the sheer risk, the logistics, and the heavy consequences of
taking on a giant like Future Valley. But beneath the shouting, the
loyalty held firm. Eventually, we hammered out a solid strategy to
breach the nearest facility, track down Takeshi’s murderer, and make him
answer for what he did.

By the time the last detail was finalized, night had completely
swallowed the Fallen Forest. Every contingency was mapped, every role
assigned.

We were ready for tomorrow.

We all piled into Olivia’s SUV and made the long drive out to the
facility.

When the building finally loomed out of the darkness, it was eerie. The
whole place was dead quiet and looked surprisingly abandoned for a site
belonging to a powerhouse like Future Valley. We stepped out into the
cool air and immediately set the plan into motion.

April hooked into the main network feed and wiped the security cameras,
hacking straight into the facility’s system with disturbing ease. The
lack of heavy firewalls or high-end security protocols felt completely
out of place. It was odd… very, very odd.

We slipped through the outer doors without firing a single shot or
triggering a single alarm. The interior was far too quiet, completely
swallowed by an unnatural silence. Narrow, sterile hallways stretched
out before us, lit by flickering fluorescent bulbs that hummed softly
overhead. The floors and walls were pristine, immaculately clean, yet
entirely devoid of life.

The main power was clearly still running, driving a low, metallic
vibration through the concrete beneath our boots.

So why the hell was no one around?

“Where the hell is everyone?” Damien asked as he kicked a random chair
sitting in the tall hallway.

April slapped his shoulder, annoyed and tense. “DAMIEN, be quiet, dude.
We literally don’t know for sure if no one is around.”

Damien sighed tiredly and raised both hands in surrender. “Sure, thing.”

Max shook his head, grinning at how April handled Damien. Olivia was
flashing her flashlight into the dark offices along the hallway until
something caught her attention, and she called out to us.

“Guys! Come down here quickly.”

We rushed over to where she was standing in the narrow corridor.

“What is it, Olivia?” I asked, intrigued.

“Look, Dan, isn’t that your father’s name on this door?”

I stepped closer, the beam of light hitting a metal plaque mounted on
the frame. It really was his name labeled on the office door.

“Y-yeah…” I could feel them all staring at me as if waiting for an
explanation.

“Let’s check it out then. Like, isn’t that your father’s office?” Max
asked.

“Yeah, you are right.”

Damien grabbed the knob and twisted it open. The door swung inward with
a low, heavy creak. The office looked impossibly clean, coated in a
light layer of dust. We stepped inside and began walking around, taking
in the wall-to-wall bookshelves and metal filing cabinets waiting to be
searched.

I walked straight over to my dad’s old wooden desk and pulled open one
of the top drawers. Tucked inside was a heavy paper file folder, and
stamped across the tab was my own name. I raised one eyebrow in
confusion.

I pulled out the file and opened it, revealing something that shocked me
to my core.

Inside were blurry Polaroid images of me when I was a small child,
except I wasn’t in a bed. I was submerged in a glowing, liquid-filled
lab tube with wires attached to my small frame. Handwritten across the
side of the photograph was an entry dated 12/5:

“My son, Dan, showed phenomenal readings. He is healthy and survived the
experiment. I’m so happy for him. He shall bring pride to my wife and
me.”

They were experimenting on me? I was baffled, my mind spinning as I
stared at my father’s handwriting. My own parents?

As I flipped through the next few pages, the medical jargon laid it all
out. It was an experimental trial involving a synthetic virus called
V-RAX, a pathogen designed specifically to rewrite genetic code and
enhance the human physical body far beyond normal capabilities.

All those years of raw strength, speed, and endurance… it was never just
hard work. It was engineered.
