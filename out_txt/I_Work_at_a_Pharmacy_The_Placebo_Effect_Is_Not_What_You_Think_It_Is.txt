When I first started working here, I never expected for it to get this
bad. God, what was I thinking? What the hell is this place anymore?

It was… so simple, so ordinary before. How did it get to this point?

I’m a pharmacist, my name is Chris. And if you want to take this as a
warning, do so, because whatever is happening in this place is
dangerous.

Do not take Liazitropin.

Do not take Imoxcalyn.

Do not take Estropeun.

Do not take Salazoprin.

If you’re confused about what I’m talking about, these are medications,
things that we dispense here. But they’re not just sold anywhere.

As far as I can tell, there’s a select group of pharmacies around the
country that are “allowed” to sell these medications to the public. They
call them “miracle cures”; I call them unethical experimentation.

When I was first approached to change my place of work, I was somewhat
reluctant. I didn’t know this town and I wasn’t really willing to uproot
my entire life for no good reason.

As a single dad, stability is quite important for my boy. So, moving
around between places when we finally have a good home-work-school
routine is not great.

When I got… the news. I was somewhat forced to take this job. I guess I
ended up uprooting my life, but I needed the money.

At the time I thought that was the biggest of my concerns. But this
pharmacy, this place, it’s wrong. What we sell here, is wrong.

It all started on my first day. It was fairly average. People come in.
They have prescriptions, they ask for what they want, we give it to
them. They chat, they ask for advice, sometimes they just buy some over
the counter stuff.

Same old, same old.

I was confused why I was given basically twice my previous salary to do
this. The pharmacy is nothing special, it’s not super modern, and we
don’t have some premium medication or have some special deal with
insurance. I had never even heard of this place before.

But that was before I noticed the queue to register box A.

Right before we opened, a massive line was formed outside our pharmacy.
It was bizarre, but as the doors opened for the day, 90% of people went
straight to register A.

I don’t know why but a colleague of mine was working there at the time.
People looked…. desperate. Like they were desperate to be first in line
to get something. After about five minutes of sales, the register was
closed for the day and I heard audible groans from most people who
didn’t make it in time.

Register A is nothing out of the ordinary. The only thing that’s
different, is a series of shelves that feature products that I’ve never
heard of.

As the day came to a close, I went to check what they were. It got me
curious what people were so worked up about.

Liazitropin, Estropeun. Imoxcalyn? What the hell was this? I’ve never
heard of these. Was this like some Ozempic fad? Can we even sell those?

I looked at the active ingredients and I remained equally confused.

Tears of Medicus… Crushed blattodeans? Leather of Yash’Abodal? What the
fuck was it talking about? Are these gag boxes?

I picked up a box of Liazitropin. The label simply read:

“Liazitropin^(TM), bring that ugly inside out!”

Doesn’t sound like great marketing. Was this some cosmetic thing? My
perusing was quickly interrupted by someone talking behind me.

“I wouldn’t touch that if I was you, big guy.” The voice said.

I spun around quickly. An acne-riddled young man with braces on and a
lab coat was looking straight at me.

“What?” I simply let out.

“Don’t let that stuff get on your skin.”

I paused, somewhat confused by his indifference.

“What is this?” I asked.

He inspected me more closely.

“Oh. You’re the new guy.” He replied.

“Well yeah, we’ve never met.” I responded.

“Right, right sorry. I tend to… forget things.”

His eyes darted around. He seemed a bit spaced out.

“I’m Dave. I work here.”

I gave him an inquisitive look, still holding the box.

“Oh right, that. That’s Liazitropin.” Dave said.

“Yes, I read the name, but what is it?” I asked.

“Did you talk to the manager yet?”

“No…” I responded.

“Then I can’t tell you.”

I paused at that. Manager?

“Just make sure you’re not depressed or something. If you are, don’t
take it.” he said.

What the hell? What?

Dave walked away towards the exit as I read the label. The side effects
were listed. This was when I started to realize this wasn’t an ordinary
job.

Side effects: Diarrhea, sleepiness, loss of memory. Extreme cases: Loss
of identity, genital displacement.

What!? I… I was so confused at the time.

Liazitropin… God. I remember the first time I saw its effects. Its
slogan isn’t very clear but Liazitropin is a depression medication.

The difference is that Liazitropin doesn’t just somewhat treat
depression or its symptoms. It’s a cure. An actual cure to an uncurable
mental condition.

I couldn’t believe it myself.

One day I was working at my counter when someone came to me asking for
Liazitropin. I told her that I didn’t sell that medication in that
particular register and asked her to try register A. She seemed normal
so my curiosity got the better of me.

“Do you know what Liazitropin does? Was it recommended to you?” I asked.

“Oh… Yes, I’m aware. My friend takes it too.” She responded gleefully.

She had a smile on her face as she said that. A smile that one could
tell was forced and fake.

“Right.” I let out.

My face showed doubt.

“Is something wrong? Should I not take it?”

I catch myself.

“No, no. I’m sure it’s safe. Please talk to my colleague, I’m sure he’ll
explain.”

“Oh okay! Thank you.”

She left the pharmacy, and I didn’t see her again the next day. A week
later, something came through the door.

It had the frame of a human being. Slowly waddling towards the counter.
People around were quite shocked. Twisted horns sprouted from every
possible corner of this creature. Skin layered with twisted
amalgamations of keratin. So much so, that it’s gait was uneven because
of the many horns sprouting from its feet.

It walked slowly and with difficulty towards the counter. And I was
shocked when I realized it was walking towards me. It stopped and gazed
upon me. And through the twisted contours of its tusk filled face, I
recognize familiarity.

It was the girl that had asked me for Liazitropin.

She was twisted and contorted. I recoiled instantly but didn’t manage to
pull away before her hands grasped mine. She gripped me as I prepared
for the worst.

“THANK YOU SO MUCH!” she cried out.

What!? I was so confused.

“I’m not sure if you remember me! But I just wanted to thank you!” she
continued.

Her hands pressed on mine. She had horns protruding from her skin, which
pierced my hands and caused me to bleed. I screamed and recoiled away.

“Oh! Sorry!” she replied.

Her mouth clanked and clicked with the noises the horns made clashing in
the inside of her mouth.

“Are you okay!?”

“Yes… I’m fine. What… What happened to you?” I asked.

“What do you mean?” she replied.

I was left dumbfounded by her response. For a lack of a better term, she
had been transformed into a monster. In spite of this, she seemed
confused by my question and indifferent to the many stares she was
receiving from other people in the pharmacy.

“I…I’m sorry. I just wanted to thank you!”

“Thank me for what?” I asked.

“You… You gave the courage to try that pill out. I was really hesitant,
but ever since I took it, I’ve felt… I don’t know. I feel like myself
again. Like I can be happy.”

I wasn’t sure if I should be glad or concerned for her situation.

“So anyways… Thanks!” she said, while leaving in a hurry.

Words escaped me then, and they escape me now, as I try to describe that
situation. I’ve never seen anything like it.

She had changed in a way I’d never seen or heard of. One that wasn’t
even possible. Was she ill? Was that really the result of the
medication?

This is was the beginning of my realization that this place and these
remedies aren’t really orthodox. They’re definitely not approved by any
government agency. This feels like experimentation. But the side effects
are beyond what I can understand.

This isn’t medicine, it’s something that fundamentally changes a person.

The problem is that they work. I’ll never forget that girl’s twisted and
disfigured face. She was smiling, but this time it didn’t seem fake.

It wasn’t long before I confronted the manager of the pharmacy to get
some answers.

I think I was too animated from my experience when I walked into his
office demanding answers.

“What is happening here?” I asked.

In front of me stood the manager. A mild man that appeared to be in his
fifties. He calmly turned to me.

“What do you mean, Christopher?”

“What the hell are these pills you people sell? Is this even legal?”

The man walked slowly to his desk and sat in his chair.

“Christopher. Do you know why we hired you?”

“I don’t…”

“Because you needed help. That’s what we do here. We help people.” He
continued.

“What?” I muttered.

“How’s your boy? Is he better?”

“What?!”

“I know that treatment of his is quite nauseating.”

I paused. My throat felt drier than ever.

“Such a shame. Such a young child being affected by something so vile.
And so… expensive”

“How the fuck do you know about my son?”

His face contorted. It seemed more malicious than professional.

“I told you Christopher. We help people.”

He rose from his chair. He felt bigger than he should be.

“You also needed help, didn’t you? So, we helped. A nice, comfy job, and
a large salary. Enough for what you needed. Am I right?”

“I…” I mumbled.

“Am I right, Christopher?” he repeated.

“Yes…”

“Good. I’m glad we’re clear.”

I stumbled knowing exactly what he meant.

“Now go do your fucking job.” He whispered.

I left, defeated. I never found out how he knew about my boy, but he had
me cornered. I can’t leave this job; I need the money.

My boy… He’s… He’s sick. This fucking manager knows about it somehow.
How? How does he know?

I…I was in such a desperate situation when my boy’s health took a turn
for the worse. He needs treatment. But there’s only one income coming to
this house. I’m alone.

When I got this job offer, I thought it was an angel’s blessing. I
immediately took it. The pay was too good to pass up, and it was the
only way to afford the treatment my son needs.

Every day has been the same since I got here. I wake up, I go to work, I
see the most impossible scenarios I didn’t think were medically possible
and then I go home to take care of my son.

I’m exhausted.

I can’t afford a nurse for the whole day. So, I take care of him when I
get home. In spite of the money I make now, it’s barely enough.

I’m not a betting man, I play it safe. I took this job and accepted
every crazy thing that happens. I need this. I need to afford the
treatment. I often go to his room when he’s already asleep.

Yesterday I noticed he had forgotten to take his pills. I woke him up.

“Hey buddy.” I softly said.

He grunted and moved around, seemingly too sleepy or too weak to open
his eyes.

“Time to take your big boy pills.” I continued.

Eventually he woke up, took them and went back to sleep. Rare are the
times I see his eyes now; I sit next to his bed as a form of penance for
working all day.

The next morning, I wake up and I do it all over again.

I’m not a monster. Even though I need this job, I also want to warn
people of these medications, these drugs.

Imoxcalyn is another one. This one might seem more familiar. I mentioned
that I thought all these drugs were like Ozempic. Some sort of novelty
drug, to lose weight or something.

Imoxcalyn works exactly like that. It’s a diet pill, but unlike other
similar products, it doesn’t just suppress hunger. It actively burns
calories.

You can actually eat whatever you want and take it, and you’ll lose
weight.

There’s one caveat with it. Imoxcalyn is extremely addictive. Suggested
doses are one pill per year. You can microdose it I suppose, but don’t
ask me how that works.

Regardless, you shouldn’t take it. Its addictive nature causes many
issues, including some for me personally. The first time I was in charge
of selling at register A I sold a box of Imoxcalyn to someone.

A man, probably in his thirties, somewhat obese but nothing out of the
ordinary. I gave him the dosage and the warnings and he thanked me and
left.

A month passes and he comes in again, goes straight at me. He’s now
skinnier, probably more than he should be, his hair is long and messy
and his nails are a bit too long.

He starts asking me for more Imoxcalyn. I winced. Fuck, this guy took
the whole box in a month? Shit, that’s not good.

I don’t know what or if we’re suppose to do anything, so I just tell him
that we can’t sell him more. It’s supposed to be one box per decade per
person, and I wasn’t even the person at register A.

He looked enraged, started yelling, and demanded to get another box.
Security escorted him out and I though that was the end of that. He had
projected his anger onto me since I was the one who sold it.

It’s cruel in a way, that we don’t sell some detoxifying agent. There’s
really nothing we have to counter the effects of these medications. We
tell them what to do, and what not to. We give the warnings, there’s
labels. People know what they’re in for.

They still mess up, I guess it’s normal, they’re still just ordinary
people.

I was having these thoughts as I closed the pharmacy and walked to my
car, when I got jumped from behind.

I fell to the ground, I was pinned. A man hovered above me. Manic,
insane and violent.

It was the Imoxcalyn client. He had been waiting for me in the parking
lot and attacked me when he saw me leave work.

“WHERE IS IT?” he yelled.

“What?!” I replied.

He was obviously talking about Imoxcalyn, he wanted more. And he thought
I could give it to him somehow.

God… His face was so deranged. Eyes bulging out of his face, teeth
elongated. What the hell was happening to him?

He pinned my arms down on the ground and his nails were so long and
sharp that they pierced my skin until I started to bleed.

He was strong, stronger than he should be. With the weight of a child,
I’d estimate.

It didn’t take long for me to start blacking out as he became more and
more violent. Blood loss and exhaustion made me unconscious.

Silence accompanied darkness. I was out for a few hours. When I woke up,
I was back in the pharmacy. Specifically in my manager’s office.

I slowly regained consciousness as I heard my manager’s words echo in my
head.

“Are you okay, Christopher?” he asked.

I was still dizzy from the experience.

“I…I don’t…” I mumbled.

“You seem good enough to talk.” He continued.

I slowly started to regain focus. I’m sitting in a chair in his office,
facing him.

“What… what happened? What happened to that man?” I asked.

The manager ignored my question and continued.

“Did you vet him?” he asked.

“Vet him?”

“Hmmm… Did Dave not tell you about the vetting process?” he asked.

“No… I don’t know… I don’t know what that is.”

“Of course. Idiot.” He mumbled under his breath. “Go home, Christopher.
Your son is waiting”.

I think the lack of blood and turmoil from that experience still
lingered in me. I merely replied.

“Y-Yes… Of course, sir. Good…Good night.”

And home I went.

I still have no idea what happened that day. I got in my car; I drove
home and I never saw that man again in my life. I don’t know how I woke
up in the pharmacy, and I don’t know what happened to that person.

A creeping thought pops into my head once in a while, something that I
try to ignore. It’s something I recall reading on the Imoxcalyn box. I’m
not sure what’s wrong with my manager. Who he is, or how he’s able to do
the things that he does.

He scares me but he seems somewhat protective of his employees. He is a
man I do not want to provoke any longer. I still try not to think about
it too much. I try to not think about what I read in the Imoxclayn box.
But I remember it clearly.

“Imoxcalyn^(TM). Active ingredients: Previous users.”

God.

I suppose I should finish off with the reason I warned people about the
placebo effect.

There is a medication called Estropeun. They’re these little red pills
that come in a box and they are effectively useless.

That is to say they’re useless if you know what they actually are.

We call them the “placebo pills” as a “joke”. But there’s nothing funny
or amusing about what they do.

Estropeun is a pill that can treat almost anything. Bad vitals get
better, tissue or organ damage reverts. A simple cold would be
obliterated, while a more complicated or terminal disease can largely
improve.

If you know it’s a placebo, it doesn’t do anything, but if you were to
take it without knowing, it works.

It would be a medically revolutionary product if it wasn’t for its
guaranteed side effects.

If you take Estropeun, you develop extreme sadistic tendencies. In
essence you lose your humanity. Estropeun users, who are often our
biggest issue, are extremely violent, lack empathy and usually attack
people. It’s typically family members. Parents, siblings, grandparents.

It’s a horrible thing and a last resort for people who are desperate for
a cure.

You cannot be aware that Estropeun is a placebo drug or it does not
function. I have no idea why. I’ve heard my manager give explanations
about it, and it doesn’t conform to anything medical or scientific that
I’ve ever heard of.

He says Estropeun, is where all placebo effects come from. Like the
actual medication is what creates the Placebo effect in humans. That
this is just a concentrated form, with a real positive medical outcome,
and real mind-altering consequences for whoever takes it.

We warn parents, or family members about what Estropeun is and what it
does. And if they’re desperate enough, they’ll take it and give it to
someone else. All you have to do is not tell them that it’s not supposed
to cure them.

I’m sure it sounds confusing. But it’s how it works. And if you are
willing to take the risk, it could potentially prolong someone’s life or
cure other smaller diseases.

You just stop being yourself. 25% of Estropeun users eventually commit
homicide, 80% of the vitcims are family members.

I should stop writing, because the next thing I say might incriminate
me. But I wonder… does it even matter anymore?

There’s a medication that I’m not supposed to talk about, at least, I’m
not supposed to talk about its effects. For public sensitivity.

It’s called Salazoprin, and we call it the last resort.

Now unlike Estropeun, Salazoprin is stronger. Estropeun doesn’t wake
people from comas, replace organs or… well I won’t say more.

But neither does Salazoprin. Salazoprin does something else.

I will read the listed results of taking it:

- Feral appearance with heightened sense of violence.

- Increased aggression towards all humans

- Spontaneous combustion

- Massive organ failure

- Spontaneous organ implosions

- Extreme allergies

- Full nervous system shutdown

I could go on, there’s at least fifteen other worse effects that occur
at rates of 50% to 80% depending on the individual.

But if that was the only thing it did, it would sound like a pretty
stupid product. Except for a little detail written on the side of the
box. It simply states.

“Salazoprin^(TM), the first step.”

“Side effects. 1% incidence rate: Cure all ailments. “

Yes, that’s exactly right. Salazoprin has a 1 in 100 chance of curing
anything. And I mean anything. Terminal cancers, life-ending birth
defects, complete body destruction from accidents.

Anything and everything you can think of, Salazoprin cures it. At a 1%
chance.

Our manager calls it, the first step to Panacea. The first development
towards a future where Humanity can be cured. The very gift of Medicus.

I’m not sure what that means and I don’t think I want to know.

I’ve experienced firsthand what it does. One day, a woman came into the
pharmacy, screamed something about the future and her life, and took one
single pill of Salazoprin.

She became feral. Started attacking people. She jumped on the counters,
and jumped on the customers. Even I was hit in the arm during her
rampage.

She was ravenous, her eyes white, mouth foaming. She threw medications,
chairs and anything she could get her hands on. I sprained my ankle
running when I got hit by a flying chair.

No one could even touch her. She was so fast. Jumping onto walls and
sticking to the ceiling. God, what was the security even supposed to do.

Eventually the woman finally collapsed and started seizing on the
ground. Blood poured from every orifice as she convulsed. Not long after
she was pronounced dead.

I don’t know why she did what she did. But from what I could tell from
what Dave told me. She was a mother. Her child was going to die, and
she’d lost her mind.

I’ll never understand why she did that. I understand not risking
someone’s life on that medication. Specially a child. But I don’t
understand why she took it there. I suppose it has to do with the
exception for the medication.

There’s an issue with Salazoprin. Besides the obvious.

It doesn’t work on children. Just adults.

I don’t know if she was desperate. I don’t…

It made me realize something. Working in this pharmacy is a simple
tradeoff. A miraculous cure for a horrible outcome.

A cure with a price.

It’s not up to me to decide who gets what medical treatment. I’m not the
president or a god, I’m a coward. And I just sell pills.

I’m a coward, because I work for the money. For my kid. In spite of the
fact that I hate what we do to these people. They’re sick, they need
help. But don’t know if we’re curing them or making them worse.

Maybe it’s wrong to do it, maybe it’s wrong to stop. And maybe it was
wrong when I stole those pills earlier today.

I’m just… so tired. I’m so fucking tired. I know I did the wrong thing.
Whatever the wrong thing was.

I just wanted to make amends. Warn people about these drugs. About a
pharmacy out there in the world, that has pills that fundamentally
transform you.

Maybe it’s some form of penance, but I don’t think it’s enough. But it’s
why I’m writing this.

It didn’t matter how much I fucking worked. It didn’t matter how much
money I brought in. Every single metric was down.

My boy’s health was getting worse and worse.

It was terminal. He only had a few months at best. I came back home a
few hours ago. Nervous, white-faced and sweating.

I know what’s going to happen, I need to be strong.

I leave you all with this warning. You now know what to look for.
Pharmacies where impossible dealings happen. Where myth and medicine
meet and where you’re as much of a patient as you are a lab rat.

You probably won’t hear a lot from me after this. Either I’m successful
and I get what I want or I fail and my manager finds out.

Either way, I doubt you’ll find me writing again.

There are more pills I didn’t talk about. Some I don’t even know, others
that I can’t even describe. I didn’t have time, I’m sorry. I hope it was
enough. They still sit on that shelf above register A. Waiting for
desperate people to buy them.

But that’s work. Now if you’ll excuse me, I have to go take care of my
son.

I… I said I’m not a betting man… I play it safe. And so, I did.

I enter his room with the stolen pills in my pocket. I don’t really
believe in God. But I hope there’s some forgiveness or clemency for what
I’m about to do.

I sit on the bed next to him. I pause at what I’m about to do.

Placebo… It’s a funny word, huh? It means you think everything is going
to be alright. Well, I don’t. I don’t think it’s going to be alright.
But it will be for you little buddy.

I take the stolen pills from my pocket. They’re red and moist from the
sweat from my hands. I know what I have to do.

Every cure has a price, and this is mine. A price I gladly accept.

My little boy turns around in his sleep, he’s tired. I am too.

His eyes open, only for a moment. And I see the face of the person I’m
fighting for every day. I will help you. I will cure you.

He smiles, he didn’t expect me to be home so early, I guess.

“Dad…?” He says meekly.

The tears start flowing down my face. I try to hold them in.

“Hey buddy.”

“Time to take your big boy pills.”
