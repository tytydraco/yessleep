Hey Reddit.
I know the title sounds insane.
I’m posting this because I’ve run out of explanations.
Maybe somebody here has one.
For context: I’m 38. Married. Two kids. I’ve lived in the same house for
eleven years.
Three weeks ago, I woke up and found a window in my upstairs hallway.
Not a new window somebody installed.
A window that looked old.
Like it had always been there.
The paint matched.
The trim matched.
There was even sun fading on the floor beneath it.
The problem is there was never a window there.
Ever.
I painted that wall myself.
I’ve leaned ladders against it.
I’ve hung family photos there.
I know that wall.
There was no window.

\*\*Q: Are you sure you’re remembering correctly?\*\*
That’s what my wife asked.
Then my kids.
Then my neighbors.
Then everyone.
My wife laughed when I pointed it out.
She said:
“That window has always been there.”
The weird thing is she wasn’t joking.
She genuinely thought I was joking.
I pulled up old photos of the hallway.
The window was in every single one.
Vacation photos.
Christmas photos.
Birthday photos.
Years of evidence.
All showing the window.

\*\*Q: Could you have forgotten?\*\*
That’s what I thought.
I actually booked an appointment with a doctor.
I was terrified.
Brain tumor.
Memory issues.
Something.
But then other things started changing.
Tiny things.
The kitchen drawer opened the opposite direction.
The bathroom light switch moved.
The hallway upstairs seemed slightly longer.
Nothing dramatic.
Just enough to make me stop and stare.
Whenever I asked my family, they looked at me like I was losing my mind.
Because to them nothing had changed.

\*\*Q: Did you start documenting it?\*\*
Yes.
The first thing I did was buy an old digital camera from a thrift store.
No cloud backups.
No internet.
No syncing.
I started photographing everything.
Every room.
Every hallway.
Every corner of the house.
At first the pictures matched reality.
Then they didn’t.
One morning I checked a photograph from the previous day.
The picture showed a door at the end of a hallway.
The actual hallway didn’t have one.
I walked upstairs.
Ten minutes later it did.
The exact door from the photograph.
My wife said it had always been there.
I checked the image again.
The timestamp hadn’t changed.
Only the house had caught up.

\*\*Q: That’s impossible.\*\*
I know.
Trust me.
I know.
It gets worse.
A week later I woke up around 2 a.m. and went downstairs for water.
Halfway down the staircase I noticed another staircase.
My staircase.
Same wood.
Same carpet.
Same dent in the fourth step where my son dropped a toolbox years ago.
Except it branched off the side wall.
It shouldn’t have existed.
I stood there for maybe twenty minutes.
Just staring.
Then I went down it.
I know.
Bad decision.

The staircase led into another version of my house.
Not a different house.
My house.
Almost.
The rooms were correct but wrong.
The kitchen was too wide.
The ceilings were slightly too low.
The hallway stretched far longer than physically possible.
Everything felt like a memory reconstructed from description.
Close enough.
Not accurate.
I went back upstairs and got my wife.
The staircase was gone.
She accused me of drinking.
I don’t drink.

\*\*Q: Did you ever see it again?\*\*
Yes.
And every time I followed it further.
Each version of the house became less accurate.
One had six bathrooms.
One had a living room larger than the entire first floor.
One had windows looking into other versions of the house.
The strangest thing was the people.
I started seeing movement.
Shadows.
Someone turning a corner.
Someone watching through a doorway.
Eventually I saw him.
Me.

\*\*Q: A doppelganger?\*\*
That’s what I thought.
But when we met, he looked just as terrified as I was.
The first thing he said was:
“There’s supposed to be a window upstairs, right?”
I nearly threw up.
Because in his version, the window had always existed.
The thing he remembered changing was the front door.
We spent hours comparing notes.
His family thought he was crazy too.
His photographs didn’t match mine.
His house wasn’t identical to mine.
Just close.
Like two translations of the same story.

\*\*Q: What do you mean by translations?\*\*
I think that’s what’s happening.
Not copying.
Translation.
Take a sentence.
Translate it into ten different languages.
Then back into English.
Eventually the meaning survives but the details drift.
I think reality is doing that.
The house remains a house.
The rooms remain rooms.
But specifics are being reinterpreted.
Over and over.

A few days later I discovered something disturbing.
If I repeatedly saved the same photograph through different
formats—JPEG, PNG, TIFF, back and forth hundreds of times—the image
slowly changed.
Not random corruption.
Not glitches.
New information appeared.
Different furniture.
Different people.
Different rooms.
The more conversions I performed, the clearer the changes became.
One image eventually showed a family standing in front of my house.
I didn’t recognize any of them.
But they looked familiar.
Like relatives I’d forgotten.

\*\*Q: Did your family see the changes?\*\*
No.
To them the image had always looked that way.
That was when I realized something.
The house wasn’t the only thing changing.
The evidence was changing too.
Everything updated except my memories.
Or maybe my memories were the thing updating incorrectly.
I honestly don’t know anymore.

Three nights ago I found the staircase again.
This time I followed it further than ever before.
House after house after house.
Hundreds of them.
Maybe thousands.
Every version slightly different.
Every version occupied.
Sometimes by families.
Sometimes by nobody.
Sometimes by versions of me.
I met seven.
One remembered two windows.
One remembered no windows.
One remembered having a daughter I never had.
One insisted he’d been trapped down there for years.
All of them believed they were the original.
All of them had evidence.
All of them were certain.

Eventually I reached what appeared to be the oldest house.
The center.
The place where all the hallways seemed to converge.
Inside was an old man.
Maybe eighty years old.
He looked exactly like me.
Or exactly like I might become.
He wasn’t surprised to see me.
He just pointed at a stack of notebooks.
Thousands of pages.
Every one written by a different version of us.

\*\*Q: What did he tell you?\*\*
He told me the house never changed.
Reality never changed.
The people changed.
Us.
Every strange hallway.
Every impossible room.
Every doppelganger.
Each one represented a different version of the same person.
Not copies.
Translations.
Slightly altered interpretations.
The maze wasn’t made of houses.
It was made of identities.
I asked him which one of us was original.
He laughed.
Then he asked:
“Tell me about the window.”
So I did.
The hallway.
The photographs.
The arguments.
Everything.
The old man’s face slowly went white.
When I finished, he looked genuinely afraid.
Then he said:
“What window?”

According to him there was never a window.
Not in his house.
Not in any notebook.
Not in any version he’d encountered.
The one thing that started this entire nightmare didn’t belong.
Not to my reality.
Not to his.
Not to any of them.
Meaning there’s another version.
One older than him.
One older than me.
One none of us remember.
I left immediately.
I haven’t gone back.
But this morning there was a second window upstairs.
My wife says it’s always been there.
The kids remember it.
The photographs show it.
The old camera doesn’t.
And I just noticed something worse.
The family in that corrupted photograph?
I know who they are now.
It’s my family.
My wife.
My kids.
Me.
Standing in front of a house with three windows upstairs.
I’ve only got two.
At least I think I do.
I’m going to check.
AMA.
