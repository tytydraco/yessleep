An old couple found him while hiking. Their trail had taken them deep
into Elderbark forest, about two hours from the nearest road. They’d
taken a moment to rest under the pine trees, laying out a blanket and
food they’d prepared, when from somewhere in the distance, they’d heard
a whining noise. The husband later detailed that he’d believed it to be
a wounded animal and, ever the naturists, they had set out to help. The
fog was thick that day, so much so that the figure only came into view
when they were about ten feet from it. The man was alone, standing faced
a way from the couple, completely naked. His skin had grown a shade of
red in the cold, but he made no effort to warm himself, instead keeping
his arms to his side as he wept. His thin frame revealed his ribs and
spine as they lurched and shook with his sobbing. The husband had
ushered his wife to remain a distance behind as he approached the man.
He crept up cautiously and levied the man with questions in a reassuring
tone, like he was trying to calm a bucking horse.

“How did you get out here?”

“Are you lost?”

“What’s your name?”

To this the man made no reply, instead remaining faced away from the
couple staring seemingly at nothing. Even when the husband had reached
the man and placed a gentle hand on his shoulder, he made no indication
that he had noticed the couple’s presence. The man’s skin was ice-cold,
so the husband sent his wife away to fetch the man the blanket they had
left earlier. She returned about three minutes later and together they
attempted to warm the man as they called the authorities.

 

 

By the time we arrived at the scene, the man had come slightly out of
his stupor. Still, he made no reply to our questions but as we talked to
him, he now followed us with his eyes. The man had no provisions with
him of any kind, and we never found his clothes. In the fog and cold
weather of that day there was no logical explanation as to how he had
wandered out here on his lonesome, the man was cold sure but for the
distance he would have had to walk he should’ve been suffering from
hypothermia. The working theory became that the man had perhaps been
abducted and left out in the woods to die. With us we had brought a
change of clothes for the man, we attempted to hand these to him but
eventually decided we would have to dress him ourselves, as we did this
the man laid there motionless. Gently, we guided the man back down the
trail. We had made it about halfway back towards the road when suddenly
the man spun back the way we had come and tried to flee. We made chase
and pleaded with him to stop but in the end, we had to tackle and
restrain him, he remained handcuffed for the rest of the journey back.

 

 

When we had placed him in the back of the police cruiser the man spoke
for the first time. “Where am I going?” he said in a shaken tone like he
had just been woken from a dream. We began calmly explaining to him his
situation and that he would have to accompany us to the police station
to answer some of our questions. To this the man became very distressed
and began thrashing around in his seat and pulling at the doors in
another attempt to escape. He remained in this state for the following
twenty-minute drive back to the station and even began screaming and
throwing verbal abuse at me and my fellow officer “You fucking pigs,
I’ll fucking kill you for this!”. He didn’t have the look of a maniac,
the wild hair or rotting teeth of a Manson, but rather he was a slight
man, with a short professional haircut and a clean-shaven face. Such
fury inside his unassuming frame seemed inhuman, like a man possessed.
When we arrived at the station, I readied myself for an almighty
struggle getting him out of the car and inside the building. But
instead, as soon as we turned into the parking lot the man became
suddenly calm and reserved, as so he had made his last desperate contest
and now his fate was inescapable he could no longer find the energy to
fight.

 

 

He was ushered into an interview room at the back of the station, away
from the whirlwind chaos of the main lobby, where an official interview
could finally begin. Initially, he did not answer any of our questions,
he had not fallen back into a catatonic state like before, he was simply
ignoring us like some petulant child. It was only when we laid out the
possible charges of indecent exposure and threatening an officer that we
managed to wring some answers out of him. He claimed his name was Marty
Krane, a banker in the local area. When asked about how he had ended up
in the forest, he stated that he had simply “felt like going for a walk”
and had not expected to be harassed for it. To the question of his
having no clothes, he answered only that he had “lost them” and would
often strip down in order to “fully embrace nature”.

“Only when returned to our natural state, can we fully understand
ourselves officer.” he had expressed in an almost mocking tone. However,
this story was a complete fabrication. When asked to recount the trail
he had taken into the woods, he couldn’t detail a single landmark or
identifiable feature he had passed, nor could he explain his complete
lack of provisions. And most importantly, there was no record of any
Marty Krane living in the area. The closest we got was a Martin Krane,
but he had passed at just age thirteen about twenty years ago. The
official report stated “misadventure” as the cause of the death, but I
was familiar enough with the case to know that was an
oversimplification.

 

 

The boy had been waiting at the bus stop with his friends when he had
“vanished”, that was the other kids’ exact words, that they had turned
away for just a moment, before he simply “vanished”. There was huge
search effort for the following two weeks; dogs, helicopters the whole
shebang. After the two weeks, they had actually begun growing worried
that they would never find him, perhaps it would’ve been better if they
hadn’t. A group of cavers had been in the process of excavating what
they believed to be an unexplored tunnel inside a local cave system. The
entrance to the tunnel had been obscured by about ten inches of rock and
had only been discovered when one of the cavers had heard wind howling
from a small crack in the wall face. The excavation took about a week
for the three of them to complete, but they went about their work with
the feverish excitement of discovery. Unfortunately for them however,
someone had beaten them to the punch. Inside the three feet by three
feet tunnel was the emaciated corpse of a teenage boy curled up inside.
The group of cavers fled to the surface in terror and called the police,
upon arrival the officers were able to identify the body as Martin Krane
thereby ending the search. The exact specifics of the discovery were
obscured from the media due to their disturbing and confusing nature; I
only later learnt of them from whispers and half-truths from older
officers around the office. The official story only told that the boy
had unfortunately been discovered deceased and that the exact events
were “still being worked out”. It certainly didn’t mention the fact that
the cave the boy had been found in was around five hundred feet below
exactly where the bus stop had been.

 

 

Growing tired of the lies and excuses, my partner took the man’s
fingerprints and left, we had each independently concluded the man was a
nut job. Being left alone with the man for the first time, I quickly
grew uncomfortable, there was something about the way he looked at me, a
disquieting grin, like he knew about some big joke that was being played
on me. It was then that he leaned in over the table and in a hushed
voice such as if someone might overhear.

 “I can tell you, if you like?” that unsettling grin again, we were
coming to the punchline now.

“Tell me what exactly, perhaps you can start with your real name?” I
attempted to assert myself as the dominant voice in the room but to no
avail, I was shaken.

“I can tell you what happened to little Martin?” His grin was now at
risk of breaking into full on laughter. I rose and stormed from the
room. I felt sick, partly due to whatever disgusting game this creep was
playing, but also because I so deeply wanted to know what he was going
to say next. I informed the front desk that I would be heading home, let
someone else throw that weirdo in his holding cell.

 

 

When I arrived back the next morning there was a grim air about the
place, more than usual that is. It was only when I got to the front desk
that I found out what had happened. Last night, about two hours after I
had left, “Marty” had been found dead in his holding cell, swallowed his
own tongue supposedly. Dumbfounded, I slumped back over to my desk.
Laying there was the paperwork for “Marty’s” arrest, I suppose it was
only fair it had been left to me, after leaving last night like I did.
Among the papers was the fingerprints we had taken. Suddenly, a deep
chill worked its way up my back, a dreadful curiosity I couldn’t shake.
Tentatively, I brought up the records of the boy that had gone missing.
With them was the fingerprints that had been taken after he had been
found in the cave. They were a direct match for the man in the woods.
