I’m typing this from my laptop, using a throwaway account because if my
dad finds out I’m posting this, he’ll lose his mind.

Something terrible happens in my hometown every single holiday. It all
started last Halloween.

My family is totally ordinary. My mom is a cook, and my dad is the
sheriff. He plays a huge role in protecting our town. The thing is, you
won’t find our town on Google Maps, and it’s completely missing from any
database. People call it a ghost town, but a lot of folks still live
here. I’m not going to name it for safety reasons — I don’t want any
trouble later on.

Last Halloween, my parents drove down to the neighboring town to visit
my grandparents on my dad’s side. I decided to stay home because I
absolutely hate my dad’s endless lectures about justice and doing the
right thing. A lot of people think being the sheriff’s kid is cool, but
I can easily debunk that myth.

When the evening of October 31st arrived, I locked all the doors, just
like my dad told me to. I was completely isolated from the outside
world. Outside the window, I could hear the distant voices of kids
rushing around, trick-or-treating, but none of them approached my house.
I was chilling on the living room couch, put a bowl of candy on the
nightstand, and was just watching a horror marathon on TV. Everything
around me was dead silent, except for the heavy wind howling outside.

Around 9:00 PM, there was a strange knock at the door. It didn’t sound
like little kids. Just three solitary, monotonous, heavy thuds against
the wood.

I grabbed the candy bowl, thinking I was about to pass it out to some
trick-or-treaters, but when I opened the door, I froze. Standing on my
porch was a little boy wearing a solid, pitch-black robe with a deep
hood. In his hands, he was holding a pretty large pumpkin. I couldn’t
see his face because of the shadows, but he clearly didn’t want any
candy.

He took a step forward, held out the pumpkin, and said in a weirdly
flat, hollow voice: “This is for you. Happy Halloween.”

He left so fast that I didn’t even have time to blink before he was
gone. He didn’t even hand me the pumpkin; he just placed it right at my
feet. I put the candy bowl back, picked up the pumpkin, and went inside.

I decided to take it up to my room on the second floor because I was
incredibly sleepy. I figured I’d just carve a scary face on it the next
morning. I tossed the pumpkin into the corner of my room, crawled into
bed, and fell asleep without a second thought.

I woke up at exactly 3:15 AM from a horribly foul smell that was
literally suffocating me. I could barely breathe. The room reeked so
badly, it felt like a rotting corpse was lying right next to my bed. I
couldn’t stand it and started coughing heavily, but when I got out of
bed, I witnessed a nightmare.

I turned on my phone’s flashlight and shined it over to the corner where
the pumpkin was. It was completely rotten, collapsing right before my
eyes and covered in a thick, black, stinking slime. Pouring out of a
massive crack in the shell were thousands of fat, blind, pale-white
maggots. It looked so revolting it made me gag.

They were crawling up the baseboard, literally burrowing into the
drywall and the wooden wall beams. I could hear the dry, sickening sound
of hundreds of tiny jaws chewing holes and tunnels inside my bedroom
walls.

Soon, there were hundreds of them inside the walls. I could hear them
squirming and gnawing. After a few minutes, they chewed right through
the electrical wiring, and the whole house went pitch black. My phone
was on the verge of dying.

I realized it was time to get the hell out of my own house. I hurried
over to the window near my bed to check the yard, and my heart
completely dropped.

Standing down there on the street was that same boy in the black robe.
But now, he wasn’t alone. Five other kids in the exact same robes were
standing next to him, staring up into my window as if they were looking
straight through my soul. And every single one of them was holding an
identical large pumpkin in their hands.

Suddenly, from the first floor, I heard the heavy, rusted creak of my
front door opening. I didn’t hear the lock click; the door just slid
open, even though I personally locked it with two deadbolts. A couple of
seconds later, someone began walking up the stairs to my room. I could
hear the loud, slow creak of every single step. Then, they stopped right
outside my bedroom door, just… breathing.

I didn’t wait to see what would open that door. In a fit of sheer panic,
I threw my window open and jumped out from the second floor. I landed
badly, breaking my ankle, but the adrenaline kept me moving. I literally
crawled across the grass to our neighbor’s driveway and banged on their
door until they let me in.

Our house burned down to the ground that night. The local fire
department ruled it as an “electrical fire” caused by faulty old wiring.
My dad forced me to keep my mouth shut about the boy, the pumpkins, and
the things inside the walls. He said the town couldn’t handle that kind
of panic.

It’s been almost a year since that night. We moved into a new house on
the other side of town, and I tried my best to forget everything.

But I’m writing this now because it’s September. Halloween is just
around the corner again. And yesterday, while I was down in our new
basement… I heard it.

That same sickening, dry scratching sound. Right inside the walls.

I think they are back, and I don’t think they are going to wait until
October 31st this time.
