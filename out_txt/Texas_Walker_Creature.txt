My great-grandfather left me his journal before he passed away, after
his days in the West. I found something that will make me stay far away
from Texas. I feel I should upload it somewhere as a warning. Maybe
someone needs to hear it. Before I’m gone and there’s none left to tell
the story.

I have seen death and taxes in their entirety. I’ve seen men killed,
arrested, charged and hanged all in the same day: the death they give
and the taxes they receive. I have seen many things out on the trails,
but this is a new form of collector. A hunter; as silent as the moon’s
rise and as sure as an end in the dirt. I had been tracking a man riding
north from San Angelo.

A man with a warrant out for his arrest on three counts of murder, two
men and a young girl. Robbed the group in the streets, then shot them in
the head. I left San Angelo three days ago, heading for a property he’d
been known to frequent south of the panhandle. I took the northernmost
trail in his last known direction. I kept to the main trails first, then
I would split off down the side trails that parted from the larger
routes, slowly carving my way westward as I marched on north.

​

I had only been riding for about fifteen miles on the main before I
found a scrap of his sheepskin coat. It lay tucked in a short bush at
the start of an offshoot trail. Dismounting my horse, I scanned the dirt
for any other signs, to find no others. Then reached down and grabbed
the piece of his coat. Roughly nine inches long and three inches wide.
It was covered in thick coagulated blood, hardened at the edges but
still damp and gooey in the middle.

​

Turning around, I tied the piece to my saddle before remounting my horse
and continuing down the trail. I guessed it to be about twenty more
miles before I started to enter a shallow valley. The valley dipped and
rose as I rid fourth. A thought kept pounding in my head as I went
through the rest of it. What could injure a man enough to warrant
ripping a chunk of his coat: a bison, a mountain lion? I would assume
either he’d be more injured if it was a bison, not having continued on,
but maybe scaring it off with only receiving a slight wound. If it were
a lion, there’d be more blood, more tearing, a body, whether it be man
or animal. If he managed to shoot it before it finished the deed.

​

  The sun is starting to lower further in the sky, waving a goodbye
across my face. I’m unsure if I’ll find the man I’m looking for. I
haven’t found any more clues as to his direction, and I can only guess.
The chestnut fur of my horse glistens with the warm umber haze of a late
evening sun. My head growing tired from the strain of scanning the land
constantly. I rode on a short while longer before finding a shallow
cavity in the side of a short cliff mound. The place where I’d make rest
for the night.

​

I took a short hike off the trail to reach the outcropping. Dismounting
my horse and grabbed its reins, guiding it to graze upon the hillside.
Still within my view, knowing he won’t stray far. He has always been a
loyal horse. I let my horse roam and ponder the sky and land as I set up
an area to spend the night. Just as the sun fully dragged a dark blanket
in its past place, I finished making my impromptu sleeping position. My
horse had also finished perusing the surrounding land when I stopped. It
didn’t take long before I had drifted into dream.

​

I was awakened by a terrible noise. Like the screaming of a coyote,
however twisted and plagued with the horrific cries and hollers of a man
in agony. In between the wails and cries for help. It howled but not
like a normal canine should. They dragged on for minutes in irregular
sessions. When it was done with its howling, or its twisted cries, it
switched to the next. Howl to cry, cry to howl; never stopping to catch
a breath. Only changing its tone and pitch before cycling to its next
vocal call. I wanted to figure out what horrendous creature was making
these sounds. However, I never moved; I was frozen, scared, but
curiously intrigued.

​

My horse seemed even more scared than I. The maker of the noise never
came closer, only yelling its wails at distance, through the valley. It
was unusual how calm my horse was. He was usually the type to buck and
snarl while trotting back and forth at the sound of a coyote, but there
he lay. At the base of my feet he stayed, extremely attentive to the
sound, angling his ears to catch more of it. It was as if he did not
want to give away his location in any way. He knew just as I did that
whatever this was, it was not to be confronted. For that I am thankful.

​

I had subconsciously retrieved my rifle from beside me and now held it
within my quivering hands. Then as suddenly as I awoke, it stopped.
Nothing more than the wind now howled in its stead. Shortly, all the
sounds of the night started once more. Even with normalcy returned, I
doubt either me or my horse will sleep again tonight. I can only hope
the feel of my rifle can assure my thoughts.

​

I stared on and on, straining my eyes against the sultry night, counting
every star that lay on the black blanket of sky. I’m unsure how long I
sat lying completely still. Sending silent prayer after silent prayer.
Patiently awaiting the passing of this painful night. Thankfully, the
drowsy tone of my mind won and I managed to fall asleep for the few
remaining hours of night.

​

I awakened when the sun’s warmth coddled my face. I sat up to see my
horse was still alert and watching; I quickly packed up everything and
stored it back on my horse; I don’t think he slept at all. His head was
tilted slightly; something off about it. I mounted, positioning myself
on the saddle. I steered back to the trail, but for the next hundred
feet or so, my horse’s head stayed focused on one point out in the
distance, no matter how hard I tried, straining my eyes; nothing of
significance stood out.  

I rode on past the first valley, eventually my horse’s demeanor
returning to normal. However, as they wore on, I could not shake the
feeling of being watched: as if I was being stalked by a ghost. The
whole time I rode, my hand rested on the hilt of my revolver, feeling
anxious. Four more hours passed. The mightful sun shone square above my
head. A deep silhouette peered from around a bend, lying in the dirt;
lifeless and stock still. I paced up to the carcass and came face to
face with a horse: its dark black coat soaking up the sun, causing it to
rot faster. The lower coat of the horse still shone with silvery strips
of reflectance, while the upper coat was tainted, matted with its own
blood.

​

Circling the crooked corpse, I noticed its spine had been removed;
Ripped out from a deep entry at the neck down to the tail. Tangled in
the stirrup was a leg and foot that had been removed below the knee. It
was mangled, the black pointed boot still being worn. Yellow milky bone
stuck out at the top of the leg, jagged and rough. Flesh and tendons
hung limp along with strips of denim that did the same.

​

A hefty trail of blood made its way through the dirt: combining together
with the individual grains to create a sort of sludge; tarnishing the
land of its precious purity. Following the path led me to a nearby
ditch, full of brush and tangled undergrowth. I stepped next to the
blood trail: guiding me like a shepherd to fresh water; a clean stream
to drink of pure innocence. Only I was granted a gruesome inverse, a
false stream.

​

Beyond the first layers of brush, stuck among the thin trunks of twigs
and branches. Stood one more corpse, slumped forward over itself. The
man I was looking for, mutilated before my feet, The life it once held
now sterile. The front of the man was clean, no blood aside from the
missing leg. I circled the body, reaching his back side. His wool
overcoat had been discarded to his side. Half of it was used in a feeble
attempt to wrap his stump.

​

The back of his shirt was torn halfway down his torso, soaked in blood,
exposing his back where a sunken strip of flesh had been stolen. Exposed
to the world was a crooked cavity where his spine used to sit. I felt
twisted to the core; never before had I seen any sight like this. I was
scared. Under the man’s curled corpse, his hand lay crossed over a
double-barreled shotgun: The name of the deceased outlaw etched onto its
barrels.  

​

I reached to remove it from his hold. Upon five of his fingers, he wore
three silver and two golden rings, a good bonus for a sickening search.
If someone had killed this man and mutilated his corpse, why wouldn’t
they take any of his belongings: none of the rings, not the guns nor
ammo he had; no reward for what they had done, just mutilation and the
sick trophies of two spines. Pocketing the rings, I stepped back,
pushing the man’s corpse flat onto the ground.

I stepped back through the brush, his shotgun in hand as a sign of the
man’s death. Once back to my horse, I threw myself atop, giving one last
look at the grotesque corpse of the horse, then at the patted-down spot
of brush the man lay in, before clicking my tongue and pulling in the
reins and rushing off north. Now that I had found my man, I had only
eight hours of riding before I reached the settlement of Lubbock.

​

Now two hours later and I feel uneasy; I have the feeling I’m being
watched, that inherent human instinct of just knowing. Still, I see
nothing when I scan every hill and mound. I just know there are eyes
laying gaze upon me. A killer: a mutilator, whoever is watching me is a
hunter and I’m nothing but a fawn; waiting to be confronted with the
nose of a gun.

​

I’m unsure how much time has passed, how much time is currently passing:
my senses are twisted and confused. I can feel myself slipping, growing
tired, my eyelids hanging low and more horses trot now a slow crawl. The
day once more coming to a close as the sun begins to set. I struggle to
stave off sleep, while still feeling as if I’m being watched.  

​

My eyes flutter open and as I come to, I realize I’m lying beneath a
cliff’s edge; my horse lies close, still yet asleep. I don’t remember
falling asleep; I don’t even remember coming to a stop to sleep. Even
now I find myself still feeling exhausted. Soft huffs and snarls grab my
attention as I turn back to my horse: now awake, ears perked up
attentively; its head tilted searching the cliff’s edge above us, its
huffs turning to soft heavy whimpers.

​

I scan over the cliff’s edge, nothing but a row of trees adorning its
crest. Then, I pick out another set of soft cries moving along the edges
of the cliff; echoing beyond them, moving closer. I watch my horse’s
head, tracking the sound, his head slowly drifting to the left, closer
to the ridge directly above us. My horse’s eyes were wide, full of a
bluish glow from the moon above. Now we both lay silent, his hush-toned
huffs and whimpers halted, focused on keeping silent.

​

We both track the noise until it reaches our ridge, then it stops.
Nothing, the most silent the night has ever been. All I hear is my
breathing, my horse’s breathing and another’s breathing coming from the
ridge directly above us. Both of us stared back into the night, to the
trees stressing the other being with us.  

A shrill scream rips from my horse as it’s dragged up the side of the
cliff. Blood rains down onto my person. I’m frozen; struck with fear,
unable to move. I remain frozen, listening to the horrid screams and
grunts before a loud crunch, followed by a squashing of flesh, silences
the screams. I sit shaking; unable to move anywhere.

I listen as the creature above me continues to rip into the body. The
trickling of blood now running down the rocks and soaking into me, where
my body touches the ground. Finally, I gained enough composure to start
to crawl away. Slowly making my way down the trail, dragging myself over
sand-covered ground. I crawled until I couldn’t hear the ripping of
flesh and breaking of bones any longer: until I cried, until my hands
bled from palming rock; never stopping till daylight broke; only once
daylight came did I finally stand and start running.

​

I ran until I collapsed again; until I thought I would die from
exhaustion and exposure. Until I could see Lubbock come into view. I
know upon my arrival, I’ll give up this career. I’ll move north far away
from Texas. I know I will never be free of that thing. I know it’s still
here: watching, silently stalking; I am afraid it will follow me until
it eventually gets me. The collector will not stop until it gets its
taxes.
