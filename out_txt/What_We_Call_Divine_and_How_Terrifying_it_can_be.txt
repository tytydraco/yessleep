This is a story about an entity beyond reckoning. What could it be? What
does it want from me? Why is a being so terrifying known as “God”? The
thought itself haunts the edges of my vision. Aren’t gods supposed to
oversee and protect little humans? Yet I tremble at the mere concept of
them. What exactly am I scared of? Is it death? Am I afraid of being
broken, of perpetual agony and suffering?

I used to wonder what true fear was. That night answered my questions.

What happened to me shattered my perception, my psyche, and the
fundamental way I see the world. That single night, when I encountered
that being in my dream, showed me exactly what I should be afraid of,
and the dread hasn’t left my bones since.

The time of the dream was 3:45 am, I was sleeping in my bedroom, all
lights off, fan spinning at high speeds. When you enter my bedroom, you
can see a computer table in front of the door; the wall adjacent to it
has windows for the balcony, and opposite the table is my bed. In the
dream, I was sleeping in the same bedroom, all lights off, fan spinning
at high speed, the exact way I slept in reality. This is my experience
from that night.

Then it started: a being walking on the balcony attached to my bedroom.
I could hear faint footsteps as the windows were open. I didn’t mind it,
as I felt it might be neighbors, but what happened next scared the shit
out of me: a sound. I could hear the sound of damarukam, just like how
Agoras dance by playing damarukam; I could hear the exact sound. Then I
woke up and looked around, my body started moving towards the source of
the sound, to the being walking towards my house while playing
damarukam, for a second i thought its shiva, my heart started pounding,
as body keeps me trembling as I enter hall and move towards the door,
with utmost courage I touched the door, unlocked it ready to open, then
the sound stops briefly, and then as I opened the door, I was hit by a
bright light and loud damarukam sound, my eyes and couldn’t withstand
and then I suddenly gasped and woke up from that dream.

That night I didn’t sleep; even now, I’m haunted by the damarukam sound,
even if it’s imaginary.

The time was 3:45 AM. I was in my bedroom — lights out, the ceiling fan
whirring at top speed. If you enter my room, the layout is simple: a
computer desk facing the door, balcony windows on the adjacent wall, and
my bed directly opposite. In the dream, I was lying in that exact bed,
under those exact conditions. Reality and the nightmare were seamlessly
stitched together. This is exactly what I experienced.

Then, it started. A presence walking on the balcony.

Through the open windows, I picked up the faint scuff of footsteps. At
first, I brushed it off, thinking it might just be a neighbour. But what
came next froze the blood in my veins. It was a sound. The rhythmic,
hollow strike of a damarukam. It was precise and primal, exactly like
the frenzied beats Aghoras play when they dance.

I jolted upright. Without my consent, my body pulled itself out of bed.
I was being drawn toward the source of that sound, toward whatever was
pacing outside my house. For a fleeting, terrifying second, my mind
screamed a single name: Shiva.

My heart hammered against my ribs. My limbs trembled violently as I
crossed the hall, compelled toward the front door by some unnatural
force masquerading as courage. I reached out and unlocked it. The moment
my hand gripped the latch, the damarukam stopped.

The silence was suffocating.

I pulled the door open and was instantly swallowed by a blinding,
white-hot light and a deafening crash of the drum. My eyes burned, my
lungs seized, and I gasped violently, ripping myself out of the dream,
chest heaving in the dark.

I didn’t sleep the rest of that night. I just sat in the dark,
paralyzed, listening to the violent thud of my own pulse. That is the
worst part. My heartbeat has synced to the exact rhythm of that
damarukam. Even now, the sound haunts every shadow in this house. I am
terrified to close my eyes, knowing exactly who is waiting in the dark
to finish the song.
