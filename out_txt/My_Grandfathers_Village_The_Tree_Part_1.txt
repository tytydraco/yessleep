This happened when I was in high school.

Summer vacation had just begun, and it was time for us to visit my
grandfather, who lived in the countryside. We spent almost every summer
there when I was younger, and I always looked forward to those trips.

When we arrived, my grandfather welcomed us with his usual warm smile.
He had already prepared dinner and was waiting for everyone to arrive.
That evening, almost the entire family had gathered at his house.

Later that night, my cousin Adam arrived. He was about my age, and we
had always been close.

After dinner, Adam and I went outside for a walk.

“It’s been a long time,” I told him. “Didn’t you miss me? And our
late-night adventures?”

He laughed.

“You’re always the first one to go home. What adventures are you talking
about?”

I smiled.

“Come on. Let’s go to our usual place for a while before we sleep.”

I was talking about a huge tree in the middle of the village. Its
branches spread so widely that they almost formed a roof. When we were
children, Adam and I used to sit beneath it and play there, away from
the scorching summer sun.

But Adam’s expression suddenly changed.

“No. Let’s just rest tonight. You had a long trip. You look tired. We
should go back.”

I noticed the change in his voice, but I didn’t argue. I really was
tired.

When I returned to the house, I passed the kitchen window and heard my
grandfather and my father talking inside.

As I walked directly beneath the window, I heard my grandfather say:

“You need to warn him. It’s not the way it used to be.”

I kept walking.

I didn’t think much of it.

The next morning, I went to sit with my grandfather.

“How are you, Grandpa?”

He smiled.

“I’m doing fine.”

I joked with him.

“Did you miss me annoying you?”

He laughed.

“Yes. But you’re too old to annoy me now.”

I told him I was going for a walk because I had missed the village.

Just as I was about to leave, he called me back.

“I need to tell you something. There are things you don’t know. This
area has changed a lot since the last time you were here.”

I listened.

“Don’t go near the tree in the middle of the village,” he said. “And if
you hear someone calling you from that direction, don’t answer.”

I stared at him.

“Why?”

Before he could answer, my mother came into the room and interrupted us.

I left, but I couldn’t stop thinking about what he had said.

Outside, I saw Adam.

I greeted him and immediately asked what he knew about the tree.

He froze for a moment.

“What? Your grandfather? I don’t know what you’re talking about.”

Then he turned toward the house.

“Come on. Breakfast is ready. I’ll show you what breakfast in the
countryside is really like.”

“Adam, are you hiding something from me?”

He didn’t answer.

He simply walked away.

After breakfast, we went to see another friend of ours, Noah.

Noah had always been cheerful. He loved jokes and was usually the
loudest person in any group.

When we arrived at his house and called his name, someone came running
toward us.

At first, I didn’t recognize him.

His face was pale. His eyes were red, and tears were running down his
cheeks.

It was Noah.

He grabbed me and hugged me tightly.

“Are you okay?” I asked.

He whispered against my shoulder.

“The tree…”

I pulled away.

“What about it?”

His voice broke.

“It took me. It took my father.”

Adam immediately grabbed him and tried to calm him down.

A moment later, Noah’s brother came running outside. He helped him back
into the house while Adam followed.

I stood there, completely confused.

That was when I realized something was seriously wrong.

And I knew I had to see the tree.

While Adam was distracted with Noah and his brother, I went toward it.

The tree looked different.

It seemed larger than I remembered, and much greener. Its branches moved
with the wind, but something about the movement felt wrong.

It almost looked like they were dancing.

Then I noticed shadows moving between the branches.

I stood there watching.

That’s when I heard a woman’s voice.

“Come here… Why don’t you come over? I have some food.”

I froze.

I wasn’t imagining it.

I wasn’t hallucinating.

There was definitely a woman speaking from somewhere near the tree.

I took a few steps forward, trying to see where the voice was coming
from.

Then someone grabbed my shoulder.

I spun around.

It was Adam.

“Come back,” he said.

“No.”

I stared at him.

“I’m not leaving until you tell me the truth.”

Adam sat down on the ground, exhausted.

Then he looked directly into my eyes.

“Do you remember Uncle Sam?”

“Yes. Noah’s father.”

Adam swallowed.

“He hanged himself from that tree.”

The hair on my arms stood up.

“You’re lying. What about the voice I just heard?”

“It’s his wife,” Adam said quietly. “She lost her mind after he died.
She keeps coming back to that tree, waiting for anyone who gets close
enough. She accuses them of killing her husband.”

He paused.

“She’s attacked people from the village before. Everyone is tired of
dealing with her now. They just ignore her.”

I didn’t know what to say.

I followed Adam back toward my grandfather’s house.

That night, I went to bed early.

I thought the whole thing was finally over.

Then I woke up.

Knock.

I opened my eyes.

It was completely dark.

Knock.

Someone was knocking on my bedroom window.

I held my breath.

Then came a third knock.

Knock.

I slowly sat up.

And from the other side of the glass, I heard the same old woman’s
voice.

“Why didn’t you come?”

There was a brief silence.

Then she whispered:

“I have enough food for everyone.”

And then the knocking started again.

But this time…

it wasn’t coming from the window.
