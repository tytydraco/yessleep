I have a popcorn ceiling. You know, the sorta rough paint or whatever
they used back in the day? I’ve been wanting to get rid of it, smooth it
out, but my husband says it’s vintage or nostalgic, so I left it be.
Lately though, I’ve noticed tiny holes appearing between the bumps. The
first time I saw one, it was the middle of the night and I was just
staring up at the ceiling. Unable to sleep. I though maybe it’s just the
shadows bending in tricky ways. But come morning, I stood up on the bed
and looked closer. It was definitely a hole. Barely a centimeter but
definitely more than what a pin could do. Like a screw had been drilled
in or something. It was completely dark inside. I could tell it went
pretty deep, though I couldn’t measure it by sight alone.

I told my husband about it and he said it was most likely nothing. Maybe
it’s always been there, he said, and we just never noticed. We aren’t
the first people to live in this house after all. So I went about my
days as normal, trying to ignore the strange holes, until I noticed
something again. I wasn’t able to sleep again. The bed felt itchy. I
made a mental note to clean the sheets in the morning. But during my
tossing and turning, my eyes landed on a different spot in the ceiling.
There was a new hole. I was absolutely sure this time. And the longer I
looked the more I realized it wasn’t just one. There were three. I
frantically began nudging my husband. There was no way he could deny
this. But, heavy sleeper he is, my pushing only made him grumble and
roll away. I stared at the holes for the rest of the night. The moment
sunlight streamed through the window I shot out of bed and started
shaking my husband.

“Look!” I told him, pointing up at the trifecta that had infected my
ceiling. I watched him open his bleary eyes as I frantically showed him
my discovery. He only shrugged and rolled to sit on the edge of the bed.

“It’s nothing, love. I promise.” He gently took my hand and pulled me to
sit next to him. “Don’t let this get under your skin.” His thumb ran
over my knuckles and I was left dejected, alone on the bed, while he
went to take his shower.

I couldn’t stop looking at the holes. I couldn’t let this go. So I took
matters into my own hands. I asked him where the duct tape was. After
finding it, I began covering up the holes. He watched me from the
doorway, holding two cups of coffee, silently following me as I either
stood on the bed or used a stool to cover every hole I could find. There
were nine altogether. Once I was done, he handed me my cup.

“Do you feel better?” He asked carefully. It made me feel silly. I took
the cup and nodded without a word.

We went to sleep in comfortable silence that night. I watched the pieces
of tape until my exhaustion finally took over. I woke up feeling itchy
again and mentally scolded myself for forgetting to clean the sheets. My
eyes groggily opened into the morning haze and the ceiling soon came
into view. Every nerve lit up with anxiety. The tape was gone. But, so
were the holes. As if they had never been there. The popcorn ceiling was
back to a pure white color.

I didn’t bother waking my husband this time. My hands went up to feel
along the ceiling where I knew the holes should be. Nothing. Some of the
bumps fell off in the path of my fingers and landed against my face. I
quickly wiped them away and moved off the bed. My husband was now
watching me, head still on his pillow, but he stayed silent. I felt bad
for how normal this must feel to him, but I was so sure this time it
wasn’t just paranoia.

The moment he left the bed I threw everything into the washer. I sat
against the wall opposite the machine. Just listening to it whirr while
my mind tried to make sense of this new occurrence. Maybe I could’ve
been convinced this was all in my head before, but with the holes
completely gone now I deeply feared what may happen next.

I washed the sheets twice, ran the comforter by itself, and doused the
bed in bug killer before replacing it all. Just to be safe. The day went
on, I was stressed for most of it, and my husband did his best to
comfort me. That night, when I took my shower, my skin again felt itchy.
Like dozens of invisible mosquitos had started chowing down. But I
didn’t see any bites. My skin was red, but that was from the hot water.
I decided to ignore it. Just more paranoia.

My body continued to itch after I laid down. After we said goodnight,
and up until I somehow managed to fall asleep. Sleep didn’t last long.
According to our clock, I woke up around 2 in the morning. My skin
burned. The mosquitos had turned into fire ants. I went to the bathroom,
hoping lotion would help. I carefully turned on the lights, not wanting
to flashbang my weary eyes, and I nearly shrieked.

My skin was covered in bumps. Like goosebumps, but they hurt when I slid
my hands over them. All up my arms and neck. I dreaded to check under my
pajamas. My face was the worst of all. Against my stomach’s wishes, I
looked closer in the mirror. Leaning over the sink and slowly examining
what had become of my face. I wanted so badly for this to be a bad
dream. Another horrible nightmare spawned from my paranoia. It wouldn’t
be the first time. I tapped a nail against one of the bumps and
flinched. The pain was horrendous.

My fear only increased when I felt movement. Something under my skin. I
zeroed in one of the bumps that laid right under my eye. It was pulsing.
I couldn’t look away, no matter how badly I wanted this nightmare to
end. The bump pulsed more and more until if finally popped.

Burning, hideous burning, encapsulated my whole face. Pure instinct made
me grab at my face, to try and ease or remove the source of my pain, but
that only made it all so much worse. Every bump I touched burst at the
contact.

My knees connected with the tiles. I felt blood oozing over my fingers.
Slowly, I pulled my hands away and stared at the pus strung across my
skin still connected to my face. I could tell I was screaming, but the
ringing in my ears drowned out everything else. I barely felt my
husband’s arms when he found me there, crying until I vomited. I
couldn’t stand the sight of those things crawling all over me.

The rest is blur. I was at the hospital for a long time. Bandages around
my whole body, but most heavily around my face. My husband was just
happy I was alive. He immediately agreed to my wishes of moving out of
that house. Even if we got exterminators, I would never be able to look
at that ceiling again.

We’re staying in hotels until we can get a new place. I still have
nightmares about the pain. About those creatures. And I can’t stand
looking at anything holes. I’ll throw up at the sight of popcorn. But
I’m alive. I’m no longer infected. And that’s all I care about.
