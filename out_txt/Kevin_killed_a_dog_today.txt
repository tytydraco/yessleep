I think that emboldened the men, made them feel much safer than they
should have. My husband leaned forward to whisper to Eli, “son… do you
see it from where you’re sitting?”

Eli leaned back in his chair peering through the kitchen and toward the
back door. He shook his head. I felt my heart hammering. His line of
sight led straight for the exit. If we could make it there we might be
able to escape earshot.

“Good,” Franklin said, looking at the corners he could see before taking
a butter knife from the table and nodding toward the kitchen, “if we’re
quiet, we can sneak up on her.”

His words hit me like a truck, “Franklin, no, we need to leave. We
can’t-”

Without saying a word, he reached forward and snatched a handful of my
hair, pulling my ear to be right next to his mouth. I did my best not to
cry out, “I need you to shut up and do what I say. You and Jaiden grab
something silver. The more armed we are the better.”

He pushed me back in my seat. I held my pulsing head. Hate rose to the
back of my throat. I had to swallow it down when Jaiden grabbed my arm
and begged not to stand. I turned to her, draping an arm over her
shoulders as I whispered into her ear, “sweetie, we need to be brave,
okay? I’ll hold you the whole way. Just stay with me, okay.”

She couldn’t respond with words, just nodded frantically. I picked up a
fork and reluctantly nodded to Franklin. He nodded in turn and all at
once, we stood from the table and waited. I expected to hear my little
girl say sprek with the glee she’d use to sing her favorite songs. But,
it never came.

Eli was the first to move, he knelt over Kevin’s backpack and slowly
unzipped its edge before digging inside. After a few moments, he
retrieved a handgun. He looked towards his father, “it’s not silver, but
it might slow her down.”

I felt hate again, but couldn’t tell my husband all the thoughts
bouncing around in my mind. No, I had to stay silent, attempt to sneak
Jaiden and I through the backdoor if possible. I held my daughter close
and nodded, “okay, let’s go.”

Each step felt like a cardinal sin. I felt mortal danger from every
muscle movement not taken under that creature’s orders. One word. One
word was all she needed to split me in two. I had to make sure she
didn’t get the chance. My husband led the way, his socks eased into each
step, doing their best to avoid Elinore’s toys and creaking floorboards.
Eli was close behind, moving behind his father in a way that felt fluid.
He pointed the gun along his eyeline as he scanned for it. Jaiden and I
were at the back, shuffling to our salvation like lepers to Jesus. I
could see it, the glass door.

The windows of our house rattled. She was going up the stairs. This was
our chance. Not-Elinore’s soft voice was growing more distant. Franklin
took his moment, turning the corner to the hall and taking a step toward
the stairs. Eli was close behind. I moved another path, straight for the
glass door. It was dark outside now. That was good, we could evade under
the cover of…

I saw her in the reflection. She wasn’t on the stairs. Both Franklin and
Eli had stopped in their tracks, knowing they were caught. In the glass
I saw her transparent, but threatening. A little girl silhouetted
against the static television in the living room. Those yellow eyes
burning through the sight line that cut through many open doors across
our massive home.

“Gus,” she said, “fetch..”

That mutt scrambled across the wooden floor, its nails announcing its
approach before it burst into the room and pounced on Franklin. I barely
managed to move Jaiden before the two of them fell through the glass
door and into the backyard, screaming and rolling in broken glass.

“Eli, sit back down.”

He began to obey, walking back toward the dining room.

“Come on,” I said to Jaiden, leading her through the broken door, “we
have to go.”

I stepped over my husband and his dog, ignoring his strained pleas for
help. I didn’t have time to help. I had to save Jaiden. I had to-

“Jaiden, stop.”

“No,” I cried out as the momentum of our escape was stopped in its
tracks. Jaiden let out a scream that rattled my eardrums like the
windows of the house. I tried to drag her, my strength only buying a
single inch of movement with every pull.

She cried for me, “Mom! Mom! Help! I can’t move! Please, help me!”

“Jaiden, go sit back down.”

Her screams became illegible as she casually turned and walked back into
the house. I never let go. Doing my best to plant my feet to stop her
advance. Yet, now it was her dragging me back into the house. I could
have let go. Ran to the pool in an attempt to drown out her command. But
nothing inside me allowed me to leave my daughter like that. So I was
dragged back through the broken door, finally letting go when we made it
to the kitchen. Not-Elinore stood over me. I lifted my head to meet her
eyes. My knuckles cracked white as I squeezed the fork in my hand. Her
face contorted in guilt once more, this time, the expression broke my
heart. She nodded to the table. I obeyed.

Not-Elinore had Gus drag Franklin back in the room and only allowed him
to stop biting when Franklin pulled himself back into the chair. He
looked horrible. His clothes and face red with blood. His flesh
glistened with shards of glass and dog saliva. I felt sick looking at
him. Not-Elinore returned to the head of the table. Wine bottle in hand.
She shook her head as she began forcing a screw into the cork, “what am
I gonna do with you all? I did warn you… So now, you must be punished”

Eli raised his arm, gun still in hand. I gasped, tried to tell him to
stop. Not-Elinore beat me to it, “Eli, stop.”

He froze. His finger twitched on the trigger. Not-Elinore didn’t even
look at him, just popped the cork and poured herself a glass. Once she
took a sip, she let out a long sigh and nodded, her eyes pointed down at
the table, “Eli, bite the tip of your gun.”

He did so instantly, his screams of fear muffled by the barrel resting
against the roof of his mouth. I shook my head, “please… y-you don’t
have to do this.”

“You’re right,” she said, “I don’t. That’s why I’m giving your husband
till the count of three to tell me what I want to know. If you don’t,
Eli dies.”

I looked at my husband and his face told me all, “no…”

“Three,” she said, taking another sip of wine.

“Just tell her,” I pleaded, “just fucking tell her.”

“Two,” she whispered.

I slapped his bloody face and shook him and my plea became a scream,
“TELL HER!”

“One.”

The fear overtook Eli, he stood and tried to run, the gun still locked
inside his mouth.

“Eli, pull the trigger.”

I was blinded as a spray of red splashed against my face. I heard a
clatter against the table. When I opened my eyes. Eli wasn’t standing
anymore and the gun had fallen onto the table. My sobs came as I felt
warm liquid pooling under my feet.

Jaiden reacted before I could stop her. She grabbed the gun and aimed it
at Not-Elinore who was taking another sip of wine. The gun fired. The
glass shattered. I heard a sound like metal on stone. Not-Elinore’s head
jerked backward. As did Jaiden’s. For a moment, no one moved. Then,
Not-Elinore did. She straightened her neck, the small mark on her
forehead a far cry from a wound.

Jaiden, however.

She turned to look at me. The bits of shattered bullet letting small
holes in her face and throat leak red down onto the table. She tried to
speak, her open mouth forming a large bubble of blood over her lips.
When it popped, she fell limp against the table.

Not-Elinore cursed, “I… I didn’t want that to happen.”

I stared at the body. I couldn’t move.

“This is all your fault,” Franklin said to me.

I couldn’t even reply, just cry as my son’s brain leaked at my feet. He
continued, “You had silver right next to her. You could have ended this.
You stupid bitch, can’t you tell that’s not your daughter!”

My words finally found their way out, soft and matter of fact, “she’s
not my daughter. But, when I looked at her face, I knew. She’s someone’s
daughter. You… you killed them… I spent so much of my life making sure
they were gentle and kind.”

“You babied them.”

“And you took them into the woods.”

“I made sure they knew how to be men.”

“And made men carve people open.”

He slammed his fist on the table, “they aren’t people! I made a living.
You think owing a towing company gets you this house. You seemed happy
enough to spend the money we squeezed out of these things. You wear
their blood with their silk. Cook with their broken bones. You’re just
as guilty as me and you can die with me! I’m never telling it what it
wants. We’re both done for either way, so it should know punishment.”

Not-Elinore chuckled to herself, “gosh… you are so sad… Franklin, I
could have gotten that answer at any time. I just wanted to know what
kind of man can do all this. Franklin, where were you holding my
father?”

He answered.

Not-Elinore nodded, standing from her seat and walking to my side. In my
ear, she whispered her name. Her real name. She gave me a nod of apology
and returned to the head of the table, “my people have power over names.
But, names also have power over us. Mommy, at any point, you can command
me to stop,” Not-Elinore looked at my husband, “you did say you were
ready to die, right?”

Franklin began to squirm in his seat, “Janet…”

“Franklin, sprek.”

His left eye split in two, blood splashing from the wound as he let out
a horrible yell, “Janet! Janet, stop her! Stop-”

“Sprek.”

Blood poured from his mouth as his tongue split in two, his words became
distorted, spoken with the tongue of a snake, “Ja…net… hel… pme…”

“Sprek.”

A wide red gash began emerging across his shirt. He yelled, hugging
himself to keep his torso together, “Jan…” he finally looked at me and
all the rage I could no longer swallow.

“Sprek.”

This time, he did not call to me. We were once again bathed in silence.
Not-Elinore nodded to herself, “good riddance.”

I looked away from the pile of steaming flesh that had once been my
husband and nodded, “are you going to kill me now.”

Not-Elinore sighed, her eyes pointed to the ceiling as she walked around
the table, “I came to your world with mercy in my heart. Besides you and
Elinore, only one other person has accepted it.”

“Elinore?”

Not-Elinore smiled and walked out of the dining room, “Elinore, come
downstairs.”

The windows began to rattle and my heart skipped a beat. I heard my
daughter’s voice on the other side of the wall, “you were so brave,
Elinore, I’ll give you your name back, but first I have one more game.
Close your eyes…”

A moment later, two Elinores walked past the dining room door. The one
with open, yellow eyes nodded for me to follow. I did.

When I made it outside, the two were exchanging words meant for them.
The yellow eyed girl gave my daughter back her bunny and shifted into a
naked woman with ash tree skin and oak leaf hair. She stood and gave
Elinore a nod, “Téigh chuig do mháthair.”

With that, she left us under a starry night sky.
