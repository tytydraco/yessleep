[Part 1]

Hello everyone. Thank you all so much for the support and for listening
to my recount of events. I have kept so much of this inside for so long,
and it means the world to me that you’re willing to hear me out and
believe me. Alright, I won’t keep you waiting too long.

The night of the intrusion I sat at the edge of my bed. My brain was
swimming with so many thoughts.

“Who is my Father?” My mouth still formed awkwardly around the new word.

Isabelle sat by the window freshening up, a bucket and bloodied rag at
her side.

“The male who sired you is of no relevance to your life,” Isabelle said
coldly.

“So then, I do have one? I have a Father?” My lips puckered at the
confirmation.

“Yes, yes, and no. This matter is complicated and would be better
discussed when you are older.” Once clean she stood from her chair and
went to her bedside. She blew out the oil lamp, before climbing into
bed.

“So then that’s why no one here looks like me? Does Mother look like
me?” I would not let her dismiss this. I wanted to know more.

“Mother looks like all of us. The parts that matter anyway.” Her
agitation was beginning to heighten as she rested on her back. Normally,
I would have backed down, but I wasn’t done yet.

“I want to see her. I want to ask her about my Father.” I needed to know
more.

“Malik, you are not to meet Mother until your eighteenth year. We have
discussed this.” She was trying to shut me down.

“I want to see her now!” I slammed my hand down on my nightstand.

“Malik!” Her head snapped towards me, a flame of indignation lighting
her eyes.

I remained silent. My skin prickled with nerves. I can count on one hand
the number of times Isabelle has raised her voice to me.

“…I wished to wait, but I believe Sophie should begin her studies as a
Priestess. It is time she began focusing on her true duties. You have
also begun to lose sight of your duties. You will no longer conduct your
work in pairs.” Isabelle sighed feigning disappointment at the notion.

“Wait, what? But Isabelle, that’s not fair. She’s my friend.” I wanted
to cry, but I stopped myself. Isabelle detested weakness.

“You are above that. Your duties are more pressing as are hers. Now go
to bed.” It was settled and I was helpless.

The next day I tried to speak with Sophie, but Isabelle had already
gotten to her. They had the mangled corpse of Mason in a wheelbarrow. A
swarm of flies danced around his twisted husk, clinging to the thick,
dribbling trails of blood that threatened to spill over the cart’s rim.
I stared into the open pits where his eyes once sat remembering how much
rage and hatred once filled them. Together they paraded it into the mine
shaft.

I tried to conduct my chores normally, but I had been waiting all day to
speak with Sophie. It was evening when I finally caught her. But she
looked different somehow. Even wiser than before. She barely gave me the
chance to speak.

“We can’t talk, Malik. Mother doesn’t want me filling up your head with
strange ideas.”

“What? Who told you that?” I scoffed.

“She did. You know, Mother. Now, I best get going. I’ll see you around,
okay?” I could tell by the way she looked at me that she had no
intention of seeing me anywhere.

“Yeah, okay.” I stifled my tears. Just as soon as I got a friend I lost
her.

But I still had hope. I held hope for Sophie for a long time. That was
until I saw what this place had done to her.

One night, Carrie had attempted to escape. She had been stowing away
supplies out in the woods and had even gone so far as to take from the
pantry while a Priestess was distracted. She had told Sophie she
intended to leave together, but Sophie wasn’t going with her. She went
straight to Isabelle.

Carrie was strung up from a wooden banister in the center of the chapel.
They had stripped her of all her clothes and gagged her mouth with a
dirty rag. Her wrists were raw from the hemp that scraped against her
supple, thin skin. She writhed weakly body glistened with sweat and was
littered with blooms of violet bruising from the punishments the
Priestess had certainly delivered throughout the day. It was evening now
and around this time we were usually eating, but Isabelle held a sermon
there instead. The topic for the night was Gratitude.

“Who protects us?” Isabelle’s voice rang clear and true.

“Mother!” We answered with conviction.

“Who provides for us?” She paced back and forth, steps measured with
intention.

“Mother!” We called.

“And when you take from us, who do you take from?” Isabelle stopped
pacing and stood under Carrie as she dangled limply above.

“Mother!!!” Our statement was definitive.

“You see Mothers kindness can be mistaken for weakness. Her generosity
may be mistaken for foolishness, but we must not allow that
misconception to stand. For we were hand-selected to defend her.”
Isabelle looked over the crowd and I watched as her eyes settled on
Sophie.

“Yes!” It was a unanimous chorus from all, but one.
Isabelle raised a hand and turned. An accusatory finger trained on
Carrie. We grew quiet then, a thick silence settling in.

“Thief. This woman, this rat, has been stealing. She intended not only
to take our food and our supplies, but she attempted to take one of us…”

They groaned and hissed as though hurt by the mere notion, but I
remained quiet. My eyes focused on Sophie. She was trembling.

“A priestess! She intended to rob Mother of a voice! She wished to
silence her!” Isabelle’s eyes were wide, piercing. Judging.

Their screams were almost deafening. Some even wept.

“We are the lucky few with a sacred duty. We are the lucky few who
interpret the word from the source and deliver it to all of you. She
wished to snipe her from right under our noses and venture off to the
outside. She wanted to take a piece of Mother. And so now we will take a
piece of her.”

From within her sleeve, she withdrew a blade. Sophie’s trembling grew
more noticeable then and Isabelle’s gaze never left her.

“Come, Sister.” Isabelle held out her free hand to Sophie and she took
it. Carrie’s movements grew stronger then, her eyes more indignant than
afraid.
Sophie was hesitant as she looked between the two, but I knew what
choice she would make. Isabelle knew too.

“She must repay her debts. She must repay all she took and all she ate.
Right?” Isabelle’s head craned slowly to the side. Like an owl measuring
a mouse.
“…Yes. She must repay it.” Sophie murmured.
“Louder.” Isabelle pushed.

“She must repay it!” Sophie spat.

In a single swift motion, Isabelle cut the gag, catching the side of
Carrie’s mouth in the crossfire. She wailed, her blood spilling down her
chin, but Isabelle shushed her.

“Is that how you wish to be remembered?” She jeered.

Carrie’s breaths came quickly. The rise and fall of her chest were akin
to a rodent caught in a snare and when she looked at Sophie her eyes
brimmed over with trail after trail of tears. Her eyes seemed to plead
then.

“Mommy loves you, baby. I’m so sorry I brought you here. Mommy’s so, so,
so sorry.” Carrie sobbed, dangling like a broken ornament.

But Sophie didn’t look at her. Instead, she looked down at her feet.
Vacant eyes searching for something they would never find.

“You are not my Mother.”

Before Carrie could utter another word Isabelle drove the knife through
her chest. The chapel rumbled to life with the sister’s howls. Carrie
yowled in anguish, but it was cut short as Isabelle bled her with a
slice across her throat. Carrie sputtered blood, coughing and jerking.
Her final words were incomprehensible as she slowly succumbed to her
wounds.

Isabelle made light work as she carved into her chest, the sharpened
blade gliding through her flesh with ease. Once she finally made her way
into her chest cavity, she carefully retrieved her prize. Isabelle
gripped Carrie’s still, bleeding heart and raised it in triumph. Sophie
kept her head down making a great effort not to look, but Isabelle took
her by her hair and forced it into her line of sight.

“Eat of her and, if Mother be merciful, may she live on in you,”
Isabelle growled, commanding rather than asking.

Sophie closed her eyes, inhaling a deep breath before sinking her teeth
into the crimson meat. She sobbed as she devoured, crying in fear that
soon boiled to rage. But she ate and she ate until she was done. Then
she bellowed out a deep, primal roar and all the other sisters joined
her. Then, Isabelle split Carrie straight down the middle. Her
intestines fell forward out of her body draping to the ground. Isabelle
peered around at all the women before stepping aside to fully expose her
corpse.

“Eat.”

The reaction to her command was instantaneous. They rose from their
seats and rushed forward hungrily. The messy sounds of their feasting
soon filled the chapel.

That night, I skipped supper.

Sophie was never quite the same after that and neither was I. Even so,
seasons came and went as the wheel of time turned, and before I knew it
my eighteenth year had come. It was a grand affair. Isabelle even had
runners traverse into outsider territory to harvest decorations and a
cake in my honor. After supper, Isabelle spent the evening preparing me.
She bathed me, trimmed my hair, and washed my feet all whilst crying
silent tears. It was unnerving, but my mind was occupied. My time had
come to seek an audience with Mother and I had so many questions to ask
that I considered writing them down. I was dressed in fine robes. They
had been hand-stitched and embroidered by Priestesses for years in
preparation for this very moment. My eyes danced over the details, each
one created to represent the milestones of my growth.

Once all preparations had been made, we headed off towards the entrance
to the old mine. Sophie and another Priestess stood watch. We had barely
spoken since she had taken her new role, but she met my eyes then. I
couldn’t discern what I saw in them, but I was certain she had meant to
say something. Regardless, the pair parted for us, allowing me through.
My steps were unsure as we ventured inside.

The cavern was humid and surprisingly warm. The stone inside was smooth
and ringed with mineral deposits. I was tempted to reach my hand out and
trail it along the wall, but when I reached out I could have sworn I was
met with a radiating heat. We traveled further down and I wasn’t sure
when it would end. It was dark and I couldn’t see anything. Isabelle was
my only guide and she moved with unmatched certainty. I was growing
impatient. I was just about to ask her how much longer we had until I
saw something. It was a glimmer off in the distance, but that wasn’t
all. I could hear something. A steady rhythmic thump. As we drew closer
I saw dancing lights, sweep across the floor from the clearing up ahead.
It was hard to describe, but they illuminated our walkway in a vibrant
array of hues. It was beautiful. My pace quickened and before I knew it
I was bolting through the threshold into a wide, expansive domed cavern.
The lights were coming from everywhere, but as I focused more and more
on what I was seeing the more I realized just how much I wanted to turn
back around. Just how much I wanted to unsee.

Wall to wall was covered in glimmering, pulsing flesh. It bloomed and
breathed. From each opening, a glimmering light escaped illuminating the
space, before winking closed to take the light with it. The place reeked
of life, of something fleshy and animalic. The musk was
all-encompassing.

“What…what is this?” My voice trembled in terror.

“That is Mother.” Isabelle’s words radiated with reverence.

This was not what I had expected. You see when I pictured Mother I
imagined a woman. A beautiful woman with a warm, wise smile. A kind
smile. Not a pulsating, throbbing maw.

“You know, I used to be a missionary. I traveled far and wide spreading
the good word of a holy Father. A false God. One afternoon, I found
myself in a quiet little town and I went door to door as any good little
messenger would. Many cast me away, uncaring and apathetic to the
gospel, but one man answered and was even so kind as to let me into his
home. But he was not truly kind, Malik. He was a wolf in sheep’s
clothing. You see I traveled alone and he knew that. He even knew how
far I was from home, because I was so stupid and trusting and had told
him. Unknowingly, I had traveled into a beast’s den. A sacrificial lamb
for him to do with as he pleased. That man drugged me over tea and the
next time I awoke I was in the forest. This forest. He played with me in
these very woods. I ran and screamed and no one heard me and he hunted
me over and over until he was tired.” She held up her arms displaying
deep scars around her wrist, “He strung me up by a tree. He cut off my
clothes and exposed my belly and he was going to gut me and watch me
bleed. I prayed to that false God. I prayed for him to save me and do
you know what he did, Malik?”

I was quiet, eyes still focused on the flesh that breathed around us.

“He left me unanswered, but Mother answered. She came and she saved me.
A lone doe stepped out from the woods. He tried to shoo her off, but
this deer was different. She too was a missionary and her message was
meant for me. Her head split open and from it came red tendrils of
reckoning that destroyed him righteously. I was so very afraid. She
stepped closer, her ribbons of viscera cutting me down and just when I
thought she might hurt me she bowed to me. I was confused, but she was
patient and in time as I looked into the light that danced within her I
understood, so I took that man and I dragged him up the mountain with
her as my guide and she led me right here where we stand now. Mother was
much smaller then, emaciated and hungry, so I fed her. I fed her for a
long time and I fed her an assortment of things. Deer, rabbit,
squirrels, hikers, you name it. And in time she grew and when she grew
she could now feed me. Feed us.”

The adjacent wall pulsed and throbbed. From one of its many openings, it
pushed out a misshapen, speckled fawn. It bleated pitifully, eyes barely
open. Isabelle took the babe cradling it as she shushed it. Then her
hand snaked around its throat as she callously snapped its neck. She
looked at me with more glee than I’d seen from her in my entire years of
living.

“In time she sought out others and they came slowly, but soon our
numbers doubled then tripled. As some women came, so did men who sought
after them and those men became fodder for Mother to feed. But one day
we fed her a man and she made something we had yet to see from her. She
made something so beautiful. She made you.” She fed the dead fawn back
into the same dribbling maw that had birthed it.

“Me?” I croaked.

“Yes, Malik. You.” Isabelle wiped joyful tears from her eyes.

“So then. I’m the only one born of her?” My mouth watered with the
threat of vomit.

“Yes, and that is why you are so special, but do not be mistaken. Though
we are not of her flesh we are flesh to be utilized by her. She is the
creator. She is the provider. This land is her back and upon it she
bears a fertile garden of protection. She is the land and when we tend
to it we tend to her. Do you understand Malik? Now it is your time to
truly take care of her. Your role, your purpose dwarfs that of a
Priestess’s duties. Malik, you get to help her create more perfect
beings.” She shook her hands with jubilation.

“You’re gonna feed me to her?” I whimpered and Isabelle chuckled as if
the notion was childish.

“No, of course not. You will understand. She will help you understand.
You will get to know her in ways we cannot and she won’t hurt you. She
would never hurt you.” She caressed my cheek, the sticky membrane still
coating her hand.

The flesh rippled and Isabelle looked to it, nodding in agreement to
something.

“I shall take my leave now. Do not fret. I will return for you at dawn.
Don’t make that face. You’ve wanted this for so long. Ask your questions
and receive your answers. Goodnight, Malik.” Isabelle turned and left me
then. Alone with her, with it.

I stared all around me trying to make out where she begins or ends. I
stepped closer, listening. The ever-present metronome of an organ
working filled my ears. A fleshy fold trembled before me and bloomed
open and I couldn’t help but peer inside. I was entranced, lured in by
her and I could hear her. The room felt so hot then. I couldn’t slip out
of my robes fast enough. Closer. I needed to get closer. I pressed
myself against her and I listened and I could feel myself sinking in.
Well, not all of me, just a very distinct part of me. As we melded
together I was encompassed by overbearing sensations. She smelled like
something earthy and green. Like petrichor and vetiver, but underneath
was the aroma of something meaty and pungent. A smell I can only
describe as life itself. I could feel small, insignificant bodies
crawling on and in me. I could feel the roots as they stretched and
grew. I could hear the birds and the critters as they called into the
night. Everything, I felt everything. She poured everything into me
before sucking it all back for herself and once I was empty I collapsed
to the floor, my vision fading to black as I passed out.

Someone was shoving me and when I came to I was greeted by an erratic
Sophie. She was covered in blood.

“Hurry, they will soon feel what I’ve done. We must leave now.” She held
an oil lamp in one hand and a hand axe in the other. My heart skipped a
beat as I realized what she must have meant.

“What did you do? Oh Mother, what did you do?” I looked at her
horrified, “Did you do this for me?”

“For you? I did this for me, for Ma. I just needed a time when that
awful woman’s mind would be too busy with her own thoughts to go
trifling in mine. Now do you want to leave or would you rather die here
as this thing’s boy-toy having never seen the outside?” She sneered and
for a moment I considered it. I hadn’t thought about the outside in a
long time and after this I wondered if it could hold up to the
experience of Mother. Perhaps, it could even surpass it. These thoughts
displeased her.

A larger orifice started to undulate and throb, threatening to push out
something new.

Sophie’s face grew pale. She chucked the lantern in its direction and it
burst. The flesh yelped as flames spread across it. She grabbed my hand
and the look in her eyes told me everything I needed to know.

Run.

I sprinted after her, gripping her hand tight as she guided me through
the dark damp canal. A plume of flames lit up behind us and something
bellowed out angrily. I could hear its heavy steps as it pursued us
through the void. I stumbled and almost fell many times, but Sophie held
me and dragged me out into the light of the moon. We made it out and I
was immediately met with the corpse of a dead Priestess that lay limp at
the entrance, the front of her face hacked and caved in from murderous
blows.

Sophie yanked an oil lamp from the front of the mine. She hurled it at
the sisters’ sleeping quarters and I watched in awe as the dry brittle
wood lit up in flames. As the shack was engulfed, I could hear the
waking sisters begin to scream.

Isabelle stepped out from our shack gripping a rusted pickaxe. Hatred
painted her face as she made her way to Sophie.

“Foul Sister slayer!” She lunged at the girl, but Sophie was ready. She
held up her axe, blocking the assault.

They dug their heels in, wrestling for dominance when the newborn beast
burst from the mine. It lunged for Sophie, but she shoved Isabelle
forward to intercept. It dove into Isabelle, crooked fangs sinking into
her shoulder and she wailed, but the creature didn’t seem to care who or
what it tore into as it ripped her apart. The sound of the sisters
burning and wailing crossed with Isabelle’s and that awful buzzing was
back. Just when I thought I might pass out, one voice rang true.

“Run! Run Malik! Get the fuck out of here!” Sophie commanded me and I
listened. I ran. As I heard that creature finally take hold of her I
ran. As the vines and leaves tried to grab me I ran. As I heard that
awful creature, my sibling? Child? chasing after me I ran. I ran until I
met a cliff and tumbled down the side of the mountain and hit pavement.
Bright, white lights shone onto me and a loud horn rang out. The
vehicle, a Freightliner I would later learn, came to a full stop just
before it could turn me into a streak on the earth. A heavy-set man
stepped out yelling obscenities at me.

“Kid are you trying to get yourself killed? What the hell are you doing
out here?”

“Please! Please, take me anywhere but here, please just anywhere but
here!” I begged and surprisingly he had listened. As we drove off I
stared at the plume of smoke that rose from the mountains I had once
called a home. The trucker had tried to ask me where I was coming from
and where I had been and I answered him as honestly as I could. His
response to my story was that he knew of a rehab program that could help
me get clean. That if I fixed myself up I could make something of myself
and learn how to be a functioning member of society. He didn’t believe
me and knowing all that I know now I can’t really say that I blame him.
 
Regardless, he was incredibly kind and I owe that man my life. He gave
me some spare clothes and a bottle of water. Dropped me off at the
nearest rest stop some hours away and ever since then I’ve been on the
road. I have spent my life as a nomad traveling and drifting seeing as
much of the real world as I could. I steered clear of mountains, but
nature still called to me. It was a part of me after all. I’m drawn to
it like a moth to a flame.

I’ve been able to work some jobs under the table and even saved up to
get myself a van. She’s a fixer-upper, but she’s mine. As for my love
life, it’s touch and go figuratively and literally speaking. I have
found that I don’t really like sticking with one person. You see when
people like you they tend to start asking questions and frankly, I don’t
have it in me to fabricate or give the answers. Once, I did try to tell
someone the truth about my upbringing; needless to say, it didn’t go
over well.

However, I had been seeing someone long-term recently. It was
unconventional. We didn’t use labels and we allowed ourselves to be free
as we traveled around. This had been going on for some months and
frankly I enjoyed her presence. I liked her. She didn’t wear me down for
answers. She’d just let me be and in turn I’d let her be. To this day I
don’t know where she’s from and I doubt that I even have her real name,
but that doesn’t matter because it doesn’t change who she was to me or
how much she meant. To me, she was known as Shiloh.

Shiloh was a gorgeous woman and as free as a bird in the sky.
Admittedly, she reminded me a lot of Sophie. Pathetic I know, but our
connection was different. It felt like something innate and spiritual.
We both shared a love for the natural world and sought adventure
together in it. We loved to go hiking and had a tendency to partake in
other extracurriculars as we ventured through greater, more profound
woods. One day we had set off for a national park and had made our way
deep within the path of a less popular trail. We’d set down our gear,
disrobed, and let nature take its course. Once she was atop me, my eyes
were focused on the bounce of her chest and honestly not much else
mattered in the moment. But then she stopped. I was about to ask her why
she had, then I felt something brush against my leg. When we looked
down, a vine had somehow fallen onto her. Except it hadn’t fallen. With
lightning speed, it coiled around her leg and yanked her from me.

I lurched forward trying to get after her and she fought against it
kicking and screaming, but it wouldn’t let up. Soon other vines had
latched onto her and her lower half was practically swallowed up by the
tangled mess of foliage. I looked up ahead trying to figure out who or
what snared her. Then I saw it. It was a cave. No, a mine shaft. My
blood ran cold. Once it had her at the entrance the vines snaked their
way all around her. She screamed and writhed as they made their way into
any and every orifice they could. Bloody tears leaked from the corners
of her eyes and the sounds of her bones snapping and cracking in and out
of place will forever haunt me. I tried ripping them off of her, but
there was no use. She could fight no longer and within minutes she went
limp, but then she began to move as if resurrected by some unseeable
force. The vines held her up, crucified by flora that had dug its way
into and through her palms, and through a hoarse, guttural voice she
spoke.

“Come…Home…” And just as fast as it had grabbed her it snatched her down
into that tunnel never to be seen again.

Like a coward, I ran. I ran until I was back at my van and began to
drive. I just kept driving. Away from the forest. Away from wherever her
influence could linger. I’m in some city now. I’ve been here for weeks
working some odd jobs. I found out about one of these Internet cafes and
found out about this site. I was hoping to find any others who might
have experienced something similar. Because I don’t know what to do. I
keep wondering if I should go back or if I ever should have left in the
first place. I just don’t know.

But I do know that I’m not quite ready to reconcile with my Mother.
