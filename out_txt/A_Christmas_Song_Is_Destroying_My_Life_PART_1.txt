I saw the first Santa hat of the season on a crowded F train traveling
from Brooklyn into Manhattan. The subway car was already bustling with 
the dregs of humanity. The busker who compensated with high volume and
tunelessness for what he lacked in talent. The couple filming themselves
making out. The two high school kids spitting fragments of Cheetos at
each other. But the Santa hat was the prime offender. Staring at the
white pom pom perched atop the red triangle, I felt my anxieties start
to kick in.

In my mind I pictured a series of bullet points:

The year was about to end.

I’d been hired to slash the budget of FastrBettr, a courier company
struggling under a mountain of debt.

I was far from meeting my deadline.

I was going to have to fire employees a few weeks before Christmas.

I’d surprised Amanda last year, whisking her off to Aspen for a week of
skiing and champagne. Now there was the unspoken expectation that I’d
improve on the previous surprise, that I’d drop to one knee, pull out a
small blue velvet box, open it and present her with an engagement ring.
My mind reeled with Christmas arrangements – Christmas Eve alone with
her, Christmas Day with her family? Or the other way around? And would I
have to buy presents for her whole family? There had been two new babies
since we started dating….

I trudged up the subway steps and walked towards the coffee shop that
was the favorite part of my morning routine.

A customer walked out of the shop clutching a cup customized with
snowflakes and candy canes. I started to walk inside. The sounds of
children singing carols over Christmas bells halted me in my tracks. The
sight of the coffee shop staff sporting their own Santa hats sent me
backing quickly away and hurrying towards the corner of Fifth Avenue and
Nineteenth Street, location of FastrBettr.

As the elevator doors closed, a breathless voice called out, “Hold the
door!” My initial reaction was to pretend I hadn’t heard. But then I saw
the red face and beseeching eyes of the person outside the dwindling
gap. Siobhan the office coordinator. Hard-working, uncomplaining,
empathetic Siobhan who’d left a plastic container of home-baked cookies
in my office on my first day. “I know you’ve got a hard job to do,”
she’d told me, understanding shining from the puppy eyes that drilled
into mine. “If you ever need a shoulder, I’m right here.”

The nicer Siobhan had been to me in the subsequent weeks the more I
found I kind of hated her.

Siobhan’s puppy eyes sparkled with gratitude as she clomped her way into
the elevator. I glanced at her snowman earrings and the voluminous green
scarf wrapped around her knee-length red coat with the white faux fur
cuffs.

“It’s the most wonderful time of the year,” trilled Siobhan, misreading
the expression on my face as approval. As if on cue, a tinny, tinkling
melody filled the elevator.

A Christmas song, one I felt I had been subjected to since I was in the
womb but whose title I couldn’t quite remember, played on Siobhan’s
phone.

“My Christmas ringtone,” she beamed. “It’s fun, right?”

Without waiting for a reply, Siobhan accepted the call and, during the
elevator’s climb to the seventeenth floor, I was a hostage to Siobhan’s,
by turns, whispering, giggling, tearful, joyous conversation with a
family member about the surprise Christmas party she was throwing that
would reunite the entire Siobhan clan for her mother’s eightieth
birthday.

I exhaled with relief as the doors opened on the seventeenth floor. I
stepped out of the elevator and began to walk to my office. Siobhan
called after me. I didn’t slow down, look around or in any way
acknowledge that she’d spoken. Instead, I walked into my office, closed
the door and took off my coat.

The sound of Siobhan’s Christmas ringtone accompanied her pushing open
my door. “I’m so sorry,” she said into the phone. “I can’t talk right
this minute but I’ll be sure to call you right back. Have a wonderful
day.”

“Chloe’s the sweetest,” Siobhan beamed at me. I had passing
acquaintanceships with at least thirteen Chloes and, since Siobhan
neglected to elaborate, had no idea who she was talking about.

I brought up a file on my laptop and stared at it, hoping Siobhan would
pick up on the cue to leave. Instead, she sat down in front of my desk.

“So,” she said. “Christmas. Where do we start? The party, obvi. Last
year was hard to top but that’s the kind of challenge I rise to. There’s

Kissmas, fun, right? Super-busy this time of year but I’ve got an in,
their drummer’s brother’s friend…”

Siobhan’s ringtone started playing again. She held up a finger to me as
she took the call: “Hiiii…I know, I can’t believe it…it’s right around
the corner, how does the time go by so fast?”

“Count to ten,” was one of the exercises my meditation teacher had

The singing. The smiles. The bucket overflowing with donations. Was
everyone in the city involved in this conspiracy to destroy my life?

I felt the wave of anger crash over me. I didn’t count to ten. I didn’t
take a deep breath. I didn’t picture a relaxing scene. I wanted my voice
to be heard. I stepped off the sidewalk and charged across the street,
headed directly towards the group of choristers.

I ignored the mass honking of horns and the shouts of furious drivers
braking to avoid hitting me. All I cared about was getting that choir to
shut the fuck up.

Then the bottom of my cardboard box fell open.

The contents, my few meager possessions, rolled across the road. I knelt
down to retrieve my letter opener. That’s when the black Honda Accord
braked sharply to avoid hitting me. That’s when the red Toyota Corolla
rear-ended the black Honda Accord.

The door of the Honda Accord opened. The driver, his face purple with
fury, emerged from the car and headed straight towards me. The door of
the Toyota Corolla opened. The sounds of screaming, crying children
spilled out.

The driver, a woman incandescent with rage, saw the Honda driver heading
towards me.

She broke into a run.

“You’re fucking dead!” fumed the Honda driver, pointing a finger in my
face. I raised my hand, momentarily forgetting I was clutching the
letter opener. The letter opener with the sharp point. The letter opener
that could, to some, could resemble a weapon…
