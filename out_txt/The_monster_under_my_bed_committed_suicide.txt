I’m writing this because my teacher said stories don’t have to be made
up to be stories. She said sometimes the things you don’t understand are
the ones worth writing about.

I live in a house that never feels fully asleep.

My parents share a room down the hall. At night I hear them talking
sometimes, but it never sounds normal. It’s always low and tight, like
the words are being kept from escaping. My mum does most of it. My dad
doesn’t answer much anymore.

During the day she can almost pass for normal. She makes food, asks
questions, even laughs sometimes. Then something shifts and she goes
quiet for hours, like she forgets how to be around people. I never know
which version of her I’m going to get.

When she gets angry, it starts small. My shoes in the wrong place. The
way I close doors too slowly. Then it becomes something else, like I’m
not someone she’s talking to anymore.

My dad never stops it. He just turns away slightly, like distance can
make it not real.

When I was younger, I thought there was something under my bed.

At first I did what kids usually do. I avoided looking at the gap. I got
in from the side. I kept my feet tucked in. But one night I woke up
because I couldn’t hear my own breathing properly anymore.

Something else was.

I didn’t tell my parents.

I stopped being scared of it after a while. It never came out. It never
tried to touch me. It just stayed there like it belonged.

It didn’t speak like a voice. It spoke like a thought that wasn’t mine.

Don’t move.

Wait.

Not yet.

It told me to stay still when the house got loud.

It told me when not to look toward the hallway.

I asked it once what it was.

It said it couldn’t remember what it used to be.

I drew it during class when we were told to draw something that didn’t
exist.

I remember pausing before I started, because I wasn’t sure where to put
it. Not on the paper, but in my head.

What I drew didn’t look like a monster in the way stories usually mean.
It looked like something that had been trying to form a shape and kept
failing halfway through. Angles that didn’t settle. Parts that didn’t
agree with each other. Like it existed in more than one position at
once.

When I showed it later, it told me not to show anyone else.

It sounded unsure when it said it.

I asked why.

It said if someone else looked at it properly, it wouldn’t stay the
same.

That was the first time it sounded afraid of anything outside the bed.

Things at home didn’t get worse all at once.

They changed in small, specific moments.

One afternoon I dropped a glass in the kitchen. My mum didn’t react
immediately. She just stood very still for a few seconds, looking at the
broken pieces, then asked me why I always made things louder than I
needed to. Her voice was calm, but her hands were shaking when she
cleaned it up.

Another night I heard her outside my room, standing in the hallway. She
didn’t come in. She just stayed there for so long I started to think she
had fallen asleep standing up. When I opened the door slightly, she was
facing away from me, whispering something I couldn’t hear properly. When
she noticed the door move, she went completely silent and walked back
down the hall.

My dad got quieter through all of it.

Not suddenly. Just less present each day. Until one evening I realised I
couldn’t remember the last time I’d heard him speak properly.

The thing under my bed got quieter too.

Like it was listening more than it was talking.

One night I asked it why it stayed.

It didn’t answer straight away.

Then it said it tried leaving once, but the house felt heavier when it
did. Like something inside it pulled back when things tried to leave.

Last night there was no arguing.

No raised voices.

No doors slamming.

Just silence that felt held in place.

Then I heard a sound from the hallway.

One noise. Not loud. Final.

After that, the house didn’t move for a long time.

The thing under my bed shifted.

It told me not to look.

Not this time.

It sounded like it already knew I would.

I got down slowly and looked.

There was no shadow waiting.

Just my dad.

He was on the floor in the hallway, half-turned toward the room like he
had tried to reach it and stopped halfway. His face was still, but not
peaceful. More like something had left it unfinished. The space around
him didn’t feel right, like the house hadn’t decided how to hold him
yet.

I didn’t move.

Behind me, I heard my mum.

Slow steps down the hall.

Careful. Controlled.

Like she already knew what state the house was in and was choosing how
to enter it.

The thing under my bed was gone.

Not suddenly.

Just missing, like it had stopped belonging there a long time ago.

And I understood, then, something I hadn’t wanted to.

It hadn’t been hiding under my bed because it was safe.

It had been hiding there because it was the only place in the house that
wasn’t worse than everything else.

And now there was nothing left in the dark willing to stay between me
and the rest of it.
