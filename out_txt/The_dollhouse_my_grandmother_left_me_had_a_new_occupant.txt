My grandmother was a collector. Not of anything particularly valuable
you understand. Just small trinkets and knick-knacks she picked up at
yard sales and antique shops. Her house was packed to the gills with
glass figurines dusty porcelain plates and shelves overflowing with
forgotten histories. When she passed away two months ago I was the only
one in the family who really appreciated her peculiar treasures.
Everyone else just saw junk. I saw stories. So when her lawyer called to
say she’d left me her dollhouse I wasn’t surprised. I was actually
touched.

It wasn’t just any dollhouse. It was a replica of her own Victorian home
complete with tiny hand-painted wallpaper and miniature claw-foot tubs.
She’d spent years on it adding details whenever she had a free moment. I
remembered playing with it as a child. Moving the tiny furniture around
imagining the lives of its silent residents. It was beautiful and
intricately made.

The dollhouse arrived in a large crate. It took up a significant portion
of my small living room when I finally got it set up on an old coffee
table. Dust motes danced in the sunlight around its tiny windows. It
smelled faintly of old wood and something else I couldn’t quite place.
Like dried potpourri and a hint of something metallic. I spent that
first evening just admiring it. Running my fingers over the miniature
roof shingles. Peeking into the perfectly furnished rooms. It brought
back a lot of good memories.

The next morning I woke up feeling refreshed. I made coffee and went
into the living room. That’s when I noticed it. Something was different.
The tiny rocking chair in the parlor was no longer facing the miniature
fireplace. It was turned towards the front door. And a small porcelain
doll was now seated in it. I blinked. I didn’t remember putting a doll
in there. I didn’t even remember seeing that particular doll before. It
was wearing a faded blue dress and had a serious expression on its
painted face.

I chalked it up to being sleepy or perhaps my grandmother had left it
arranged like that and I just hadn’t noticed. My grandmother was known
for her little surprises. I carefully picked up the doll and placed it
back in the small wooden box where I found a few other miniature
figures. Then I went about my day. It was nothing. Just a small detail.

When I came home that evening the doll was back in the rocking chair.
This time it was holding a minuscule book. Its tiny pages were blank of
course. But the doll’s posture was distinctly different. Its head was
tilted slightly to the side as if reading intently. A shiver ran down my
spine. I knew I hadn’t put it there. And I definitely hadn’t left it
holding anything.

I picked up the doll again. This time I placed it back in the box and
taped the lid shut with masking tape. I even placed a heavy book on top
of the box for good measure. I told myself it was the house settling or
a trick of the light. But a part of me felt a growing unease. My
grandmother had always been a bit eccentric. But this felt different.

That night I woke up to a soft scraping sound. It seemed to come from
the living room. I lay in bed for a moment heart pounding. Then I forced
myself to get up. I grabbed the old baseball bat I kept by my bed and
crept into the living room. The room was dark. Moonlight streamed
through the window illuminating the dollhouse. Nothing seemed amiss. I
was about to turn back when I noticed it.

The masking tape on the doll’s box was torn. The heavy book I’d placed
on top was pushed to the side. And the doll itself was gone. I scanned
the dollhouse. My eyes finally landed on the miniature bed in the master
bedroom. The doll was lying there under a tiny quilt. Its painted eyes
stared up at the tiny ceiling.

I didn’t scream. I didn’t even gasp. I just stood there. Holding the
bat. Staring at the doll. It wasn’t possible. It just wasn’t. There was
no explanation. My hands started to tremble. I knew then that it wasn’t
just my imagination. Something was happening. And I had no idea what to
do.

The next day I tried to explain it to my friend Sarah. She just laughed.
Said I was stressed and needed a vacation. She suggested I throw the
dollhouse away. But I couldn’t. It was my grandmother’s legacy. A piece
of her.

Over the next few days the doll continued to move. It would be in a
different room each morning. Sometimes it would be sitting at the dining
room table. Other times it would be standing by a window as if looking
out. Once I found it in the tiny bathroom sitting on the miniature
toilet. That was when I started to feel truly terrified.

I began to notice other things. Small items in the dollhouse would
rearrange themselves. A tiny vase would appear on a different shelf. A
miniature rug would be rolled up. It was always subtle. Always just
enough to make me doubt myself. To make me question my own sanity.

Then yesterday I found something new. A tiny handwritten note on the
miniature kitchen table. It was so small I almost missed it. The
handwriting was delicate and elegant. Like an old-fashioned script. It
just said “You’re next.”

I didn’t sleep at all last night. I sat in the living room watching the
dollhouse. Waiting. The doll was in the tiny attic looking down at the
miniature version of my living room. Staring at me. Its painted eyes
seemed to follow my every move. I could almost hear it breathing. My
grandmother’s dollhouse is no longer just a memory. It’s a prison. And
I’m starting to think I’m not the only one living in it.
