Out of anything that’s happened to me in my life, this is by far the
most bizarre, disturbing, and scariest. For privacy, I won’t be giving
out my real name or anybody else’s in this story, so I’ll just go by
Kyle. This all happened around 2 years ago from now when I was 21. I
was, and still kinda am, a pretty social guy. And when I say that, I
don’t mean like I’m a major extrovert, but I just find it easy to talk
to people and make friends or acquaintances.
I say all this because sometimes I meet people that I probably shouldn’t
hang around with or be associated with, but what can I say, that’s just
kinda how I am. I’ve always hung around the wrong crowd since middle
school, and it just got worse when I went into high school, but that’s
besides the point. At the time, I lived in a small apartment that
definitely wasn’t the best. I swear I saw a new rat in there every week,
but it was definitely better than nothing.
Really, my life at that point in time wasn’t the best. That’s why when I
met Larry, who was my neighbor, I was genuinely happy. He seemed like a
really chill dude. He was a normal-looking guy, and I would always see
him just walking around outside on his phone every time I stepped out my
door. Then one time we just started talking. Nothing serious, just how
we were both brokes in our twenties trying to survive.
Sorry, I feel like I’m rambling. So long story short, me and Larry
became pretty cool over a few weeks. That’s why when he told me he was
having a little hang out with a few of his friends and asked me if I
wanted to join, I immediately said yes. I remember telling him, “so it’s
basically like a party?” and for some reason, Larry looked at me with a
kinda mad face and firmly said, “no, not like a party” with a pissed-off
tone. Which I found pretty weird, but I just brushed it aside and
thought nothing of it. I asked him what day since I had work during the
week, and he told me Saturday. Which was great. So I thought.
Larry’s place was about three or two doors down from mines. I got
dressed—nothing fancy, probably just blue jeans and a black tee. I went
over and knocked on Larry’s door, and when it opened, the person
standing on the other side wasn’t Larry. It was this other guy. He asked
what I wanted. No hello or hey, just that. I only responded by saying
Larry, and the guy yelled back for him. Larry walked over and said it
was cool for me to come in.
I walked inside, but something in my gut told me no. Something felt
seriously off, from that guy’s attitude to the way Larry was staring at
me, almost like he didn’t know me. But that wasn’t even the half of it.
When I walked inside, I immediately noticed how big Larry’s place was
compared to mines. I was not expecting his place to be so big; I kinda
thought it was gonna be similar to the way my place looked inside.
Another thing I noticed was the amount of people there. It was
definitely not just some simple hang out. It had to be around 20 to 27
people in there. And something very strange I noticed was that everybody
was wearing purple. I don’t know why, but some had purple shirts, purple
hoodies, and some had purple shoes.
I stuck out like a sore thumb. I felt very uncomfortable and wanted to
turn back because everybody kept glancing at me. Then Larry walked up to
me and asked if I wanted a drink. Before I could even say anything, he
guided me to the kitchen and poured me a drink. Without thinking, I
drank it, which I immediately regretted. Everything just felt very off.
But after a few minutes, it started becoming like a normal party. People
were drinking, smoking, making out, and the music was loud—but not loud
enough for people to come knocking. I started to loosen up, but I
noticed something. Every few minutes I would look up and see Larry just
staring at me. Every time I would catch him, he’ll stare at me for a few
more seconds and then go back to partying like nothing happened. I did
start to feel a little tipsy and tired, but I tried not to fall asleep
because I did not want to spend the night there.
But of course, the sleepiness caught up to me. I went over to the couch
and laid down. I told myself I was just gonna close my eyes for a few
seconds, but I eventually dozed off and knocked out. Next thing I
remember was waking up, and everything was pitch black. There was barely
any light, and I wouldn’t have seen anything if it wasn’t for the
moonlight coming through the window.
I got up slowly and stumbled around. I tried to feel my way around and
look for the exit, but it was hard cause I couldn’t see anything because
of how dark it was. As I was looking for the exit, the realization of no
one else being in there hit me. I was the only one left. As soon as I
figured that out, I heard talking and footsteps coming from another
room. I knew I shouldn’t have, but curiosity got to me. Blindly, I
slowly followed the noise, and it led me to one of the back rooms.
I was standing in front of the door, which was completely shut, but I
was able to quietly put my ear up to it and listen. I heard talking, but
I couldn’t make out what they were saying. So what I did next was so
damn stupid, and I still regret it to this day. Feel free to call me a
dumbass, cause I don’t know why I did this, but I cracked the door open
just enough where I could see into the room.
I guess the sound of the door opening was too loud, because as soon as I
did it, everyone turned their heads to look right at me. My heart
started pounding so fast and hard. In that split second, I was able to
see that there were around 10 people in there—way less than how many
people were there earlier. They all had masks covering their faces, like
a black ski mask or balaclava, except for Larry. Larry was standing
there without a shirt on, and in his left hand, he was holding a leather
belt.
I didn’t know what the hell was going on. Then Larry quickly walked over
to a drawer in the corner of the room, opened it, and took out
something. It was a pocket knife. I just stood there in disbelief until,
without any warning, Larry started sprinting straight towards me. I
snapped back to reality and turned to run.
I was so scared, and I still couldn’t find the front exit because of how
dark it was. I started to hear Larry banging on the walls and screaming
in anger behind me. I was running around in a panic in complete
darkness, until my hand hit a doorknob. Hoping it was the front door, I
threw it open, slammed it behind me, and locked it. I was standing in
total darkness, with no idea where the hell I just trapped myself. I
scrambled my hands along the walls, desperately feeling around until my
fingers hit a light switch. I flipped it on, and to my surprise, I was
in a small bathroom.
My heart was pounding so hard, I was completely scared out of my mind.
Then, Larry started pounding and banging on the bathroom door. I looked
around and, to my absolute luck, saw a window. Larry started kicking the
door, and the wood began to crack. Without a second thought, I threw
open the window. It was really heavy, but my adrenaline kicked in. There
was no time for rational thinking. I jumped out of the window and
luckily landed unharmed.
I ran straight to my place and basically slammed the door shut. I locked
it and grabbed my phone to finally call the police. Right then, I heard
loud bangs at my front door. It was Larry. He was trying to break my
door down. I yelled at him to go away and that I’m calling the cops.
Then Larry yelled something that I will never forget. He screamed at the
top of his lungs from the other side of the door, “I’M GOING TO FUCKING
KILL YOU!!!” That was it. I was on the phone with the cops and let them
know exactly what was going on. They told me to stay calm and that they
were on their way.
Then, the banging abruptly stopped. I built up enough confidence to look
out my peephole, and I saw Larry walking away, his back facing me. I let
out a sigh of relief and just stood there waiting for the cops to come.
About 15 minutes later, the cops showed up. I of course explained
everything, and they went to go look around over at Larry’s place. But
they couldn’t find anybody. Everyone that was there, including Larry,
was gone.
And I remember thinking, I definitely didn’t hear any cars leaving or
nothing. The only thing the cops could find were empty cups from the
party earlier, but that was about it. I honestly feel like the cops
didn’t take me seriously because I wasn’t in serious physical harm, and
to this day, thinking about that still pisses me off.
I honestly have no idea what Larry and those other people were doing. He
seemed like a very genuine guy at first. But from the way that guy
greeted me when I knocked on the door, to everyone wearing purple, to
those people in the room with the masks… I have no idea what those
people were up to, and honestly, I’d like to keep it that way.
