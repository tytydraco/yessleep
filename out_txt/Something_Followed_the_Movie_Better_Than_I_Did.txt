I had pulled up to the cinema in my Honda Accord. It was a midnight
show. There was only one other car in the parking lot. I walked towards
the multiplex, the one I had been going to since my teenage years, the
same one from when I was a kid, actually, though I hadn’t thought about
that in years until I was standing in the lobby again. I had stuffed my
jacket with chip packets. They did not check for foods you brought and
did not enforce the “only food from the theatre” rule.

The smell of carpet cleaner and caramel popcorn filled the air. I had
forgotten my drink, ordered one from the counter, and was holding it in
my left hand. The theatre was empty except for four or five people
rather than me. I went up to seat F12 and made myself comfortable. I
reclined the seat, took the chips out of my jacket, and settled the Diet
Coke on the seat beside me. The trailers started playing. Then a man
came and sat right beside me. He took my armrest and made himself
comfortable.

He was pushing his forties, I think, and wore a tuxedo with a top hat.
It looked ridiculous because the colours didn’t match at all, like he’d
just worn whatever he had. A beige tuxedo with a green matte top hat.

Then he started mumbling. A whisper at first, but he increased it so
only I could hear it. He was saying every line of dialogue on the
screen, the actor would say a line and he would repeat it word for word,
even before the actor in the trailer finished saying it. When the movie
started and the production music filled the theatre, he sang along too.

I moved seats to F6. I could see him slowly turn his head through my
peripheral vision. I looked at him for a second, he wasn’t blinking, and
there was a wide frown on his face. I turned back to the screen. He
crept up, moving like a cartoon character. Elbows folded, palms
perpendicular to the ground, knees coming up and down with each step.
The frown turned neutral, then as he got closer it turned into a smile,
eyes staring right into mine. He mumbled every word of the scene, still
looking at me, still smiling. His neck stayed turned towards me the
whole time. It was as if he knew the whole movie line by line.

I got up and asked the teenager at the counter if there were seats
booked near mine, and if there were only five tickets for the whole
show. He checked and confirmed, five people booked for the night,
excluding me, and nobody else in row F.

I asked where the washroom was and went. Standing at the urinal, I heard
the old door creak open. It was him. His eyes fixated on me and a smile
drew on his face straight away. He stood uncomfortably close even though
other urinals were free, mumbling again, the movie dialogue, I thought.
I didn’t wait around to find out. I walked out through the bathroom,
some distance away, and he brushed straight through me while dancing his
hands in the air. Moving. I went back and washed my face.

I went out for a smoke instead, needed a Marlboro more than I needed to
think about any of it. Through the smoke I saw his shoes first, ragged,
expensive leather, feet parallel, pointed at the door, not moving. I
asked him to kindly go. He just whistled. I went back inside a different
way so I wouldn’t get tricked again, but he was already at the screen,
swinging his neck through the big wooden door as he entered, hands
gripping the frame. His mouth was open just a little. It wasn’t a smile
or a frown. I couldn’t place it.

I thought about leaving. But the vacation I’d fought months of gruelling
work to get kept me there. Stupid decision.

All the others except a woman were asleep in their seats. I returned to
F12. Hadn’t seen him for a while, but halfway through the movie I heard
mumbling behind me again, except now the dialogue was lagging seconds
behind what was playing on screen, not ahead of it, like something had
slipped out of sync. I looked back on instinct.

He wasn’t behind me. He was in the seat directly beside me, the one I’d
started in, sitting exactly the way I had been sitting three hours
earlier, jacket open, hand resting where mine had rested on the armrest,
mouth stretched into that same unnerving smile, and he lifted his hand
and wiggled his fingers at me, slow, like he’d been waiting the whole
runtime to do it once I finally turned around.

I bolted through the wooden door and reached my car. Drove straight to a
motel so it wouldn’t follow me home. It never did. I haven’t seen it
since.

I’m about to check out, writing this sitting in the motel room. I
checked my car before I left. I checked the boot too.

I think it’s gone.

It’s gone.

It has definitely gone.
