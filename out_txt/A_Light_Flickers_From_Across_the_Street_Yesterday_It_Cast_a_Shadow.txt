It looked like a simple faulty bulb at first. It should have stayed that
way, but of course I was too curious for my own good. Moving to a new
place means your brain often picks up odd habits, to form a new sense of
normalcy, and this was one of them. Staring out the window at night
while drinking a cup of tea that is. Lots about the habit was familiar.
The stars, the warm aromatics sliding down my throat, the cold air
seeping in through the crack in the window. What wasn’t familiar was the
fact that I was thousands of miles away from my home country, looking at
a sky that should have glowed with afternoon sun, and –then– the light
flickering from inside a flat across from me. Now, in Scotland, and the
UK in general, utility issues are as common as a mosquito in the
summertime. Hell, I had dealt with at least six in my own flat during
the two months leading up to this incident. It should have stayed a
utility issue. I wish it were a utility issue. 

When I saw it I thought that it was, along with one of my more mundane
impulsive thoughts. I thought, wouldn’t it be cool if I put the flickers
into morse code. I suppose that thought came from nostalgia as I had no
concrete knowledge of morse code. I did, however, use it to decode
random sounds in Five Nights at Freddy’s in middle school. So –again–
nostalgia. I should have just read a book or played a video game, but
curiosity compelled me into grabbing my computer. I pulled up a morse
code translator and sat by the window. Once I figured what qualified as
dots and what qualified as dashes, I could parse out a sentence. I told
myself if it was complete garbled nonsense, I would stop. It wasn’t. 

What it read was;

“Daniel 413, you shall know me”

My name isn’t Daniel so I dispelled that notion. The numbers made me
raise an eyebrow though, when I was typing it out I thought it was the
beginning of a phone number. Then the rest came and I was left unnerved.
I kept going though, typing away into the translator. At multiple points
I felt the shame of potentially encountering the person living in the
flat, meeting eye contact with a stranger whose home I had been watching
for half an hour. But it seemed the flat was empty. Not just of
inhabitants but of curtains, furniture, or anything hanging on the wall.
This fact continuously creeped me out, somehow even more as the light
went out. I turned my attention back to the translator which had
generated almost an entire paragraph at this point. 

“All the world is dirt All the dirt is soil All the life shall return to
it in kind Humanity shall see each others faults as derived from my eye
before in the endless wheel of heaven extinguishes another star I know
you then as I knew you now I know the baptismal water you have shunned
Know the creations you have made It is your’s and many’s faults The
cloaked figure shall return from the grave He was once buried in Only in
the graveyard shall every mass atone its sins”

I stared back at the dark flat, searching in the inky black for a reason
to look away. A face in the veil to terrify me. But there was only
darkness. Even so, I had a lingering fear that something was watching
me. And it didn’t go away. I closed the translator tab and huddled in my
small bed. Trying, in vain, to scroll far enough away from the words
still echoing in my mind. It took me more than a couple hours to fall
asleep that night. 

Don’t get me wrong, I was intrigued just as much as I was terrified. Not
only the fact that the lights spelled out more than just random letters,
but the cadence of the sentences was enough to know that this wasn’t
just some random hoax. And even if it was, it was an extremely
entertaining one. I came to know from my research that ‘Daniel 4:13’ was
a bible verse. One, pertaining to an angelic vision had by King
Nebuchadnezzar. I grew up catholic and since my teenage years, have
adopted a more agnostically pagan view of theology. Suffice it to say I
wasn’t very up to date on my bible knowledge. The only passage I
remembered was the one about Bathsheba which wouldn’t do me any good in
this case. I didn’t do much research at the time, as I was wrapped up
with classes and job applications. Not before another message came to me
through the lights. 

This time I made sure to record the time the flickering started; 8pm
GMT. This time the flat appeared completely changed somehow. Instead of
the creme paint adorning the walls, they were a deep green flashing in
and out of illumination. There were also stairs at the back of the room
that appeared to lead to nowhere. Like not just out of my line of sight,
I could see a blank wall at the top of them. I was perplexed to say the
least but I had to focus on the lights. 

This was the second message;

“I extend my greetings once more As much as I begrudge in doing so I
used to beacon through lighthouses Communicating to kings of the sea and
land They lapped up my narratives like eager hungry kittens with warm
honied milk They took my words throughout their entire bodies It was
their reason to breath But they changed They made their own stories Sang
their own songs Gained their own reason to sustain themselves As if I
were just a guiding force to further their selfishness Not their saviour
Not their reason for life Now I am reduced to you For reasons I know not
You shall know the true meaning of death And you shall thank me for it”

I was confused and quite frankly perturbed the second night the lights
went out. Up to this point I thought this was some kind of religious
ghost fixated on bible verses and sermons but a lighthouse? What could
that mean? My thoughts swam and before I could even think to research
further, the light blew out in my room. I was alone in the dark, with
that terrible feeling of eyes on me. With the hairs on the back of my
back standing up, I decided that meant I needed to go to sleep. Or at
the very least try. 

I was drowning. But I didn’t feel like I was drowning. It felt like how
you imagine drowning feels as a kid. Calm. Peaceful. Sinking. I realized
I wasn’t alone. There were others, floating alongside me. Family,
friends, and strangers. There must have been millions sinking with me
into the darkness of the vast ocean. Until it was not just darkness. It
was hands. Dozens of large appendages. Cracking their bones and twisting
skin through the water to pluck and drag our limp bodies down one by
one. I tried to swim away, tried to move back to the surface but I was
swiftly dragged down at a breakneck speed. I saw the light fade from the
surface until there was only darkness. Then a large hooded figure
emerged, so vast was it that it loomed over desolate mountains of stone
and effigy. It had no face , nor eyes, nor mouth to speak. But somehow
it screamed. 

Or at the very least, I did when I woke up. It took me several moments
to come down from my panic, my heart still beating a mile a minute
despite the desperate intakes of air, and even more for me to feel
comfortable getting out of bed that morning. I just couldn’t help but
feel that the giant in my dreams was the one watching me. Even now, I
can still feel its nonexistent eyes observing my every movement. My
every thought. Every word I write upon the page. It was reasonable to
assume that this whole thing was getting to my head. That’s why I had
that nightmare. That’s why I was feeling such overwhelming dread. It had
to be. That’s why I decided that it would be good for me to not look out
my window the next night. Nothing happened out of the ordinary and my
dreams were relatively forgettable. So I thought I had found the
problem; I was fixating too much on something that, for all I could
know, was just an elaborate string of strange phenomena.

I thought so until yesterday. When I got home from classes, admittedly
late as I stayed at the library for longer than anticipated, my room was
dark. Cold, especially as the harsh winds that afternoon had blown open
the window wide. I groaned and paced to close it. That’s when I saw the
lights of the flat across from me, flashing rapidly. With no rhythm like
the last time. Nor reason. The paint on the walls was blood red. Arches
and staircases appeared to have no end to them. Every corner of the room
was splayed with pristine geometry that –when I looked closer– appeared
to be moving. Oscillating and rotating as if pulled upon the belts of a
smooth machine. What I was seeing couldn’t be possible. Shouldn’t be
possible. And yet there it was. Then the light went out. 

I exhaled deeply, as I realised I had been holding my breath in fear.
Just as my brain began processing what it had seen moments prior, the
light flooded the flat again. The walls were placid and creme again.
Blank like a canvas. But that became a temporary relief as a figure
could be seen in the middle of the room. Suddenly time slowed as I
observed the shifting creature. It seemed like a force pulled my view
impossibly close to the thing. It wanted me to witness it. To describe
the creature is an entirely different, and more difficult task. 

A fox head, lined with the eyes of a human, goat, and cat. Surrounded by
several arms covered in blood, all moving, breaking and twitching
perpetually. It was floating about 5 feet off of the ground, staring at
me with all of its eyes. The shadow it cast spelled despair and loss
deeper than I had ever known. A recognition of every wrong I had done.
Every horrid anger I had felt. Every contempt. Every bitter sadness.
It’s hard to explain but I was stuck there for what seemed like weeks.
Stone cold, hands of the creature enveloping me in the vast touch of
entropy. Their grasp guided me through a forest of stone pillars. A
greyscale world with no sound or warmth. The wind was sour and musty.
Stale like there had been nothing forever. My feet never met the ground
once I walked past the horizon, and I fell. The sinking feeling of
falling never ended. Not for a moment. I can still feel it even now. 

I gasped awake the next day. The room around me was frigid and the
window was still open wide. The only evidence that what happened last
night was real, the only reason I’m writing this now, is because of what
was on my windowsill. Whatever that thing was, it had left two bloody
handprints all upon the stone.
