A while ago, I was in a critical financial situation, so I started
looking for anything that could make me some quick money. I went around
the street where I grew up, asking people if they needed their lawns
cut, rubbish removed or backyards cleaned. One of the first people I
asked was a neighbor I had known for years. I didn’t know him
personally, but I knew his face and had spoken to him several times. He
was in his fifties and had always seemed like a quiet, friendly man. He
immediately offered me €200 per day, which was far more than I expected.
The only problem was that his backyard was enormous, around a thousand
square meters, covered with dead leaves, broken furniture, bags of
rubbish and rusted metal. There were two houses on the property. He
lived in the main house, while the other was a ruined five-bedroom house
that had been abandoned for as long as I could remember. I didn’t own
any proper equipment, so my cousin lent me his minivan to remove the
rubbish. I offered him €100, but he refused. He had recently become a
father and was struggling financially too, so he understood how
important the job was to me.

On my third day, I brought lunch but forgot to bring water. I ate around
midday, then continued working under the sun for several more hours. By
that point, I was exhausted and extremely thirsty. I was standing about
fifty meters from the abandoned house when I decided to walk to the main
house and ask my neighbor for some water. At that exact moment, I looked
at one of the old house’s windows and saw him standing behind it. He was
staring directly at me. It was definitely his face, or at least I was
convinced it was. We looked at each other for around five seconds before
he slowly moved away from the window. I walked over and knocked on the
old front door, but nobody answered. I knocked twice more and called his
name. The house remained completely silent.

I walked to the main house, around two hundred meters away, and my
neighbor opened the door within seconds. I asked him for some water, and
instead of giving me a glass, he handed me a cold one-and-a-half-liter
bottle from his refrigerator. “A glass won’t be enough if you’re working
out there,” he said. “Keep the bottle.” Before returning to work, I
asked why he had been watching me from the abandoned house. His
expression changed immediately. He looked over my shoulder toward the
ruin and asked exactly which window I had seen him in. When I pointed it
out, he seemed genuinely uncomfortable. “It wasn’t me,” he said. “You’ve
probably been in the sun too long.” Then he lowered his voice. “But if
you see anyone in there again, don’t approach them and don’t talk to
them. Come straight here and tell me.” I asked if someone had been
breaking into the property. He didn’t answer. He just told me to drink
the water and stay near the van.

I convinced myself that the heat had made me imagine it, so I continued
working for another two hours. By then, the sun was disappearing and I
was ready to leave. As I loaded the last bag into the minivan, I looked
toward the abandoned house and saw someone standing in its doorway. From
that distance, it looked exactly like my neighbor again. The person
wasn’t waving or calling to me. He was simply standing there, with one
hand resting against the doorframe, watching. I remembered what my
neighbor had said, so I got into the van without acknowledging him. When
I reached the entrance, I discovered that the gate was closed. It had
never been closed while I was working there.

I drove back to the main house and knocked because I needed the gate
opened, and my neighbor still had to pay me. Nobody answered. I called
his phone and heard it ringing inside the house, but he didn’t pick up.
While I waited, I heard slow footsteps crossing the gravel somewhere
behind me. I didn’t turn around immediately. I stood there listening as
they came closer, one step at a time, then stopped. When I finally
looked, nobody was there. The abandoned house’s doorway was empty, but
its front door was now closed. I knocked harder and called my neighbor’s
name. From inside the main house, I heard movement, followed by what
sounded like the front door being locked from the other side. A few
seconds later, the gate at the end of the driveway began to open by
itself.

I ran back to the minivan and started driving toward it. The gate moved
painfully slowly, and for several seconds the gap wasn’t wide enough for
the van. As I waited, I checked the mirror. The figure was standing
behind the van, close enough that I could now see his pale face. He
looked like my neighbor, but his clothes were different, and he was
smiling in a way that made me feel as if he expected me to recognize
him. I locked the doors. He walked toward the driver’s side, but the
gate finally opened enough for me to squeeze through. As I drove away,
he didn’t follow. He just stood in the middle of the driveway. I looked
back once more and saw my actual neighbor watching from an upstairs
window of the main house. He immediately stepped behind the curtain when
he noticed me looking.

My payment arrived later that night, along with a message apologizing
for not answering the door. My neighbor claimed he had been in the
shower and had opened the gate remotely when he heard the van. I knew
that wasn’t true. His hair and clothes had been completely dry when I
saw him upstairs. Still, I needed the money and didn’t want trouble, so
I decided not to go back and tried to forget about it.

A few days later, my cousin called me. He had been cleaning the minivan
before installing his baby’s car seat and found a black plastic bag
pushed underneath the rear bench. Inside were eleven printed photographs
of me working in the backyard. Most had been taken from the windows of
the abandoned house. Some showed me carrying bags, eating my lunch or
drinking the bottle of water. One was a close-up of my face. The final
photograph showed me standing at the main house that evening, waiting
for somebody to answer the door. Whoever took it had been standing only
a few meters behind me. My cousin wanted to know how the bag had gotten
inside the van. I still had the keys the entire time, although one of
the rear doors sometimes failed to lock properly. Someone could have
opened it, but that didn’t explain why they had left the photographs
there. There was no note and no warning. It felt like whoever had taken
them wanted me to know I had been watched.

I became angry instead of frightened. My cousin was angry too, so we
drove back together the following afternoon. We wanted answers, and we
wanted them quickly. The gate was open, but my neighbor’s car wasn’t
there. We knocked on the main house for several minutes without
receiving an answer. My cousin then noticed movement inside the
abandoned house. Someone had stepped away from an upstairs window. We
walked over, shouting that we knew about the photographs. This time, the
front door wasn’t locked. It opened slightly when my cousin knocked. The
smell inside was terrible—a mixture of damp wood, cigarettes and spoiled
food. Despite the condition of the exterior, someone had clearly been
living there. There was a mattress on the floor, empty food cans,
bottles of water and stacks of newspapers. On one wall were dozens of
photographs. Most showed my neighbor entering and leaving his house, but
several were of people walking along the street. There were even
photographs of my cousin’s minivan taken days before I started the job.

Then the front door closed behind us. A man spoke from the hallway and
asked why we had come back. At first, I thought it was my neighbor, but
when he stepped into the light, I saw that he was thinner, older-looking
and had a large scar running from his ear to his neck. He resembled my
neighbor enough to be mistaken for him from a distance. He told us the
photographs weren’t meant for me. They were meant for his brother.
Before we could ask what that meant, my actual neighbor appeared outside
and began shouting for us to leave the house. The man inside immediately
became agitated. My cousin grabbed my arm, and we pushed past him before
running outside.

My neighbor locked the door behind us and finally admitted the man was
his older brother, who had serious mental health problems and had been
secretly staying in the ruined house. He claimed his brother was
harmless and that he hadn’t told anyone because he was afraid the
authorities would take him away. When I asked why he had hired me to
work so close to the building, he said he believed his brother had left
weeks earlier. He also insisted he had no idea how the photographs
entered the van.

We left and never returned. My neighbor transferred the money he owed
me, plus another €400, and asked us not to involve the police. We
reported everything anyway, but when officers searched the property, the
abandoned house was empty. The mattress, food cans and photographs had
all disappeared. My neighbor told them his brother didn’t exist and that
we must have confused him with someone else. They couldn’t do much
without evidence, especially because my cousin had left the photographs
inside the house when we ran.

About a month later, my cousin called me after putting his baby to
sleep. Someone had left another black plastic bag inside the minivan,
even though it had been locked in his garage all night. There was only
one photograph inside. It showed my cousin and me standing in the
abandoned house, looking at the wall of pictures. It had been taken from
directly behind us.

Written across the bottom in black marker were the words:

I TOLD YOU NOT TO TALK TO HIM.

At this point we decided to not talk about this anymore, although
whenever we hang-out for a beer or something, it is briefly mentioned.
I’m thinking of going back there, I know he is too, he fixed his lock on
the back door of the mini van and texted me today telling me ‘’We need
to talk’’.
