I walked quickly wherever I went. Move with a purpose, Sergeant Matthews
said. It was drilled into me day after day during basic. That’s how I
moved, confidently, shoulders back, chin up.

Tonight was different. 2 a.m., exiting downtown onto Bathurst Bridge
with not a soul in sight. I walked leisurely. The brisk October night
air was intoxicating. The moon hung proudly in the night sky, casting
shadows over the many rail cars that lay out at rest below. The
silhouette of the trees in Fort York Garden reminded me of the netting
over my tent, a safe shroud of webbed darkness. The many towering
high-rise condos ahead barely flickered with activity.

Who the hell could live like that, I thought idly.

As I walked across the bridge, I took a moment to soak in that peace and
leaned against the railing.

Smoke ’em if you got ’em. My ex-girlfriend’s mom howled in my mind. That
pudgy Spanish fella was kind enough to hand me one as I exited the pub
just 20 minutes before. Gave me the flipped one too, said something
about it being lucky. We shared one more before I made my way to this
railing on what I deemed to be a tranquil evening.

A weekend was not enough to see such a sprawling city full of culture
and vibrance. It was all I got before I had to head back to base. Who
cares about all that anyway? I wasn’t here for culinary excellence and
culture. I was here for Claire.

How sweet it was to meet someone like Claire in the short life we have.
Like a confluence of pure, gentle spring water that’s waited to form an
unstoppable rushing river all its life. That meeting, that confluence,
only lasted a weekend as well, and I was not about to let that slip by.
That river was going to form and rush around an eternity.

So there I was, smoking the lucky cigarette and making my own destiny.
Claire was just getting off her shift from the hospital and was only a
30-minute walk away from the pub. She should be home by now, but best to
let her freshen up quickly before our fiery reunion. Patience was never
my strong suit, but tonight wasn’t the night to risk speeding through a
perfect moment.

I took one last drag of my cigarette before lazily flicking it over the
edge onto the tracks below. Flashes of James Dean movies playing on my
grandfather’s shitty TV inspired a level of coolness, albeit fleeting,
as I fought the swarm of butterflies in my gut. Only two directions to
go.

I turned to start making my way to Claire’s building when I stopped dead
in my tracks.

Standing away from me at the end of the bridge was a woman. Her blonde
hair floating on the breeze shrouded her face, especially from this far
away. Her red heels glittered in the moonlight with a glimpse of her
finely toned yet delicate calf showing her sun-kissed skin, a remnant
from that perfect summer. Her favorite pendant catching the moon like a
glowing firefly.

It was her. It was Claire.

Funny how all those days of training and building mental toughness fade
all in an instant. I wanted to chuckle, but instead found my mouth agape
at the sight of the reason I flew all the way here.

Move with a purpose, I thought as I stepped one foot in front of the
other.

How sweet it will be to hold her again, just like that perfect August
night, to press my lips to hers and breathe in her soul as I cup my
hands around behind her ears, drinking in as much of her as I can.

I felt a vibration resonating within me.

Wait. I fumbled through my pocket to realize it was a call. Odd at this
hour. I’m right where I need to be.

I pulled the phone out to silence it when, flashing brightly in the dim
night, my phone showed a photo of a woman. Her smile lit up the center,
her green bucket hat playfully resting on her head, lips glistening from
freshly applied gloss, striking blue eyes that I got lost in every time
I saw them. And in large, bold lettering: Claire.

Confused, I looked up again. Claire stood right there facing away from
me, stoic and confident. But that was her. That had to be her. She knew
I was on my way.

I looked down again and figured I would play along.

“Well, hello, little angel. Couldn’t wait another 20 meters?”

“Jason, I have to tell you something.”

“Go ahead. I’ll be right behind you shortly.”

“You should go home. I mean. I can’t do this. I’m not ready.” Her voice
trembled.

“Wait, what are you—”

“I know how much we’ve become so close since we met. But I realized that
I’m not ready for this. I was just starting to be okay alone, but we’ve
been moving too fast. You’ve done nothing wrong. I just know that I
can’t meet you at the same emotional level of commitment right now, and
that’s not fair to you. I’m sorry. Really so sorry. I know you came all
this way. I just can’t.”

I stared at Claire’s back, and only just then noticed that her arms hung
at her sides, not moving.

“Claire, wait. Why not just face me and we can talk? I’m literally right
here. I respect if you don’t want me to come up, but can we not just
talk for a second?”

“I really can’t,” she sobbed. “I have to go. My bus is just pulling up.”

Silence.

There’s no bus around. Just me here and Claire at the end of the bridge.
What the fuck is happening? There’s no way after all the hours-long
talks we had, this can happen. And what bus? She’s right here.

A text notification.

I know you’re close by, but I’m going to stay with my parents. Again, I
can’t tell you enough how sorry I am.

Shuddering, I looked up to see Claire still standing there, unmoving.
Only two directions to go.

Despite my confusion, Claire still captivated me just as much as the
night I first saw her. The breeze cooled me to the core alongside the
unshakable feeling that something was unimaginably off. Her arms hadn’t
moved an inch, nor had her delicate frame. How the hell did she text me?
Is this some kind of sick joke? I couldn’t believe for one moment that
Claire would play such an off-colour prank. It wasn’t in her character.
And that’s what I loved about her. She wasn’t trying to steal the
spotlight. She shone like a perfect rock you find on a beach somewhere
after sifting through to find a few good skippers. She cared deeply for
those around her and was so in tune with where and who she was.
Remembering this and taking her in from only a hundred paces away, I
could only recall how I felt when I first saw her on that summer night
on the other side of the country.

I started walking toward her. Move with a purpose. It was all I could
think to keep my head on straight despite what had just happened.

Move with a purpose.

I could now make out the base of her slender neck and how much we both
loved having my hand there.

Move with a purpose.

Only 5 meters to go.

“Jason?” she called, still facing away from me, so I barely registered
it as a whisper.

I stopped again. That was her voice. Unmistakably, hers. What a dumb
gag. Not to mention the night chill had started to turn from refreshing
to uncomfortable. Time to savour the moment I flew all this way to have.

I reached out my hand and gently placed it on her shoulder, yet she
still did not turn. Maybe this is a girl thing. Wait until the last
moment to maximize the romance or something.

“Claire, what’s got into you tonight? You had my head spinning for a
second back there.”

No response.

Okay, I get it. Let’s get to the moment we’ve been dreaming about for
months now. 

I slowly turned Claire around and guided her in close to me. She smelled
like daisies and vanilla. One more stimulated sense nearly sent me over
the edge. As I turned her into me, her head turned to the ground. An
overwhelming urge to comfort her swept over me as I placed my hand
exactly where I dreamed to place it ever since I arrived. As my cool
hand tucked under and behind her ear and through her soft hair, I gently
turned her head upward to face me.

It was as if the world stopped turning, the city lights went out, and
the ground disappeared beneath us and all that remained was me and her
floating in the moonlight. Just inches from her perfect glistening lips,
I noticed all but too late that her pale blue eyes did not rise to meet
mine. In the place of the gateways to her soul lay nothing but tanned
skin.

Move with a purpose, I thought in a feeble attempt to steel my panic.
