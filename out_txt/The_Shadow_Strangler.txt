“I want y’all to give a warm welcome to our newest employee. She
recently just graduated from Texas A&M, though we won’t hold that
against her, hook ’em horns. Everybody, meet Penelope, or as we’re going
to call her around here, Penny Callahan.” That is how my new boss
Douglas Crane introduced me to the team. I couldn’t help but roll my
eyes when he threw up the horns, but I didn’t expect anything less from
a T-sip.

Like he said, I just recently graduated from Texas A&M. I come from a
long line of Aggies. My father was the last one to graduate from there
and though I kept the family tradition of attending college, career-wise
we went in different directions. My dad Roy recently retired from the
police force as a detective. I went to college for business and
eventually decided that banking is where I needed to be. I worked at the
bank for only a few days when I got a call that nobody wants to receive
in the middle of the night. My father had passed away, which was crazy
because I had just spoken to him two nights prior while we were having a
celebratory dinner, congratulating me on my new career.

My dad and I were always close because I was the only child. Growing up
he always took his work home with him. For breakfast, I would have case
files with my Cap’n Crunch. He was there physically, but mentally his
mind was at work. However, he knew how to balance it just right. He
would pick his battles. He would go to my volleyball games, but never
took me to practice. He was at my high school graduation, but couldn’t
make my college graduation. Dad told me that since he paid for my
tuition that he didn’t have to attend, which I thought was fair enough.
He was a bit strict, but he was always fair.

The even crazier part is how they found him — inside an abandoned
chicken restaurant called Klucky’s where unspeakable horrors occurred.
They officially deemed him the notorious serial killer in the local
area. My father was named The Shadow Strangler. My whole world was
shattered in an instant.

When I received the call to identify his body at the morgue, I wanted so
badly to tell them it wasn’t him. But when they lifted that sheet, I
could tell it was him just by the way that his hair looked. I honestly
couldn’t believe it. My dad was gone, and I was officially all alone.

Since he was a big piece of the police force, they didn’t want to go
public just yet about him being the Shadow Strangler. They wanted to
conduct an investigation before they went public to the press. However,
at the crime scene, they did find his fingerprints all over murder
weapons that were identified as such from previous crime scenes. They
also found a diary that was their smoking gun when it came to a
confession. I asked for access to this diary, but was denied since they
found the latest victim and him in the same building. They had him dead
to rights I was told.

I made arrangements at the funeral home. He had requested to be buried
next to my mother as well as my grandparents. Nobody knew about the
accusations, and I sure as hell wasn’t going to tell them. So he had a
proper funeral with pretty much the entire police department there, as
well as my boss Douglas, who was there the entire time to support me.

“Penny, I’m so sorry about your father. Take all the time you need, the
bank will still be there waiting for you when you return.” Douglas
embraced me with a hug as we were staring at the open casket in the
funeral parlor. It was warm and genuine, and he was the closest
connection I’ve ever had to a person since my dad.

After he was buried next to Mom, Pepaw and Granny, I went home and sat
on my couch for three days straight. I didn’t eat, I didn’t talk to
anybody, and I didn’t even turn the lights on. I was devastated that my
father was linked to being such a monster. A part of me didn’t want to
believe it, but the evidence was telling me it was true. He always told
me to trust the evidence. That’s when I received a text from the coroner
Dr. Sandra Okafor. She asked to meet me for coffee because she had
something she wanted to discuss.

We met at a local coffee shop on Main Street. She was already there
waiting for me, dressed very professionally with her hair up in a bun,
dark-rimmed glasses, a tasteful button-up top, and an ankle-length black
skirt with slingback black heels. She immediately stood up when she
noticed me come through the doors and walked over to greet me. She gave
me a handshake and directed me towards the table.

“Penelope, thank you so much for meeting me on such short notice. I know
that emotions are still raw, but there’s something that I had to share
with you.” As soon as she said that, a barista came by and asked me for
my drink order. She was absolutely gorgeous, but I couldn’t focus on her
right now. “It’s really no problem, Dr. Okafor, I’ve just been sitting
in darkness for three days, not doing much. What is it that you needed
to share with me?” I said to her with my eyes burning and my face puffy.

“I’m sorry to hear that, but I will keep this brief. There is a bit of
an inconsistency with the report. Everything in it points to your father
being the Shadow Strangler, however, I’m beginning to question that.”
She took a sip of her coffee before she continued. “It’s my
understanding that your father was in fact right-handed, correct?” I
nodded my head up and down concurring. “That’s what I thought. However,
the wound patterns on the victims indicate that the Shadow Strangler was
in fact left-handed. With such precision and execution, for a lack of a
better term, somebody right-handed couldn’t have possibly done it. At
least that’s my theory.”

The gorgeous barista brought me my latte as she smiled and winked at me
before walking away. I had to shake my head and once again focus on
Dr. Okafor. If what she’s telling me is true, this could be the evidence
I needed to prove my father’s innocence. It was a lead I hoped would
finally give me a definitive answer. I thanked Dr. Okafor for her time
as I took my coffee and headed back to my house.

Even though I drank my latte like it was water, I eventually passed out
from exhaustion from not sleeping for a few days. That’s when I had a
weird dream. I was inside Klucky’s, standing over a dead body. I looked
over at the swinging kitchen door and out came my father. “Penny, this
isn’t what it seems. Don’t trust everyone. He’s still out there.” He was
clenching a knife in his right hand that was dripping blood in massive
puddles on the floor — a recreation of the scene he’d been framed for.
The dream was so surreal that I woke up in a panic. It was the middle of
the night and I was starving. I looked in my pantry and of course, there
was nothing to eat, so I decided that instead of ordering from DoorDash
that I was going to go to Waffle House for sustenance.

I pulled up to the yellow lit building and sat myself in a corner booth.
After the waiter took my order, which was an All-Star Special and
coffee, I looked over and noticed Detective Briggs, Dad’s old partner.
He was rugged and looked like he could beat you to a pulp just by
looking at you, but every time he saw me, he instantly went into teddy
bear mode. He set down his fork that had a punctured waffle on it and
swiftly walked over to me and sat in the opposite booth.

“Penny, it’s so good to see you, sweetheart. I haven’t seen you since
the funeral, how are you holding up?” he said as he grabbed my hands. I
had to fight back tears and wanted to tell him that my life had been a
living nightmare and that I dreamt about my dad a couple of hours prior.
However, all I could mutter was I was doing all right, and that I was
going back to work in a couple of days. He patted my hands and
apologized for the hundredth time since I’ve seen him this week. He then
leaned in, as if to tell me a secret. “You know, I think this whole
investigation is full of shit,” he said as he looked around to see if
anybody else was listening. “Your dad was an outstanding member of
society, and one of the good cops to boot. There’s no way in hell that
he is the Shadow Strangler. Even on your dad’s worst days, he never so
much as raised his voice at a suspect.”

I looked at Walt and echoed his statement. That’s when it occurred to me
that in my dream, Dad said don’t trust everyone. I couldn’t help but
have a sneaking suspicion that Detective Briggs was the Shadow
Strangler. I couldn’t be certain, but I felt a little uneasy with the
way he was looking at me. A look he had never given me before. “You know
what? I just remembered I have to pay my water bill!” I said to Walt as
I dropped a fifty-dollar bill on the table and left in a hurry. I felt
like an idiot as I sped off out of the parking lot. As I looked through
the window, Walt was still sitting there dumbfounded.

With an empty stomach, I got back home and tried to get some sleep. I
didn’t get much, but when I did dream, I dreamt of Dad again. The same
building, the same situation. Dad was still holding the knife dripping
blood over a dead body. Except he said something different to me this
time. “He’s still out there killing. Look at his left hand.” As soon as
he said that, a shadowy figure emerged behind him with a rope, and right
as he was about to strangle my dad, I woke up. Armed with Dad’s
direction, I hoped I could finally catch this psychopath.

The next morning, I went to the old precinct to see Walt. Luckily, he
was in his office and after checking in and grabbing a visitor’s badge,
I met with him face-to-face. He had his chair facing away from me. When
he turned around and put his hands on his desk, I noticed his left hand.
Walt had a huge burn on the back of his hand. I tried to play it off by
asking him what happened. He looked down at it. “Oh this? After you left
Waffle House to pay your water bill, the waitress came by to refill my
coffee and let’s just say that she missed the mug,” he said as he waved
his hand in the air. Once again I got an uneasy feeling as I apologized
for my behavior the night before and told him that I had some errands I
needed to run. I was a little more subtle this time.

I wasn’t lying this time. I had full intent to get some things done
before I returned to work the next day. This included putting together a
thank you basket for my boss. So I went to Target and stocked up on some
of his favorites. While I was there, I decided to go ahead and buy a
couple of security cameras, just to be safe. I felt a little paranoid,
but better safe than sorry.

That night, I once again had a dream about my dad. The same scenario
played out, except I got to see my dad get strangled to death. As his
limp body hit the floor, the Shadow Strangler fled the scene. After a
few seconds, Dad’s eyes opened. “Penny! He’s going to strike again
tonight. Your gut may tell you one thing, but your heart is what you
need to follow.” His body went limp, and my dad passed away again. I
woke up in a cold sweat and couldn’t breathe. It took me a few minutes
to calm myself down, but I ended up just sitting there for a few hours
before it was time for me to go to work.

It felt weird going back to work after being gone for so long, but the
show must go on, even when a big role is vacant. I had to learn how to
put makeup on again, and if I didn’t pay attention, I would’ve walked
out with one black heel and one navy heel. Luckily I caught myself
before I left. I grabbed my purse and the thank you basket and drove to
work. I decided to get there a little bit early so that I could give the
basket to my boss. I looked in the parking lot and his Land Rover was
already there.

I hopped in the elevator and pushed the button for the 12th floor, which
was his entire office. The doors opened up, and I could instantly see
nothing but burnt orange colors with a nasty Longhorn everywhere. The
Aggie in me shuddered. I stood there with the basket in hand, but no
sign of him anywhere. That’s when he popped out of his private bathroom.
We made eye contact as he began to speak. “Penny! I’m so glad to see
you. We’ve missed you badly around here,” he said as he walked up to me
and gave me a hug. He looked down and noticed the gift basket as a grin
spread across his face.

“Is that for me?” he asked with every single tooth present, and he
pointed down towards the Milky Way midnight chocolate bar. However, when
he pointed with his left hand, right below his thumb, I noticed a bite
mark as well as bruised knuckles and a slash on the back of his hand. I
looked down and gasped. We made eye contact again as he looked at me and
chuckled. “The wife and I got a little too rough last night,” he said as
he winked at me. I responded with an awkward chuckle back. I handed him
the basket and headed towards the door. As he set it down on his desk
and settled in to do paperwork, he called my name. As I turned around, I
noticed he was writing with his left hand. He thanked me for the basket
and once again told me he was glad I was back. I just smiled and rushed
out the door.

The rest of my workday was a crawl. The minutes felt like hours, and my
heart rate did not go down at all. Was my boss the one my dad was
telling me was the real killer? How did I not know he was left-handed?
Did I blame an innocent man for being the killer? It’s the absolute
worst when you’re asking yourself all the questions and getting no
answers. I had to think of something fast. If he was going to strike
again tonight, I couldn’t let another person die. I was going to follow
my boss and see what he was up to. I left a little early and turned on
the waterworks to sell it. After that, I scoped out the parking lot and
waited for my boss to leave.

Right around 5:30, he walked to his Land Rover, and I began to follow
him inconspicuously. He didn’t go home; he went straight to a local bar
and grill. Through the window, I could see him ordering a double shot of
whiskey and a T-bone steak. The entire time, I could see him scoping out
a table full of young women. While he was chewing a bite of his steak, I
saw him slip off his wedding ring. He put it in his pocket, swallowed
his steak, and approached a girl who couldn’t have been older than 20
years old.

From my perspective, it looked like he had asked her what she was
drinking and bought her a round for her and her friends. One round
turned into two, then three, and then the waiter brought over some
mozzarella sticks. As the evening progressed, they got more and more
friendly with each other. They eventually began making out at the table.
I read the body language and determined that they were going back to her
place. All of her friends were gone at this point, and it was just the
two of them. So he walked her to his Land Rover, opened the door for
her, and they began to drive off.

Right as I was about to start my car, I heard a tap at the window that
scared the hell out of me. It was Walt. He hopped in the passenger seat
and asked me what I was doing. I began to tell him that it sounded
crazy, but my dad had come to me in a dream and told me about the Shadow
Strangler. I told him that my boss was the number one suspect. As I was
about to go on, he put his hand up as if to stop me and told me that he
had the same dream about Dad. With no time to lose, he told me to drive
and follow my boss.

As we tailed him, we arrived at an apartment complex. The front doors
were shut for maintenance; a sign on the door said the back alley
entrance was the only way in. They flirtatiously read the sign and
strolled to the back alley. After we lost visual, I began to speak. Walt
cut me off and told me to be quiet as he rolled down the window. We both
heard screaming. We sprung from the car and ran towards the alley.

When we got to the back, he was on top of her and strangling the poor
girl. She was kicking and flailing like a fish out of water. I told Walt
to stay put as he drew his gun. I ran out of our hiding spot and got his
attention. “Douglas!” I yelled at him. He stopped strangling the woman
and got up to face me. “Well, well, a Penny saved is a Penny earned,” he
said, grinning ear-to-ear as he pulled a switchblade out of his jacket
pocket. He began to approach me. “I’m sorry you had to see me like this.
I was going to get you next, but this worked out perfectly. Now I can
get two for one.”

He got about 10 feet in front of me before Walt jumped out of his hiding
spot and pointed his gun at him. “Freeze, Douglas, you motherfucker!” He
did as he was told for a few seconds before he flashed another smile at
me. He then bull-rushed Walt as bullets plunged into Douglas’s chest. He
spat up some blood and fell limp on the ground. Astonished and relieved
at the same time, I embraced Walt with a hug before he ran over to check
on the girl. She was alive, but unconscious. He called 911 on his phone
as we waited for the paramedics.

After a while of taking questions from officers, Walt and I were both
allowed to go home. I know it’s a little morbid, but I slept like a rock
that night. As I began dreaming, I was no longer inside an abandoned
Klucky’s. I was at my father’s headstone. As I was looking at it, I felt
a hand on my shoulder. I looked over and it was him. He had a proud look
on his face as he smiled at me. “Baby girl, you did amazing!” As we
began to hug I also began to cry. “Why are you crying?” he asked me with
a concerned look on his face. “Because I’m never going to see you
again,” I said, as I hung my head low. He took his index finger and
raised my chin. “Hey, I’m not going anywhere. Thanks to you, I’ve been
granted a new ability. I am going to see you every single night for the
rest of your life. It is my job from this day forward to make your
dreams come true. I’ve been given a gift, and I’m going to use it on
you.”

I couldn’t help but begin to cry once more, except now they were tears
of joy. I get to see my father every single night, and to me that is the
sweetest dream that I could ever have. I’m not sure what he meant by
making all of my dreams come true, but I could think of a few things for
starters.
