In the summer of 2024, I travelled to Japan on my own. Before setting
off, I dyed the left side of my fringe a white-gold colour (I’m Chinese,
and my natural hair colour is black). After travelling from Tokyo to
Osaka, I felt a persistent sense of unease; to describe it in detail, I
felt very cold, even though it was July and temperatures in both Osaka
and Tokyo were above 35 degrees Celsius. The very next day, I took the
train to Kyoto. After getting off the train at around 2 o’clock in the
afternoon, I spotted an elderly fortune-teller near a local pagoda (I
can’t recall the exact name, but it was next to a tourist attraction).

Let me explain: in East Asian culture, some people can predict one’s
future destiny by observing the lines on one’s palms and one’s facial
features. I found this quite intriguing, so I asked the old man to read
my fortune.

He asked for my date and time of birth, then said some rather generic
things, such as ‘You’ll face difficulties when you’re 35’. Just as I was
beginning to think he was simply spouting nonsense to swindle money, he
uttered a crucial sentence: ‘You must go home immediately.’

I was completely baffled, but after leaving, I carried on with my
sightseeing as normal. However, at six o’clock in the evening, just as
I’d finished my meal and was about to go for a stroll along the banks of
the Kamogawa River, I witnessed a scene I shall never forget for the
rest of my life. A man dressed exactly as I was—with black hair and
platinum-blonde bangs on the left side—suddenly appeared on the opposite
bank of the Kamogawa River, standing perfectly still, making no other
movements, with his hands hanging at his sides.

That day I was wearing a white shirt and grey trousers—which might be
quite common—but the fact that the left side of my fringe was dyed
platinum blonde could hardly be described as a coincidence. I can
honestly say that I have never seen an Asian person, either in real life
or online, who has dyed the left side of their fringe platinum blonde,
just like me.

I was terrified at the time. Taxis are very expensive in Japan, but I
immediately hailed one to take me back to the train station. I wasn’t
wearing my glasses at the time, and as the man was so far away, I
couldn’t make out his face—nor did I dare to try. All I knew was that I
had to get away from there.

After returning to Osaka, I felt a little more composed, though I was
still somewhat frightened. I messaged a friend of mine in Japan about
the incident, and he said he wasn’t sure either—there were no relevant
ghost stories or legends. But in China, there’s a legend that when a
person is about to die, they will see their double—that is, a second
version of themselves appearing in the world.

I began to reassure myself: surely it was just a coincidence? Perhaps
that man shared my sense of style and had dyed one side of his fringe
platinum blonde? I didn’t want to overthink it, so at 9 o’clock that
evening, I decided to head out to find an izakaya for a late-night
snack.

Just as I stepped out of the hotel entrance and was checking my map,
that person appeared again. This time, he was in a window of the
building diagonally opposite the hotel entrance, on about the third
floor. It wasn’t that I was observing particularly closely; it was
simply that the half-platinum fringe was far too striking and
distinctive, especially in East Asia. The person was standing right by
the window, looking down; he was even wearing a white shirt—exactly the
same as the man from that afternoon.

I froze completely. I practically ran back to the hotel reception, told
them I was being followed, and asked them to call a taxi to the back
entrance. I then insisted that a member of staff accompany me to collect
my luggage from my room. They were quite puzzled as to why I was acting
this way, but I didn’t want to explain too much, given the language
barrier. I still had one more night’s stay at the hotel, but I checked
out straight away. As soon as the taxi arrived, I got in straight away
and told the driver I wanted to go to the nearest station with a direct
service to the airport.

But I was absolutely terrified on the way, so I asked the driver to take
me to the nearest police station. Once there, I showed them my passport
and told them I was being followed – that this person had been tailing
me from Kyoto to Osaka, and I felt my personal safety was at risk. The
police didn’t believe me; they simply asked what I wanted them to do. I
said, ‘Just take me to the airport.’ They eventually agreed. In the
police car, I immediately booked a flight back to China leaving in three
hours’ time; my original ticket was non-refundable. It wasn’t until I
boarded the plane that I began to feel a little more at ease.
