The first time I noticed something was wrong was the clock that hangs on
the wall behind my desk, but I don’t think that’s where it started. No
I’m almost certain it started with my commute.

It was innocent enough. A bizarre and unsettling sensation to be sure,

obviously made the trip. Put my turn signal on, stopped at red lights,
braked round the corners. But any conscious recollection or memory of it
was gone. Like I was on auto-pilot. Like I was a zombie.

Like I said, innocent enough. Weird, but not unheard of, especially on
long familiar drives. But that’s where it began. I’m sure of it.

I work in an office and it’s not terribly important what precisely I do.
Suffice to say it’s technical, involves a lot of spreadsheets and
numbers and files and pointless meetings, and could harshly be described

turned the needle over from boring tedium into crushing drudgery was
Steven. My boss.

Steven

through the majority of the other junior associates and now had taken a
lateral transfer with plans to aim for C-division senior. I was a
climber. But Steven saw me, and he hated me immediately.

Steven saw me. He knew I was a climber aiming higher and he had no
intention of allowing that to happen. He knew, I knew, and he knew I
knew. I could see his hate coming from a mile away but I had dealt with
office sharks before. I knew what his plan was. I had to be perfect. Any
mistake, any slip-up, any breath of defiance or frustration and my
ladder would be cut short under his perfectly shined oxfords.

This is why I had taken with saccharine gusto to every task Steven
dumped on me and never outwardly complained in the slightest. No I had
to keep my mounting loathing for him inside. So I’d smile. I’d turn
about in my chair. I’d lean back facing the window with a random file

played with myself. Not very fun, but it helped calm my seething

barely remembered. And so the pain of her daddy missing her birthday,

that’s normal anymore, or if I’ve too much hate now to feel anything
