The summer after my eighth grade year, my parents hired a nanny. More
for my brother, who had just finished the third grade and tended to get
himself into trouble if left unsupervised.

Ashley, or Ash, as she invited me to call her, was an instructor at my
brother’s karate dojo, and had recently graduated from the local
university. She was looking mostly to make some money before starting a
teaching job in the fall.

_______

Ash wore the same outfit to both the interview and on her first day
watching us: a blue and white striped dress and a denim jacket. I
remember thinking she was very dressed up and worried that her wearing a
dress meant she was going to be a strict hardass all summer. I was
almost relieved the second day when she showed up in jeans and a t
shirt.

Instead, she was very chill. Mostly left us to our own devices all day.

This lasted for about a month.

_______

The Sunday night after July 4th, our parents sat my brother and I down.
Their faces were somber.

They told us Ash’s family had experienced a tragedy over the weekend.
She would be away for the foreseeable future. All she told them was that
something had happened and she didn’t know if she would be back, at all.
My mother, ever the good Catholic, said it might not be a bad idea to
even say a few prayers for her.

_______

July was a revolving door of family friends and relatives that mostly
just ferried us to and from swim practice.

Ash had let us do our own thing, but being home truly alone without
adults felt like a whole new ballgame.

Then they sat us down again, at the end of the month, and told us she
was coming back for the last few weeks of summer. She wanted to get back
to some normalcy after a rough month, if my parents would have her. They
gladly obliged.

_______

And so, on the first Monday morning of August, Ash came back. Same
striped dress. Same denim jacket.

At the time, I thought she was just still grieving whoever she had lost.
Just needed some space, which after a month of the house to myself I was
more than happy to give her.

Looking back, something was off about her. Very, very off.

_______

The initial incident was around noon.

I was making a sandwich in the kitchen when she called to me in a voice
that sounded as though she was freaking out, but trying to sound
controlled.

“NICK PLEASE CHANGE THE CHANNEL.”

“Sure thing, just let me p”

“NO. DO IT NOW PLEASE.” She snapped. But in a tone that suggested she
didn’t know what snapping actually sounded like.

I set the stuff down and walked over to the TV, where she sat, the
bottom of her dress pulled under her feet like she was trying to use it
as a makeshift blanket.

It seemed a little silly that she would be panicking this much over the
show on the TV, one of those paranormal travel shows that looked to be
focusing this week on the phenomenon of mimics in the Appalachians.

I grabbed the remote and switched it off.

“Thank you.” She said, not looking at me, but instead at the TV as
though she had just seen a ghost.

“Uh huh.”

I took my lunch downstairs. The interaction had unnerved me, and I
didn’t really want to be near her.
….

I was laying in bed that night, trying and failing to fall asleep.
Something about that interaction was still weirding me out.

I got out my iPad. It didn’t take long to find a news article about it.

Ash and her fiance, Nate, had been staying with her family in a secluded
rental cabin somewhere in Tennessee. On the night of July 2nd, her mom
and grandmother had left to go for a short walk on a nearby trail. She
and Nate had been sitting in some chairs on the back deck with glasses
of wine when her mother had called to her from the woods bordering the
property, claiming to have fallen and hurt herself.

They had set their glasses down and made their way into the woods
without so much as a second thought, only to quickly become lost.

The last thing Ash remembered before waking up in the ICU two days later
was one last glimpse of the cabin as it disappeared behind some trees.

There was still no sign of Nate.

Her mother and grandmother had never been missing to begin with. Their
walk had only been 20 minutes, never strayed from the path, and they had
become concerned when they had returned to an empty deck, save for 2
half full wine glasses.

There was a picture of the couple included in the article from when they
had gotten engaged. I couldn’t help but notice something.

Ash had looked identical that day to the picture. Same hairstyle. Same
clothes. Even the same sandals.

_______

And she showed up the next morning looking, again, the exact same. Like
she had gone home, went to bed fully dressed, and just gotten up and
immediately driven to our house.

And yet everything looked pristine. Like she had 7 versions of the same
outfit all hung up neatly in her closet and just washed six of them
every Sunday.

I thought of the story, of the voice of her mother calling to her from
the woods, and found myself questioning whether it was even her that was
currently sitting on our couch, again tucking her feet into her dress.
……

Something strange happened again that afternoon.

I went upstairs, again, to make lunch, and again found her sitting on
the couch. Laptop open. You could have told me she hadn’t moved since
she sat down initially and I’d have believed you.

It’s what she was looking at that stuck out to me.

It was an Instagram post. The picture was the same one from the news
article, of her and Nate right after he proposed. She seemed to be about
to post something about him, but hadn’t actually written anything. The
cursor just blinked in, and out. Over and over.

And she just sat there and stared at it. There was no expression of
grief on her face. Not that I was the authority on such things. But she
seemed be…analyzing it. The face of Ash in the picture. Her hair. The
way she was posed.

It almost felt like she was trying to study the picture, the way an
actress hired for a biopic might study the person she’s playing.

I noticed something else that stuck out to me, and maybe she was just
trying to move on - she didn’t have a ring on.

_______

I had orientation the next day. So, while Ash still came over, my mom
and I spent the bulk of the day at the high school getting me ready.

We had barely pulled out of the driveway when my mom asked me how things
were going with her.

“A little weird, but fine. I guess.”

“Weird. How so?”

“For starters, she’s worn the same thing three days in a row.”

I could tell by the look she gave me that I had no bearing to speak on
that, having worn the same pair of shorts for the last three days.

“That… and she’s been acting kind of weird.”

“How so?”

I told her about the tv show and the thing with the laptop, making sure
to frame it more as I’d already been sitting there as opposed to looking
over her shoulder.

She looked at me. Asked me how much I knew already. I told her about the
article I had found.

“My point, Nick, is that people grieve differently. The dress could be
her thing to relive one of the happiest moments of her life. And you
don’t know what was going through her mind when she was on her laptop.
She may have just felt numb at that point.

And they were high school sweethearts. It’s hard to put eight years into
words, especially when you’re clinging to hope that your partner might
still be alive.”

We both knew she was grasping at straws with that last sentence. There
had been a funeral a few weeks after everything went down. A closed
casket, because there was no body.

“Just… let her grieve. It’s…what, 2 more weeks of playing video games in
the basement while she sits upstairs? Is that so hard?”

She was right. It wasn’t.

As if to underscore what my mom had just said, there was a slideshow at
the beginning of orientation. A cheesy thing, all about high school and
making memories. But one of the pictures made my mom gasp and wipe away
a few tears.

It was of a couple at prom. Clearly a couple, as opposed to just
friends. They were slow dancing. Looking at each other as though they
were the only ones in the room. They looked much younger, but it wasn’t
hard to figure out it was Ash and Nate.

_______

Grieving.. didn’t explain what happened the next day.

My brother and I were wrestling in the basement. As often happened with
a stocky, overly confident kid and a gangly, awkward teenager with the
build of a wacky waving inflatable tube man, things quickly got heated.

He’d come after me with a metal baseball bat. I’d managed to overpower
him and shove him into the couch, where I’d held him there.

Ash must have heard the commotion, because she came racing down. But she
didn’t intervene. She just… stood there, watching us.

Still looking the exact same as she had the previous three days. Same
dress. Same jacket. Same hairstyle. Her dress covered her feet, but had
she picked it up, I’m sure she’d have had the same sandals as well.

And she stood there, watching us no different than you’d watch the lion
enclosure at the zoo.

And then I realized my brother could barely breathe the way I had him
pinned to the couch. I let him go, and she promptly turned around and
walked back upstairs. Like the show was over, and so it was time to go.
No stern talking to or yelling. Just.. gone.

She did it so nonchalantly that I was almost surprised when my dad got
home that afternoon and she told him what happened.

He sat us down after she left. Told us the bat incident was too far, and
we were both grounded the rest of the week. This was a Thursday, so it’s
not like we were to just sit there doing nothing for a couple days, but
still.

We tried to tell him about how she’d sat there, studying us like rats in
a science experiment, but he wouldn’t have it. He told Mom when she got
home, and that was that.

_______

Perhaps it was just as well that we were grounded, because on Friday,
things got really weird.

We were sitting there on the couch across from her. It was beginning to
feel like Déjà vu. She’d walk in, sit on the couch, pull her dress up,
and then not move unless it was absolutely necessary.

The TV stayed on the travel channel. I knew it had been on other shows,
even remembered watching baseball highlights that morning, but the
memories were hazy at best. It felt like we were trapped in some sort of
cycle.

I was reading a book that I’d meant to read for a while. Something funny
happened, and I chuckled. Maybe 30 seconds later, she chuckled as well.
Same beat to it. But no humor at all. I caught the back end of it and it
was almost like she had forced herself to smile and hack it out, like a
cough.

“What’s so funny?”

“Oh. Nothing.”

About ten minutes later, I laughed again, this time at some stupid bar
story in my book. It was a good, long wheeze, the kind that takes you
completely off guard.

It was a minute this time.

Then, I watched her forcefully smile and make the exact same noise, for
the exact same amount of time. I couldn’t quite tell if she was by now
mocking me or something.

That bar story finished off in a way that left me sitting, my face in my
hands, shaking in silent laughter. And she followed right on cue a
minute later, a forced fit of belly laughter that might have actually
been funny had the TV not been discussing eight people getting murdered
with an axe in Kansas a century ago.

I got up, excused myself, and went to the bathroom. I could tell she
didn’t have an answer for that.
….

That fifth day wound up being her last with us, in part because of some
very fortunate timing with my dad, who walked in from work just in time
to hear himself calling my name.

I came running down the stairs.

“Hey, Dad. What’s up?”

“… Nothing. That wasn’t me.”

I looked at him, confused. Then I looked into the kitchen, where the
voice had come from.

Ash was hurriedly packing her things. Her face was beet red.

“I’m sorry. I’ve been….getting into doing impressions lately. I thought
I was alone.”

My dad smiled politely. “No worries at all. You were spot-on!”

I forced a grin. “Very!”

She grinned and hurried out.
…

My parents sat us down once again that night.

Ash, they said, had some new teacher trainings she had forgotten about.
She asked my parents to tell us she would miss us.

We would be on our own for the rest of the summer during the day, which
was more than fine with us.

_______

I have no idea what would have happened the remainder of the summer, or
what her end goal was with us. If that was truly her.

Life went on, and eventually we forgot about her completely. She came up
on occasion while reminiscing, but nothing aside from that.

Then I ran into her the fall after I graduated from college.

I was at a football game at my alma mater. Our annual rivalry game. I
was talking with a friend when someone called my name.

“NICK!?”

I turned around.

Ash was walking toward me, along with a broad, stocky dude. Her husband,
it turned out. She moved for a fresh start the following year and met
him at a bar one night. He was a former football player at our alma
mater.

She was about six months pregnant, and absolutely glowing.

I couldn’t help but notice as well that she was wearing the same outfit
she had worn that summer. The same dress. The same denim jacket. The
same sandals. And her hair was exactly the same.

That normally wouldn’t have stuck out to me. It was a preppy school, and
people frequently dressed up for tailgates.

Except for one thing.

It was about 30 degrees and lightly snowing.
