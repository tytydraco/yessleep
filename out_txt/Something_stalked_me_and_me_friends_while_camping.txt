This happened three years ago, when I was 15. I’m 18F now, and even
though I’ve tried to convince myself there was a perfectly reasonable
explanation for what happened that summer.

In 2023, my friends, our parents, and I went on our annual summer
camping trip. We’d been going to the same campground since we were
babies, so it was a place filled with some of my favourite childhood
memories. We knew the trails, the creek, the best places to ride our
bikes, and which bathrooms were worth avoiding after dark. It was
familiar enough that none of us ever really thought of it as somewhere
we could be in danger.

As we’d gotten older, our parents had started giving us more freedom. We
could ride our bikes around the campground without anyone following us,
spend hours down at the creek, and wander through the surrounding woods
as long as we were back before dark. At the time, it felt like the best
thing in the world. We were finally old enough to do our own thing, and
we took full advantage of it.

The campground was in the Pacific Northwest, in an area where people
regularly told stories about strange things they’d seen in the woods.
Bigfoot sightings were practically part of the local culture. We’d heard
all the usual stories around campfires, but they were just that:
stories. We’d scare each other with them occasionally, but none of us
genuinely believed there was anything out there.

That year, our parents let us have our own campsite a couple of spots
away from theirs. We got to choose where to pitch our tents, organise
our own stuff, and stay up later than usual. Most nights, we’d sneak
snacks from the parents’ coolers, sit around playing cards, and talk
about whatever random teenage drama was happening at the time.

Looking back, I think the fact that we’d been going there for so long
made us a little too comfortable. We knew the area well enough to
navigate it without thinking, and we’d stopped paying attention to the
things that probably should have made us cautious.
The day everything happened, we’d spent most of the afternoon down at
the creek. By the time we started heading back, the sun was dropping
behind the trees, and the woods had that strange, dim lighting they get
just before dark. We switched on our headlamps and started along the
familiar trail. We weren’t worried. We’d walked that trail dozens of
times, and we knew we were only a few minutes from the campground.

What I remember most is how ordinary everything felt at first. We were
talking, laughing, and arguing about something completely unimportant.
There was nothing in the air that made us feel like we should turn
around. No obvious warning, no sudden feeling that we were being
watched.

Just the sound of our shoes on the dirt, the occasional snap of a twig
beneath someone’s foot, and the trees stretching out on either side of
us.
Then, somewhere behind us, we heard something moving through the woods.

At first, we barely acknowledged it. Animals were common out there, and
we’d heard plenty of rustling in the undergrowth over the years. But the
sound continued alongside us, keeping roughly the same pace as we
walked.

We stood there for a moment, listening.
Nobody said anything about Bigfoot. Nobody even made a joke.
Then one of my friends quietly asked, “Did you guys hear that?”

When we got back from the creek, we made dinner and sat around the fire
eating s’mores and playing cards. It was getting dark, and we could hear
the occasional cracking sound coming from the woods, but we just assumed
it was animals.

We ended up talking about Bigfoot for a bit, joking about how funny it
would be if we actually saw something out there. Eventually, the fire
started dying down, and we decided to head to bed. The boys said they
were going to the long-drop toilets first

There were two sets of bathrooms at the campground, both long-drop
toilets. The closest ones were about a three-minute walk from our
campsite.
That night, the boys decided to head down to use them while we stayed
behind in our tent. Our parents were at their own campsite a couple of
spots away, packing things up and getting ready for bed. We could
occasionally hear them talking, along with the crackling of the fire and
the usual nighttime sounds of the campground.

We’d only been in the tent for a few minutes when we heard something
outside.
At first, it sounded like footsteps moving through the dirt near our
campfire. We assumed it was one of the boys coming back for something
they’d forgotten, so we didn’t think much of it.

Then a shadow fell across the side of our tent.
And I don’t mean the quick shadow of someone walking past a light. It
was large. Large enough that all of us noticed it at the same time.

The fire was still burning outside, casting light against the tent
walls, so we could make out the shape of something moving between the
flames and the fabric. Whatever it was, it seemed much taller than any
of us.
We went completely silent.
I remember sitting in my sleeping bag, staring at the tent wall and
trying to make sense of what I was seeing.
The shadow stopped moving.

For what felt like forever, it just stood there.
I checked the time afterwards, and while I can’t say for certain how
long we sat there, it felt like at least thirty seconds. Long enough for
me to start wondering why whoever was outside hadn’t said anything. If
it was one of the boys messing with us, why weren’t they laughing? Why
weren’t they saying anything?

We could hear the fire popping outside, but there was no voice, no
movement from our parents’ campsite, and no indication that anyone else
had noticed something was wrong.
Then we heard footsteps again.
Slowly, whatever had been standing outside began moving away from our
tent. We listened as the steps grew quieter, eventually disappearing
into the darkness beyond our campsite.
Nobody moved for a few seconds.
Then one of my friends whispered, “What the hell was that?”

None of us had an answer.

We stayed huddled in our sleeping bags, listening for any sign that it
was coming back. I remember being torn between wanting to unzip the tent
and look outside and being absolutely terrified of what we might see if
we did.
About five minutes later, we heard voices approaching.
It was the boys.

We recognised their voices immediately, and I don’t think I’ve ever felt
such an intense wave of relief. They were talking and laughing as they
walked back from the bathrooms, completely unaware that we’d spent the
last several minutes sitting in silence, convinced something enormous
had been standing outside our tent.
We called out to them through the fabric. “Wait. Were you guys just
here?” They sounded confused. “What? No. We just got back.”
We asked again, because we genuinely thought they were messing with us.
It would’ve been exactly the sort of prank they’d pull, and we assumed
they’d somehow managed to get back without us hearing them approach.
But they insisted they hadn’t been anywhere near the tent.

Eventually, we settled down, although I don’t remember sleeping
particularly well that night.
The next morning, we discovered something that made the whole thing even
stranger.
We’d brought sausages to cook for breakfast and had left them in a
cooler near our campsite. When we got up, we realised they were gone.

At first, we assumed someone had moved them or that we’d forgotten where
we’d put them. We checked around the campsite and asked the boys if
they’d taken them as a joke. They hadn’t.
We asked our parents, too, but nobody knew anything about it.

The cooler hadn’t been put away properly, so an animal getting into it
wasn’t out of the question. We knew that. We were camping in the woods,
after all, and wildlife getting into food was hardly unheard of.
But there was one detail that bothered me.
Whatever had taken the sausages had been right beside our tent, at the
same campsite where we’d seen that enormous shadow the night before.

Is this supernatural or just some greedy camper trying to scare a few
teenagers?
