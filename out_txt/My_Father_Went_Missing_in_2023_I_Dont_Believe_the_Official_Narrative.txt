On March 6, 2023, my father, Mark Granger, disappeared. At the time, he
was employed by McGuire Realty and had been hired to renovate a vacant
home roughly 7 miles outside the town of Craven’s Junction, KY. He
arrived at the property a few days prior to a blizzard that swept across
the state. Winter Storm Albert buried much of Ellington County under
heavy snow, cut off roads for days, and claimed the lives of 22 people
across the state.

That was the last time anyone can say with certainty where my father
was.

My dad and I spoke by phone some mornings while he was staying at the
property. Our conversations were usually pretty brief, but at no point
did he mention anything unusual or give me any reason to believe he was
in danger.

He had spent most of his life outdoors.  Being alone at an isolated
property for a couple weeks wasn’t unusual for him, and certainly wasn’t
something I would have considered dangerous under normal circumstances.

As the storm passed and reports of fatalities throughout the state began
to emerge though, I became concerned. I attempted to contact him
repeatedly over the following days. In total, I called or texted him
about twenty times. None of those attempts ever received a response.

After the third day, I filed a missing person’s report with the
Ellington County Sheriff’s Department. Several days after that they
performed a search, and found his pickup truck at the house, but no
other sign of him.​

According to investigators, they believe my father attempted to leave
the property once the storm became severe. Their theory is that he
walked outside to his truck, discovered it wouldn’t start because of the
cold, and attempted to return to the house. At some point during that
walk, they believe he became disoriented in the whiteout conditions and
wandered into the surrounding woods, where he ultimately succumbed to
the elements.

There is something about that explanation that doesn’t sit right with
me.

My dad’s truck was parked roughly thirty feet from the front door and
was facing directly toward the house. Even in near-zero visibility, it’s
difficult for me to understand how someone with his level of experience
could become so disoriented that they were unable to find a building
only a few steps away. If he had remained near the truck, simply walking
forward would have brought him back to the residence. It is a question
that I don’t think the investigation ever answered, and from what I can
tell it seems they are content in their explanation.

I remain grateful for the efforts of the Ellington County Sheriff’s
Department and the numerous agencies and volunteers who participated in
the search. My criticism has never been towards those who searched for
my father, but rather at the investigation that followed and the
conclusions that authorities reached.

I’ve made this post in an attempt to garner more attention surrounding
his case. I am currently performing expeditions out into the forest
where he disappeared. If you’re interested in following the case
further, please consider checking out our site www.findmarkgranger.com

If I find anything else interesting, I’ll be sure to let you know there.
