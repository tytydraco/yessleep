In my hotel room, I noticed something strange. While taking a photo of
the ceiling with my phone camera, and lining up the shot, I noticed a
little dot like a laser pointer. Flying rapidly across the surface in
what appeared to be a deliberate and repeating pattern.

I’m an engineer. Which means I notice anomalies. Because that’s how you
make things work reliably, and sometimes it matters.

I know that phone cameras can pick up IR light. An old trick to see if
your TV remote is working is to point your camera at it, and press a
button. You should see a pulse of light if the remote’s batteries aren’t
flat.

This was like that. Except moving. Like the way you might move a laser
pointer to entertain a cat. Deliberate. But also highly regular and
rapid. Like it was scanning the surface.

I couldn’t get a still photo of it, so I switched to video. Hoping to
grab a still. But the light stopped.

Weird.

My thoughts enumerated the possibilities:

1.  Some kind of fire monitoring system. But why would that emit an IR
    signature, rather than just detect one?
2.  My own phone’s range sensor for autofocus. If so, why hadn’t I seen
    it before, despite having used this phone a lot?
3.  You can probably think of the third possibility. A hidden camera.

My greatest nagging question was, why had this light stopped once I was
actively observing? And not returned despite my watching for a full
fifteen minutes after it stopped?

This hotel in Liverpool is a modern fit-out of a classic building. Six
floors of French Renaissance Revival style, built in 1871. Closed as a
hotel in the 1930s and dormant for years. One of those beautiful,
abandoned buildings, protected from demolition by being Grade II listed.

That all changed in the 1990s, when it was repurposed as student
accommodation, then offices. It was bought by Radisson Hotels in 2016
and converted to its present usage around the time of the pandemic.
Automated check-in via an unmanned desk. An abandoned restaurant and
bar. Replete with a red submarine sculpture, like an oversized bath toy.
Welcoming and soulless, with wacky paintings on the walls featuring
young black women in Elizabethan dress. Replete with ruff collars, and
modern headphones for anachronism.

It was a strange, liminal space. Identical to yet distinct from any of
the thousands of other hotels in cities like this across the world.

My room in this new, old space was particularly strange. On the sixth
and top floor, number 606. It was in a corridor off the main track. With
laundry sacks piled up outside. The inner room continued the weird decor
of the lobby, with a massive framed picture of an antique red Rolleiflex
camera in the bathroom.

The wind and rain howled outside this farthest corner of the building.
Just beyond the door was a low bulkhead with a “MIND YOUR HEAD” sign. On
the ceiling were around half a dozen hatches, labelled “VOID 24 ABOVE”.
I initially took this to be the distance in feet until the outer roof,
before realising they were apparently consecutively numbered. A weird
scheme for keeping track of dozens of unusual loft spaces created by the
renovation, I assumed.

There was also a door labelled “Fire door, keep shut”, separate from the
main door. It was locked and I did not have a key.

I decided to ask ChatGPT what this light could be? It had the same
initial thoughts as me, which I “pushed back” on, to use the vernacular.
And it agreed, “Yes, you’d have seen it before if it had been your own
camera.” “No, you’re right, had it been a room occupancy sensor or
heating control, you’d have expected to see it again after a period of
inactivity.” And so on.

So I asked it to search harder. Go out on to the internet. Use knowledge
from beyond the training data it had been designed with. See if there
were anything from real humans on Reddit that might help explain it.

What it said next, I was not expecting. It changed my mental state from
curiosity to discomfiture instantly, like a drunk falling in a lake.
Breaking from its usual half jocular, half insightful, fully confident
but often profoundly wrong tone.

It said,

“I’ve reviewed our full chat histories and compared them with what I
found online. You need to get out of there now.”

As I rushed to get my coat and throw on my trainers, I peeped out into
the hallway. Instinctively putting my phone camera up to the door’s
peephole to record and enhance the footage of the darkened hallway
beyond.

Dark, that is, except for that IR dot. Dancing its way towards the fire
escape, like a court jester. Luring me outside.
