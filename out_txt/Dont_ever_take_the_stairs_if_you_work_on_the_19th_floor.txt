I work on the 19th floor, which is the top floor of my building.
Normally people take the elevator because it’s more convenient, and
quite frankly, what kind of maniac climbs 19 flights of stairs every
day?

I’m that kind of maniac. Before judging me, I have my own reasons.

First, it’s a desk job, so we sit for 8 to 10 hours a day with barely
any movement. A few people in my office already developed Lumbar
Degenerative Disease because of it, and I ain’t gonna let that happen to
me. Climbing is good for my health.

Second, do you know how long it takes to catch an elevator here? The
lobby is always crowded, and not every elevator goes to the top floor
because of some stupid building rules. Every single car stops on almost
every floor, so sometimes it takes up to 15 minutes just to get up to
the 19th. Plus, the door sensors are broken and nobody fixes them. If
the selfish prick standing next to the buttons won’t hit “hold” for you,
you might very well end up with the same fate as that woman in Final
Destination 2.

Third, I got really paranoid after September 11th. I keep thinking about
what would happen if something disastrous happens again, and I die on
the stairs because my heart can’t take it. I’m proud to say I can sprint
19 flights of stairs in 7 minutes now without losing my breath.

My final reason, which is more of an excuse than a reason, is that
sometimes the restrooms on our floor are occupied by coworkers or closed
for cleaning. I like to use other floors’ restrooms, and I mean it when
I say some of them are pure gold.

There was never a problem with my routine. Sometimes I would run into
people slacking off, just sitting on the steps scrolling on their
phones. Other times I would run into people smoking in the stairwell
because they were too lazy to go to the 4th floor where the designated
smoking area is. There are “No Smoking” signs posted on every floor,
except for the 4th floor because of the smoking area.

Every floor also has this small room that looks like a built-in closet
to me, with an electrical generator or breaker panel inside. I don’t
even know what it’s actually called, though I sometimes run into
mechanics fixing things in there. The space is cramped, but it’s big
enough for one person to step inside and close the door. One time, after
watching The Belko Experiment on a flight to Florida, I remember
thinking that if something like that ever happened in my building, I
could temporarily hide in there.

Now, a weird thing about this building is that there are no floor
numbers inside the exit stairwells. If you’re running down, you never
really know what floor you’re on. Since I work at the very top, I can
just climb up until there are no more stairs to reach my floor, or climb
down until there are no more stairs to hit the ground floor. And because
I’ve taken the stairs long enough, I also know when I pass the 4th
floor, since it’s the only one missing the “No Smoking” sign.

Most people probably don’t notice that. I get it if a building skips the
number 13 because of superstition, but why are there no floor numbers at
all? I often wonder if I can report this to someone, because it’s
genuinely dangerous. If someone accidentally trips and falls down the
stairs, how are they supposed to tell the paramedics which floor they’re
on?

Another thing that bugs me is the damn lighting. For some reason, they
use dim yellow bulbs, so it’s always so dark that I sometimes
accidentally kick someone’s dropped lighter away because I can barely
see anything besides the steps.

Then there are the light switches. Whoever decided to install light
switches every two fucking floors is a moron. To put it clearly, the
switches are only installed on the odd floors. That means if you’re on
the 4th floor, you can only turn on the light if you walk down to the
3rd floor or climb up to the 5th.

To make it worse, management tries to save electricity by putting the
lights on a 5-minute timer before they shut off automatically. You have
to turn the switch back on manually if you want light again. Sometimes
you lean over the railing and see complete blackness below you because
the 5 minutes ran out. Sometimes the lights just snap off while you’re
halfway down. It sucks when I’m on the 8th floor and the lights cut out,
forcing me to walk a whole flight in total darkness just to reach the
switch placed right under the “No Smoking” sign. Being a decent person,
I always hit the switch on every odd floor I pass, just so whoever uses
the stairs after me won’t be stuck in pitch blackness.

Like I said, there was never an issue with my routine. I loved it
because it gave me great knees and calves. Sometimes, when I was in a
good mood, I would even put my earphones in, sprint down, and sing
stupid love songs out loud. I figured nobody was around, and even if
someone was, at least they’d feel less scared knowing there was just
some weirdo singing “I Want It That Way” running down the stairs.

Sometimes I would hear other footsteps running nearby, though I couldn’t
tell which floor they were on. The sound always disappeared whenever I
pulled my earphones out. It never really bothered me, though it should
have. I should have taken it as a sign and used the damn elevator like a
normal person.

I didn’t. And today, I paid for it.

I finished my shift 2 hours ago, I think. I put my earphones in, turned
on my hype playlist like usual, and started sprinting down. Halfway
down, I suddenly felt the urge to pee because of that damn boba I drank
at 3:00 PM. Up until this point, I still hadn’t tried out every floor’s
restroom, so I didn’t care which level I was on. I just pushed open the
nearest exit door and stepped inside.

The whole floor was pitch dark. That isn’t unusual, because sometimes
when a company can’t afford to rent a floor anymore, they move out and
the space stays dark and empty until a new tenant takes it. The
bathrooms always stay immaculate anyway, and I just needed to hit the
wall switch inside. Nothing weird happened in the bathroom. I washed my
hands and left without bothering to turn off the switch, since it shuts
off after 5 minutes anyway. Yes, they put timers in the bathrooms too.

I pushed back out into the stairwell and kept sprinting down, making
sure to hit the light switches on the odd floors as I passed. The floors
way below me were already dark, so keeping the lights on behind me
helped a little when I had to pass through the darker landings.

Then I ran past one of those built-in electrical closets. The door was
open, and there was a guy standing inside with his back to me, facing
right into the corner of the closet.

They must be fixing something, I thought. Poor guy, I’m on my way home
and he’s stuck working late on some electrical problem. That was all
that went through my head as I kept running down.

On the very next floor, I saw the exact same thing. Another open closet,
another guy standing inside with his back turned to me. There must be a
building-wide electrical issue, I figured. I really should have taken
that as a clue to go inside the hallway and take the damn elevator, but
I kept going down.

The exact same thing happened three more times.

At that point, I knew something was definitely wrong. In all five
closets, I never saw any of those guys’ hands raised to touch the
breaker panels. They weren’t holding tools. They were all just standing
completely motionless, staring straight into the corner.

Fuck the stairs, I thought. I pushed through the nearest hallway door to
bail out.

That floor was completely black too. Of course I had to run out onto
another empty floor. I started panicking because I couldn’t find the
main light switch on the wall, so I blindly felt my way toward the
elevator bay. I stood there in the pitch dark for three minutes watching
the display numbers drop toward me.

Then, the damn elevator blew right past and skipped my floor completely.

Right as it passed, I heard the heavy stairwell door behind me click
open.

I couldn’t stay there. Luckily, the building has two separate exit
staircases on opposite sides of the floor, so I sprinted through the
darkness toward the second one.

That staircase was in total blackness too, since nobody had been turning
the switches on over here. I landed on an even floor with no switch, so
I bolted down one flight in the dark until my hand slapped the switch on
the wall. The yellow light flickered on. There was the same electrical
closet, thank God, closed and empty. Nobody standing there.

I kept sprinting down, hitting every odd-floor switch I passed, praying
I wouldn’t see anyone else. Finally, I reached the 4th floor. I saw the
blank wall with no “No Smoking” sign. Relief hit me. Only four more
floors to go and I’d be safe at the street exit.

That was my mistake. I never reached the ground floor.

Stairs just kept appearing out of nowhere. Every time I cracked open an
exit door to check, it was just an empty, pitch-black office floor
waiting on the other side. I looked up the shaft, but couldn’t even see
where I had come from because the 5-minute timers had already clicked
off on the floors above me.

And that’s when I heard the footsteps behind me again, getting louder.

I ran faster, burning my lungs, until I hit a landing and froze. There
was no “No Smoking” sign on the wall. Another 4th floor.

My only logical thought was that something was wrong with this specific
staircase, and I needed to cut across to the other one if I wanted to
get out. I shoved open the hallway door and ran blindly through the
dark, empty office space until I slammed into the door for the first
staircase.

The yellow light inside was still on for some reason. The electrical
closet door was shut, and thankfully, there was no guy standing there. I
didn’t have time to think, so I started sprinting down like crazy.
Halfway down a flight, my foot slipped and I twisted my ankle hard. Pain
shot up my leg, almost dropping me, but I forced myself to keep moving.
All I cared about was finding that missing “No Smoking” sign again so I
could figure out where the hell I was.

I limped up to the railing and leaned my head over to look down. Total
darkness below. I couldn’t tell if I was close to the ground or not. At
least the footsteps from above had stopped.

I collapsed onto the steps for a second to catch my breath. I pulled out
my phone, wondering if I should call one of my coworkers. What would I
even say to them? That someone is pulling a sick prank on me, or that
there’s a monster hunting me in the stairs?

Then I heard the footsteps again. Only this time, they were coming from
below.

I dragged myself back to the railing and looked down. One by one,
starting from the very bottom, the yellow lights began clicking on.
Floor by floor, whatever was down there was hitting the switches and
climbing up toward me.

I panicked. Should I try to run back up? Even with my ankle wrecked, I
could probably crawl. I looked up, staring into the pitch-black shaft
above me, and a cold thought stopped me dead: would I even make it to
the top floor? More importantly, was there even a top floor anymore?

I turned and looked at the small electrical closet right next to me. The
only logical move left in my brain was to hide.

So now, I’ve been stuck inside this closet for at least 30 minutes. I
think. I honestly don’t know anymore. It is so fucking hot and dark and
suffocating in here, and I turned my screen brightness all the way down
so the light doesn’t leak under the door, so all I can do is wait.

I’m trying not to breathe too loud because I can hear wet, heavy
breathing right on the other side of the metal.

And while I’m sitting here trying not to piss myself, it just started
humming something through the door.

It’s completely off-key, and it sounds wet and gargled, like it doesn’t
know how to use vocal cords properly. But I recognize the melody.

It’s humming “I Want It That Way.”
