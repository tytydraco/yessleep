Sparta and Athens were at war. Sparta amassed an army of 500 battle
ready men, willing to bleed for the sand they were raised on. They stood
in the courtyard under the sun, their shields braced and swords
sharpened, their bronze helmets and armour glimmering beneath the
relentless sun.

​

“Perthus”

“Present”

“Midas”

“Present”

“… That marks 500 men, my lord.”

“Very well. Thank you, General Tullus”

King Archidamus started off towards the front of the gates where his men
faced.

“You are all aware why you stand in this courtyard today. Today, you
will march alongside your brethren and bleed beside them in battle. You
will starve. You will thirst. You will curse. Many of you standing here
today will die in the arms of your brothers you grew next to. I am not a
cruel king. No. I love you all deeply. You fight for Sparta, so you
fight for me, for our families, for your wives, your children, and all
those you hold dear to you. Now march. March out of these gates. And
still your eyes to not bat even a glance back here, lest your heart
breaks apart your valor.”

The king stood just outside the gates where the sand met the walls.
There, he shook the hands and kissed the heads of every face that left
the kingdom. And while the count was correctly established, he kept his
own record silently in his head as he waved every man off. From the
first soldier to the last, he counted 500 exactly.

And King Achidamus, as pure as he was in heart, knew that not many would
return. If at all. A terrible price to pay for war.

​

The king, generals, families, and soldiers that stayed back to defend
the kingdom watched as that army of 500 slowly shrank over the cloudy
horizon.

​

From 500.

To 400.

Then 200.

Then 50.

And then 1.

Then none…

​

Many months pass, the kingdom grows anxious with every passing full
moon. Then one night, a scout surveying the area from the walls sees an
army growing over the horizon. At first 10, then 100, then more, until
eventually a mass is revealed. It’s the army they had sent.

​

The king and all the generals line every man up back at where they were
counted all those months ago the next day to cross out the names that
made it back and leave the ones that didn’t.

​

The generals are divided to effectively speed up the count. Hours pass
as the king plans his compensations and condolences to the families of
the men deceased.

​

But to the shock of the king and his generals, each and every single
name was crossed off. Every Spartan that was sent is here and alive. Not
a single man was slain.

​

But that rapidly growing joy was extinguished and replaced with
confusion when one of the generals states the impossible. Much to
everybody else’s dismay.

​

*There are 501 men standing in the courtyard right now*

​

The king is struck. The generals are dumbfounded. Even the men
themselves are confused, turning to themselves in attempts to recognize
every face beside and behind them.

​

Minutes pass as the king and the generals discuss amongst themselves.
The men grow restless and loud.

​

“Count again”

The king orders.

“Count again and triple check every name on that list”

The generals follow.

​

Hours pass again

The king’s puzzled look never left his face.

The generals return, the confusion still displayed on their faces.

​

*501*

“Again, and this time write the new lists from scratch. Name every man
on that paper and then compare both. Single the outlier out,”

The king was sure this would work. How could it not? This was logic.
Unbreakable, reality set, logic.

​

Once the records were written, the generals began comparing both lists
meticulously. Their eyes squinted with caution so as to make sure no
mistakes were possible.

​

And none were.

On the old list, there were *500* men. Not one man was miscounted.

​

Yet…

​

On the new list

There were *501*

​

The Spartans grew restless immediately. The generals beckoned them all
to remain silent.

Even the king himself shared the same sentiment all the men expressed,
not out of anger, not out of frustration, but out of confusion…

​

The generals scanned the lists again, counting every single name written
with ink on parchment and comparing each. But it felt like their minds
were bending around an invisible pole. Their brains tried to single the
name out, yet everyone belonged. Everybody there was meant to be there.

​

How? There were 500 men sent out into the battlefield, the king himself
shook the hands and kissed the heads of every man that walked out the
walls that day.

​

“Enough! Walk down the isles of every soldier and confirm the names from
both lists in the order they were written.”

One single general was sent to count, and while this took much longer,
the margin for error was greatly reduced. It was impossible to count a
name wrong.

​

Hopeless.

​

The general returned, his face displaying what his heart felt as he
walked back.

​

“I counted. Every single name there, on both lists, I counted. But
everyone is meant to be here, there were no errors, my lord!” Exclaimed
the general.

​

Then they tried sending a different general, then another, then all of
them at once to confirm, they counted with the old list and the new
list, they counted with just the old, then only the new, then they told
every man to step out if his name was called and then return into
formation from both lists, they asked every soldier if he knew the man
behind him, everything worked. Everything was alright. It was true.

​

But that was the very thing that wasn’t supposed to happen.

​

“Leonidas”

“Present

“Theodore”

“Present”

“Alexander”

“Present”

“Theron”

“Present”

“… 501…”

“But we counted the original list! We did this three times! How is this
possible?”

“I do not know…”

​

A people defined by their mathematical prowess, structured power,
tactical supremacy, and disciplined nature had fallen apart in those
walls.

​

Not because they died.

Not because there was a mutiny.

Not because of anything damaging.

And that was the horror.

​

The sun gently hugged the sands over the horizon now. It was some time
before midday when the count started.

​

It was now dusk. And not a single satisfying conclusion was made.

​

Eventually, the king waved everyone off back to the city. And in a last
desperate but lazy attempt to count again, he committed himself into the
same gesture he’d done when he first sent them out.

​

Yet every hand was the same, every head sent off was there that day.

​

The Athenians lost the war. Sparta rejoiced in all their riches, in all
their glory. All the men there that day grew old with their families and
spoiled their kin with their wealth.

​

The military arsenal men recorded the numbers of every spear, every
shield, every sword, every pair of sandals…

​

and every helmet.

​

499 were returned over time as every soldier passed away peacefully. 2
remained, a trace of the inexplicable horror that happened on the day of
their return.

​

The 500th man sat by his fire. Embracing the comforts of his home. He,
like every other man, let the memory of that day fade away. It was of no
use. If human certainty couldn’t confirm the impossible, then what was
the point in living your life around that one day?

​

The night was silent. Cold. It reminded him of the camps they would
sleep in under the moonlight.

​

His wife slept behind him, as did his three warrior sons he’d raised.

​

The flames twisted and danced in ways that made him reminisce about his
life.

​

He remembers being behind Nikolas and in front of Andus.

​

He remembers King Archidamus kissing his head.

​

He remembers the way his heart broke as he thought back to his family
while they marched

​

He remembers helping Georgus out of the crevice he’d wedged himself into
before the arrows pierced them both.

​

​

​

​

​

Then he remembers Georgus dying…

​

Then he remembers the first arrow striking his chest.

He remembers the second one pinning his arm to the sand.

He remembers the third lodging into his throat.

He remembers himself gargling on his blood as his tears mixed with the
hot crimson blood on his cheeks as he prayed for his family’s safety.

He remembers the fade to black.

The way his chest stopped rising.

​

He *died* staring into the corpse of his dead friend beside him.

He felt the warmth of his skin leaving his body, so why is he sitting
here embracing the fire’s warmth?

​

Like the rapid darkening of a mountain as the sun sets behind it, his
heart raced as he kept going back over these details.

​

There was blackness… Thick, foggy, suffocating darkness that swallowed
his soul into his final resting place.

​

And then there was a helmet in front of him as he and his men marched
under the moonlight back into the city walls.

​

His eyes never blinked once despite the foot gap between him and the
fire. Tears had started to well up in his eyes as he unraveled his life
before and after the war.

​

Under the dead of night, he ran to Byron’s house, his old bones working
as if remembering the way he charged into his sandy casket. The casket
that was never there until tonight.

​

Marcus barged in with a thunderous thud that shook the stone house.
Byron’s eyes shot up as he instinctively reached for his sword and got
into a defensive position.

​

“Byron, lower your sword. We MUST speak NOW.”

“What?”

“Do you remember the ridge? Do you remember the battle we fought?”

“Yes? What of it? Why do you ask? Why did you storm int-”

“Byron, sit down with me, look into my eyes, and remember that entire
battle. From the very first foot you set into battle to the last shield
you struck.”

​

Marcus and Byron sat down, the cold of the night encroaching the light
that the old oil lamp cast. Every shadow stretched longer than they
should have. Was the world itself breaking apart? As if it too realized
alongside Marcus the inconsistency in reality that transpired that day.

​

“Think… Byron, think… Who was in front of you?”

“Perthus”

“Behind you”

“Theodus”

“And what happened as you charged alongside Georgus and I with our
shields raised?”

Byron parted his lips to answer but the words died on his tongue.

​

“What. Happened. To us, Byron?”

“… We…”

“…”

“No it can’t be…”

“Georgus slipped his foot into a cracked rock and couldn’t free himself.
His shield dropped in front of him. I remember removing my shield from
our formation to help him out before the arrows reached us.”

“But…”

“But the arrows did reach us…”

“No. That can’t be. Why? What happened?”

Byron recalled every second. He remembers the moment Marcus broke the
shield wall to cover Georgus. He remembers the arrow that shot into his
chest. He remembers how he fell beside Marcus, his death a result of
unbroken camaraderie that had no easy choice to make. He remembers how
Marcus fell too, a long piece sticking out of his neck.

​

“I remember… All that. The arrow in your neck… The arrow in my chest… I
remember it all. *I remember defeat. So WHY ARE WE LIVING A VICTORY!?*”

​

Their old bones shook with an uncertainty they’d never felt before in
their lives. Everything up until now was certain. They fought, they won,
they lived.

​

“I do not know, Byron…”

“Our lives are a lie, we are living a lie! Why did no one ever question
this!?”

“We had no reason to. All that was there was us winning the war, but
digging back into our minds has led us down to a more confusing truth.
Who are we?! What are we?! Who did we… Fight…?”

“… Who *did* we fight?”

​

Nothing was making sense. The terror only deepened and every attempt to
clarity only served to further this unanswerable, yet correct problem.

​

Who were the archers?

Who were the hoplites?

What was the formation?

What armour did they wear?

Whose banner did they carry?

What battle had they charged themselves into?

*WHO WON THAT BATTLE IF THE VICTORS WERE THE ONES WHO WERE SLAUGHTERED
THAT DAY*

​

“Remember the count, Marcus?”

“We never singled him out. Everyone there grew up with us. Everyone
there trained alongside us. All 500 of us.”

“And yet, there was…”

“501 that returned…”

​

The following month, both men died of natural causes. And in that time,
their families say they changed. They smiled less. Like they knew the
calendar that marked their death.

​

500 shields were returned to the barracks, scratched and battered

500 swords, dull as rocks and chipped all over, were returned too.

500 pairs of worn and dusty sandals.

*And 500 helmets…*

Like there was always supposed to be.

Because there always was.

​

And on a battlefield far away lay the buried skeletons 500 dead
Spartans. Dismantled and broken, slashed and hacked, pierced and
stabbed. A grizzly scene that no one saw.

Because there was never a scene. And no Spartans ever died.

​

“Yes, Standus?”

“Had that scar always been on father’s neck?”

“Yes, Standus, it always has been.”
