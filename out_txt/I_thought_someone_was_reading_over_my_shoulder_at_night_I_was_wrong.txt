I live alone, which is probably worth mentioning before anything else.
My apartment is on the third floor of a building with a locked entrance,
and the only other living thing in it is a plant I keep forgetting to
water. I don’t have a roommate, a girlfriend who stays over, or a cat
that could explain things moving around in the dark. It’s just me.

I have a pretty bad habit of scrolling on my phone in bed. I know
everyone says that, but I mean I’ll get into bed at eleven and look up
to find it’s almost two. Usually it’s Reddit. Sometimes I’ll read an
entire argument between strangers about something I didn’t care about
ten minutes earlier. If I find a good horror post, I’ll go through the
comments looking for people pointing out details I missed, then click on
another one.

My bedroom is small. The bed is pushed against the wall on one side, and
I sleep on the other side, facing the door. There’s a narrow dresser
across from me and a chair I use as a second laundry basket. I keep the
curtains shut because there’s a streetlight outside. With the lamp off,
the phone is usually the only light in the room.

I used to like that. I liked being able to see the screen and not much
else. It made it easier to forget about work, or the dishes in the sink,
or whatever I had to do the next morning.

The first time I noticed anything, I was reading a post about a guy who
kept finding wet footprints in his kitchen. I remember that because I
was halfway through a comment explaining why the story didn’t make sense
when I thought I saw my bedroom door move.

I looked up. The door was still open a few inches, the way I’d left it.

I checked the hallway anyway. Nothing. I went to the bathroom, got some
water, and went back to bed. It was one of those little movements you
catch out of the corner of your eye that turn out to be nothing. I
didn’t even think of it as an incident until later.

A few nights after that, it happened again. This time it wasn’t the
door. It was something dark near the right edge of my phone, just
outside the part of my vision I was concentrating on. I turned my head
and it was gone.

I assumed it was my hair. I’d been putting off a haircut, and a few
strands had started falling into my eyes. I pushed them back, kept
reading, and forgot about it.

Over the next week I noticed it maybe four or five times. Always late,
always when I was lying on my left side with my phone in my right hand.
I’d be deep into something and there would be a shape off to the right.
Not a person, exactly. More like the suggestion that something was
occupying space where there shouldn’t be anything.

The strange part was that I never saw it when I was looking for it.

I could put the phone down, turn on the lamp, check the floor between
the bed and dresser. Nothing. I even moved the chair into the living
room because the clothes hanging off it looked vaguely human in the
dark. That made no difference.

I figured it was a combination of eye strain and reading scary stories.
Once you get it into your head that there’s something in the room, every
shadow starts looking suspicious. I stopped reading horror for a few
days. I watched cooking videos and looked at apartment listings I
couldn’t afford. I still caught that shape once, while reading a long
thread about a landlord dispute.

That bothered me more than I expected.

I made an appointment with an optometrist. I didn’t mention the feeling
of being watched. I told her I was seeing something at the edge of my
vision at night. She checked my eyes, asked about flashes of light and
floaters, and told me to come back promptly if I developed new symptoms.
Nothing unusual showed up in the exam. She said fatigue and a bright
screen against a dark background could make visual disturbances more
noticeable.

I took that as permission to stop worrying.

For a while, I did. I turned on the bedside lamp when I used my phone. I
made an effort to go to sleep earlier. I even bought one of those little
clip-on lights, which was ridiculous because I wasn’t reading an actual
book.

Then I started noticing something I couldn’t explain as easily.

One Saturday I was lying in bed in the afternoon, waiting for a load of
laundry to finish. The curtains were closed, but there was plenty of
light leaking around them. I was reading an article, not anything scary.
I saw the shape again.

Instead of looking toward it, I stayed still.

It was hard to describe what I was seeing because I wasn’t looking
directly at it. There was a darker area just beyond the right edge of my
phone, and a narrow, lighter strip beside it. I thought it might be the
gap between the curtains and the wall. But when I moved the phone a
little to the left, the shape stayed where it was.

I moved it back. The shape was still there.

I looked straight at it. There was nothing but the dresser and the wall.

I sat up so quickly that I knocked my water bottle off the nightstand.

For the first time, I had the distinct impression that the shape had
been close to me. Not across the room. Close enough that moving the
phone should have revealed more of it.

I went into the living room and stayed there until the laundry finished.
I remember feeling stupid for being frightened in broad daylight. I also
remember leaving the bedroom door open when I went to sleep that night,
which doesn’t make much sense now.

After that I tried to avoid using my phone in bed. This lasted about
three days. Habits are embarrassing things. I would tell myself I was
only checking the weather and find myself reading comments twenty
minutes later.

I also started testing it, though I didn’t think of it as testing at the
time.

If I lay on my back and held the phone directly above me, I didn’t
notice anything. If I sat upright against the headboard, nothing. But
when I rolled onto my left side and held the phone a little in front of
my face, I would sometimes become aware of that shape to the right.

It didn’t happen every time. And I want to be clear that I never saw
anything walk into the room. I never heard footsteps. I never woke up to
a figure standing over my bed. Most nights, the room was exactly what it
had always been.

That made it easier to talk myself out of the other nights.

The first time I thought it was a head, I almost laughed.

I was reading in bed with the lamp off. There was a long comment on the
screen, and I had stopped scrolling to finish it. Something pale
appeared at the edge of my vision. I kept my eyes on the text, partly
because I was afraid it would vanish again if I looked up.

I could make out a rounded shape, a darker line beneath it, and a thin
projection that seemed to point down toward the phone. I stared at the
words without taking any of them in.

It looked like the side of someone’s face.

More specifically, it looked like someone leaning over my right shoulder
to see what I was reading.

I turned my head. Nothing.

I got out of bed and turned on every light in the apartment. I checked
the closet and under the bed, even though I knew how absurd that was.
The front door was locked. The windows were locked. There was nowhere
for a person to hide, and nobody could have moved out of the room
without passing me.

I slept on the couch that night.

The next morning, I told a friend about it. Not the whole thing. I said
I’d been seeing what looked like a face in my peripheral vision when I
used my phone in the dark. He asked whether I’d been getting enough
sleep and sent me a link about sleep-related hallucinations. He wasn’t
being dismissive. If he’d told me the same story, I would have sent him
the same link.

I started leaving the bedroom lamp on all night.

That helped for a little while. I didn’t see the face as clearly with
the room lit, although sometimes I still got the feeling that there
wasn’t quite enough space between my shoulder and the air beside it.
It’s difficult to explain without sounding ridiculous. I would find
myself shifting toward the wall, making room on the other side of the
bed for something I didn’t believe was there.

One night I caught myself apologizing under my breath after moving my
elbow.

That was when I realized how much this had gotten into my head.

I decided I needed to stop treating it like a haunting and start
treating it like a problem. I moved the bed away from the wall so I
could sleep on either side. I cleaned the bedroom, washed the sheets,
and took the mirror off the dresser. I checked the carbon monoxide
detector. I bought a cheap phone stand so I wouldn’t always hold the
screen at the same angle.

For a few nights, I was fine.

Then I woke at around three in the morning with the phone still in my
hand. I’d fallen asleep reading. The screen was dim, almost black, and
my arm was bent awkwardly in front of me.

There was something beside it.

I didn’t see a whole face. Just the line of a forehead sloping down, a
nose, and a little patch of pale skin where a cheek should have been. It
was close enough that I couldn’t understand how I’d been holding the
phone there without touching it.

The head was lowered toward the screen.

I was completely awake by then. I know how people describe waking
dreams, and maybe that’s what this was, but I could feel the seam of the
pillow against my cheek and the numbness in my fingers from holding the
phone too long. I could hear a car outside trying to start. I was awake
enough to be terrified of moving.

The phone went dark.

I don’t know whether I blinked or turned my head first. I only know that
when I looked again, there was nothing there.

I didn’t go back to sleep. At six, I got dressed and went out for
breakfast. I stayed away from the apartment until late afternoon.

I considered telling my landlord someone might have gotten inside. But
what would I have asked him to do? Change the locks because I thought I
saw part of a face next to my phone? I checked the doorbell camera in
the hallway through the building app. Nobody had come to my door during
the night.

For the next week I slept with the lights on and left my phone charging
in the kitchen. I was tired at work and irritable with everyone. The
shape didn’t appear. I started feeling better.

Then, last Thursday, I brought the phone back into bed.

I was waiting for an email that had nothing to do with any of this. I
checked it, answered it, and opened Reddit out of habit. I wasn’t even
thinking about the face until I saw a pale strip beside the screen.

I didn’t turn around.

This time I kept my head still. I watched the text in front of me and
tried to understand the shape beside it. I could see a little more than
before. The curve of a forehead. The bridge of a nose. A dark hollow
where an eye ought to be.

It was leaning toward the phone, exactly as I’d thought.

I slowly moved the phone down toward my chest.

The head didn’t follow it.

I held the phone there for several seconds, then raised it again. The
shape remained in precisely the same position, its face still angled
toward the place where the screen had been.

That seemed strange, but I was too frightened to think about why. I
locked the phone, pulled the blanket over my head like a child, and
stayed there until morning.

I know how pathetic that sounds. I don’t have a better explanation for
what I did.

I’ve spent the last few days trying to decide whether to write this. I
keep thinking that if I put it into words, I’ll have to admit what I
believe is happening. I also keep thinking about the way it holds its
head, as though it is interested in whatever I’m reading.

I think that’s what bothered me most. The familiarity of it. Anyone
who’s ever had a friend lean over to see something on their phone knows
the posture. You don’t have to look directly at the person to understand
what they’re doing. You can tell from the angle of the head, the way it
comes close to yours, the space it takes up beside you.

I had been assuming the same thing about whatever was next to me.

Tonight I tried something different. I waited until I could see that
pale edge again, then kept my head still and moved only my eyes. I
didn’t look far enough to see the whole face. I don’t think I could have
made myself do that.

But I saw enough to understand why it never followed the phone when I
moved it.

I’ve been writing this for nearly an hour now. At first I was worried
about what would happen if it saw me describing it. I almost stopped a
few times. Then I remembered what I noticed tonight, and I realized I
didn’t need to worry about that.

It’s here now. I can see the side of its face beside the screen. Its
head is still lowered, like it’s reading over my shoulder.

But it isn’t reading.

I could see the white of its eye when I looked sideways. Its eye was
turned all the way toward me.

It hasn’t been looking at my phone at all.
