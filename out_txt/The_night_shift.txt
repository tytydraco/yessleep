I’ve spent a long time thinking about whether I should tell this or not.
The few times I’ve told family members or friends, they looked at me
strangely, as if nothing I experienced had been real or as if I were
trying to get attention with a made-up story. I’ve never managed to
convince anyone that it really happened, but I swear it did. Many years
have passed since then, and there are still nights when I wake up
remembering what I saw. Maybe writing it down is the only way to get it
off my chest, although part of me would rather forget it forever.

This goes back to the summer of 2005. I had just finished the school
year and needed a summer job, something that wouldn’t be too demanding
and that paid reasonably well. That was when I got a position as a night
security guard in the parking garage of the shopping mall in my city. It
was a simple job, and honestly, I thought the hardest part would be
finding something to do during the long empty hours inside the security
booth until my shift ended. After that, I would just go home and sleep
all day. To me, it sounded like the perfect summer. The parking garage
was completely underground, so I would be protected from the heat, the
rain, and the wind while the rest of the city suffered through the high
temperatures of July.

On the first night I arrived early, partly to make a good impression and
partly because I had nothing better to do at home. I spent a while
talking with the guard from the previous shift while he explained how
everything worked. Every hour I would have to leave the booth and do a
round of the parking garage to make sure everything was in order.
According to him, nothing ever happened, but he insisted that I always
carry the walkie-talkie so I could contact the guards patrolling inside
the mall if any problem came up. The explanation didn’t take long, and
when his shift ended, he said goodbye, wished me a quiet night, and
left.

I was completely alone in the booth. There wasn’t much there: a deck of
cards, a radio, a small television, and a rather old coffee maker. For
me, that was more than enough to get through the night. Anyone else
probably would have been bored, but at that time I loved it. I was
getting paid to drink coffee and watch television. The night began
without incident. Little by little the parking garage became completely
empty, and from the booth’s window you could see almost the entire area
without having to go outside. Even so, every hour I had to do my round,
so I spent ten or fifteen minutes walking through the aisles between the
columns, checking that everything was still in order.

It was during one of those rounds that the walkie-talkie started making
noise. Nothing specific could be heard; it was more like white noise
that came and went intermittently, as if someone were trying to transmit
from very far away. I didn’t pay much attention to it because those
devices were already quite old, and it was common for them to produce
interference or pick up strange frequencies. I assumed one of the guards
inside the mall was talking on the radio, or that the device itself was
malfunctioning. The noise eventually disappeared on its own, and I
continued working. The hours passed almost without my noticing until it
was time to go home.

The next night I repeated exactly the same routine. I arrived a little
early and chatted with my coworker while he finished his shift. However,
just before leaving, he said something that made the hair on the back of
my neck stand up. “You didn’t hear anything strange last night, did
you?” I told him no, that everything had been completely normal, and
asked whether I should have heard something. He simply smiled awkwardly,
avoided answering, and changed the subject as if he regretted having
said anything at all. That reaction stayed in my mind for the rest of
the night, and although I tried to forget it, a feeling of unease
quickly settled in.

The first few hours passed just like the previous night, but the
atmosphere was no longer the same. The silence felt much heavier, and
every time I went out to do a round I had the strange sensation that
someone was watching me from some corner of the parking garage. I tried
to convince myself that it was only my imagination, brought on by my
coworker’s comment, until the walkie-talkie crackled again. This time it
was different. Beneath the static, I thought I could hear a voice, badly
distorted and almost impossible to make out. Puzzled, I asked over the
radio whether any of the other guards had tried to contact me. The
answer came immediately, perfectly clear and without interference: none
of them had said anything. We spent a while trying to find an
explanation for the sound. Maybe another security guard was using the
same frequency, maybe the device was faulty, or maybe someone had
pressed the button by mistake, but none of those explanations felt
convincing.

Little by little the loneliness became heavier and the silence more
uncomfortable until, suddenly, a loud bang echoed through the entire
parking garage just seconds after I had finished one of my rounds. I
left the booth terrified and walked slowly toward the sound, sweeping my
flashlight across everything in front of me. Then I saw it. A soccer
ball was lying motionless in the middle of the aisle. It was impossible
for it to be there. Five minutes earlier I had walked through that exact
spot, and there had been absolutely nothing there. There was also no one
who could have thrown it, because the parking garage had been completely
empty for hours and I was the only person down there. Without thinking
twice, I turned around and ran back to the booth. I locked the door,
tried to calm myself, and decided that I would not go outside again
until dawn.

After a while the shock began to wear off. I tried not to think about
the ball while watching some random television program, repeating to
myself over and over that there had to be a logical explanation. Then
the walkie-talkie came to life again. This time there was no
interference and no static. The voice came through with absolute
clarity.

“Look at me!”

I slowly raised my eyes and saw a child standing perfectly still on the
other side of the booth’s window. His clothes were dirty and worn,
stained with blood. His skin was so pale it almost looked transparent,
and his eyes… his eyes were nothing but two black, empty sockets that
seemed to swallow the light of the parking garage. I froze, unable to
look away, feeling my entire body stop responding until, finally, I lost
consciousness.

I woke up the next morning in a hospital. I never asked who had found me
or how long I had been unconscious, because deep down I didn’t want to
know the answer. Without thinking twice, I handed in my resignation that
very day, and I have never set foot in that shopping mall again. Many
years have passed, but I still remember that voice calling me through
the walkie-talkie and the stare of that child. And no matter how much I
have tried to convince myself that it was all the result of exhaustion
or my imagination, there is one thing I have never been able to explain:
that soccer ball was not there the last time I walked past that spot.
