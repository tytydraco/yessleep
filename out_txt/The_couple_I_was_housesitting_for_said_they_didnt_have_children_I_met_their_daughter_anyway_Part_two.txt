The morning after, a wave of dread washed over me from not getting any
sleep. 

As I got up, the pain in my arm had grown worse. My skin felt tight, and
the sleeve of my shirt seemed to rub against it more than usual. I
wasn’t fat, but I definitely wasn’t underweight at all, so I was
confused.

Slowly and carefully, I lowered my sleeve, expecting to see the swollen
scratch across my forearm.

Instead, I found a few neat butterfly closures holding the wound closed.

I just stared at them.

I hadn’t put these on myself.

So who had?

Though I was terrified, I had been promised money for taking care of the
house and Percy. Besides I wouldn’t be able to let go of the fact that
something bad can happened to Percy or anything that Raph and Darlene
had loved so much. That same morning, I found him eating from his bowl,
so I gently caressed the fur on top of his head.

That’s when I noticed something out of place.

The pantry was wide open.

I glanced back at the fridge where the list was.

The words replayed in my head.

Please keep the pantry closed.

Maybe I forgot to close it.

After that day, I started noticing other strange things. Some cans of
food—and a bottle of water—had gone missing, even though I was convinced
I hadn’t taken any.

One night, I was planning on watching TV after giving Percy his
prescription medicine. I went looking for a blanket I had brought from
home, but no matter where I checked, I couldn’t find it anywhere.

I checked my car, too.

It wasn’t there either.

On Thursday night, when I checked the doors like I always did and
suddenly I stopped.

 Darlene and Raph’s bedroom door was wide open. Nobody has been in
there–especially me or them.

I hadn’t slept properly in days. Maybe that was affecting me.

By that point for about that week, I was starting to question myself.

Had I really left the pantry open?

Did I actually bring the blanket with me?

Perhaps I did take good care of my arm and was too tired to remember?

Was I eating too much at the point I forgot I had anything during the
day?

Something wasn’t right about this. Or it might be something wrong with
this fucking house.

I began checking the doors twice before going to bed. Sometimes even
three times. I kept telling myself I was tired. That I was constantly
overthinking.

Maybe there wasn’t anything wrong in the house. Maybe there was
something wrong with me. Am I really going to let myself go just because
of simple shit?

On Friday, I finally had the courage to check the attic. The thoughts
and constant anxiety over Percy’s behavior had been getting to me. I
know anybody would’ve told me not to go up there, but I had to. Besides
I had to give myself a reality check that I’m just acting paranoid. Even
if it’s just the fact that I’m in an old house that would be from horror
movies with the lazy act of a slasher, ghost might own this place or
that big houses would lead to somewhere you don’t want to see.

I brought a broom with me—just in case there really was a raccoon or
something up there. I slowly creaked open the attic door.
