I work as a maintenance man at a casino. Yesterday, like every day after
closing, I went to check the machines. When I had checked everything and
was heading out, I caught a jukebox blinking out of the corner of my
eye. I didn’t understand how that was possible because I had checked all
the machines. One by one.

I walked closer to it and looked at the screen. For a brief moment, my
breath caught. Instead of the usual music artists and songs, there were
titles that I knew very well. The list was endless.

The first one was First Day at School.

Year: 1962.

Price: a lock of hair.

I looked around to see if it was some stupid joke from management, but I
was completely alone. I pressed play.

Instead of music starting, I heard my mother’s voice in my head.

“Don’t be afraid, Joshy. You’ll see, you’ll like it there.”

I could feel how nervous I was and how my hands were sweating. I could
feel my mother hugging me, and I breathed in the scent of her jasmine
perfume.

Nervously, I walked into the building, climbed the stairs, and opened
the door. My future best friend Peter was sitting at a desk.

“If you want, you can sit with me.”

I accepted his offer with a smile. Then the sound of a bell filled the
room. The teacher came into the classroom. I had a feeling that, before
she closed the door, I saw something in the hallway. She introduced
herself to us with a smile, and then the vision disappeared.

I looked around and found myself back in the casino. I just felt a pain
in part of my head. When I touched it, a piece of my hair was missing.

Confused, I looked at the machine and noticed a framed piece of paper
next to it. It read:

“Every memory has a price.”

I scrolled through the other things the machine offered. There was
almost everything. My grandmother’s death, my first day of high school,
my first kiss. That’s where I stopped and looked closer.

First Kiss

Year: 1972.

Price: a fingernail.

I hesitated for a moment and then pressed play. I found myself with
Clare. She was wearing that beautiful pink dress. I could smell the
lilacs we were standing beside. She smiled at me, and I leaned toward
her. I remember how her lips smelled of strawberries.

I pulled away from her and watched the look in her eyes while my heart
was pounding. Just before the vision disappeared, I saw a figure on the
other side of the street, walking toward us.

Then I was back, and a stronger, burning pain shot through my thumb. My
entire nail was gone. It scared the hell out of me.

I wanted to leave, but somehow I couldn’t. I had to see what was next.

Marriage to Clare

Year: 1980.

Price: a finger.

“No,” I whispered.

I wanted to leave. I should have left. I know that. But I missed her so
much. I wanted to see her again so badly. I couldn’t help it. I pressed
play.

I could feel my whole body shaking as I stood at the altar. I saw Peter
standing to my right, patting me on the shoulders.

“Relax, buddy. You got this.”

I looked around and saw the figure sitting in the second row of
witnesses. I wanted to lean toward Peter and ask him if he could see it
too. But then the doors opened, and I saw her walking toward me.

That beautiful white dress. I knew it was the most beautiful thing I had
ever seen in my life. She slowly walked up to me and smiled. I couldn’t
take my eyes off her.

I could barely make out what the officiant was saying, so he had to
repeat the question. I stammered out a yes.

When he asked Clare, I felt fear wash over me as I waited for her
answer, followed by a wave of relief when she said yes.

“You may kiss the bride.”

Then I was back again. I screamed in pain and looked at my hand. My
little finger was gone. In its place was a bloody wound.

It wasn’t as painful as I had expected. It was like getting your hand
caught in a door. I stared at it for a moment, and instead of turning
around and leaving, I moved back toward the screen.

This time will be the last, I promised myself. I scrolled through the
screen, and my heart stopped.

The Day I Saw Clare for the Last Time

Year: 1988.

Price: an eye.

I pressed play.

It was a normal summer morning. The sun was beginning to warm everything
outside, and we were standing in front of the house. She smiled at me
just like she had on the first day we met. I felt her embrace, her kiss,
and heard her tell me that she loved me and that we’d see each other
that evening.

I wanted to stop her, but that’s not how memories work. She got into the
car and waved at me. I waved back, and when she disappeared from sight,
I turned around and headed back into the house.

When I turned around, the figure was standing a few steps behind me.

I can’t describe it. Only the feeling I had when I looked at it.

Emptiness. Sadness. Fear.

I found myself back in the casino again, and a terrible pain tore
through me. Pain unlike anything I had ever experienced before. It felt
like the entire left side of my face was on fire. And where my left eye
had once been, there was only darkness.

“It was worth it,” I whispered. “It was worth seeing her, but not
anymore. Now I’ll leave.”

I just wanted to see what was at the end. Now there wasn’t just a year.
There was a full date.

September 6, 2026.

Price: life.

I stared at it in confusion. I started backing away, wanting to run, but
I bumped into something. I didn’t need to turn around to know what it
was. I could feel it.

The sadness. The fear. The emptiness.

It grabbed my hand and pressed play.

I found myself at home, sitting on my couch. The TV host was saying:

“Good morning, viewers. This time, we’re going to begin with today’s
hero, Henry.”

The TV kept playing, and I couldn’t do anything except sit there. I saw
the light in the living room flicker slightly and a fly buzzing around
the television.

When the host announced that the broadcast was over for the day, I saw
the front door slowly open, and it walked inside.

I woke up back in the casino. This time, I didn’t wait for anything. I
immediately ran outside and headed home.

The first thing I did was open the fridge and pour myself a shot. One
after another.

I didn’t wake up until morning.

For a moment, I thought it had all been a dream, but then I looked at my
hand and touched the left side of my face, where my eye used to be.

Now I’m sitting on my couch, listening to the TV host say:

“Good morning, viewers. This time, we’re going to begin with today’s
hero, Henry.”
