The title makes it seem much more official than it is. I started giving
last rites to road kill when I was pretty little, not an ordained
minister or second hand to the Pope or anything. I was just a dumb kid.
I remember sitting in the back of my mom’s minivan on the way home from
church when we stopped at a red light. At the light, in the gutter
between the sidewalk and main road, was a possum. Rather, what was left
of one. The thing laid crumpled and rigid in the shape of a sloppily
written L as its tiny paws attempted to drag itself up out of the puddle
in its last moments alive. Jellied blood leaked from its open maw as it
stared up towards passerby, begging even in death for some kind of
assistance. 

“Mama, that puppy needs help.” I whined, pointing out the long dead
creature to my distracted mother. She looked to where I was pointed and
let out a disgusted sigh. 

“Honey, the only thing we can do for that creature is send him off to
Jesus.” She said, as the light turned green. As we drove off I watched
her do the sign of the cross and kiss the side of her forefinger. I did
the same. It was an easy enough ritual and after a few weeks it became a
habit I adopted well into my teenage years. Dead animal in the road?
Sign of the cross and seal it with a kiss. That was it. No blood
sacrifices or chanting required. Just a quick ritual to give a proper
send off to the beings people forget about. 

As the years passed, and I continued doing this simple gesture, I
noticed that after I kissed my finger, clumps of pale blue light started
floating around the car. Sometimes they stayed stagnant and sometimes
that floated frantically throughout the car like ping pong balls. I
asked my parents about them a few times but that only resulted in my
getting a fresh pair of glasses at the start of fifth grade. My eye
sight turned out to not be the issue, I still saw the flickers of light
during long car rides. 

It was only after I got used to these orbs that they began to shift into
something more fluid. Larger shapes began to form from the balls. Lumpy
blobs that eventually grew into clearer visions of raccoons, birds,
cats, and deer. 

I was fifteen when the rules of my strange tradition clicked for me. I
had just come off the high of sneaking my first joy ride in my dad’s old
corvette when I spotted a bloated fawn laying beside the dark country
stop sign. I gave God the sign and signed it off with a kiss of my
finger. Like magic, a tiffany blue fawn appeared next to the driver’s
side window. I couldn’t believe it. I thought at first it was the miller
lite making me see things. Of course now I know that wasn’t the case.

My teenage self though, having no experience with such vivid
apparitions, slowly extended a flat palm out to the creature, like one
would pet a horse. I remember waiting with baited breath to see if this
thing would tear off my hand but to my delight she bumped the top of her
head into my hand. The fog of her essence phased through my hand with no
give at all. I imagine it’s a warmer version of gliding your hand
through a cloud. I was mesmerized but didn’t push my luck. After one
long wave of my hand against the deer’s ephemeral body I began to drive
home. She trotted alongside me the rest of the drive, bucking into the
drivers side door during each stop to get more pets, which I happily
gave. It was only when I fully turned off the car that she vanished out
of existence. 

I walked away from that night feeling like I was living in a fairy tale.
A princess that could take an animal’s spirit on one last joy ride
before they ascended to heaven. Even with my mystical secret, my life
continued as normal. Everything was ordinary aside from my benevolent
driving passengers, which were truly the highlight of my life. I
listened to death metal with squirrels that sprang around the car as the
bass of my shitty speakers boomed. I hot boxed with alley cats that
enjoyed pretending I was a scratching post. It was this living series of
these beautiful memorable moments that culminated in the last drive of
my life. 

I wish I could say it was a grand road trip with all of my loved ones
but unfortunately it was just a commute home from my part time job at
the pizzeria. I was coasting along the freeway when I spotted a mass of
fur on the side of the road. I signed them off absentmindedly and as
soon as my lips parted from my finger a little raccoon was plopped in
the passenger seat of my old reliable Mini Cooper. 

Without much thought I reached my arm over to pat his head. To my
surprise, the air had some give. I could feel his soft wet fuzz in
between my finger tips. I couldn’t press my hand through him. He was
tangible. My body tensed up a bit as the realization of a wild animal
being loose in my car set in. 

“Hey…little buddy…” I said weakly. The critter lunged for my face and
tore into the meat on my cheeks. I was met with a barrage of tiny claws
and dagger-like teeth. I instinctively reached up to cover my eyes. With
both my hands off the wheel I had to fully commit to yanking this
frenzied beast from my face. My car swerved along the roadway. In my
panicked state, I didn’t think to take my feet off the throttle.  I
stomped my feet on both the pedals in a desperate plea to control the
situation but none of it mattered, the thing held on like a face
hugger. 

I woke up from the ordeal in the passenger side of a police cruiser. I
felt nothing at all, calm even. I looked down at my hands and to my
surprise they emitted a faint blue light. I was translucent. I should
have been shocked by this but unreasonably apathetic to everything. I
took a chance to survey the scene before me. In front of the windshield
was a crumbled evergreen mini and a barely dented semi truck. An officer
was taking notes as a middle aged man with a beer belly frustratedly
described his side of the story. This was jarring, sure, but what
grabbed my attention the most was the parade of teal animals stretched
out along the rolling hills beyond the rumbling roadway. Hundreds of
deer, squirrels, cats, raccoons, possums young and old were frolicking
amongst themselves. I watched as a group of pearlescent blue foxes
stretched out. Downward dog first then they shook out each appendage
with the same gusto as a freshly wet dog. 

I stared out at hordes of deer as they leaped and bounded up the
hillside to my right. My eyes followed them up the hill until they
caught a consistent, unmoving glow from the rear view mirror. To my
horror, I was met with the back seat of the police cruiser. Stuffed in
the backseat was an amalgamation of hazy spirits sat with grumpy
expressions, pressed against one another like sardines in a tin. If
you’re picturing a crowded New York subway you’d need to ramp that image
up by ten. Body on top of ineffable body writhed against one another in
a fruitless attempt to get more space. 

I locked eyes with the soul of an old woman, who pleaded to me in
Spanish for help, when I realized the rules of the ritual I had taken up
over a decade ago. I had one drive to myself before joining the crowd in
the backseat. The realization hit all at once like how I imagine the
sensation of splatting against the semi must have felt. 

The officer hopped back into the idling car after the truck driver drove
off. I immediately reached out to grab him, lunging for him like the
raccoon had to me. I was mortified to watch the officers’ nonexistent
reaction as my hand phased through his cheek like it was nothing. I
shrieked in his ear. When that garnered no reaction I began kicking and
screaming, shoving him, punching him, waving my arms in front of his
face until we pulled into the police station. 

As he pulled into a parking spot all I could do was slump down in my
seat and cry. I tried to savor my last moments before joining the
claustrophobic tangle I had subjected my old passengers to.
