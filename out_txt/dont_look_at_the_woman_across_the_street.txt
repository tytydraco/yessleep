Despite it being the peak of summer, Slovakia maintained a cool
temperature. Although Slovakia is not the usual vacation spot, one
particular couple thought otherwise. The town was small, and not a
tourist ridden area, so not only was it affordable, but the area had a
sort of charm to it. At least, it did for Lydia.

“I can’t believe you didn’t make food for the both of us for a fourteen
hour flight.”

Lydia almost didn’t hear her husband at first, staring out of the cab
window in awe. Fields of tall grass stretched for miles, small abandoned
castles looming over the horizon. The cab drove closer to the quaint
town up ahead. Tall buildings made of yellow bricks and mud, cathedral
churches that had ivy growing over it. Not regarding the other cars and
the modern clothing on the pedestrians, it looked like a set piece for a
historic film. Finally processing her husband’s words she looked back at
him.

“I mean, that’s kinda selfish, no? Making your own sandwich and not
making one for me?” Simon spoke again, arms folded and bottom lip jutted
out obscenely as he glared out the window.

Lydia sighed. “You said yourself the flight served food, I just wanted
something extra in case I started getting nauseous. You were very
welcome to bring your own snacks or food.”

“Well you could’ve easily made an extra one for me. But no, it’s always
about you and your comfort. Even this damn trip is for you. We haven’t
had a vacation since our honeymoon, and you wanted to go here of all
places? It’s summer and it feels like Autumn here.” Simon huffed.

Lydia frowned deeply. “I’ve always wanted to come here since I was a
child, my great grandparents were from here, you know that. Besides,
originally it was just going to be my sister and I before you insisted
on coming with me instead.”

“Sure as hell I am, not letting my wife go to a different country on her
own…”

Lydia sighed deeply, knowing she would not win this argument. “Do you
always have to act like a child…?” She muttered, but Simon heard, much
to her regret.

“Damn you,” Simon spat. “You’re always the victim aren’t you? Fucking
hell, I can’t ever catch a break can I? I try my hardest ya know? Men
these days are always cheating and shit but I have stayed loyal for ten
whole years. But no, you don’t ever seem to recognize the good things I
do—”

Simon’s rant was abruptly cut off when the cab driver lit a cigarette.
The couple looked forward. A stern older looking man. Lydia noted to
herself that he looked like he could own a lighthouse. The man had long
wild hair, a silver beard that matched in length and a well kept
mustache. His skin was pale and weather beaten. He seemed to have a
consistent look on his face, very serious. Although, Lydia caught his
gaze, he was looking back at the bickering couple awkwardly a few times
through the rear view mirror.

Simon seemed like he was about to make a comment on the cigarette, but
his wife beat him to it. Lydia leaned forward, intrigued by this
interesting character. “Sir? Sorry but I have to say, you look like
you’re something straight out of a mystery novel.”

Simon glared at her. “Lydia, don’t disturb the man by saying such silly
things—”

However, Simon was cut off once again by a earnest laughter from the
older man. His shoulders bobbed up and down with the pressure of such a
hearty laugh. “A mystery novel?” The driver repeated, his English was
good, but his accent was thick and his voice deep. “Eh, first time I
heard sum’ like that.”

Lydia smiled slightly, the smoke from his cigarette curling around her
features. “I mean it in a good way! It means you have character.”

“Ah very good! I aim to please.” Once the man had started speaking, his
stern expression melted into a one of warmth. “First time in the great
Slovakia?” He asked.

“Yes actually! My ancestry is Slavic.”

“I see, I knew ya seemed familiar.” The driver joked. “My name’s Goran.
If you need driven anywhere, the hotel has my number.”

The cab came to a stop in front of an old building. Deep carvings etched
into the walls, with moss growing vibrantly all the way from the bottom
to the top.

“Oh, nice choice Lydia, did you purposefully choose the most dilapidated
building here?” Simon grunted as he got out of the cab. Lydia took a
deep breath and followed suit, with Goran helping with the baggage.
Simon went inside to check into the front desk with a quick stride,
Lydia lingered back with Goran.

“I’m sorry if my husband bothered you at all… Just had a long flight.”
Lydia rubbed her hands together to gather some warmth to the chill the
evening brought.

Goran shrugged, still huffing on his cigarette. “Men like him are not
uncommon, long flight or not.”
A pause settled over them before Lydia nodded, her hands wringing
together more from nerves now. “Well, thank you for driving us, I’m sure
we will call you tomorrow to… go somewhere.”

Her gaze went from him to the building right across the street from the
hotel. This structure looked more decrepit than the hotel. Where the
hotel’s appearance had a charm to it, the building across the street
looked like it could topple over any second. It was a farmhouse, with
rotted wood and two stories, not as tall as the hotel obviously, but
bigger than an average farm house. Not to mention it didn’t quite fit
with it’s environment, the town a bit more modernized. It was almost
like the house had time traveled from the past and landed here,
awkwardly squished between newer houses, where it should belong in a
field with an animal farm.

It was only a quick glance over, but Goran noticed her look. “I see the
old farm house has caught your attention.” He hummed, looking at the
building alongside her. Before Lydia could ask any questions, he
answered them. “This building was here even before I was on this Earth.
Anytime they try to tear it down, strange occurrences happen with
construction workers dying from debris falling on them. So, they just
kept it here. Not to mention, the woman inside.”

Goran paused, a dramatic stretch of silence laid over them. Lydia
chuckled softly. “Ooo, I love a good folklore story.” However when she
looked at the man who had warmed up over time, his face was scrunched up
once again, his wrinkles accentuated the grim look on his face.

“’Suppose it’s whatever you want to believe… but promise me this. Do not
look at the woman. Do not look at the windows. Especially the windows on
the second floor. Just mind yer business. Give me your word. I wouldn’t
want to see a nice couple getting mixed up in such things.”

Lydia hesitated, only because she felt uncomfortable with the sudden
tone shift. Then, she nodded. “I promise.”

Goran stomped out his cigarette, saying his farewells and continued on
his way. Lydia went into the hotel, only to see her impatient husband
standing there.

“Took you long enough, they want to check your passport.” After Lydia
checked in, they walked down the carpeted halls. Lydia recounted Goran’s
story when they got in the elevator. Once she finished, Simon snorted.

“And let me guess, you actually believe him?” Simon pushed forward once
they got to the second story, Lydia following after him a couple steps
behind.
“I mean…” Lydia hesitated, now feeling foolish. “He seemed really
serious.”

Simon let out an exaggerated laugh, pausing to revel in his amusement
before unlocking the hotel room. “You’re so naive. He knows you’re a
tourist so he’s probably just messing with you.”

Lydia decided to drop the topic, the exhaustion from their long flight
getting to her. That night, the couple stayed in, watching a movie on
his laptop as they lounged in bed, slowly drifting in and out of sleep.
That was before Lydia woke herself up at a chilling realization.

“Wait, we are on the second story right? Goran specifically said to
beware of the second floor to that farm house…”

Simon hung his head back against the bed frame, groaning loudly.
Silently, he got up and strode over to the window. Lydia’s eyes widened
as she straightened up quickly.

“Simon! Don’t look!”

But Simon already opened the curtains. Lydia watched with bated breath,
Simon’s expression unreadable. From where Lydia sat, the curtains still
blocked her view from the outside. But she could see the colors of the
sun slowly setting. Then, he finally managed a sound. “Huh.”

“Huh? Huh what? What do you see?” Lydia demanded in a panicked tone.

Simon paused again, and before Lydia was about to demand an answer, he
spoke slowly. “There is a woman… but, she’s not doing anything.”

Lydia searched his face, trying to decipher whether he was lying or not.
“Are you pulling my leg or are you actually serious?” Her voice was
unyielding, and Simon scoffed.

“Of course I’m not fucking lying. Come look for yourself.”

“Uh, no way. I don’t mess with that stuff, real or not. Just, tell me
what she looks like.”

Lydia saw his squint for a second before answering. “Well… she’s wearing
like, a shawl, with uh… what do you call the things that cover your
head?” He gestured vaguely to his own head, his eyes not leaving the
window.

“A babushka?” Lydia asked.

“Yeah, that… but uh, I can’t see much else, she’s facing away from me.
The room is completely empty it seems… and she’s just staring at a wall.
She hasn’t even moved…”

Lydia finally strode over, grabbing his arm and yanking him away from
the window. She quickly shut the curtains, keeping her gaze glued to the
floor. She swung around and glared at him. “I told you not to look! But
no, you always have to prove me wrong don’t you?”

Simon blinked, as if waking up from a daze. His face quickly contorted
into anger once her words processed. “This doesn’t prove anything! It’s
probably just a squatter, someone saw her and started a rumor.”

The couple bickered until the adrenaline burned them out. Simon fell
asleep easily, whereas Lydia laid awake for a while. However, as soon as
her eyelids began grow heavy, she heard her husband talking. It was
common for Simon to snore, but in all ten years of their marriage, Lydia
had never heard him sleep talk. She jolted upright.

“Simon? Did you say something?”

She leaned closer, trying to hear what he was murmuring. To her horror,
he wasn’t speaking English. He was speaking Slavic. Lydia could only
understand a few words, like “wait” and the question “what is your
name?”. Lydia knew for a fact that he did not know any Slavic, he was
barely wanting to learn the basics. How could he speak so fluently?

Lydia couldn’t sleep after that. She immediately grabbed her laptop,
researching anything she could on this town and that farm house. The
only thing she could find was the building was historic, which anyone
would know by just looking at it.

Lydia stayed up all night, trying to wake up Simon to no avail. There
was one word he kept repeating, a well known word in several languages.

“Mama… mama…” He whimpered all night.

Once the sun had risen, Simon jumped up from bed, rushing to the
bathroom and projectile vomiting. That day was spent inside the hotel
room. Lydia took care of him, and she asked about his dreams. He simply
said he couldn’t remember.

The couple didn’t talk about why he could be sick. It could be several
reasons, being in a different country prone to new illnesses was
probable, or even post-travel sickness. But there was another
explanation that the husband and wife did not want to say out loud, even
if it frequently came into their thoughts.

Lydia kept checking on him as he writhed and squirmed in bed, groaning
constantly. A cold sweat had formed on his skin, anytime he ate it would
immediately exit in one way or another. And to be blunt, he smelled like
soiled diapers.

At some point, Lydia had to go out of the room to get some food for
herself and Simon. And to catch a break. She must have looked like a
mess, tired and hungry, but she didn’t rightly care. The cafeteria had
an all you could eat buffet, she waited in line, a bit shaky. Ahead of
her in line was two Slavic women talking in English. Lydia was spacing
out until their words registered to her brain. Lydia glanced up, they
looked like they were going to a funeral, wearing all black and fancy
dresses.

“Did you hear about the farm house across the street? Pretty spooky
huh?”

“Eh? I guess, when babushka was alive she always told that same story
over and over again. It got boring at some point, I mean, in a small
town like this, that’s really the only exciting thing about it. It’s
been milked dry for years. Glad I finally moved out of this damn town.”

“Well at least you have one exciting thing! In my town the most
interesting thing is that there’s pheasant who’ll sneak into your house
at night to steal your socks!” The women laughed.

Lydia built up her courage, fixing her hair and wrinkly clothes before
tapping one of their shoulder’s. “Excuse me, I’m sorry to bother but… do
you think you could tell me more about the house across the street? The
story I mean?” The women looked back at her, a hint of judgment on their
face as they looked over Lydia. “I’m just a tourist and really into
folklore.” She explained quickly, flushing at their piercing stares.

The women glanced at each other, one woman shrugged. “Sure. There’s
different versions to it… but I believe the most popular one was a story
of a woman with an abusive husband. Apparently, the woman had a baby, a
girl. The husband didn’t want a girl, so at night he threw the baby into
a river. When the woman found out, she was hysterical. In a fit of rage,
she killed her husband. Now, I’m not sure if this last part is true, but
when the villagers went to check on them, they were both found dead, the
husband’s corpse dressed in a baby’s outfit, his body so decomposed that
he could fit in them. They found the woman cradling him in her lap in
the middle of the room.”

“What… what happens if you do look at the woman?” Lydia’s voice cracked.

“Uh… I didn’t know anyone personally who looked at her. The bodies were
found on the second floor, so from what I heard, the only way you can
see her is from the second floor of this hotel. But I heard whoever does
look, goes missing shortly after.” She shrugged. “Usually with ghost
tales, as long as you have holy water you’re safe or whatever. But… from
what I know, once you look at her, you’re done for.”

They exchanged a couple more words before Lydia rushed back to the room
with the meals. Lydia sighed in relief to see her husband wasn’t
missing, in fact, he hadn’t moved at all. When she relayed this
information to the bed ridden Simon, he just grunted tiredly.

“You’re working yourself up.” He grumbled tiredly. “I’ll get over this
sickness as long as I rest up and keep taking medicine… you’re only
making me worse with your stress.” Simon grunted.

Lydia began to cry. She grasped his hands, then, she prayed. Simon
looked surprised, they weren’t religious, nor did they talk about
religion much. After a moment, he wrenched his hands away. “Jesus Lydia!
Get a hold of yourself! Here you go again, making yourself the victim.
I’m the one in pain aren’t I?” He sighed deeply as her tears fell
heavier. “Look, we’re both tired okay? I can see you need rest too. Get
cleaned up and go to bed alright?” He turned over in bed. “And get me
some warm water while you’re at it.”

Lydia quickly made her way to the bathroom, slamming the door shut. As
she began to change and drew a hot bath, she found herself relaxing
slightly. Perhaps she was just making a bigger deal out of this than it
actually was. Maybe she was just fatigued. Maybe some part of her was
tired of taking care of her husband constantly and worrying for him.

After her bath, she checked on Simon one more time, seeing some warmth
come back to his cheeks as he slept peacefully. In fact, his cheeks had
grown a bit… chubby? No, perhaps they were just swollen. Since Simon had
claimed the bed, Lydia set up a space on the couch. Despite being a big
uncomfortable, she fell asleep, her exhaustion finally winning out on
her.

Lydia didn’t know what time she woke up, all she knew was it was dark
outside. Slowly sitting up, she checked on Simon in bed. She felt
around, only to feel the cool sheets against her fingers. Her sleepy
self quickly woke up as she panicked, rummaging around the sheets.
“Simon? Simon!” She hurried to the bathroom, but he wasn’t there either.
Immediately she went downstairs to the staff, asking if they had seen
her husband.

But Lydia was such a mess, and with the staff mostly speaking Slavic,
they could only offer clumsy comfort to the poor woman trying to speak
but her words only breaking into rushed out mumblings and choked sobs.

A staff member must have called someone to translate, because Goran
walked into the lobby. “Oh dear, what happened to ya?” When he was only
met with panicked words and hyperventilating gasps, he gently led her
outside to get some fresh air. “Lean on me if you need to, try to focus
on repeating my breathing, okay?”

After calming down, Lydia took a deep breath. “Simon, my husband… he’s
gone…”

Goran raised an eyebrow. “Was his stuff still in the hotel?”

“Y— Yes but… I don’t know… you’ll think I’m crazy…”
Suddenly, the silent night was broken by a sound.

Both Goran and Lydia snapped towards the source, their gaze landing on
the house across the street. A curdling wail echoed the empty streets,
so loud, so anguished. A baby’s cry. So wet and such a disturbing sound,
crying out, “Mama! Mama!”

“Oh fuck…” Goran muttered, then turned to face Lydia. “Your husband
looked at the woman, didn’t he?”
