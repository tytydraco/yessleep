[Translation: Hello. I am speaking to you in mime. My transformation is
complete! I passed the test in case you were wondering. And I expect to
be seeing you all when I play at Radio City this summer. They’ll fold
soon. Anyway that’s all I’ve got. This here is the last letter. Read it.
And after that I’m sure you’ll want to read letter one and two and three
and four and five too.]

Judge,

It’s snowing now. Is it snowing where you are? Well I guess even if it
is it could have stopped by the time you get this. Because I’m writing
you a letter. It started last night I think. Or possibly two nights ago.
It doesn’t matter exactly really. It’s snowing still. It hasn’t stopped
since. Now everything is blanketed in this thick layer of soft white. It
looks like there are little flecks of glitter in it when the sun shines
upon it and it almost hurts to look at. 

My house is chilly now. I’m  really quite cold. The door situation
doesn’t help one bit.

Have you ever let the snow dig into your bare skin? It’s an awful
feeling. You put it in your palms and it melts and your hands get numb
until they sting. The same thing happens if your socks aren’t long
enough. You get the sensation in a ring around your ankle. I seldom go
out when it’s snowing outside because of it. 

You think it would stop them from running out in the street. Some of
them are barefoot. Their feet must be pulsing by now. They’re rampaging.
It might be everyone in the neighborhood. They’re all very scared.
They’re breaking windows and they’re crawling out with TVs and
microwaves and toasters. I’m not sure how any of it helps.

They collected in the street earlier. When they were all together it was
sort of like a parade. It wasn’t a bad show. They’ve broken up now.

Do you wanna know why they’re doing all this? It’s because of the
headline in today’s paper:

GOVERNMENT COMPUTERS DETECT MORE THAN 2,000 SOVIET NUKES HEADED TOWARDS
AMERICA

This is only the headline. It seems no one had bothered to read the
whole thing. This actually happened two days ago. It was a glitch in
computers that lasted for six minutes only. And they’re still going.
They’re still outside. I can hear them shuffling through the snow.

I only hope I can make it out of this safe. It’s taking great effort to
steady my hand enough to write as I sit here curled under my desk.

Anyway  don’t believe I finished telling you about the night I killed
the bear with my last letter. I think you could tell it stretched on
longer than that. I thought stopping it there would make for a mighty
fine cliff hanger. I’m sure I got you!

Oh and by the way I’m sorry if my handwriting is still a bit messy. I
haven’t gone to the doctor for the rib or the arm. Here you go now:

As you’ll remember I was standing over the bear with its head broken
open and I was looking down at bits of brain matter floating up in its
skull. I was a bit shocked that I had actually killed it then. I really
hadn’t planned. I guess from there the rest of the night was just a big
scramble to finish up. 

Admittedly I had frozen for a minute there in the river. There was so
much stuff I’d planned on smoothing out later but with the bear dead
there was no time. I had to conduct it all immediately.

You should know already that I’ve planned to stuff the bear. But in
order to do that I’d first have to secure and transport its corpse. In
the little research I had time to conduct I settled for a utility
trailer since I wasn’t strong enough to carry it all on my own. But I
never had the time to secure the trailer or a car.

I crawled out of the forest. It was quite the struggle given my newly
broken rib but I had to act fast or I’d never have the bear stuffed. 

Fortunately I know right where the river is. I walk over it quite often
so I wasn’t lost. I knew there was a bridge just down the stream. It
actually wound up being much closer than I thought it would be. So when
I crawled up I wasn’t lost fortunately. Though I still had some distance
to travel.

When I came round the street it was all still. I could hardly see in
front of me. The streetlights on the side of the road did seldom to
help. All was dark around me. And I heard through the dark this little
noise. Like a little staccato crunch. It sounded rhythmically and there
was metallic rumbling in between each time.

I found the source of the noise before long. Down the street and in
front of their house my neighbor’s twins were kicking a can through the
street. They noticed me.

“Hey mister,” one of them said.

“What are you two doing out so late?” I asked.

“Is it late? I didn’t think it was that late.”

“Do you have any idea what time it is?”

“No. Do you?”

“Well. No.”

“How do you know it’s late then?”

“Yeah,” the other one piped up. “You know how early it gets late out in
the winter?”

“It’s summer,” his brother said.

“Oh.”

“Uh. Never mind all that,” I continued. “What exactly are you doing?”

“We’re playing kick the can mister.”

“Kick the can?”

“Yeah. Wanna know how to play? We can tell you.”

“Sure,” I said.

“Alright. You gotta get two people. Then you got a can. Like this one
right here. And then you kick it. Kick it back and forth.”

“Is there more?” I asked.

“No,” said the other boy. “That’s it.”

“Oh.”

“It’s a lot of fun if you’re able to get it right. Wanna play?”

“No thanks,” I said. 

“Alright. Good night.”

I began to walk away. I was a little irritated with those boys for
waiting my time. But just before I left the sight I realized something.
I turned around.

“I do have the question though.”

“Hm?” said the boy who told me how early it gets dark in the winter.

The boy’s brother came up and elbowed him.

“Come on Jackson. Mom says we’re not allowed to talk to strangers.”

“Oh. Sorry.”

“No no,” I said. “I’m not trying to give you anything. It’s just a
question about your parents.”

“What is it?” said the other boy.

“Well. I just wanted to know. Does you dad–or your mom–do either of them
have a utility trailer in the garage? Or attached to the back of their
car.”

“What’s that?”

“Oh. You know. You attach it to the back. It’s real big and flat. It’s
got it’s own set of wheels. And you put stuff on it.”

“Does it have walls?”

“Some do I guess.”

“I think you’re asking about a wagon sir. It’s red and it says radio fly
on its side.”

“No no no. Not that. It’s bigger. It’s like an adult wagon. You attach
it to the back of a car. An adult car”

“We attach our radio fly to dad’s car all the time,” said Jackson.

“You’re not supposed to.”

“Oh.”

“Now.” I turned to his brother. I believe his name may have been Jonah.
“Do you know what I’m talking about?”

“I think so. It says radio fly on the side. Dad always keeps one of them
on the back of his car. Look.” He pointed to the driveway. “He’s got it
on there right now.”

I looked up. It was a utility trailer attached to the back of their dads
car. A crudely attached metal plate had the words “Radio Fly” scratched
into it. It wasn’t red though.

“Is that it?” Jonah asked.

“Yes,” I said. “That’s it.”

I required some help so as I was retrieving the utility trailer I asked
them to come along. They were the nearest and potentially only option
and they obliged for some reason. I promised I would bring them home
afterwards. I held to that promise in case you were wondering.

Amongst the junk their dad had left in the trailer was a rope. I tied it
to that little hook thingy that connects the trailer to the car and used
that to the trailer and I left the rest of the stuff in their lawn and
put it back in the trailer later. It was pretty heavy(not that I was
having much issue). I brought them to the river and parked it and Jonah
asked me:

“What are we out here for? It’s late anyway.”

“You’re gonna help me put something on the trailer. It’s quite heavy so
I’m going to need your help.”

“Then we can go home?”

“Yes.”

Jackson said: “What are we moving?”

“I’ll show you two. Follow me. It’s just past these trees.”

I parsed a branch and we all went through with the trailer dragging
behind us. It trampled a few shrubs and weaker trees but I’m sure their
replacements will go back soon. In a few moments the bear came into view
with its brain still open and a few flies buzzing around it already.

“Woah,” they both said as I swatted the flies away. “Is that a bear?”

“Yeah. I need your help moving it to the street.”

“Wait…” began Jonah. “How come we have to move him? Why don’t we just
ask him nicely?”

“Because he’s dead. Are you done?”

“Yeah.”

“So we’re gonna have to pick up a bear?” said Jackson.

“I just said that.”

“Bears are pretty heavy. My dad can lift pretty much everything but I’ve
never seen him carry a bear.”

“Well. I’m sure together we can lift more than your dad.”

“Oh. That’s good.”

“Is that all?” I paused. It was all. “Alright. Follow me.”

Fortunately by bringing the trailer I’d vastly shortened the distance
we’d have to carry the bear ourselves. That didn’t make it any less
difficult to pick it up but it did help.

So we all gathered round the bear. The two boys were at the front and I
was going to hold up the back. I counted down from three and we all
lifted. My whole body was shaking as we made the few steps from the
river to the trailer. I could especially feel it near my ribs. One of
them is most definitely broken. It was also quite apparent that the boys
were hardly putting any effort in and that didn’t help at all.

We plopped the bear onto the trailer(it was mostly me doing the pushing)
and I had take a moment to handle the pain in my chest. The trailer was
much heavier now but dragging the bear behind me was much easier than
carrying it.  So the boys followed me as I pulled it up to the street
and then I let them go on home.

You already know this but before returning the bear to my home and going
to bed I decided I ought to to go put in my order for the taxidermy shop
and then once that was done I lugged the bear into my home and put the
utility trailer back on just as the sun rose and fell into my bed
finally and slept peacefully until it rose again the next day.

That was all months ago now. The taxidermy store never contacted me the
whole time. I actually went ahead and canceled the order last night. Now
that should lead me to today but in the months since then I’ve racked up
quite the count of grievances (especially with this bear’s corpse) that
I really feel the need to air out right now.

It really stinks. I guess that ought to be expected though. It really
knocked me out the first day but I would’ve been prepared for it had I
not been in such a daze. It’s also quite cumbersome. There’s just this
big lump on my floor. Right before my back door. And it’s too heavy to
move.

Those things aren’t real issues. I mean they still tick me a little but
I can live with them and I have been anyway. I have two real issues with
it.

The first might just be how it looks. I guess it’s face. I thought maybe
I’d get used to it but I never did. The bowl that became of its skull
never dried up. The juice is still there. It’s sort of pink. It ripples
often whenever my floor vibrates which happens to be quite often. And
the bits of brain still float up in it. They’ve gone stale now. Sort of
shriveled up like raisins of meat. They bob up and down when the juice
ripples. 

It was so unappealing in fact that I tried sticking a football in the
hole once. I figured it ought to patch it up and it ought to fit in
there just right. It was the same color as the bear’s fur. The holes in
its head seemed big enough too. I acquired one which the neighbor’s kids
tossed into my back yard and left there. The idea actually sprung once
I’d seen it still there the fourth night and the row and realized they
probably wouldn’t have minded if I took it.

Anyway I stood over the bear and just kind of shoved it in there. It
didn’t go as I expected. The juice splattered everywhere. It got all
over the bear’s face. It got all over my face. It drenched my clothes.
It was cold. My shirt was stained with a plink splotch which remains to
this day. It has since gone stale.

And once I had made room from the juice I went ahead and kept shoving
the football in there. I imagined it would slide in easily but it really
wasn’t budging. It was making this wet farting noise each time I pushed
and I was really starting to get irritated.

I brought the football out of the bear’s head. It was dripping and
shriveled bits of brainmatter were sticking to it. A few peeled off and
fell down in the puddle of brain juices that was already forming on my
floor. I realized looking in that I had made contact with the bear’s
complete brain. Or what was left of it anyway. I was aware I had taken
out a frontal chunk but I was not aware just how much was intact up
until that moment. I had squished it with the football. It was so flat
like a pink little pancake in the bottom of its cranial hole. 

Anyway I wedged it in there tight and it wasn’t any easier than it was
before. I made sure the part with the white stitch was showing. I
thought it was cute. Like a frankenbear. Only it was still dead.

I got it in as deep as it could go. I guess it was a bit big for what
I’d expected. It sort of pokes out of the bear’s head where I’d expected
it to blend in but oh well. I can’t get it out now.

It didn’t help anyway. It still looked ugly as ever. There was this pink
and squishy rim around the hole where the ball protruded now which you
could see over it anyway. And the hole had adopted the shape of the
ball. Now it really looked like a vagina. So I just covered it with a
towel. Now I don’t have to look at his face anymore.

But that’s not even the true worst of it. I still hear it at night. I
still hear the bear. And I know you’d tell me based on that alone that
there are in fact multiple bears and I am well aware of that but I know
for a fact this isn’t a different bear. The noise doesn’t come from my
garden. I hear it in my living room.

Yes. It treats my house like it treated my garden. It treats it like the
woods. It sniffs around and lugs its massive body about the place
without a care. And of course it still scratches wood. Each morning when
I wake up I find new strikes through these walls. Oftentimes I can make
out legible messages. Frequently it lobs accusations at me. I’d rather
not repeat them.

It was like this just last night. I couldn’t sleep. I haven’t been able
to since that night I brought it in. And this morning when it was time
to get out of bed my entire living room was in disarray. Scathing
messages scratched into my walls. At my feet as I stood in the bedroom
doorway there was a little smashed VHS with the black tape sprawled all
over my rug.

Judge I have to admit something now. I’ve been going back to the grocery
store a lot more lately and heading to the back and meeting up with that
sleazy man and watching his tapes in that dark room. I swore I’d never
return and yet I found myself so easily doing so. Isn’t it funny how
that works? 

It’s become a nightly habit of mine. Sometimes I run over there in the
morning. The selection of videos has remained the same. This includes
the one I told you about. The one with Popeye. The man still leaves it
there though he told me he’d remove it. I don’t even mind all that much
anymore when it comes on.

I try to keep all our interactions highly impersonal and usually he
obliges but one time he just insisted on speaking to me.

“You know I used to run a movie theater,” he said. “You know.”

“I actually didn’t know that.”

“Well you do now.”

“…”

“It used to be pretty popular. And we showed the same stuff I’ve got
back here. One day people just stopped showing up. I had to relocate.”

“…”

“Turns out letting people view this stuff alone makes them come around
more often. I actually recognize some of the guys who come by often from
when they used to show up with their buddies. They come alone now.”

“You get other customers?”

“Of course I do. You think you’re the only one?”

That conversation sort of rubbed me the wrong way so the next time I
went by I pocketed one of his tapes before I left and brought it home.
And Judge when I was going home after that I noticed something odd. You
know the streets that lead me to the store are just by the forest. I’m
sure you’ve been around a forest. They’re always humming with sounds of
life. Always the crunch of leaves or the buzz of some bugs. But on that
day I heard nothing. Real and pure silence so quiet I could hear my own
saliva dripping down my throat. And there was no other sound.

I’m actually not too miffed this tape was destroyed. I don’t own a TV
anyway though I’d certainly like to. When this fact forced me to head
back I watched them all and realized which one I’d taken. Really I’m
more offended the bear dug through my home so intrusively like this.

Well all that leads me to today I guess. You already know half of it.
The whole time I’ve been writing this people have still been doing as I
told you. I’ve been under this desk a while longer than it takes for two
thousand nukes to make contact and you’d think they’d have realized this
by now but they’re still going.

In fact some of them were in my backyard just earlier. They had this
conversation:

Voice 1: “Wait a minute. This guy’s still in his house.”

Voice 2: “He’s not scared of the bomb?”

Voice 1: “No. Doesn’t seem like it.”

Voice 2: “Maybe he planned it.”

Voice 3: “You mean he’s Stalin?”

Voice 2: “Stalin’s dead. The reds have a new guy.”

Voice 3: “Oh.”

Voice 1: “Well. I guess I mean he helped plan it. I think he’s working
with them. He’s working with the communists.”

Voice 2: “That makes sense.”

Voice 3: “Yeah. Come to think of it I don’t even see him around much.”

Voice 2: “Then he has to be a communist.”

Voice 3: “We oughta do something about this. Let’s charge ’im.”

Voice 1: “Wait. We don’t know what machines he’s got. We need to
prepare.”

Voice 2: “Good idea.”

Voice 3: “Yeah.”

And so they all scampered off through the snow. I could see them through
the window. Even through the snow I could tell they were trampling my
bushes. I could recognize one of their voices as my neighbor’s. The one
with the fake lawn. And all three of them were waiting for the bomb’s
arrival out in the open.

I think I ought to tell you now Judge that this is the last letter I
will ever write you. You’ll never get another one from me. No one will.
I’ve made the decision just recently. This is actually the reason I’ve
cancelled my taxidermy order.

I’m giving up Judge. I’m giving up on people. It feels so good to say
that. Or to write it. It feels good to leave it all behind finally.

You remember where we met Judge. I’m sure you do. I’m talking about the
plane but I guess we met far before that. We were part of the same squad
but I never introduced myself. You and I were the only survivors of our
poor squad. One of those boys–I can’t even recall his name–put out a
radio signal without encoding it. I’m sure you’ll recall. A lot of it
from there is a blur and I can only assume that it’s for a good reason.
I woke up hours later in a body bag in a morgue.

We didn’t talk until the plane though when the war was over and we were
all going home. You were the only familiar face around and even though
I’d never got your name I sat next to you. You turned to me as we lifted
off and said:

“You heard about John Kerry?”

“Who?”

“Kerry. John Kerry.”

“No. Who is he.”

“Soldier. He won three purple hearts.”

“What’s that?”

“Come on. You know what a purple heart is.”

“I don’t.”

“Oh. Well. Basically you get one for being injured. It’s just a little
ribbon I guess.”

“Oh. That must’ve hurt. Wouldn’t wanna be like him.”

“If you get three you go home. He went home before us.”

“Then we should’ve got injured before.”

“Damn right.”

“Did you get any purple hearts?”

“One.”

“Did I?”

“How am I supposed to know?”

“Would they tell me if I did?”

“Yeah. I think they would,” you answered.

“Oh.”

“So who do you got waiting for you? Who are you excited to see back

“Uh. Parents I guess. A friend or to from back in school if they still
care.”

“Girlfriend?”

“No. I don’t have one.”

“Oh. Well I’ve got one. Man am I glad to see her again. She’s great. Got
a brother too. Can’t wait to see that little punk.”

“Sounds like she is.”

“You’ve never even heard about her.”

“Oh. Sorry.”

And we talked like that the whole flight home.

I know of many men from the military who shut out people completely
after their return. I knew it back then. Even from the wars of old. I’d
thought they were insane my whole life. But I realize now that they’d
always been right. Only now have I stopped denying.

One of the men on our plane they wheeled out in a chair. He was quite
injured. You and I were just behind him as they brought him home. I’d
expected it to look all pretty but the spot where we landed looked just
like where we’d just left from. There was a crowd of people before us
watching us come out the plane. He raised his hand and he smiled and he
gave them a peace sign. In response the entire crowd raised their own
fists in the air and uncurled their middle fingers. They held it as we
all walked out the plane. Until the very last man had come home.

In the years since I last saw you I’ve been fighting against this thing
in me. I tried very hard to get along with everyone here but I don’t
think it’s possible. I don’t know why. I don’t know who the problem is.
I feel like a tourist in my home town. I can’t speak my own language
without being bombarded. But I’ve been here my whole life.

I’m really just so tired of it all. It never changes. I realize that
now. I’ve only remained here under the pretense that it may someday
change and I’ve got no reason to abide by this now.

So I’ve taken the liberty to cut the bear open myself. Not to have her
stuffed. I’m going to crawl inside her. 

That’s another thing. I realize now it was a lady bear. I checked her
genitals. And as I was cutting her open I cut into her teats. Milk
sprinkled on my cheek then. I think now that perhaps I was a bit too
harsh. She was only doing what comes natural.

So anyway I’ve carved out this nice big cavity for me to fit in. I’ve
even patched up her head good as new. My arms control her arms and my
legs control her legs. My cheeks touch right up against her tonsils. I
can see and eat through her mouth. I feel comfortable inside. The meat
is nice and warm inside. It presses up against my rib and hurts terribly
but that’s not so bad. It’s simple to ignore.

In a moment Judge I will climb in the bear and sow it shut from the
inside and say goodbye to the human world. In a moment you will never
hear from me again. I can hear now pairs of feeting climbing through the
snow towards my house. In a moment they’ll see me. I doubt they’ll even
be able to see me fully before they die. They never could. And so it
shall be their end.

You might hear a bear coming round your own home. He may scratch against
your trees and sniff your beehives. That bear will be me. I am the bear
now. I only hope you understand Judge that I mean no harm. I’m only
visiting.
