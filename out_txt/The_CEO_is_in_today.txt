“The CEO is in today.”

I overheard that being spoken by a woman this morning, who was talking
with a man.

She wore a form-fitting navy blue dress and a black cardigan. Her makeup
was immaculate. He wore a perfectly fitted charcoal suit. His skin was
unblemished. Their clothes did not move with them.

“That is great to hear,” he said.

The two beautiful people entered offices off to the side and closed
their doors, presumably to do management things. My eyes strained as I
continued to look at my computer screen in the dim room, the work I had
been doing for the suddenly becoming rather difficult to do.

I had been with the company for four years, working a remote job I
couldn’t describe for people I could not see, with coworkers I could not
see. I had received one of those job offer texts while living on
unemployment, and been desperate enough to answer.

This morning, I had woken up to no power in my apartment, texted the
number that had given me the offer, and been texted an address that sent
me here, to a nondescript office in a nondescript building just a few
minutes from my place.

I looked around for a blind that I could open maybe, to let some light
in. That was when I realized that, despite being on the seventh floor,
there were no windows.

People continued to file into the room, sitting down at computers and
going to work. There was no sound of monitors turning on or passwords
being put in. They simply sat down and began typing in one fluid motion.
Eyes almost purely white in the glow of their screens.

The man and woman exited their offices in perfect tandem and looked at
each other.

“The CEO should be here soon,” she said.

“Perfect,” he said, “I’ve been meaning to speak with him.”

I looked around to see if anyone else had heard them. But no one had.
The sound of typing continued unceasingly, if not faster. I heard a
whine as something overworked itself.

A man sat next to me, his jaw clenched, sweat pouring down his face as
he typed frantically. Veins bulged in his face.

Twenty minutes passed, and yet I saw nothing indicating that a person
had entered the office. The sound of frenetic typing was now as though a
jackhammer was being pounded into my skull, but the people in the room
were completely silent.

Then the light flicked on in the conference room at the front, which I
had not seen anyone enter.

The perfect man in the suit approached me. I realized I was drenched in
sweat. But he gestured at the guy next to me. At some point, the guy had
clenched his jaw hard enough to break a tooth, because a thin line of
blood was now dripping from his mouth.

“The CEO has heard of your work. He would like to speak with you now.”

The guy nodded. He stood up, and in the brief moment when he pushed his
chair in I noticed a dark patch on his crotch and a corresponding line
running down the leg of his light chino pants.

He and the man walked into the conference room and shut the door. The
walls were solid, so I could not see nor hear what was going on.

There was silence for about ten minutes. Then a hum came from the room.
It lasted thirty seconds and was no more intense than the sound of a
hotel room air-conditioning unit turning on.

The door opened. The perfect man stepped out. The guy who had been next
to me did not. The perfect man carried a small black bag, containing
something wet that he either did not notice or did not care enough to
acknowledge was dripping. He dropped the black bag into a trash bin and
went back into his office.

No one else acknowledged this. I figured that was in my best interests
as well.

I realized I had not gotten any work done in the eleven minutes since
they had entered the conference room. I had been typing, but no words
had appeared on the screen.

The CEO was in today. I had better get back to work.
