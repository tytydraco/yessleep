I moved into the cheapest 1-bed in Seminole Heights last month. $950 in
this market. I knew something was wrong when I saw the listing had been
up for 198 days.

Mr. Hask met me in the parking lot. Didn’t go inside. Just handed me a
key with a green diamond keychain and said, “Don’t paint the walls. And
don’t acknowledge the second occupant.”

I laughed. “You mean like a roommate?”

He didn’t even blink. “I mean don’t talk to it. Don’t look at it
directly if you see it. Don’t leave out a second plate or any of that
polite bullshit people do. You ignore it, it mostly ignores you. Most
people last three months.”

He got in his truck and left.

The apartment was… fine. Too clean, actually. Like it had been deep
cleaned with bleach that morning. All the closets were empty except one
had a single wire hanger swinging slightly, even though the AC wasn’t
on.

First 5 days, nothing. On day 6, I started noticing the resetting.

My trash would be taken out. Not emptied - TAKEN OUT. Bag gone, new bag
in. My coffee mug would be washed and put back in the wrong cabinet. My
shoes by the door would be lined up perfectly straight when I always
kick them off crooked.

I thought Hask was coming in. I put a piece of tape on the door. Tape
was never broken.

Then the bathroom stuff started.

I’d shower at night. Wake up in the morning and the mirror would be
fully fogged. The bath mat would be damp. And there was a second
toothbrush in the holder. Cheap, clear plastic. Wet.

I threw it away. It was back the next morning. Wet.

I started sleeping like shit. Every night at 3:07 AM on the dot, I would
snap awake with that feeling that someone is standing right over you. My
bedroom is pitch black, but I could feel heat next to me in the bed.
Like someone had been lying there for hours.

On night 11, I couldn’t take it. I ordered a Wyze cam on Amazon. Stuck
it on top of my dresser, pointed at my bed.

The next morning, I made coffee and opened the footage at 2x speed.

11:23 PM - I get in bed. Fall asleep.

3:07 AM - The blanket on the OTHER side of my bed lifts up by itself.
Just… lifts. And sinks. The mattress dips with weight. You can see a
long indent form, about my height, right next to me.

It doesn’t move. It just lies there, facing me, for 6 hours.

9:14 AM - My alarm goes off. I sit up, get out of bed. I leave for work.

And the indent stays.

On the camera, in real time, at 9:15 AM while I’m in the kitchen
watching on my phone, the indent is still in the bed. The blanket is
still shaped like a person is under it.

I walked back into the bedroom, heart hammering so loud I could hear it.
The bed was perfectly made. No indent. But the sheets were warm.

That’s when I called Hask.

“Mr. Hask, what is in this apartment?”

Long pause. “You been acknowledging it?”

“No. I swear, I haven’t said anything to it.”

“Good. Keep it that way. And kid? Stop watching it. It doesn’t like to
be watched.”

He hung up.

That night I unplugged the camera. I tried to sleep facing the wall. At
3:07 AM I woke up and I could FEEL it sitting up. Staring at the back of
my head.

I broke.

I whispered without turning around, “What do you want?”

The air in the room went cold. Like freezer cold. And I felt something
lean down right next to my ear and it whispered back in a voice that
sounded exactly like mine, but like it had been practicing:

“Now I get to stay when you leave.”

I turned on the light. No one there. But my closet door was open. And
all my clothes were pushed to one side. The other side was empty, but
the wire hanger was gone. And there were clothes hanging there now.
Clothes that weren’t mine. A faded Tampa Bay Bucs shirt. Jeans that were
my size but not my brand.

The next morning I got an email. It was a lease renewal form. Already
filled out. For 12 more months. Signed with my signature. But not my
handwriting.

I called Hask screaming. He finally came over. He stood in the doorway
and wouldn’t come inside. He looked older.

He said, “Oh. It likes you.”

“What the fuck does that mean?”

“This unit has had a second occupant since ’89. Before my dad owned it.
It needs a first occupant to… anchor it. If you acknowledge it, it
learns you. Then when you try to leave, it doesn’t have to stay as the
second anymore.”

He slid an envelope across the floor to me. Inside was a stack of
photos. Polaroids of all the previous tenants. In every single one, they
were taking a photo of their empty apartment on move-out day. And in the
mirror, or the window behind them, there was a second person. Wearing
their clothes. Smiling with their face. A little bit off.

At the bottom of the stack was a Polaroid with no tenant in it. Just the
empty apartment. And one wire hanger swinging in the closet.

Hask said, “You got until noon to get your real stuff and go. Leave the
key. Don’t take the Bucs shirt. And whatever you do, don’t look back at
the bed when you leave.”

I’m in my car right now typing this on my phone. My real clothes are in
a trash bag in the trunk. I left at 11:58 AM. I didn’t look at the bed.

But my Wyze cam is still plugged in. And it just sent me a motion
notification.

It’s a clip of the apartment, empty, 12:03 PM. The closet door closes by
itself.

And then someone who looks exactly like me gets into the bed, under the
blanket, on the side I used to sleep on.

And the indent on the other side lifts up.
