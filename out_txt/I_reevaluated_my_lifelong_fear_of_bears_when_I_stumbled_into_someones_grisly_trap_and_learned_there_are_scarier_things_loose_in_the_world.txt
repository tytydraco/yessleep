As a child, I used to have nightmares of an enormous brown bear sneaking
up and eating me while I played in our backyard, as my dad stood smoking
a few feet away on the porch, completely unaware that I was being
mauled. I could only wake up when I either ran away and escaped into the
house, or when the bear ripped my throat out with its teeth. The dream
had convinced me that I could be safe, so long as I was vigilant and ran
fast, and also gave me a feeling of disgust towards smoking, smokers,
and cigarettes that lasted until the nightmares of childhood were
overcome by the temptations and indulgences of adulthood. But I could
never shake my uneasy, borderline paranoid, feeling about bears.

Now in my early thirties, I occasionally work up in the Rocky Mountains
and always keep my head on a swivel whenever I’m outdoors because of all
the stories I’ve heard from the locals. Tales of bears eating food from
campsites, breaking into houses, attacking pets in their backyards; as
well as the constant reminders like signs and bear proof trash cans kept
me from ever getting comfortable enough to drop my guard. Thankfully,
I’m not big on hiking, my work keeps me indoors, and I tend to stay in
rentals and hotels that are as far inside of town as is practical, so I
know that I am reasonably safe. But I knew that I was vulnerable when I
went outside to have a cigarette, usually once an hour. Smoking is an
unhealthy habit, but it is one of the vices in life that I can afford to
blow off some steam, and I liked to tell myself that keeps me in touch
with the outside world by forcing me out into it. This cope nearly
killed me before smoking could get the chance to.

My boss and I had finished work earlier than we had expected and ended
up back at our rental accommodations a little before five in the
afternoon. The rental in question was billed as being a ‘ground floor
condo in a ski resort with keypad access,’ but in actuality, it was more
of a hole in the wall apartment with a buzz-in system that forces guests
to rely on the front desk worker to let you inside the building. The
unit was partially subterranean, with the bottom of the two bedroom
windows sitting at ground level with the pavement, while the other rooms
were windowless and drab. We got through the foyer and walked down the
plain and roughly carpeted hallway, passing the communal laundry room,
to where our condo waited for us.

I changed into casual clothes and rushed back out of the building
through a side door to get my first hit of nicotine since our lunch
break and was sure to remind myself to take a closer look at rental
listings to avoid another place like this in the future. I lit up my
menthol-infused delicacy and began puffing the stresses of my day away.

The off-season lent the place little of its intended charm, and as such
there were few people around to watch; but I did notice that there was a
row of four small houses that faced my direction, standing across the
narrow street, with each house having a gated brick enclosure for
keeping their trash cans from scavenging bears. However, when I turned
my attention to the condo building’s dumpster, near where my boss parked
his car, I noticed that it was left out in the open and lacked any
obvious anti-bear measures. I field-stripped the last bit of my
cigarette as I approached and threw the butt into the dumpster. I was
thankful that I didn’t have to struggle to open the lid, akin to a
frustrated bear.

An hour and a half later, I stepped out again for my after-dinner smoke,
hoping to get away from my boss’s incessant discussion of work-related
topics. The phone reception in the mountains was exceptionally poor away
from the complimentary Wi-Fi, and so I turned my attention to the cars
in the parking lot. I noticed two cars with local tags were parked near
the side door where I stood, and three out-of-state tags were parked
near the front door. I ruminated as to why the out-of-towners found
themselves here and nearly missed the unmarked silver van that had
pulled in front of the building’s dumpster.

I feigned disinterest and watched as a rough looking guy, with unshaven
rugged features, wearing heavy hunting clothes got out of the driver’s
seat and struggled to lift a contractor bag out of the side cargo door.
I carelessly watched him struggle for a few moments before I felt the
urge to go and give him a hand. I stripped my cigarette and began the
short trek over, as he managed to get the bag up and over into the
dumpster on his own.

I stopped when he finally noticed me and paused for a moment, as though
I had caught him doing something illegal, or he was just genuinely
surprised to find a six-foot something man closing in on him. “How’s it
going?” The guy didn’t say anything; he fled back into the van and drove
away without closing the cargo door and didn’t stop until he was out of
sight. Naturally, I was suspicious, and I peered into the dumpster,
finding that the bag appeared stuffed solid with items of varying shape,
and that it thankfully showed no signs of blood. I picked up a small
chunk of dislodged cement and pelted the bag with my hardest throw, and
I was reassured by the crunching of small pieces of metal, instead of
the squishing of flesh. I discarded my butt and buzzed into the
building, being sure to stop and tell the lady at the front desk what I
had seen.

“It was probably one of the locals. We let them use our dumpster for
their overflow sometimes. I’m sure there’s nothing to worry about.” If
she was lying, she was good at it. I thanked her and warned her that
would likely be in and out several more times before the night was over.
“See you later.” She seemed annoyed that I would make her do her job a
few more times.

I returned to the condo and found my boss occupying himself with
answering emails in his bedroom, leaving me free to engage in my
favorite pass time of reading between acts of Law and Order reruns in
mine. While I would never smoke a cigarette indoors, I was sparingly
smoking a marijuana vape pen and discreetly exhaling out the window
which opened out to the parking lot on the opposite side of the building
from the main door. As a consequence, I didn’t step out again until
after eleven, when there was one less car in the parking lot. The
blackened isolation of night felt all-encompassing, with only the faint
yellow light of the two streetlamps that bookended the block keeping the
suffocating darkness at bay. I thought about the day I had ahead of me
and was winding down for bed, when I heard heavy breathing alongside the
sound of something digging inside the dumpster.

An icy feeling seized my body. I knew instantly that I was in my dreaded
nightmare scenario, and I was stuck out in the open with a wild bear. I
heard panting coming from behind the dumpster, accompanied by a rhythmic
pounding on the metal, as if the bear was trying to break a container
open, but I wasn’t eager to find out if I was right in my assumption. I
slowly crept to the front door and buzzed the front desk, while the
pounding behind me intensified, and the bear began growling in
frustration. The front desk still wasn’t answering my continual button
presses, and the bear was frenzied, sounding as if it were trying to
smash the dumpster into pieces.

“Let me in, damn it!” My voice echoed in the newfound silence of the
parking lot. I waited a few seconds, hoping that the bear had been
spooked off or was disinterested in me, but I knew I needed to look back
when the silence persisted.

The brown bear was standing on its hind legs, towering several inches
over me, with a pained expression on its snouted face that I had never
seen before; like anger was burning it from the inside out. Its eyes
locked onto mine, and my presence seemed to give it a respite from
whatever it was feeling. I was momentarily moved by the beast’s abrupt
change of heart before it snapped back to its previous ferocity. The
bear fell back on all fours and roared as it charged me. I yelled in
fright and ran alongside the building, looking for another way inside. I
heard heavy thudding closing in on me and I could nearly feel its
panting on my neck. I saw a way up onto one of the brick walls around
the garbage cans of an adjacent house, via a stack of plastic storage
totes. Adrenaline helped me bound up the wall with relative ease, as
though I had practiced parkour in lieu of smoking, and retreated far
enough to risk looking back.

The bear was making tremendous noise, moaning and growling
interchangeably, and scratching at the wall, not trying to climb, but
fighting its own apparent discomfort.

I was confused. I’d never heard of a bear behaving this way before, but
I wasn’t going to waste my chance to escape by waiting around to ask
questions. I dropped back to the ground on the opposite side of the bear
and ran back to the front door, in the hopes that someone would be back
at the front desk. I pressed the button repeatedly, but the door still
wouldn’t open.

The bear roared in agony, the likes of which seemed unimaginable. I was
compelled to try and see if I could tell what was hurting it so badly. I
saw no signs of traps on any of its limbs, but when its writhing exposed
the beast’s belly, I saw a significant amount of blood beginning to
matte its dark fur and smear all over the ground and dumpster. It
wretched up blood between cries of pain, and then I saw movement beneath
the fur of its stomach, as raking protuberances clawed to the surface
and then disappeared. I backed away but I couldn’t bring myself to tear
my eyes away long enough to run away, and so I watched a skeletal hand
claw through the bear’s soft underbelly with a silent scream on my face.

The bear tore at its chest with its claws, desperately trying to either
kill whatever was excavating its way out of its chest, or to put itself
out of its misery. A second hand had erupted from the furry flesh and
joined the first in ripping and tearing the poor brown bear’s body,
giving the creature inside greater leverage. The bear’s roars and
labored breathing became shallower, and it scrambled back into a
quadrupedal stance. A primal, monstrous screech escaped from the beast’s
mouth, which did not sound as if it could have been made by a bear’s
larynx.

I tried the button again, but I still couldn’t get a response. I looked
back and saw that the bear laying prone on the pavement, the light
fading from its eyes, and a final weakened breath rattling from its
lungs. Blood pooled underneath its body and mouth as its killer
continued to jostle the corpse in its own effort to escape. A pair of
shoulders broadened the bear’s neck, and gore covered hands went to work
rending the throat muscles aside, revealing a bald ghoulish head with
pinned and pointed ears, trying to free itself from its muscular prison.

I couldn’t understand what I was seeing, and I questioned whether I was
having an actual nightmare, or if I had been drugged, but I couldn’t
move from where I stood. I was alone with whatever was crawling out of
the carcass of what was my worst nightmare, and I couldn’t even pick up
my feet.

A pale emaciated torso had emerged from the ruined animal and was
pulling itself along the ground, leaving a trail in its wake, rubbing
blood into its skin and seemingly restoring pallor to itself. The
monstrosity continued to shriek, gaining an increasingly human timbre as
it pulled further from the bear’s body. The torso terminated in a trail
of blackened and putrid entrails, which seemed to draw blood and
displaced organic material towards the incomplete ends, as though it
were attempting to repair itself.

I’d seen enough and my body finally unfroze from my spot near the
buzzer. I ran to the front doors of the building and began beating my
fists against them, while shouting for help with all the force that I
could muster. I heard the squishing and squelching of blood and tissues
as the monster continued with its abhorrent reconstitution, and its
wails of agony became the screams of a feral human. The crunching of
bones and tearing of skin became unbearable, and I bloodied my hands
beating on the doors and pleading to get away from the grisly sight.
“Let me in!”

At long last, I saw someone down the hall stick their head out of their
front door and I felt a wellspring of hope. “Hey!” I pounded harder, to
ensure that they saw and heard me. I know that they saw me, but they
didn’t move from their front door. “Hey! Let me in!” They stood
motionless. “Please let me in!” My bleeding hands probably made me look
like an insane homeless man who was smearing filth all over the glass of
their front door. The head ducked back out of view and didn’t return. I
was still on my own with that thing.

The scene behind me had fallen silent while I was distracted, save for
the sound of panting, as if an old man had run a marathon and then
crawled out of the grave. Its bloody mouth was buried in the bear’s
flesh, and clawed hands were massaging the pelt, to wring every drop of
blood from the carcass. I saw that the torso had regained legs up to its
mid-thigh, and its skin was regaining a life-like complexion, but what
it was doing beggared belief, even if I’ve seen enough movies to know a
vampire when I saw one.

Despite the rapid regeneration, the creature’s skin bore signs of having
been burned on a metal grate, in addition to various bear related
puncture wounds. It occurred to me that this being was placed in the
trash, before the bear helped itself and gave the monster access to its
blood. The meal was now doing the consuming. It had evidently exhausted
the blood available on the first side of the bear’s body, and it
repositioned itself to a fresh spot with surprising agility considering
its diminished state. It was about to sink its teeth back into its kill,
when it finally noticed me.

Its whole body became rigid, and it rose its head up to get a better
look at me. The bear appeared to have lost its appeal, and I could see
the delighted and ravenous hunger in its blackened eyes and felt the
void of where its soul should have been reaching out to try and swallow
mine into its darkness. It attempted to bound over the bear but was
hindered by its unfinished legs and was forced to crawl instead, which
it did with great coordination and speed; rapidly closing the distance
between us. Its clawed hands slashed at me, like a primal beckon to be
consumed.

I didn’t know where to run to, but I was a sitting duck if my only hope
was that the door would open. It adopted a galloping crawl as it got
within thirty feet of me, and I took the first few strides of my escape
before headlights came into view. The creature barely had time to react
before it was struck by the silver van that I had seen earlier, which
had sped down the street in the moments prior.

Two men wearing black tactical gear jumped out of the van, carrying
pump-action shotguns and zip ties, who approached the stunned creature.
It lifted a broken arm in defense but was powerless to stop a shotgun
blast from taking its clawed hand at the forearm. The creature let out
an animalistic screech and desperately tried to lap up the blood that
oozed from the stump. The man that I saw earlier now stepped out of the
driver’s door, poorly trying to conceal his rugged features by wearing a
black ski mask over his head, and he strode over to the creature with
more trash bags tucked under his arm. “It looks like we’re back to the
drawing board.”

The trigger-happy man racked his shotgun and pointed the bore of the
barrel straight at its pale bulbous head. “Shall I put him out for the
night?”

“No. I think we can try some of the other theories we’ve been meaning to
test.” He pointed in the direction of the bear’s splayed remains. “Be
sure to pick up after the creature. We wouldn’t want that bear to turn
while we aren’t looking!”

The third man looked nervously towards the dumpster. “Do you think it
opened up all the lead boxes?”

I felt a hand rest on my shoulder from behind me and I almost shouted in
surprise before I saw that it was the lady from the front desk, who now
had the air of a woman who had found a stranger digging around her
nightstand drawer.

“Quickly, get inside!” Her other hand grabbed me by the arm, and she led
me back inside through a propped-open front door. “Are you hurt,” she
asked as she locked the deadbolt behind us.

“No. I don’t think so,” I was too busy watching the men wrestle with the
creature to check myself for any injuries beyond my hands. “What’s
happening?” A muffled shotgun blast rang through the lobby, and I could
see that the creature’s other arm had been blown off at the shoulder.
“Where did it come from?” My head couldn’t process information fast
enough and I found it hard to stay on my feet.

She gave me a serious look. “Go straight back down to your unit and be
sure to shut your windows and lock your doors.” She stepped between me
and the view of what was going on outside, although I caught sight of
someone trying to carry a writhing contractor bag. “Just go to sleep.
Everything will be fine in the morning.”

I stood waiting for some kind of reassurance that somehow everything was
normal, but I quickly realized none were coming and so I shuffled down
the hall to our unit, doing nothing to stop the drip trail of blood that
came from my injured hands, and without saying another word. By the time
I had made it into the condo, the reality of what I had seen had finally
dawned on me, and I rushed to the bathroom so I could vomit.

I returned to my bedroom after purging my stomach and dressing my
wounds. My book laid open on the bed, and the TV was still playing the
same episode of Law and Order, as though the entire fantastical episode
where an intense commercial break. The vape pen still sat on the
windowsill, near the partially open window.

A man’s voice urgently asked, “Where did the other hand go?” I rushed
over and slammed the window shut and fought the chill as I processed
what I heard.

I tore the room apart looking for any sign that the wayward hand might
have found its way into my bedroom. Was it even possible? Did I even
hear him correctly? I couldn’t take the chance and didn’t stop until I
had checked below, beneath, between, behind, and inside every hiding
spot and was satisfied that I was safe from its clawing grasp. I fell
back onto my bed and watched the colorful lights and listened to the
orchestrated sounds of the shows and infomercials that played throughout
the rest of my sleepless night. I couldn’t pay attention to the curated
stimulation that was playing out onscreen in front of me, and my mind
was fixated on each detail of the encounter as I replayed the incident
over and over again. In quieter moments, I thought I could hear
something tapping on the window, but I couldn’t bring myself to get up
and investigate until after dawn, where only smudges on the outside of
the glass left potential circumstantial evidence of what happened the
night before.

I tried to hide my injuries and exhaustion from my boss as we loaded out
of our lodging to move onto the next job, but I couldn’t hide my grimace
when I saw the pavement had been power washed between where the bear
fell all the way to where the creature had crawled to. “Is everything
alright?” He asked, with a manicured eyebrow raised.

A younger woman, wearing the same uniform as lady who had manned the
front desk the night before, came rushing out the main entrance with
paper clutched in her hand. “Wait! Can I talk to you before you go?” My
boss’s intrigue shifted to the woman, who handed the paper over to him
when she was within reach.

“What’s this?” He asked as he turned the sheet over in his hands.

“A revised invoice for your stay,” the woman began.

“Revised?” I could tell that he was gearing up to fight back against
anything they could possibly bring up against a renter. “What are you
trying to charge me for, exactly?”

The woman was seemingly caught off-guard. “We’re not charging you
anything. We’re going to comp your stay for the inconvenience of having
to hear them clean so early in the morning.” Her eyes met mine, and I
knew that she was aware of more than she would admit about last night.
“Sorry again for any inconvenience and we hope that you’ll choose to
stay with us again soon.” She then returned inside, using a key card.

My boss smiled, probably pleased to have the condo comped, and looked
back at the wet spots on the pavement. “They probably had to clean up
after a bear.”

I felt sick at the mere mention of bears but tried to play it off. “What
makes you say it was a bear?”

“There’s no lock on the dumpster and a bear will eat anything.” He
folded up his new receipt and put it inside his jacket pocket. “I
thought I heard something messing around outside my window last night!”

Needless to say, I quit smoking, and while I wouldn’t say I am cured of
my phobia of bears, I can say that I know there are infinitely worse
things to be afraid of.
