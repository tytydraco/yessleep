We were three girls against the world. Maya, Sofia, and me. Chloe.

We met freshman year and bonded over cheap wine and bad decisions, the
kind of friendship that forms when you are all drowning together. After
graduation, we got a cramped two-bedroom in a crumbling building. It was
ours.

The job market was a slaughterhouse. Hundreds of resumes, hundreds of
rejections. We worked shitty service jobs, ate discount groceries, and
pretended we were not terrified.

Maya found Quickwork! first. She came through the door waving her phone.
“You guys. I found it.”

Sofia looked up from her laptop. She was the practical one, the skeptic,
the one who read the terms before clicking agree.

“A job site,” Maya said. “But like a good one. They actually pay you.”

I was on the couch with cold ramen on my lap. “Another scam.”

“No, look.” She shoved her phone in my face. Clean interface. Smiling
briefcase. Quickwork! Your Dream Job. Delivered. “I did a survey. They
paid me fifty bucks.”

Sofia raised an eyebrow. “Fifty bucks for a survey?”

“That is desperate,” Maya said. “And we are desperate, aren’t we?”

We were.

Sofia signed up that night. I held out a week. Then the electric bill
came and the landlord started knocking.

“Just try it,” Sofia said. “What is the worst that could happen?”

The first few weeks were fine. We quit our shitty jobs, paid our bills,
and bought groceries that were not expired. For the first time in
months, we could breathe.

The tasks were weird but harmless. Surveys. Data entry. Watching videos.
Logging emotional responses to strange images. It felt like a game that
paid incredibly well for reasons nobody explained.

Then they changed.

Photograph a blue car parked at an address. Follow someone for three
blocks. Purchase rope, bleach, and a child’s teddy bear. Leave them in a
locker at the bus station.

I stared at the screen. “Guys. This is weird.”

Maya shrugged. “It’s a scavenger hunt. The money is real.”

Sofia was studying her spreadsheet. “The items are connected.”

“Connected to what?”

She didn’t answer.

The tasks became more invasive. Find where someone worked. Photograph a
building’s security system. Acquire a specific knife. Sharpen it. Leave
it at this location.

“I want to stop.”

Maya shook her head. “We’re so close.”

Sofia grabbed my arm hard enough to leave fingerprints. “You cannot quit
now. They know where our families live.”

On day sixty-six, the final assignment arrived at 6:66 in the morning.

Job Title: The Offering
Location: 666 Perdition Road
Time: Midnight
Payment: One million dollars each

Sofia stared at the screen.

“The rope. The bleach. The teddy bear. The knife.”

Her voice dropped.

“We’ve been building a murder kit, Chloe.”

The church was condemned, but the pews were full. Everyone sat facing
the altar, humming under their breath.

A man in a grey suit stood beside a smiling briefcase.

“A young woman is waiting for you,” he said. “You have prepared
everything. All you have to do is finish.”

The girl was tied to a chair.

I stumbled backward. “No.”

Maya stared at me. “Chloe, we have to.”

Sofia was crying as she walked toward the altar.

I turned for the door.

“No.”

The man looked at me. “You accepted the assignment.”

“I didn’t accept this.”

“You did when you completed Task One.”

Sofia whispered, “I’m sorry.”

Then she picked up the knife.

I ran.

I heard the scream before the church doors slammed behind me.

I did not touch the knife. I did not touch the girl. I tried to stop
them.

I still hear her.

Maya and Sofia text me sometimes.

Come back.

You don’t understand.

The next one pays more.

The man said there is no resignation from Quickwork. Once you are in,
you are in.

I deleted the app the night I escaped.

This morning, it was back on my phone.

No icon. No notification.

Just the smiling briefcase.

Under it was one message:

TASK 67 — CHLOE
PAYMENT: $2,000,000
ACCEPT / DECLINE

Masterlist
