The scent filled the room.

It poured from the bottle, though the stopper was still in. It came in a
great soft wave from the table and the chair and the walls, and it was
every evening in the hallway of the flat, every morning at the bathroom
door, every coat she had ever hung on a hook beside mine. It was not
thin or sharp any more. It was whole. It lay over the cabin like a hand
laid on a sleeping face, and I breathed it in, and in, until my eyes
streamed.

Somewhere, very far off, the stove roared.

I looked at it, slowly, because it was hard to turn my head. The iron
door was red along its seams. The fire behind the glass had gone from
yellow to white, and the stovepipe ticked and sang in the wall. The heat
came across the floor in a long steady wave, and the air above the stove
shivered like water.

By the door the pack hung on its hook, and the brick of ice turned
slowly on its cord in the roaring warmth. It gave off a faint pearl-blue
glimmer. It did not drip, and it did not shrink. It did not sweat. It
hung in the heat of the room, clear and heavy and perfectly whole, and
it seemed to me that the light inside it was drawing breath.

I stood with the bottle in both hands, wrapped in her, and I wept
without making a sound.

I don’t know how long I stood there. When I came back to myself the
bottle was still in my hands, warm as a living thing, and I set it down
very gently in the centre of the table, with the stopper in and the bone
finger pointing at the ceiling. I took my hands away from it one at a
time, as if it might wake.

I needed to be warm inside. It was a very small, very practical thought,
and I clung to it.

I filled the kettle at the tap and set it on the stove, which was now so
hot that I had to hold my sleeve over my hand to move the iron lid. I
took the tin from the shelf and the mug from the rack, the same mug as
before, and I put a spoonful of leaves in the pot and waited. I stood
with my hands flat on the counter and watched the kettle. It began to
tick and then to mutter and then, with a long rising note, to boil. The
steam came out of the spout in a thick white plume and rolled up the
wall.

I poured, and the steam rose into my face, and I breathed it in.

I made the tea slowly. I stirred it and let it stand, and then I stirred
it again, clockwise, three times, and I didn’t know why. I held the mug
in both hands against my chest, my chin over the rim, the way you hold a
thing that’s alive. The first mouthful went down like a lit coal. It
tasted dark and sweet, and it tasted of her, faintly, from the water,
and I let it. I drank all of it, to the dregs, standing at the counter,
and I felt it spread through my stomach, and then my chest, and settle
into the warmth already there.

It was not enough. I could feel that, as I set the mug down. The heat
was inside me, and it was only just beginning.

I wanted it in my skin.

The bathroom was small and white, with a basin, a mirror above it and
the shower stall in the corner. I turned the tap as far as it would go
and stood outside the stall while the water ran, and the steam began to
come, first a faint breath and then a rolling fog, climbing the tiles
and the ceiling and the glass. I undressed in the heat. The writing lay
across me like a map. It ran down both arms in fine black lines and
spread over my chest in a dense, even branching, round the ribs and over
the shoulders and in a single close band across the collarbones, and I
looked at it for a while in the dim light and did not feel afraid.

I stepped into the stall and let the water take me.

It was scalding. I put my face into it and my hands against the tiles,
and the heat poured over my back and neck and went into the skin like
something being written there. I stood with my eyes shut and I thought
of nothing at all. Behind me the scent rose with the steam, and the
whole room smelt of her.

When the water had gone from hot to merely endless, I turned it off, and
stepped out, and wiped the mirror with the flat of my hand.

I looked.

The man in the glass was pale and wet and a little thinner than I
remembered, with the hair flat on his forehead and the bones of his face
showing, and his shoulders and arms and chest were traced all over in
dark script. His hands were at his sides. His face was perfectly still.

Then he smiled.
