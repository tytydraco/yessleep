It was a gloomy Saturday. There wasn’t much to do, so I decided to go
for a walk, taking Hunter, my dog, with me as usual . I packed
everything we needed and off we went. The path wasn’t far from home so
we got there quickly.

At first, everything was normal. Hunter walked cheerfully by my side. I
was relaxed (I love foggy days), in the best mood imaginable. That day,
there weren’t many people on the trail so it was practically just Hunter
and me wandering in the forest. We walked for over an hour. We stopped
every now and then to eat, drink and appreciate the beautiful view.

As we continued our way, I noticed something quite… strange. You see,
foggy days are supposed to be calm and silent but I felt a shift in that
moment. I couldn’t hear the usual sounds of birds or anything but the
soft wind (I know it’s a well-known bad sign, but in my defense I had
this belief that if I respected nature it will respect me in return). I
noticed too that Hunter became agitated out of nowhere. I decided to
continue anyway, attributing the odd quietness to maybe a nature thing
and used to Hunter’s nervous nature. He didn’t want to follow but I
managed to make him start walking again as anxious as he was with some
treats.

A couple minutes passed and the atmosphere was still the same. Hunter’s
anxiety kept growing. At this point I was a little confused and worried.
The silence now somehow felt like an uncomfortable pressure. I didn’t
give it a second thought at the moment as I was more concerned about my
dog’s behavior so I decided it was time to head back home. As I turned
around to start walking, Hunter stopped me in my tracks. I looked back
at him and the sight surprised me. I had never seen him so still. His
tail was tucked between his legs, his ears pinned back against his head,
eyes were firmly fixated in something at the distance I couldn’t
distinguish. I tried to get his attention, but it was useless. No matter
what I did he wouldn’t move.

It was then that I could finally notice what Hunter was looking at. It
seemed like a group of people but there was something unusual about
them. They looked… weird. Their clothes were torn, almost decayed. Their
gaze was stuck on the ground as if it was the most interesting thing in
the world. I couldn’t hear any conversation or sound of voices. Maybe
they were tired, Maybe they came from a difficult hike, I didn’t know.
The only thing certain for me is that I wanted to go home with Hunter
right then.

I wouldn’t say I was scared but my heart was pounding with anxiety. I
pulled the leash once again but Hunter didn’t move, he started barking
violently. As I said before, this was out of character from him. I
looked back at the group and my heart skipped a beat. They had gotten
closer….far faster than they should have, considering how far away they
had been. How could they have done that without making a single sound?
It wasn’t possible. It didn’t make sense. Hunter’s barks were exploding
in my ears. My mind was racing. I was worried about Hunter, confused by
what I was seeing, and desperately trying to remain calm. It occurred to
me to try talking to them in an attempt to dissipate any illogical
thoughts. As I was about to speak I happened to look down for a moment.
That was when I realized something….

They had no legs. They were floating, as if they were merging with the
fog around them. Their pale, expressionless faces came into view as I
slowly raised my gaze.

I just stayed there, frozen in place. Everything became a background
noise and all I could do was stare as they got closer and closer. Just
when they were about to reach us, they turned toward a large rock by the
side of the path and disappeared behind it. A while passed before I
could move again. I slowly began walking towards the rock, my hands
trembling slightly. Hunter, no longer disturbed, followed me without
resistance.

Behind the rock there was nothing. No trace of them at all as if they
had never been there.

I didn’t know what I was expecting to find but I wanted an explanation
in that moment. I searched the trail and the surrounding trees, but
everything looked exactly as it had before. I felt Hunter pulling me
toward the way back, as if he were encouraging me to leave things as
they were. I petted him on the head and went straight back home without
saying a word.

As time passed, it became a distant, unusual memory. I still go on walks
with Hunter to this day and nothing like that has ever happened again
even though we’ve returned to the same trail several times. Still,
whenever the air feels strangely quiet and Hunter begins to grow
restless, I don’t wait around to find out why. We turn back the way we
came.
