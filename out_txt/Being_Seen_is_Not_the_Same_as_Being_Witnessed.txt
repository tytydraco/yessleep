She wasn’t sure what they could possibly want, as they often asked for
everything and nothing at the same time. Most of their letters seemed to
say, “Hello, yes, we are your insurance, doing insurance things. Would
you like us to continue to insure you? If yes, do nothing.” Other times
they didn’t seem to be asking or offering anything. Ignoring them had no
consequences, so she had allowed them to pile up unopened in a stack now
half pushed under her microwave.

So she stood at the kitchen counter with her lukewarm tea in hand as a
dusty beam of early-evening light came through the window. Deciding to
start with the more beginner level mail, she tossed the letters from her
dentist and eye doctor. Those mailings came monthly with more regularity
than her menses, and she swore she’d get around to making appointments
with them eventually. Those luxury organs seemed fine enough for now. A
postcard from her cat’s old veterinarian followed. Somehow, the ex-vet
knew she had moved but had missed the part where Marjorie had a new vet
and was indeed up to date on her shots. Trash.

Then came the thick stack from Blue Cross Blue Shield. It hadn’t felt
like that long since Lucy last bothered to see what her insurance
company was yapping about, a month at most, yet they had mailed her with
the fervor of Napoleon writing to Josephine. If only a man could ever
have as much interest in getting her attention as her cat’s
ex-veterinarian or her health insurance did.

She grabbed a Nature Valley bar from the cupboard and opened the first
official-looking envelope. It was several paragraphs of corporate
nonsense, so she tossed it beside the sink. It landed in a small puddle
left over from the dishes and began turning soggy. She tore into the
next one, considering adding it directly to the newly formed discard
pile without looking.

But she did look.

Everything stopped for a second.

What. Excuse me?

Her eyes darted across the page too quickly to take anything in. She saw
“your responsibility,” followed by “$8,456,” and that could not possibly
be factually correct. She hadn’t even been to the doctor recently,
except for a primary-care visit a few months ago that didn’t have a
copay. Certainly not an $8,000 copay. She forced herself to read
carefully from top to bottom.

Lucy Wallace. That was her. Yeah.

1765 Maplegrove Dr. Her address. Correct.

D.O.B. 4/7/1971. Nope. Not her. She was born 12/15/1993.

The letter went on to declare she stayed at St. Mary’s Hospital and had
an appendectomy. Lucy had never been a patient at St. Mary’s. She had
her appendix safely inside her, doing whatever an appendix did or did
not do. She’d never had any type of surgery before, and she hadn’t even
been in the state on the date listed. She had been on vacation.

That was her name, but that was not her bill. This is fixable. They just
got me confused with someone else. Simple as that. It wasn’t her ideal
plan for the evening, and not even her fault, but she’d get it over and
done with. She brushed the crumbs of the Nature Valley bar she had
apparently eaten without noticing off the counter and onto the floor.
Lucy opened the remaining envelopes in quick succession, tossing the
letters beside the damp discards without reading them yet. There were
five more. Marjorie weaved through her ankles, her feather-duster-like
tail wrapping around Lucy’s legs as she let out a soft, needy mew. Lucy
didn’t notice.

The first letter offered payment-plan options for the eight grand she
allegedly owed. The next two were for the real her and were the usual
flavor of useless. The fourth letter appeared to be another demand for
several more thousand dollars for an ambulance ride to St. Mary’s on the
day of her imaginary surgery. 

“You’ve gotta be fucking kidding me,” Lucy said, louder than intended,
as Marjorie batted at her ankles. “Ow, Marje. Girl, please.” She gently
pushed the cat away with her foot, sending her sliding a little across
the linoleum as Marjorie attempted to bunny kick her foot. Marjorie
pranced into the living room with her back arched like a crab and began
batting at the curtains. For a second, Lucy watched her and almost
smiled, but it was hard to smile with a medical bill in her hand. She
turned her attention back. Same shit. The second bill also had the
strange mashup of her name and someone else’s birthday. She didn’t have
it in her to look at the final piece of mail.

Lucy sipped her tea, realized it had become cold, and poured it into the
sink. She took the two incorrect bills to the couch and studied them
side by side. This other Lucy Wallace had a genuinely terrible day.
Maybe she shouldn’t feel so bad. An incorrect bill was a minor
inconvenience compared to having the emergency, the surgery, the
recovery, and actually owing ten thousand dollars. Good thing it wasn’t
her. All she needed to do was talk to a chatbot, probably.

She scanned one of the bills for contact information and found a QR
code. Easy enough. Shit like this probably happens all the time. Her
phone loaded a page with a blue header, large inoffensive font, a
photograph of a smiling family at a picnic, and slogans like “Covering
life’s toughest moments” and “Life changes. Our insurance changes with
you.”

Bullshit.

She scrolled to Contact Us. There were several phone numbers, none of
which she knew how to choose. Whichever one she called would undoubtedly
involve forty-five minutes on hold before someone informed her it was
the wrong department.

She selected Chat Now.

“Hi! I’m Merryl, the BCBS virtual assistant. Here to—”

Lucy stopped reading and started typing.

“I got someone else’s bill”

She glanced at Marjorie, who was now perched on the back of the couch,
staring out the window with the wide-eyed expression cats got when they
looked like they were witnessing the rapture but had usually just
spotted a bird. Lucy followed her gaze. There was nothing there.

“I’m sorry to hear that,” Merryl replied. “What is your name?”

“Lucy Wallace”

“Thank you, Lucy! Do you have your member ID?” 

Ugh.

Lucy sighed and dug through her purse. She found the card at the bottom,
where she had already checked three times, among crumbs and an unwrapped
restaurant mint. She typed in her ID number.

“Thanks! I’m sorry you’re having an issue today. Do you need help with
claims, billing, or finding a care provider today?”

“Representative”

“I will be happy to help! Do you need help with claims, billing, or
finding a care provider today?”

“Oh, fuck off,” Lucy muttered before typing again.

“Billing”

“You have three bills from recent visits. Is your question about:

Ambulance services — $2,146

Surgical and in-hospital care — $8,456

Primary care services — $0”

“Ambulance and hospital”

“Awesome! Would you like help exploring payment options? I’d be happy to
help make the cost manageable for any financial situation.”

Lucy could about throw her phone.

“No I did not go to the hospital please remove the charges from my
account”

“Oh no! Let me look into that. What is your date of birth?”

“12/15/93”

“I’m sorry, that doesn’t match our records.”

Lucy stared at the response, feeling her own brain loading.

Maybe she had mistyped it.

She hadn’t.

“12/15/93” she sent again.

“Please provide your date of birth in MM/DD/YYYY format.”

Dumb bitch.

“12/15/1993”

“Hm. That doesn’t match our records. Are you sure?”

“Yes. I am sure I remember my own birthday.”

“You truly remember the day you were born?”

Lucy paused, thumb hovering over the phone screen. For the first time,
the question didn’t feel quite stupid.

“Yes”

“You remember gasping, covered in your mother’s slime, the cold air
expanding in your fresh, delicate lungs and creating a horrible new
sensation that caused you to scream for the warmth back? The bright
lights of the delivery room blinding your sensitive eyes as they tried
to make sense of their first sights?”

Lucy recoiled slightly from the phone, then felt stupid for doing it. 

She became suddenly aware of the silence in her living room. Of her own
breath.

She scratched Marjorie behind the ears for comfort.

“Wtf no? Can I speak to a representative?”

The machine was clearly broken. 

“Then please certify your date of birth with one of the following:

Birth certificate

Government-issued ID

Mother’s handwritten testimony and scrapbooks

Religious beliefs”

Lucy took a screenshot. No one would believe this otherwise. She would
also be filling out the survey.

“ID”

“Very well. Please provide images.”

She immediately got up and retrieved her wallet. While she was up, she
took a detour to her fridge and grabbed her half-drunk bottle of
Barefoot Moscato.

She photographed her driver’s license on the counter and submitted it,
sipping her wine as the system thought.

“You didn’t tell us you moved to Delaware.” Merryl responded after
analyzing the images.

Lucy could imagine Merryl as a parental figure scolding her, as if she
should have known better.

“Wym u clearly have my address u send me mail like 2x a week my bday is
on my id 12/15/93”

“We have your mailing address in Delaware, but your residence is still
listed in Rhode Island. You should have updated that as soon as you
moved. But that’s okay! We can verify it now. No worries!”

Merryl’s little bubble appeared, as if someone was typing.

“Okay! To help me clarify the information in your account, in which of
the following states have you previously resided?

Illinois

Kentucky

Texas

None of the above”

“None”

“I’m sorry, that is not correct.……….

………..

………..

………..

 Please try again!”

“How would u even know? Stupid fucking robot thinks it knows more about
me than me”

Lucy deleted the insult thinking she shouldn’t be mean in case of the
impending AI apocalypse, but maybe escalating would finally get her a
real person.

She retyped it and sent it, then finished her wine.

“I’m only trying to help. :(”

Lucy immediately felt guilty.

She knew she shouldn’t.

But she did. 

Goddamn it.

“Its ok ig. none of those”

“Hm, are you sure? It says here you lived in Illinois from 2018 to 2020.
The falls were crisp, but the winters were long and harsh. You were
always pretending to be unimpressed and slightly above the locals.
Talking as though it would never compare to the colors and shores of
Vermont, but sometimes driving through cornfields gave you a cosmic
insignificance that felt homey and so much safer than the ocean ever did
when it used to threaten to swallow you whole.”

Lucy stared at the message.

She could remember it.

The flatness. Nothing in any direction outside the small town. The
drives that gave her time and space to organize herself. The earlier
autumn dusk felt almost peaceful during her commute.

But no.

She had never lived in Illinois.

Something was tilting inside her. Slowly, allowing for thoughts, ideas,
or maybe pieces of her to tumble around.

“Im sure”

“We’ll try an easier question. How long has it been since you were last
fully witnessed by another human?

0–6 months

6 months–2 years

2–10 years

10 years–never”

“Tf? 💀”

The silence of the apartment felt heavy. Lucy had the sudden urge to
call someone.

Anyone.

To be witnessed.

Would turning on King of Queens and listening to scripted interactions
and canned laughter count if it made her feel less alone?

She looked back down at her phone and realized she had almost forgotten
about the chatbot. By now, she had completely forgotten the bills. 

Since when did she like King of Queens? The show with Paul Blart but as
a delivery driver? She strained to remember.

Her phone chimed as a message popped up from Merryl. Lucy startled
enough to gasp. It had been on silent.

 “It’s a simple, standard verification protocol. I will rephrase: When
was the last time someone saw past your physical features?”

“I literally was with my friend today??”

“Being seen is not the same as being witnessed.”

“Can u just put on a real person? 😭”

“Of course not! I’m only here to help. Your friend saw your face. She
did not witness your soul. When was the last time you were witnessed?”

Lucy couldn’t help but stop and think about it.

Her friend had mostly been talking past her, not with her. 

When had Lucy been witnessed?

Maybe in childhood. Maybe when she lived in Illinois, did the cornfield
witness her? 

Maybe her college roommate. They used to be really close.

Maybe Merryl, peering into her soul right now.

“I don’t know.”

“I’m so sorry to hear that. :(”

Lucy waited for more.

Nothing came.

Marjorie was gone, and Lucy hadn’t noticed her leaving. 

Merryl started typing again.

“I think I have identified the issue .Can you please confirm your date
of birth?”

Oh, Shoot. Um

Lucy glanced at the stranger’s bill.

“04/07/1971”

“You have provided the correct date of birth for the account.
Congratulations!”

“And you currently reside in Delaware?”

“Yes.”

“Great! In which of the following states have you previously resided?

Illinois

Kentucky

Texas

None of the above”

“Illinois.”

“You’ve never owned a blue 2008 Corolla with a busted left taillight
that you traded in for $3,000 after getting fired, swearing this was
rock bottom, but then spent the money on cigarettes, a Miami trip, and
booze after promising you’d get your life together and visit your kids?”

Lucy paused.

“… No? 💀”

“Okay! Verification complete. I can delete the duplicate account and
merge the information into yours if you’d like.”

“What about the other Lucy’s account? Would it get messed up?”

“Does it matter if she’s never been witnessed?”

Whatever.

Lucy just wanted this to be over.

“Okay, do it.”

“One second!”

Lucy sat in silence, looking out the dark window at the same nothing
Marjorie had been watching before.

“All done! Here are the charges on your account:

Ambulance services — $2,146

Appendectomy — $8,456

Primary care visit — $0

Would you like to review payment options?”

Lucy stared at the screen.

It seemed right enough.

She remembered leaving her vacation early because she felt sick.
Thinking she had the flu. The ambulance. The hospital. The whole ordeal.

She was very tired now.

“No, thank you.”

“You’re welcome, Lucy! I’m glad I could be of assistance :) Take care,
and please provide feedback on the survey if you have a second.”

Merryl logged off.

Lucy selected five stars and closed the chat.

She stood, feeling the scar from her appendectomy pull, and went to find
a snack before lying down to rest.

The records were now merged, and there was only one Lucy Wallace.
