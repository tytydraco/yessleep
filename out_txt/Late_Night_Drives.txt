I am an anxious person. Most people that have met me wouldn’t agree with
that statement but the few that know me well would. I act outgoing,
friendly even. But deep inside I overthink EVERYTHING. This is some
context to explain some of the things that I’ll do in this statement. As
I don’t believe most people take these kinds of precautions.

I used to work nights at a local grocery store in my small town. I won’t
name the place or the town, just know that late at night it got very,
very quiet. Like 3 or 5 people an hour after 9. Maybe a small rush when
the factories got out. My shift was 2PM to 11 PM. It wasn’t the best
shift as it put me on a different schedule than I was used to but it
paid better than working during the morning. Working there you’d see the
occasion odd interactions. One dude pissed in the soda corner once for
that one TikTok thing about peeing on college campuses. I don’t envy the
manager that had to deal with that. But otherwise, these shifts were
boring and quiet.

The main issue I had with working late nights was the drive home. I
lived about 20 minutes away from the store. It wasn’t the longest drive,
but it certainly wasn’t easy. Oftentimes, I was tired after work and
would find myself struggling to stay awake. I had always found car rides
relaxing and slept whenever I was on a road trip. This proved to be an
issue when I got my driver’s license.

I remember my dad used to tell me all about his nights as a construction
worker. Driving long distances between sites late at night. Struggling
to stay awake. He’d tell me about how he’d scream, slap himself in the
face, blast music. Anything to stay awake.

And I’ll admit, I was in a pretty rough place mentally back in those
days. I was a year out of high school after spending most of those years
planning not to live that long. I was taking a “gap year” but had no
plans or motivation for going to further schooling. I wasn’t seeing or
talking to my family all that much due to our schedules not lining up.
It looked like I was destined to spend the rest of my days as a cashier
at a dusty old store.

And so, on those late nights driving home, I would let myself nearly
drift off. My fear of dying waking me up right as I hit the shoulder of
the county road. I’d repeat this cycle often. I worried frequently about
swerving the other way and taking some poor soul out with me. I didn’t
necessarily want to die but I wasn’t doing anything to stop myself.

Eventually, I decided to stop my little game of nearly falling asleep at
the wheel and instead began to play music to try to keep awake. This
didn’t really work as I’d ignore the music and nearly fall asleep
anyways. So, I began to sing along to the music, often loudly and poorly
as my vocal cords don’t really work like they used to since COVID killed
my range and gave me a chronic cough. But it did its job and soon every
drive home I’d be singing my lungs out to some 2000s pop song.

Now, the store I worked at had two parking lots. One was a nicely lit,
large parking lot for customers right out the front. The other was on
the side, had two streetlamps, one of which that didn’t work, and was
slightly smaller. It was the employee parking, where my colleagues and I
were supposed to park.

Most of the night shift staff ignored this rule and parked in customer
parking for safety and convenience. I was not this intelligent. I
struggle with parking especially in tight spaces and hate crowded
parking lots. So, my car would be sitting in the dingy, poorly lit
parking lot all by itself, often about 50 feet away from the building.

I am a young biological female in America. I am rather lanky and don’t
work out that often. I can run but not for long and often I would be
leaving that building alone. It was safe to say I was on high alert
every time I walked out to my car. I had heard scary stories about
people laying under cars waiting for its owner to come so they can slit
their ankles and kidnap them.

As a precaution, I would yank open my door and remain a foot away from
it, hopping the remaining distance so “they” couldn’t reach me. Then, I
would spin around and check the back of my car, turning on all the
lights and making damn sure that I was the only living thing in that
vehicle. I had also heard stories about people waiting in the back of
vehicles to attack the unsuspecting drivers from behind. Anxiety was a
way to describe it. Or full-blown paranoia. Perhaps that’s why I don’t
fully believe if this all is truly real.

I drive down a state highway a good portion of the way to my house.
Halfway through turning down a county road to the small backroad I live
on. This county road is quiet, often populated by deer and the
occasional state patrol sitting right where it meets the state highway.
The road has no signal so in the days before my YouTube music
subscription, I would be singing to myself acapella. This would be a
more unpleasant part of the drive since I usually forgot the words
halfway through and would half-heartedly mumble the melody.

One night, I was driving home after a particularly drudging shift and
was doing my nightly routine of singing while I drove. This time it was
some 80s rock music I didn’t usually listen to but enjoyed. I reached
the county road and predictably my music cut out. I sang the rest of the
chorus but ended up forgetting the rest. I sighed and stopped singing
all together.

Resigning to driving in silence, the only sound being that of the engine
rumbling. It was sort of peaceful in a way, the silence. But after a
while I began to feel a slight tingling at the back of my head. A weight
in the back of my car. Like someone was staring at the back of my head
intently. It unnerved me as I had done my usual checks to make sure no
one else was in the car. I got the sudden imagery of looking into the
rear-view mirror and seeing someone staring back at me. Panic began to
surge in me. I knew it was irrational, likely a paranoid thought worming
itself into my head and taking over my mind.

But I just couldn’t shake the feeling that there was someone in the
backseat of my car. Waiting for me to acknowledge or look at them. So, I
began to talk.

Back when I first started getting paranoid about strangers being in the
back of my car, I would start talking like they were real. Saying shit
like: “If there’s someone in this car, I will drive off this upcoming
bridge and kill us both.” Or in between songs, “and this one goes out to
you, creepy guy in the backseat!”. Stuff to somewhat lighten the mood or
comfort myself. Even if it was a little dark.

This night, I decided on a simple: “Hello?”. It was small, unsure, and
I’m pretty sure I was trembling. After a moment of silence, it did
nothing to ease my fears but by that time I had reached the light of a
nearby tavern. My music cut back in and I did my best to push the
thought from my head.

The next night, I drove home with the dome lights in my car on. It made
it a little harder to see the road, but it gave me a bit more peace of
mind. But I couldn’t just keep doing that all the time, I was worried
the glare would make me miss seeing a deer or something. So, not even a
week after the first instance, I was back to my usual routine.

The same thing happened, shortly after turning onto the county road, my
music cut out. This time it was a song I knew a little better, so I kept
singing. I was almost halfway to the tavern when I started getting tired
of singing. I slowly began to trail off when suddenly the sensation of
being watched returned.

A thought popped into my head that something very bad would happen if I
stopped singing. Panic rose in my chest. I don’t know what would have
happened, but I was too scared to find out. So, I kept singing, reaching
the ending of the song and moving onto a random one I had listened to
before. I reached the tavern and just like before the sensation faded
somewhat but the fear remained.

I often would talk about these fears to my coworkers, and they’d brush
me off with a chuckle. Saying things like “It’s a small town, people
don’t break into cars here” or “you’re just working yourself up”. I told
myself they were right, steeling myself to do what I had been too scared
to before. I planned to look into the rearview mirror when I felt that
presence. I was going to to face my fears head on and turn on the lights
to confirm that it was all in my head.

That night, I did my checks. Even going as far as to check the trunk
behind the rear seats. I got in my car and drove. I didn’t sing this
night, just played my music and let it cut out when I rolled onto the
county road. I held my breath, waiting for that sensation. The tingling
of eyes on the back of my head or the thick feeling to the air. And soon
enough, I felt the presence behind me. It felt closer than before and
more threatening than usual. Normally it didn’t feel like anything
aggressive, just observation. Watching and waiting. But now it felt
outright hateful.

After a moment of utter horror, I gripped the steering wheel and forced
my gaze off the road and into the rear-view mirror. I stared at the dark
mirror for any sign of life but found only darkness. I scanned and
scanned until my eyes caught onto the tiniest glint of light reflecting
into the mirror. Two small dots of light reflecting back at me.
Horizontally the same height and about a few inches apart. I held my
breath and I swear I heard the soft sound of breathing.

My stomach dropped as I realized I was staring back at what I thought
was two eyes. My heart pounded as I kept my eyes locked on the lights.
My throat closing up with the panic I felt. I felt as if I looked away,
the person behind me would attack or hurt me. I wanted to scream, cry,
say something, do something but I was frozen in fear. All I could do was
stare back, terrified out of my mind.

The sound of a blaring horn jolted my gaze back to the road. I was on
the wrong side of the road, speeding towards another set of headlights.
I swerved into my lane quickly. My tires made a squealing noise in
protest. I managed to keep my car on the road but now I was too afraid
to look back into the rearview mirror.

With a shaky hand, I reached up to the button to the dome lights. With a
quick motion I pressed it and looked into the rearview mirror. Nothing.
Empty seats and the bag I had left back there. My heart was pounding as
I sped back home. I ran when I got out of the car, terrified that
whatever or whoever was in the backseat would get me.

After that I requested to be moved to the morning shift. I took
alternative routes home, regardless of how out of my way they took me. I
swapped to listening to the radio instead of music from my phone. But in
my remaining night shifts on the drive home I would see little shadows
in the corners of my headlights. Figures moving at the corner of my
gaze, disappearing when I’d look at them head on. I don’t know how much
of it was paranoia, but it all felt real. Incredibly real.

I still don’t drive on that county road late at night anymore. I got a
YouTube music subscription and listen to my music and podcasts
downloaded. I still get that sensation from time to time but I usually
just turn on the lights. It helps but I still think about that encounter
and wonder what would have happened if I had kept driving down that
road, looking into those eyes.
