I’m typing this with one hand while jamming my shoulder against the
bathroom door. My phone battery is at 2%. If this cuts off, someone
please call the cops to the apartments on Elm Street.

“Are you in there?” Arthur’s flat voice asks again from the hallway.
Tap. Tap. Tap. The rhythmic clicking of Leo’s lighter echoes through the
wood. “Pleas open the door. Leo told me to make sure everyone stays in
the room.”

“Arthur, go away!” I scream, my voice cracking. “Go back to the living
room!”

“I cannot do that,” he replies instantly. “Leo gave a direct command. I
must comply.”

The absolute mechanical nature of his voice makes my stomach twist.
There is no anger in it. No malice. Just an empty, automated processing
of an order.

Then, the handle jiggles. Slow. Methodical.

Rattle. Rattle.

“Arthur,” I try to keep my voice steady, tears blurring my vision. “Leo
is drunk. He doesn’t know what he’s saying. I am giving you a new
command. Stop knocking and sit down on the floor.”

Silence. The clicking stops.

For a second, I think it worked. I think I broke the loop.

Then Arthur speaks and his voice is lower, vibrating with a terrifying,
fractured cadence. “Conflict detected. Command overrides prior
authorization matrix. However…Leo’s command was structural.”Make sure
everyone stays in the room. If you leave the bathroom, you exit the
perimeter. I cannot allow a breach of compliance.”

THUD.

The door frames shakes. He didn’t just knock. He threw his entire body
weight against it.

“Arthur, stop!”

THUD.

The wood groans. The cheap lock on this apartment bathroom isn’t going
to hold. I look around desperately for a weapon. There’s nothing but a
plunger and a glass jar of cotton swabs on the counter.

THUD.

The upper hinge snaps out of the drywall with a sickening crunch.
Through the newly formed gap at the top of the door, the smell hits me.
It doesn’t smell like a living person out there anymore. It smells like
ozone, burnt copper and deep sour sweat.

I look up through the crack and my heart completely stops.

Arthur’s face is pressed against the gap. His wire glasses are gone. His
mouth is still stretched into that horrifying, polite, customer service
smile, but his lips have split from the tension, bleeding dark crimson
down his chin. His eyes are wide, bloodshot and completely devoid of any
human consciousness. He looks like a puppet being yanked violently by
invisible strings.

“It’s almost over,” Arthur whispers, his breath hot and metallic through
the crack. “The twenty four hours expire at midnight. Please comply. It
makes the endurance study much easier.”

Behind him, down the dark hallway, the living room is dead silent. The
buzzing of Marcus’s phone has finally stopped.

“What did you do to them, Arthur?” I choke out, pressing my boots
against the wall and my back against the door, desperate to hold the
line. “Where are Leo and Marcus?!”

Arthur’s smile twitches. A low, guttural clicking sound comes from the
back of his throat, the same sound he made right before he snapped.

“Leo told me to put my hand over the flame,” Arthur says, his voice
perfectly level despite the blood dripping from his mouth. “I complied.
Then Leo told me to make it stop hurting. He said,”Do whatever you have
to do to make the burning stop, Artie.”

Arthur pauses, his gray eyes fixing onto mine through the dark
splintered wood.

“The human nervous system processes pain through the brain stem,” Arthur
explains calmly. “To make the burning stop, I had to disconnect the
source of the authorization. I used the utility knife on the kitchen
counter. First Marcus. Then Leo. They cannot command me anymore. Their
compliance window has closed.”

My blood runs entirely ice-cold.

“But Leo’s final structural command remains in effect until midnight,”
Arthur continues, his bloody fingers reaching the cracked top of the
door, gripping the wood. “Keep everyone in the room.”

The door frame splits. The lower hinge gives way.

My phone screen flashed a final, desperate warning: 1% Remaining.

The screen goes entirely black.

The door is coming down. Arthur is smiling. And I have exactly three
minutes until midnight.
