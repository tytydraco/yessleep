I was being followed.

Walking through the woods for a few hours longer than I had hoped, not
quite lost but not quite sure of my exact position either, one idea kept
coming back to me.

I was being followed.

The human brain is a wonderful thing. It is always working, always
taking information in, whether you realize it or not. Modern life makes
most of its functions useless. Many of the primordial tasks that were
essential to survival and that made up the majority of our cognitive
workload are now totally redundant.

But we have longer memories than we think, and given the right
circumstances, and a little pressure, it’ll slip right back into gear.
The same gear that was of the utmost importance for millennia.

I was getting the message loud and clear. I was being followed.

Calling it a gut feeling is giving my gut a little too much credit. We
all have gut feelings, which way to drive to work to avoid traffic, what
to order at a restaurant, what to wear. You might feel a little urge, or
a small pull one way or the other. That’s the normal sensation of your
gut telling you what to do.

This was not a gut feeling. This was a jumbotron inside my head, with
bright red lettering telling me, in no uncertain terms, that I was being
followed.

My only problem was I had no idea why.

I entered Warner State Park at just after two in the afternoon, hoping
to enjoy a nice walk through some of the trails. It’s an area I had been
to many times before, and earlier today as I looked at the large wooden
trail map, I decided I should do the full park loop. This clocked in at
just around eight miles, which given my usual speed meant I should be
done no later than six in the evening. The day was pleasant, and I was
looking forward to a relaxing time in the outdoors.

By six thirty I realized I had a problem. The mile markers all lined up,
and I should have been about ready to finish the loop, as I had passed
the final mile marker over a half hour ago. In just a minute or so I
should see that big map board again, and the parking lot behind it.
Maybe I took a little too much time enjoying the sights or the air and
my pace slackened, or maybe I’m just not in as great shape as I thought
I was.

The problem arose when I saw a marker for mile number nine. How could
there be a ninth mile on this trail? It was only supposed to be eight.
Not even. The original map had to be wrong, I figured, and I assumed
that maybe it was just a bit over nine miles with all the curves taken
into account. Like how a 5K race is really 3.1 miles. Sure it’s a three
mile race, but even at the three mile point you had to go just a little
bit farther. Maybe I just had to deal with the decimal place now.

I picked up my pace and fifteen minutes later I saw a marker for mile
ten, and it was getting dark.

Stopping for a moment to think, I pulled out a flashlight and a snack
from my backpack. I always carried a few essentials with me, band-aids,
some painkillers, just in case something bad ever happened. I wasn’t
sure what was happening to me yet, but I knew it wasn’t good.

The trail can’t go on forever, and I must have stumbled onto one of the
longer trails by accident. The park itself wasn’t too big, so for a
worst-case scenario in which I accidentally went on the longest trail in
the park, which was a dozen miles, I would only have to keep going for
another hour.

The trees made the darkness seem much greater than it really was. The
sky was still decently bright, but the trail was dim, and growing dimmer
by the minute. Soon I had to turn on my flashlight to see the trail,
even though the sky was still a deep purple.

My thoughts changed too. It felt like my vision became a little more
focused and my ears a little sharper.

Awareness, that’s a nice way of putting it.

Everyone is aware, at least to a certain extent, but all of a sudden I
was aware on a deeper level.

I noticed my head naturally starting to scan the tree line to my sides,
following noises I heard in the brush. Occasionally I felt the urge to
look behind me, though I don’t know why.

That was the most unnerving thing about it. As far as I could tell
everything was the same as it ever was, just a bit darker now. I was
walking down a path I did not know, and suddenly I was taking account of
everything. Within all that, I kept feeling the need to check behind me.

But for what?

I would be walking, and naturally my eyes would drift to one side or the
other, and my flashlight along with it. Looking into the trees and
brush, I would see… nothing. Trees, bushes, grass. It was always the
same. At the same time I’d hear something, something very quiet. A small
rustle of grass or a bush or a tree branch. I’d turn and look in the
direction and see… nothing again. Probably just the wind. Or maybe a
squirrel. A small animal, or a bird, or the wind. Except I never saw any
of them. Or heard them, except for the sounds of things moving through
the woods, and that could all just be the wind. It seemed like it was
starting to pick up now, and I could feel it on my back.

And every few minutes I’d look behind me.

My mind had gone through a complete shift. I didn’t think anymore about
things I did just a few hours ago when I was enjoying my walk. I didn’t
think about my friends or family, or the news, or that time this week I
accidentally said “you too” to the barista at the coffee shop telling me
to enjoy my drink.

The truth is, I wasn’t thinking about anything at all. I was just aware.
Aware of everything.

That’s when I got the message.

I was being followed.

If you put a gun to my head I couldn’t give you a single reason, or
fact, or shred of evidence for this. At first it felt like a joke.
Except within a few moments of thinking about that message, it wasn’t
funny at all.

I kept hearing noises from the woods, but that was almost definitely
just the wind. I didn’t even really know what the difference between the
wind moving grass and something else would make. The wind was at my back
and it felt like something was always right there because of it.

Paranoia is a term that seemed to jump out of the ether to me, and it
brought me some relief.

I’m just being paranoid, I thought to myself. You’re a little lost and
it’s getting a little dark so you’re paranoid. Totally normal reaction.
And I felt better. A lot better actually. I kept walking and felt a
renewed stride in my pace, and my brain returned to its normal state of
being. I thought again about movies and television shows I wanted to
watch, or how my favorite hockey team was doing, and about music and my-

Then I heard another rustling in the woods that sounded like it was
within six feet of me, and within a split second my brain shut down
completely. Thoughts, ideas, all of these things were thrown out the
window in the blink of an eye, and suddenly I was alone, lost, and dark
in an unfamiliar woods. And I was pretty sure something was following
me.

You can’t think yourself out of a wet paper bag, but mindless action is
a death sentence. My mind began working in overdrive. I sped up and all
of the tiredness I had felt from a long day of hiking went away. It felt
like I was walking on a cloud, and to be honest I couldn’t even feel the
lower half of my body. Or the top half.

Adrenaline. Must be.

I could only form thoughts in short phrases or single words. Trying to
break through the computations with anything more complex than that was
impossible. All focus was on my surroundings.

All of a sudden I was moving at a much quicker pace, and taking
everything into account as I moved. Every time the wind blew I could
hear how it moved all the trees and bushes around me, what the correct
level of sound should be made by it given how strong the gust was. My
eyes moved around, and with the help of my flashlight, I had an
understanding that was far more primitive and sophisticated than I had
ever had before.

Then as the trail turned my flashlight lit something large and unnatural
and I almost jumped when I saw it.

It was the map board.

For a split second I felt relief wash over me, but then the thought came
back.

I am being followed.

I sprinted to my car. Got inside still wearing my backpack. Didn’t
bother to buckle the seatbelt, started the engine, shifted into drive
immediately and floored it.

About a minute later as I got back onto a paved road I let out a sigh.
All of a sudden my normal sensations rushed back to me like taking a dip
in a warm bath. I could feel my legs and arms again. I could think
again. I put my seatbelt on, got my backpack off, and threw it in the
passenger seat. I turned on my radio to the usual sports talk station. I
realized how tired I was. I drove home without a hitch.

But every once in a while, I felt the urge to look in my rearview
mirror.

Huh.
