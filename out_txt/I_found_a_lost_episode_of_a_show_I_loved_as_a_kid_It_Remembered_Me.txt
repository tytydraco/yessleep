Nostalgia can be a powerful force, destructive or supportive. Very
destructive in this case…

When my parents were clearing out their garage, they called me up about
a couple of boxes they found of my stuff. I had just wanted to look for
old cartoon DVDs or VHSes. Getting to enjoy some relaxing old cartoons
after being laid off from another job. After digging through a few
boxes, finding some boxes of other cartoons, I picked up a single DVD
with a handwritten title “ANNOYING ORANGE: Lost Episode”. I hadn’t
watched Annoying Orange in years! My brother Danny and I used to quote
their stupid jokes at each other constantly.

No cover.
No studio markings.

It made me laugh, remembering the friend in high school that always
joked about being able to find ‘lost episodes’ since his dad worked with
a broadcasting company. He had a bunch of them for sale, even some that
were legit now, with all the shuffling from company to streaming and
back. I’d picked up a few but, after looking through the other boxes,
out of the ones I’d bought off him back then, apparently this one was
the only one that had survived every move since. Well, either way, I
brought back the best of the haul that I could fit into a bag, thanked
my parents for the time, and returned to my dinky little apartment to
relax for the next few days, weeks, months, until I could get interviews
again. 

Eventually, after a day of vegging out and steamrolling through four
seasons of other old cartoons, I picked up the DVD and popped it in the
player, figuring I could use some bad jokes to pick up my spirits. What
I saw when it turned on though, was just a blank, black, screen, with
only one option, “Play”. 

No episode title.
No credits.
Nothing else.

Laughing already, at the laziness of my friend, I hit play and settled
back to enjoy the ride. At first, it was the same bad good show as
usual, The typical orange, the counter, the other fruits nearby, but
something was weird about it. There was no background music, no canned
laughter. No real editing, They were just sitting there, staring at each
other. 

Then, Orange eventually looked toward the others and said, “Hey.” I
started to smile at first because that’s exactly the voice that I
remembered. Then Orange said it again. “Hey.” A long pause. “Hey.” My
smile faded as no other fruit seemed to be reacting to Orange though, it
started to feel weird without any other sounds coming from the screen,
just the Orange turing to look right at me. “You got older.”

It was really getting weird. I looked to the side, and now none of the
other fruits were even moving, they seemed frozen, while Orange was just
sitting there now, staring at me, with a grin that was looking even
stranger than before. Wider. Almost even more exaggerated. 

The video fully froze then. Except his eyes are still moving. Taking in
the room beyond the screen, almost like he could see MY room the way his
eyes were lingering on certain spots. 

“You stopped watching.”

I blinked, it really sounded like he was addressing me. This episode was
turning out really weird, like some kind of side project or something
from the team. I paused the episode, aiming to turn it off, I didn’t
need to be freaked out right now. Before I could pop out the DVD, the
Orange blinked. While it was still supposed to be paused…

I thought I must have resumed it by mistake. That had to be it. So I hit
pause again, and this time, the other fruits seemed to come to life,
slowly turning towards the screen, just like Orange. This time, it was
Pear that spoke, looking directly at where I was sitting up now. “He
came back.”

Then Orange laughed. 

Not his usual almost cackle, it was too deep, too long. Almost layered
like several echoes on top of each other, like they were trying
something different here, but instead it just sounded…creepy. I
immediately turned off the DVD. Ejecting it and tossing it back into its
cover. Then from the blank screen, I heard it, “HEY”

The light started to flicker, maybe it was some bad wiring. At least,
that was the thought until the kitchen corner started to act out too,
lights, microwave, blender, all turning on and off. Then I heard
another, “Hey!” coming from the kitchen. The voice sounded exactly like
Orange. “Hey, buddy!” 

I definitely didn’t answer. The kitchen light went out. Then another
light flickered on farther down the hall. “Where’d you go?” The worst
part is, it still sounded more playful and inviting than scary. I was
left thinking Orange, or whatever was in my kitchen actually missed me. 

Walking slowly in, I looked around, but there wasn’t a huge orange in
sight. Checking the always empty fruitbowl, now there was an orange
sitting alone in the middle. I… definitely didn’t buy that orange. I
don’t even remember buying any fruit for the last couple of weeks. I
could only walk up slowly, not wanting to cause some kind of reaction in
case there was a face attached to that orange. Step by step I
approached, until I could look in and was able to confirm that there was
no face on that orange. It was a regular, every day orange. Just as I
turned away to try and forget about all this, I heard it again, “HEY!”
coming from behind me.

Still just fruit.
But there was a smile now.
I tossed it. 
I had the feeling that losing that job hit me harder than I thought. 

Shaking my head, I returned to the television to put on something else,
but stopped when I saw the video was back on, and it had unpaused again.
Except, this time, the counter being shown was a lot more familiar. 

That was my refrigerator, the same dent beside the handle.
Same cabinets.
Same overhead light.
There was even my coffee mug.
There I was.

The animation now matched my kitchen. Orange was looking around again,
and when it ‘spotted’ me, that grin came back and I heard, “There you
are!” The camera was turning slightly to take in my living room, with
the TV showing that grinning orange, and something seemed to pass
quickly behind me on the screen. Spinning around quickly, I didn’t spot
anything behind me, but I did hear that deep laughter coming from the TV
again. Looking back, the orange was almost cackling. 

On screen, Orange said, “knock knock.” Then someone knocked on my door.
Orange called out in a sing-song voice, “who’s there?” I definitely was
not going to answer. 

Knocking again.
Orange grinned onscreen. “Nobody.”

I had to check the peephole, just in case, and there was actually no one
there. I stood there looking at an empty hallway for three seconds
before turning back to the TV when I heard the laughter on screen
again. 

Orange suddenly looked past me through the screen, turning back to the
kitchen itself, “Knife.” 
That was all. Slowly turning back to the kitchen, I saw one was missing
from my knife block. Then I heard it. Metal scraping on tile. The
kitchen lights turned off again. The laughter was coming from both
locations, the TV and the dark kitchen. 

Cabinet doors opening.
Something round rolling across the floor.
A refrigerator door opening.
Then Orange’s voice, calling out from my bedroom now. “Hey!”

Instead of checking that out, I ran to the DVD player and yanked out the
DVD, twisted it until it cracked, and chucked it into the nearest trash
bin. 

Everything stopped. 

I grabbed the trash bag and rushed downstairs to toss it. Walking back
in, the apartment was normal again. Kitchen light was on, blender was
off, microwave timer was reset, I’d have to figure that one out again. 

There was an orange in my fruit bowl. 
I tossed it again. 

After looking through my apartment to be sure, I still didn’t find the
knife, but I gave up on it. Small price to pay. That was my thought,
until I turned the TV back on again. 

Black screen. 
Two options now though, “Play” and “Continue”

I immediately tried to eject the DVD again. Nothing happened. I checked
the slot. Empty. The screen flickered and I looked back to see new words
on the screen, “DON’T BE ANNOYING.”

Sitting back down in shock, I saw that same orange pop back up on the
screen. In the original kitchen again. “Remember when you and Danny
watched me?” Somehow it knew my brother’s name. “You two LOVED hearing
my jokes every Saturday!” It was right. I used to watch those Saturday
morning cartoons with him every weekend, but Danny died years ago. Then
I heard it, from down the hall, leading to the other end of the
apartment, “Hey!” Not in Orange’s voice but Danny’s. It was using his
voice now. Every time I watched this thing, it started acting out more,
reaching deeper into my mind. The kitchen was starting to look like mine
again. The orange was back in the basket. 

I shut off the TV. Fully unplugged it. Disconnected the DVD player.
Turned off my cell, just to be sure. Unplugged my blender and microwave.
Everything I could find that was electric. 

Silence.
Thirty seconds.
Then, “Hey!”
Right next to my ear.

Before I could see where that knife ended up, I ran, but I kept hearing
that laughter. 

From upstairs.
Then downstairs.
Inside the elevator.

I had to get out of the building, but even then, it kept following.
Streetlights flickered as I passed.

Digital billboards glitched with orange smiles.
Store displays all show the same orange face.
Even a grocery window full of oranges seems to turn toward me when I
passed.

Eventually, I ran into a park, and the voice was quieter. When I ran out
of it, the volume increased again. I had to get out, into a forest.
Maybe that would stop it all. I eventually found myself running down a
service road, into a forest. Dodging between trees as I heard the
laughter behind me. Coming from two sides, three, six, but a lot quieter
now. “Hey…”

After wandering for a few hours, finding the points farthest away from
that laughter, I spotted an orange glow in the distance. I froze for a
second, until I realized it came from a Campfire. Several people were
sitting around it, enjoying the warmth. I stumbled into the clearing and
looked around. Someone, an older guy, made his way over. He saw me
looking back into the forest and asked, “what followed you?”

“An orange.”
Everyone looked at me. One guy almost laughed, a young girl giggled.
Then, from the woods, 
“HEY!”
It made them both stop.

The older guy shook his head, “don’t answer it. Don’t look at it.”
I stood stock still, frozen on the spot, as I heard something rolling
closer. The guy looked down when an orange rolled up against his boot.
He simply nudged it back beyond the light. Then I heard another, and
another. Several oranges rolled into the clearing from every direction
before stopping just outside the firelight. One after another, grins
started splitting the oranges and I heard, “hey!” repeated over and over
again. Making bad jokes, laughing, mentioning Danny again, then names I
didn’t recognize.

The people around the fire went noticeably still, but no one looked at
the oranges. The older guy guided me to the Campfire. Showing me to a
seat, he checked on some soup that was cooking over the fire before
looking back at me. “Some things chase you because they’re hungry. Some
chase you because they need to be noticed.”

“HEY!”

Eventually the voice got quieter again. The older guy pulled out a book,
like a large storybook, and set it down next to him. “You’re safe here
until sunrise. After that, you owe the fire a story.” I felt so
confused, 

“You want me to talk about it?” 
“That’s usually how this works.” 
“What if telling the story gives it another audience?”
From inside the woods, I heard a faint, “hey…”
