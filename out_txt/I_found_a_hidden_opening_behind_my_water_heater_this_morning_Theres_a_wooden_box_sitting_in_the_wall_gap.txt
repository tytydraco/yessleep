I’m posting this while sitting in my truck in my driveway because I
honestly just need to write it down to make sure I’m not losing my mind.

​I’ve lived in this place for about two years. It’s an older suburban
house, built in the late 70s, and like any house that age it has its
quirks. Squeaky floorboards, the occasional pipe rattle, normal stuff.

​This morning I noticed a slow drip coming from behind the water heater
unit in the utility closet near the garage entrance. I grabbed a
flashlight, knelt down, and leaned around the back of the tank to check
the line.

​That’s when I noticed the drywall.

​Behind the heater, down low near the baseboard, a neat rectangular
section of drywall, maybe 18 inches high and 12 inches wide, had been
cut out. It wasn’t kicked in or damaged, it was a clean, straight-edged
cut. Because of where the water heater sits, you literally cannot see it
unless you press your face against the cold tank and shine a light
straight back.

​I thought maybe a previous owner or contractor cut it to access a pipe.
I stuck my phone in and took a photo with the flash on.

​When I looked at the picture, it wasn’t plumbing or wiring.

​It opens up into the dead space between the internal drywall and the
original exterior block wall. But someone had placed a small, dark
wooden crate inside, pushed about three feet down the gap. On top of the
crate sat three things: a small brass key with no markings, a single
heavy iron nail bent almost in half, and a Polaroid photo turned
completely face down.

​Here’s where it gets weird. I reached in with a long pair of pliers to
hook the Polaroid and slide it out. As soon as the metal tool clicked
against the crate, I heard a sharp, distinct intake of breath from
inside the wall above my head.

​Not a breeze. Not a pipe creak. It sounded exactly like someone pulling
air through their teeth when they get startled.

​I froze. I pulled my arm back so fast I scraped my wrist on the cut
drywall edge. I sat on the cold concrete floor of the closet for a solid
two minutes, just listening. Nothing. Just the quiet hum of the house.

​I didn’t touch the photo. I didn’t touch the key. I closed the utility
closet door, locked the door into the main house, and walked outside.

​Why would a cutout be placed specifically behind a heavy appliance where
no human adult could fit to place those items by hand without moving the
unit first? And should I move the water heater and try to pull the
Polaroid out, or should I just patch the drywall and leave it alone?

​I’m going back inside in a few minutes once my hands stop shaking. I’ll
update if I hear anything else, but right now I’m genuinely afraid to
open that closet door again.
