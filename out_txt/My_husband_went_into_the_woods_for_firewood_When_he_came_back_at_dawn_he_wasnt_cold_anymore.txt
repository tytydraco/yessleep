My teeth chattered, my hands shivered. I looked over at John. He was
wrapped in his blanket, blowing smoke. 

“Can we add more wood?”

“But only a little, Mary. It needs to last until morning.”

“I know. I’m sorry. I’m just so cold.”

“It’s not your fault. It’s this goddamn winter. Not even my grandfather
had to live through snow in late April.”

He walked to the fireplace, but there was no more wood there.

“There should be more in the basement,” he muttered and walked down.

He was down there longer than usual. I could hear baskets and bags being
thrown around. After a while, he came out, rubbing his forehead.

“There’s no more wood.”

“What?!”

“The wood. We used it all.”

“Jesus, John.”

He looked at the fire. Only two logs remained; their ends glowed red.

“We have an hour, maybe two at most. I’ll have to go out and get more.”

I gripped my blanket tight.

“John, it’s almost nighttime; they’re gonna be out soon.”

“I might make it before them.”

“John, no, please.”

“We’ll freeze if the fire dies.”

“But, John…”

“No, there’s no time to waste.”

He grabbed his axe and walked out of the cabin. I walked to the window
and watched him, but he quickly disappeared between the white-coated
trees.

I curled up in my blanket and lay by the fireplace, watching the sun out
the window. When it had almost fully set, I ran to the window, but John
was still nowhere to be seen. I went back to the fireplace and watched
it slowly disappear over the horizon.

Something must have happened to John, but as much as I wanted to mourn
him, a new problem appeared: the cold. The sun kept the house warm for a
while, but at night it was freezing inside. I ran to the basement and
got all our baskets and anything else that could burn.

The wood from the baskets burned quickly, but it put at least a little
heat in the room. I was finally warm, but thoughts about John soon crept
in, my poor man. Would he also end up in a tree, white and bloodless, or
worse, one of them? I shook my head. This wasn’t what I wanted to worry
about; maybe he managed to hide and wait out the night. I threw another
basket in and moved closer to the fireplace.

Loud bangs echoed, waking me. The fire was almost out. My fists could
barely close. The bangs sounded again. I wrapped up in my blankets and
got up. 

“John!” I cried. He stood by the window, staring in.

“Mary, please let me in. It’s so cold here.”

I ran to the window and gripped the door handle, but before I twisted, I
looked at John again. His eyes didn’t move, and his body didn’t shiver.

“Mary, please, open the door.”

“How do I know you’re not one of them?” The words almost stung as I said
them.

“Mary, it’s me. You hear my voice. Just open the door.”

My grip tightened around the door handle.

“John, I don’t know.”

“Open, Mary, please. I’ve been out all night collecting wood for us so
we can be warm.”

He showed a few sticks and smiled. I looked back at the dying fireplace.

“Mary?”

“John, I just…”

“I’m so cold,” he said, his voice shaking.

“Okay.”

He smiled again. In the distance behind him, a glimmer of orange showed.
The sun was rising. I must have slept for a long time.

“John, love, please wait a few minutes. Once the sun comes up, I’ll know
it’s you.”

He glanced over his shoulder at the horizon, then back at me. His eyes
were wide, and the pleading in his face was gone.

“Open the door, Mary. Now.”

“John, please, just a few minutes.”

“Open it.”

I clenched my jaw.

“John, just wait.”

“Mary!”

“No.”

“You goddamn witch!”

He disappeared from the window, then ran into the door. The door bent on
its hinges.

“John, stop!”

He ran again. The whole house shook.

“Please, no!”

My heart beat against my ribs. I heard him take a few steps back, then
another bang. The lock was bent, barely holding. One more hit and the
door would go. I heard him step back again and hit it. The door flew
open. John stood before it. His skin was completely pale, the bottom of
his clothes ragged, and there were a few small holes in his neck. I
clenched my frozen fists.

“I got you,” he whispered.

But before he stepped in, the first ray of light came through. John
quickly looked behind him. A screech echoed. As the light shone on his
skin, he turned to dust. 

I stood there panting. At least he brought the wood. I wedged the door
back into its frame and carried the sticks to the fireplace. They were
so cold they burned my palms. The fire ate them slowly, but luckily they
burned at all. 

“Thank you, John,” I whispered.

Outside, past the trees, something screeched again.
