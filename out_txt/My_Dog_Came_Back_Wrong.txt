(Im not good at punctuation and spacing. So some sentences might be a
little weird)

Before I start, I’ll first say that this takes place in the northern
part on Canada. I have a Labrador retriever named Mia. She’s the
sweetest dog you could ever have. she barely barks and clings to you
like a toddle. it’s adorable.

I’ve had her for almost six years.

She’s one of those dogs that follows you everywhere. If I go upstairs,
she waits at the bottom until I come back down. If I’m cooking, she lays
on the kitchen floor and watches me. When I sit on the couch, she
somehow manages to take up most of it despite being the one who
technically weighs less than me.

She’s also incredibly friendly.

That’s why what happened still doesn’t make sense to me.

I live pretty far north, in a small community surrounded by dense
forest. There aren’t many houses around us, and once you get past the
road behind my property, it’s basically trees for miles.

At night, it gets really dark.

Not city dark.

There are no streetlights where I live. No glow from buildings. Once the
sun goes down, the forest becomes one huge black wall.

Normally, Mia refuses to go very far from the house after dark.

She’ll go onto the porch, sniff around, do her business, and immediately
come back inside.

That night was different.

It was around 11:40 PM when I let her out.

I remember the exact time because I was half-asleep on the couch and was
trying to convince myself that I should just go to bed.

Mia walked onto the porch like normal. I stood by the doorway and
watched her. She sniffed around the steps, and then the grass.Then she
stopped. Her entire body went rigid.

I thought maybe she’d spotted a rabbit.

“Mia?”

Her head slowly turned toward the forest.

She stared.

I couldn’t see anything.

At first, I didn’t think much of it. There are plenty of animals out
here. Deer, coyotes, foxes, bears. Every once in a while, something will
wander through the property.

Then Mia barked.

One loud, sharp bark.

I stepped outside.

“Mia, come here.”

She didn’t move. She barked again. But this one sounded different. It
wasn’t her normal bark. It was deeper, Angrier.

She took a few steps toward the tree line.

“Mia?”

She barked a third time, then started growling. I could see the hair
along her back standing up. I looked into the forest, nothing. Just
trees.

“Mia, come inside.”

She didn’t listen. She took another step, then another. I started
feeling uneasy. I grabbed my flashlight from beside the door.

“Mia!”

She suddenly sprinted toward the forest.

“MIA!”

She disappeared into the darkness.

I froze.

For maybe five seconds, I just stood there.

Then I heard her barking from somewhere beyond the trees.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Then silence.

Complete silence.

I can’t explain how wrong that silence felt. The forest had been full of
noises a second earlier. Wind moving through branches. Insects.
Something rustling in the bushes, then nothing. I yelled her name.

“Mia!”

No response.

“Mia!”

Nothing.

I grabbed my boots and jacket. I didn’t even bother putting them on
properly. I just shoved my feet into them and ran outside with the
flashlight.

I know what you’re probably thinking.

Why would you go into the forest after your dog?

Because it was Mia, my sweet baby who helped me thought the most
difficult times. I wasn’t going to leave her out there. I followed the
direction she’d run. The grass was wet, and my flashlight beam shook as
I moved.

“Mia!”

Nothing.

I kept walking.

Every few seconds I called her name.

“Mia!”

Nothing.

“Mia, come here, girl!”

Still nothing. I reached the tree line. I stopped. I really didn’t want
to go in.

Something about that place felt wrong. The kind of wrongness you feel
when someone is watching you. I don’t know how else to describe it. I’ve
lived around forests my entire life. I’ve walked through these woods
during the day plenty of times.But standing there at night, looking
between the trees, I suddenly had the overwhelming feeling that I
shouldn’t go any farther.

Still, I stepped inside.

“Mia?”

My flashlight moved across the ground.

Pine needles, roots, branches, But Mia was nowhere to be found. I kept
going. Then I saw something. At first I thought it was a piece of red
cloth.

I walked closer, It was blood.

A lot of it.

There was a large splatter across the grass and leaves.

My stomach dropped.

“Mia?”

My voice cracked as I looked around frantically. There was no body, No
collar, no fur, and definitely no Mia. I started calling her name again.
Louder this time.

“MIA!”

Nothing.

I searched for another twenty minutes and found no trace of her.
Eventually, I had to turn around; I remember walking back to the house
crying, and I don’t mean a few tears, I was completely devastated.

Mia was gone.

I kept thinking about the last thing I’d seen.

Her standing at the edge of the forest.

Growling.

Then running.

I barely slept that night.

I kept waking up thinking I’d heard her scratching at the door.

But every time I checked, there was nothing.

Morning came.

I called some people I knew and told them what happened.

We searched the woods.

Nothing.

No body.

No collar.

No tracks that made any sense.

Just that blood.

By the second day, I had started accepting that she was probably dead.

I hated myself for it, but I did.

I kept thinking about how I’d let her outside.

How I should’ve stopped her.

How maybe if I’d run after her faster…

I couldn’t stop thinking about it.

Then, on the third night, something happened that I still can’t explain.

It was around 2:13 AM.

I was lying in bed when I heard scratching.

Three slow scratches.

From the front door.

I sat up.

My heart started pounding.

Scratch.

Scratch.

Scratch.

I got out of bed.

I walked downstairs.

I didn’t turn on the lights.

I stood in the hallway and listened.

Nothing.

Then I heard it again.

Scratch.

Scratch.

Scratch.

I looked through the window beside the door.

There was something standing outside.

My first thought was that it was a deer.

Then it stepped closer.

It was Mia.

I nearly collapsed.

“Mia?”

She lifted her head.

Her tail wagged.

I opened the door.

She walked inside.

I grabbed her and started crying.

I didn’t even care that she smelled like wet dirt And blood.

I hugged her so tightly she probably couldn’t breathe.

“Oh my God. You’re okay.”

She just stared at me.

That’s when I noticed something.

Her eyes.

They looked… wrong. Not only that but the uneasy feeling from that day
of the forest was even stronger now.

Mia had always had warm brown eyes. These looked almost black in the
hallway.I convinced myself it was just the darkness or maybe the
lighting. I checked her for injuries.

Nothing. Not a scratch, not even a drop of blood. I couldn’t understand
it. I’d found blood in the forest the day she went missing and I was
sure that the blood was hers’. And somehow she was standing in my
hallway like nothing happened.

I gave her water, she didn’t drink.

I gave her food, she didn’t eat.

She just stood there staring at me.

“Mia?”

Her ears twitched.

She slowly tilted her head.

I laughed nervously.

“What’s wrong with you, girl?”

She didn’t move.

Then she smiled.

I know how that sounds, dogs don’t smile like people but this wasn’t her
normal happy expression. Her mouth slowly opened.

Her lips pulled back.

Her teeth showed.

She looked like she was trying to mimic a human smile

every fibre of my being told me to back away from whatever that was and
I did.

But she followed me, still having that unsettling grin on her little
face.

One step.

Then another.

It felt like watching a baby taking its first steps or looking at an
animal coping a human’s walk. Her walking was unsteady.

I took a step backward.

She took one forward.

“Mia?”

She tilted her head again.

Then she made a sound I’d never heard her make before.

It almost sounded like she was trying to speak.

A low, breathy noise.

Something between a whine and a whisper.

I didn’t sleep that night.

The next morning, she was acting normal again.

She ate.

She drank water.

She followed me around.

I almost convinced myself that the night before had been my imagination.

Until I noticed something outside.

There were muddy paw prints leading from the porch.

They went across the yard.

Straight toward the forest.

I followed them.

They stopped about twenty feet from the tree line.

And then they continued.

But they weren’t paw prints anymore.

They were footprints.

Human footprints.

Bare feet.

Leading from the forest toward my house.

I stood there staring at them for a long time.

Then I looked back at Mia. She was sitting in the doorway.

Watching me.

Her head was tilted slightly. And she was smiling that unsettling grin
that still haunts me till this day.

I don’t know what came over me but I picked up a sharp stick, held it up
to Mia, and stabbed down. Blood splattered everywhere, and there lying
the ground, was Mia’s lifeless body on the grass.

But one thing unsettled me more than anything else, under Mia’s skin, I
found something resembling a secondary creature that looked to be
wearing it.

Next thing I knew? I was already looking for other places to live.
Mostly in cities or busy neighborhood. I couldn’t stand being alone.
Especially knowing that there are creatures out there that wear your
skin.
