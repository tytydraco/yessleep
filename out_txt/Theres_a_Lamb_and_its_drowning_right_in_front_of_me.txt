I would like to say maybe I didn’t remember the drive or really any of
this at all but I’d be lying. I remember that drive in excruciating
detail. I’ve replayed it hundreds of times looking for something I
missed; a strange car, a wrong turn, some sign I’d been drinking more
than I thought. Every time I come up empty. 
I was prepared for the long drive to my aunt’s house. It was going to be
right after a 12 hour work day and I’d be driving about four to five
hours so as soon as I got off work I drove straight there. Dirt still
crusted up under my fingernails and shoes full of mulch. I had packed
the night before, prepared a playlist to keep myself awake and, for the
first time in two years, bought myself a pack of black American Spirits
that I’d be ripping all along the way until the box was empty and
crushed up in some gas station parking lot somewhere. I started the
drive with C. Duncan, scraping out of work in my twenty year old
shitbox. Igorrr when I turned onto 59 south. The Garden when I stopped
at the seedy gas station in Wedowee. Crippling Alcoholism once I became
sandwiched between a cop and some asshole buddies on their bikes. I
would pass the double yellow lines if it weren’t for the fact I had an
opened bottle of vodka in the back seat and I couldn’t afford any more
trouble. Four and a half hours later, No Leaf Clover right as my tires
dipped off the side of the old cracked driveway and into the overgrown
lawn of my aunt’s house. I’d been here hundreds of times in the past and
could draw out the entire thing blindfolded but this night it didn’t
look at all like the cute pastel green historical home I’d loved. The
paint was peeling away to grey weather rotted slats, the large front
door bowed and swelled sagging crookedly in its frame and the now
colorless shutters had fallen into the knee high weeds of the flower
bed. 
  I remember the yard too, and the surrounding neighborhood. The dogs
barking behind their chain link fences and the neighbors down the road
smoking out on their porch. I remember taking the opened bottle of
liquor and blunt from under the seat of my car, leaving all my cleaning
supplies, turning the key in the warped front door and stepping inside
the old house. There was nothing weird about this. I drove a road I had
a hundred times to get to a house I had been in a hundred times.
When I got into the house, my headache from all the cigarettes and
exhaustion really set in. It was filthy. 
I was being paid to clean the house. 500$. I really needed that money.
But I wouldn’t start cleaning tonight, no, I would be drinking a little
under half the bottle, saving the rest for the next two days I’d be
stuck here, and falling asleep on the dusty ratty couch in the living
room.
I threw my phone and keys on the old side table, clicked on the tv and
flopped onto the couch dust shooting up and then cascading back into my
face. I watched the shitty romcom dvd that was left behind in the player
and let my body sink further and further into my stupor.
I remember my phone vibrating on the side table next to my head. Once.
Then again. I considered answering it. Instead I rolled over, closed my
eyes, and drunkenly fell asleep during a skip in the disc about an hour
into the movie. Looking back, I think that’s the worst decision I made
that weekend.

My eyes were singed by the rays entering through the windows when my
lids scraped dryly up over them. I groaned and tried to roll away from
the light before realizing something was wrong.
Firstly, I was no longer on the couch. I was lying flat on my back in an
empty room on the floor. For a few seconds I just stared up at the
ceiling, trying to figure out what happened. Maybe I had rolled off the
couch. Maybe I had gotten up in the middle of the night to piss and
passed out somewhere. Neither explanation made much sense.
My whole body was sticky with sweat and stuck to the gnarled carpet
beneath me. I could feel every crumb and bit of dirt pressed into the
back of my arms and legs and neck. The carpet itself felt damp.
The next thing I noticed was the heat. The room was impossibly hot and
thick, like I had just woken up in somebody’s mouth. The air felt wet.
Every breath sat heavy in my lungs.
Then came the smell. Putrid warm vomit hung in the air mixed with
pockets of sickly sweet mildew and rotten food. The smell was so strong
it felt physical, like it was coating the inside of my nose. I thought I
was going to throw up. When I tried to roll onto my elbows and sit up I
noticed the third horrible thing. My head swam violently.
A beam of sunlight shot through the side of my vision and pierced
directly through my skull. I collapsed back onto the floor again.This
was much worse than a hangover. And I’ve had many.
Shivers ran from my toes all the way into my stomach.
The smell was stinging my nose so I tried breathing through my mouth
instead, but it felt like somebody had shoved a dry boulder down my
throat. The taste of the air was somehow even worse. I gave up trying to
sit up.
For a while I just lay there, staring upward and trying desperately to
get enough breath in my lungs to try again. That was when I noticed the
final thing.
This is not even a little bit how I remember any of the rooms in this
house. I had been coming here since I was a kid. I knew every room. I
knew where every hallway led. I knew which bedroom belonged to which
cousin. I knew where my aunt kept the towels. I knew exactly where I had
fallen asleep last night. And this wasn’t it. The ceiling looked low and
wide.
The rafters opened overhead in a way that reminded me of ribs connecting
into a spine running through the center of the room. A broken string
light hung from the middle.
I stared at it. The longer I looked, the more wrong it seemed. The
ceiling looked lower than it should have been. Not by much. Just enough
to make me uncomfortable.
I rubbed my eyes.
The ceiling stayed where it was.
I laughed once. A nervous little breath. “Jesus Christ.”
My voice sounded weak and dry. I finally managed to get myself onto my
elbows. The room tilted. My stomach lurched. I squeezed my eyes shut and
waited for the dizziness to pass. When I opened them again the ceiling
looked lower. I froze. I stared at the rafters. The rafters stared back.
I know how stupid that sounds, but that’s what it felt like. Like they
were watching me. Waiting.
Slowly, as the taste of vomit and mould started to burn deeper in my
lungs, my breathing became tighter instead of improving. The room seemed
to tighten with it. I inhaled. The ceiling seemed lower. I exhaled. It
seemed lower still. I blinked hard.
What the fuck was going on?
I sat up too quickly and immediately regretted it. The room spun. My
vision flashed white. I threw myself onto my hands and knees before I
could fall backwards. The carpet scraped against my palms. The smell
seemed stronger now. Hotter. The air pressed against my skin like a damp
blanket. I looked around, trying to find the doorway. The room suddenly
felt much smaller than before. The walls weren’t touching me. Not yet.
But they felt closer. Close enough that I became aware of them. Close
enough that I couldn’t stop looking at them. The rafters overhead
groaned. A long, low creak echoed through the room. Not the normal creak
of an old house. Something deeper. Something strained. The sound
reminded me of wet wood bending under too much weight. The ceiling
dipped. Only an inch. Maybe less. But I saw it.
I know I saw it.
The panic hit me all at once. I scrambled forward on my hands and knees.
Another groan rippled through the room. The walls seemed to bow inward.
The ceiling sagged lower. I could feel those big rib-like joists hanging
just above my back. Threatening. I couldn’t see much. My eyes watered
every time I opened them fully. The sunlight pouring through the windows
seemed brighter now. Not warmer. Brighter. Painfully bright. I crawled
toward where I hoped the hallway was supposed to be. The room groaned
again. The walls pushed closer. The ceiling dipped lower. I dropped onto
my stomach. There wasn’t enough room to crawl properly anymore.
The space ahead of me had narrowed into something that looked more like
a tunnel than a hallway.
The smell of vomit and mould burned in my lungs. The walls squeezed
tighter. The ceiling pressed lower.
I dragged myself forward with my elbows. The rafters scraped across my
shoulders.I could feel them. I could fucking feel them.
The entire house seemed to compress around me. I dared to open my eyes
wider. Ahead of me I could see a small opening.
A tunnel. A gap.
Something barely large enough for a dog door. Hopefully large enough for
me. I clawed toward it. My fingernails dug into the carpet. Splinters
stabbed into my palms. The ceiling scraped across my back. The walls
crushed against my shoulders and hips. I couldn’t take a full breath
anymore. I was one second away from being completely crushed. One
second.Two more feet. One more foot. Then my hands hit wood. A frame. I
wrapped both arms around it and pulled. The house groaned. The ceiling
pressed down harder. I kicked and clawed and screamed and yanked myself
forward. Then suddenly I was through. I spilled out of the tunnel and
onto the floor beyond it gasping for air like I’d just crawled out of a
grave.
