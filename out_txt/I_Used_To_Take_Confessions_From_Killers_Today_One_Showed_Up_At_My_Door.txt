I was a priest for ten years. In that time, I spent a lot of time taking
confession. I would often be brought in to meet with people serving
sentences for the worst of the worst; the true lost souls. And they
would pour into me every sickening detail of what they had done. They
would even share details of crimes for which they had not been
convicted. Of course, I was restricted under canon law to not divulge
any of this. So, all those forgotten bodies will remain buried and
forever lay within me. I was the life raft in their sea of sin; nothing
was hidden from me. Except for one case.

 

 

A man named Michael. He was only twenty-four years old when he was
convicted. He had murdered and molested four children - of this all were
sure. The bodies had been buried in his backyard, and the police had
found him in possession of the indecent photographs he had taken. Still,
when I met with him after he had been given his life sentence, he still
denied it. He told me in detail how the devil had supposedly taken him
hold and made him do all the things of which he was accused. He had
fallen within himself, protecting himself from the horror of what he had
done with mad stories. Despite this, he had not allowed his attorney to
enter an insanity plea.

 

“I’m not crazy sir and I will die before I say I am”

 

He had held this story through all our numerous meetings. Eventually, I
had to begin refusing to see him, there is only so much of this business
one can take, even a priest. I left the priesthood soon after that, for
other personal reasons I will not divulge here. All this to say he was
the last person I had expected to see show up on my doorstep yesterday.

 

 

I had been in the kitchen making dinner when the doorbell rang. I had
been expecting an important parcel for some time, so did not make any
hesitation in opening the door. I really wish I had, for standing there
in front of me was Michael. Here, for reason uknown to me, was the
embodiment of all I considered cruel and evil. But there was no hellfire
behind his eyes or cawing of crows to beckon his arrival. What stood
before me was a man ruined. He was unwashed and his long black hair was
strewn wildly around his shoulders. He retaled to me of how they had
finally found the real culprit of the crimes for which he had been
accused, who had then detailed to the officers in great deal how he had
set up Michael. All of this was of course ridiculous. He was just as mad
as when we had met. He wasn’t even sticking to the same story now, what
about his apparent possession? The man must have escaped; it would
explain his miserable state. But why come here?

 

“To get to the truth of it, I don’t have anyone else Father. My mother
disowned me soon after my arrest and I don’t have anyone you would call
friends.”

 

I don’t know why I did it. Maybe, it was some long-held guilt caused by
my leaving the priesthood and something in the way he had called me
father, so full of hope and reverence. Perhaps, it had been the terrible
mood I have been in for some time, that lowered my defences. Whatever it
was, despite all reason, I let him in.

 

 

Having ushered him in, I returned to the kitchen. I didn’t have a
particular appetite anymore rather I was more interested in observing
him. From the kitchen door I could see into the living room where
Michael was sat. I watched him in this fashion while I finished my
cooking, half expecting him at any moment to produce a blade from his
coat and rush me with it. But this didn’t happen, instead he remained
seated quite politely, offering me only the occasional glance as if he
too was observing me. Having finished, I finally returned to the living
room and asked him if he would be wanting any food. Despite my
insistence, he adamantly refused. I can’t imagine he would’ve had a
cooked meal in some time, but I did not fancy pushing him, so I let it
be.

 

 

We sat in silence for some time as I ate. He remained sitting idly,
surveying the space with curiosity. He seemed particularly interested in
the various framed images of my family. It was then that I finally
considered the ultimate question. How had he known I lived here? Not
once had I mentioned my home in any of our meetings. Part of me didn’t
want to ask him in chance it might disturb his mood, but the question
would not leave, it was eating me up inside.

 

“So, Michael, if you don’t mind my asking. How did you know where to
find me?”

 

He detailed to me how after wandering the streets for the few days
following his release, he had recalled our meetings. He said that our
meetings had been a source of great comfort for him while he was inside,
that I was the only one who treated him with kindness. He’s clearly not
a great judge of character, I also remember those meetings, I was
terrified of him. He continued about how he had gone to my old church
and asked for me. That another priest there had given him my address.
Was I really going along with this? I mean the man was a pathological
liar. All I knew was, I intended to give my old church a not so kind
phone call about giving my address to convicts – if I ever got the
chance.

 

 

Once I had finished eating, Michael finally asked me the question he had
clearly been waiting to ask since he arrived.

 

“I’m sorry to ask, and you are within your right to tell me to fuck off,
but with it being so awfully cold tonight…”

 

I had known this was coming and had been tearing myself apart
considering my answer. I mean if his story was true, this was an
innocent man in need of help. And even if he wasn’t didn’t it fall upon
my priestly responsibilities to help this man. What was I saying? I had
no “priestly responsibilities”, I wasn’t a priest. This was a crazed
murderer in my home. Though, he didn’t have much of a crazed look about
him. Finally, I sent him upstairs to shower while I considered.

 

 

He came down ten minutes later wrapped in a towel I had given him. While
he had showered, I had produced a set of my own clean clothes for him, a
going away gift. I had decided that it was not within my
“responsibility” to put myself in danger for this man, but I could still
do him this kindness. I was about to tell him all this when, having
turned to return upstairs to change, I had seen his back and been
stopped in my tracks. All along the mans back was this gruesome wound,
like he had been sliced down the spine and peeled open. However, the
wound had not naturally healed and scarred over. It looked as though the
skin had been welded shut, the flesh weaving itself like a stitch over
the wound. Was this from abuse he had suffered in prison? It’s common
knowledge how inmates treat those convicted of crimes relating to
children. Could I really send a man in this state back onto the streets?
I mean maybe he was innocent, after all the church had believed his
story enough to trust him with my address. I gave him the couch.

 

 

It was a long time before I was able to get to sleep that night. With
every bump or creak, I would shoot up thinking it was him coming
upstairs to butcher me. But eventually, I surrendered to my exhaustion
and fell asleep. I don’t know long I was out before I was awoken by a
voice. It took me a minute to parse through my dreary daze before I
could tell what was being said. It was Michael.

 

“Father, are you awake?”

 

I took a moment to swallow my fear before replying.

 

“Yes.”

 

“Will you pray for me Father?”

 

Another moment. I had not told him I was no longer a priest. Maybe that
was wrong.

 

“Yes, I will.”

 

Suddenly, the shuffling of feet as someone went down the stairs. In my
tiredness, I had imagined he was calling up from downstairs. No, he had
been outside my door. Another long time followed before I fell back
asleep.

 

 

It was then that I had begun to dream. I remember at the time feeling
that this dream had been incredibly vivid and important. Though now all
I can remember is the end. I had been standing before my parish again. I
was delivering a sermon, but no one was looking at me, each engrossed in
private conversation. Then the ground had begun to shake. I woke up. The
ground didn’t stop shaking. Something was shaking my bed. A weight
pressed into my back. Hot air licked the base of my neck. It was a
moment longer before I realized what it was. Someone had crawled into
bed with me. Right behind my head, Michael whispered to me.

 

“It’s so cold…”

 

I couldn’t move. My body filled with such terror that I felt I would
throw up. I just waited like a man on the executioner’s block. An hour
must’ve passed before I regained control of my body. I believed that he
must’ve have now fallen asleep, as he had begun to stir and prod into my
back. Slowly, I rose to leave, snapping my head around to watch him as I
did. That had been a mistake. He was staring back at me. He had not
risen to stop me but merely followed my movement with his gaze. I
slipped back into bed, believing that in leaving he would surely grow
angry and kill me. Once I had settled myself back down, his arms slipped
slowly around my chest, pulling me into him. I laid there awake the rest
of the night, feeling his breath against me the whole time. Often, I
would turn to face him, plotting escape once more but once I met that
silent gaze all courage would flee me.

 

Only once the sun had risen to a point where the whole room was now
flush with its light did I find the strength to rise from bed. But now
he was no longer there. This horrified me most of all, it meant he must
be hiding somewhere, waiting. Why was he taking such glee in torturing
me like this. What had I done to deserve it? Cautiously, I emerged out
onto the landing. I crept down the stairs. Then through the living room.
The kitchen. The bathroom. No one was there. Still, I couldn’t rest. I
burst out the front door and got into my car. I even checked the rear
mirror as I did, expecting him to fly at me from the backseat. I opened
my phone and furiously tapped in the number for the local penitentiary.
How had I recalled it now, so long since I had worked as a priest, I
don’t know. It seemed to be summoned to front of mind in my time of
need. A lady’s voice came through from the other side. Frantically, I
asked her about Michael. Had he been released? Had he escaped? Was he
considered dangerous? She didn’t reply right away. When she eventually
did her voice was softer, almost ashamed.

 

“I’m sorry to tell you sir but Michael ******* is dead.”

 

She explained in full grisly detail about how it had happened. There had
apparently been a leak of the prisoner’s private information. It
included within it details of their conviction, even those of which that
had been kept a secret for their own safety. A group of fellow inmates,
currently unidentified, had approached him in the laundry room. He had
been beaten to death. This did not shake me as I might’ve expected it
to. It was like I knew what was coming when I called, maybe since I had
opened the door. That all seemed so far away now. Everything felt far
away.  Solemnly, I returned inside. Waiting for me was Michael, sitting
on the couch. Now he looked as he truly was. His whole body was covered
in the welts and bruises. Above his eyebrow, was a cut about six inches
long and his right cheekbone was caved in. His eyes, now devoid of all
colour, were staring into me.

 

He’s still here, watching me now. I don’t know why he’s here or if he’ll
ever leave. The wound on his back is still there, it seems to ripple and
pulse with his now shaky breath, why had the women not mentioned how he
had gotten it? What I need is to get away from the house and clear my
head, but there’s something about his gaze. I think I’ll stay with him a
while.
