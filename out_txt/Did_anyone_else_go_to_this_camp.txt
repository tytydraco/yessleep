Recently, I got together with some old friends and the topic of camp
came up. Most of us were in Girl Scouts, or just had parents who didn’t
like us very much, so we all got to experience the joys of a summer
sleepaway camp. But I was the only one who was sent to Camp Parish; a
relatively smaller campground for some girl scout troops. I was a little
shocked when my friends informed me I have never talked about it before,
as I really cherish the memories I made there. So I told them the basic
stuff.

I was around 7 when I went, my first and only year there unfortunately.
The cabins were so nice and we got to swim in a beautiful lake. The
counselors were also so much fun! The conversation went on and
eventually our lunch meet came to an end. As I drove home though, I just
couldn’t get it out of my head. I could’ve sworn I talked about camp all
the time, but really thinking about it now; I don’t think I’ve brought
it up in ages. So I decided I’m going to write down what I remember
here, like an online diary. Maybe others have been there and I’ll find
other past scouts! Before I jump right into it though, I’ll explain a
bit more about Camp Parish.

Camp Parish was moreso the local name for it, the official name I do not
remember and neither does my mother. Surprisingly. But if you went
there, you’d definitely know it by that name. The reason why it was a
smaller campground was because it was actually a huge property that was
cut in half. One side of the lake was for girl scouts, the other side
was for the boy scouts. The only time we saw the other campers was on
Sundays, cause scouts is also a church thing. I didn’t care much for
that part, I’ll be honest. But despite it being small, we still had a
lot of room to run around. Especially in the forest and the lake shore.

I’ll start off with my clearest memory. I’m hoping the more I talk about
it, the more I’ll remember. I also have some pictures laying around, I
think, those will help jog some memories. My clearest memory, despite
how positively I remember camp, is actually about an injury I sustained.
Left me in the infirmary for half a week. I missed archery over it.
Still bitter about it, as you can tell. I think this was around my
second week there, of the 8 weeks that took place over the course of the
summer.

I had gotten the injury during the beginning of the week’s activity:
Lifejacket Safety. Less of an activity and more of a lesson, but those
were kinda the same thing throughout the duration of camp. This was
getting us prepared for later in the month when we would take canoes out
further onto the lake. We learned how to safely harness the lifejackets
and how they worked. I remember being specifically fascinated by the
water-activated light each vest had.

One by one, lined up on the edge of the dock, we jumped into the water
to make sure our vests were secured. It was a nice break from the
heatwave that had started our way. I was one of the last to jump in, I
remember cause I was getting antsy for my turn. I was way too eager,
swimming was and still is something I love to do. Finally I got to jump.
The water was murky but I knew it was pretty deep so I jumped up high,
wanting to see how deep I could get before the lifejacket ricocheted me
back to the surface.

The water was so refreshing. Cold but not freezing. For the briefest
second, I was able to open my eyes. Bubbles surrounded me from my
submergence. I couldn’t see very far in front of me, but I caught
glimpses of the small fish that called that lake home. Then it happened.
I’m not sure how, I couldn’t have been that close to the dock. But as my
lifejacket did its job of forcing me back up, my leg scraped against the
dock. One of its posts. I breached the water screaming. Everything
between that and laying down in the infirmary is a blur.

Like I said, I was out of commission for the rest of the week and missed
out on archery. The nurse was incredibly nice though and brought me a
stuffed animal from the trading post. A consolation for the pain, I
guess. It was a raccoon, I think. It had the camp’s crest on a little
bandana. I have no idea if I even still have it, but I treasured it the
rest of my time there. Named him Ranger. Every night while I was in
there, before the mandatory lights out, my cabinmates would visit me and
talk about how archery was going along. I know they were just trying to
keep me included, but it only made me feel worse. Knowing how much I was
missing out on.

After I had healed enough to be let out of the infirmary, the first
thing I wanted to do was check out the dock. I’m not sure what led to
the compulsion, but I guess I just wanted to see how my leg had managed
to scrape against the dock post like that. Two friends came with, cause
we weren’t allowed to go anywhere without a group of three. Honestly, we
shouldn’t have been at the lake without a counselor but we didn’t fully
comprehend that rule at the time.

I stood on the shore, studying the dock. My friends told me how far they
had seen me jump. It all just seemed unreasonable. Even if a current had
somehow pushed me back towards the structure.

Well, typing all of this achieved my intended purpose, but now I’m not
sure how to feel about the result. I, obviously, hadn’t remembered this
beforehand but after we got back to the cabin my friends wanted to see
the scar. I know, morbid little girls. It was solely for curiosity’s
sake. When I removed the bandages, we all looked at the scar. Still not
fully healed, but it was getting there. My bunkmate was looking down
from her perch and offhandedly remarked:

“That’s not a wood scratch.” We asked her how she knew that. She said
she had fallen out of a tree just earlier that year. We compared scars
and she was right. The ones from her arms were jagged and patchy. Mine
was a singular slash up my calf. There wasn’t anything in the lake that
could make a cut like that, as far as we knew.

Strange memory. The scar is gone now. Fully healed over from the years
since. But this was fun nonetheless. I think I’ll write up more, as the
memories come back to me. Thanks for reading this far if ya did!
