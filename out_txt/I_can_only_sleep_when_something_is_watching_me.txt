I’ve lived alone since last August. Maybe September, the months stopped
having edges somewhere in there. He moved out and I stayed, and that was
the whole breakup. No fight, nothing to point at.

The apartment felt bigger afterward. Not in a good way. I alphabetized
the spices. I bought plants I already knew I’d kill. The fridge hums all
night and I can tell you what note it’s on.

What I couldn’t do was sleep. Music made it worse, and then podcasts
did, because I’d end up listening to the pauses. I went on one date and,
well. Yeah.

One night I was lying there with the lamp on and I thought about the
deli across the street, the flat roof of it, and how anyone standing up
there could see straight into my bedroom. I thought about that for a
while. Then I was asleep. I slept until nine, which hadn’t happened
since he left.

That worked for about two nights. After that I was awake at one, then
two, then checking the clock to work out how many hours I’d get if I
fell asleep right now. I counted the ceiling tiles, and I get a
different number each time. I turned the pillow over. The fridge hummed.
I thought about the roof across the street because that was the trick,
and nothing happened.

Sometime later I gave up and opened my eyes, facing the window. The
curtain had a gap. I noticed something different on the deli roof. I
watched for a bit and saw a silhouette.

I fell asleep watching it. I slept the whole way through, and felt
better than I had in months.

I couldn’t sleep again the next night. I tried my phone, which made it
worse. I tried reading and got through the same page four times. I got
up, drank water, and got back in.

I remembered the last night. The deli roof. I looked there.

The gap was still in the curtain. I looked through it, and this time it
was a bit more recognizable. An average-height shadow. It was very
familiar, like a man.

Then I woke up in the morning.

I thought about it all day. I kept losing my place at work because I was
thinking about what I saw on that roof. It looked like a man, and it
looked familiar, and that was the part that scared me.

But it had also helped. Every time it was there, I slept. That part felt
nice, and I hated that it did.

That night I couldn’t sleep again. This time it was hot. I took off my
pajamas and lay on top of the sheet in my bra and panties. It didn’t
help. The hotter I got, the worse I felt, and I couldn’t tell why.

I lay there for what felt like hours. The sheet was damp under me and I
could hear my own breathing.

I kept my eyes on the ceiling because I didn’t want to look at the
window, and that was how I knew I wanted to.

I looked. The roof was empty.

I stayed looking at it for a while. It should have felt like relief. The
gap in the curtain was exactly the same and there was nothing in it, and
all I could think was that it had gone somewhere.

The room smelled different, warm and stale. I hadn’t noticed when it
started.

Then I turned my head and it was standing by the closet, close enough to
touch if I’d sat up. Same height as before. Facing the bed. The lamp was
on and I could see the closet door behind it, the handle, the sleeve of
a jacket I’d left hanging, and I still couldn’t make out a face. I’m not
sure it had one.

I didn’t scream. I told myself to move and my arms stayed where they
were.

I don’t know how long we stayed like that. At some point I stopped being
afraid, and that scared me more than anything else had.

I don’t remember falling asleep. In the morning the room was empty.

After that night I couldn’t sleep again. I tried everything I’d tried
before: the phone, the books, the ceiling tiles, the water. I lay there
with the lamp on and the roof empty and the room empty. I checked the
closet. I checked it again. I left the door open, then shut it, then
opened it.

It was the same the next night and the night after that. By the end of
the week the days had lost their edges the way the months had.

Then I started missing emails, making mistakes, forgetting things. I
felt like I was going insane. Now I can’t even remember how many days
have passed, and it’s night again. I’m writing all of this because maybe
it will make me sleepy, or maybe I can find help.

Whatever that thing was, it’s still in my head and it still scares me.
But I haven’t slept in days. This is the point where my life might be in
danger, and that thing is the only thing that can put me to sleep.

I don’t know how to invite it or call it back. Even if I find out, I
don’t know if I want that.
