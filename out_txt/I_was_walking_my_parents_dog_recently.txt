A few nights ago I was out walking my parents’ dog, Milo. Milo is a
small Jack Russell with a big attitude and an even bigger love of walks.
He’s so devoted to them that he regularly tries to convince other
members of the family that he still needs his walk for the day, just so
he can sneak in a second (or third) lap of the neighborhood. The moment
the leash comes out, his tail goes full speed and he’s usually the one
dragging me out the door.

This walk was different. It was late, the street was quiet, and the only
light came from a couple of streetlamps and the occasional porch light.
From the moment we stepped outside, Milo kept pulling back toward the
house. He’d take a few steps forward, stop, look around, then turn and
tug in the opposite direction. For a dog who treats every walk like a
grand adventure, this was strange. Something had him spooked, and I was
clueless as to what.

Our walks tend to be short anyway. We go down the block, around the
corner, and turn back once we hit the alley. That night I figured I’d
keep it to the bare minimum and get him home. We’d made the turn at the
alley and were on our way back when I heard it: a heavy thud in the
street right next to me.

Curiosity got the better of me, so I walked over to see what had made
the noise. At first glance, I could tell it was a dead bird. Then I
looked closer and realized it was half eaten. And this was a big bird, a
pigeon or something close to that size. It had hit the pavement with
real weight, and there was a lot less of it than there should have been.

That’s when I put two and two together. A bird that size had clearly
been someone’s dinner, and whatever had been eating it was almost
certainly still up in the tree directly above Milo and me. I looked up
into the branches, saw only dark leaves and shadows, and felt the hair
on the back of my neck stand up.

Milo, to his credit, stayed calm. He held his ground like a good boy,
though I could feel through the leash how badly he wanted out of there.
Frankly, so did I. We made a beeline straight for my parents’ front
door, and I’ll admit my pace was closer to a jog than a walk.

Once Milo was safely inside, I told my parents what had happened. My mom
was immediately curious and wanted to see it for herself, so I headed
right back out into the dark, back to that same spot in the street, to
snap a picture of the half-eaten bird. I crouched down under the same
tree, took the photo, and walked back to the house.

It was only once I was inside, door shut behind me, that it hit me. I
had just done the exact thing every doomed character does in every
horror movie ever made. Strange noise in the dark? Go investigate. Clear
sign of a predator lurking overhead? Go back for a closer look. Alone,
at night, solo with a camera? Perfect.

Surely I would have been the first to go.
