The problem with “NO SOLICITING” signs, in my experience, is that they
don’t work for shit. On the contrary, the “solicitors” seem to take it
as a dare, a bluff to call as to whether or not you will actually
enforce the rule. And I most certainly am bluffing. I’m incapable of
getting rid of anyone that comes to my door: salespeople, landscapers,
roofers, window installers, exterminators. I have an intense compulsion
to be polite, and to avoid conflict, rudeness, offense, at all costs. In
part, this compulsion can be explained by the yes-ma’am-no-sir
environment I was raised in. But beyond that, the compulsion is driven
by fear. For me, “solicitors” are a near constant source of anxiety and
dread. I jump at every knock, startle at every ring of the doorbell. I’m
haunted by visions of these “solicitors” getting offended by me somehow,
by me not taking their pitch seriously, by me not buying what they are
selling, and then the “solicitors” exacting some sort of
vocationally-specific revenge on me: the spurned exterminator releasing
jars of spiders or cages of rats under my porch, the landscaper
spreading the seeds of invasive weeds, the window guy throwing rocks
through my windows, or using his window expertise to enter my home
uninvited. After all, every single one of them, by their very nature,
knows exactly where I live. But yes, I still have the sign.

My wife on the other hand, at eight and a half months pregnant, is 176
prenatal pounds of no-nonsense. If I don’t get to the door first, my
wife will simply open the door, say, “No,” and close the door in the
“solicitors’” face before they can get out word one. She cares little
about their feelings, and even less still about what they’re selling.
Her approach to dealing with the door-knockers and bell-ringers has
caused my own fear to increase exponentially as I imagine my poor,
pregnant wife falling victim to the sadistic plots of scorned
“solicitors”.

Now that I’ve found the “NO SOLICITING” sign to be completely
ineffective, I have taken to hiding. I avoid the front half of the house
during the day, ducking beneath windows that face the street, or just
keep the blinds down 24/7. But the cunning of the “solicitor” is not to
be underestimated. They seem keenly aware of my habits and movements, as
if they have me under constant surveillance, and they always seem to
arrive at our door just as we are leaving the house for a walk, or the
moment we pull into the garage.

The garage invaders are the worst of all. We somehow never notice them
until we are fully parked in the garage, like they’ve been waiting in
the bushes for us to return, and then suddenly there they are in our
garage or just beyond the garage door. The more polite among them
announce themselves clearly so as not to alarm you, but others don’t
announce themselves and just scare the absolute shit out of you as you
step out of your car to find a stranger in your garage with you.

The SafeSoap salesman was of this latter,
scare-the-absolute-shit-out-of-you camp.

I pulled into my garage on Sunday morning after a quick trip to the
library, grocery store, and coffee shop. At the library, I had picked up
a massive tome my wife had requested on “preparing for your first
child”. (One look at the size of the book and another at the size of my
wife’s belly, and you’d know that the kid would already be on to solid
foods by the time she finished the book, but I kept this to myself.)
After the library, I had picked up some essential groceries, and then
had stopped at our favorite coffee shop to get a coffee for myself and a
low-caffeine London fog for her.

Impressing no one at all, I decided to get everything into the house in
one trip by holding the groceries under one arm, and the big baby book
under the other, and balancing the two hot cups on the spine of the book
wedged under my arm. I made it out of the car maintaining this balancing
act well enough. But when I turned to kick the car door closed, and saw
the short man standing there in the shadows of the garage, I lost
control of the book and the cups on top of it, and the stack of book and
cups fell first against the door of the car and then onto the ground,
splashing tea and coffee onto the car, and the garage floor, and all
over the big baby book.

The man stood just inside the garage door, disrupting the beam of the
garage door’s safety sensor so I couldn’t have closed the garage on him
even if I wanted to. My wife would have wanted to, I knew.

“Is now a good time?” the man asked. He was short, with muscles that
stretched the openings of his t-shirt sleeves. He had a bleached blond
crew cut, and a tattoo under his right eye—some very small but
complicated symbol I couldn’t interpret or even see clearly from a
distance. When the man smiled, I could see he was missing one of his
left canines, and that one of the right canines was capped in gold. He
wore a large blue backpack that bulged.

“Sorry, not exactly,” I replied, as I grabbed a large handful of tissues
from the car and started to wipe off the baby book.

“Well, I won’t take up too much of your time then. We’re here in your
neighborhood, today only, with a truly great deal on SafeSoap-10-in-1,
and since your family is expecting a little one, I just couldn’t live
with myself if I didn’t give you the opportunity to make your house a
cleaner, safer place for the little angel.”

And like that, I felt the sinking feeling I always feel when the pitch
has started and I know I am powerless to stop it.

I picked up the book, and then the groceries, and started to walk
towards the door to the house, trying to express through my movements
just how busy the day ahead of me was, hoping the man would notice. The
man ran past me saying, “Oh, let me get that!” and opened the door for
me. I thanked him and walked through the door, meaning to hold the rest
of the conversation there in the doorway with me in and the other man

“I’m sure you’ve heard of SafeSoap-10-in-1. Maybe your neighbor Helen

thoroughly and rigorously tested on animals. The man asked a question

“If you had to estimate, how many gallons of soap do you think you use

By now I was completely lost, and hadn’t answered any of the man’s

“…and that’s number 4 on that info sheet you’ve got there. This stuff
isn’t just for washing your hands. It will work great as a body wash,

I put the info sheet down on the counter and reluctantly put out my

Red, irritated, rough.” He stroked the backs of my hands. Then the man

“Ha, yes, you got me. Mine are pretty bad too. But I haven’t been using

With this last bit, he interlaced his fingers—which still appeared slick

“Typically?” I asked, still struggling to follow, the man spoke so fast.

“Look I get it, I can see you’re skeptical. I would be too.” And with

That night, I had trouble falling asleep. I lay awake in bed next to my
sleeping wife, trying not to think about the short blond man with the
gold tooth. At 1:15 a.m. I glanced at the clock, and then fell asleep
moments later. I had terrible, blue-tinted nightmares where I was
drowning. Then I awoke with a start, and glanced at the clock, which
read 1:20 a.m.

I lay in bed, tossing and turning for a few more minutes, and then sat
bolt upright. The garage. I had forgotten to close the garage. It had
been open all day, I realized. And all night.

I slipped out of bed, careful not to wake my wife, but as soon as I had
closed the bedroom door I was running. I crossed the living room to the
kitchen, and as soon as my foot hit the tile floor, something went
wrong. I was suddenly horizontal, airborne, then lying on my back on the
cold, hard, wet tile floor. Why was the floor wet, I wondered.

“Lee?” my wife called from the bedroom, but I didn’t respond. I was
trying to get to my feet again, but was finding it almost impossible on
the slippery tile. I gripped the side of the kitchen island and used it
to pull myself up, steadying myself against it. I reached for the pull
cord on the light hanging over the center of the island, and pulled it
on. The first thing I noticed as my eyes adjusted to the light was the
wet, blue film on my hand. Looking from my hands to the floor, I could
see that the entire white tile floor had a shiny blue cast to it.
Looking up from the floor, I saw the short blond man standing at the
sink, quietly scrubbing a plate in the sink with his blue hands.

I started to back away from the island too quickly, and slipped again,
landing hard on my ass.

“What the hell are you doing!” I yelled at the man, pulling myself up
again.

The blond man appeared to be soaking wet, and I realized that he was
covered with the blue soap, like a bucket of it had been dumped over his
head. The man didn’t look at me, and didn’t reply.

“Lee?” my wife said again, the voice closer, no longer filtered by the
bedroom door.

“Honey, get back in the bedroom, and lock the door. Call the police,
honey. Now!”

I heard the bedroom door slam closed, and heard my wife’s muffled,
frightened voice from beyond the door. Then another, much closer voice
saying, “Wait til you see…”

I slipped to the floor a third time, startled, and the voice repeated
itself.

“Wait til you see.”

The voice was coming from the other side of the kitchen island, and was
deep and rough, and gave me the impression that someone was talking
through a radio with a poor signal. I could hear a faint squeaking sound
from the other side of the island. I pushed myself along the floor this

island and out of the kitchen. Once I had cleared the tile, I got to my
feet, and looked around the other side of the island. There on the floor
was a large heap of a man, kneeling, his face downcast, scrubbing at the
floor with one of our dishcloths.

“Wait til you see how it shines. How it sparkles,” the heap said, and
then started to rise. The man got off his knees and moved into a low
squat, then he stood up, and it took so long for him to stand up because
there was so much of him to stand. The motion was like the unfolding of
a ladder, or the extending of a monstrous tripod, the body moving upward
and upward, until the man was finally standing straight up and facing
me. The man was a giant. His head stretched past the hanging light so

neck up he was in shadow. The man had to cock his head slightly to one

“I’m sorry my colleague made such a mess of things earlier,” the giant
said, and I could feel the bass of his voice. “I just wanted to make

I was terrified beyond what I had previously believed to be my max

clean you could lick it.”

“Lick it,” the giant said.

“Yeah, no, it looks amazing,” I stuttered, “never seen it so clean.”

“Then lick it.”

“Yeah, no, I…I get your point. You’re saying it’s clean enough to eat
off of, and I would agree with you.”

“No,” the giant growled, “I’m saying LICK IT!”

and breathed in only the sour, blue liquid. I felt the liquid flowing
through my nose, burning my nose and throat, and the dim room turned
dark blue as the liquid flowed over my eyes. The blue started flashing,

from where? And then the pressure was gone from my shoulder and I rolled

the door to the garage in time to see an impossible dark shadow ducking
through the door, and a smaller shadow following after it.

“please!” or “police!”.

I lowered my head back to the floor, because I knew I wouldn’t be able
to get up on my own. I heard my wife running out of the bedroom and
screaming, “Lee, oh fuck, Lee!” and I was suddenly panicked as I

to push myself up but just slid around stupidly, and fell back to the

“Just hold on, babe,” she said to me, and I heard her running off to
open the front door. And I heard the doorbell ring, and the banging on
the door once more, and the shouting of “police” that sounded to me like
“please”, and I wanted to tell my wife to hide, not to open the door,
because I didn’t want whatever they were selling.
