How can a tiny bump in the flow of life turn into a rock and leave my
face stained with blood and pain? Well, despite desperately wanting to
stop thinking about it, I still haven’t been able to get it out of my
head for days.

What I’ve written might resemble the delusional lines of the main
character in John Katzenbach’s The Madman’s Tale, who clings to his pen
like a squid and writes about his experiences on the walls. And you
wouldn’t be wrong to think so. But I’ll get to that later. Because this
situation really isn’t normal.

I won’t drag this out.

I used to work in insurance at a company whose name I’d rather not
reveal. My entire day consisted of my hands dancing across the keyboard
while my eyes remained glued to the screen. It was just another day like
that. Everything was going quite normally. I was incredibly excited
because I’d be having my lunch break in 30 minutes. So I was working
quickly to finish my tasks early.

Just then, an email arrived.

I assumed it was a message telling me to archive a file, so I clicked on
it. But that wasn’t the case. It was from someone whose name I didn’t
recognize. Instead of a name, there was an email address beginning with
2101cooper@gmail…

That’s when it clicked. I realized it was Cooper W., a man who had
joined our company a month ago.

I’d seen the guy two or three times at most. And I’d only spoken to him
during one of those encounters. It was a three-minute conversation. I
introduced myself and welcomed him to our company. If you’re wondering
why I did that, well, my boss told me to.

Fortunately, he sent his assistants to show Cooper around the offices.

As for the way Cooper spoke, he was remarkably calm. He sounded like a
very friendly psychologist.

Anyway, here’s what his message said:

“You’re making out with your girlfriend when suddenly the phone rings.
You answer it, and the person on the other end says, ‘What are you doing
with my daughter?’ You tell your girlfriend about it, and she says, ‘My
father is dead.’

SO WHO WAS ON THE PHONE?”

I burst out laughing.

This was a classic, cliché creepypasta. Honestly, I had no idea how many
times I’d read it before.

I replied:

“I pissed myself 20 times reading this. Thanks.”

I sent the message. He didn’t respond. I went back to work.

Everything was normal up to that point.

The next day, I was working again. There were about 30 minutes left
until the usual lunch break when another email arrived.

I opened it.

Same person.

2101cooper@gmail…

This time, the message wasn’t structurally identical to the previous
one, but it belonged to the same genre.

“Two Americans are walking through a forest when they come across a
massive grizzly bear. One of the men immediately takes a pair of running
shoes out of his backpack and starts putting them on. The other man
looks at him in disbelief and says, ‘Are you insane? You can’t outrun a
bear!’ The man tying his shoes calmly replies, ‘I don’t have to outrun
the bear. I just have to outrun you.’”

At first, I paused.

Then I burst out laughing again.

The joke was NOT funny. In fact, I’d laughed precisely because it was
NOT funny.

I wanted to write something in response, but there was no need to get
overly familiar. So I simply sent a smiling emoji and went back to work.

Two days passed like that.

And the same thing kept happening.

Thirty minutes before lunch, an email would arrive. The message would
always come from Cooper. And every time I opened it, I’d read one of
those supposedly hilarious jokes and chuckle to myself.

To be honest, as much as I enjoyed this, I’d be lying if I said I didn’t
find it strange.

Personally, I send my friends something every day. But he wasn’t even my
friend. As far as I was concerned, he was just another person.

I figured he probably wanted to become friends and didn’t know how to
start, so he was sending me these cliché jokes.

The next day came.

Due to a series of problems at work, my boss had practically torn me
apart with profanity. He even threatened to take my tie, throw it in the
trash, and leave me homeless. You know, that sort of thing.

Naturally, I was in a terrible mood.

I was trying to put my feelings aside and focus on my work when it
happened again.

Same time. Thirty minutes before lunch.

Same person.

Same kind of joke.

This time, it went like this:

“A man is searching for something beneath a streetlamp in the darkness
of the evening. A police officer approaches him and asks, ‘What did you
lose?’ The man replies, ‘I dropped my keys in that dark park across the
street.’ The officer looks confused and asks, ‘Then why are you looking
for them here?’ The man answers, ‘Because the light is here!’”

I didn’t laugh.

But the corner of my mouth lifted ever so slightly.

I just stared at the screen. Without replying, I went back to work.

I had a few invoices to deal with. I tried to put my bad mood aside and
get them sorted.

Just then, another email arrived.

I clicked on it.

And…

I froze.

The message said:

“Wow. Your laughter nearly burst my eardrums.”

At first, I couldn’t understand what was going on.

Then…

I understood.

And the moment I did, I felt my blood turn to ice.

A thought crossed my mind.

“Is this guy… listening to me?”

“Oh, no, I was just—”

I literally jumped out of my seat.

It felt as if someone had thrown an anaconda at me. I instinctively
turned around, and there he was.

Cooper.

He was wearing a red-striped tie and holding a yellow folder. It had the
word “Invoices” written on it.

His face carried an expression that was both surprised and amused.
Although the smile was definitely more prominent.

“What?” I asked, completely bewildered.

“Seriously. I saw you while I was dropping off this folder. You were
reading the message and—”

“No, no… How did that happen?”

My brain had completely shut down. I couldn’t think straight because of
the tension and confusion.

There was only one thing going through my mind.

Why had this guy responded to me?

“Nolan, like I said, I was just passing by and saw you.”

That wasn’t what I meant.

Cooper glanced at the yellow folder in his hand, the one labeled
“Invoices.” Then he looked at me.

He smiled.

“See you.”

“Wait! I haven’t even asked you a question.”

“You did,” he said without looking back. “At least, I think you did.
Have a good day!”

And just like that, he disappeared.

At that moment, a single question formed in my mind.

Was this guy reading my mind?

The next day came.

You can probably guess what happened, can’t you?

You’re absolutely right.

It happened again.

Thirty minutes before lunch.

Cooper sent me another one of his wonderful cliché jokes via email.

I clicked on it.

Here’s what it said:

“A doctor tells his patient, ‘I have two pieces of news for you. One is
bad, and the other is worse.’ The patient says, ‘Tell me the bad news
first.’ The doctor replies, ‘According to your test results, you have 24
hours left to live.’ Shocked, the patient asks, ‘What could possibly be
worse than that?’ The doctor says, ‘I’ve been trying to reach you since
yesterday.’”

I took a deep breath.

I just stared at the screen.

Even though I couldn’t make any sense of the joke, it didn’t look
remotely funny.

I started typing something.

“Cooper, okay, everything’s fine, but why?”

I stopped for a moment.

I stared at the blinking cursor beside the text I was writing.

That sounded a little too confrontational.

I deleted the message and started writing again.

“Hello, Cooper. Sorry, but considering what happened yesterday as well,
I really don’t understand what you’re trying to do. And I’m
uncomfortable with it. I’d appreciate it if you could refrain from doing
this as much as possible—”

Suddenly, I froze.

I felt an emptiness somewhere around my diaphragm. My heart was pounding
in my throat.

I tried to breathe, but I couldn’t.

My breath was gone.

I opened my mouth.

Nothing.

I tried breathing through my nose.

Nothing.

I took my hands off the keyboard. With my trembling hands, I pulled my
water bottle out of my briefcase. I tried to drink, but I couldn’t.

Naturally, panic spread through my body like an electric shock.

Thank God, I eventually managed to regain control.

I spent a while trying to take deep breaths. Every breath I took came
with a faint wheezing sound. Gradually, that too began to fade, and I
recovered.

“Oh, my God…” I muttered.

My heart was still racing because of what had happened.

At that moment, I thought I was going to cry, despite being a grown
adult with a job and responsibilities.

Thank God I wasn’t someone with asthma.

I genuinely felt as though I’d experienced what an asthma attack must
feel like.

I slowly turned back to the screen.

And the moment I did, my pupils shrank.

The message I’d been writing…

It was gone.

I could swear on my life that I hadn’t pressed the Delete key while my
hands were on the keyboard during that episode.

My computer wasn’t infected with a virus either. The assistants ran
virus scans every night.

It couldn’t have deleted itself.

Then why…

Why had it disappeared?

“Because you didn’t like the joke.”

Just like the previous day, I jumped.

I immediately turned around.

And…

There was Cooper.

He was looking at me with a smug expression, as if I’d just said
something incredibly vulgar about his mother.

“Cooper, what the hell is going on?!”

“Nothing is going on, Nolan.”

“What do you mean, nothing?”

“…”

“ARE YOU READING MY MIND—”

Then a woman…

spoke.

“Shhh! Quiet!”

I froze.

I genuinely froze.

I stared at Cooper with wide, bulging eyes. I also looked around, hoping
to find a woman somewhere nearby.

No luck.

There wasn’t a woman around.

But…

“Quiet! People are working here.”

It had happened.

The person speaking in a woman’s voice was Cooper.

Cooper…

Was a man.

But he’d just spoken in a woman’s voice.

And it wasn’t some artificial imitation.

His vocal cords had seemingly changed pitch in an instant, producing a
perfectly natural, beautiful female voice.

At that moment, I felt dizzy.

I placed my hand on my head. I thought I was going to faint, but I
didn’t.

Cooper approached me and placed his hand on my shoulder.

The smug expression he’d been wearing a moment ago was gone.

He was smiling.

“Do what I told you…”

“W-what?”

Cooper took a deep breath.

“I want your brain to send signals that rhythmically contract your
facial and diaphragm muscles, causing bursts of air to escape from your
lungs… Simultaneously increasing your heart rate, lowering your stress
hormones, and allowing the endorphins released to help your body relax
rapidly.”

He stopped speaking.

Then he walked away.

What…

Why?

Why, though?

What was happening?

I couldn’t make sense of any of it.

I know that’s a cliché thing to say, but I genuinely had to say it.

I’m serious.

Even today, remembering what happened that day sends chills down my
spine.

It was terrifying.

Having your breath suddenly cut off, followed by hearing a man speak in
a woman’s voice…

It was simply horrifying.

That day, I could barely concentrate because of the shock and fear.

Over and over again, Cooper’s smug expression kept appearing in my mind.

Every time his face came to mind, it seemed to change.

Its expression gradually began to melt away. His pale skin seemed to sag
downward, replaced by blood.

And that only made me more frightened.

Eventually, I couldn’t take it anymore.

I wanted to talk to Cooper about what had happened, but there was no way
I could do that in my current state of mind.

So I went to my boss and told him I wasn’t feeling well that day.

After a few scoldings, I finally managed to get permission to leave.

I hurried home.

The next day…

Oh, God.

It was happening again.

His face, still melting in front of my eyes, kept frightening me.

Guess what happened.

THE SAME THING AGAIN!

What did Cooper send me?

OF COURSE, ANOTHER UNFUNNY, CLICHÉ AMERICAN JOKE!

When?

OF COURSE, THIRTY MINUTES BEFORE LUNCH!

An email arrived.

I didn’t open it.

I tried my best to ignore it.

I thought maybe if I did that, he’d leave me alone.

Believe it or not, nothing happened.

Cooper didn’t suddenly appear behind me, nor did he speak to me.

Nothing happened.

I was relieved, so I happily continued working.

The workday was over.

The employees were rushing to get ready, eager to head home as soon as
possible.

I was one of them.

I put my files into my briefcase and left the office building.

After walking for a while, I got into my car.

I didn’t feel like cooking today.

And I was craving kebab like crazy.

So what should I do?

Obviously, I needed to open my navigation app and find a nice kebab
restaurant!

I picked up my phone and searched for a good kebab restaurant on Google
Maps.

Once I’d decided on my destination, I hit the road.

While driving, I turned on the radio and rested my elbow on the window,
enjoying the journey.

My favorite song came on.

I hummed along softly.

When my favorite part of the song, the chorus, came on, I got incredibly
excited.

I was just about to sing along at the top of my lungs when—

BANG!

My eyes widened.

That…

That was a gunshot.

And it had come from very close by.

I turned to my right.

BANG! BANG! BANG!

CRAAASH!

The sound of breaking glass.

I screamed and ducked my head.

When I looked to my right again, I saw that my car window had shattered.

I screamed again.

Naturally, I lost control of the car.

It swerved wildly from side to side.

BANG!

Another gunshot.

And…

It had probably passed right over my head.

Somehow, I managed to hit the brakes.

The car came to a sudden, jolting stop.

I lurched forward and hit my head against the steering wheel.

I screamed again.

With the immense surge of panic inside me, I shouted:

“WHAT THE HELL IS GOING ON?!”

Silence.

Not a human scream.

Not the sound of traffic.

Nothing.

I put my hands against my head and slowly lifted it.

Trembling, I looked around.

Nobody…

Nobody seemed to care.

Not even the other cars.

But there had just been a shootout!

Just then, a soft voice spoke through my left window.

“Nolan.”

The voice was familiar.

I slowly turned my trembling head toward it.

Cooper.

He was holding a massive automatic…

Rifle.

Cooper and a rifle.

I couldn’t say anything.

My mouth moved, but no sound came out.

Cooper smiled. Then, seeing the state I was in, he ran his hand through
my hair.

He started gently stroking my head.

“Oh, my God. It’s okay. It’s okay. I’m serious. Nothing’s wrong. Look,
Nolan, nothing’s—”

“WHAT DID YOU DO?! WHAT THE HELL IS THAT RIFLE?!”

Cooper took his hand off my head. His hand remained suspended in the
air.

I continued.

“WHAT THE HELL IS YOUR PROBLEM?! WHAT DO YOU WANT FROM ME?! I’M CALLING
THE POLICE!”

Cooper laughed.

He shook his head.

“Unfortunately…”

“…”

“They can’t see me.”

“W-w-what?”

“They can’t see me…

Or you.”

“COOPER, GET THE HELL AWAY FROM ME—”

He pointed the rifle at my forehead.

He didn’t say anything.

His expression suddenly changed.

It turned into something horribly unhappy and serious.

“Smile,” he said in a flat voice.

I was about to say something when he brought the rifle even closer.

“SMILE!”

I didn’t.

I wasn’t exactly in the mood to smile.

“SMILE! SMILE!”

“I CAN’T SMILE!”

Cooper lowered the rifle.

He slightly opened his mouth and raised his eyebrows.

“Oh, is that so?” he said, pretending to be surprised.

I had absolutely no idea what happened next.

Everything except Cooper went dark.

The trees, the road, the sea…

Everything was swallowed by darkness.

My car, too.

But Cooper remained untouched by it.

He was just standing there, smiling…

Looking at me.

He dropped the automatic rifle onto the ground.

“Calm down,” he said, then covered my eyes with his hands.

And somehow, I began to feel drowsy.

I felt incredibly relaxed.

There was no reason for it.

I was just…

Relaxed.

But it was a strange kind of relaxation.

That’s why I allowed Cooper to keep his hands over my eyes.

He helped me stand up.

Ten seconds passed.

But those ten seconds felt like an entire hour.

Cooper removed his hands from my eyes.

At first, I blinked a few times.

Then my vision cleared.

I was in a gray, dimly lit room.

Cooper stood in front of me, smiling, with his hands behind his back.

There was a light bulb on the ceiling, casting a faint glow.

But…

How had I gotten here?

“Through a mind game,” Cooper replied.

I was stunned again.

Okay.

This man really was reading my mind.

“Cooper,” I said in a trembling voice.

“Nolan… Do you know the benefits of laughter?”

I didn’t say anything.

Cooper continued.

“Nolan… Do you know the benefits of laughter?”

Again, I said nothing.

Cooper sighed.

“Nolan… Do you know the benefits of laughter?”

“W-well—”

“Nolan… Do you know the benefits of laughter?”

“Cooper, please, stop.”

“Nolan… Do you know the benefits of laughter?”

“Cooper.”

“Nolan…”

We both fell silent.

Cooper’s eye began twitching.

I spoke.

“Look, Cooper, I—”

“YOU LITTLE SON OF A—!”

I froze.

Cooper continued.

“WHAT ARE THE BENEFITS OF LAUGHTER?! WHAT?! WHAT?! WHAT?! WHAT?! WHAT?!
NOLAN! WHAT?!”

At that moment, I couldn’t take it anymore.

My head began spinning violently, and I collapsed to the floor, passing
out.

Yes.

I suppose this was how it had to end.

I slowly opened my eyes.

Everything looked blurry.

I blinked a few times, and my vision gradually cleared.

Then I saw him in front of me.

But…

Why was his face gray?

Wait…

What the hell is that?

I squeezed my eyes shut, then opened them again.

My vision finally became clear.

And I screamed.

“WHAT… WHAT IS THAT?!”

It wasn’t Cooper standing in front of me.

It was a woman.

But…

Her face was gray.

Her head was tilted backward, her gaze directed slightly toward the
ceiling.

Her mouth was wide open, and there were nothing but black voids where
her eyes should have been.

The veins in her hands…

Or perhaps her bones…

Were visible.

This…

This…

“Nolan.”

I turned to my right.

Cooper was standing there, smiling at me.

I looked at the woman, then at Cooper.

I was trembling uncontrollably.

I felt like throwing up.

Cooper slipped his hand into his pocket.

He pulled out a voice recorder.

He brought it close to the woman’s ear and pressed the button.

There was laughter playing on the recording.

Exactly two different kinds of laughter.

When the sounds ended, he rewound the recording and played them again.

That…

That was my laughter.

Yes, yes.

My laughter.

And now Cooper was playing it for the woman standing right in front of
me.

He murmured:

“Sweetheart… You can do it… Come onnn… Come onnn… You can do it.”

He spoke incredibly calmly and gently.

After Cooper played my laughter for her, nothing happened.

The woman didn’t move.

She didn’t react.

She simply stood there.

Good God.

Because she was dead…

Cooper began crying softly.

“Please, please, come back to yourself, sweetheart. Please. Come on,
sweetheart… No… No… PLEASE!”

I discreetly tried to stand up.

But there was no use.

I was tied to the chair.

But…

Only my waist was restrained.

My arms were resting on the table.

Why, though?

Cooper turned toward me, tears in his eyes.

He sniffled and sobbed.

Then he forced a smile.

“Smile…”

I looked at him.

He continued.

“Nolan, smile.”

I said nothing.

“Can’t you do it?” he asked. “Seriously… IS IT THAT HARD? HUH? IS IT?!”

“…”

“SHE NEEDS TO HEAR IT! SHE LIKES IT! SHE LOVES IT! SMILE! SMILE,
PLEASE!”

“Y-your wife is dea—”

“NO! SHE ISN’T!”

“G… GO TO HELL!”

Silence.

Cooper stared at me for a while.

Then he slowly nodded his head.

At that moment, a piece of paper and a pen appeared in front of him.

I looked from Cooper to the paper in shock and terror.

Cooper smiled.

“Well, if you can’t do it…”

He pulled an automatic rifle out from under the table.

“Then write. Write something that might inspire me to laugh.”

“W-what?”

“Idiot. Write. Write down what happened. Maybe it’ll inspire a joke…
Nolan… I was the father in that first joke. I was the doctor in the
second one. I was the bear. And now, I’ll be the same thing again. With
you. Write. Write it down so I can look at it and write something too.
That way, we can laugh together. And my wife can come back to life.
You’ve got 30 minutes.”

“But—”

He changed the ammunition in the rifle.

I backed away.

There was nothing I could do.

With my hands still free, I began writing the very words you’re reading
right now.

He’s right in front of me.

My God, he’s right in front of me, and I have no idea how I’m supposed
to do this.

Every now and then, he glances at what I’m writing, but thankfully, he
isn’t looking at the words I’m writing to you right now.

Is this guy a psychic? A demon? I can’t tell.

I don’t want to die.

God, please—

(Beyond this point, there’s a long line extending toward the left. The
paper is also covered in blood.)
