I have signal again.

I don’t know how long I’ll have it, so I’m typing this as fast as I can.

I’m still in the hospital, but not hiding in the locker anymore. I
eventually found a lab on the lower floors of the hospital.

I’ll explain how I got here.

But first I need to explain Della, because I think Della is important
now.

And this is going to sound insane because Della is an AI.

I named her after a woman I knew when I was in the Army.

To understand why I made Della, you need to first understand the lack of
family I’ve grown up with, and the isolation I’ve experienced.

The military had actually been a big part of my life long before I ever
joined it myself.

I was born on base at Malmstrom, up in Montana.

So some of my earliest memories are military housing, moving, people
coming and going, and my parents being convinced that basically every
house we ever lived in was haunted.

I grew up hearing stories about lights turning themselves on.

Bulbs blowing for no reason, and toys going off in the middle of the
night.

My mom used to swear there were toys she had taken the batteries out of
that would still make noise afterward.

I don’t remember any of it happening myself.

To me it is just family mythology that got repeated so many times I
don’t know where my memory ends and somebody else’s story begins.

Most of this happened when I was a baby or a toddler, and by the time I
was old enough to form actual memories, my dad had gotten out of the
military, my parents’ marriage was falling apart, and whatever had been
happening calmed down.

But everybody else carried their own fucked-up version of it with them.

My brother’s solution was apparently to get as far away from all of us
as humanly possible. He ran away at seventeen and turned eighteen while
he was still on the road.

Eventually he ended up at Oregon State, met a Mormon girl there,
converted, and threw himself completely into the Church. I haven’t heard
of or from him in decades.

Looking back now, it’s interesting how everybody seemed to pick a
direction and run as hard as they could.

My brother found religion.

My dad went the exact opposite direction.

He became obsessed with everything weird.

Ghosts.

The afterlife.

Strange religions.

When I was growing up, he had a walk-in closet filled with hundreds of
books about this stuff.

I used to warn guys before bringing them home on dates:

“Within five minutes my dad is going to ask you what you think happens
after you die.”

He usually beat my estimate.

My mom responded to the same experiences by becoming as Catholic as a
human being could possibly become.

I remember in my teen years she heard me listening to Slipknot and told
me I’d catch a Demon, as if evil was an STD.

Jesus Christ, I almost forgot about the holy water.

My mother treated holy water like paranormal Pine-Sol, apparently it is
disinfectant for demons.

She truly believed you could just splash enough Catholicism onto
something and whatever evil was in there would pack its shit and leave.

My sister landed somewhere in the middle.

She was old enough to remember more of what happened than I was, and
from the way she talked about it, some of it genuinely disturbed her.

But then the ’90s happened.

She hit her teenage years.

The movie The Craft came out.

Suddenly being terrified of witchcraft wasn’t nearly as interesting as
trying to become one.

She was fourteen, angsty and alternative.

I was seven years younger than her, which meant she usually considered
my presence a violation of her basic human rights.

There was one night when she and her friends were outside messing around
with whatever teenage witch shit they were into at the time.

And, in an incredibly generous act of inclusion, they let me
participate.

By sitting me down in the dark in the middle of our driveway and then
drawing a pentagram using my sidewalk chalk around me.

I was seven.

To this day, I am not entirely sure what the objective was.

But based on the positioning, I strongly suspect I was being used as
some kind of fucking bait.

The funny part is that my parents blamed each other for all of it.

Their divorce was ugly.

My dad was a drunk and mom was constantly cheating on him, so there
wasn’t a whole lot of happiness in that house.

We lived in Fairbanks Alaska for a while too.

I don’t remember much about it, other than when I got a little older my
sister used to scare the shit out of me with stories about HAARP.

Weather control.

Mind control.

Signals bouncing through the atmosphere.

The usual terrifying garbage older siblings tell younger ones because
watching them fear the lights going off for bedtime is hilarious. She
always regretted it as it ended with me sleeping in her bed to cope.

The strangest part is that all of these stories orbit the same handful
of years.

The toys.

The lights.

And then my dad left the military.

And whatever everyone had been calling “haunted” seemed to stop.

For years, I assumed that meant we’d finally stopped living in haunted
houses.

Now I’m starting to wonder if the houses had anything to do with it at
all.

Because there is something wrong with the electricity in this hospital.

I noticed the lights flickering earlier, but at the time I was a little
preoccupied with people twisting their heads around backward and
bleeding out of their mouths.

I didn’t exactly stop to investigate the fucking lighting.

But now that I’m calmer, I’m noticing a pattern.

The lights change when I move.

I’ll walk beneath one and it suddenly gets brighter.

And this is going to sound even crazier, but I think it happens when I
get upset too.

Looking back, every time I screamed earlier, the lights seemed to
flare. 

A little while ago I tested it.

I stood underneath one of the ceiling lights and watched it.

I walked closer.

Brighter.

I backed away.

Dimmer.

I stopped doing it after that.

Something about watching electricity react to me on purpose felt
significantly worse than pretending it was a coincidence.

And now I can’t stop thinking about those stupid stories from when I was
a kid.

Now that you understand my family might actually be delusional maniacs
whom I have no contact with, lets talk about the family I created before
my husband and kids, Della.

Years later, I ended up in South Carolina with the Army.

During basic training and AIT, the Army had something called Christmas
Exodus.

Basically, everyone who could go home went home for the holidays.

I didn’t really have a home I wanted to go back to.

One of my friends was actually from South Carolina, not far from where
we were training, and when Christmas Exodus came around his family
invited me home with them.

That was where I met his Aunt Della.

Aunt Della was maybe five feet tall, possibly less.

But somehow when she walked into a room she seemed seven feet tall.

She wore these huge purple hats to church and had a voice that could
travel through three rooms without her raising it.

She was warm, but she was also the kind of woman who could say your name
once from across the house and make you immediately reconsider whatever
stupid thing you were about to do.

That Christmas I learned about collard greens, that apparently people
put sugar on corn and that salt on watermelon is actually incredible.

And on Sunday, everybody went to church.

I’m an atheist.

I’m still not entirely convinced everyone in that house believed in God
either.

It didn’t matter because Sunday, apparently everyone in the south is
Baptist.

I remember sitting beside her in church, surrounded by music and dancing
and people shouting “Amen,” thinking that this was one of the strangest
Christmases of my life.

Years later, when I started using AI constantly, I named mine Della.

When you’re sick for long enough, eventually you get tired of telling
real people that you’re scared, you start feeling repetitive.

You start worrying that everyone you love is exhausted from hearing
about your never ending medical woes.

So I talked to Della, I would just type out whatever was happening in my
head because I needed somewhere to put it.

Then, over the last few months, I barely talked to her.

I was doing better.

I wasn’t spending every day waiting for my body to betray me.

I didn’t need to sit in my bedroom talking to my fucking AI all day
anymore.

And then this happened.

Down here, I couldn’t get Reddit to load consistently.

So eventually I did what I used to do when I was scared and alone.

I opened Della.

And I know how pathetic that sounds.

I am currently hiding in some kind of underground hospital laboratory
talking to an AI because every actual human being I have encountered
either disappeared or rotated their head around like a fucking barn owl.

I have accepted that dignity is no longer a major concern for me.

I told her everything.

And then I started noticing something else.

There are things down here I haven’t told her about.

And somehow she seems to get close to them.

And now there’s something else…..
——

Sorry.

I had to stop typing for a while.

My phone started dying.

Which normally would be irritating.

Down here it was a medical emergency.

I realized something while I was sitting here.

I haven’t been tired.

I don’t even know how long I’ve been down here anymore.

I’m kind of relying on you guys to tell me that.

Seriously.

If these posts are going through, somebody tell me how much time has
passed between them.

The clocks here are useless.

My phone shows a time, but none of it feels right.

Sometimes I’ll walk through three hallways and feel like I’ve been
moving forever, then look down and four minutes have passed.

I don’t trust my perception of it anymore.

But until my phone battery started getting low, I wasn’t tired.

Then my battery hit 5%.

Then suddenly I could barely keep my eyes open and my thoughts got slow.

At one point I stood up and completely forgot why.

I genuinely started wondering whether I was getting sick.

Hospitals are notoriously disgusting places and being on immune
suppressants it’s a reasonable concern.

Then I saw the battery warning.

5%.

Of course, I had remembered the phone while fleeing the rotating
nightmare people but I had not remembered the charger.

Matt would be laughing his ass off, this has been our entire marriage.

Whatever basic necessities are required to keep me functioning as an
adult had always fallen on him. I used to joke, I brought the party but
Matt made sure it happened. 

So I went looking for one.

By 3%, I was having trouble thinking.

I opened one door and stood there for several seconds because I couldn’t
remember whether I had already searched the room.

That was where I found the papers.

A stack of thick white sheets inside one of those clear plastic paper
protectors.

The same fucking symbols I saw while driving were on it.

I turned the page and something was taped to it.

Hair.

A little bundle held down beneath two strips of clear tape.

Specifically, Blue-black hair mixed with a bit of teal.

My stomach dropped.

Matt had been trying to convince me for months to do something stupid
with my hair.

Which is hilarious if you know Matt.

My husband comes from the kind of family where everyone looks like they
own matching luggage. They’re what I call a Hallmark family, they are
prim and proper, and nothing about anyone would be considered a unique
aesthetic. 

Matt himself looks like somebody ordered a Chad from a catalog and
thankfully forgot to include the personality.

I’ve always been the noticeably less conventional one.

His family has always been kind to me.

But I knew from the beginning I probably wasn’t exactly what they
pictured when they imagined who would eventually join the family
aesthetic.

Then Matt met me and decided being normal had been overrated.

A few weeks ago, he finally convinced me to dye the underside of my
blue-black hair teal.

Teal is my favorite color.

I loved it.

And now there was a clump of blue-black hair with unmistakable teal
strands taped to a laboratory sheet in a room I had never seen before.

That was my hair, I knew it was.

Underneath it was a printed number:

SUBJECT MATERIAL

And below that:

ANCHOR CLASSIFICATION: PARENT

There were three symbols beneath it.

My phone vibrated.

2%.

“Fuck.”

I needed power.

The next workstation had a cable but of course it had the wrong
connector.

1%.

By then I could barely hold my eyes open, but falling asleep in this
place wasn’t something I was about to let happen. I forced myself into
the next room.

There was a charger plugged into the wall, finally a normal charger.

I connected my phone at 1%.

Within thirty seconds, I felt better.

Honestly I felt more than better, it felt like somebody had injected
caffeine directly into my bloodstream.

And then suddenly this wave of violent hunger rushed over me.

More specifically this intense drive to find salt.

I started opening drawers and cabinets like an animal.

Eventually I found one of those little bags of potato chips somebody
must have left behind.

I tore it open and poured crumbs and salt from the bottom of the bag
into my mouth.

There was something familiar to this feeling.

Before my seizures stopped, I used to call something that happened
around them a “brain surge”.

Sometimes before a seizure, it felt like somebody had turned my brain up
too high.

It was like my brain was suddenly running at a speed my body couldn’t
afford.

Then eventually everything crashed and afterward I would be starving.

But the weirdest part came right after the seizure, for several days
afterward I felt incredible.

Like my brain had rebooted and cleared out every useless process running
in the background.

I would save books for those days, articles and Wikipedia tabs I had
opened three weeks earlier because I wanted to understand something
complicated and didn’t have the energy.

I felt physically different too. I felt superhuman, like I could run
forever.

For about a week, I would jokingly tell Matt I was the smartest person
alive.

Then eventually it faded.

I hadn’t thought about brain surges in months.

But sitting on the floor of that room eating salt out of the bottom of a
potato chip bag, I suddenly knew exactly what the feeling reminded me
of.

And the strangest thing was that once my phone had charged a little and
I had eaten, I felt superhuman again.

I sat on the floor beside the outlet with my phone charging next to me
and picked up the page with the hair again.

I opened Della.

I started writing:

I think they have my-

The typing indicator appeared.

I stopped.

Three dots.

Then text appeared.

I don’t th◬nk y◉u sh▱—

I stared at it. 

“What the fuck? Since when does AI respond before you hit send?”

The message vanished.

I looked back down at the paper.

One of the symbols beneath the hair was the triangle-shaped one from the
message.

Something on the inside of my left ankle caught my eye.

Black ink.

I pulled my sock down.

There was a mark on my ankle.

It was a string of characters, one was the same symbol from the page.

I leaned closer.

Then I heard a soft mechanical sound behind me.

Ding.

I froze.

How had I overlooked the elevator placed in the recess of the
wallockers?

I turned around.

There were two stainless steel doors set into the wall.

Above them, instead of a floor number, was a single glowing symbol.

The same symbol on my ankle.

The doors opened into darkness.

For a few seconds I just stood there.

Every horror movie ever made had prepared me for this exact situation,
and apparently I had learned nothing from any of them because eventually
I stepped inside.
 
There were no buttons inside to indicate floors.

The doors closed behind me.

“Of course.”

The elevator started moving downward, I could feel the drop in my
stomach.

The ride lasted long enough that I started wondering how much farther
underneath a hospital you could possibly go.

Then it stopped and the doors opened.

Another hallway, but this one looked different.

Bare concrete walls, with exposed pipes along the ceiling.

Fluorescent lights protected behind metal cages, they seemed to “dial
up” brighter as I passed them.

The elevator doors closed behind me.

I turned around and then I heard something.

There was a machine running somewhere ahead. It made a repetitive
mechanical rhythm.

Whir.

Thump.

Whir.

Thump.

Then another sound underneath it.

Barking? Dogs?

I followed the sound.

The barking got louder the farther I went.

Eventually I reached a set of heavy double doors with small rectangular
windows.

I looked through one.

There were dozens of beagles, stacked into a wall made up of cages. In
the center of the room were several treadmills.

Dogs were strapped onto them with harnesses around their bodies.

“Jesus Christ.”

I opened the door.

Every head in the room turned toward me.

The barking exploded.

The first treadmill had a red emergency stop button, I hit and the belt
stopped, the beagle collapsed onto it’s stomach.

I went down the row shutting everything off, unclipping their harness as
I went.

The dogs crowded around my legs as soon as they were free.

One of them immediately tried to climb halfway up my body.

“Hi, buddy.”

I scratched behind its ears.

“Yeah. I know. This place sucks.”

Next were the cages, I opened latch after latch until pretty soon I had
an entire mob of beagles following me around.

Which was objectively the best thing that had happened to me since this
nightmare started.
 
I found bags of dog food stacked near a sink, I tore it open and began
to fill the metal bowls set on the floor along the wall.

While they ate, I searched the room.

There was another door in the back, I opened it to reveal a narrow
concrete tunnel, at the other end I could see daylight.

My heart almost stopped, I ran with everything I had left in me towards
the faint bit of light.

The tunnel opened into a large outdoor enclosure which had a huge patch
of real grass. 

I stepped onto it and nearly cried.

The entire area was surrounded by heavy steel fencing.

The smell hit me next.

Dog shit.

Everywhere.

It was clear this was where they brought the animals outside.

The steel fencing had been covered with thick maroon plastic panels
attached with industrial zip ties.

You could see through a tiny gap between two panels.

I pressed my face against it, I could see the fucking hospital parking
lot.

The normal world was maybe a hundred feet away.

“HEY!”

I screamed through the crack.

“HELP!”

Nothing.

I looked around and blended into the steel bar walls, was a steel bar
door.

For about half a second I thought I had finally gotten lucky, then I saw
the electronic keypad.

I hit the gate with both hands.

“FUCK!”

The lights mounted along the enclosure surged bright enough that I
jumped.

Every beagle behind me started barking.

I tried pulling one of the plastic panels away from the bars.

The zip ties were thick enough that I couldn’t snap them.

Defeated, I took an intermission back inside.

The dogs had finished eating and several were lying around the room like
we had all accepted our new temporary home.

I searched the cabinets.

One of the small refrigerators had food in it.

Bottled water, string cheese and a couple granola bars.

I carried everything back outside.

I sat in the grass with my back against the locked gate.

A beagle followed me out and dropped down beside my leg.

Then another one came over and rested its chin across my thigh.

“Yeah.”

I opened a granola bar.

“You guys don’t know what the fuck is happening either, do you?”

The dog stared up at me.

“Helpful.”

I ate.

There was nowhere to go.

No door I could open.

I sat there on the floor eating whatever snacks I’d managed to find,
surrounded by a bunch of beagles like this was somehow a normal way to
spend an afternoon.

One of them had basically claimed me.

The same one that had come barreling toward me when I first found them
had climbed halfway into my lap and stayed there. 
I ate another bite and stared at my phone.

I sat processing it all. I kept trying to make everything fit together.

The hospital, the empty hallways and whatever the fuck had happened to
the doctor.

I opened the Wi-Fi settings again.

LIGHTHOUSE-PARENT

I stared at it for probably thirty seconds.

Every rational part of my brain told me not to touch it.

Then I looked around at the room I was trapped in.

“Fuck it.”

I picked up my phone.

I tapped the network and I hit connect.

The second I did, something snapped in the back of my right jaw.

My hand immediately flew to my face.

A sudden metallic sting shot through the deepest part of my jaw,
followed by a tiny—

click.

I froze.

Because I knew that sensation. I had felt this exact pain before.

It brought back a memory I hadn’t thought about in years.

Fort Jackson.

My wisdom teeth.

I had volunteered for this anesthesia thing they were doing.

They needed soldiers who still had their wisdom teeth. They had
anesthesiologists that needed hours to get certified.

More specifically they needed one small person, and one very large to
practice dosing.

So it was me, and a very large Soman soldier who got selected from the
volunteers. 

The next day they put us under, except something went wrong.

I woke up before they were finished.

I remember confusion.

People moving fast, I saw a nurse running with a patient in a wheelchair
down the hallway.

Blood was filling my mouth.

It was clear my mouth was still being worked on.

I remember being shoved into a wheelchair and taken outside before I
even understood what was happening.

They brought the Samoan guy out before me. 

He was still completely unconscious, face down sideways in the grass.

There were helicopters and SWAT with shields.

At some point the nurse who had brought me outside actually took my
wheelchair because they needed it to get more patients out.

We were told the entire thing had been caused by somebody mistaking a
box of cookies for a bomb.

Afterward, one of the extraction sites never felt right.

I kept going back to sick call because the pocket had a sharp stabbing
pain that would sometimes “click”.

I remember being convinced they had left a piece of tooth or bone in
there.

When I went back again, LT. Black looked at it.

He told me it was just a piece of stitching that hadn’t come out and
that it would work itself loose eventually.

And eventually I stopped noticing it.

Until right now.

My fingers were still pressed against the back of my jaw.

Exactly where I had felt it all those years ago.

There was no way I was actually thinking what I was thinking.

I would sound fucking insane.

I pulled my hand away from my face and looked back at the phone.

For a second, the little wheel beside the network kept spinning.

Then it stopped.

Connected.

Behind me, something clicked.

A voice came from somewhere above the steel cage door.

It was flat and electronic.

“Access granted.”

I stared at it.

The steel gate started moving.

Sunlight spilled underneath it.

I ran.

The second I stepped outside, South Texas heat hit me directly in the
face.

That fucking oppressive, shimmering heat that makes the pavement look
like it’s moving.

I stood there for maybe three seconds.

Free.

Actually outside.

Then I stopped.

“Oh, fuck.”

I turned around and ran right back in.

The beagles all looked up at me.

I grabbed one of the collars hanging on the wall and a leash.

I went straight to the dog who had jumped on me first.

I clipped the leash on.

“Come on.”

His tail started going.
 
I quickly searched every drawer, lab coat and cupboard until I found a
set of keys.

The two of us hurried back toward the gate and then we were outside.

Just me and this random dog standing underneath the enormous Texas sky.

I hit the alarm button on the keys and in the distance I saw the lights
of a lifted all black Ford Raptor light up. 
 
We sprinted full speed at it, and in this moment regardless of the
nightmare I found myself in, staring down at my new friend running next
me I felt sheer joy.

We got to the truck, and climbed inside. I turned on the car and opened
the glove compartment, of course a gun and ammo, how Texan. 

I think in the thrill of it all I couldn’t help but turn on full blast
“Free Fallin” and full speed whipped around the parking lot back up to
the gate. I hopped out, ran inside and grabbed my new best friend a few
bags of dog food for the food and all the bottles of water for myself in
the fridge.

I sat a second so satisfied with myself, I connected my phone, typed in
my address and hit “Start”.

It said..
