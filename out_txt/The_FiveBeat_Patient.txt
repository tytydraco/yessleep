It was shortly after eleven at night when the brass bell above the
clinic door chimed. I was the only physician on the night shift in the
small town hospital. Along with the icy rain storming in through the
opened door, a woman stepped inside who immediately commanded the entire
room.

She was stunning, perhaps in her late twenties, with long dark hair and
intense green eyes. Her light summer dress clung damply to her skin in
places. Despite the storm outside, her posture was completely calm,
almost weightless.
“Good evening, Doctor,” she said. Her voice was deep, velvety, and
possessed a tone that sent an immediate, delicate chill down my spine.
“Forgive the late intrusion. But I’m feeling so dizzy. My circulation is
failing.”
“Please take a seat on the exam table,” I replied, striving to maintain
a professional tone. The intimate silence of the clinic suddenly felt
charged.

She sat down, brushed a dark strand of hair from her face, and looked
straight at me.
“I feel so unbelievably cold,” she whispered.

Before I could pull back, she took my hand and gently placed it against
her wrist. Her skin felt delicate, like polished marble, but it was
freezing cold. A heavy, floral scent of lily-like perfume drifted into
my nose. “Do you feel that?”

I slipped on my latex-free examination gloves and routinely felt for the
radial artery. My index and middle fingers rested precisely over the
vessel. Nothing. No wave of pulsation. No beat against my fingertips.
I shifted my fingers two millimeters medially, varied the pressure, held
still, and looked at the second hand of my watch. For twenty full
seconds, the reading remained absolute zero. No human body with that
blood pressure could be sitting upright and speaking. “I… I’ll grab the
digital blood pressure monitor and the ECG unit from the cabinet,” I
stammered, rattled, turning to move away.

She smiled. It was a beautiful, sensuous smile that paralyzed me
completely for a moment.
“You’re looking in the wrong place, Doctor,” she said softly. She
clasped my wrist and slowly guided my hand up from her arm toward her
neck. Her eyes held my gaze captive. The line between a medical
examination and a creeping seduction blurred entirely.

I pressed my fingers against the carotid triangle beneath the angle of
her jaw. There was a pulse. But it obeyed no known physiology. Beneath
the flawless skin, there was no single sinus rhythm. I felt a chaotic,
racing motion—four, perhaps five different heartbeats pounding
completely out of sync and in sheer panic against one another. It felt
as if a trapped swarm of small creatures was thrashing against her
carotid artery from the inside, trying to burst through the tissue.

My own breath hitched. In my chest, I suddenly felt a staccato, painful
tugging: my own heartbeat instantly began to lose its steady rhythm,
desperately trying to synchronize with the chaotic cadence beneath her
fingers.
I tried to pull my hand away, but her grip around my wrist locked like
an iron vise.
The sensuous smile lingered on her lips, but her green eyes abruptly
lost all human life. They turned rigid, dark, and deep like two
bottomless voids.

“Easy, Doctor,” she whispered in that same velvety voice, as the wild,
alien pounding beneath her skin turned my fingertips numb. She pulled my
hand just a fraction tighter against her throat. “Don’t fight it. It
only hurts until your heart gives up its own rhythm.”
