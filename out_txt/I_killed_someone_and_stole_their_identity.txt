It feels good to actually write it down, and I know you’re going to
judge me. But you need to know how desperate I was at the time.

I may have seemed happy to all my friends, but I was so lost at home.
Beautiful wife and two of the most wonderful children. But after a
while, I just couldn’t take it anymore. She was still every bit the
person I’d fallen in love with, but over time we just lost interest in
each other. I suppose it just became about the kids. The household did
not allow for much quiet time either, and eventually day-to-day life
just became stressful. Couple this with the fact she “couldn’t work” due
to the 2- and 5-year-olds in the house, meaning I had to spend my nights
driving Asda food across the UK.

I was lonely, stressed out and broke. My only claim to fame is my
apparent uncanny resemblance to some media darling politician guy. But
that’s not going to pay the bills. I’d happily trade his face for his
money. People loved him; no one cares about me anymore.

So imagine how it felt to wake up for my shift to my wife, teary-eyed.

“Oh. You’re crying already.”

“I’m pregnant.”

What!? Again!?

“No! We can’t do this. We’re fucked as we are!”

She looked me in the face and told me she couldn’t get rid of it. I
thought she must be mad. We screamed in each other’s faces until the
kids started crying. The last thing I said as I left for work was,

“I can’t stand this family.”

I didn’t mean it. I swear.

She shouted after me,

“You just wait until you get back!”

I got to work at 9pm and picked up the manifest for my pickup. Down
south. The first 2 hours were typical for me, just cruising along
motorways, window open, radio on. I was still ruminating on the argument
when I passed through an area of the countryside. Rich people town. I
must’ve been gawking at the massive houses or something, ’cause I felt a
bump under my wheels as I passed through a dark lane.

Fuck.

I’d never done that before…

I got out, shaking with fear. I could see whatever I’d hit was still.
Dead. I crept closer to it, hoping to God it was a deer. Then I saw him.

His head had been flattened by my wheels, and the road was smeared with
blood and brain matter. Total carnage. I had to stop myself from
throwing up.

My life is over, I thought.

I checked the dark lane both ways for lights. None. After holding back
more puke, I searched the poor fella’s jacket. A wallet with a LOT of
cash, for me at least. Two phones. One shitty burner phone. And the
other a brand-new iPhone. I tapped the power button to be greeted by a
picture of me.

“Me?”

I looked down at the man’s splattered face. I could recognise something
through the tissue and teeth.

“Is that?”

I checked the phone again. I couldn’t get in, but through the
notifications, messages and pop-ups, I could see it was him.

My doppelgänger. Mr fucking fantastic. My wife had mentioned how he’d
helped all these charities and shit. Didn’t pay attention. I knew he had
power, money and the lucky bastard was a bachelor.

Well, I suppose he’s not so fantastic now.

I was fucked. New baby on the way, and I’d just killed a beloved
politician. At worst, it’s jail. At best, I’d never drive a truck again.

But then, of course…

I mean, what are the chances of this happening!? It’s almost as if the
universe had given me this opportunity. I could finally leave
everything. Her, the kids, stupid job. I could finally be a somebody.
Someone who people look up to and talk about.

Tell me, what would you have done in my position?

I swapped our clothes and came up with a cover story. I remembered how
the snooty bugger talked. I could pass for him easily. I called the
police and, in my best posh accent, told them who I “was”, and informed
them of how I’d witnessed some poor truck driver fall out of his cab
after leaving his handbrake off. They arrived and didn’t ask many
questions. I was famous, after all.

“Your house is the big white one at the top of the road, isn’t it? How
come you were down here?”

I told him I just wanted some fresh air. But truthfully, I don’t know
why he was out on that road that night. The police took my statement,
and I was let on my way to the big white house. It was huge, with an
iron gate and a long driveway. Security let me in without a word. You’d
be surprised what being famous can get you.

I’d finally got the life I’d always wanted. All the best food, a lovely
house, attention, validation, money. I’d made it. This was the life I
could’ve had. I deserved this.

I walked up the driveway, gawking at the house when I heard…

“Get him!”

I felt the slam of a rugby tackle which cracked my entire back and
knocked all the air out of my lungs.

Then came the handcuffs. Then the Miranda rights. Back of a car. They
finally told me at the police station.

9 different women.

And the things he…

…I mean, I had been accused of.

Some of the victims stayed anonymous, others didn’t. There was evidence,
messages, CCTV. Turns out Mr Fantastic wasn’t so fantastic after all.
They’d been investigating him for a while and when they arrested me,
they seized things I hadn’t even had time to find yet. Hard drives, that
kind of thing.

I had no choice but to finally confess it all to them. It took them a
long time to do the necessary tests and verify that I indeed am not a
monster.

Of course, I was still in a whole other world of trouble now.
Manslaughter, obstruction of justice, plus numerous other things which
amounted to a good 7-year sentence. I remember seeing my wife again in
court the day I got sentenced.

The look on her face. It made the room colder.

They led me dangerously close to her as they took me away. I heard her
snap,

“You just wait until you get back.”

So here I am, writing to you from jail. It’s pretty rough here. Of
course, I caused a stir in the media, so they love to laugh at me here.
Mr Plastic they call me.

However, my biggest fear, the one that keeps me up at night. It isn’t
how I’ll survive my sentence, the other criminals or the lifetime of
public scrutiny.

It’s my wife.
