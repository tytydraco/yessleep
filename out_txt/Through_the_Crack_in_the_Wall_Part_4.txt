Friday was weird to say the least, at least up until its end. 

I sat at the bottom of the stairs for an hour accompanied by a lone
cricket before Randy and his brother showed up. It was my fault for
being early, not that it mattered. Anything to get away from home for a
while, the factory air kept the headaches away so I didn’t mind. Randy’s
brother was a bit younger than him, probably in his late teens still. It
shocked me how alike they looked, more so than the fact this was my
first time meeting him. Tall and white, with a scraggly ginger beard
trying its best to grow. They even dressed the same, with matching blue
hoodies and grey sweatpants. The only difference was that Manny(his
brother), wasn’t wearing a beanie and had a full head of ginger hair on
his head.

“So, you wanna make the exchange now or after we head in?” Randy asked,
pulling a ziploc bag out of his pocket.

“Now’s good, you have no idea the week I’ve had.” I responded, pulling
out a wad of cash. Nausea slowly began creeping in from withdrawal. We
made the exchange and promptly shoved the new items in our pockets. He
introduced me to his brother. We shook hands and traded greetings before
I turned to Randy and asked, “So, what did you bring?”

He pulled out a second bag from his pocket and dangled it in front of
him on display. 

“Acid? What’s wrong with weed or liquor?”, I asked, staring at the tabs
resting limp in the bag.

“Dude, trust me. I haven’t tried it yet but everytime we go in with
something different it changes.” Randy quickly snorted the air.
“Remember how it was different with the pot and the vodka? Well, I came
here earlier this week and tried it with some of that good snow, and
woo! Dude, I swear I felt superhuman. The art didn’t even matter to me,
the rush was so heavenly!”

Manny cut in. “So this place is actually real? You sure this dumbass
can’t just handle his own shit?” He cocked an eyebrow in anticipation of
a response.

I cracked a smile before replying, “Yeah, it’s real alright. I don’t
blame you for being the skeptic, especially when you have this idiot
explaining it to you.” Randy sent me a face that just said “really”
before reaching into the bag to reach for some tabs for each of us.

Buzz, buzz. 

Before he could hand them out, his phone whirred away in his pocket.
“Hold up…shit, I gotta take this. Can’t believe I forgot…”

He put his phone to his ear and started talking. “Hey…yeah, sorry I
forgot. C’mon, can’t we do this tomorr…fine. Yeah,yeah, I get it…You’re
gonna have to meet me here dude…you know that abandoned factory in the
woods off that dirt trail? What, no I’m not planning on doing
anything…then come armed dude, I don’t care. Yeah, alright, see you in
ten. I’ll be up on the second floor.” 

After he hung up I immediately interrogated him. “Don’t tell me you got
a deal set up. I’m not sure I feel comfortable with someone else coming
here.”

“Don’t worry dude, I have the pot in my car. I’ll just run out to grab
it and we’ll meet ‘em upstairs before we head in.”

“You said they were coming loaded though. I don’t have a gun, and
honestly I wouldn’t put it past you to have lost yours somehow.”

“Don’t worry, she’s harmless. She’s only bringing it out of concern for
her own safety.”

She? I don’t know why I was surprised about her being a woman. Randy
sold to all types of people, so it shouldn’t come as a shock. “Wait a
minute, do you call EVERYONE dude?” 

“Oh shut it. Here, both of you take one. I’ll be right back.” He said,
popping a tab on his tongue before handing the ziploc bag to me. I
popped one on my mouth and handed the bag to Manny. He put a tab on his
tongue and handed it back to me.

“Your brother is a moron.” I said, shoving the bag in my jacket pocket.
“You’re not wrong there, but he’s my brother so I’m forced to love him.”
I chuckled, and motioned him to follow me. I led him up the stairs, to
the right, then another at the corner, before stopping us where the
intersection would spawn. 

Randy met up with us quickly with a third ziploc bag and maybe 8 minutes
passed by before I heard footsteps trodding up the stairs. Once I saw
who was approaching us, I was justifiably shocked.

“Hey Jane, over here!” Randy shouted down the hall towards her.

 “Just so you know, I did come bearing arms.” Jane shouted back towards
us, brandishing a compact 9mm handgun. She seemed a little on edge,
which was rational since we were just three guys standing alone in a
dark abandoned factory. 

“Jane? What are you doing here?” I asked as she got close to us.  Shock
seemed to hit her too, but her shoulders relaxed a little upon noticing
me. “John? You hang out with Randy?” She replied in almost a jeer. “Is
this what you do, hang out with drug dealers in decrepit buildings?”

The question embarrassed me a little. Jane was a small and skinny woman.
Her dark brown hair never looked like it had seen a brush, which gave
her a slight unkempt look. Despite this, I always thought she was cute.
Which is why I always made it a habit to talk to her at work when I
could.

“Nah, I’m actually here for something else. I would try to explain it
but, you’d probably think I’m crazy.”

“Mhmm, is whatever it is the reason you’ve been ignoring me at work
then?” She retorted. I didn’t realize she liked talking to me so much,
and the thought filled my face up with a warmth that embarrassed me
further.

“Kind of, it’s just been a bad week for me.” I was quickly reminded of
the experience I had the night before, and a chill ran up my spine.

“Well you better stop it from now on, the days go by so slowly when
you’re giving me the silent treatment.” She went to punch me in the
chest before getting close to me. A sour expression went across her face
before she backed up. “Oh, uh…”

I was immediately aware of how unhygienic I came off as, a consequence
of not taking a shower.  “Sorry, the water in my apartment building got
shut off. Like I said, it’s been a bad week.” 

“Hey not to interrupt here dude, but can we get this deal over with
already?” Randy interrupted. Jane sighed and handed him 2 folded
twenties. He handed her the small bag of weed and went over to talk with
Manny. “So, what are you doing here? Are you a client of his?” She said
staying a comfortable distance away from me. 

“Yeah, but I’m not here to buy. Like I said, you wouldn’t believe me if
I explained it to you.” A partial lie. “Well then, what’s your poison?”
She asked. I shoved my hands in my pockets, and gripped the bag of black
tar tightly. “I don’t really wanna talk about it.” She shot me a
concerned look but ultimately decided not to pry. We stood in an awkward
silence before I tried to break it. 

“I didn’t know you smoke.” She smiled sheepishly before taking her time
to respond. “Helps take the edge off a little. I guess it’s been a bad
week for me too…” she stopped, caught up how she should form her next
words. “My mom has been dealing with cancer, she’s had it for a while
now. Unfortunately, chemo doesn’t seem to be helping her all that much
lately, and it’s already hard enough on us financially. The only reason
I work at that shitty burger joint with you is because I need the second
job to support her, no offense.” Those words seemed to sucker punch me.

“Wow, I’m sorry. You uh, never told me about that. Hopefully she pulls
through.” I scratched an itch crawling up my arm as I tried to ignore
the awkwardness of the conversation.

She sighed and her eyes cast downward, “Don’t be, I honestly try not
think about it. It wouldn’t be so bad if my jackass of a brother would
help out too, but he acts like he isn’t even part of the family. I guess
he hasn’t been the same since…” I winced at the mention of her brother.
She must’ve realized cause she immediately followed up. “Sorry, I forgot
about that…” 

Pins softly pricked me in the chest. She’s probably one of the only
people I’ve ever told about Ricky. I don’t even remember why. Maybe it
was because she was so easy to talk to or maybe it was because I had a
crush on her. It’s crazy the things you’re willing to spill to someone
you find attractive. Either way, it’s not like I told her much just that
he passed when I was a kid.

“What kind of cancer is it, if you don’t mind me asking?”

Her voice softened. I could tell she did mind the question, but decided
to answer anyway. “Breast. Stage two, bordering three. With a little
time I’m hoping the chemother-“

“Hey, yo what the fuck?!” Manny cut her sentence short. “What is that,
has that always been there?!” 

Randy laughed, way too proud of himself. “Chill little dude, this is
what I’ve been telling you about.” 

“No, no I’m not sure about this man.” Manny stated with a twinge of fear
in his voice. “Calm down dude, trust me. It’s truly heavenly.” Randy
shot me a look. “The gate has opened for us, you coming?” The corner
remained as it always was for me.

“Nah, the acid still hasn’t kicked in for me yet I guess.” Jane furrowed
her brow upon hearing me. Randy simply shrugged and put his hand on
Manny’s back to help lead him through the gate. The pair walked towards
the corner, and without warning, phased through the wall. 

“So that’s what it looks like from this perspective” I mumbled.

Jane, expectedly, wasn’t nearly as nonchalant about it. She looked
absolutely dumbfounded, and nearly tripped over her feet stumbling
backwards in shock. “What just happened to them?!” She screamed. 

“It’s okay just calm dow-“ 

“Don’t tell me to calm down. They just walked through the fucking wall!”
Her voice was colored in a mix of confusion, fear, and slight anger at
my response. I didn’t blame her, it was strange for Randy and I when we
found the gate and seeing someone pass through it on the outside
certainly would’ve disturbed anyone. Trying to decide what words I
should use, all I could think of was, “This is what we’re doing here.
Honestly, I don’t even know how I’d describe the place other than it’s
name: Paradise.” This didn’t help put her at ease. She was still in
complete shock and was stumbling over her words.

“What? P-Paradise? Wait, no I don’t know what’s going on here but I
don’t want any part of it.” She turned away and was about to storm off
before I stopped her. “Hold on, I’ll try to explain. Shit, so Randy and
I were setting up a deal here last Friday when we got high off some
doobie he brought and we found this extra hallway that led us to…I don’t
know, some place.”

Yeah, that didn’t really explain much, and honestly it would’ve been
better to just let her leave. I was about to give it another attempt
when I noticed the walls breathing. The acid was beginning to take
effect for me. Looking at Jane, I noticed her face starting to swirl
just a tad. “Listen, I’m going to go through. I’m not asking you to
follow, but if you decide to, take one of these.” I pulled out the bag
of acid tabs and tossed them to her. Her face was painted in what I can
only imagine was a mix of worry and fear. “Have you taken acid before?”

“Uh, yeah a few times in high school but I didn’t really like it. How
are you acting like this is all normal?” There was even more confusion
in her tone now. I didn’t really have a response to that, even now I
wouldn’t be able to think of one. Maybe it was just one of its weird
side effects, like once you’re in its web you simply accept it.

I turned over to see the newly formed intersection. Big bubble letters
in multicolored graffiti tempted me with the words: “Gate to Paradise”.
I paced over to the crack, and with no hesitation, slipped through. 

This time it was the craziest it’s ever been. The moonlight flashed
multicolored light in beat to a muffled bass driven techno song. My mind
was opened to exhilarating thoughts of happiness and I couldn’t help my
foot from tapping to the beat of the song. Manny stood in front of me
with his eyes bulging out his head and his mouth agape. 

“Pretty cool, right?” I quickly spotted the words coming out of my
mouth, forming a speech bubble in front of me. Randy was racing towards
me from down the hall, accompanied with a new and most fantastical
sight. Next to him was the green alien from our first night. He wasn’t
on the wall or floor though, instead he stood in front of me, flat like
a cardboard cut out. Now MY mouth was agape and my eyes were bulging. 

“He’s off the wall…” I simply said as Randy got and the painting got
close. Randy laughed, “Dude, when I’m telling you I couldn’t believe it,
well you get what I mean.” His words lingered in the air as a speech
bubble before disappearing. “Also, I checked the timer, we have 58
minutes. I went ahead and set one for 55.” I honestly wasn’t even paying
attention to him. I still couldn’t believe that a painting of a green
alien had a somewhat defined form now and was standing right in front of
me.

“Well freak, are you going to keep staring or are you going to introduce
yourself?” The alien said. His words came out as a speech bubble in
front of him, and even though he didn’t make a sound I swear I could
actually hear him. It was more like I imagined his nasally voice so
vividly in my head that it was practically manifesting itself to
reality. “That’s John, my idiotic friend. Don’t mind him, he’s what we
call rude.” Randy said.

I was so caught up in the shock that I completely disregarded his jab at
me. Even for Paradise, this was a bit much. “Wait, I’m a freak?” Is all
I managed.

“Have you seen yourselves? You’re all weird looking, actually you’re
hard to look at. Actually, you’re really hard to look at.” The alien
said in the form of a fat speech bubble. Damn. I couldn’t believe a
talking painting was gonna make me self conscious. “What is it we’re
supposed to do here?” Manny said. His head had already started
involuntarily bobbing to the rhythm of the song playing.

“The only thing we can do. Party! The party is downstairs though, on
account of the beast’s cage being located up here.” The bubble in front
of the alien read. 

“There’s a party downstairs?” I asked, rationality betraying me as I
disregarded the second half of his sentence. 

“Yeah! Let’s go. Glip, yours truly, is going to bust it down on the
dance floor!” I imagined his voice hitting a tone of excitement and I
watched as his form awkwardly proceeded down the stairs. Exchanging
looks with Randy and his brother, we followed his descent, but not
before I took a quick glance outside. It was nothing but a void of neon
lights bouncing off what looked like a mass of silhouettes bouncing to
an unknown rhythm. In my stare, every thought melted away into pure
bliss.

I forgot about the ghost in my bathroom, I forgot about the strangeness
of this version of Paradise, and I forgot about the outside world in its
entirety. The only thought circling in my head was to have fun and
party.

The source of the music was obviously downstairs, as it grew much louder
the closer we got to the open area. At the bottom, dozens of flat,
cardboard cut-out of different characters gathered in a disco lit up by
unknown light sources. Some of them twisted their flat bodies to look at
us, mostly with disgust, confusion, or fear. After a few seconds though,
they would shrug or look away before continuing to dance. The music was
simply intoxicating, and at that point we were almost helpless to stop
ourselves from getting into it. 

Manny took to the floor first, busting out moves I didn’t think any
relative of Randy was capable of. Randy whipped out all sorts of lame
moves like the running man and shopping cart. I wasn’t even paying
attention to what I was doing, but I’m sure my moves were just as lame. 

The atmosphere was electrifying, the lights painted the room in a
brilliance that couldn’t be described, and each dance I performed seemed
to expunge no energy. The characters that were a part of the party took
many shapes and sizes, reflecting tons of distinct art styles. It was a
rave that could only find itself in a dream, but we were taking part in
it in a reality that simply shouldn’t exist. We danced and danced,
waving our hands amongst the odd shapes of the crowd. Amongst the crowd,
I noticed the odd ways these 2-dimensional beings moved to simulate
dance. Some of them were uncanny, some made sense, and a small few
twitched violently with pained expressions on their faces.

It never dawned on me just how bizarre of an experience it was while in
the moment, I simply didn’t have full autonomy of my brain.

Eventually, Manny started a dance competition against Glip in the center
of the room. A crowd formed around them as they began. A photorealistic
dog and a shark in a baseball cap cheered Glip on as he awkwardly
shifted and bent  to do what moves his limited form would allow. The
crowd would spontaneously break into a chant of his name, voices of a
large crowd materializing with the huge white bubble that would pulse
above them. Whenever Manny would start, his brother would be the only to
cheer his name, even if he was objectively the better dancer. Tired of
the spectacle, I went over to rest at the top of the stairs. 

After a few more attempts, Glip was almost unanimously declared the
victor and Manny went to shake his hand. Glip reached out but the second
they made contact, he whipped his hand back like he’d been shocked by
electricity. Randy made a poor attempt to moonwalk over to me, plopping
next to me on the stairs once he got close.

“This is wild, dude.” He said, staring off to the crowd. “Probably the
best one yet.” 

“Yeah, it certainly is something.” I mustered back. 

“Hey, thanks for letting me bring him along.” His voice had suddenly
become serious, “from what I hear, it’s been getting rough at home and I
think he really needed this.”

“He needed a dance battle against whacky cartoons?” My poor attempt at a
joke. 
 
He cracked a partial smile, but his face quickly became stern before he
continued. “Listen, I just think he needed to get out for a while. Have
some fun, y’know? Our piece of shit dad didn’t even care that he left
the house without a coat.” He let out a long exhale looking off as if he
was afraid to make eye contact with me. I had never seen this side of
him. Even in high school, I had always known him to be the class clown,
the kind of guy who’d make jokes to lighten the mood when necessary.

“I just wish I gave him that sweatshirt because it was cold outside and
not because I couldn’t stand looking at the bruises on his arm. I’d try
to take custody of him but it’s just not possible. Hell, you know better
than anyone that my van is not suited for two people living in it.
That’s not even mentioning the drug possession charge on my file.” 

His words nearly sobered me up. When I was 19, I had my first overdose.
My mom came bursting into my room when she heard me choking on my own
vomit in my sleep. She rushed me to the hospital when she found I was
unresponsive and my lips and nails had almost turned blue. When my dad
found out what happened, he kicked me out of the house. I still have
pains in my neck from having to sleep in the front seat of Randy’s van
for a year. 

For the first time, the illusion of heaven we were in was beginning to
break and thoughts of Ricky began to creep up on me. 

“I don’t know how he keeps weasling his way out of the tips to CPS I’ve
been sending.’..”

I desperately tried to change the subject, maintaining the facade of
bliss that was trying to course through me. A shitty thing to do, but
far from the worst thing I’d do that night.

“So, what was cocaine like here?” Randy’s face remained stern as I
asked. The question seemed to rest on him for an uncomfortable amount of
time before his face lit up and a dopey smile creeped along his face.
“It was absolute euphoria dude. When I say superhuman, I mean I felt
like I was in some time dilation or something. Even the timer itself
seemed to tick down slowly. “ 

“So, kinda like you had super powers?”

“Yeah, I guess. If that’s how you wanna describe it.”

This statement made me wonder what version of Paradise my poison could
bring. “Hey, how much longer do we got by the way?” I quickly followed
up. He slid his phone out of his pocket and took a quick glance at it.
“Shit, only 10 minutes. Were we really dancing that long?” I shrugged
and looked down to see Manny still have the time of his life. To my
surprise, most of the characters had already dispersed and disappeared
without me realizing. Glip was hopping on over to us with two new
characters at his side. A nun that looked like she had been ripped out
of a Popeye cartoon, and a hooded figure that had the 8-bit appearance
of a retro video game character. 

“Hello, freaks. Just wanted to say that your friend there is pretty
good, just not Glip good.” I rolled my eyes upon reading this. “Also my
buddies here are trying to settle a debate so I thought we’d just come
and ask you straight up.” 

The nun spoke first, I imagined her voice as a young woman with, for
some reason, a Boston accent. “Are you angels? You three must be because
you look otherworldly.” 

I traded confused looks with Randy before answering, “No we’re not
angels.” 

“See, I told you. They’re demons here to cause little mischief.” The
hooded figure responded. His voice came to me as a Transylvanian accent
speaking through a walker talkie. 

“No, we’re not demons either dude.” Randy said, almost laughing. 

“There, you both got your answers. They’re just freaks, so let’s bounce
already. You three should probably get going now the party is ending.
You don’t wanna be around when the beast’s cage unlocks. Or stay, might
do your looks some justice.” With that, the three characters hopped past
us and disappeared. 

I was reminded of the Gatekeeper, and turned to Randy to ask, “Did you
check the Gatekeeper? He isn’t off the canvas like them is he?”

“No, he isn’t. I didn’t go into that creepy ass hallway like you did,
but when I looked down it seemed like he was etched on the wall like
always.” This bit of news comforted me a little. Maybe the drawing was
more of a bluff than anything, not that I’d ever stick around to find
out. By then, all of the characters had disappeared and Manny laid
sprawled out on the floor with a big smile on his face. The music that
was playing had already faded into the low rhythmic hum that was present
the first night.

Suddenly, I was alerted to the faint sound of footsteps coming from the
gate down the hall. To my surprise, it was Jane. I shot up when I saw
her, rushing toward her to usher her in.

“Hey, you actually decided to come.” I said with a little too much
excitement.

She grasped her forehead with her hands and a pleasured smile began to
twist on her face. “Whoa, okay. Yeah, I see why you guys would come
here.” She stopped dead in her tracks, overwhelmed by the sensations
this place brought, before collecting herself and letting out a relieved
sigh. “Phew, that is…something. I-I can’t even describe this feeling.” 

“Pretty amazing right, and you just missed the best part!” I sounded
pretty proud of myself.

“I’m not going to pretend to know what that means, but I’ll definitely
say I like whatever this is.” Her face was an expression of dopey joy
that I can’t say I’ve ever seen her share. Her time was cut short though
because-

Buzz, buzzz

Randy’s phone had begun whirring in his pocket. He pulled out his phone
and faced it towards me to show that the timer was going off. 

I was immediately faced with disappointment. “Well I hate to break it to
you, but we gotta leave.”

Jane frowned in a way that reminded me of a child who was told they
couldn’t get candy at the grocery store by their mom. “What? But I just
got here.” She seemed even more disappointed than me.

“We’re on a timer here, and if we don’t leave the gate might close so we
need to head out.” To this day, I’ll never understand why I lied to her.
It would’ve been so much better if I hadn’t. Even if I didn’t fully
understand them myself, I should’ve at least tried to warn her of the
repercussions of staying too long.

I went to usher her out when she backed off and said, “Hold it there
smelly, I can leave on my own.” With that, she made her exit and I
walked down the stairs to help Randy with his brother. He was struggling
to lift him up as Manny fought back against him with a pathetic
struggle. 

“Dude, help me with his legs. He won’t listen to me.” Concern was strewn
all across his face and  he kept glancing over to the stairs with an
increasing worry that was spiraling into a frenzy. I wasted no time in
getting over to Manny, who was mumbling about something about staying
forever while being dragged slowly towards the stairs. 

I hoisted him up by the ankles and we struggled to carry him up the
steps. I was carefully trying to gauge our time, the stairway felt more
and more like a mountain with each slow labored movement we made to
climb towards its peak. Slowly, and carefully we lugged him up. He
finally quit his struggle after a while, and he passed into a sleep. We
were beginning to make steady progress.  

But we were only halfway up when the hum faded away, signaling the 1
minute mark of the timer. A wave of drowsiness hit me all at once,
making my limbs go numb and giving me an uncontrollable vertigo. I
couldn’t help it when I lost my grip, and his feet plummeted down
against the concrete, forcing him out of Randy’s grip as well. He slid
down a quarter of the way of the stairs, his head knocking with a hollow
thud on each step. 

“What the fuck dude?!” Randy shouted. He shoved me in a rage, nearly
pushing me down the stairs but I miraculously found my balance just in
time. 

“I’m sorry, I’m sorry, the comedown just hit me all at once.”

“Hurry up and help me get him! We don’t have any time left.” He
practically was spraying spit out his mouth when he screamed at me.
Blood was trickling down the back of Manny’s head onto the concrete
beneath him. Time was desperately running out and we needed to get him
out right then if we were going to make it.

It didn’t matter though, before I could reposition myself the world was
already drained of its color.

I’m so sorry, Randy. I’m so sorry, Manny. I’m a piece of shit, I know,
and no amount of good I could ever achieve in my lifetime will ever make
up for it. You don’t have to forgive me. I know I’ll burn in the deepest
pits of hell for what I did. 

The fear overtook me and I ran. 

Even when you screamed at me through sobs to help you. Even when I heard
the crackling of fire and the swarm of locust, I never looked back. I
just slipped through the crack alone.

My only solace in sharing that trip with you is that no ghost lay
waiting for me in the bathroom that night. I think I sat in the shower
for hours thinking about what I’ve done. It had to be, because I only
got out when the water turned cold. My shame is only amplified by the
fact I visited paradise the following day, as if nothing happened. And
the only crime I’ve committed that wracks me with more guilt than the
injustice I’ve committed against you two, is the one I foolishly let
happen the following week.
