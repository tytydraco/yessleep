I never thought I’d be typing something like this out, but I don’t have
any other choice. I’m writing this on my phone with the screen
brightness turned all the way down. I’m crammed inside my bedroom
wardrobe, trying not to breathe too loudly. If my battery dies before I
hit post, at least this draft might stay on my device for whoever finds
what’s left of my farm.

It all started about two weeks ago. I live out in the countryside, miles
away from the nearest town. It’s quiet out here, just me, my house, and
my livestock. But then my animals started disappearing. First it was a
couple of sheep, then a pig. Naturally, I thought it was a rogue wolf or
maybe a coyote pack tearing through my fences. I went out and set heavy
steel foot traps all around the perimeter of the pens.

The next morning, I went out to check them. None of the traps had been
sprung. Not a single one. But two more sheep were gone.

That was the moment I realized I wasn’t dealing with a normal animal.
That night, I decided to camp out near the pens with my 12 gauge
shotgun.

12:00 AM passed. Nothing. 1:00 AM. Total silence. 2:00 AM. Still
nothing.

Around 2:30 AM, exhaustion got the better of me, and I drifted off
against the wooden wall of the shed.

At 3:00 AM sharp, a horrific, high pitched screech snapped me awake. It
was the sound of my sheep screaming in pure terror. I froze, my heart
hammer locking in my chest. Slowly, agonizingly, I cracked open the shed
door just a few inches to peek outside.

What I saw shattered my mind.

Standing in the moonlight was a creature easily three meters tall. It
had a massive, grotesque hump on its back that made its silhouette look
heavy and twisted. But its limbs were wrong, horribly wrong. It had
three impossibly long arms. Two were attached to its left side, and one
dangled from its right. Each hand ended in four razor sharp claws. Below
its towering frame, its feet were turned completely backward.

It was holding two of my sheep in its left hands like ragdolls. With its
single right hand, it sliced one of the sheep open with a flick of its
claws, tore out its guts, and began devouring them raw.

I couldn’t breathe. I couldn’t move. I slowly backed away into the
shadows, left the shed without making a sound, and sprinted back into my
house.

I locked every door, drew the blinds, and sat in the dark clutching my
gun until the sun came up at 8:00 AM. When the daylight finally filled
the yard, I looked out the window. The grounds were empty. The morning
light made the whole thing feel like a fever dream. I tried to
rationalize it. “I was tired. I imagined it. There’s no way something
like that is real.”

I went to my computer to search online for any local sightings or weird
animal attacks. No signal.

“That’s impossible” I muttered to myself. “I paid the bill on time.”

I walked outside to check the telephone pole down the driveway. When I
got close, my stomach dropped. Every single cable had been cleanly
severed, dangling uselessly in the air.

Panic setting in, I ran over to my 4x4 truck. I jumped in and turned the
key. The engine didn’t even click. I popped the hood, and my blood ran
cold. The wiring harness was ripped to shreds. I walked around to the
side of the truck and saw the tires. The front left and back right were
completely shredded. These weren’t knife cuts. Deep, gouging claw marks
ran right through the thick rubber.

It wasn’t just a predator. It was intelligent. It was isolating me.

Walking to the city was out of the question. It was miles through open
terrain, and staying outside for that long meant being exposed. I was
trapped.

From that day on, my life inverted. I slept during the day when the sun
was high and stayed awake all night, sitting in the center of my living
room with my shotgun rested across my knees. Every night, more animals
disappeared.

A few days ago, I did a headcount before trying to sleep. Out of my
entire farm, I had less than 13 animals left. Just nine sheep and four
pigs. I felt completely helpless.

Then came yesterday afternoon.

I was looking out the kitchen window into the treeline while the sun was
still up. Standing behind a thick oak tree was the creature. It looked
different, it was visibly taller and thicker than the night I first saw
it. Like it was growing larger with every animal it ate.

It tilted its head out from behind the trunk. That was the first time I
saw its face. It had tiny, milky white eyes that stared straight into
mine. And then, its face split open. It gave me a wide, sickening,
demonic smile, unhinging a jaw that had to be nearly a meter wide. Big
enough to swallow a pig whole.

I rubbed my eyes in sheer terror, and when I opened them, the space
behind the tree was empty.

Last night passed in eerie, suffocating silence. But tonight… tonight it
came for the house.

About twenty minutes ago, an ear piercing shriek shattered the night air
right outside my porch. It wasn’t a roar. It was a wet, sharp, desperate
pig squeal, screeching out of that massive jaw like a distorted
recording.

I panicked. I ran upstairs, slammed my bedroom door, and squeezed myself
into the darkest corner of my wooden wardrobe.

Now I’m sitting here in the pitch black. The screaming outside stopped a
minute ago, but I can hear something heavy dragging itself across the
hardwood floors downstairs. The footstep pattern is wrong. Two steps
forward, one strange scrape from those backward feet.

It’s coming up the stairs now. The pig squeal just echoed from the
hallway right outside my room.

If anyone finds this, stay away from the farm. The Rustler is real, and
it’s right outside my door.
