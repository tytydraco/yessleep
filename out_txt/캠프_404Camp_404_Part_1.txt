I know how this sounds, I really do.

If I was reading this story from someone else, I’d probably assume they
were exaggerating. A mysterious campground hidden in the mountains, a
strange job listing, rules that sound like they came straight out of a
horror movie, and a place that seems to know things it shouldn’t.

I would have thought the same thing, actually, I probably would have
made fun of whoever posted it.

The problem is I’m the person this happened to, and I’m writing this
because I found out something about Camp 404.

It doesn’t show up on maps, and it doesn’t even have a website.

It doesn’t have any records that I can find, but somehow, it was waiting
for me.

I found the job because I was broke, that’s the embarrassing part.

I wasn’t looking for some crazy adventure. I wasn’t trying to find a
haunted location or explore some abandoned place in the middle of
nowhere.

I needed money, I was desperate.

The job listing was simple.

“Remote campground assistant needed. Housing and meals provided.”

That was all it said.

No company name, no website/photos, not even a single review. Looking
back, I don’t know how I ignored how suspicious that was.

But when you’re desperate enough, your brain starts negotiating with
itself.

I remember thinking: “Okay, that’s weird.”

Then immediately: “But hey free housing, free meals provided.”

That was apparently enough for me, and so I applied. The response came
way faster than I expected.

I thought I’d hear back in a few days, instead, I got a message about
twenty minutes later.

It said; “Accepted. Arrive before sunset.”

That was it, I was a little weirded out but hey, desperation like I
said. Still, I was surprised, no interview request, not even a single
question about previous related experience, not even the dreaded “Tell
us about yourself” question.

Just those four words.

I stared at the message for a while, I actually laughed a little because
it sounded ridiculous.

It sounded like something that would happen in a horror movie right
before the main character makes a terrible decision.

I even joked to myself that the next message would probably tell me not
to go into the basement or not to split up with the group.

The directions they sent me were strange.

They gave me a route through the mountains and specifically told me not
to use GPS after a certain point.

The reason they gave was “signal interference.”

At the time, I thought that was weird.

Not terrifying, just weird.

I figured it was probably some remote area with bad reception. I’ve
driven through places like that before.

The first couple hours were completely normal.

The roads were fine, the weather was good.

Nothing seemed wrong.

Honestly, I started feeling stupid for being suspicious.

Then the houses disappeared.

The roads got smaller.

The trees got thicker.

And eventually, I realized I hadn’t seen another person for a long time.

No cars.

No gas stations.

No buildings.

Just forest.

My GPS stopped working about thirty minutes later.

At first, I wasn’t worried.

Bad signal happens.

I waited.

The map didn’t load, I restarted my phone, nothing.

The weird part wasn’t that the GPS stopped working, the weird part was
that the map itself was gone.

Not frozen or infinitely loading, just gone.

The screen just showed:

LOCATION NOT FOUND

I remember staring at it and just thinking “seriously?”

Not because I was scared, because I was annoyed.

Which, looking back, was probably the dumbest reaction possible.

That’s when I saw the sign, it looked ancient.

The wood was cracked, and moss had grown around the edges, but the
writing was surprisingly clear.

캠프: 404

There was an arrow pointing into the trees.

I slowed down and looked around, there was nothing else.

No buildings, no other signs, and definitely no indication that there
should be a campground anywhere nearby.

I checked the directions again.

Nothing.

I checked my phone.

Still nothing.

I knew I should have turned around, there was something about that sign
that really bothered me.

Not because it looked scary.

It didn’t, that was actually the problem, it looked normal, too normal.
So I followed the arrow.

Camp 404 looked completely ordinary, even slightly boring if I’m being
honest.

That was the first thing that made me uncomfortable, if it had looked
abandoned, I would have left.

If it had looked creepy, I would have trusted my instincts, but it
didn’t.

The cabins were clean, the grass was cut and the paths were well
maintained. Someone was taking very good care of this place.

I walked into the office and rang the bell on the counter, the sound
echoed through the building.

I waited, afew seconds passed, then a minute.

I was about to ring it again when I heard someone moving behind me. A
man stepped inside from out of nowhere, I hadn’t seen anyone else when I
came in.

He looked completely normal, and introduced himself as Kang Tae-hyun.

He looked at me for a few seconds before saying anything, then said with
a sigh; “You’re late.”

I looked at him, honestly a little taken aback “I’m pretty sure I’m
not.”

He didn’t respond, he just stared at me like my answer meant something.
Something I didn’t understand.

Then he reached under the counter and handed me a key.

The tag said: CABIN 7

“That’s it?” I asked. He didn’t respond.

“Are you going to explain the job?” Nothing.

I started wondering if he was ignoring me or if he just wasn’t someone
who talked much.

Finally, he handed me a folded piece of paper.

“Read this before tonight.”

I assumed it was a list of campground guidelines and rules, and at
first, it was.

Lock your door.

Keep food stored properly.

Don’t enter restricted areas.

Normal stuff, nothing special, then I got to the strange ones.

“If you hear someone calling your name from the forest, do not answer.”

I stopped reading, and actually looked toward the window.

There was nothing outside.

I kept going.

“If someone knocks three times, do not open the door.”

That one bothered me, but the last rule was the one I couldn’t stop
thinking about.

“Never open Cabin 8.”

That was it.

No explanation, no reason, just a warning.

Never open Cabin 8.

Honestly, I should have just left, I was getting a little uncomfortable,
I know that’s what everyone reading this is probably thinking.

And honestly? You’re right.

That was the last normal chance I had, but I didn’t leave, I really
needed the job.

I told myself there was a logical explanation, I told myself the rules
were probably some weird prank.

I told myself I needed the money.

So I went to Cabin 7.

The first few hours were completely normal, I unpacked and then ate the
food they left for me. I really tried not to think about the paper
sitting on the table, but the strange Cabin 8 “rule” was too strange,
too specific.

Eventually, I convinced myself I had overreacted, I tend to overthink
everything so I thought it was my weird brain just going into overdrive.
Especially after meeting the campground caretaker, he was a little too
quiet, and honestly a little strange.

Then, at exactly 2:13 AM, someone knocked on my door. Three times.

I froze, I sat there staring at the door, thinking of the previous
“rule”.

Then I heard a voice; “Hey.”

My stomach dropped.

Because I knew that voice… it was mine.

Not someone who sounded similar, not someone imitating me.

My voice.

The person outside my cabin spoke again, very quietly this time. Almost
like they were trying to warn me.

“Don’t let this place remember you.” And then absolute silence. I didn’t
sleep after that.

And when the sun came up, I looked outside my window, cabin 8 was no
longer at the end of the campground, it was directly across from my
cabin.

And the door was already open.
