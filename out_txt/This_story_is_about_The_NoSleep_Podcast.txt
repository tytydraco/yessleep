The no

I never really knew my grandfather. He wasn’t a bad man—just distant. My
mother spoke about him from time to time, but there was always something
unsaid beneath her words. Still, I decided to visit him one day,
bringing a cake as a small gesture.

He looked surprised to see me but welcomed me in. We sat, talked for a
while, and then, after a moment of silence, he leaned forward, studying
me closely.
You look tired, he said. Is everything alright? I hesitated, then told
him the truth. had a nightmare… about a podcast.”

At that, his face drained of color. His hands trembled slightly as he
set his cup down. Do you remember the name?he asked, voice barely above
a whisper. I swallowed. It was called ‘The no.The moment the words left
my mouth, he stood up so fast that his chair nearly tipped over. His
eyes were wide, fearful.

You need to leave. Now. What? Why.This wasn’t supposed to happen!His
voice cracked. Go home. Forget about this. Please.Confused and shaken, I
left. When I told my mother what had happened, she sighed. Your
grandfather is getting old. Don’t let it bother you.

That night, for the first time in weeks, I didn’t have a nightmare. But
the next morning, we got the call—my grandfather was dead. It didn’t
make sense. He had been fine just yesterday. After the funeral, we went
to his house to sort through his belongings. I wandered, not really
knowing what I was looking for, until a single book caught my attention.
It was old, its leather cover worn and cracked. Curious, I opened it—and
my blood ran cold.

Page after page detailed something impossible, something horrifying. My
grandfather had worked for The NoSleep Podcast. But according to this
book, they didn’t just write horror stories. They received them. They
collected them. And the way they did it They had made contact with
something.

I know how this sounds. I know you think I’ve lost my mind. But listen
to me—how else do you think they come up with these stories? Every week,
new horrors, endless nightmares. They don’t just write them. They hear
them. They record them. And my grandfather—he had been part of it. I
wish that was where it ended, but it’s not. Because after I read that
book, I had another nightmare. And in it, I saw him.

The Devil. He smiled at me, his voice like fire and whispers.Do you
think your grandfather can protect you? Since then, I haven’t dared to
close my eyes. Every time I blink, I see him—closer than before. So I’m
begging you. Listen to me. The NoSleep Podcast is not what it seems.
