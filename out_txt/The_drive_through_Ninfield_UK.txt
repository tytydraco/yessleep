Let me give you some background for this.

My friends and I had only recently got our driving licences.
Like most people who had just passed, we started looking for excuses to
go driving. Sometimes we’d go together, sometimes we’d go alone. There
wasn’t really a destination. We’d just get in the car, put some music
on, and drive around for a few hours.
We live in East Sussex, UK, so we’re surrounded by country lanes, little
villages and roads that get pitch black at night. During the day,
they’re beautiful. At night, they’re something else.
There were three of us, and normally we spoke every day. But that
particular week, we hadn’t really spoken much at all. We were all just
going out driving on different days.

I went out on the Tuesday night.

It was 10:59 PM. I remember that clearly because I was fiddling with the
radio, trying to get my own music playing, when the station suddenly cut
to the 11 PM news.

I set off, driving through Uckfield, Ringmer, Hailsham and Polegate.
Eventually, I decided to head towards Bexhill, off the A27, following
the road past Stone Cross. I hadn’t driven that way much before.

I ended up taking a side road off the A259 and wound up in a tiny
village called Hooe.

There was almost nothing there.

It was dark. Not normal dark, either. The kind of dark where your
headlights illuminate the road, but everything beyond them just
disappears.

I kept driving.

Eventually, I reached Ninfield. It was slightly bigger than Hooe, but
still incredibly quiet. I passed the King’s Arms pub and carried on. A
little further along, I passed another pub, the Blacksmith Inn. It
wasn’t lit, though, so I couldn’t really make out the shape of it as I
drove past.

Then I came across the road closure.

The road ahead was blocked because of roadworks. I figured I’d turn
around and head back, but I really needed to piss.
So I pulled over near the closure, got out of the car and started
walking.

That’s when I saw the forklift.

It was sitting beside the roadworks, and there was someone inside it.
Just sitting there, on their phone. I remember thinking how strange it
was. It was roughly 11:40 PM.

Who sits alone in a forklift at nearly midnight?

But I didn’t think much of it. I walked away from the road towards some
bushes because I wasn’t particularly interested in having some random
person watch me piss.

I finished and started walking back towards the car.

Then I stopped.

The forklift was dark.

The person was gone.

I stared at it for a few seconds. There was nowhere obvious for them to
have gone. I hadn’t heard the engine. I hadn’t seen headlights. I hadn’t
heard footsteps.

But whatever.

I got back into the car and started the engine.
The area I’d parked in was a bit tight, so I had to reverse out and do a
three-point turn. As I pulled away, I looked in my mirror.

The forklift was occupied again. There was someone sitting inside it.I
looked over my shoulder.

Nothing.

The seat was empty. I checked the mirror again.

Nothing.

I actually stopped for a second. I remember thinking, *What the fuck?*

I convinced myself I was tired. I’d been driving for hours. Maybe I’d
imagined the whole thing. So I carried on towards Hooe. And that’s when
I saw the light.

A torch.

It was sitting somewhere ahead of me, shining towards the road.
I slowed down. At first, I thought it was a pedestrian. The light was
far enough away that I couldn’t make out anything else.

I kept approaching.

But the strange thing was, the light didn’t get any bigger.

I drove closer.

Still the same size.

Closer.

Still the same.

Then, suddenly, when I was almost beside it, the light became incredibly
bright.

I flinched.

And realised it wasn’t being held by anyone.

It was a torch, just sitting on a fence post.

In the middle of nowhere.

At nearly midnight.

I don’t know why, but that scared me as much as anything else that
night.

At that point, I wanted out.

I put my foot down.

The car didn’t move.

I looked down.

The car had stalled.

In neutral.

I tried starting it.

Nothing.

Again.

Nothing.

I started to panic.

Third attempt.

The engine finally turned over.

I pulled away immediately, driving past the torch.

For a second, I thought that was it.

Then I glanced in my rear-view mirror.

The figure was there.

It was standing exactly where the torch had been.

The light was behind it, but somehow its body was still almost
completely black. I could make out its shape now — short, hunched and
horribly still.

I kept driving.

Then it moved.

Not towards the road.

Towards me.

I watched it in the mirror as it started coming closer.
One step.

Then another.

I couldn’t tell how it was moving. It almost looked like it was gliding,
because every time I looked away and back at the mirror, it seemed to be
significantly closer.

I floored it.

I don’t think I’ve ever driven that fast down a country lane.

And I didn’t look in the mirror again.

Not until I was well out of Ninfield.

I told my friends what happened.

They laughed.

They didn’t believe me.

Until they decided to go themselves.

One went on Wednesday with another mutual friend who didn’t even have a
licence. The other went on Friday. Neither knew exactly what I’d seen
until afterwards. And both came back with stories of their own.

The friend who went on Wednesday said his radio started playing up as
they drove through the area. Static. Cutting out. Coming back. Then
disappearing again. He also said they’d seen strange, dark figures
around the roads. After hearing that, they decided they’d had enough and
left. But the friend who went on Friday stayed longer.

And that’s where things get strange.

They drove through the area and started noticing houses.

Except the houses had decorations outside.

Halloween decorations.

Not subtle ones, either.

Figures. Faces. Things hanging from windows.

The sort of decorations you’d expect to see in October.

Except it was summer.

Halloween wasn’t coming.

It wasn’t even close.

And apparently, some of the decorations looked old.

Like they’d been left there for years.

They drove past them and kept going until they decided to leave.

Afterwards, we compared everything.

Three people.

Three separate nights.

None of us had been together.

We’d all seen strange things in roughly the same area.

The figures.

The radio problems.

The decorations.

And my experience with the forklift.

For a while, I tried to convince myself there was a normal explanation.

There probably is.

Maybe someone really was working at the road closure. Maybe I’d simply
failed to notice them getting out of the forklift. Maybe the figure was
a person. Maybe the torch had been deliberately left on that fence post.
Maybe the decorations were for some completely unrelated reason.

But there’s one thing I still can’t explain.

When I saw that figure standing in the torchlight, I could see the light
hitting the road behind it. I could see the fence. I could see the
ground. I could see everything the torch illuminated.

Everything except the figure itself.

It was like the light went around it.

And when it started coming towards me, it didn’t look like it was
walking.

It looked like it was just getting closer.

I’ve driven down plenty of dark country roads since. I’ve seen plenty of
strange people. I’ve seen plenty of things I couldn’t identify. But I’ve
never gone back through that part of Ninfield at night.

Because sometimes I wonder what would have happened if I’d stopped.

And sometimes I wonder something worse.

What if the thing I saw wasn’t following me?

What if it had been waiting for me to drive past?
