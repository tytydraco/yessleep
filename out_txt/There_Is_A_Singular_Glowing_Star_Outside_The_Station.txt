The dust from laying on the cold steel floor clung to me like an extra
layer of skin. As I pried myself up from the ground each bone in my body
crackled and shifted back into place. It was as if I had been asleep in
this unrecognizable place for centuries,  I woke up with no idea how I
even ended up here. It was more than that, I woke up unaware of who I
am, or what anything is. Once I stood up on my bare feet I was able to
recall a few things, of what once used to be home, how life used to
function, and that I am human. Even with all of this knowledge, I still
did not understand myself, not even a single detail of my life before
this.

My first memory, not of being ripped from the womb by a stranger’s
hands, instead was rising from my barren bed laid upon the floor, and
approaching the porthole of the station’s hall. All that stood before me
was a pitch black nothingness, and set in the middle of it all, was a
singular glowing star no larger than a freckle upon someone’s face. 

I set the gray tip of my pointer finger against the glass right over the
star, and ran it down to the metal that entrapped the porthole. My eyes
followed the greasy dust trail I left behind with my touch. Similar to
the line I had created, tears fell down my cheeks and splattered beside
my bare feet. I was afraid to die in a place I could not even begin to
process, yet the struggle of not understanding if I had even lived
before this moment laid ever present. 

With nothing else to fill the brain, I recall every second that went by
as I stood and sobbed my confusion away. I was there for exactly five
minutes and forty two seconds, counting down each moment as it fled
right by me. 

With only two ways to push forward, I decided to shift left and make my
way into the dim hall. It was faintly illuminated by the weak light
gifted to us by the star peering through the portholes. Each step I took
felt like a failed attempt, I had to drag my feet across the bumpy
ground to just barely lift them into the air. 

After endless concrete walls on my left, a metal door came into view set
in one of the blind spots of the portholes, leading the area to be set
in darkness. With a light tap of my palm, the door slowly opened with
the grating of steel echoing throughout the halls. Despite everything
being so grim, I felt a small appreciation for how easy it was to enter,
as I didn’t have the strength to push it with any force. 

Wires dangled from the ceiling and swung about like snakes reaching out
in an attempt to sink their fangs into me. I was startled at first, but
was calmed by the sound of a fan shooting up a cloud of dust into the
room and creating a false wind. I stepped inside, only able to see
thanks to an attention red light above the in-wall fan. The moment I
reached the machine I dangled my arm out towards the off switch, but
before I even managed to press the button it shut down on its own. 

The wires wiggled about for a few seconds longer before returning to a
stillness, and the room became easier to make out. Cabinets and drawers
were scattered in uneven patterns alongside the edges of the room. I
spent upwards to an hour attempting to dig through everything the room
held, but all I found was endless empty sheets of paper and unused
journals, attached alongside a singular ballpoint pen. 

I gathered a few sheets and folded them into the pockets of my grime
covered sweatpants. Tucked into my left arm was the journal, and in my
right, both as a weapon and a tool, I clenched onto the pen of black
ink. 

As I made my exit I turned left once more and continued down the path. I
approached the next checkpoint of light and spotted a trail of footsteps
going in the opposite direction I was headed. 

It had not been there previously, I would have made note of it far
earlier than now. Without any other purpose, I changed trajectory and
set off behind my only lead.

I did all that I could in a place like this, I jotted down each moment I
have spent in this unfamiliar world. As I walk further into these halls
I continue to mark each moment no matter how pointless it seems to be.
While I wrote I felt the overwhelming pain of the countless thoughts
rattling my head hush away and giving me a sense of focus.

With a return back to where I had awoken, I could see the outline of
where I had rested. The footsteps did not halter beside my first memory,
instead they pushed onward and into the unexplored hall. 

Sound was an unprecedented sensation to me for the first while I had
been aboard this station. Yet, as I ventured deeper into the dark my
ears began to pick up on the light humming that filtered the air. If I’m
correct, I assume it to be the sound of all the fans in each room I pass
by all blaring at once. Perhaps, if I ever get a chance, I’ll shut them
all off and enjoy a complete silence. 

I pass through another zone lacking light and approach the next
porthole. I found that the trail had arrived directly in the middle of
the hall, and vanished. There were no more footprints, no sign of
turning around, and not even a hint at where they could have gone. 

There was no attempt to continue the search for them. I just went on
forward until I arrived at the next door just a few dozen steps away.
This time it was outlined by the star, and I was able to push it open
with ease yet again. 

The room shared only a few slight differences with the one I had entered
previously. I was thankful of the lack of wires in this one, but the
dust continued to persist with aid from the ever blasting fan. This
time, I didn’t even need to approach it for the machine to shut off
automatically. 

I was met with the same result from the drawers, piles of untouched
paper filled them from corner to corner, unless someone used ink
invisible in red light. With how easily the drawer slid open, I’d assume
these are used quite often, but everything looked decades old. I dug
through over a dozen of the containers and found nothing new, all until
the very last one where I spotted something written on the side of the
drawer. 

My head creaked closer to get a better view, with it I made out four
words. 

“Afraid of the stranger.” written across it in black marker.

I set my pen between the spine of the journal and set it onto the murky
ground. I pulled my knees closer to my chest and remained locked onto
the text. My eyes recognized the writing far sooner than my brain did. I
picked the journal up once more and flicked it open.

The words on the drawer had the same handwriting as my own.

I had written this sometime before my first memory, there was no doubt
about it. 

As if the station wanted me to flee the room, the red light that had
given me sight for so long had blinked away without a warning. With my
valuables in hand, I launched back up to a standing position and speed
walked right out the doorway.

I found myself sitting beneath the porthole and staring into the dark
room I was in before. My knees pointed to the ceiling and my journal
laid in my lap. Morbid curiosity was getting to me, I wanted something
to exit the room to give me another sign that I wasn’t alone here, yet
nothing ever happened. Thankfully as well, because if something did, I
would have fled. 

It was stupid, and as I walk down these halls once more I recognize the
lack of care I showed myself. I would much rather be alone aboard this
cement and steel hell than something lingering alongside me. 

As an hour of nonstop walking ensued, I came across a familiar, but
uncomfortable sight inside the light. Another trail of footsteps going
down the path that I came. 

This time, I did not shift myself around and follow, I continued onward,
because it was becoming clearer to me that whatever was roaming, was
trying to keep me there.
