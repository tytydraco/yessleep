My boyfriend and I had a fight the other day. It was the first real one
we’ve had ever, at least in my version of the relationship. In his, he
said we’d had our disagreements, but this time around had been better.
Until now, anyway.

It was honestly really stupid and I don’t even remember how it started.
I mean obviously I know, I was gonna go out to a girls’ night with a few
friends but he didn’t want me driving. I told him I was gonna take my
car and have one drink and I’d be back home, like usual, but he was
acting really strange about it. It finally came to a boiling point when
he hid my car keys, and I’m not trying to be super controlling, but I
felt like that was too much. I’ve since apologized to him but I did
raise my voice a little bit about it.

“What the hell is your problem?” I said, throwing the jacket he’d left
on the couch aside to look for my keys. Clark watched me from where he
stood by the front door. He’d hidden them before I’d even started
getting ready for the last-minute hang tonight, like he had a sixth
sense for my incoming texts.

“Just let me drive you,” he said. “I don’t know why you’re choosing this
hill to die on.”

“It’s my car,” I said. “I can drive it myself. I don’t know why you have
this thing about me not driving anywhere.”

“I don’t know why you have this thing about you driving everywhere,” he
said. “Look, we can just go together, I’ll say hi to them and drop you
off.”

“It’s fine. I don’t need you saying hi to my friends.” Like I needed his
approval to see them, for Clark to scope out the area because he
couldn’t stand to see me with other people besides him even for an
evening.

“Look, I’m gonna just walk there,” I called, leaving from the back door.
“It’s a twenty minute walk, and maybe I’ll have some long thoughts about
some things.”

My car sat dark in my duplex’s driveway as I strode past it. Clark
caught up to me and took my arm.

“What?” I said, trying to shake him off. “What is it? Because I hope
you’re about to give me my car keys back now.”

“Look, I didn’t want to make a big deal out of it,” he said, face
downcast, “but this is trauma related. It’s about the past life thing.”

I stared at Clark. He was still holding my arm. His grip faltered.

“I just…”

He sighed and sat down on the curb.

“I just have all this trauma left from losing you the first time, I
think. It makes me too worried about you sometimes.”

He looked up at me with an unreadable expression. The streetlight
overhead and behind him carved deep shadows into his face. All I could
see was a glimmer of an eye.

“Is that what happened?” I said quietly. “I was in a car?”

“It was dark,” he said. “I mean, I don’t really remember.”

“Were you there?”

He didn’t say anything for a moment.

“It’s all kind of blurry,” he said slowly. “I think I watched it happen,
but what I remember most is the pain of losing you. I didn’t want to be
without you again. Sorry. It’s just really hard for me to talk about
right now.”

“That’s why you get worried when I drive anywhere,” I said.

Clark stood and pulled me into an embrace.

“I can’t let you go again,” he said into my neck.

I breathed him in and listened to the crickets in the distance. My car
sat still in the driveway, hunched in the darkness outside the
streetlight like something waiting just inside the mouth of a cave. I
felt afraid looking at it, for reasons I couldn’t quite grasp in the
moment.

“You can drive me,” I said, and felt him relax. “I’ll get a ride home if
it ends up going too late. Okay?”

I stepped away from him, holding his hands, still looking into the
cavernous darkness of his eye sockets.

“Okay,” he said, and tilted his head so I could see his smile and the
twinkle in his eye. “Let’s get going. You’re gonna be late.”

We talked and made up as I got my things and we got in his car. I
apologized to him for snapping at him and we both promised to do better
and be more aware of the other person’s trauma. A healthy relationship
in action! I feel like communication is so much easier when the other
person knows you so well. Like he knew all the possible things I’d say
before I said them.

“Do you remember anything else from last time?” I asked as Clark pulled
out of the driveway. “Or is it just me?”

“It’s just you,” Clark said. “It’s always just been you. I wish I’d been
able to remember the stock market, or sports games to bet on, or
something, but I guess that’s outside the terms of the deal.”

“The deal?” I asked. “Was there actually a deal?”

“Oh, no, not actually,” he said, switching the song on the stereo. “I
meant more like, in the metaphorical sense. Of a deal. I just spent so
much time, you know, wishing things had been different, going to sleep
thinking that I’d do anything for one more chance with you, or just the
ability to do it all over again. And then one day, I woke up, and it was
just…like that. I was back before we’d met.”

“Would you do it again,” I said, “knowing that the same thing might
happen? Maybe you can’t dodge fate.”

“Maybe it’s just a question of persistence,” Clark said, looking at the
road. “You try for hard and long enough, you can bend history to what
you want it to be. It’s about having the right mindset.”

Something about what he’d said in that conversation was making it stick
in my head, something weird, but I think I was also feeling too heated
and maybe looking for things to fight about with him. But that entire
conversation, from me running out the back door to him driving me to the
party, is really vivid in my head still, like my mind wants me to
remember it. I’m just writing it down here mostly to look back at it
later and see if there’s anything that jumps out at me.

That was the extent of the fight, though. Aside from that, things have
been really good for the last few months. We’re actually thinking of
moving in together when my lease is up. We’ve had conversations about it
where Clark has mentioned things he remembered that caused a little bit
of friction, things that I didn’t even know I was doing, and I promised
I’d stop doing them.

Really, the only problem at this point is that our Mila (my cat) and
Tammy (his cat) hate each other. Like, hissing and growling and arching
their backs at each other hate. Clark said we’d been able to make it
work with our cats before, but my feeling is, if we know we’re gonna be
able to make it work, why rush it at all, you know? Why put our cats
through that shock if we could ease them into it and take all the time
we need?

I was relaying all this to one of my friends at the girls’ night, minus
the past life part. I trusted her for a lot of things, since her teenage
years had been a bit rougher than mine. She was a regular at food banks
and protests.

“Don’t rush it,” she said, shaking her head. “And make sure you really
know who he is.”

“I’ve seen his place,” I protested. “I know he’s not a slob or
anything.”

“I’m just saying. A switch flips with some men after you’ve been living
with them for a while,” she said. “They imprint on you or whatever. Then
they start thinking you’re their mommy.”

“He’s different,” I said, with the giant secret of our past life love
hovering in the air behind me. “He’s real. You wouldn’t get it, but he
is.”

“If you say so,” she said.

Then my other friend grabbed my arm and it was time to dance.

Later, I passed by my friend again as I was leaving the bathroom. She
was holding a glass of wine and yelling into some man’s ear over the
music about how to act at protests.

“The key to opsec,” she was saying, “is to assume your shit’s already
compromised.” She noticed me, and smiled and raised her drink before
turning back to the man. “Which it is.”

I thought about Clark’s sixth sense for my texts, and the chess move.

When I got home, I changed my phone passcode to something completely
random. Maybe I was overreacting, but it made me feel safer. I was
probably just being paranoid, though.
