People underestimate the security that monotony brings. It’s tough at
first, but you get used to anything.

5,475.

These are the days I have spent like this. For fifteen years my day has
been perfect, and I am finally at peace with myself.

I wake up in the dead of night to jot down dreams and thoughts in my
journal. At dawn I wash up and sit down to write stories, letters, or
notes. Sometimes I write letters to the parents of my former guitar
students. I never send them, but I like to remember how talented their
little girls were. I’ve been unemployed for quite a while now, but I
loved my job and I miss it. 

Around 8:30 I take a walk outdoors; it’s very helpful for clearing my
mind and breathing in a bit of the freezing morning air. It’s a real
cure-all to start the day off right. At noon I have lunch, while I spend
the afternoon reading, studying, or watching television on weekends. I
eat dinner very early, at 4:00 PM, and go to sleep at 10:00 PM.

Boring? Perhaps, but especially for an introvert like me, it isn’t hard
to get used to at all. You learn to love the monotony, until the day
comes when someone decides to turn everything upside down.

Frank showed up at the door. A large, smartly dressed man with his usual
uncompromising demeanor.

“Come on, let’s go to dinner,” he told me.

“But you told me later. How come?” I shot back, attempting a smile that
turned out awkwardly.

“Stop asking questions. Come on, move it!”

“Alright… but at least let me get ready. I can’t walk around in these
clothes a size too big, I look like I’m in pajamas. I look ridic-”

“Come as you are. Hurry up, there’s not much time.”

When I sat down at the table, I noticed that the annoying buzzing neon
light hanging above my head had finally been fixed. Damn it, about time!
With a place so run-down, no wonder customers never come back.

Today I was alone.

“Here you go, John, just as you requested,” Lucia said, placing the
plate on the table.

“You have an hour. We’re closing up,” Frank added.

Fifteen pork chops and an extra-large side of French fries. Today I’m
treating myself!

The smell of meat immediately filled the room. Fat, salt, pepper. An
irresistible aroma. I leaned over the plate and grabbed the first chop
with both hands.

The first bite was a release. My teeth sank into the warm meat, tearing
off a piece that was still steaming. I chewed quickly, without even
caring to swallow properly, at the risk of choking.

“How is it? Is it to your liking?” they both asked me in unison.

I looked up, my mouth still full.

“Magnificent. Tender, juicy, cooked just right. Lucia, pass my
compliments to the kitchen.”

“C-c-certainly,” she replied, taking a step back.

I didn’t understand why she was looking at me like that. Maybe I was
overdoing it. I was a glutton, I admit it. But it’s not every day you
get to eat this well.

I kept devouring the chops one after another. I grabbed them by the
bone, tore the meat away with my teeth, and sucked off every last scrap.
Every now and then I drooled from how hungry I was. I couldn’t stop.

After an hour, I had almost finished everything.

Frank’s walkie-talkie began to crackle, cutting through the silence.

“It’s time. The injection room is ready.”

Frank looked at me.

“You heard him? Let’s go.”

I stopped. A couple of half-eaten chops remained on the plate. A pity. I
would have liked to finish them.

I stood up and followed him. As I walked down the hallway, the sound of
the chains around my ankles echoed through the cells.

One final thought crossed my mind.

Maybe the pork had been overcooked…it didn’t taste quite like my dear
school girls.
