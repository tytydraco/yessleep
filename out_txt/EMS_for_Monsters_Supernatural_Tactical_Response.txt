The first time I died was right before I started my new job. I had been
working as a paramedic in Seattle/ King County for 3 years. I was
exhausted by the job but needed the money. Despite what you might see on
TV, emergency services aren’t all that exciting. Every call isn’t full
of beautiful people you pull from a burning building, or mouth to mouth
on the hottest guy or girl you’ve ever seen at your local college. It
isn’t full of rare, incredible, amazing medical stories ripped from the
headlines. There isn’t a man with an unexploded WWII mortar up his ass,
no groundbreaking medical case, there isn’t a woman who got impaled on
rebar and is stuck to another person who is also impaled by the same
rebar.

Most of the time the 911 calls and emergencies you respond to aren’t
even worth telling anyone about, let alone a headline.  God I would love
if that was the case. I love the excitement, the real emergencies, the
trauma cases with severe injuries. Most of the time its full of people
wasting your time or being a drain on the system. 

“Hello again sir, I saw you yesterday, you drink too much and fall and
hit your head again? You did…….. well let’s get a c-collar on him and
get him to the hospital.” (The staff is not happy to see him. Again.) 

“Sir, itchy feet is a problem for your doctor, not the ER. Make an
appointment with your doctor.” 

“No ma’am, I am not taking you to the hospital so a nurse can wipe your
ass.” (Yes, I had to tell someone that, several people actually).  

We do have meaningful cases, we do save lives, but it’s not as exciting
as people might think. 

 

My partner and I had just dropped another “sandwich run” (When the
patient doesn’t have anything wrong with them or a real medical
emergency, but they might say they do. Eventually, in the ambulance or
in the hospital bed, they will ask: “Hey you guys got any sandwiches?”
or ask about food.) It’s like being a sandwich delivery service where
you bring the person to the sandwich at the hospital.

My partner was a new guy named Sean. He’d only been around for a month.
He was a little shorter than me, blond with green eyes, and a few
freckles smattered across the bridge of his nose and his cheeks just
under his eyes. He had just graduated from school and was new to the
streets and I was still teaching him about the reality of the job.
Before he was working in EMS, he had been working in sales at Nordstrom.
Not exactly a lot of cross-over between tailored suits and putting in
IV’s and calculating dosages, but people in EMS come from every
background.  

By contrast, I had been on the job for 3 years and had plenty of street
smarts before I had even started as a paramedic. I’ve got ocean blue
eyes and dark hair from my Italian side of the family that I brushed
back as it grew out. My hands had lifters callouses from lifting
patients and equipment all day, and sometimes the skin under my nails
peeled because of all the hand sanitizers. I still use moisturizer every
morning and when I get home, but it’s a losing battle in the winter. It
was starting to get wavier and even a bit curly in the back. Making an
appointment to get someone to cut your hair is hard when you work
nights. Not surprisingly, most businesses aren’t open at 3 AM on a
Tuesday. I had lost most of my tan after working nights for so long, but
I was applying for a daytime shift to try to get back to the land of the
living. Still love working nights and the people I worked with, but it
would be nice to be able to have a normal life. Then again, considering
what my life looks like now, guess normal is out of the question.  

 The city had been winding down as most normal people went to bed or
returned from a night out. Sean was talking to a woman in light blue
scrubs with her hair in a messy bun and dark circles under her eyes at
the nurse’s station. Sean had just finished stumbling through an awkward
patient report to the nurse when he committed a cardinal sin known in
emergency services and hospitals everywhere. 

“First call of the night, it’s been really quiet tonight.” Sean said.  

The nurse, her name was Gabby, stiffened and looked at him with eyes
that looked even more aged and tired. Gabby was in her 20’s like us but
night shift and working in the medical field ages you. The dark circles
under her eyes were even darker under the florescent lights. “Did you
seriously just fucking say that?”. Sean’s eyes widened and he started
stammering. Gabby leaned back in her old black office chair with cracked
armrests and looked at me expectantly. Another nurse and a medical
assistant peaked around the corner.  

“Who just said that?” the older MA asked with a frown. He immediately
figured it out after looking over his reading glasses at Gabby’s annoyed
stare. “Didn’t anyone tell you never to say the Q-word in the ER?” he
asked Sean.  

Personally, I didn’t really believe in the “Q-word” being a jinx. But
I’m also not usually one to tempt fate. That night though, I was bored
and felt like stirring things up. “Yeah, Sean don’t say it’s quiet! It’s
considered bad luck if you say it’s been quiet. Please don’t say it’s
quiet or we will get a bunch of calls and the emergency department will
get flooded.” Some of the staff physically cringed every time I said
“quiet”. Others just gave me a death glare that screamed “I hope your
next patient pukes on you”. I told Sean to hurry up and get back in the
ambulance as i walked through the sliding doors to the ambulance bay.
Once we were back inside the rig, I told him it was no big deal and not
to worry. Still not a good idea to say the Q-word unless you want to
piss off all the nurses.  

That night we got told to “post” (drive to the area and standby for any
9-1-1 calls that came in) over in Issaquah, which was unusual, but I’d
worked over on the east side before. The night was still, and the bite
of the winter air was waiting for us just outside the heated ambulance.
We were lucky to have heated seats in our newer model ambulance. The
windows were already fogged up from the heat and our breath against the
icy air outside. The roads were completely empty, the roads were
sparsely lit by the old- style orange streetlights that cast an
unnatural glow across the asphalt and the trees. I parked us in a
parking lot at the High Point trail head just off SE 79^(th) Street,
North of Tiger Mountain.  

The nice thing about Washington is there’s so many evergreen trees. Even
in the winter there’s lots of greenery. The trees by the trailhead stood
resolute and quiet, some of the leaves and ferns swayed in the night
breeze. The only sound was the radio and the engine’s hum in the
ambulance. In the winter we leave the car running a lot to stay warm.
More than one climate activist has chastised us for burning gasoline and
“contributing to global warming” It’s negative degrees at 3 am in the
winter sometimes- sue me. Right after you go after the corporations. I
made sure the doors were locked and rubbed the condensation away with my
arm, then wiped the cold water on my arm off on my pants.  

I felt around behind my seat where I had put my backpack before the
start of the shift. I felt the edge of the book I had been reading
during the shift and pulled it out. The top corner of the corner was
folded slightly, and I smoothed out the bend and hoped it wouldn’t
become a permanent crease. I pulled the blue nitrile glove I was using
as a bookmark out from between the pages. 

“Good book?” Sean asked. “What’s it about?” Sean was a reader too, he
had been reading Six of Crows and liked talking about books and TV
shows. We both liked the sci-fi and fantasy genres.  

“It’s pretty good.” I said as I put the glove back over page 137. “I
like it so far it’s about a man who-” 

We heard some yelling outside the ambulance. I put the book down on the
dashboard and rubbed the condensation off the window again. If there was
a fight or some crazy person out there yelling at us it would be time to
find a safer spot to park. If it was a walk-up, someone coming up to us
because they saw the ambulance and it was an emergency, we would get out
and help. You never knew what it was if someone knocked on your window
or waved at you. Sometimes it was just a friendly “Hi”, most of the time
it was a big middle finger because they got the bill later or it was
someone asking if we had drugs in the ambulance. I saw two figures in
the distance by the tree line at the other end of the parking lot. I
lowered my hand to the gear shift in case we needed to drive away
fast.  

The two figures stumbled out into the orange streetlight on the other
side of the parking lot, and I could immediately tell something was
wrong. There was a woman and a man supporting each other as they limped
towards us. Both of them were covered in mud, and there were leaves and
there was dirt and twigs stuck to their clothes and hair. The woman on
the left was limping but had the man’s arm draped over her shoulders.
Her right arm was hanging completely limp as she kept pushing forward. I
could tell it was broken. It looked too long and misshapen and had some
dark bruises I could see from the ambulance. She had some kind of cloth,
maybe a t-shirt, wrapped around her left foot that was covered in mud.
The man had blood all down the front of his shirt and on the side of his
face, and his clothes were torn in several places. His left arm ended in
a stump halfway between his shoulder and elbow that was covered in white
gauze that had soaked through in bright red blood.  

“Oh, shiiiit………” I breathed out. I opened the door and slid out of the
seat. “Sean get the back open!” 

“What’s going on?” He yelled over the side of the ambulance. I could
hear him open the back doors of the ambulance before he walked around
towards me. The man and the woman were still hobbling towards us, the
man was waving his bloody stump in the air. I started running towards
them. I couldn’t quite make out what they were saying from the other
side of the parking lot. I could make out…. “Coming…… lights… turn….”  

The two of them suddenly stopped. I kept coming towards them and I could
see the horror on their faces. The night air seemed so still it could
shatter. The parking lot and trees were silent, and all I could hear was
my own breath. Their eyes seemed to widen as they stared right through
me. Time seemed to slow as I turned. I saw Sean walking towards us with
a puzzled look on his face.  

I saw a pale, hunched shape crawling out of the trees out of the corner
of my eye. Sean was still walking towards us with his hand in the air.
In the space of a breath, there was a pale flash that launched towards
him and slammed into him and he flew through the air and out of sight
behind a tree. I could hear wet crunching that echoed across the dead
silence. A pair of glowing yellow eyes peered around the corner of the
tree trunk.  

“It found us.” I heard the woman whisper. I hadn’t realized they were
that close to me  

I could feel the icy grip of terror squeezing my chest. Everything in my
body screamed something was wrong. Part of my brain was screaming at me
to run. Something else deep inside me held me frozen in place, as if the
slightest tremor or movement would give me away and make me the next
target. All of me knew without a doubt that those glowing yellow eyes
didn’t belong to an animal. I also knew Sean was dead. I still don’t
know how, but I could feel it in my bones. Maybe some evolutionary
instinct deep in my DNA.  

“We need to get to the ambulance. We need light, we need fire,
something. Thats all that keeps it away.” The man covered in blood
whispered in a shaky voice. Blood was slowly dripping from his ravaged
arm. “I thought we could make it before nightfall.” 

The glowing yellow eyes disappeared behind the tree again. The wet
crunching came back louder. The sound of breaking bones and growls
slithered across the asphalt. The three of us slowly began to walk as
slowly as we could towards the ambulance. I carefully put my boots down
heel-to-toe to avoid ay noise. The man and woman next to me tried to be
quiet but their injuries made them drag and scrape their feet across the
loose gravel of the parking lot. The glowing yellow eyes peered out from
behind the tree again, and the creature reached a long, pale hand around
the tree. The hand looked human, but the skin was pale white, and the
fingers were too long, and ended in long, curved white and yellow claws
soaked in blood. The creature’s claws dug into the bark, and it made a
thick cracking sound as the claws raked jagged marks into the wood.  

The creature began to crawl forward on its hands and feet. Its face
finally came into view under the orange parking lot lights. It looked
somewhat human, it’s body was naked, pale and emaciated and its arms and
legs seemed wrong and ended in deadly claws. Its glowing yellow eyes
were sunken, and its cheek bones were sticking out. The creature’s mouth
had no lips, it looked like they had been ripped off and the flesh had
been peeled back to reveal rows of bloody sharp teeth that had bits of
flesh stuck between them. I walked slowly forward as it stalked closer
to us. The man and woman walked faster, not caring about making any
sound. The creature sniffed the air and dodged out of sight behind the
ambulance. I broke into a run for the ambulance while it was out of
sight. It reappeared on top of the ambulance, opened its bloody maw and
a hungry, hollow growl reverberated down to my bones.  

The monster launched itself off the roof of the ambulance so hard that
it rocked on its chassis and its bloody claws left gashes in the metal.
It sailed over my head, and I turned around as it landed on top of the
man and with a slash from its claws the man’s leg was cleaved off and a
spray of hot scarlet spattered the white parking lines. He pushed the
woman away from him as he fell to the ground.  

“AUSTIN!!!” The woman screamed as she fell backwards. She scrambled back
on her hands and tried to stand to run.  

Austin’s face was frozen in terror as the creature stood over him and
licked its bloody claws. Then something in his brain must have clicked
because he began screaming. The creature stared right at him as it
picked up his severed leg, his bloody claws digging into the flesh of
his thigh. Austin kept screaming, the sound barely drowning out the wet
sounds of the creature devouring the flesh and breaking his femur in
between its teeth. Austin was bleeding badly as his severed femoral
artery frantically pumped blood to a limb that was already gone. The
blood loss caught up with him and his face went white, and his eyes
rolled back into his head as he passed out. The creature ravenously
devoured the rest of Austin’s leg, its razor teeth slicing through the
flesh and bone with ease. It ate what was left of it whole, bones and
all. It turned to the woman who was still crying as she was still trying
to get away.  

I turned and ran for the ambulance while the monster was focused on the
leg. Light. We needed light. If Austin was right, the lights from the
ambulance would help keep it back. We stood no chance. The monster
charged at me; it was so fast the only thing I saw was its glowing
yellow eyes and its’ ravaged mouth full of horrifying razor teeth. It
slammed into me and sent me flying into the side of the ambulance hard
enough to crumple the metal siding of the patient compartment. My vision
blacked out as I fell to the ground. The freezing pavement bit into the
skin on my hands and face and helped me focus. I looked up and saw the
creature holding the woman by her neck and squeezing as her feet kicked
on the ground under it. 

I staggered as I got up and felt my way toward the ambulance. I could
feel the creature’s eyes on the back of my head as I tried to open the
door. The door wouldn’t open as I pulled on it repeatedly. It was
locked. I looked over and saw my keys on the ground just under the
ambulance. I didn’t have time to go for them. The creature was already
focusing its deadly glowing yellow eyes directly on me. I felt behind me
for the side compartment of the ambulance. There were accident supplies
in there, including road flares. I felt the latch click and reached
inside for the bag at the bottom of the compartment. 

I felt the length of the road flare and picked it up. The creature
coiled itself and launched itself at me again as I twisted the cap to
activate it and it burst into a brilliant, searing red light that washed
across the parking lot. The creature sailed through the air and wailed
as the light washed over its face. The creature slammed into me, and I
could feel something in my chest break. Its glowing yellow eyes looked
directly at me. It breathed directly into my face and the hot breath
reeked of rotting meat and made my eyes water. The flare had rolled
behind it and everything was awash in a red light. The creature held me
in place with an iron grip stronger than anything I had ever felt.  

I reached down into my pocket and felt my folding knife I kept concealed
for personal defense. I slid it out and flicked it open with my wrist as
quickly as I could. The creature reached a clawed arm back and I could
see the fresh blood dripping off its long, curled claws. It stabbed
through my stomach, and I felt blood leak down into my waistband. I
could smell the copper and the salt of my blood, it released steam that
rose between us as it hit the cold winter air. I stabbed the creature in
the ribs as it looked right at me, then pulled the knife out and stabbed
it over and over. The creature howled and I kept stabbing it.  

Then it lowered its head and ripped my throat out. It pulled me close,
and I felt its teeth tear through my windpipe and jugular and felt
lightheaded as my blood sprayed all over its chest. I just focused on
stabbing as much as I could. Stab. Stab. Stab. I felt the creature jerk
against me, but it wouldn’t die.  

The light wavered and I felt myself getting weaker and felt my vision
get darker, but I kept stabbing it everywhere I could. Suddenly the
light changed, and the creature jerked and howled in a shrill, alien
echo. The woman appeared over the creature’s shoulder, and I hear and
smell the hiss of burning flesh. She pushed the flare right into its’
back and I could see its chest and internal organs through the red,
magnesium powered radiance. The creature let up and flailed its arms
trying to pull the flare out. I felt something heavy drop and realized
it was my intestines falling to the ground. The steam glowed red from
the light as it let off heat in the icy winter atmosphere.  

I stepped forward on unsteady feet as I felt my throat get slick with
blood. I could feel it running down my chest and into my pants. I
couldn’t breathe. I couldn’t speak. I felt like I couldn’t think and my
whole body felt like it couldn’t have lifted a grain of sand. But I
stumbled forward and slammed the knife up between the 4^(th) and 5^(th)
ribs. I hit something hard. I worried maybe I missed the heart, or I had
hit bone instead. I could feel my strength failing so I sawed the blade
back and forth through the skin and muscle. It felt like cutting through
ice or bone.  

The monster shuddered and gasped as the searing fire from the flare
spread over it’s body at it was engulfed in fire. I could tell I was
going to die. I could feel the heat from the fire and I didn’t care. I
barely felt it. I just wanted to go to sleep. I grabbed the monster
tight as I kept working the blade back and forth. Something broke. I
heard more screaming as my vision went completely black.  

And that’s how I died. 

The first time, anyways.   

 

I woke up in the middle of the forest the next morning at the base of an
enormous tree. I had no idea where I was, but I knew I was supposed to
be dead. My clothes were torn through to my stomach and there was blood
all over my shirt and my pants and dried blood caked all over my skin.
Some of it was still wet. I don’t know how long I wandered through the
woods for. I think I lost my mind for a while there. I knew I had died.
I knew, intellectually, that the injuries I had gotten were fatal. My
throat had been ripped out and I had been eviscerated. Part of me wanted
to dismiss it as a dream or a hallucination. But I remembered those
yellow eyes and the smell of rotting meat on the monster’s breath. Then
I felt down over my white uniform shirt and could feel the ripped
clothes and the smooth skin of my stomach below it.  

Eventually I stumbled across a hiking trail and started following that.
I just kept walking. Then I collapsed at some point. I don’t remember
much. I was told some early morning hikers encountered me and called 911
and the park rangers.  

When I woke up again, I was in a bed in a windowless room with an IV and
not much else. I knew from my time in emergency medicine and
transporting patients to every hospital in the county that this wasn’t a
normal hospital room. I knew something was definitely wrong when the
nurse came in wearing a black jumpsuit with Kevlar around the neck
accompanied by two guards with military shotguns. Benelli M-4’s with
lots of different colored shells in the sidesaddle. The nurse walked to
the side of my bed and checked the IV.  

That’s when I noticed that I had been chained to the bed with handcuffs.
Sometimes we do that with police suspects, detainees or dangerous
patients. Except these handcuffs were darker and heavier and had strange
markings on them. The nurse backed out of the room and the two guards
stayed at the foot of the bed. I tried asking them questions or asking
them to uncuff me, but they remained still as statues. I kept asking
questions and one of them moved their finger from the side of the
trigger guard to the trigger itself. I knew when to shut up.  

Another hour later and a doctor came in. I assumed he was a doctor
because of the white coat. But unlike most doctors he had an enormous
revolver on his hip and several syringes strapped to his chest on a
leather shoulder holster. He had wild, greying hair and eyebrows and
wrinkles around his eyes. There was a large white scar descending from
the middle of his left cheek towards the middle of his jaw. I noticed a
very unusual dark metal ring on his left hand that had engravings not
unlike the handcuffs on my wrists. He flipped through several pages on a
clipboard silently before looking at me.  

“Good……” He looked at his watch. “Afternoon! I am Doctor Volker. It’s
good to see you awake. We were beginning to worry you might not wake up.
You’ve been through quite the event. And not a scratch on you. Consider
yourself very, very lucky.” He looked me up and down. I didn’t know him
that well, but I could tell that Dr. Volker was holding something back
and was slightly unsettled. It was like he was expecting something. 

“Open wide and say ahhhhh” he said over his glasses.  

“Uhhhh. AAAAAAHHHHHHHH.” I opened my mouth as far as I could. He looked
at my teeth from top to bottom, pulled out a pen light and shined blue
light into my mouth.  

“Wonderful!” He seemed genuinely pleased. He sat down on my bed and put
a hand on my leg. “I’m sure you have lots of questions but let me tell
you what’s happening right now.”  

“You encountered a creature known as a “Wendigo”. Malevolent spirit
possesses a weak-willed person who has desperately resorted to
cannibalism. They are supernatural creatures who feed on the flesh of
humans and any meat they can get and are known to be particularly
dangerous and active in the winter and at night. You encountered 2
hikers who had been lost for days who had been running from one.
Unfortunately, they hadn’t escaped before sundown and the creature
followed them down to you. There is some lore that suggests that someone
who kills a wendigo may become possessed by the malevolent spirit, but
you don’t appear to be undergoing the transformation. You should have
died based on the damage to your clothes and the significant amount of
blood. However, you haven’t come up on our tests for any common
regenerative conditions.” He looked at his clipboard.  

“Negative for lycanthropy, vampirism negative, iron, steel, silver and
holy water and oil tests negative, and showing no signs of possession.
This isn’t an exact science, mind you. We don’t understand most of it.
Science can’t even explain most of it.” The two guards seemed to relax.
The doctor nodded at one of them and they came forward and unlocked the
handcuffs by sliding his thumb across the engraved markings. I flexed my
wrists and sat up more in bed.  

“So……. I died?” I asked. I already knew the answer.   

“It would appear so. And I have no idea how you seem to have recovered.
I’m afraid we can’t allow you to leave the building, but we won’t keep
you chained up now that we have tested you thoroughly for any
supernatural viruses, diseases, parasites, possessions or malevolent
influences.” I sat back and didn’t say anything else. The doctor
regarded me over his low set reading glasses perched near the end of his
nose and waited to see if I had anything else to say. After several
minutes of silence, he left.  

I don’t know how long I sat there staring at my hands. They still had
dirt and blood caked under the nails and around the cuticles. I really
had died. I think they kept me there for another week. They had me do
all sorts of tests. Usual battery of blood, saliva, spit and urine. Then
they had me do more unusual tests. Holding holy books like the bible and
the Qur’an and the Talmud. Walking backwards. Hopping on one foot.
Putting raw steaks on a plate in front of me to see if I reacted. I
assume they were beef. I really, really hope they were beef. Strength
tests and tests where they cut my skin open with a scalpel just to see
if anything happened. They even threw water on me and asked me to walk
over some fresh dirt they poured on a doorway. They did the same with
salt.  

Eventually they “discharged” me. By “discharged” I mean they told me to
never, ever, under any circumstances talk about what I had seen or where
I had been. They told me if I said anything, I would disappear and end
up back in the facility under a different floor. They told me that I
would be given a doctor’s note, and my job had been told that I was in a
horrible accident with a drunk driver that had killed my partner. I was
sedated later that night. I never even saw a nurse inject anything into
my IV.  

Then I woke up back in my apartment. I had lots of text messages and
voicemails from coworkers and friends and the fire department. There
were even dying flowers that had been put in front of my door. Building
manager must have let them in. I threw them out along with the rotting
food from my fridge. I was finally able to check the date. A whole week
had passed since that night at the trail head. I got a notice about
Sean’s funeral. He was cremated. I doubt there was enough left of him to
bury. It definitely wouldn’t have been an open casket. After seeing that
creature eat a whole human leg, bones and all, I doubt the ashes were
actually his. I went to the service out of respect. Gave my condolences
to his girlfriend, a nice blonde girl named Susanna who was an MA at one
of the hospital’s upper floors. I’d only met her once before. His
parents and little sister were nice. They flew out from Montana. They
barely said a word. I think they were in shock like I was. It felt like
they wondered why Sean had died instead of me. I don’t know. Maybe I was
just projecting some survivor’s guilt. His sister hugged me and couldn’t
stop crying.   

The next few days, I probably drank my body weight in alcohol. I was
told in no uncertain terms I could take my time coming back to work.
Physical therapy, counseling, anything I needed. To be honest, I think
that’s the most unbelievable part of this story. 

Eventually, there was a knock on my door. A tall, severe looking black
man in a refined suit and a wide brim hat with a weathered briefcase
introduced himself. “Hello, I would like to talk to you about
the…incident from last week.” he said carefully. The way he said it I
knew he knew more than the “official story” of a drunk driver on a
back-woods road. I stood looking at him expectantly in the middle of the
doorway. Then I gestured to my couch. I could tell by the way he moved
that under the suit he had some muscle. He moved carefully,
purposefully, and looked around my apartment with a calculating gaze.  

His very nice brown dress shoes clicked on the floor of the apartment as
he pulled a chair out from the kitchen table and sat down. “You can call
me Mr. Bishop.  I know what happened in the woods. I believe you handled
yourself as well as could be expected. You also survived.” He paused.
“Well, “survived” is a strong word. You………..recovered. Regardless, I
would like to offer you a job. Someone with your skills and ability to
handle a crisis of the…….. unusual variety is in short supply. Consider
this a job offer.” He left a black card with silver lettering on the
table and stood up and left the apartment quickly. I sat there for a
while just looking at the card without picking it up.  

When I finally did, it read “STR”. It had a phone number on the back.  

 

A week later, I called. The voice that answered refused to spell out
what “STR” stood for. They told me to show up at their office the next
day for a job interview. Mr. Bishop was waiting for me. He told me that
STR stood for Supernatural Tactical Response. Some people in the
industry called it “Satyr”. He personally didn’t care for the name. He
told me he found satyrs to be rather annoying. I couldn’t tell if he was
joking. 

He told me that he had founded an organization, working with the
government, that was dedicated to discrete and private emergency
response services aimed at supernatural occurrences and populations.
There were violent monsters out there, such as the ravenous wendigo,
that were nothing but violent and destructive and needed to be put down
for the good of all. Members of STR would be expected to help with
termination or extermination of supernatural entities that were deemed a
threat to humanity or potentially alert the public to the existence of
monsters and the supernatural.    

The other element was that there was a need for individuals and entities
within the supernatural world to get medical care. A banshee couldn’t go
to a hospital for medical care. It’s a hospital, full of dying people.
She would probably not stop screaming, and then she would end up in the
psych ward. A cyclops would obviously draw stares. They have one eye.
And that’s not to mention that they’re huge. A cambion that passes would
probably be fine, but not one that has demon skin or vertical snake
pupils or horns or a tail, same goes for other demonic creatures and
demi-humans. A vampire obviously can’t go to the hospital. And sometimes
their human subjects or thralls needed medical care or a transfusion.
Try explaining why your girlfriend has holes in her neck and she’s half
dead because she’s missing over a liter of blood.   

STR was meant to be a combination of specialized first responders that
could do anything and everything. Members were meant to be paramedics,
surgeons, peacekeepers, crisis solvers, and armed SWATT for the
supernatural. STR had people in place to make sure that the supernatural
cases went to STR and not regular cops or firefighters and paramedics.
They get a call like “Help! Help! My girlfriend just grew fangs and
claws and she’s trying to eat me!” and send it to STR. That way, the
right people show up for the right crisis. Just like how you don’t send
police to a house fire, and you don’t send an ambulance for a murder.
You don’t send a ladder company from a firehouse to a ghoul infestation
that’s eating all the corpses in the cemetery. You send STR. 

There was a very long training process. They knew I already had medical
training, but they trained me again anyways. There was a whole new set
of protocols to follow. And a lot of new material to cover. What do you
do if your patient has 2 hearts? 4? What about if they have 6 stomachs
or 2 brains? There was also extensive weapons training, and at least
half of the training consisted of studying ancient texts, mythology,
cryptozoology and anatomy for creatures that had never been publicly
published. 3 months later, and I was working on a new rig, under a new
company, with much better pay and benefits. Plus, a LOT of hazard pay
and death and dismemberment life insurance. I still don’t know how I
came back to life, but I think it was something in those woods. My name
is Alex, and I am a member of Supernatural Tactical Response.
