‘You get what you pay for’ doesn’t even come close to explaining my last
apartment. It was cheap, but it was still almost more than I could
afford. A top floor apartment in a run down building, nothing glamorous,
pipes hissing and bumping around, thin walls, an unreliable elevator,
but still manageable. It was my new home. It was good, for a while. 

For the first couple of nights, I was passed out on my bed from hauling
and rearranging what little I owned, but after that, I started hearing
something strange. 

Footsteps. 
Above me. 

Not creaking from the building settling, not bumps from branches hitting
the roof once or twice, clear enough steps moving around on what could
be the attic or something. The first day after I noticed it, I was able
to trace it to my living room. They seemed to be making circles around
the room above, slow steady footsteps, not stomping, simply walking in a
circle. When I asked the landlord about it, he seemed confused too. “No
workers are gonna be up there that time of night. You’re on the top
floor. There’s no sixth floor. There’s a little maintenance space above
part of the hall, but nothing over your apartment. I’ll ask the super.” 

Shrugging it off, I assumed that was it. The super or janitor were just
weirdly moving things around or maybe had zoned out. Not a big cost for
living on the top floor, I planned to just ignore it and move on, maybe
turn up the volume when I vegged out on the couch. After a couple of
nights of hearing that, I decided to go up and check it out, maybe I’d
make a new friend and get some extra help around the house if they were
already up. 

Finding the stairs up, I went to check on the space above me, but it was
locked. It also looked like a half-floor or something. Not enough height
to fully stand up, with a narrow door. So the new friend idea was a
bust. That night, they started moving heavier things around. It sounded
like furniture, maybe a couch or some shelves? Every night for the next
week, I heard some scraping or shuffling, step after step that seemed to
start around midnight. Then the routine started changing when I turned
up the volume of the tv enough that it could block out most of the
noise. The footsteps moved from just the living room. They started
moving in another pattern, from above my living room, to the kitchen,
the hallway, the bathroom, ending above my bedroom, then back again. A
new pattern, with the footsteps sounding a bit more solid, but not like
stomping…yet. 

After a week, the landlord finally got back to me via text. He told me
no one had gone into that room in weeks, maybe months. That couldn’t be
right. I texted back that I heard someone the night before, and every
night for the past 2 weeks or so. Since just after I moved in! He didn’t
believe me, said it might be an animal stuck up there or something, and
that the super would go up there to chase it out. 

Frustrated by the lack of concern over a possible homeless person or
madman squatting up there, I decided to record the noise that night.
Every time I tried though, my cellphone didn’t pick up anything. It had
me thinking that I really was just imagining it all, but it sounded so
real. Annoyed that I couldn’t prove it, I decided to ignore it for a
while, and it seemed to work, the footsteps went back to their shuffling
more than full steps. 

Eventually it seemed to calm down again. I decided to have a friend over
after work, we’d both had a long week and needed to relax for the
weekend. 

They heard it too. 

They confirmed that it sounded like someone was taking slow steps every
few seconds. I had told them about it over work after I moved, and they
thought it was something like a ghost story to freak them out before I
invited them over. After turning off the tv and looking up to listen
more clearly, they looked a little freaked out anyway, “Why does it
sound exactly like your apartment?” 

I hadn’t really noticed it before, simply thinking it was cluttered, but
it DID sound like they were following the same floor plan. We decided to
turn the tv back on at a much louder volume and try to ignore it again.
The night was pretty much over after that, they decided to split after
finishing a movie and it seemed like they definitely wanted out of there
asap. 

The following day, I tried the landlord again, waiting until I could
talk to them face to face. This time he got visibly uncomfortable. He
asked me, “Have you responded to it?” Which got me really confused. 

“What?”
“Knocked back. Banged on the ceiling. Said anything?”

When I told him I hadn’t he got this relieved look on his face and just
said, “Good, keep it that way.” before walking away. I stood there,
confused and wondering what that whole interaction was about before
realizing he hadn’t answered any of MY questions…or maybe he did. 

Feeling even more weirded out, I returned home and tried ignoring them
again. It sounded like the landlord knew something, but didn’t want to
share. Maybe it was an illegal apartment, maybe a family member was
staying without paying or something. Either way, I was going to ignore
it all. I was. Really. That was the plan, until the footsteps stopped
one night. Right. Above. My head. 

Then they knocked three times. 
Whoever “they” were. 

I tried to resist the urge, I didn’t want to, but I couldn’t help
letting out a quiet, “hello?” No response other than the footsteps
moving on again, more clearly this time. It annoyed me, but I thought
nothing more about it. Maybe they were just happy to be acknowledged? 

That’s what I thought, until I returned from work the next day. In the
elevator there was a new button. I was supposed to be on the top floor,
5, but now there was a button with a number 6 on it, just above mine. I
stood there for a long time before texting the landlord to come and see
it. The landlord arrived after a few minutes and saw the button. He
immediately went pale, told me not to press it and just ignore it. The
next morning it was gone again, and the panel was smooth, like it never
existed. I wanted to press it last night, but the landlord stayed in the
elevator the whole ride up. Going to the stairs, I went back up them to
see if the door was unlocked now or something, but the door was gone.
The stairs ended in a fresh plastered wall now. 

That wasn’t the end of it though. Even more strange things started
happening. One morning, I woke up and saw a vent near my kitchen wall,
it looked brand new, but I hadn’t heard any cutting or installation work
last night. I even smelled some fresh cookies coming from it. From
above. Another day, I heard water coming down from above. Not rain
water, but water from a sink or something else. Then they turned on
their own tv, I could barely hear the muffled sounds coming from above,
sometimes a show, sometimes sports, but they seemed to settle on one
channel a night. It was getting louder now, and the footsteps were still
continuing, regardless of any other changes. 

Since the landlord was ghosting me now, I decided to check up online,
see if I could find any records of the building, maybe some old tenants
of the same floor. I found a picture from when the building was first
completed. It had five floors. There were some articles about the
building changing hands every few years, then an article about a fire on
a sixth floor. Another about reconstruction up to the fifth floor. Then
another article with the full six floors. Again and again, back and
forth, until the last articles I could find were all mentioning the
reopening with only five floors. 

After that, I noticed something else. The footsteps, they stopped moving
in their usual circle. Now they were following me. A few steps behind,
every time, stopping when I stopped, and continuing when I started
again. I would go into the kitchen to make dinner, open some cabinets
and close, then I would hear the sound a few seconds after, the cabinets
slamming shut, almost like they wanted to make sure I heard them closing
the cabinets too. I would get out of the shower and they’d have their
own still running. The footsteps started around 10 seconds behind me.
After a few days they were around 5 seconds behind, clattering plates
and noise from the kitchen getting closer behind me every day, until one
day I realized they were matched up, even when I cut a shower short, or
stayed longer than I would have. 

At that point, I was almost ready to throw in the towel, looking up new
apartments and thinking of taking any penalties for breaking my lease
early. The night I heard them catch up, I lay awake in bed long after I
should have. That’s when I heard it. Whispering above my bed, or maybe
they were just that muffled, but I could finally hear a voice coming
from above. They seemed to be talking to someone, maybe on the phone,
but when I strained my ears to hear it more clearly, I ended up wishing
I hadn’t. The voice I heard above my bed was my own. My voice. Talking
about something I couldn’t catch clearly. I heard a few things about new
apartments I’d looked up, work, and finally, hearing clearly for a
second; 

“There isn’t an apartment below mine.”

Nope. Nope nope nope. I went to stay over at a friend’s home that night.
I started texting my landlord about moving out, even if I had to get my
things shoved into a storage locker before finding a new place and couch
surfing until then. I still had to get some things for work out of
there, but when I did, that time I saw the button for the sixth floor in
the elevator. Tapping it, nothing happened, so I went the other way.
Checking the stairs again, I saw that there was a sixth floor landing
now. With a door and everything, but I was NOT going to check that right
now. I just grabbed some things and left again. 

At least, that was the plan. 

Turning around to walk down from the landing, I heard someone crying
inside. They didn’t sound like me, instead, it sounded like someone
younger. I know I shouldn’t have gone in, but if someone else had been
lured up here for some reason and I left them there, I would’ve felt
awful. I would have been like the landlord, ignoring the problem in
front of me to protect myself. Psyching myself up, I tried the door and
found it unlocked. Taking the first step into the apartment, I was
stunned by what I saw. 

It was MY apartment. 
At least, at first glance. 
Same furniture arrangement.
Same dishes.
Same photos.
But subtly wrong.

Walking up to one of the pictures, I was standing next to a woman I
didn’t know in one picture. Another had me visiting a landmark, some
giant dinosaur. 
There were a few others, all with strangers or in places I’ve never
gone. 
Walking into the kitchen, I checked the fridge, and all of the food
inside had strange brands on them. Close enough to the ones I bought,
but…not. 
I walked into the bedroom and found a closet of different clothes. My
size for sure, but nothing in my style. 

Walking around the apartment, I saw another door, leading to a staircase
that kept going up. Shutting that door quickly, I realized that the
crying sound was gone. It had been gone since before I saw the pictures,
and now I heard footsteps again. Below me. 

They walked from the living room to the bathroom, to my bedroom, and
stopped there. I heard the bed creak a little as something dropped into
it and got comfortable. I started rushing out of the apartment when my
cellphone buzzed again, ringing with a call from the landlord. Picking
up with trembling hands, before I could say anything I heard his tired
voice on the other side, “You weren’t supposed to answer it.” I shouted
at him, demanding to know what the hell lived on the sixth floor, and he
replied with no emotion in his voice, “Nothing. That’s the problem. It
builds itself out of whoever notices it.” 

Rushing out of the apartment, I started running down the stairs. I had
to get out of there. I had to not think of it anymore. 

Running down.
Five.
Four.
Then, at the next landing, I saw Five again. 
Running down the next. 
Six
Every visible landing had a “Six” now

Every floor said the same thing. 
Then the knocking started. Behind every door. At the same time. 

I was freaking out too much, I looked for the fire escape and rushed
down the stairs there, the alarm blaring after forcing the door open.
Stumbling out of the door, instead of the alley I was in a forest.
Looking around confused, I heard the knocking getting closer, coming
from the other side of the fire escape’s door, so I started running. 

Moving through the woods, the knocking kept coming, from the trees, from
branches, from the ground close to my feet. Turning away every time the
knocking got too close, I eventually saw an orange glow in the distance,
like from a Campfire. Crashing through the last bushes, I almost fell
into the clearing, and saw several people turning around to see me.
Normal people and none of them looked like me. I started babbling,
trying to tell them what happened, why I was there, “There was an
apartment…” One of the people said, “Above yours?”
 
Knock. Knock. Knock.

It was coming from the air above the clearing, 
Only I looked up. 
The others didn’t.
Nothing but stars.
 
An older guy in a green hooded coat held up a hand, “don’t answer.” He
waved me closer to the Campfire, “Stay where you can see the fire.” When
I asked why, he turned back to the tending the fire before responding,
“Because if you wander far enough away tonight, you’ll find your
hallway.”
