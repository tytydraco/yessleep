As I sit, I stare up at the distant canopy. The sun is scarcely visible
through the thick, green leaves. My stomach aches terribly. I grasp at
it, feeling around. Every slight movement sends pain crawling up my
back. I try not to move, hoping to ease the torment, at least
temporarily. Amid the searing pain in both my legs and abdomen, the
thought and smell of death creep through me.

Squirrels surround me. I’m sure I’ll be like them soon.

It’s an uncomfortable thought. Even now, I try my best to push it away.
Still, it’s hard not to feel anxious about what’s about to happen. A
sudden shaft of sunlight pierces through a break in the leaves, blinding
me. I let out a small groan, using my last remaining strength to raise
my hand and shield my eyes. The leaves around me rustle and whisper
amongst themselves. Even now, I can’t tell if it’s the wind or that…
thing.

When I first arrived, I considered myself fortunate to have found such a
beautiful place. I was like a rabbit, unaware of the coyote that would
soon emerge from the grass and claim it as food. The trees stood in
long, uneven rows, their branches intertwined overhead. Beneath them,
the grass grew untouched, and small flowers appeared wherever the
sunlight managed to reach. It was a place that seemed to have been
forgotten by the rest of the world.

But amid all these immediate sensations, one feeling always lingered at
the back of my mind.

I had the growing suspicion that someone, or something, had entered the
forest, and it didn’t seem to enjoy my presence. I would always look
over my shoulder, only to see trees. The calls of birds and the chatter
of squirrels temporarily distracted me. I’d use any excuse to look
forward, never wanting to look behind me. Even with the possibility of
something stalking me, this place was better than the outside world.

I didn’t bring any food or water. I knew I wanted to get away from
everything. I thought this place would offer a silent escape from the
world. Soon after arriving, I walked through the green fields and
beneath the tall trees. Whenever the sun fell below the horizon, the
forest became silent. As daylight disappeared, the forest filled with
darkness. I was always afraid of encountering a black bear or bobcat and
being attacked. I didn’t want to die that way.

I’m not sure how long I walked. Hell, by the time I thought about it, I
was already limping. It must have been hours. Moonlight shone down on
the land ahead, bathing everything in a slick, silver fire. At least I
could see. With every passing step, my paranoia intensified.

My legs had already begun to ache. I’d never been particularly good at
walking long distances. My face was slick with sweat, which soaked into
my hair and clothes. Thoughts circulated through my mind, distracting me
from my walk. Did I really want to be out here? I thought I did, but I
was beginning to realize that I didn’t. I had only convinced myself
otherwise. Quickly, I became panicked and turned around, heading back
the way I came.

I shoved my way through the trees, running through grass and leaves. The
moonlight quickly vanished, leaving the landscape in almost complete
darkness. I kept running, my hands stretched out in front of me. My eyes
watered, and my knees shook. For the first time, I regretted coming out
here. The smell of wet bark and damp earth filled my nostrils.

I felt around in the dark, my hands acting as my eyes. Everything was
recognizable: a slightly damp tree, another damp tree, and then…
something smooth and dirty. I quickly pulled away. For a second, I
didn’t react. It must have been something explainable. Then I heard the
air subtly shift. I screamed and ran backward, desperate to reach the
moonlight.

As I ran, the thing behind me slowly walked, pushing aside the grass. I
looked behind me in a desperate attempt to catch a glimpse of the being.
I could see nothing but darkness and the subtle, silver outlines of
trees. Before I could react, I slammed forcefully into a tree. My head
took most of the impact, and I was immediately knocked unconscious. I
can still feel the swelling pain on the side of my head.

When I awoke, presumably hours later, the sky was painted in soft shades
of orange and pink. As I looked around, I realized I was near the edge
of a clearing. I was disoriented, and my vision was slightly blurry, but
I managed to get to my feet. Through the tangled, thorny bushes, I could
see something concrete. Was it a manmade structure? Maybe I wasn’t as
far from people as I thought.

My knee buckled, sending me stumbling into the bushes. Sharp thorns
scratched at my skin as I struggled to get back up. I finally managed to
stand, holding my injured knee. As I peered over the bushes and looked
at the structure, it became clear what it was. A well. It was mostly
hidden by the earth, but its upper half was still visible. Covered in
vines, it was hard to say whether it had been used in the past few
decades.

I limped over to it and peered down into the darkness. Nothing. I picked
up a rock and dropped it inside. Whatever it struck was solid.

“No goddamn water!” I yelled in frustration.

This was the only hint of humanity in the entire forest. My tongue felt
thick and dry against the roof of my mouth. Even swallowing had become
uncomfortable. I tried to steady myself against a tree, but my legs
trembled beneath me. The clearing ahead seemed to drift in and out of
focus. In the far distance, something stood at the horizon.

He remained motionless, staring intently at me.

“Hey… man! I need water!” I yelled, my voice dry and weak. I began
moving toward him, desperate for help. The man did not move. My eyes
began to close. For a moment, I diverted my attention to the ground.
When I finally found the strength to look back up, the man was gone.

From what I’d seen, I wasn’t sure that was a man at all. Either a
hallucination or that… thing. I collapsed, pressing myself against the
dirt. After a moment of rest, I got back up and continued into the
forest. I tried to remember where I had entered, but I couldn’t find it.
I was lost, but I continued searching. Again, night fell. The familiar
dread entered my mind once more. As the wind blew, the leaves moved.

I couldn’t tell whether it was the wind or that thing following me. With
every step, the forest began to feel more primal and desolate. Was I
going the right way? I wondered. I sat down near a tree. The cold air
passed through me as though I weren’t there. Again, the leaves rustled,
even when the wind wasn’t blowing. Every time, the sound came from
behind me. It was always getting closer.

When I woke up, a migraine woke with me. I rose and looked around.
Trees. I couldn’t say I expected anything different. My knee seared with
pain whenever I put even the slightest bit of weight on it. That day,
the migraine blocked out most of my thoughts. I tried to think, but the
persistent pain distracted me from anything else.

I walked toward what I hoped was an exit for hours, but I never arrived.
I only reached more trees. Tall trees that seemed to ignore my presence.
Nature appeared indifferent to my suffering, but I didn’t care. My
migraine left me confused and disoriented.

My mouth was dry. Whenever the forest began to quiet down, that thing
started following me. I could hear its footsteps against the ground. It
would walk, then suddenly stop, seemingly afraid to get too close. At
this point, I didn’t scream or run. Not because I didn’t want to. I
certainly did. I was simply too exhausted to.

My body had deteriorated. I couldn’t even tell whether whatever followed
me was real anymore. Did it want me to think that? Did it want me to
question its existence and let my guard down? I turned around. Nothing.
Whenever it came up behind me, it was almost as though I could feel it.
My knee gave a soft pop whenever I walked. It hurt.

Exhausted, I fell to the ground. As I stared around, my eyes settled on
my shoes. They were dirty and old. Slowly, I pulled them off. A foul
odor escaped the moment I removed the first one. My feet were mostly
numb, and I was too afraid to see what they looked like. I pulled off my
sock, and some of the damaged skin came away with it.

My foot was deathly pale in places, with patches of red and dark
bruising. Between my toes, the skin was raw and damaged. I gagged.
Surprisingly, it hurt very little. Quickly, I pulled my sock and shoe
back on, hiding my feet from view. I brushed my hair back with my hands
and looked around. It was still only midday. Nothing lay ahead of me
except more walking.

I tried to cry, but no tears came. My lips were dry. I wanted to get out
of this place. Even if I escaped, though, there was nothing waiting for
me outside. I had always felt detached and alone. I was never able to
deeply connect with people, and the only person I had ever truly
connected with was gone. I missed feeling alone even when I was
surrounded by people. Now, I was simply alone. My migraine began to
subside slightly.

I could see squirrels in the trees and occasionally watch rabbits run
by. I might have tried to catch one, but I was too slow. Only now did
the pain in my feet begin to seep in. My head hurt. I was too tired to
walk anymore. If I was lucky, perhaps someone would find me sitting
there. Commotion stirred behind me. It could have been the animals,
indifferent to my presence, or that thing toying with me.

After I thought I had regained enough strength to walk, I stood back up.
Pain shot through my feet, spreading up my legs. I grunted and groaned,
but persisted. My legs were still somewhat numb, though I could loosely
control them. Dizziness clouded my mind. My vision narrowed, and my ears
rang. With all my remaining energy, I tried to stay upright, using the
trees for support.

After a minute or so, the numbness in my legs began to lessen, leaving
the pain more pronounced. Another minute passed. As I pushed myself away
from a tree, I fell. I hit the ground hard. My stomach began to clench
and heave. My body tried to vomit, but little came up. I retched until
the feeling finally passed. It was painful.

My abdomen continued to contract, but eventually, it stopped. By then, I
knew getting up wasn’t an option. My eyes locked onto the forest floor.
I grasped at the ground and pulled myself into a sitting position
against a tree. Its bark pressed roughly against my back. When I finally
settled, it was nearly dark. I knew the thing would soon return. I had
no idea what it wanted from me.

There was no moonlight that night, only darkness. I couldn’t see a foot
ahead of me. Not long after, he arrived. He came from behind me, as
usual. His footsteps were long and cautious. I trembled, holding my
eyelids shut. Slowly, he came closer and closer. There was a moment of
hesitation between each footstep, but he continued. Eventually, he
walked around me.

His footsteps stopped directly in front of me. Silence, except for the
faint movement of the wind. He backed up. Something fell from his arms
and landed on the ground. I flinched. For what seemed like hours, he
paced around me, something dropping with each slow, methodical footstep.
Whatever fell was both meaty and dry. I looked up, but saw only
darkness.

Eventually, the pacing came to a complete stop. The smell of rotten
fruit and spoiled meat filled the air. As I looked through a small crack
between my fingers, I could see that the sun had risen. With hesitation
and fear, I finally lifted my head. Looking around, I could see what the
thing had been doing all night.

All around me lay a neat circle of dead squirrels and sticks. Between
each stick was a squirrel, and between each squirrel was a stick. I
looked around in disbelief. After a moment of hesitation, I tried to get
up. My feet were too injured to carry me. As the sun rose completely, I
could see more clearly. Something weighed down one of my pants pockets.

I reached inside. It was rectangular. After pulling it out, I realized
it was a phone. I must have left it in there. The screen was cracked. I
didn’t think I could walk or move anymore. I could only sit and wait,
hoping someone would find me before that thing returned. I wasn’t sure
which possibility frightened me more. My fingers were sore as I typed
this. There was barely a single bar of service, flickering on and off,
and very little battery left. I hoped this would go through before the
phone died.

Maybe I was near civilization. Maybe I could crawl there and get help.
But I wasn’t sure I had the strength to do that.

I don’t want to die.
