Most of us have had to deal with bad roommates before, right? Whether it
be an inconsiderate family member on vacation or an annoying sibling
hogging all the hot shower water. Bad roommates are not uncommon,
especially in today’s world. The cost of living is sky high and rent
keeps climbing, so I was keen on finding a roommate for my first year of
college. After interviewing weirdos and borderline creeps, I found
Jamie. He was a tall, lanky, socially awkward, but overall nice guy. So,
I chose him as my roommate for my first ever apartment.   

In August, he helped unload all my boxes from my car, helping me carry
them up three flights of stairs to our apartment. He was always the
first to volunteer to do the dishes. He never complained about cleaning
the kitchen or the bathroom. He was quiet, amiable, and seemed like
the perfect roommate. The only part I didn’t love was how late he stayed
up. 

He was a night owl, staying awake all night long despite his early
morning shifts before class. But what could I really say? He was the
picture-perfect roommate I had dreamed of while scrolling through
countless roommate applications over the summer. He wasn’t ever too loud
at night, and I could only hear the tv sometimes when he got really into
watching anime. So, I let it be.   

That was until I started hearing weird noises at night. 

I was used to the creak of our old floorboards or the hum of the
bathroom light, but I started to hear a faint scratching sound. 

 Scratch Scratch Scratch.   

The next morning, I put out mouse traps. I assumed we had rodents
crawling around in the walls, and that was the last thing I needed
before finals.  
  
But after an entire week of hearing the scratching all night long,
despite the traps I had put out, I finally asked Jamie if he heard it
too. I figured he had to since he’d stay up all night long.  

He glanced down the hall toward our bedrooms, before answering me, 

 “Must be the rats. I’ve never heard anything. Hope your traps work.”

I agreed, but his reaction felt off. Again, I was tired from sleepless
nights so I just let it go. I hoped the traps would work too. 

But the scratching continued.  

I grew tired of it, and finally one night I covered my ears, squeezing
my eyes shut, trying to force myself to sleep but the scratching just
got LOUDER and LOUDER. Until it stopped. Relief washed over me. I could
finally sleep.  

Scratch scratch scratch.  

I jolted upright in my bed. The scratching was closer now. I could hear
footsteps in time with the scratching, moving down the hallway toward
me. I watched as the flickering bathroom light trickled underneath my
door was swallowed up step by step. Maybe it was Jamie playing a sick
trick on me all along.  

Scratch scratch scratch.  

I heard my door handle jiggle. Thank God I locked my door out of
paranoia each night. Then I watched as the lock turned over, frozen in
place, as the handle followed.  

The door slowly swung inward to reveal nothing. No Jamie. No intruder.
Nothing.  
 

I climbed out of bed. If Jamie wanted to mess with me, I’d give him a
piece of my mind.  
 
I searched the apartment for twenty minutes. Checking his room, finding
only dust. Checking our bathroom, only to find my shower products. I
even checked the fridge, looking for the special chocolate Jamie always
kept in there, yet all I found were my meal prep containers. I tried to
remember the last time I had seen Jamie. I could picture him carrying my
boxes up the stairs, doing the dishes, or watching anime on our couch.  
 
But that wasn’t right. My mom had helped me move in. I hated doing the
dishes, so I installed a dishwasher. I didn’t even own a tv. I’d never
put out applications for a roommate. I’d never interviewed anyone. I’d
never met Jamie.  
 
Scratch scratch scratch. 

I turned towards the sound. It was coming from Jamie’s bedroom.  

Scratch scratch scratch. 

I didn’t live alone. But I couldn’t remember what I’d been living
with.   

I type this as I hide next to the fridge. Listening to that incessant
scratching. I wish my traps had worked.
