Maybe you’ve read my other story, or maybe you haven’t. It doesn’t
really matter, as this case isn’t related at all. The only thing you
have to know is that I worked for a secret government agency that hid
and killed the supernatural.

The biggest ongoing investigation I ever worked on concerned a secret
cabal known by the Agency as “Conventus Nocturnus”. Yes, it’s just Latin
for night meeting, I know. I swear it’s what they call themselves.

They were (and still are) a coven of vampires. In addition to countless
deaths, you’ll surely be glad to learn that a lot of crime rings can be
traced back to this particular group of vampires. It seems being
superhuman and forced to live at night makes you a prime candidate to
lead the darker sides of humanity.

I’ve come across Nocturnus quite a few times during my career, and even
led an investigation against them for quite a while, but I will sadly
have to admit that I never managed to dismantle the organisation, or
even come across a tangible trail of its leader, known to me only as
“The Chancellor”. Still, the many problems I did manage to create for
the association are some of my greatest achievements.

I guess I will start at the beginning. As such, this case won’t directly
involve Nocturnus. It is instead the first time I ever came across one
of their more important operatives, which, for the sake of this story,
I’ll refer to only as I knew her at that time: the Mistress.

For those who want to learn more about Agent Christmas, I’ll have to ask
you to wait a bit more, as, if I got my dates in order, Agent Christmas
wasn’t even born when this took place.

Instead, you’ll have to tolerate Agent Vegas, one of the partners with
whom I worked the longest.

Vegas was once a criminal who tried turning informant when he learned
his business was secretly run by a bloodsucking creature of the night.
Apparently, murder, prostitution and extortion were completely fine in
his book, but he had to draw a line at exsanguination. While the FBI
laughed him out of their office when he insisted a bit too much on the
vampire part, the Agency secretly took him in and trained him into one
of the most dangerous agents I ever met. Everything he lacked in
morality, he more than made up for in raw intensity. As far as the
Agency went, this made him one of their best.

Her Haven

It was just another day at the office. I had been going through the same
newspapers over and over again, hoping to find something worth my time.
Nowadays, most of our investigations start on the Internet, but back in
the day, we had to work with what we had: newspapers and the good old
rumor mill.

Like most workdays, Vegas had spent the entire day at the gym, getting
ready for his real workday. Vegas was by no means a lousy investigator,
he just had a different way of doing things. While I spent my days
scouring the news for breadcrumbs, Vegas spent his nights in shitty
clubs and terrible bars, listening to what the underworld had to say.

Five o’clock hit and, having no lead to work on, it was time to go home
and drink myself to sleep like I always did back in the day. Just as I
was getting to my car, Vegas caught up to me, his jacket thrown over his
shoulder so he could show off all the muscles he kept under his shirt.

“Hey,” he called out, “what you up to tonight?”

I shrugged, fiddling a bit faster with my keyring.

“Wanna go out?” he asked. “I know a dump where the girls are hot and the
lights are dim, so they won’t see all the wrinkles eating away at your
face. Seriously, ese, you need to take a load off.”

To this day, I don’t know what made me accept this man’s invitation, and

to accept that Vegas just had that much charisma (he didn’t).

The night was well underway. Vegas had spent most of it eyeing random
girls, wondering which one he was going to work his magic on. I had to
keep reassuring him that I was enjoying myself whenever he remembered to
pretend to care about me.

Then, just as I was about to call it and let Vegas loose on this
establishment, she came in: a bona fide creature of the night. I had

greyish skin that would have appeared sickly on any human, but somehow
fed into her hypnotic allure. Stark black hair, as dark as the void left
behind when her soul left her husk behind, flowed gracefully on her
shoulders and back, carried by a wind that didn’t blow in this dingy
club. Under the dim neons that illuminated the place, I could see the
yellow glow in her eyes, or at least I could imagine it. She was a
predator out on the hunt; there were no two ways about it.

I went to point her out to Vegas, but he had already spotted her, trying
his best not to look suspicious as he turned back toward me.

“Not tonight, ese. We’ll circle back tomorrow,” he explained, as quiet

He stared at me. “I’m not joking,” he said. “We can’t do this right

I knew he was right, of course. Vampires are dangerous even to a fully

clean at that point in time, and I dreaded getting blood on them, even
if indirectly.

I nodded. “You’re right, you’re right,” I relented. “I’m going to
bounce, then; I’m not breathing the air this thing touches. Share a
cab?”

“Nah,” he replied. “Gonna hang a little more. Nos vemos.”

We shared a formal handshake and I left the club. I wasn’t going home,

To prepare an ambush, for example.

I threw the door open and was welcomed by a great hall. I was currently
in a doorway hiding beside giant stairs that led to a second story. Once
more, sheer luck had saved my life: I could see double doors that seemed
to lead outside. I didn’t have time to appreciate the majestic
chandelier that acted as a false sun in this windowless mansion.

I sprinted through the hallway, Jeeves’ slow steps echoing closer and
closer. 

As I burst through the exit and out into the sunlight, I heard a loud
snarl behind me.

“FUCK YOU! DIE!” yelled Jeeves, in a surprisingly loud voice.

I was pushed down the stone steps at the entrance of the mansion by the
cold steel that bit into my back with unnatural force. I had the
foresight to twist my body so I stumbled down the steps on my side
rather than my face. This surely saved my life, but the same can’t be
said about the couple of bones I broke on the way down.

I must have stayed there, broken on the stone pathway for a solid
minute, trying to force myself back up. 

Jeeves never came to get me. 

When I finally pulled myself back to my feet, I understood why: Jeeves
looked at me from the comfort of the main hall, his face twisted by
anger. His outstretched arm was cut clean off by the sunlight radiating
into the mansion from the open doors.

Apparently, Jeeves had done such a good job that his Mistress had seen
fit to reward him with eternal servitude as a full-fledged vampire. How
sweet. Now this isn’t a shocking twist, all things considered, but it
was one I was delighted to learn at the time.

After throwing him the bird, I walked away as fast as my battered body
allowed me, before he got the sense to fetch staff that weren’t as
cursed as him to get me. Even though I was still technically stuck in a
vampire’s estate, I couldn’t help but admire the grandiose hedge maze
that took up a good part of the mansion’s gigantic front yard. I walked
down the concrete walkway, reached the outer black iron fence, painfully
climbed it, and I was free.

By the time I got to a payphone and called up Vegas, the Mistress and
Jeeves had already cleared out, using a secret tunnel that led all the
way back into the city’s subway system. They had left behind a
collection of dead thralls.

This had been a very intricate setup and, as I would eventually learn,
the Mistress was particularly mad that I had ruined the haven she had
spent so much of her unlife building up to her refined tastes.

She had lost a majestic library filled to the brim with medical works
dating all the way back to the Renaissance. Most of the titles were
written in French or German, something that meant little to us at the
time, but was actually a big clue about the Mistress’ vocation and
allegiance before her unlife began. I won’t spoil it, but know that she
was a monster long before she grew fangs.

After this case, I took two months off of work. It wasn’t nearly enough
to fully recover, but I never could stand time alone with my own
thoughts.
