I practically threw myself into the bean bag chair in Mike’s room. I
remembered the room being a lot bigger. The bean bag too. Mike shuffled
in behind me without much enthusiasm, scratching at his stubbled chin
before dropping into the gaming chair at his desk.

“Feels weird being back here again.” I smiled at him.

“Yeah.” Mike sighed.

I kept smiling anyway. I’d left this sleepy little town eight months
ago, the town where I grew up. I moved to St. Louis for college. It was
a completely different world, and I loved every second of it. Finally,
no more of the same boring streets. No more of the same people.

Mike, though, had stayed behind.

He was my best friend. We’d been getting into trouble together since
elementary school. We’d even come up with our own signature.

MM. Mike and Matt.

Let’s just say there was a little too much MM graffiti around town.
Though according to Mike, there still wasn’t enough.

It felt strange being here again. Strange feeling like a kid again.
Sure, I’d only turned nineteen recently, but everything felt different
now. Less carefree.

What bothered me a lot more, though, was Mike’s family. I understood why
Mike was mad at me. The moment I told him I was leaving for college, he
got angry. He said I was abandoning him.

What I didn’t understand was why his family was acting so distant. When
Mike invited me over, I figured Paul and Cindy would be happy to see me.
They’d known me for years. But when I arrived, the house felt empty.
Completely empty.

There wasn’t a soul in sight.

Mike had immediately ushered me upstairs to his room, so I never even
got the chance to wait and see if anyone would come out to say hello.

“Want some pizza?” Mike asked flatly.

The whole thing felt like two strangers trying to make conversation.

And honestly, I felt like shit.

The pizza was already on its way. We didn’t spend much time deciding
what to order. Double pepperoni from our favorite place. We both liked
the exact same thing.

Still did.

“Mike…” I tried to keep the conversation going. “I’m glad we ran into
each other today. I… haven’t seen you in a really long time. And, you
know… I’m sorry.”

“Sorry for what?” Mike asked, looking at me curiously.

“You know.” I shrugged. “All of it.”

“You don’t need to be sorry, Matt.” Mike said without much emotion. “It
happened. I got over it.”

I nodded.

I could tell something was still bothering him, but I didn’t want to
push it. If he said he was over it, then fine.

“So where are your parents?” I asked, trying to change the subject.

“I dunno.” he answered immediately, sounding almost half asleep.
“Probably in their room.”

“Oh.” I said awkwardly.

The room fell silent again. Neither of us knew what to say.

After all these years of friendship, I couldn’t believe Mike and I
suddenly couldn’t hold a conversation anymore.

“Want some weed?” Mike asked, giving me a tired look.

“Jesus, Mike…” I sat up in the bean bag. “Are you high right now?”

Mike slowly nodded and attempted something that was supposed to resemble
a blink.

A huge weight lifted off my chest. Mike wasn’t angry at me.

Maybe we hadn’t drifted apart as much as I thought.

He was just completely stoned.

I was sprawled out in the bean bag chair, taking hits and staring at the
ceiling. Mike was lying on his bed, and for the first time all night, we
were actually talking. Almost like we used to.

We traded old stories and memories.

Like the time we hid inside the Halloween festival maze and scared what
felt like half the town until I threw up from eating too much candy. Let
me tell you, a haunted house maze smells a lot worse when somebody pukes
inside it.

Or the time we slipped cat shit into Mrs. Jackson’s desk drawer. God, I
hated that woman.

She was a real old witch, and unfortunately for us, she also taught
math.

Mike and I had always been like brothers.

“Huh…” I muttered between coughs. “This stuff is strong, Mike.”

“Yeah.” Mike said lazily. “It is.”

“How long have you been doing this?” I sat up in the bean bag. “Is this
all you do now? Smoke weed?”

Mike propped himself up on one elbow and blinked at me like his brain
was running on low battery.

“I work too.” he finally said, sounding offended.

I laughed. Mike laughed too.

I knew he worked at the convenience store in town stocking shelves, but
I also knew he barely did anything else. He smoked, hung out, and
drifted through life.

“Jesus, Mike…” I stretched and yawned. “You’ve got it made. You do
whatever the hell you want. What do your parents think about it?”

Mike’s expression darkened and he dropped back onto the bed.

“They’re not exactly thrilled.” he admitted after a long pause. “But
lately they’ve had… other things to worry about.”

The moment he said that, somebody knocked on the bedroom door.

“Shit, Mike, the weed.” I whispered.

“It’s fine.” Mike waved me off as he got up from the bed. “They know I
do it.”

I stared at him.

Back when we were still in school, Cindy and Paul used to fight with
Mike about this stuff all the time. It felt weird that they suddenly
didn’t seem to care anymore.

Then again, what are you supposed to do with a nineteen-year-old?

Maybe they were just happy it was only weed and not something worse.
Mike shuffled over to the door and cracked it open.

“Hey, your pizza’s here.” Paul said nervously. Then he noticed me. “Oh.
Matt. Didn’t know you were here. Been a while.”

“Hey, Paul.” I greeted him with a smile.

Paul barely acknowledged me.

Which was strange.

When we graduated high school, he was the one buying us alcohol. Hell,
that was around the time he told me to stop calling him Mr. Thompson and
just call him Paul because I was a man now.

But tonight he looked different. 

Agitated. Distracted.

Like he was in a hurry to be somewhere else.

“Did you really have to do this tonight?” Paul muttered to Mike,
probably thinking I couldn’t hear him.

“Come on, Dad.” Mike said, taking the pizza from him.

“Mike.” Paul hissed quietly. “Please… just stay in your room tonight,
okay?”

Mike didn’t answer. He just stared into his father’s eyes.

Then he slowly closed the door.

“Listen, Mike, if it’s a bad time, I can come back another day.” I
offered.

“No.” Mike replied coldly. “Stay.”

I felt uncomfortable.

Paul had always welcomed me into this house. Once he even told me I was
practically his second son.

Besides Mike, they also had a pair of twin girls. Mike’s little sisters.

I think they had just turned four earlier that year.

I felt uncomfortable.

Not because of the weed. Not because Paul clearly didn’t seem happy to
see me tonight.

It was because Mike hadn’t said a word about any of it.

He sat back down at his desk, flipped open the pizza box, and started
eating like everything was completely normal.

“Mike, you sure you want me to stay?” I asked again. “I’ve got some
stuff to do with my dad tomorrow in town anyway… maybe I should just
head home.”

“No!” Mike snapped. Then he immediately softened his voice. “No… don’t.
We haven’t seen each other in forever. Let’s hang out a little longer.”

He looked like someone putting off bad news.

The last time I’d seen him act like this was when he had to tell his
parents he’d failed three classes.

“You sure everything’s okay?” I pressed. “How are your sisters doing?”

“They’re fine. They’re doing fine.” Mike said quickly. “But come on,
tell me something about you… any girls?”

I smiled. I could tell something was bothering him and he didn’t want to
talk about it.

I figured maybe this was all because we’d barely spoken in almost a
year. Maybe he still felt like I’d abandoned him.

Whatever was going on, though, I couldn’t figure it out.

“There are girls.” I admitted awkwardly. “And parties. But as a
freshman, nobody really invites you anywhere yet.”

“That’s not a problem.” Mike said, suddenly sounding more like himself.
“Give it time. You’ll get invited everywhere.” He grinned. “Just make
sure you invite me too.”

“You got it, man.” I grinned back at my old friend. “Now pass me that
pizza before I starve to death.”

What I hadn’t accounted for was gluten.

My newest enemy.

I’d found out right before leaving for college that my stomach probably
couldn’t handle it anymore, and pizza had turned out to be a pretty
terrible choice.

My stomach churned. The haze from the weed and the comfort of being full
disappeared fast, replaced by an increasingly uncomfortable cramping
sensation.

“You good?” Mike asked around a mouthful of pizza crust.

“Yeah…” I groaned. My stomach let out a loud growl. “Mike, man. I gotta
use the bathroom.”

Mike didn’t answer.

I just noticed him glancing nervously between me and the pizza box.
Almost like he didn’t want me leaving the room.

Or like he’d somehow forgotten where the bathroom was.

“Mike, seriously. I have to go.”

I pushed myself out of the bean bag.

“Okay… okay…” Mike muttered through a full mouth. “Just hurry back. My
dad’s got… something important going on.”

I didn’t feel like arguing about the weirdness of the situation.

What the hell was I supposed to do? If you’re seconds away from shitting
yourself, you’re going to the bathroom.

Mike just kept chewing his last slice while staring blankly at the
floor. The amount of weed he’d smoked had definitely done something to
his brain.

I waddled out of the room like a penguin.

The house was quiet. Dim. Sure, it was already around ten at night, but
still.

The place felt strangely empty. I slipped quietly out of Mike’s room.

I didn’t want to get either of us in trouble after Paul had specifically
asked us to stay put. I figured I knew why. Not just because Mike’s
little sisters were probably asleep, but because Paul did a lot of
business with clients in Europe. Because of the time difference, he
often had meetings late at night.

Still, I didn’t care if he was talking to the President.

I needed a bathroom.

Clenching my teeth and holding my protesting stomach, I made my way
carefully down the hallway. I knew exactly where the upstairs bathroom
was.

I’d been here a thousand times before.

But no matter how quietly I tried to move, desperation has a way of
speeding you up. I picked up my pace.

As I passed Paul’s office, I noticed the door was open.

I expected him to step out and scold me like some little kid sneaking
around after bedtime.

Or at least glare at me as I slipped past.

Instead, the room was completely dark. I only stopped for a second.

God, I wish I hadn’t.

I knew there was a massive leather chair in that office. I’d sat in it
plenty of times when I was younger.

But someone else was sitting there now. I only caught the silhouette for
a moment in the darkness, but it was enough to know it wasn’t Paul.

The figure was too tall. Too thin. Actually, it was taller than anyone
I’d ever seen in my life.

And its eyes…

Its eyes seemed to catch the light. For a second I thought maybe it was
wearing glasses.

Or maybe it wasn’t. Maybe its eyes were reflecting the darkness the same
way a cat’s do at night.

The figure didn’t move. Neither did I.

I decided it was probably best to keep walking toward the bathroom.

I ended up spending a little longer in the bathroom than I intended.

Not just because my stomach was trying to kill me, but because I
couldn’t stop thinking about the tall figure sitting in Paul’s office.

Who the hell was that? A coworker? Was that why Paul didn’t want us
leaving Mike’s room?

By the time I finished, at least twenty minutes had passed. I hoped
nobody had noticed me wandering around, but there wasn’t much I could do
about it.

I carefully opened the bathroom door, trying not to make a sound. Then I
slowly pulled it shut behind me.

The moment the latch clicked, Paul’s office door opened.

Paul stepped out.

He was rubbing a hand through his short hair, pacing nervously. He
looked like he’d just gotten terrible news. Or maybe some important deal
had fallen apart. Whatever it was, he looked shaken.

Then he saw me. The color immediately drained from his face.

“What are you doing out here?” he whispered. There was genuine fear in
his voice.

“I just…” I stammered. “I had to use the bathroom.”

“Shhh.” He immediately pressed a finger to his lips. “Quieter. Shhh.
Come here.”

I had no idea what to make of his behavior.

I’d seen Paul stressed before. I’d seen him angry.

I’d even seen him so exhausted that Mike and I practically tore the
house apart without waking him up.

But this was different. It was like every emotion had hit him at once.

“Sorry…” I said quietly as I walked over. “I really had to go.”

“Okay. Okay. Just keep it down.” Paul whispered. “Go back to Mike’s
room. Right now.”

He gently guided me down the hallway like some kind of security guard
escorting a lost kid.

As we passed, I noticed the office door was closed now. Paul seemed
desperate to get me away from it.

Like I was some escaped pet that needed to be put back where it
belonged. A few moments later, I was standing outside Mike’s bedroom
door.

Paul immediately hurried back toward the office.

I didn’t go inside. For some reason, I felt like I needed to see why he
was in such a rush.

The office door clicked.

I leaned slightly, trying to see who was coming out. But Paul stepped
directly into the doorway, blocking my view.

“No, of course not…” Paul said. His voice was trembling now. “No one… it
was just me…”

I couldn’t see anybody. Couldn’t hear anybody either. Yet Paul was
clearly talking to someone.

And he sounded terrified.

Suddenly Mike’s bedroom door flew open.

My old friend grabbed my wrist hard enough to hurt and yanked me inside.

“Where the hell have you been?” Mike grabbed my shoulders the second I
was inside.

“I was in the bathroom, Mike. Jesus, relax.” I tried to pull away from
him.

“Did you see him?” Mike leaned in so close it made me uncomfortable.
“Did you see my brother?”

“What?” I stared at him.

I had absolutely no idea what he was talking about.

I’d known Mike and his family since we were kids. Our parents had been
friends for years.

Nobody had ever mentioned a brother. Mike didn’t have a brother.

“Out there!” Mike hissed, almost shouting despite trying to whisper. “Is
he out there? Did he see you? Did you see him? Tell me!”

“No…” I answered, thoroughly confused. “I don’t know. What the hell are
you talking about? Mike, you don’t have a brother. What is all this?”

Mike let go of me.

Then he started pacing. Back and forth. Rubbing his face.

Walking circles around the room like a man on the verge of a breakdown.

He looked terrified. Completely lost in whatever was happening inside
his head.

I was starting to get scared of him.

What the hell was wrong with everybody tonight?

Slowly, I took a step backward toward the bean bag chair. I had no idea
what was happening.

No idea what I was supposed to do.I just stood there awkwardly, feeling
completely out of place.

Then someone knocked.

At least, I think it was a knock.

It didn’t sound like the one Paul had used earlier.

Three heavy thuds rattled Mike’s bedroom door.

Slow. Deliberate. Separated by long pauses. Like some kind of coded
signal. 

Mike froze.

He was standing only a few feet from the door. I watched his eyes widen.
I watched him swallow. His breathing became quicker. His hands trembled
slightly.

Pure panic spread across his face.

And despite having no clue who could possibly be outside that door…

I suddenly felt afraid too.

Mike didn’t move.

He stood in front of the door like a statue. I could see his fingers
twitching nervously, but otherwise he stayed perfectly still.

Whoever was on the other side of the door seemed content to wait.

I couldn’t hear footsteps. Couldn’t hear anyone speaking.

Nobody called out for Mike to open the door.

Was it really his brother out there? His brother that didn’t exist? Then
who the hell was it?

The knocking came again. This time louder. More deliberate. More
rhythmic.

Mike squeezed his eyes shut.

His face twisted, looking dangerously close to tears.

“Shit…” he whispered, his voice cracking.

I wanted to say something.

Anything. But I couldn’t make myself speak.

I just stood there watching him. Watching him fall apart.

“Please stay quiet.” Mike said, looking at me with pure terror in his
eyes. “Don’t move.”

Slowly, carefully, he reached for the doorknob. 

For a moment he only held it. As if he were thinking about something.

Something he wasn’t supposed to do.

Then he turned it.

The door opened just a crack.

My heart skipped a beat.

I had no idea what to expect. What was about to walk through that door.
Or who.

Paul’s fear. Mike’s fear.

By now it had become mine too, even if I still had no idea what I was
supposed to be afraid of.

Mike remained standing in the doorway. Looking through the narrow
opening.

Upward. Far upward.

So high that for a second I thought he was staring at the ceiling in the
hallway.

“No. Not now.” Mike said suddenly.

I hadn’t heard anyone speak.

Not a single word. It was like Mike was answering the darkness itself.

“Please…” he said again to the empty hallway. “Nobody. I said not now.”

I stood there frozen.

I couldn’t see whether someone was standing outside the door because
Mike was blocking my view.

But watching him stare upward and carry on a conversation with someone I
couldn’t hear was terrifying.

“No…” Mike lowered his head shamefully. “I know. Yeah, but…”

Silence.

His words died in his throat. He looked like a little kid getting
scolded by a parent.

Head lowered. Body trembling. Eyes fixed on the floor.

“I’m sorry.” he finally whispered after swallowing hard. “A friend.”

Another long pause followed.

I had backed myself all the way into the farthest corner of the room by
then.

The entire situation was absurd. But I didn’t dare make a sound.

“Okay.” Mike finally said, relief creeping into his voice. “Soon. I
promise.”

He nodded.

As if agreeing to something. Then slowly, very slowly, he closed the
door.

The lock clicked.

Mike immediately let out a massive breath.

His face had gone completely pale. Like he’d just survived a near-fatal
car crash.

“What the fuck was that?!” I snapped, equal parts terrified and angry.

Mike swallowed hard.

Then he stepped closer to me. I had nowhere left to back away.

“My brother.” he said in a trembling voice.

“Mike, are you fucking serious?” I snapped. “You don’t have a brother,
asshole! What the hell are you talking about? What the fuck is going on
here? Jesus Christ…”

“Shhh.” Mike lunged toward me. “Not so loud. He’ll hear you.”

As soon as he reached me, he tried to cover my mouth with his hand.

I shoved him away.

“Get the fuck off me!” I snapped and pushed him hard.

Mike stumbled backward and crashed onto the floor.

He glared up at me.

“Mike, what the fuck is all this?” I demanded. “Who were you talking
to?”

“I told you.” Mike sat down heavily on the edge of his bed. “My brother.
He’s been living with us for a while.”

“No.” I shook my head immediately. “No. I know you, Mike. I know your
family. You don’t have a fucking brother.”

“But… we do.” Mike muttered quietly. “We just didn’t know it. None of us
did.” He lowered his eyes. “But he was always here.” His voice dropped
even further. “He was always here.”

I didn’t know what to say.

I just stared at him.

What the hell was he talking about? Who was this brother supposed to be?
Had the entire family lost their minds?

“Mike…” I finally managed. “What is wrong with you people? Who is your
brother?”

“He lives here.” Mike’s voice was barely a whisper. “He’s here with us…
all the time.”

I swallowed.

Whatever Mike was talking about, whoever, or whatever, he meant, one
thing was obvious.

He was terrified of it. Just like Paul.

Then a voice exploded from somewhere downstairs.

“THAT’S NOT WHAT YOU SAID!”

I nearly jumped out of my skin.

Mike froze.

Sitting on the edge of his bed, he began trembling like a terrified dog.

“THAT’S NOT THE DEAL WE MADE!”

The voice sounded familiar. At first I couldn’t place it.

Then I realized it was Paul. He was yelling at someone. Or something.

“No!” Paul shouted again. His voice was growing more desperate with
every word. “I SAID NO!”

Mike slowly slid off the edge of the bed and onto the floor.

He curled into himself, shaking violently. Like a psychiatric patient
having a breakdown.

“Not them!” Paul screamed. “Please… enough. You said it wouldn’t be like
this!”

The worst part was that nobody answered him. At least nobody I could
hear.

It sounded like he was arguing with empty air.

“Please!” Paul practically shrieked. “Stop! WAIT! YOU CAN’T DO THIS!”

Mike pulled himself into a fetal position. His hands clamped over his
ears.

And then he started repeating the same words over and over.

“No. No. No.” His voice cracked. “It’s happening again. No. No. No.”

And me?

Every instinct I had was screaming one thing.

Run.

So I did.

I jumped onto Mike’s bed. One leap took me to the wall.

Another to the window. The latch popped open.

I threw myself through it without thinking. I didn’t care that we were
on the second floor.

I didn’t care how far the drop was. I just wanted to get away from that
house.

Away from whatever was inside it. 

Leaving my friend behind.

I ran all the way home.

I’m not proud of it, but what else was I supposed to do?

The entire family had either lost their minds or… There really was
something in that house.

Something that terrified them that much. And whatever it was, I wasn’t
sticking around long enough to find out if it wanted me too.

Was it cowardly to run?

Maybe.

But I was terrified.

The second I got home, I ran straight to my room, threw myself onto my
bed, and pulled the blankets over my head like a frightened child.

I was genuinely scared for my life. I didn’t even know what I was afraid
of.

I didn’t know what was inside Mike’s house. But the panic was real.

And sprinting through the dark streets of town had only made it worse.
The next thing I knew, it was morning. I crawled out of bed drenched in
sweat.

For a moment it all felt like a nightmare. But I knew it hadn’t been.

I remembered the look on Mike’s face. I remembered Paul screaming into
the darkness.

I spent the entire day at home.

No matter how many times my parents asked what was wrong, I couldn’t
tell them.  I didn’t want to.I was terrified of whatever that thing was.

Part of me felt that if I didn’t talk about it, it couldn’t hurt me. But
as the hours passed, guilt started eating at me. I’d left Mike behind.

I couldn’t stop thinking about it. Still, there was no way I was going
back there alone.

So I came up with the next best thing.

I walked back to Mike’s street.

I stood there from a distance, watching the house like some burglar
planning a break-in.

Then I called the police.

I don’t even know what I expected them to find.

But whatever was inside that house, somebody else needed to see it.

Somebody else needed to know. The dispatcher was polite.

She said she’d send a patrol car.

So I waited. And waited.

Eventually a cruiser finally pulled up. I was almost relieved when I saw
it.

I jogged over as the officers stopped in front of Mike’s house.

“Afternoon, kid.” one of them said through the open window. He was
young, with a scruffy beard. “You the one who called us?”

“Yes, sir.” I said quickly. “Can you please check on the family inside?
Something really weird is going on in there. There was yelling and—”

The two officers exchanged a look. The one in the driver’s seat, wearing
sunglasses, just shrugged.

The bearded officer looked back at me.

“Son… this house?”

“Yes.” I nodded. “My friend lives here with his family. Something’s
seriously wrong.”

“He lives here?” the officer asked. His expression changed. “Son,
nobody’s lived here for decades.”

I stared at him.

“What?”

“You’re not messing with us, are you?”

“No.” I said immediately. “Look.”

I pulled out my phone. I remembered the picture.

Last year. Prom night.

We’d taken it right in front of this house before leaving.

One last picture together. Me and Mike. I opened my photos.

And it wasn’t there.

Not that picture. Not any picture.

I searched again. And again.

My hands started shaking. Mike wasn’t there.

There wasn’t a single photo of him on my phone.

Not one. Cold sweat ran down my back.

It was as if he’d never existed at all. Slowly, I looked up at the house
where Mike supposedly lived.

And there, standing in the upstairs window, was a tall black silhouette.

Waiting. Watching me.

With those strange, pale eyes glinting in the darkness.
