Hello,

I’m a nurse on an adolescent psychiatric unit, and I work nights. I’m
not going to say where. I’ve been there eleven years, and I’ve never
written anything like this down before, let alone put it somewhere
strangers can read it, so if this is badly written, that’s why.

Lately, we’ve had a run of kids come in catatonic. The parents drive
them up to the doors, and some of them have to carry them in. The kids
are awake, and their eyes follow you around the room, but they don’t
move, and they don’t respond to anything, and the whole time they’re
mumbling under their breath.

They barely blink. Not never, but so little that you start to notice it,
and once you notice it, you can’t stop counting. I watched a boy go a
minute and forty seconds without one and then do three fast in a row,
catching up, like he’d remembered he was supposed to.

Six of them came in on a Tuesday.

Everyone’s first question is whether they knew each other. They did not.
Four different schools, two of them living over forty minutes apart,
different family doctors, different referral routes, three through emerg
and three straight to us. We pulled every piece of information we could
to connect them. I looked for two days because I wanted a connection to
be there, but there was nothing linking any of them. 

Around eleven that first night, one of them started up. Not mumbling
anymore. He was saying something about a computer, I couldn’t get all of
it, and then he was screaming, “Get it away, get it away, get it away,”
and by the time I was through the door, he was already up, and he ran
headfirst into the wall. He opened his head on the first one. He didn’t
stop, he didn’t put his hands up, he didn’t slow down, he went back at
the wall still screaming, ” Get it away,” with the blood running down
into his mouth. We got him down and held him, and he was still trying to
slam his head backwards into the floor while three of us had him.

There was too much blood. Far more than a wound like that should give
you, and every one of us in that room knew it at the time, and none of
us have talked about it since like something had been turned up. You
could see the exact spot where his head went in, right there in the
padding, the shape of it, and the spatter going out from it in lines
like something thrown, and once we had him down and he’d stopped and the
room went quiet, you could hear it dripping.

About ninety seconds later, the room next door started, and then the one
across from it, and within a minute, all of them were going. Same words.
“Get it away.” And that sound of it, thud and then thud and then thud,
out of four rooms at once down a hallway. We called code white on
multiple rooms at the same time. I have never done that. Hell, I didn’t
even know you could.

The sixth room was silent. You always check on the quiet one first,
that’s the job, so that’s where I went.

He had turned himself round on the bed to face the door. Sitting up,
hands flat on his knees, watching me come in. Four rooms of screaming
and thudding were going on behind me in the corridor, and this boy sat
there like he’d been waiting on me all night. And he was still mumbling,
straight at me, with his eyes on my eyes, and he did not stop.

I stood in that doorway and said his name and got nothing, and said it
again and got nothing, and then somebody screamed for me out of room
three, and I turned my back on him and left.

We sedated all six. We phoned six sets of parents at two in the morning
and told them their children were safe, which was the word we used. Then
I sat in the break room with my coffee going cold, and I did not have a
single idea in my head about what I had just watched.

 The sixth one was the only one still going. The other five were under
and quiet, and he was lying there in the dark, carrying on with it
exactly the way he had been all night, and he was the only one of them
I’d been able to stand in front of and get anything out of at all. So if
any of them were going to tell me what this was, it was him.

I took a handheld recorder out of the equipment cupboard, went back down
to room six, set it on the shelf by the door and left it running the
rest of the shift. I have never done anything like that in my life, and
I couldn’t have told you then and can’t tell you now what I thought I
was going to do with it.

I listened to it in the office at the end of the corridor with the door
shut. It was nothing; it was a boy breathing. So I slowed it down, and
it was still nothing, so I brought the pitch up and slowed it down
again, and somewhere in the middle of all that, I finally heard it. “Get
it away.” The same words the others were screaming, except underneath,
and slower, and pitched down so far that it doesn’t come out of a human
throat as anything you’d call a voice.

They’d been saying it since the day they came in. All of them, every
hour of every shift, into the pillow and into the dark at a speed you
can’t hear without slowing down a recording, while we walked past them
with charts. On Tuesday night, they didn’t start saying something new;
they just got loud.

I went in to check on him the next morning. He was in bed, on his back,
mumbling the way he always was, and I was standing over him when I heard
something above me, like somebody dragging a fingernail along a surface,
slow, right over my head.

I looked up, and there was a face in the ceiling of room six.

It’s scratched into the tile, thin white lines in the paint, and it’s
about four feet across. The eyes are two short marks, and there’s
nothing in them, no pupils, nothing, just the marks. Its mouth didn’t
stop where a mouth would stop. It went up past the cheeks and kept going
out to where the ears would be, then past them, past the edges of the
face entirely, so that the smile was wider than the head it was on. The
mouth was also open. Whoever put it there took the time to draw every
tooth, dozens of them, all exactly the same size, going right back into
the corners where a mouth doesn’t have corners.

It’s nine feet up. There’s nothing in that room to stand on and nothing
to climb, and that boy has been sedated or restrained or watched since
the minute he arrived. There is no version of this where somebody put
that there.

I looked back down, and he was standing in front of me. Out of the bed,
on his feet, close enough that I could feel him breathing on me, and I
never heard him move, and the frame never made a sound. I went backwards
so fast I hit the wall, and my legs went out from under me, and I ended
up on the floor looking up at him.

He didn’t help me, and he didn’t come any closer. He stood over me the
whole time I was getting myself up, with his head tilted down to keep me
in his eyes, still mumbling, and when I was on my feet, he was still
doing it.

I got out of that room and stood in the corridor for a long while, and
then I decided I needed help with this, and I went out to the car.

I felt something was wrong. It was quiet, and I’ve parked in that garage
for eleven years, and it is never quiet. There’s the ventilation,
there’s the doors on the level above, there’s traffic off the road
outside, there’s always something. That morning, there was nothing at
all, not one sound.

I got about twenty feet from my car, and I heard a bang behind me.
Metal, hard, like somebody had put their whole weight into the side of a
car. Then another one, closer. And then footsteps, running, coming up
fast behind me on the concrete.

I ran to my car, opened the door and locked it. I looked in the rearview
mirror and saw a face. The same face that was on the ceiling. A wide
smile, sharp teeth, vacant eyes. Once it moved towards the car, I
started it and drove out as fast as I could.

I was panicking. I called my friend from nursing school, who has always
been into this kind of thing. Ghost stuff, missing persons, all the
podcasts about it. I was screaming at her about what I’d just seen, and
she let me get all of it out, and then she told me to come to her.

I’m writing this from her spare room. I called in sick for the first
time in four years, and I haven’t been back to that building since.
She’s been up half the night on her laptop, and she says she knows
somebody who might understand what this is, and we’re going to see him
tomorrow.

I need somebody to tell me what I saw. That’s the whole reason I’m
putting this here. I don’t care if you think I’m making it up, I’ve read
this back six times and I know how it sounds. I want to know if anyone
has had kids come through their unit like this, or heard people say the
same four words, or seen anything with a mouth like that.

Anything at all. Please.
