“You still read those dumbass things?”

I jumped a bit when I heard the sudden voice. I turned to see my older
brother peeking into my room with a sneer. I didn’t hear him coming.

“It’s not reading,” I said, “there are no words.” I looked back down at
the page. 

“Those are for kids.”

“Screw you. They’re fun.” I had already made it through 5 Waldo books,
so I figured I could do all the rest. Something about the colors and
details of the setting were so interesting to me. I would judge the
outfits of the random pedestrians and imagine what their lives were
like. Sometimes I even forgot to look for Waldo. “You know, my friend
Thomas from England told me it’s called Where’s Wally over there.”

My brother frowned. “What did I tell you about talking to British
people?”

“I’m sorry. I won’t do it again.”

My brother walked over and looked down at the page. “You know, I saw a
weird one of those in the library once.” He sounded serious all of a
sudden. I looked up to see his expression had hardened.

“What was weird about it?”

“Well, it was like a year ago, so I don’t remember that well. But I
think it had no title, and there was a creepy looking guy. Mr. Carpenter
saw me with it and got really mad. He took it away from me. I couldn’t
find it in the library after that.”

“You’re lying,” I said. My brother was always pulling pranks on me and
trying to mess with my head. Although the image of Mr. Carpenter in my
mind did make me feel some doubt. Everyone at school knew to avoid him
and his library. There was something off about that man.

“Whatever, loser. I’m gonna go play Xbox.”

For the next couple weeks, I forgot all about that conversation and
continued to find and complete every Waldo book I could. When I finished
the final one, I felt an unexpected loss. It was like I was saying
goodbye to an old friend. As stupid as it sounds, I almost teared up.

I was walking down the school hallway the next day when I came across
the library. I suddenly remembered the conversation with my brother. I
looked around to check if anyone was nearby, but the hall was empty. It
was early in the day, and the only other students were in the cafeteria
for breakfast.

I peeked my head into the library and immediately heard a soft rumbling
sound. I jerked my neck to the sound and saw Mr. Carpenter asleep at the
front desk. He was snoring.

My heart started racing as I entered the room slowly. I had to know if
my brother was telling the truth. Pretty quickly, I found the section
where the Waldo books were kept. I only found ones that I had already
finished. Then I looked back over to Mr. Carpenter and the open door
behind him. His office.

I slowly crept behind his desk and into his office, keeping an eye on
him as I went. Inside, I turned to see stacks of loose papers and books.
I studied the spines of the books, but they were all normal kid’s
books. 

Then I noticed one of his drawers was slightly open. I pulled it out.
Inside was a book with a blank, black cover. I picked it up. It was
chilling to the touch.

Mr. Carpenter stopped snoring. 

I jerked my head to the side to see him shifting. I froze.

After a moment, he started snoring again. I breathed in relief.

I kept the book at my side and slowly made my way back to the door. When
I was halfway out the door, I heard Mr. Carpenter’s chair creak.

“Hey you! Stop!” he screeched.

I ran down the hallway at full speed and didn’t look back. I prayed that
he didn’t recognize me. 

I was tense for the rest of the day. I couldn’t focus on any of my
classes. I just kept imagining Mr. Carpenter charging into the classroom
and coming straight for my throat or being called to the principal’s
office. But it never happened. Eventually, I was back home in my room. 

I pulled the black book out of my backpack. I opened it up to the first
page to see in large red letters, “FIND ME.”

The next page was normal looking. A colorful town square with hundreds
of people marching in a parade. It looked exactly like our Christmas
parade. I laughed to myself. There was nothing creepy about this.

Then I started to notice. It was little things. Some of the people had
unusual proportions. They were too wide, or too tall and skinny. 

The strangest thing was that I couldn’t find the main character at all.
I went over the page a hundred times but never saw anyone that obviously
stood out. All I did find was a wanted poster on one of the brick walls.
An image of a man wearing a prison jumpsuit with black and white
stripes. But the poster was folding over at the top and covering the
man’s face. Beside the poster was a dark hand reaching around the corner
of a shady alleyway.

Still, I could only laugh. This was my brother’s idea of scary? And he
always made fun of me for being a wuss. I turned the page.

This time, it was a concert with a massive stage and crowd. My eyes
immediately fell on the man in the prison jumpsuit at the center of the
page. His black and white outfit stood out among the otherwise colorful
concertgoers. The other people near him were looking at him with odd
expressions and keeping a bit of distance despite the little room there
was. Weirdest of all was that he was facing the opposite direction, so I
could only see the back of his head.

The next page was a crowded mall, but there were less people than
before. I very quickly found the man because there was another large gap
around him and the others on the page. The ones near him had fearful
expressions and seemed to be hurrying away from him. He was still facing
the other way, but his head was turned ever so slightly toward the page,
showing an inch of his pale face.

Now I was starting to feel uneasy.

The next page almost made me drop the book. I immediately spotted the
man at the center of a beach. He was turned perfectly sideways,
revealing a half of a twisted smile. But what really horrified me was
his surroundings. 

They were wounded and bloody bodies all around him. Some seemed to be
clinging to life and crawling away. At the edges of the pages, I could
see police charging in.

“What the hell?” I said out loud. I had never seen anything this
graphic. The hairs on my arms stood on end, and my hand trembled as I
turned to the next page. This time, I did drop the book in shock.

The man was all alone in a purely white background, standing far too
close to the page. His eyes were wide with glee. I yelped as the book
slipped out of my hands.

After a minute of deep breaths, I found myself picking the book back up.
I turned to the next page to find it completely blank. The rest of the
pages were like that.

I rushed to the game room to find my brother.

“Fucking campers!” he yelled into his headset.

“Nathan! I need your help!” 

“What do you want?” he asked without looking at me.

“Can you come with me to the library tomorrow? I really need to return a
book.”

“Do it yourself, dipshit.”

“Please!” I begged. He finally turned to meet my eyes.

“We can’t anyway. Didn’t you hear? Mr. Carpenter has gone missing. He
wasn’t at school today.”

“What?!” I asked. But I saw him this morning. “Maybe he’s just sick or
something?”

“No, I heard some teachers talking about it. Apparently, his wife called
the police. She’s really worried about him.”

I wordlessly turned back and ran into my room where the book laid on the
floor. I decided to look back through it one more time. Maybe I was just
imagining things.

What I saw this time left me even more confused. The pages looked
similar to how they did the first time, but with one crucial difference.
The man was nowhere to be found.

I looked in the spots where I saw him before, and at every inch of the
page to see if he somehow moved but couldn’t find him anywhere. 

He was just gone, like he was never there.

I turned back to the very first page to find one last horrifying
surprise. The title had a little bit extra added to it:

FIND ME

BEFORE I FIND YOU
