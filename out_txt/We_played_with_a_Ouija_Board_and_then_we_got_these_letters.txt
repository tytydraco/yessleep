When I was 15, I was invited to a hotel party. If you’re not aware,
that’s when a parent rents a room at a hotel for a group of kids and you
enjoy the amenities. It might be a hood thing, but they were one of the
highlights of my childhood.

This one particular time, one of the girls at the party had a Ouija
board. I had seen the scary movies. I knew better than to play with it.
But everyone in the room was playing, and of course I didn’t want to be
left out. So, I joined the circle on the floor and placed my finger on
the planchette with everyone else’s.
D was the first to speak. “Is there anyone here with us?” she said in a
low voice, then she laughed. That’s how they did it in the movie.
We sat for a moment, and then I felt the planchette jerk. We all gasped
at the same time as it went right to YES.
We blamed each other, scared. D laughed some more. “I moved it, y’all!”
She was laughing loudly. “The looks on y’all’s faces!”
But as she laughed, the planchette moved again, and none of us were
touching it. We looked at D, and her mouth was wide open as she stared
at the board. From the corner to YES, to the corner, to YES.
I looked at her like; “There is something in here with us!
We paused. Then we sat back down and placed our fingers on the
planchette again. That’s when K asked, “What is your name?”
The planchette slid slowly across the board. F-R-I-T-A.
We looked at each other, and then Keisha asked, “Did you die here?”
The planchette went to YES.
D said, “Oh naw. Did somebody murder you?”
The planchette went right to YES again.
Then there was a heavy knock at the door. We all screamed, of course. It
was unexpected. We gathered behind the door, and D slowly opened it with
the rest of us huddled behind her. It was the man from the front
counter, bringing the extra blankets we’d requested.
We laughed about how scary we’d been. But when we went back to the room,
we couldn’t find the planchette anywhere. We flipped the mattress. And
side note, if you ever want to sleep comfortably in a hotel room, I
don’t suggest you do that before you sleep there.
We sat up the rest of the night gossiping until we checked out the next
day. We never really talked about that night, until we all got these
letters. About a month after the party, stuffed in the vents of our
lockers. Each chain letter said the same thing.
Dear (our name),
You opened a door that you should have left closed. You didn’t close our
session, and now you have to help me gain power. Copy the following
letter body and follow the directions to avoid further consequences.
Dearest Friend,
I hope you have been enjoying your day when this letter finds you. You
are now a link in a chain. I am Frita. If you write this letter and send
it to 7 friends in 7 days, your life will continue going as it is
planned to go. But if you do NOT, you will have bad luck starting in
seven days for the rest of your life. You cannot give this letter to
anyone who has already received it. You should not ignore your link in
the chain, as bad luck is waiting to follow you. Your seven days starts
now. Good luck.
We all got the letters before our lunch period. D and K thought it was a
joke, but I didn’t. I used my free time in third hour to write the
letter out word for word. I had 3 out of the 7 done, and I put the
finished ones in lockers after lunch. By the end of the school day, I
had all of them written and delivered.
D laughed it off. She swore it had to be a joke. I even felt a little
foolish for following directions, as she teased me over the phone,
saying “7 days” in the voice from The Ring.
But the next day at lunch, I knew I’d made the right choice. K, D, and M
all said they’d had nightmares about a woman named Frita, with half of
her head missing. They argued about whether her eye was in the socket or
dangling.
I sat there silently, listening, until M nudged me. “What about you? Did
you have any dreams about Frita?”
“No. I wrote the letters and passed them out yesterday. My momma told me
not to play around with chain letters.”
They laughed at how superstitious I was and changed the topic.
But later that day, in fifth hour, I heard the guy whose locker I’d
shoved a letter into talking to his friend. He was describing the same
nightmare. A woman named Frita, one eye hanging out, half her head
missing, yelling at him to send her letter! His friend laughed him off,
until he handed him a note, and when he unfolded it, of course, it was
the chain letter.
I felt a little guilty about passing the letter on, but I was grateful,
because I had already had intense nightmares that kept me up late.
This was a Friday. Social media wasn’t a thing yet, so I had no idea how
far those letters had spread until Monday, when we came back to school.
Almost every conversation on the bus mentioned Frita. Everyone on my bus
had gotten a letter. Everyone, in every class, had gotten a chain
letter.
Tuesday, Mary was panicked at lunch. Her nightmares had gotten worse,
and now she had no one to send the letters to, since everyone in school
already had one. We told her to write them to the teachers and the
principal. She laughed, but she was scared she’d get in trouble. K and D
admitted they’d written letters too.
Wednesday, M came to school with dark circles around her eyes. She said
every time she closed her eyes, she could see Frita, and each time,
Frita was getting closer. She said she felt like Frita was trying to
touch her.
D joked, “It’s Frita Krueger, and she’s coming for you!” But M did NOT
laugh.
M wrote the letters to the teachers and the assistant principal and
snuck them into their rooms Friday morning, before the school
announcements.
Monday, we got to school and walked to our lockers, catching up about
our weekend. We got to M’s first.
Out of the vent of her locker stuck a lined sheet of paper, like the
chain letters we’d received the week before. She pulled it out and
looked at us as we stared at her. She stared at the folded paper like it
held the results of a fatal diagnosis.
She creased it between her fingers. “Maybe I shouldn’t read this one.”
We all shook our heads and looked at one another. And then M opened her
locker.
Right on top of her books sat a planchette.
She held it up, and I almost passed out from fear. M unfolded the paper
and read the note silently. She just stood there.
“What does it say?” K asked.
M stood there a few more moments, silent, maybe from the shock. D pushed
her shoulder. “WHAT DOES IT SAY?!”
M began to read, her voice breaking as she held back tears. “I broke the
chain. I have bad luck forever.”
D laughed. “Well, friend, as my uncle would say, if it wasn’t for bad
luck, some people would have no luck at all.”
M just stood there, frozen. She looked at the note, then back at us.
“I’ll catch up with you guys later.”
None of us had a letter or a planchette in our lockers.
After that, M didn’t complain to us about the dreams of Frita anymore.
At that age, we didn’t really know what bad luck looked like, and we
honestly just wrote the whole situation off.
Until M’s 16th birthday. Her parents threw her a big party that ended
with an overnight stay in a hotel room. It was so much fun. One of her
cousins snuck us some Smirnoff wine coolers, and we were blitzed.
We got to the hotel room, and that’s when M pulled out the Ouija board.
All the laughing and small talk stopped as we looked at Mary like she
was out of her doggone mind.
“Let me explain,” she said. “We have to close the portal. We opened it
the last time we used it, so we have to close it.”
I instantly refused. M insisted. “In the book I read, it has to be the
same people in the same place.”
We all sat down and placed our fingers on the planchette. M spoke.
“Frita, are you here?”
The planchette went to NO.
We all looked at M, kind of scared, but she continued. “Where is Frita?”
The planchette scrolled across the board. R-E-L-E-A-S-E-D.
K blurted out, “What do you mean, released?”
The planchette went to NO.
We looked at each other. M asked again. “WHERE IS FRITA?”
The planchette scrolled across the letters. F-R-E-E-D.
We looked at each other again, speechless. Then the planchette scrolled
without any prompt. T-H-A-N-K Y-O-U. Then down to GOODBYE.
We all sat there in shock. Then the board flew out from between M and D
and crashed against the wall.
We never used a Ouija board together after that. And honestly, life did
go on. I don’t know if M had bad luck or if she was just dealt a bad
hand in life, but things always seemed to go wrong for her. Every time
something went wrong, M would blame it on not sending the chain letters
in time.
We remain friends to this day. But the reason I had to write out this
whole experience is because of what recently happened.
M has an eighteen-year-old son. I’m his godmother. One day while we were
at lunch, he kept smiling at his phone and texting his “friend.”
Eventually I gave in and asked what her name was.
He said, “Frita.”
And without missing a beat, he went back to the conversation he was
having with her. He laughed and giggled as he sat across from me.

So now I’m wondering, was that just a coincidence? Or should I warn M
that her son is “friends” with a girl who has the same name as the
entity that cursed her so long ago…
