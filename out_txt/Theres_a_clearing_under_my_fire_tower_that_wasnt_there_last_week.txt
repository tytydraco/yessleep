This is my third season working a fire tower in the Midwest. I am not
going to say which state or which forest. That is not important, and I
do not want anyone trying to come out here.

The job is simple. I live in a small cab at the top of the tower for the
season. I watch for smoke. I check in on the radio at set times. I write
down the weather, the wind, and anything I see that looks wrong.

Most days, nothing looks wrong. That is the whole point of the job.

I noticed the quiet first.

It was not quiet up in the cab. The wind was still hitting the tower.
The metal still creaked. What went quiet was one part of the woods below
me, off to the southwest.

No birds. No bugs. Nothing moving in the trees.

At first I thought I was making a big deal out of nothing. Parts of the
woods go quiet all the time. A hawk goes over and everything shuts up
for a while. I wrote the time down in my log and kept watching.

The quiet did not move on. It stayed in the same place.

That was when I got the binoculars out and actually looked at that
section. There was a clearing there.

I do not mean a small open spot. I mean a clean, round patch of bare
ground with no trees in it at all. No stumps. No fallen logs. No brush.
Just flat dirt, like the trees had never been there in the first place.

I checked the map in the cab three times. There was no clearing on it.
On that map, that whole section was marked as woods, the same as
everything around it.

I got on the radio and called dispatch.

“Dispatch, are we doing any clearing or logging southwest of my tower?”

“No. Nothing scheduled out there.”

“Then I need someone to look at a spot for me. I have a clearing that is
not on my map.”

There was a pause before she answered.

“Say that again?”

I gave her the location the best I could. I told her it was round, bare,
and big enough that I should not have been able to miss it before.

She told me she would note it and have someone check when they were in
the area. She did not sound worried. At the time, I was not that worried
either. I was mostly annoyed, because I thought the map was wrong and
nobody had told me.

The next morning, the quiet was wider.

I know how that sounds. I could not hear a line on a map getting bigger.
But I could sit in the cab, turn my head, and tell where the normal
sounds stopped. Everything on the other sides of the tower sounded fine.
Birds, bugs, wind in the leaves. On the southwest side, there was
nothing.

When I looked through the binoculars, the clearing was bigger.

Not by a lot. Maybe a few trees worth of ground on each side. But it was
bigger, and I had not heard a single tree fall during the night.

I plotted it on the map in pencil and wrote the date next to it. Then I
called it in again.

Dispatch told me a ground crew had driven the access road near that
section the day before. They said they did not see a clearing. They said
the woods looked normal from the road.

I asked if they had gone in on foot.

“No. From the road it looked fine, so they moved on.”

“From up here it does not look fine. It is bigger than yesterday.”

“I will pass it along.”

Nobody passed anything along. Or if they did, nobody called me back.

By the fourth day, I was marking the edge of the clearing every morning.
I stopped trusting my eyes and started using the fire finder in the
center of the cab to line it up. The edge kept moving. Always out.
Always a little closer to the tower.

Animals started coming out of that section. Deer first. Then smaller
things I could barely see from that height. They all moved away from the
clearing. None of them went back toward it.

That night, the quiet reached far enough that I could hear the
difference from the stairs. I went down one flight, just to check my
supply shed, and the sounds from the southwest side were already gone at
that level too. No crickets. Nothing in the grass.

I went back up and locked the cab door. There is not much of a lock on
it. It would not keep anything out. I locked it anyway.

Yesterday morning, the clearing was close enough that I did not need the
binoculars.

Yesterday afternoon, dispatch stopped answering the way they had been. I
would call in and get static, then her voice cutting in for a second,
then static again.

“You are breaking up. Repeat your last.”

“I said the clearing has doubled since this morning. I need to know if I
should come down.”

I never got a clear answer to that.

This morning I woke up to no sound at all outside the cab.

The clearing is under the tower now. It is not just the southwest side
anymore. When I look down the stairs, the ground at the bottom is bare
dirt in a clean circle around the base. The supply shed is still there.
My truck is still parked where I left it. There are no trees standing
anywhere close enough for me to reach.

I have not heard a bird since I woke up. I have not heard wind in
leaves, because there are no leaves left to hear it in.

The radio is on. It is putting out low static and nothing else. I have
tried every check-in time and a few times in between.

My phone has one bar. It will load a text page like this, slowly, and
not much else. Calls will not go through.

I have water up here for maybe two days if I am careful. I have food for
a little longer than that.

I am not climbing down into that clearing. I do not know what happened
to the trees, and I do not know what happens to something that was
standing where the clearing decides to be next.

If anyone reading this works a tower, check your maps against what you
can actually see. And if part of your woods goes quiet and stays quiet,
do not wait four days to take it seriously.

I waited too long, and now the quiet is all the way up to me.
