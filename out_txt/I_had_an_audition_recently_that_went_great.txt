I’m sure some of the people reading this know the struggles of
auditions. Just getting in the door for one is a hassle in and of
itself. Then you have to prepare material, make yourself look nice, and
prepare to potentially humiliate yourself in a room full of strangers.
It’s not always the best experience. This last one, though, was really,
really weird, for lack of a better word. At least, I think it was. I
kind of spaced out half way through. I remember everything leading up to
it,
 but once I was in the room, my memory gets kind of hazy.

My agent contacted me last week, telling me one of the local theatres
was hiring actors for an upcoming production. She said it was a massive
deal for them, that they got some investor raising their budget, and how
it was a big opportunity. All I had to do was prepare a classical
monologue. I hadn’t done Shakespeare since college, but dusting off
Timon of Athens didn’t take too long. 

It was pissing rain when I got there. The weatherman said clear skies,
so I had to use my binder as a makeshift umbrella in an attempt to keep
my hair dry. The lobby was dim and cramped, a dozen actors lining the
wall making it feel even smaller. Headshots of the leads in their
current show lined the eggshell paint, peeling some from the plaster. I
couldn’t believe this was the place that scored a “massive investment”,
but I’ve been in worse waiting rooms for dumber jobs.

“Here to audition?” asked the man in front.

He hunched over a plastic folding table, a stack of headshots and
resumes at his side. His forehead gained a few wrinkles as he looked up
at me, and endeavored a pleasant smile as I stumbled in. 

“Yes sir.” I affirmed. I gave him my name and added my picture to the
pile.

“Have a seat at the end. They’ll call you when you’re up. Break a leg.”

I sat at the end of the dusty bench. I had my lines down, but I kept
mumbling them under my breath.

“Timon of Athens, huh?” The girl next to me chimed.

“Excuse me?” I was too focused to hear.

“I like that show! I wasn’t sure what exactly to prepare so I just went
with Romeo and Juliet!” Her voice practically bubbled out of her.

“That’s always a good choice, I feel like it can read for anything.” I
wasn’t prepared for conversation, but tried to match her energy.

The man at the front of the line was called in.

“That’s what I was thinking! It’s weird they didn’t say what the show
is. Do you think it’s an original?” Her blue eyes were so intense it
almost hurt.

“It wouldn’t surprise me. Apparently they got some miracle investor, so
it could be their own work or something.”

“Maybe it’s about you!” She stated.

“What?” It caught me off guard.

“It could be about wanting to end it. Your pitiful existence!” She had
stopped blinking.

I sat there, speechless, staring back at this woman. The fluorescent
bulbs hummed louder with each second her eyes bore into me. The paint
peeled further, and the rain outside began to pour.

“What the fuck are you talking about?” but then it’s like everything
snapped back. I know for a fact she said that to me, but it was like I
had imagined it. Like that conversation happened in a dream, but when I
said that, I woke up. The lights resumed their normal volume, and the
rain softened in an instant.

“I…said I hope it’s a musical?” The girl said, cocking an eyebrow and
leaning away from me. She looked down at her script, intently pretending
I had vanished.

I looked to the end of the line, and found we had moved up four spaces.
I spun my head and found more people had replaced our spot in line. I
shook my head and rubbed my face. I still don’t have an explanation for
what that was, but at the time I guessed it was nerves, repeating my
monologue in my head.

The stage door opened, another name was called. The man at the front
stood up and followed the proctor, but as he entered, I swear I could
hear something. Faint echoes of a raucous audience, laughing and
clinking glasses. It sounded like an intermission on Broadway, rather
than the run down theatre I found myself in. 

At the time I didn’t think about that. I was excited! It had been so
long since I performed for a crowd this size. Even if it was just an
audition, getting to perform in front of that many people is why any of
us get in the business. My elation was halted, however, when I noticed
the guy next to me. At first, I thought he was mulling over his lines
like the rest of us, but then he seemed to get stuck.

“He who the sword of heaven will bear,
Should be as holy as severe,
Pattern in himself to know,
Grace to stand, and Virtue to go,
Grace to stand and Virtue to go,
Virtue to go, go,
Virtue to virtue,
Stand stand,
Virtue to go,
Grace to virtue to stand,
Virtue go, go, go, GO, GO, GO, GO, GO!”

I can still hear him screaming when I imagine it. It was like every
utterance was a knife in his throat. He began clawing at his chest,
ripping the buttons from his once-pressed shirt to find the bare skin
beneath. Blood and flesh caked his nails as his chest gave way like wax.
As he dug deeper into himself, something began to wiggle and writhe
within his chest. Thunder clapped so loud it felt like the lightning had
struck at my feet. The mass rotated to stillness, and split open to
reveal a brilliant blue eye, staring at me with the intensity of the
woman in front of me. The man removed his hands from his chest and
grabbed my head. 

“GO!”

He screamed me back into reality. I flinched and hit my head against the
wall. I had dozed off? I must have, but I was also next in line to
audition. Hair had matted to my forehead and I felt like I had just ran
a marathon. The air tasted heavy as I sucked it down, trying to steady
my breathing. I looked back down the line, every head pointed to their
pages. My gaze fell on the blue-eyed woman behind me. She was ahead of
me before right? 

“I’m sorry did I take your spot?” I asked.

“Timon of Athens, huh?” She responded, her head staying low.

“You…what?”

“I said I hope it’s a musical!”

I jumped as the door opened. A wave of white noise from the crowd washed
over me. The old proctor stepped out, but didn’t call my name.

“Are you ready?” She tilted her head.

“For sure.” I said, standing uncertainly.

“Are you sure?” She stood motionless.

I looked to the lobby. Every face in line stared at me, their
expressions ranging from elated to furious. All sound halted. The lights
didn’t hum, the mumbling of practiced speeches, even the raging storm
outside fell mute, save the man at the table’s pencil, scribbling away.

“N-no…” I stated. “No. This is wrong, I can’t-” I was interrupted by a
deafening snap. The pencil broke, and though he didn’t look toward me, I
could see a vague imprint of the man’s expression through clouded glass.
I only glanced at it. I couldn’t bring myself to look for more than a
second.

“Come now, they’re waiting.” The proctor ushered behind me.

I looked through the door. A layer of black curtains withheld the
unmistakable shine of a spotlight. The noise of the audience returned,
promising me hundreds, no, thousands of eyes. So many voices, all here
to see me. I didn’t waste time looking back to the lobby. I made my way
to the stage.

“Break a leg.” The proctor’s voice whispered into my head.

As I parted the curtain, the crowd was revealed to me. Three people. In
a tiny black box theatre that could hold maybe forty. I paused, unsure
of what I was seeing. A beautiful woman sat in the center, beaming with
anticipation. The two men on either side of her could have been twins.
They looked as alike as they did ancient, neither of them displaying a
hint of emotion.

The center of the stage was taped off, forming a triangle with a music
stand in the center. Without telling them to, my legs took steps to the
stand, my hands outstretched. They found placement on a stapled-together
script.

“The King”

I flipped the page and I read. I think I read. No, I know I read. I read
the first line, then the second, followed by the fifth and continuing
onto the eleventh. The noises I made were words. I read the words. As I
read them, the crowd roared. They loved it. They loved me! The woman
ahead of me in line cheered from the mezzanine. The eye in the man’s
chest wept with joy. The proctor kissed the twin on the right. The man
at the desk clapped until the bones in his hand turned to dust. The twin
on the left tore his tongue from his mouth. My mother waved at me from
the aisle. She was so proud. I read line four, then thirty, then ninety.
I uttered emotions I’ve never felt, thoughts left unthought, and wore
them across my being. I laughed while drowning and cried while lost. I
coughed out soliloquies through the ashes of my father. My mouth, a
vessel for the rich among the high seats. My words, a temple for the
groundlings. The woman in the center, once merely beautiful, now
all-encompassing, raised her hands and body to the sky. “YOUR KING” she
proclaimed. Icy, unseen tendrils gripped my fingers, propelling my
speech towards what would have been the ceiling, had it not been
replaced by everything else. My eyes, once closed, now open to all that
would be mine.

And then I found myself almost home. I felt faint and a bit queasy. The
ground was completely dry, so I guess it hadn’t been raining for a
while. I know it’s not the best story, it’s like I blacked out right
after I signed in, but I need to know is this a condition that anyone
knows of? I have to know soon, because I apparently got the part! I got
an email yesterday saying I’m going to be playing “The King”. Of what,
I’m not really sure, but I guess I nailed it. I’m just hoping whatever
state I was in sticks around for the role. If you’re in town you should
see me! Bring a couple of friends, I’m sure it’s going to be great!
