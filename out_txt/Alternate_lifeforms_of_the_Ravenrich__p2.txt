Acraptor

Acaecoraptor sonorus

Threat Level: Letum (4/7)

Safety Protocol

This species is highly social. Upon encountering a single individual,
immediately search the surrounding area for additional members of the
group.

Observe the sensory filaments running along the creature’s back. If they
remain flat, the organism most likely has not detected your presence.
Raised filaments indicate that it has either heard or detected your
scent.

Do not produce unnecessary noise or sudden movements. Prevent even a
single member of the group from hearing or smelling you.

The species possesses echolocation. If an individual prepares to emit an
acoustic pulse, immediately stop all movement until the scan is
complete.

If the organisms remain unaware of your presence and are not
approaching, remain completely still until the group has moved a safe
distance away.

If an individual approaches without displaying obvious hunting behavior,
quietly increase the distance between yourself and the organism,
preventing it from coming closer than approximately 2 meters.

If the organisms are already aware of your presence and no shelter is
available nearby, run in as straight a line as possible, avoiding sharp
turns and rough terrain while continuously monitoring your surroundings.

It is strictly prohibited to approach the organism, speak in its
vicinity, or breathe loudly.

Taxonomy

Domain: Eukaryota

Kingdom: Animalia

Phylum: Chordata

Clade: Amniota

Clade: Parareptilia

Order: Dracomorpha

Family: Acaecoraptoridae

Genus: Acaecoraptor

Description

Despite its appearance, which strongly resembles members of the family
Dromaeosauridae, this organism is neither a theropod nor a dinosaur.
Instead, it belongs to the clade Parareptilia.

Adults measure approximately 5–7 meters in length and weigh around 500
kilograms.

The body is barrel-shaped, supported by long hind limbs adapted for
rapid locomotion. The forelimbs are relatively small but exceptionally
powerful. Unlike those of theropod dinosaurs, the wrists are capable of
extensive rotation. These limbs are used for attacking prey,
transporting carcasses, and carrying eggs.

The tail is long and highly flexible, accounting for at least half of
the organism’s total body length. A fin-like expansion at its end
improves maneuverability and acceleration while moving through water.

The neck is relatively long, and the head is large. The teeth are
dagger-shaped and serrated, representing another example of convergent
evolution with theropod dinosaurs.

The paired eyes are absent. The eye sockets instead serve as attachment
points for the powerful muscles controlling the lower jaw.

The skull bears pronounced supraorbital crests, as well as an additional
crest above the nasal region. These structures are considerably larger
and more prominent in males. The crests function to amplify both
incoming and outgoing sound.

A parietal eye is present on the top of the skull, similar to that found
in modern lizards and tuataras. Like theirs, it is used to detect
ambient light levels rather than to form images.

Running from the base of the neck to the middle of the back are
feather-like projections formed from modified scales. These structures
function as highly sensitive tactile organs capable of detecting
physical contact and subtle disturbances in the surrounding air.

Hearing is exceptionally well developed. Olfaction is also highly
sensitive; however, its effectiveness on Earth is reduced due to the
unfamiliar chemical composition of the local environment.

Behavior and Ecology

Acaecoraptor sonorus is an obligate opportunistic predator.

The species possesses a highly developed nervous system and advanced
cognitive abilities. Its intelligence is considered comparable to that
of modern canids.

Individuals typically live in relatively simple family groups consisting
of a dominant breeding pair and their offspring of multiple generations.

Communication is carried out primarily through ultrasonic vocalizations.

During hunts, the group divides into two functional roles. One portion
of the pack drives prey toward a predetermined location while the
remaining individuals remain concealed in ambush before launching the
attack.

The species does not exhibit a fixed breeding season. Both parents
participate in raising and protecting their offspring.

The preferred environmental temperature ranges from 20 to 40 °C.

Additional Information

Despite its relatively high intelligence, no confirmed cases of
successful domestication have ever been documented.

Remember, citizen: a wild animal always remains wild. Minimize all
contact with the organism whenever possible.
