For reasons unknown, every year we go on a family trip to Branson,
Missouri.

My brother and I have lots of fond memories from these trips growing up,
but as we got older the magic of that poor-man’s version of Gatlinburg,
Tennessee started to lose its appeal.

But family is important and we still go, only now after the family
activities are over for the day my brother, we’ll call him Donnie, and I
go out to the bars.

This year Donnie brought his fiancée Rachel, and one night when we were
out at the bars we ran into a group of locals who we ended up making
friends with.

There were three of them, we’ll call them Steve, Mike, and Emily. Steve
had on a Cleveland Browns jersey and my brother, being a huge fan,
connected with Steve immediately. Being single in my early 20s, I was
immediately attracted to the 20-something blonde who kept touching my
arm as if she were trying to reassure me that I actually had a chance of
sleeping with her.

We spent most of the night laughing and getting to know each other. The
bar had a beer pong table so Donnie and I played a few rounds against
Steve and Mike.

When the night ended it was like none of us wanted to go home, but
closing time left few options to keep the party going, complicated by
the fact that our new friends lived 45 minutes outside of town.

They told us they were having a party the following night (or later that
same night since it was like 3 AM), and invited us to come. We agreed,
the three of us looking forward to something different than another
night out at the bars in downtown Branson, and exchanged numbers.

Things were normal all the next day as we hung out at the theme park
with my parents. We were texting the locals and got the address. The
three of us were really excited.

Later that night we said goodbye to my parents and set out.

The address was a little ways out of town, about a 45-minute drive
according to the GPS. Donnie was driving, Rachel was in the passenger’s
seat and I was in the back.

Rachel and I picked up a couple of “road sodas” and were having a good
time, teasing my brother for being the designated driver as we listened
to some pregame music.

Right outside of town the road turned into a winding state highway that
wove its way through the trees.

A little buzzed already, I thought it alone was creepy. The way the
trees just hung over the road like arthritic claws gave me the chills.

About 30 minutes into the drive a thick fog came out of nowhere and
covered the road like a blanket. We couldn’t see a thing and Donnie had
to slow the car to a crawl.

Cell service had gone out a few miles back, save for the GPS pings that
were still guiding us to our destination, although my phone was showing
our little dot gliding in parallel to the line that represented the road
we were driving on.

There weren’t any other cars on the road either. It was just the 3 of us
in Donnie’s SUV, until we came to the man standing in the middle of the
road.

At first I thought it was a trash bag. After all, seeing a man in a
head-to-toe white cloak, hood drawn and staring at the ground, is not
something you expect to encounter in rural Missouri at 10 o’clock at
night.

Donnie had been going maybe 20 miles per hour, so we had plenty of
reaction time, but he must have been as shocked as I was, considering he
didn’t actually stop the car until we were maybe 10 feet in front of
whoever it was.

I rolled down my window and leaned out of the car and yelled something
like, “Hey asshole, want to get out of the road?”

No reaction.

Donnie laid on the horn.

Still no reaction, so I downed the last few dregs in my beer can,
crushed it, and chucked it out of the window at the cloaked figure
standing in the middle of the street.

It hit the side of his head with a metallic thunk.

No reaction.

Just as Donnie was starting to back up, the man’s head snapped up like a
spring-loaded bobblehead whose head someone had been pushing down and
just released.

The rest of its body stayed unnaturally still as the thing stared
directly at the car.

It had the body and build of a man, but it didn’t look like any man I’d
seen before in my entire life.

Instead of eyes, there were just 2 sunken holes like dark craters in its
face. There is no reason that thing should have seen us, but it did. I
knew from the way it tracked the car as we reversed that it could see
us.

In place of its nose was a little slit like that of a snake, and its
skin was ghostly white, the same color as its robe. It looked as if
someone had poured a can of white paint all over.

Crouching down and taking a few creeping steps forward, its focus locked
on to our car.

Rachel screamed as Donnie slammed his foot down on the accelerator.

The thing leapt into the air. It jumped so high and fast that I barely
saw it shoot up into the fog, then I heard a loud thunk as something
landed on the roof.

The driver’s side window exploded, shards of glass shooting inward and
spraying all over Donnie and Rachel. I didn’t realize it at the time,
but a stray shard of glass lodged itself in my arm.

I could hear my heart thumping in my ears, adrenaline and shock rooting
me to my seat as a pale white hand shot in through the window and
started fighting Donnie for the steering wheel.

Everything was chaos and my brain was having a hard time processing what
was happening.

I remember trying to move, to help Donnie, and being unable to move
before I realized it was my seat belt. I remember the fangs of its
canines as the thing contorted its way through the window and sunk its
teeth into Donnie’s arm. I remember the sound of Donnie and Rachel’s
screams, and how cold and scaly the thing’s skin felt as I tried to pull
it off Donnie.

But then the car was upside down, and all I remember is blackness.

My head was killing me when I woke up. It felt like it had been split in
two, and it took me a moment to take in my surroundings and connect the
dots of what had happened.

I was laying in the grass and looked over to the other side of the road,
where the car I had just been in was overturned and smoke filled the
night air.

Wanting to call 911, I looked around for my phone and it was gone.

Rachel was gone, but I found Donnie laying in the middle of the street.
He was staring up at the night sky, eyes wide open and his face frozen
in a look of shock. His stare was blank and vacant, no soul behind his
eyes.

Blood was everywhere. His shirt had been ripped open and where his
stomach should have been was just a bloody hole in his stomach filled
with a mixture of blood and guts. It looked like something had been
chewing on his organs.

I don’t know if I cried then or not.

It was the gunshot that brought me back to my senses. The sound cracked
against the night and I heard the bullet ricochet off the ground just a
few feet away from me.

Fight or flight kicked in at that point, and I chose the latter and took
off into the woods. I didn’t know who was shooting at me or where the
thing that had attacked us was, and didn’t want to find out.

I just ran into the woods, and kept running for probably over an hour,
and even then I didn’t stop, as much as I wanted to just collapse on the
ground. The whole night I kept walking through the woods, wondering what
that thing was, what had happened to Rachel, crying for her and my
brother, and jumping at every little snap of a twig or rustle of leaves.

The sun was just coming up, bright and radiant as ever, as if nothing
bad had happened the night before.

I made it to a gas station, covered in blood (I also still had the glass
shard embedded in my arm), and the man behind the register gave me a
terrified look when I asked to use his phone.

Nevertheless, he said that was fine and told me he had to run to the
back office to get it.

While he did, I noticed the TV he had hanging on the wall behind the
register. It was showing the news, who were reporting on a story about
an overturned vehicle found on the side of the road about 30 minutes
outside of Branson, and a body lying in the middle of the street.

Then my face came up on the TV, wanted as a person of interest.

I’m not sure if I made the right call or not, but the only thing I could
think of was “who was going to believe me?” so I left the gas station
before the man came back with the phone.

I’m writing this to you, Reddit, to profess my innocence.

Recently I saw my parents interviewed on the news. They’re a part of the
search parties looking for Rachel and I, and they were sobbing, telling
the news that I deserved the justice coming to me.

I’ve thought about Steve, Mike, and Emily a lot since that night.

Sometimes when the librarians aren’t looking I can sneak into the local
library, and I use their computers to try to find them (and make this
Reddit post). Thankfully my beard is so thick now that no one recognizes
me; it probably helps that I smell terrible too, so no one wants to get
too close to me either.

So far I haven’t had any luck, but I did find a news article from about
five years ago.

A group of cousins disappeared after meeting 3 strangers at a bar and
being invited to a house party. Their car was found overturned on the
side of the road about 30 miles outside of town.

They ended up finding one of the bodies, but the strange thing was it
was a few days later and over five miles away. Police described it as
being in a similar state to Donnie’s when I’d found him in the middle of
the road. They specifically said he had survived on his own for a few
days, judging by the condition he was in when he’d been found.

“He looked like he had been hunted,” one officer was quoted in the
article.

As I’m writing this, I look up from the computer to notice 3 people
enter the library, 3 people who look very familiar. I’m not sure if I
should get a closer look or get out of here. Will update if I can.
