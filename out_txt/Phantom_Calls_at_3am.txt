Back when I was in seminary and attending Bible college, money was
tight, so I took an overnight job as a front desk attendant at a local
hotel.

Days were packed with classes, reading, and lectures. Nights were long,
empty, and dead quiet. It was perfect for catching up on homework,
writing papers, and studying scripture while the rest of the world was
asleep.

My shift partner was an older security guard. He was a big guy,
hilarious, and completely relaxed. He was the kind of person who could
turn an eight-hour graveyard shift into smooth sailing just by shooting
the breeze and cracking jokes. We had our routine down. He would do his
rounds, come back to the desk, and we would kill time talking about
life, theology, or whatever random thought popped into our heads until
dawn.

Then came one night that started like every other, but ended up changing
how I look at empty spaces forever.

It was right around 3:00 AM. The lobby was in total silence except for
the low hum of the ice machine in the side hallway. Out of nowhere, the
main desk phone blasted its ring tone.

I picked it up, putting on my standard customer service voice: “Front
desk, how can I help you?”

What came back through the receiver wasn’t a guest asking for extra
towels. It was a blood-curdling shriek. It was a woman screaming at the
absolute top of her lungs, a sound filled with pure, raw terror and
complete helplessness. Before I could even ask what was happening, the
line went dead.

My heart dropped into my stomach. I looked down at the caller ID
display.

**Ext. 1666.**

I stared at it. That didn’t make any sense. Our room numbers didn’t go
up to four digits, and we definitely didn’t have a room 666, let alone
1666.

Panic set in fast. I radioed security right away. “Hey, can you come up
to the front desk right now?”

He must have caught the shaking tone in my voice because he didn’t joke
around. He got up there as fast as his legs could carry him. While he
was rushing over, I pulled out the heavy master directories and phone
extension binders from under the desk, flipping through pages with hands
that wouldn’t stop trembling.

I finally matched the extension digit. It wasn’t a room. It was routed
to the indoor pool area.

“Someone’s hurt or stuck at the pool,” I told him, grabbing the master
key card.

We sprinted down the long, dimly lit hallways, preparing for the
absolute worst. A drowning, a bad fall on the wet tile, or someone who
broke in after hours. We pushed through the heavy glass doors into the
pool room.

Nothing.

The water was completely still, reflecting the dim blue nightlights.
There were no splash marks on the deck, no open doors, and no sign of a
single soul.

We stood there for a second, catching our breath and taking a sigh of
relief. Maybe someone hit a panic button by mistake and ran off. We
walked back to the lobby, trying to rationalize it.

As soon as our feet hit the tile behind the front desk, the phone rang
again.

I answered it. Total silence on the line. Then came that same dreadful
shriek, followed immediately by harsh static fuzz.

I looked at the screen. **Ext. 1664.**

“Ballroom,” I said, looking up at the guard. He was staring back at me
in complete disbelief, the humor entirely gone from his face.

We walked down to the ballroom corridor. I unlocked the heavy double
doors, clicked on the overhead lights, and swept the entire space. Empty
tables, stacked chairs, and total silence.

By the time we walked back to the desk, the phone rang a third time. At
this point, my brain was fighting against the fear, trying to find a
normal explanation. *It has gotta be kids,* I thought. *Some teenagers
snuck in or found an unmonitored line and are messing with us.*

So, I decided to ignore it.

Over the next ten minutes, the phone rang six more times. Each time, we
just watched the light flash on the console.

The guard looked at me, trying to regain his usual composure. “Damn,
they’re persistent,” he muttered. He reached out, picked up the
receiver, and put on his tough security tone: “Quit your shit, we’ve had
enough of this tonight.”

He smiled for a split second, waiting for a response. Then, right in
front of my eyes, his half-smile completely melted into a blank, pale
stare. All the color drained out of his face.

He slowly held the phone out toward me.

“It’s for you,” he whispered.

My skin crawled. I took the receiver from his hand and pressed it to my
ear.

“Hello?” I asked.

Nothing.

“Hello? Look, if you’re playing games, I’ve had enough now. Thank you,”
I said, trying to sound firm, and slammed the receiver down onto the
base.

Not even two seconds after the phone clicked down, all hell broke loose.

It wasn’t just the front desk phone anymore. Every single phone on the
first floor started ringing simultaneously. The desk line, the back
office line, the kitchen line, and the bar phone down the hall were all
screaming at the same time in an erratic, un-synced choir of electronic
noise.

At that exact moment, the glass automatic sliding doors at the front
entrance, which required motion right in front of the sensor to open,
began sliding wide open, closing, and snapping open again. Over and
over, right into the cold night air.

I felt a paralyzing fear slam into my body, a deep, ancient panic I
didn’t even know I was capable of feeling. My mind went completely
blank. I couldn’t think, and I couldn’t process.

I bolted.

I ran straight out the opening automatic doors into the parking lot,
gasping for air like I had been underwater. The guard came running out
right behind me. We stood on the cold asphalt, twenty feet away, looking
back through the glass windows into the illuminated lobby where the
phones were still ringing off the hook.

We were both breathing heavy, terrified to step foot back inside.

The guard looked over at me, his voice barely above a whisper.

“What if this was a test… and you failed?”

His words hit me like a physical weight, especially given what I was
going to school for. I didn’t know how to answer him. I just stayed
quiet, closed my eyes, and began praying silently right there in the
parking lot.

I prayed for a solid couple of minutes, asking for peace, protection,
and the strength to step back in. Slowly, a small swell of courage came
back.

“What if?” I finally responded to him.

I turned around and walked back inside. The moment my boots crossed the
threshold, the ringing stopped. The automatic doors shut tight and
stayed closed. The lobby was dead quiet. It was like the last fifteen
minutes had been entirely erased.

We sat in dead silence for the rest of the night. Neither of us said a
word.

At 7:00 AM, the morning maintenance guy walked in with his coffee cup,
signing in at the desk.

I leaned over, trying to sound as casual as possible. “Hey man, when you
get a chance today, can you check the phone lines for the pool and the
ballroom? They were going haywire all night, giving us random phantom
calls for no reason.”

He stopped, took a sip of his coffee, and looked at me completely
puzzled.

“What are you talking about?” he said. “Those lines have been physically
disconnected for years.”

He shook his head, muttered, “Must be kids messing with the main box,”
and walked down the hall.

I never worked another night shift at that building again.
