I’m writing this from three years ago. Or maybe it’s just today, now. It
feels like both for me. It’s as if I’ve just woken up from a prolonged
nightmare. Maybe everyone who’s just ended a bad relationship feels like
that, but then again I’d be willing to bet the story of mine is worse
than most.

But I’m getting ahead of myself. What I actually wanted to say is that
when I was walking here, I saw a hawk disemboweling a pigeon on
someone’s lawn.

The two images merged in my mind when I saw it: the hawk on the lawn,
and the clock eater in the alley. The hawk’s shoulders shimmying as it
bent its neck down to tear strips and ribbons out of its prey again and
again in graceful staccato, its motions jerky and yet fluid and
perfectly timed. The buzzing and the mandibles in the darkness, and the
segmented brass arm and the screams.

Now, I’m here with my ex-boyfriend again in this coffee shop, the same
place we went on our first date in a past life.

Look at me, revisiting what happened for no reason. As if writing it
down for a bunch of strangers makes what happened any more real than
just a nightmare. All I actually did today was wake up and see one
animal eating another. And then I got here and ordered a coffee. A
coffee, if you can imagine that! I drank nothing but tea for the last
year I was with him, which is another thing I’ll never fucking miss.

And you know what also really pisses me off is that the signs had all
been there from the start, if I had actually bothered to look for them.
The little verbal tells. The coincidences and the escalation. I had
rushed past them in my own desire to be known, to find a shortcut to
true love.

But then again, maybe you can’t blame me for everything. I was that same
person going into it that I always was: hard-edged, but trusting, if I
felt like someone seemed like the right one for me. And that man had the
time to get awfully good at seeming.

I wonder, when he talks to me this time, what I’ll say.

“You have a great evil in your heart and need to be killed before you
hurt anyone else” is certainly an option. Who hasn’t wanted to say that
to an ex?

But when he finished his coffee, he walked past me to the front door and
left. I was facing him and looking directly at him, and yet his gaze
barely lingered on me.

So that’s it, then.

Only I have any memories of what happened in the past life. The rest is
gone, lost forever to history, as if it never happened in the first
place. I hope for her sake, that other me, that it didn’t.

But who knows. She might still be out there, somewhere, still trapped in
it.

Maybe she’ll even be posting about what’s happening to her again, like I
did. If my memories are correct (and I’ve tried so hard on alternating
days to preserve them and to block them out), she’ll start posting about
the events after he meets her in person for the first time.
