Hum

Foedicephalus festinans

Threat Level: Letum (4/7)

Safety Protocol

Pay attention to unusual vibrations occurring within your body or
surrounding environment.

If strong vibrations are detected, immediately inspect the area to
locate the source.

Upon visual contact with the organism, avoid open areas. Seek narrow
passages or locations containing obstacles that restrict the creature’s
movement.

If shelter is unavailable, move between environmental obstacles and
avoid remaining directly in the organism’s path.

In open terrain, when the creature approaches within approximately 2
meters, immediately drop to the ground, lie face down, and cover your
head with your arms. If possible, continue reducing the distance toward
the nearest shelter, repeating this action whenever the organism
approaches.

Movement directly away from the organism in a straight line is strictly
prohibited.

Taxonomy

Domain: Eukaryota

Kingdom: Animalia

Phylum: Chordata

Class: Amphibia

Order: Polypoda

Family: Foedicephalidae

Genus: Foedicephalus

Description

Foedicephalus festinans, commonly known as Hum, is a massive predatory
amphibian measuring approximately 4 meters in length and weighing
between 150 and 200 kilograms.

The organism’s most distinctive feature is its disproportionately large
head, reaching approximately 2 meters in diameter. Under normal
conditions, the remainder of the body is almost impossible to observe
due to a dense aerosol cloud surrounding the organism, composed of water
vapor, metabolic byproducts, and other biological compounds released by
the creature.

The body of Hum contains numerous specialized siphons. Ten ventral
siphons located on the underside of the body generate an air cushion
between the organism and the ground, allowing temporary low-resistance
movement resembling gliding above the surface.

This system requires extremely high energy expenditure and can only be
maintained for limited periods.

An additional propulsion siphon releases a mixture of methane, water
vapor, and biological compounds produced by symbiotic microorganisms
inhabiting the gastrointestinal tract. This gas emission allows rapid
movement but significantly reduces maneuverability.

Three pairs of fins extend from the lateral sides of the body and are
used for stabilization and directional control. The elongated tail
contains a vertical fin and functions as the primary steering mechanism.

The eyes are large and highly sensitive in low-light environments. A
third eyelid protects the visual organs during attacks and movement
through dense obstacles.

The mouth can open to an extreme angle due to the high flexibility of
the lower jaw. Large, reinforced teeth are used to crush the bones and
protective structures of prey.

The skeleton consists of lightweight hollow bones while maintaining
considerable structural strength.

Reproduction occurs in freshwater environments. Juvenile individuals
feed on carrion and aquatic organisms until completing metamorphosis and
transitioning to a terrestrial lifestyle.

Adult individuals obtain most required moisture through food
consumption.

Hum possesses highly developed vision and olfactory senses, while
auditory perception remains limited. Sounds produced through
vocalization or movement occur below the human audible range but
generate powerful low-frequency vibrations detectable by living
organisms over considerable distances.

The organism’s levitation system is primarily used during hunting.
Outside of hunting periods, individuals spend up to 20 Earth hours per
day resting, sleeping, or digesting food.

Behavior and Ecology

Hum are obligate solitary predators. They demonstrate high levels of
intraspecific aggression and gather only during periods of extreme
hunger or reproduction.

The breeding season on Earth occurs from late April to early July.

Sexual dimorphism is weakly expressed.

Individuals prefer open environments near bodies of water. The largest
recorded populations are located within the Sinister Valley and
surrounding regions.

Despite possessing thermoregulation abilities, Hum prefer warmer
environments, including natural thermal zones.

Activity is primarily nocturnal.

Intelligence is considered low. Behavior corresponds to that of an
instinct-driven primitive predator.

Primary prey includes:

deer;

roe deer;

moose;

angel slugs;

other medium-sized organisms.

Attacks on humans, wolves, dogs, and mimics are considerably less
frequent.

Additional Information

The genus Foedicephalus is believed to contain an unknown number of
additional species currently under investigation.

Information regarding newly identified members of the genus will be
released after completion of further research.

Remember, citizen: awareness may preserve your life. Always remain alert
and observe your surroundings.
