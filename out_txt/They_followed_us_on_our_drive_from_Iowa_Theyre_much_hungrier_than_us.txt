Hi, my name is Ellie. I’m 16 and I think I have finally decided to open
up and write about my experience from 3 years ago. I have suffered with
keeping this period of my life to myself for far too long and today
something suddenly gave me an urge to simply write it all out. That
being said I hope whoever reads this respects my decision to keep some
parts of this intentionally vague for reasons you may or may not come to
understand.

6:00 AM, my eyes are sunken inside my skull. I tried talking to this Car
Rental Agent, why even bother?

“Shouldn’t you be in school?”

His mouth aggressively flapped, spewing saliva on my face whenever he
looked down to speak to me. It was utterly infuriating to be spoken down
to like this, especially then.

“I have the money…?”

“Go home kid.”

At some point, you should just forget it. I was not going to be lectured
by a man who had such an unappealing face like that—never have I seen
someone’s skin so droopy, just a tug could’ve ripped it all off—Pop
would’ve made quick work of him.

We decided to get the car a different way—Oliver was the best driver out
of us 4, he spent a fair amount of time driving for his Mama whenever it
was feeding time. Ollie was a good kid.

It took two of us to break through the window. I still feel the sharp
pain in my hands. My nails were streaming blood from underneath, palm
jaggedly sliced across with the shard deeply embedded into my dermis.
Rebecca squealed like a pig when I had to take one out her thumb.

Chicago, that’s where Auntie was—only a state away meant we could get
there in at least four days if the car kept going—she was young, her
skin never drooped since I had last seen her. She could be mean
sometimes but I knew for sure she wouldn’t feed like Pop. I missed her
lemonade back in Iowa.

“You think she’ll be surprised when we knock on Auntie Annie’s door?”

I felt embarrassed by his comment, I didn’t like being questioned.

“Why would she Petey? I told you I called her on the phone back at Pop’s
before we left.”

“She’s not a feeder like Pop?”

“She ain’t… one of the normals, promise.”

Petey was the youngest of our group, he was my little brother ever since
I found him trying to sleep in an old campsite off the side of the road
a few years prior—he always told me he never knew his Mama and got
raised by one of those catholic schools before he ran away.

Whether I believe his story or not didn’t matter, I never cared who
Petey was before he was my brother.

1:20 PM. I had to sit shotgun to give directions, meaning Petey was
stuck with Rebecca for hours of driving. Ollie drove as fast as he
could, which was not fast at all. All the other cars flew past us on the
empty road—luckily the normals driving never checked to see our driver,
or just didn’t care.

“Ellie, Ollie!”

Rebecca’s voice quivered our names. I felt like I knew what she was
going to say, and it made me feel sick. I clenched up, goosebumps
popping up along my body in reaction to a pain I hadn’t felt yet.

“You seen somethin’ Rebecca?”

Ollie was as scared as me, I could see him gripping both his hands over
the steering wheel till the skin on his palms went red and swollen,
pouring out in between his knuckles. He looked like a fresh, ripe tomato
by the time he said anything.

“Someone ain’t going past us… it’s a pickup truck, I think there’s two
of ’em.”

I didn’t look behind me but I heard the two bickering on some of the
details. The car began to felt suffocating, it gave me butterflies. I
hated it.

“Either of ’em Pop?”

I almost had to tear into my throat to push out those words. Just
speaking made me feel like regurgitating.

“I can’t tell but they look like men. Driver’s got a bushy beard.”

We all sat in silence. At that moment I had only just realised our
smell, it was horrendous. We smelt like spoiled milk. Fleas had gathered
onto our heads, nipping at our scalps. It was overpowering, I tried to
hold my nose…

BEEP! BEEP! BEEP! BEEP!

The sound blasted through the shattered window, enflaming our ears and
jolting through my entire body. Ollie’s tight grip lost a hold as we
skidded across the road, smashing violently into a rail. My face
collided into the interior door—as I had desperately begun to tear my
face away from the handle, my nose began to spew out blood like a
clogged up tap. When my ears recovered from the truck’s blasting horn, I
heard the noise of the engine’s rumble coming closer. But.. it suddenly
only started to get quieter, and quieter until I could hear nothing at
all… did they drive off? I wondered if they were a group of normals
after all.

Ollie stopped moving. His head laid on the wheel he had tried so hard to
keep holding. My vision felt blurry, I tried focusing my gaze out of the
window… two hideous sets of gungy yellow teeth stained in dried red
juices grinned hideously back at me. Their bloodshot eyes furiously
staring into mine. I saw their nostrils flare up at the sharp, coppery
smell of the blood trickling down my chin. The driver’s beard begun to
dampen as it soaked up the bubbly saliva uncontrollably sizzling from
his gaping smile—the truck had never left, these men were hungry.

I heard the engine’s rumble explode as our car tore across the road. I
whipped my head from the window to see Ollie looking straight ahead, I
turned back—Petey and Rebecca seemed fine—I didn’t try to speak of what
I saw, it would just make it worse. I glanced at my wing mirror. The
truck was still there, I don’t even think they started the engine.

7:00 PM. It was when the sun had started to fall that Ollie stopped the
car and pleaded to stop for the night.

“I’m not gon’ drive anymore! If you really wanna keep going then someone
else is sitting here.”

I wasn’t about to argue, I was also exhausted. We had driven miles away
from those feeders. Besides, it looked like they had never even tried to
follow us.

“We ain’t anywhere near Chicago, haven’t even left Iowa yet.”

I heard Petey mutter something along those lines. Whatever it was, it
made Ollie furious.

“As if we were getting to Chicago today anyway. Who drove us this whole
way again?”

“Who was too scared to break into the car? Rebecca was less of a pussy
than you.”

“Petey, out the car. Now.”

Petey stormed out the car as per my request. He would always argue if he
didn’t agree with something—it was immature, very unlike the rest of his
personality. I think he believed he was better than this life, I did
too. Sometimes I wish I never found him—I feel that I caused all his
pain and frustration, but somehow he still loved me.

I slammed my door, and walked around the car. He wasn’t there. I
panicked, and mistakenly screamed out his name. Unbeknownst to me, he
had sat on one of the grassy hills next to the road. I slowly walked
over—I knew he wasn’t in the mood to fight with me, but I was still
cautious.

“You can’t be like that the whole trip y’know?”

“I’m right, everyone in that car knows it except for him.”

“…We’re all tired. So ’re you. I understand you want to get to Aunt’s
quick, but we cant do that if Ollie is too tired to even drive with his
eyes open… yeah?”

“I’m scared. They were feeders, but neither of ’em were Pop… you think-”

“Maybe Pop forgot about us, he don’t care one bit for you or me.”

I waited patiently for Petey’s next response. He seemed as if he was
contemplating if he wanted to inflict me with his next words.

“Can I tell you something? Promise you won’t tell nobody.”

“What is it?”

“Few weeks back Pop woke me up and took me out of bed… I think he led me
out of the house, down to the truck. We drove for a fair few miles n’ it
smelt even worse than usual, I was thinkin’ he killed something in the
back. Then Pops stopped down a road near a great big house, said it was
all his. I didn’t understand one bit so he took me out, just a bit away
from the house into this little bit of forest. I see this hole and I can
tell he dug it, there’s a massive dirt pile and one little shovel laid
next to it.”Let’s see what’s in it” he says… it’s a coffin. I still
don’t get it at first so I ask him, he just says “listen.” so I do.
There’s banging, and muffled shouts. He had a lady in there and she
wasn’t dead-”

He stopped, lips quivering furiously. Then he made this strange noise,
like a whimpering dog. I was about to respond when he opened his mouth
to continue.

“He passed me the shovel. He asked me to cover it up… I felt like I had
to do it, so I just kept piling on dirt over and over. Then I couldn’t
hear nothin’. So that’s when Pop looks down at me n’ he says…”Don’t go
misbehaving like our Rebecca, will you boy?” Then in an instant I was
back in my bed and waking up the next morning, like nothing happened.”

I didn’t understand it at first. I looked at Rebecca sitting in the car
playing with her hair and she looked back at me… it was unmistakeably
her, I had spent enough time with her to analyse and remember each and
every individual pimple and freckle plastered all over her face. Petey’s
story must’ve been a dream. Eventually he agreed to rest for the night,
he even helped to set up the tents. I decided to sleep in the car, it
felt uneasy sleeping outside—I don’t think Rebecca slept in her tent
either, but I couldn’t be sure.

This is all that I’m willing to write today, Thank you.
