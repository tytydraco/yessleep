When I was a kid, I was terrified of storms. The anxiety of knowing a
severe storm or tornado was going to happen was almost unbearable. And
even when the inevitable stormfall happened, there was still a lot of
fear.

Is this bad enough that we need to take shelter?
Will it get worse?
What’s going to happen?

The many half formed thoughts and feelings that ran through my head
whenever a bad storm was overhead. I grew older and somewhat wiser and I
learned to curb the anxiety and fear. Yes, the storms will happen and
yes, when they are bad they have the potential to do serious damage. But
the chances of that happened are slim and taking shelter will always
help. It also helped that as an adult I moved away from home to an area
of the country that doesn’t get tornados, just the occasional severe
storm in spring.

It was just a few days ago that I got the call from my mom that my uncle
had passed away. He lived just down the road from my parents and was a
pretty reclusive guy. It didn’t really come as a shock to any of us, he
had been suffering from cancer for a while now. So I travelled back home
to attend his funeral and help my parents go through his house.

My parents were doing some renovations on thier house so they suggested
I just stay at my late uncle’s house while I helped out. I wasn’t the
biggest fan of that idea, I know he had been my uncle but it still felt
strange and uncomfortable to be sleeping in a dead man’s house. But it
was better than staying in a hotel so I sucked it up. The first day went
by as normal as one would expect. Going through some of my uncle’s
possessions but also taking moments to cry and mourn him.

It was at the end of the first day that I was scrolling on my phone and
catching up on what I had missed from the day that I noticed it. The
next day has a chance of storms and a those storms were going to be
severe. I had that familiar anxiety from childhood rush into my body,
but I had learned over the years to push it down. Just breathe, don’t
think too much about it, it will be okay. I decided I would try to be as
busy as possible tomorrow and just ignore the storm entirely and
therefore ignore my anxiety entirely.

Being busy caused the time to pass by quickly, before I knew it, it was
starting to get dark outside. I looked up, surprised that I had been
working straight until evening until the realization hit me and a spike
of anxiety went right through my chest. The sun wasn’t setting, storm
clouds were coming over the horizon sending the world into a fake
twilight. I tried to get back to being busy but my anxiety had spiked
and it felt like I had a flashing siren in my brain going “Storm
Incoming! Danger!” I gave into my worst instincts and just paced inside
until the storm hit.

When the storm hit, it was a strong one, the wind and rain hit
immediately like a curtain closing and I could feel the windows and
house taking the brunt of the wind. I sat in the kitchen, anxiously
looking out the window, waiting for it all to be over. At a certain
point, the sound of the rain changed to the sound of little pebbles
pinging off the windows and that is when I got very scared. It was now
hailing, and hail is the next step towards a storm being severe or even
a tornado.

I jumped up from my seat where I was pretending to be calm and started
to rush to an interior room of the house so I would be safe. As I
started to get out of the kitchen the noise of the hail changed subtlety
but in a way that was noticeable. The sound of the hail slowly started
to become more regular and rhythmic, less like the chaos of nature and
more like a constant tap. That eventually faded away and was replaced
with what sounded like someone rapping on the window with their knuckle,
but it came from every window.

The knocking continued, becoming more and more insistent like many
people were outside and rapping on the window to get my attention. The
noises then changed, dull thudding all around the windows, almost
sounding like someone slapping their open palm against the window but
all over. The noises grew louder and more violent until I was sure the
windows were going to shatter.

I stood still, unable to process what I was hearing and truly feeling
terrified. Before my brain had the time to tell me what I needed to do,
the storm abruptly let up. The sounds stopped and the rain and wind
calmed down immensely. I let out a shaky breath I had not realized I’d
been holding and walked over to slump down in a chair in the kitchen. I
chalked the weirdness up to my anxiety ratcheting up all of my nerves
and making everything seem way worse than it was. The odd endorphin rush
of the relief that the storm had passed made it so I was more happy on
everything being over than what had actually occurred.

It was two days later when another storm was due to hit in the afternoon
when I made a grave mistake. I knew a storm was supposed to hit that
afternoon but I wanted to go out and grab something for a late lunch
since I had been distracted with cleaning my uncle’s place. My uncle’s
house didn’t have a garage attached, there was just a covered area for
parking that was detached from the main house and was about 50 feet
away. As I pulled into the covered garage a storm hit. The rain came
down out of nowhere and the wind started to pick up. Once again, I got
that familiar fear in my gut and I started to mildly panic. Had I not
been so prone to anxiety and fear I would have stopped to think and just
rode out the storm in my car. But my fear and lizard brain took over and
that was exacerbated by the loud clap of thunder that rang out while I
was in the car.

I let out a scream and in a blind panic, made the decision that the
house was much safer and that I needed to be there RIGHT NOW and got out
of my car and took off at a sprint to the front door of the house. The
rain soaked me immediately and while I was reeling from the shock of
that cold rain, the wind hit me like a train. It felt like I was getting
buffeted from all directions by the wind. As I got closer to the front
door the wind started to hit me head on and it was so intense that it
felt like it was trying to push me backwards, but still, I kept going.
The wind felt like it was growing stronger and stronger by the second
and it was becoming more and more difficult to move forward until I
started to get pushed back. No, I realized, not pushed back, pulled
back. It felt like many hands were grabbing me, on my arms, legs,
sleeves and ankles, and were just slowly pulling me backwards away from
the house. True fear gripped me in that moment and I surged forward,
sprinting like I never had before. I felt myself break free from
whatever was trying to hold me back and I made it to the front door and
inside the house.

I slumped down with my back to the front door and slid down it to sit in
the floor. The storm still raged outside and as I started to try and get
control of my breathing I heard hail start up again. I sat still, hoping
that whatever was going on would end but I didn’t get to be so lucky.
The sound of the hail slowly morphed until it sounded like a knocking on
the front door my back was pressed against. The knocking grew louder and
louder until it was all I could hear. It sounded like the front door was
going to break down until suddenly, it stopped. The storm had passed. I
looked out the window and the sun was starting to come out.

Relief filled my body like a drug and I fully collapsed to lay down on
the floor in exhaustion.

“It’s over.” I said out loud to myself as a way to calm myself down.

“The sun came out and it is over”

As I said that out loud one part of the phrase stuck in my head.

“The sun came out”

The realization dawned on me and anxiety started to replace the relief
in my body. I hadn’t been living in an area with storms for a while now
so I had forgotten. During severe storms, if the sun comes out before
the storm system has fully passed it fuels the storms and makes them
stronger from the heat.

As if on cue, a large boom of thunder reverberated over the house and
the sun slowly started to disappear behind the clouds. I fumbled my way
off the floor and stood up. Wracked with anxiety and fear I was gripped
by the paralysis that only true anxiety can cause. A million thoughts
were whirring through my head but I knew I had to hunker down and get to
safety and let the storm pass.

Before I could set myself into motion, another loud boom of thunder rang
out followed by the power going out. It was only the late afternoon but
because of the darkening storm clouds overhead it looked like the sun
had just set. I made my way to the kitchen with the little bit of light
I had left to check outside and see how bad the storm raging. I didn’t
get a few feet before I heard a sound coming from my jeans pocket. It
was an alarm blaring from my phone. I pulled it out and saw the
notification.

Tornado Warning

For the first time that day, I didn’t freeze in fear or let my mind go
blank. I knew what I had to do. My uncle’s house had an old root cellar
but it was only accessible from an outside entrance. I would run outside
the front door and go around to the side of the house and get to safety.
I started moving immediately and got out the front door. The rain and
wind were starting to pick up and I knew I needed to move fast.

I turned the corner of the house and could see the doors to the cellar,
but that was when the wind picked up again. It was whipping across my
body so powerfully I almost got slammed sideways into the house itself.
And the howling, the wind was howling so loudly it was piercing through
my skull. I could swear that the howling of the wind started to sound
like tortured screaming, the screaming of a thousand voices all in
agony. I had to get out of this storm and get to safely but the wind
started to grab me again. What felt like fingers started grabbing me all
over my body and pulling me back, away from the cellar. The screams
coming from the wind started to change and I could hear words within
them.

“Help me!”
“Save me!”
“No no no!”
“I don’t want to die!”

It was like the voices of lost souls screaming out from the storms that
took their lives and directing it towards me. They wanted me to join
them in their agony. I had to run, I had to get away. The wind was
pulling on me so hard I was sliding backwards. Many invisible hands
grabbing onto me and pulling me backwards. All I could do was scream,
until a new sound pierced the cacophony of wind and screams.

The sound of a freight train.

My blood ran cold, I knew what that meant. That was the sound of a
tornado. A tornado was touching down at that exact moment. I struggled
in vain to break loose and run too safely but I couldn’t break free. The
wind blasted even harder and I started to get pulled up towards the sky
into the tornado. My last thought was an odd one, vindication, I knew I
had a reason to have this anxiety about storms my whole life and what
was happening to me was proof. I blacked out feeling oddly smug.

I came too in a hospital bed. I learned from my family that I was found
under some rubble of my uncle’s house which had the roof taken off by
the tornado. My leg was broken and my head was hit pretty hard with a
concussion. I also had many tiny red marks all over my body. The
doctor’s said that they were probably from the hail of the storm or
debris hitting me. But I knew what they were and when I took a closer
look I confirmed it, they were all finger marks. Marks from being pulled
into the storm by the tortured souls that lived within.
