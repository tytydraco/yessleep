There is a ride at the Crimson Carnival that doesn’t appear on any map
or listing. No one else seems to realize it’s there, but as I’m walking
along the midway, it snags at my attention the way a bur snags at your
clothing. Against your will. It’s just there, suddenly, and I can’t look
away. A strange certainty inside me gives me the feeling that I was
meant to find this ride. Or, perhaps more accurately, that this ride has
been trying to find me.

Like all of the rides at this carnival, it is old and a bit rundown, but
this one is particularly worse off than the others. Two-seater brown
cars pause briefly to pick up riders, before disappearing into the
darkness behind the entrance. Discordant music emanates, off key and
wrong, and lights around the entrance flash a sickly yellow and orange
color. It looks, from the outside, like some form of a haunted house
ride, promising papier-mâché monsters and twisted skeletons. Perhaps the
odd jump scare here and there. But the outside gives little away.

Around me, other carnival-goers wander by, like their attention is
pushed away whenever they near the ride. Not a single other soul so much
as glances this way. So why can I see it? And how? It feels like a sign,
of some sort. Like I was meant to find it, and who am I to deny fate?
What did I come to this carnival in search of, anyway? Escapism?
Entertainment? Something tells me I’ve found it.

So I climb the creaking metal steps and move up to one of the empty
cars. I hesitate before climbing in, my gut telling me that I should
think better of it. Turn back now. But why? It’s just a ride, one of
many here tonight. What’s the worst that could happen?

When the car pauses, I take my seat. It’s uncomfortable, the floor
strangely sticky, and there’s no seatbelt or bar to hold on to, so my
hands just rest in my lap.

“Welcome, welcome, to the Hopeless House!” chants a bright male voice
from somewhere I can’t determine. But the words are all bent and
distorted, and the inflections is on the wrong syllables. “Please keep
your limbs attached and inside the car at all times. Guests attempting
to exit early forfeit their right to safety, and the ride is not
responsible for what comes after. Enjoy!”

As we depart, we pass a faded white sign with red ink that reads one
way, and the words stick out in my mind. Then we turn abruptly towards
the darkness beyond the entrance, where the flashing lights fade, and
the off-kilter music only grows louder. A deep and sudden dread begins
to weigh me down. Why was I so quick to do this? Why did I not think
better of it? I turn to look back at the carnival behind me, the
laughing visitors and ice cream stands and bright lights, feeling a
sudden pang to leap out before it’s too late.

But it is already too late.
