This happened this weekend.

I live in Albuquerque, NM. A normally very arid desert city but with a
forested mountain, the Sandias, to our East.

I’ve been trying to get outdoors more this year. Off-roading, hiking,
camping, all that. Some last minute away from humanity therapy.

A couple days before I went (yes, very last minute) I was looking at my
map app, trying to find somewhere quick and close to spend a last
weekend out before it got too cold for the very light summer gear I
have.

There’s a dirt back trail headed up towards the peak of the mountains
from the North, connecting to a main road at the last leg on the way up
to the mountain peak. I’d seen this trail on my map before but had been
passing over it for something more adventurous. It was rated an “easy”
off road trail and it’s close enough I could decide to drive it any
time.

We’ve gotten an inordinate amount of rain recently, and saw it was
supposed to rain this weekend too. I decided that made it worth
exploring, wondering if the weather had added a little more challenge to
the drive, and would scare off some other weekend adventures to gain me
a little more solitude. I figured I’d scout out any points of interest
and a camping spot on the way up, turn around when I hit the main road,
and play it by ear.

Friday after work I double checked the dispersed camping rules and
weather, packed a little food and water, and loaded a duffel bag with
essentials. I left late Saturday morning, grabbed some lunch in the
nearby town, and was off pavement early in the afternoon. A half mile in
I aired down my tires, mostly for the sake of my own comfort. Checked my
phone and as expected in the mountains, no more cell coverage.

I bumped along for awhile, just enjoying the green and trees and light
showers. The trail leads mostly through something like an uphill valley
before later starting a steeper climb with switchbacks.

Sorry for the long setup. We’re getting to the part that makes me post
this here, and idk what’s relevant and what’s not to the goings on of
this evening, night, and early morning.

I came across a simple, buckshot riddled sign, half hidden in foliage.
All I could make out was “trail” and “cave”, and could see the pull off
shortly ahead. It was early yet and the rain had broken for the moment,
so I pulled in to the wide, unpaved parking area and parked off to the
side, behind some beat up maroon wagon I couldn’t guess the model of.
The trail head was obvious at the far end, the only real break in the
trees and bushes, a white SUV backed in next to it.

I checked the trail length on my downloaded area map, not wanting to
lose too much time, round trip hike would be about 3/4 of a mile, so not
bad. Grabbed my water, secured the truck, and walked to the trail.

There was a man dressed for a jog and couple leashed dogs leaving the
trail and headed to the SUV as I approached. He gave me a brief, nervous
greeting as we crossed each other. He stared at me a little too harshly
and his dogs both already had their hackles up. I figured maybe I
surprised them, idk.

Returned a greeting and not giving it a second thought I started down
the trail. It turned out to be a mild to comfortably widthed unprotected
cliffside trail, littered with the occasional puddle and path choking
fallen boulder, moderately winding uphill.

I got a couple nice photos of the valley I’d been driving up. I kept on,
and not being used to thin air and exertion I was getting slightly
winded and had my eyes down to where I was stepping. Around a wide
inside curve then a blind corner and I looked up. Ahead was a concrete
staircase and above to it’s left, metal spiral stairs leading to a
grated landing. A very odd sight attached to the side of a mountain.

The ground was washed out at the stairs. I grabbed the rail and pulled
myself up the height of two missing steps. The concrete of every step
was half crumbled away, I went up on the toes of my boots, then a short
walk to the spiral staircase.

I have to admit, I have a fear of heights. This thing hangs half over a
dead drop, steep, and it’s so narrow I had to go up half turned
sideways. But, to be fair, it’s solid and in good condition. About two
stories high I think.

I turn onto the landing and see the unimposing natural cave. Four people
could stand side by side in the entrance. I can see what I think is the
end of it not too far inside. Almost quaint.

I take a few steps in and the feeling hits. I’m not supposed to be here,
someone doesn’t want me here, someone’s watching.

I see what I thought was the back is actually a very low overhang. If
you tread through a puddle while you crouch very low you could go deeper
inside. I think about the handgun I left in the truck. I call out
“Hello”.

Nothing. I haven’t heard anything at all to provoke the feeling. I
crouch low, and take a picture. Half because, well, cool little cave,
and half to try and flash some light deeper to see anything more. A
camera phone flash, it doesn’t help much.

That feeling just ticked higher, redlining, I need to leave, and do so.
My acrophobia doesn’t even register as I quick march awkwardly down the
spiral. My back facing the landing and cave feels exposed, to what I
don’t know. I quickly hit bottom, step away and look back up. Nothing.
Jog back to the top of the concrete steps, look back. Nothing.

The panic finally starts fading as I pick my way down the steps, and
gone not much farther down the trail. I leave the trail and just feel
weird, like the feeling of a barely avoided traffic accident.

I passed no one else up or back down the hike.

SUV is gone, wagon I’m parked behind is still there, empty, no one else.
I get back in the truck, start it, and just look at the trail entrance
for a minute. Nothing. My downloaded music and security of the vehicle
wear off the rest of the oddness. I pull around and out, driving up the
mountain. I see a couple fully paved “no overnight parking” lots that
lead to picnic areas and more hiking opportunities. I see some rough
uneven spots one could pull off and maybe stay the night. And further up
I see the entrance to what I’ll later decide, maybe unwisely, to be my
camping spot.

I drive the rest of the mountain, the trail deteriorating enough to
actually occupy my mind. Roll into and through a picnic/rest stop area
and onto the main road. Try to go to the peaks parking area, all closed.
Turn around and backtrack to my dirt road.

Light is starting fail, sunset comes quicker in the mountains. I bypass
a couple potential spots. Too close to the road, too near the trail, too
exposed. I want some privacy, mostly just to not be noticed and bothered
by whoever patrols that area in case I misunderstood the rules.

The spot I eyed earlier, have to turn off and sharply down, immediately
into a vehicle eating mud pit. Legitimate risk of really getting stuck
in it for the night if I don’t make it through, but I see a spot to the
left after, as well a continuation to the right.

Pop the truck in 4wd and rail through the pit and decide to drive
through and check the right for more seclusion. It dead ends maybe 30
yards down. A hallway of trees and bushes not more than 15 feet wide all
the way. Get out and check it out. Trees and foliage keep it completely
shrouded from the trail. Not entirely level but that’s ok.

Now here’s where the bad decisions might start.

I’m definitely not the first person to be here. Scattered beer cans,
assorted miscellaneous other trash, a pile of dirty blankets pushed
under a bush at the back, and an eeriely placed folding chair. The
chair, seemingly in good condition, was set up under a tree in the far
back corner, under low hanging branches. It was set up right against the
trunk facing inward towards the center of the clearing. The seat was
clean of any debris of the tree above it. It had a voyeuristic vibe to
it.

It looked more like the remains of a homeless camp than anything else.
But I was out of time, out of light. I take a long look around, call out
to anyone who might have the spot claimed. Make a 10 point turn and get
the truck turned around in case I need to leave in complete dark, pull
halfway back down from the end, and drive a tire over a low stump as an
impromptu leveling block.

I arrange my gear and settle back in the front seat with a couple
burritos and an audio book.

I have to break here, but it wasn’t a peaceful night.
