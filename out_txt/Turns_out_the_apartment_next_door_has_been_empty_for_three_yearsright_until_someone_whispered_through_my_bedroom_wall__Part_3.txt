The bell above the coffee shop door chimes. It is a cheerful, everyday
sound, but it slices through me like a razor.

A commuter in a sharp gray suit steps inside, letting a gust of cold
morning air chase away the smell of roasted beans. He doesn’t look back.
He doesn’t see the shape crawling over the concrete. It is moving with
terrifying speed now, its spine flexing sideways in sickening, rhythmic
jerks, its bare, bruised feet trailing behind like dead weight.

I bolt upright. My chair screeches against the tile flooring, a sharp,
violent noise that draws a few annoyed glances from the nearby tables.

“Hey, buddy, you alright?” the barista asks, holding a steaming paper
cup.

I can’t answer. My throat is a desert. I back away from the window, my
eyes locked on the plate glass. The figure is halfway down the block.
The morning sun hits it directly, but the light doesn’t reveal a human
face. Its skin is the same mottled, asphyxiated purple as its feet,
stretched tight over a skeletal frame. It has no hair, and its clothes
are nothing but shredded, filthy rags that flap uselessly against the
pavement.

The phone in my hand vibrates again.

“Don’t look away. It’s rude.”

My legs move before my brain can process the panic. I dump my half-empty
coffee into a trash bin and sprint toward the back of the shop. I need
an exit, a rear door, an alley—anything that isn’t that glass
storefront.

“Sir? Employees only!” a voice calls out behind me.

I ignore it, shoving past a swinging wooden door into a narrow hallway
that smells of bleach and sour mop water. At the far end is a heavy
metal fire door with a glowing red exit sign. I throw my weight against
the panic bar.

The door resists for a horrifying second before popping open with a loud
metallic clang, spilling me out into a dim, brick-lined alleyway.

The air out here is freezing. I turn left, running blindly toward the
main street, my mismatched shoes slapping unevenly against the wet
asphalt.

My phone chimes. A frantic, rhythmic stutter of notifications.

Buzz.

“We liked the coffee shop.”

Buzz.

“It smells like roasted skin in here.”

Buzz.

“Why did you run? We have your passport.”

A wave of nausea hits me so hard I have to lean against a brick wall to
keep from collapsing. They are inside the shop. If I go back to the main
street, I’ll be out in the open. If I stay in the alleys, I’m cornered.
I have no money, no ID, and the only person who knew the truth about 4A
is lying dead with his neck snapped.

I look down at the screen, my vision blurring with tears of pure terror.
I need to type. I need to leave a record. If I disappear into the
concrete, someone has to know what is living behind the walls.

With shaking thumbs, I hit reply.
