The white answer sheet stared back at me as I blinked several times,
trying to clear my vision.
My grip around the red pen got stronger, and I marked an F on this
paper.

The answers weren’t wrong.

The calculations had been well presented.
And each question had been attempted. To be honest, I wouldn’t even say
attempted. This student knew clearly what they were doing.

But I was obligated to obey “the rules.” I knew better than to go
against them. I couldn’t risk anything more than what it has already
taken from me.

I’m sorry, Audrey.

I helplessly sighed and picked up the pile of freshly graded papers and
put them aside, attempting to clear my table, which still looked like a
nightmare.

The caffeine had been doing its job and keeping me sane for all these
hours, but as the effects wore off, exhaustion crept in and the familiar
anxiety that had been a part of me for a decade reemerged.

My name is Mr. Smith, and I am a 49-year-old respected mathematics
teacher at X High School. I have specialized in this field since an
early age, and due to this reason, my teaching job has taken me to
plenty of places. Upon turning 40, however, I concluded traveling had
been enough for me and my family and decided to settle down in a very
small town. The type of town where everyone seems to know a little too
much about each other, each secret, each gossip reaching every household
within a matter of a few hours. It only consisted of one high school,
and I would say I had become fairly well known, not particularly for my
teaching skills but for my infamous reputation for being brutally strict
with grades. Each new batch of students seemed to arrive already knowing
who I was. Their seniors and siblings made sure of that. I could always
feel the unease rising as I entered my class.

I didn’t possess sadistic tendencies, and I resented being viewed this
way, but what other option was I allowed to have? So I put on a cold
demeanor and did nothing more than what my job required.

I preferred staying on my own and tried not to interact with any other
teacher because every new person I was willing to let into my life, the
more people “the rules” could harm.

Now I know the only question anyone reading this has. What rules do I
keep talking about, and why have they so heavily influenced my life?

To understand what I’m talking about, you need to understand the
context. When I first arrived in this town, there was a job vacancy at X
High School that immediately needed to be filled. The requirements
matched my skills, and my experience held an impressive reputation of
its own, having taught students across various schools and institutions
throughout my career.

However, one thing unsettled me. The pay was double the amount I was
making at my previous school. That made me hesitate more than it should
have. It felt like a setup since I had never been presented with such a
sum.

But since my wife had already made a few friends and my daughters felt
right at home, I took up the job, trying to suppress the weird gut
feeling.

On my first day, I got to school slightly earlier to set up my desk and
prepare materials for my first class, but instead, I was met with a
small piece of white paper with three rules:

1.) Exactly one student must fail every academic year.
2.) You mustn’t tell anyone about this rule.
3.) You are not allowed to leave this job under any circumstances.

“Failure to comply with these rules will result in severe consequences.
May be severe or fatal. Choose your next step wisely.”

Naturally, as any sensible man would, I crushed the paper and threw it
in the bin. I’m pretty sure it was a prank by my soon-to-be students,
and soon I brushed it off and went on with my day.

The semester had passed by, and I had totally forgotten about those set
of rules. Then came midterm examinations. I had been impressed by how
exceptionally well each student in my class had performed in their
midterm examination.
Not one student had failed.

Needless to say, I felt genuinely proud of my class. As I handed out
their results, I couldn’t help but smile. I felt like a proud dad.

But that feeling only lasted a few seconds because the instant I was
done handing out the papers, my phone rang.

It was my wife.

My daughter had been involved in a severe car accident, and she was
being rushed to the hospital.

The blood drained from my face.

And suddenly it clicked to me. That small piece of paper, those set of
rules, wasn’t just a stupid prank. It was a warning.

Or a coincidence?

My daughter faced severe injuries, but somehow she was still alive. The
doctors kept saying it was a miracle, but I knew the real reason behind
it.

I knew “the rules” wanted to scare me enough so that I was aware that in
the future, if I ever disobeyed, my family would bear the consequences.

I wish I could say I learned my lesson.

The next time, I was the target. This time, the students had worked hard
and earned high grades. I knew the cost had been severe the last time,
but I somehow managed to convince myself it was nothing but just a
coincidence.

I prayed to God and handed out the result sheets. Cheers erupted in the
room, and the next second, nothing happened.

A few minutes had passed, and still nothing.

Students started leaving, thanking me and appreciating my hard work as
an instructor. I wished them luck for their next semester.

Now a few hours had passed, and nothing happened.

I almost laughed at myself. How did someone like me even believe in
something like that?

By my bedtime, I was convinced I had been worried about something I
shouldn’t have been and dozed off to sleep.

That night, I had terrifying nightmares. No, not terrifying, absolutely
bloodcurdling nightmares.

I wasn’t ready for what came next, and the memory of it still haunts me
to this day.

I had been visited by an entity.

It was red but not the red of blood. It was the color of something that
had been buried alive and left to rot beneath the earth.

Its body was impossibly thin and slender. The skin clung tightly to its
bones.

There was no face, only a pair of enormous, hollow eyes sunk deep into
the red flesh.

I vividly remember this entity’s image, but I can’t recall anything it
said or did to me. All I know was that I was in excruciating pain, as if
my whole body had been left to burn.

And then I woke up.

I was standing beside my bed, with a knife in my hand.

I never dared to go against the rule after that.

After grading this semester’s final examination papers, I returned to
the mundane routine I followed. Had my classes from 8:00 AM to 12 PM and
had lunch. As the bells rang, the hum of students filled my classroom. I
gathered all the marked papers and handed them back to the students, who
were already holding onto their breaths, followed by stares filled with
anticipation.

Then I came across Audrey’s paper. I placed it softly onto her desk.

She clutched onto the paper. The look that came after shattered my
coldness that I had been using as a shield.

Her eyes had started to water as she attempted to hide her face with her
marked paper.

The classroom was filled again with hushed conversations.

In a stern tone, I announced that the class was dismissed and they were
allowed to leave.

I sat down at my chair and got working on my laptop again. The classroom
had nearly emptied, except as I looked up, someone was standing in front
of me.

Audrey.

“Hi, Mr. Smith. Can I talk to you?”

“If this is regarding your grades, Audrey, I apologize. I am unable to
help you. You know the grading criteria.”

“But I answered everything,” she said in a hesitant tone, almost as if
she was helpless.

“I know.”

“And the calculations are correct.”

“I know, Audrey.”

Her voice became quieter.

“Then why did you fail me?”

I didn’t answer.

She stood there for a moment, holding the paper with both hands. I could
see her trying to keep herself together.

“Please,” she said. “Just listen to me for a minute.”

I finally looked at her.

“I really can’t fail this class.”

Something about the way she said it made me pause.

“My grades are the only thing keeping me in this school. If I fail, I
lose my scholarship. If I lose my scholarship, I can’t afford to stay
here.”

She swallowed.

“My parents already told me they can’t pay for another year.”

I looked down at her paper.

“I’ve been working after school too,” she continued.

“I’ve barely been sleeping. I studied for this exam for weeks.”

Her eyes were beginning to water again.

“I know everyone thinks I’m just another student complaining about a bad
grade, but I’m not asking you to give me something I didn’t earn.”

She placed the paper on my desk.

“I’m asking you to look at it again.”

I felt something twist inside my chest.

Because I had looked at it.

More carefully than I had ever looked at a student’s paper.

Every answer was right.

Every calculation was correct.

She had earned the grade.

And I was taking it away from her anyway.

“I’m sorry,” I said quietly.

She shook her head.

“You’re not.”

I looked at her.

“You’re not sorry,” she repeated. “You’re just scared.”

For a moment, I couldn’t speak.

She picked up her paper again and wiped her eyes.

“I don’t know what happened to you, sir. But whatever it is, please
don’t let it ruin my life.”
She walked toward the door.

And that was when I looked at the red F again.

For years, I had obeyed those rules because I was terrified of what
would happen if I didn’t.
I had watched my family suffer. I had convinced myself that obedience
was the only way to protect them. But how long was I supposed to be
afraid?
Another year?
Another decade?
Until every decision I made belonged to something else?

Audrey stopped at the door.

I stared at her.

Then at the paper.

For the first time in years, I wondered whether the rules were actually
protecting me or whether they had simply taught me to be afraid?

I picked up my red pen.

“Audrey.”

She turned around.

I took a breath.

“Come back here.”

She slowly walked toward my desk.
I turned the paper over.
And beneath the F, I wrote a single letter.
A.

Whatever happens, I will face the consequences, but I cannot ruin some
innocent student’s life because of the fear of what is going to happen
to mine.

I refuse to live in fear.

I waited for a few minutes to see what would happen.

Nothing.

Soon, a few hours had passed, and the school day had arrived at an end.
I don’t want to jinx it, but nothing has happened till now, but from my
experience, I knew well that something life-altering had been coming my
way.

I still hadn’t known what.

Like my students, I was filled with anticipation as I got to my
doorstep. Preparing myself to face the worst.

I unlocked the door.

Nothing.

Except

There had been a woman I couldn’t recognize.

It was like I had seen her somewhere.

I just couldn’t place a finger on who she was.

As soon as she saw me, she jumped happily and ran towards me, wrapping
me in a tight hug.

I didn’t hug her back.

She referred to me as her husband.

I was never married.
