I moved into my apartment in October.

It was supposed to be temporary.

Six months, maybe eight.

I had just started a new job and didn’t know the area very well, so I
wasn’t looking for anything special. I found a one-bedroom apartment in
an old three-story building about twenty minutes from work.

The rent was cheap.

Too cheap, probably.

But the building looked normal.

Brick exterior.

Small parking lot.

Three floors.

Nothing fancy.

The landlord showed me the apartment on a Saturday afternoon.

I remember asking him why the place had been sitting empty.

He told me the previous tenant had moved out unexpectedly.

I didn’t ask questions.

I should have.

My apartment was on the second floor.

There was another apartment directly above me.

3B.

Mine was 2B.

The landlord mentioned that 3B was empty.

Apparently it had been vacant for months.

I didn’t care.

Honestly, I liked having nobody above me.

No loud music.

No footsteps.

No neighbors dropping things at midnight.

I signed the lease.

I moved in the following weekend.

The first few days were completely normal.

I unpacked.

Bought groceries.

Went to work.

Came home.

Nothing strange.

Then, about a week after moving in, I heard footsteps upstairs.

It was around midnight.

Slow footsteps.

Back and forth.

Across the ceiling.

At first, I assumed my landlord was wrong about the apartment being
empty.

Maybe someone had moved in.

Maybe the listing was outdated.

I didn’t care enough to investigate.

The footsteps continued for about ten minutes.

Then stopped.

The next night, they happened again.

Same time.

Around midnight.

Same pattern.

Walking from one side of the apartment to the other.

Back.

Then forward.

Back again.

It became annoying.

After about a week, I started noticing something strange.

The footsteps always stopped at exactly 12:17 AM.

Every night.

I thought I was imagining it.

So one night I checked the time.

12:11.

Footsteps.

12:14.

Still walking.

12:16.

Still walking.

12:17.

Nothing.

Complete silence.

I laughed at myself.

I figured maybe there was some mechanical noise in the building that
happened around the same time every night.

I mentioned it to a coworker.

He told me old buildings make weird noises.

Fair enough.

Then I heard something else.

A chair scraping across the floor.

It happened directly above my bedroom.

Once.

Then again.

Then again.

Like someone was dragging a chair into position.

I got annoyed enough that I went upstairs.

I knocked on 3B.

Nothing.

I knocked again.

Nothing.

I tried the doorknob.

Locked.

I texted my landlord.

“Someone’s making noise in 3B.”

He replied almost immediately.

“Impossible. It’s empty.”

I thought he meant nobody had moved in yet.

So I asked:

“Then who’s upstairs?”

He didn’t answer.

A few minutes later, he called me.

He sounded serious.

“Don’t go up there.”

I asked why.

He said the apartment had been locked for months.

I asked if he had given someone a key.

“No.”

“Then what’s making the noise?”

He said:

“Probably pipes.”

I didn’t believe him.

But I went back downstairs.

That should have been the end of it.

It wasn’t.

The following night, I woke up at 3:06 AM.

I don’t normally wake up during the night.

But something had woken me.

I lay there in the dark.

Listening.

At first, I heard nothing.

Then I heard three knocks.

Above me.

Not loud.

Just…

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

I waited.

Nothing.

Then three more.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

I turned on my lamp.

The knocking stopped.

I checked my phone.

3:06 AM.

I stayed awake for another hour.

Nothing happened.

The next morning, I called my landlord.

He told me he’d check the apartment.

He didn’t.

Instead, he said he’d spoken to the owner of the building.

Apparently, there was a problem with the plumbing.

I believed him.

Mostly.

Until I got home from work.

There was a piece of paper taped to my door.

It wasn’t a note from my landlord.

It wasn’t official.

It was just a small piece of notebook paper.

Three words were written on it.

“STOP KNOCKING BACK.”

I stared at it for probably thirty seconds.

I hadn’t told anyone about the knocking.

Not my landlord.

Not my coworker.

Nobody.

I pulled the paper off the door.

I went inside.

Locked the door.

Checked every room.

Nothing.

I called my landlord.

He didn’t answer.

I called again.

Nothing.

I considered calling the police.

But what would I even say?

Someone left a weird note?

I threw the paper away.

That night, I didn’t sleep well.

At 3:06 AM, the knocking started again.

Three knocks.

I sat up.

Waited.

Three more.

Then I heard something that made my stomach drop.

A knock came from my bedroom door.

Three times.

I didn’t move.

The apartment was silent.

I stared at the door.

Three more knocks.

Except this time, they weren’t coming from outside my bedroom.

They were coming from the hallway.

I grabbed my phone.

I called 911.

While I waited, I heard footsteps.

They moved from the hallway toward my bedroom.

Slowly.

One step at a time.

Then they stopped.

Right outside my door.

I could hear someone breathing.

I stayed completely still.

The dispatcher asked me if the person was trying to get inside.

I whispered yes.

Then the bedroom door handle moved.

Slowly.

Down.

Up.

Down.

Up.

The person outside never spoke.

The police arrived about five minutes later.

By then, nobody was outside my door.

They searched the apartment.

Nothing.

They checked the hallway.

Nothing.

Then one officer asked me something.

“Did you hear anyone upstairs?”

I said yes.

He looked at the other officer.

Then asked:

“How long?”

I told him.

He didn’t say anything.

I asked what was wrong.

He told me they’d had previous calls from the building.

Not from my apartment.

From 3B.

I asked what kind of calls.

He said noise complaints.

I laughed.

“Yeah. There’s definitely somebody up there.”

He shook his head.

“No.”

I didn’t understand.

He said:

“That’s the problem.”

Apparently, 3B had been searched several times.

Nobody was ever found.

But tenants kept reporting the same things.

Footsteps.

Furniture moving.

Knocking.

Sometimes voices.

The previous tenant in 2B had moved out after six months.

The tenant before him lasted four.

The tenant before that?

Two months.

I asked why the landlord kept renting the apartment.

The officer just shrugged.

He told me to contact the property manager in the morning.

I didn’t sleep.

The next day, I went directly to the landlord’s office.

I demanded to know what was happening.

He looked exhausted.

He told me there was a history with 3B.

I asked him what that meant.

He said the previous owner had used the apartment as storage.

Years ago.

Before he bought the building.

I asked why nobody had access to it now.

He said the locks had been changed.

I asked for the key.

He said he didn’t have one.

That made no sense.

He owned the building.

He should have a key.

I asked him again.

He finally admitted something.

There was no current key for 3B.

The lock had been changed after the last tenant disappeared.

“Disappeared?”

He went quiet.

I asked what he meant.

He said the tenant had left without telling anyone.

But all of his belongings were still inside.

His car was still in the parking lot.

His phone was still plugged in beside his bed.

His wallet was still on the kitchen counter.

He was just gone.

I asked if police had investigated.

He said yes.

Nothing came of it.

I asked why nobody told me.

He said he didn’t think it was relevant.

I nearly laughed.

I told him I was moving out.

He said that was fine.

But I had to give thirty days’ notice.

I told him absolutely not.

He said he’d let me leave without the penalty.

That should have made me feel better.

It didn’t.

That night, I packed a bag.

I planned to stay at a hotel.

Before leaving, I decided to check my apartment one last time.

I walked through every room.

Kitchen.

Living room.

Bathroom.

Bedroom.

Everything looked normal.

Then I noticed something.

My bedroom ceiling had a small brown stain.

I hadn’t seen it before.

It was directly beneath the floor of 3B.

I touched it.

Dry.

I assumed it was an old water stain.

Then I noticed a second one.

And a third.

They formed a line.

Almost like footprints.

On my ceiling.

I stepped back.

That’s when I heard the footsteps upstairs.

12:11 AM.

One.

Two.

Three.

I checked the time.

12:12.

The footsteps continued.

12:13.

12:14.

I grabbed my phone and started recording.

12:15.

The footsteps stopped.

12:16.

Nothing.

12:17.

Silence.

Then something happened.

A single footstep came from directly above my bedroom.

But this one sounded different.

It sounded like someone stepping onto a wooden floor.

I heard another.

Then another.

And then…

A chair scraped across the floor.

I stared at the ceiling.

The scraping stopped directly above me.

Then I heard three knocks.

Not upstairs.

Inside the ceiling.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

I ran out of the apartment.

I stayed in a hotel that night.

The next morning, I called the police again.

I showed them the recordings.

They listened.

One officer asked me something.

“Where exactly were you standing?”

I told him.

He asked me to send him the video.

I did.

Later that afternoon, he called me.

He said they had checked 3B again.

Still empty.

Then he said something else.

There was an access panel in the hallway ceiling.

Maintenance had opened it.

Inside was an old electrical space.

Nothing unusual.

Except there were footprints.

Dusty footprints.

They went through the crawl space.

I asked where they led.

He said they ended above my apartment.

I didn’t know what to say.

He told me not to return until the landlord had changed the locks.

I agreed.

That evening, I went back with my landlord to collect the rest of my
things.

He brought a maintenance worker.

We entered my apartment together.

Nothing seemed wrong.

Then the maintenance worker stopped in the hallway.

He pointed upward.

“Do you hear that?”

We listened.

Nothing.

Then…

A tiny sound.

Three knocks.

Everyone froze.

The landlord immediately told us to leave.

The maintenance worker refused.

He said he wanted to see where it was coming from.

He got a ladder.

Opened the ceiling panel.

Shined his flashlight inside.

Then he went completely pale.

I asked him what he saw.

He climbed down.

He didn’t answer.

The landlord looked inside.

His face changed too.

I asked again.

Finally, the maintenance worker said:

“There’s another door.”

Inside the crawl space.

A small wooden door.

Hidden behind insulation.

It wasn’t on any building plans.

The landlord said he’d never seen it.

The maintenance worker opened it.

Behind it was a narrow space between the walls.

There was a mattress.

A flashlight.

Empty food containers.

Bottled water.

A blanket.

And dozens of photographs.

Photographs of the tenants.

Not old photographs.

Recent ones.

Me walking into the building.

Me getting into my car.

Me carrying groceries.

Me sleeping.

Me sitting on my couch.

I felt sick.

The police came.

They took everything.

They searched the building for hours.

They found another entrance to the hidden space.

It connected to 3B.

But 3B wasn’t empty.

Not completely.

There was a small section behind the bedroom wall.

Someone had removed part of it.

There was a ladder leading down into the crawl space.

The person had been moving between apartments.

The police never found them.

But they found something that made me stop sleeping entirely.

The photographs weren’t just of me.

They went back years.

Different tenants.

Different apartments.

Different people.

And in almost every photo…

The person taking the picture was standing inside the apartment.

The last photograph was taken two days before I moved in.

It showed my bedroom.

Empty.

Freshly painted.

Waiting for me.

On the back was a date.

And underneath it, four words.

“HE DOESN’T KNOW YET.”

I moved three states away.

I never contacted my landlord again.

I never looked up what happened to the building.

I didn’t want to know.

For almost a year, I convinced myself it was over.

Then, last month, I received a package.

No return address.

Inside was a photograph.

Just one.

It was a picture of my current bedroom.

Taken from the hallway.

I live on the third floor now.

There isn’t an apartment above me.

I checked.

Twice.

On the back of the photograph, someone had written:

“12:17.”

I haven’t heard anything yet.

But every night, I check the clock before I go to sleep.

And tonight…

It’s 12:16.

Goodbye.
